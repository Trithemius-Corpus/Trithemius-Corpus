*Distich by Rudolf Langius, canon of Munster, on Saint Anne.*

Great parent Anne, mother of the fruitful Virgin, hear the vows which pregnant women bring to you.

*Tetrastich by Jodocus Badius of Ghent, on Saint Anne.*

Anne, ornament of the world, mother of the mother of Jesus, may your grandson, for the merits of your daughter, give us the stars. Holy Anne, parent of the first fruits of our salvation, help us with your offspring and with your grandson.

*Rudolf Agricola, in his poem on Saint Anne, among other things, says:* 

Anne, parent of the highest parent, venerable mother, you who first open to peoples the way of salvation: from you all diseases flee, and pallor and pain, and all harmful things that settle in the mind and in the body. Whatever it is among men that wearies cares and hearts flees from you, as clouds driven away are wont to flee the winds.

*Rutgerus Venrai, canon regular of Hainaut, to the image of Saint Anne.*

Here pour forth devout prayers, knee bent, where Anne the parent sits blessed with her offspring. Do not doubt that you will obtain whatever you ask of her, for powerful Anne continually shines through her prayers.

*Distich by the same on Saint Anne.*

Hail, Anne, parent of the mother of Jesus Christ; we ask you, drive every wickedness away from us.

*Rosary of Saint Anne by Jodocus Beisselius, patrician of Aachen, to the reverend father lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict.*

Whoever shall wish to read this rosary for devotion in honor of the most blessed mother should begin with the Lord's Prayer together with the salutation of Saint Anne, which he should always repeat after two verses; this salutation is as follows.

Hail, venerable Anne, mother of the mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored before all women. Blessed are you by the Lord, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, and Jesus Christ, born of the virginity of your offspring. Amen. Our Father. Hail, mother of God.

Anne, may it be permitted to wretched me to utter your praises; for, if you allow it, I shall bring you garlands and roses. Hail.

Anne, you shine, venerable offspring of the kings of Judah, and also renowned through Joachim, your pious husband. Hail.

Anne, long barren, you wept in the love of your husband, the one glory and one honor of married women. But let her, by holy posterity, worship God. Hail.

Pious Anne, you seek offspring, and not for vain uses; 'Rejoice,' he says, 'behold, as a mother you shall have offspring, exulting.' Hail.

Anne, you shall bear offspring blessed with such great virtue that she shall shine with virginal honor as mother. Hail.

Anne, you shall bear pleasing offspring whom the world does not contain; God himself willingly enters her chaste womb. Hail.

Anne, from your blood at last shall arise blood which, when poured out, shall bring hope continually to wretched sinners. Hail.

Anne, so great an expectation befitted your birth-giving; it is no easy thing to be regarded as the mother of the mother of God. Hail.

All ages too shall proclaim you blessed. Our Father. Hail, mother of God.

Anne, you conceived Mary pure from evil, it is believed, and soon had her holy for God. Hail.

Anne, in your womb you bear her whom figures marked out, whom the holy fathers foretold as to come. Hail.

Anne, you bear the enclosed paradise, the tree of life, blessed and sacred with all the honor of the field. Hail.

Anne, heavy with child, you bear the garden which the flaming sword of the cherubim guards, glowing night and day. Hail.

Anne, you bear the flower that surpasses white lilies, and [unclear]. Hail.

Anne, you bear the bride of the true Solomon, and a house adorned inside and out with every gem. Hail.
