A devout mind will always find a way to honor the most holy Anne with due praises. In fasts and tears, and in alms and prayers, in acts of penance and disciplines, in song and readings, the most blessed mother is to be honored by us. But it is no small offense that we find no fitting history concerning the holy mother Anne which is in every respect suitable to her honor and feast. For the history now in use in many places, in diverse forms, is varied and not, as it seems to many, sufficiently elegant, since it is less worthy of such merits and in very many places seems apocryphal. Nor is there only one, but several are found, as seemed good to each person, with additions that are less approved. What shall I say about the office of the Mass, which we see is confused in many places by a similar variety? An office is sung by very many which a certain devout person, changing the meanings of the words but retaining the melody, drew from the Saturday office of blessed Mary; this seems less useful for devotion, since hearers, deceived by the similarity of the melody, think that the celebration is not about Saint Anne but about the Mother of God. If the subject is acceptable, at least let the melody be changed. Yet all these things could easily be corrected, if the authority of some pontiff intervened; relying on it, learned men in these matters could compose a history and an office regulated in sense and melody and approved by the Scriptures.

*That the most blessed mother Anne rewards her worshippers in manifold ways both in this life and in the life to come. Chapter 14*

If the frequent display of miracles wins reverence for the saints, blessed Anne is most worthy of the highest honor, for she daily bestows so many and such great benefits upon those who worship her according to their vows that there is no one who could recount them all. Indeed we know many miracles of Saint Anne, both new and ancient; but it is not our intention now to turn our mind to these, which we have learned are called into doubt by many, not because they contain any falsehood in themselves, but because most modern men, who seem learned to themselves, do not believe miracles but disparage them. Therefore we do not wish to give occasion for disparagement, nor to expose the present treatise to suspicion, lest they say that we write not correct things but apocryphal ones. For no one understands, no one believes, no one grasps how many benefits God daily confers on the lovers of Saint Anne, unless devout experience instructs him. But we know that through the invocation of Saint Anne many powerful, learned, noble, and ignoble men, virgins and women, young and old, have often been freed from the greatest tribulations, snatched from dangers, and miraculously helped in necessities.
