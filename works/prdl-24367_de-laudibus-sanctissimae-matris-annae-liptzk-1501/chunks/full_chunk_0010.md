For the grace of God is not well received when it is examined with human curiosity. While flavor is sought in discourse, inward sweetness is lost.

The Holy Spirit despises a proud dwelling. Therefore stop up reckless mouths, restrain tongues ill-skilled in speech, and do not speak falsehood against the Lord. Do not tear apart the devotion of the simple, lest it be counted heavily against you as a scandal. Let no one think me, in saying these things, an enemy of the liberal arts; let no one believe me a slanderer of the learned, since I have always cultivated and loved letters, have always been accustomed to honor learned men, and have sometimes defended them even to the point of hatred and persecution from the unskilled. But let those who seem learned in their own eyes take care, lest, in despising the devotion of the simple, they think them to be recalled from reverence for Saint Anne. But if any such men are to exist, I would believe no one learned to be among their number, since true learning is accustomed to aid devotion, not to rebuke it. Many very learned men also, still living today, splendidly exercising their talents in verse and prose, have dedicated them to the merits of Saint Anne.

*Against Those Who Tear Apart and Despise and Attack the Feast and Memory of Saint Anne. Chapter 4.*

Hear and take note, whoever you are who reviles the festive memory of the most holy mother Anne, who attacks her honor with impious loquacity, who with reckless presumption blasphemes her worshippers. What displeases you in the most holy mother of the Mother of God? Do you doubt her merits? Do you prove her degenerate? She is holy; she is beloved by God. Foolish and senseless man, who fault the good in the servants of God which you ought to have imitated; who withhold the honor owed to the saints; who persecute the worshippers of most blessed Anne without cause. For you reproach them and say: “Why do you take up new feasts, you who do not celebrate the ancient ones?” Listen, profane man. Does the feast of Saint Anne seem new to you, when from the records of the ancients it is proved to be most ancient? If it is new, how could the holy fathers have celebrated it in antiquity? It is not new, in whose honor Saint Augustine is said to have composed an elegant sermon a thousand years ago. It cannot be new, concerning which very ancient poems are extant. Or how will it be able to be new, whose golden name is found inserted in the most ancient calendars? But to you it is both new and rare, you who have never celebrated her memory. Can the memory of the most blessed mother Anne be new, whose house, in which she had been accustomed to dwell in ancient times, is read to have been consecrated as a church under her name not long after the Passion of the Lord?
