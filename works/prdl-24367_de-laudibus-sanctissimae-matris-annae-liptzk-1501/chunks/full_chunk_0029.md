So many and so great miracles concerning the blessed mother Anne have become known to me that, if I wished to write them all, I would be forced to enlarge this treatise excessively. The greatest of all miracles is if, by her merits, we may deserve to receive compunction and forgiveness of our sins; if we may overcome evil habit; if we may arrive at eternal life. For this end, and no other, she is to be venerated by us.

*Chapter XV.*

Because in these last days, as the charity of many grows cold, iniquity abounds, it is very necessary for us, my brothers, that just as we fall almost daily by sinning, so also through frequent zeal for holy exercise we may be renewed every day in good will. For there was never any institution so holy among human customs that with time it did not grow lukewarm from its first fervor. Thus all the orders of religious life, failing with the passage of time, have departed from their first institution; and unless by the diligence of the elders they had often been reformed to the regular paths of life, they would always have remained in their deformity. Among Christians too something similar is happening, for today they are found to have fallen far away from the first institution of the faith. Where now is the former fervor of our faith? Where the devotion of the people? Where the holy conduct of priests? Charity has grown cold, and all zeal for religion, together with it, has for the most part perished from human beings. Who would believe how great a loss the evil morals of Christians have brought about? Behold, as God permits it because of the crimes of human beings, the unbelieving Saracens, Turks, Tartars, and the other enemies of the Catholic faith, after slaughtering Christians, have brought Asia, Africa, the Hellespont, and a great part of Europe under their power, so that the twentieth part of the world which once was Christian is not Christian today. And in truth, unless the compassion of almighty God preserves the remnants left in Europe, in a short time an end will have been made of the Christian name. Since, therefore, weighed down by our sins, we are not able to obtain God’s mercy, the suffrages of the saints must be implored by us with diligent prayers, so that, appeased by their merits, the eternal Judge may grant us pardon and peace, which we do not deserve. And lest devotion fail over time, as is its custom, it must be labored over with great care, so that through the mutual exhortation of the brothers perseverance may follow. Hence a praiseworthy custom has grown strong in the Church of God: that the faithful establish congregations in honor of certain saints, which, for the increase of devotion, they call fraternities.
