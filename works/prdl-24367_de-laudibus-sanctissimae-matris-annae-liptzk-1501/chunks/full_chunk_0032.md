It is a fraternity plainly useful and always to be honored, which gives form to faith by works, raises up hope, stirs charity, and kindles devotion. For through it sluggishness is shaken from the minds of the faithful, and the fervor of holy renewal is inspired. He who attacks it is mad; he who speaks against it is more deranged than a deranged man; he who strives to destroy it begrudges Christian salvation. Many things indeed still ought to have been said in praise of this most holy fraternity, had not the occupation of household affairs claimed me for itself. But what has been said by me against its adversaries was uttered not with the intention of railing at them, but of correcting and amending them. Yet I trust that there are few who set themselves against the holy mother and her fraternity.

*Conclusion of this work and apology. Chapter 16.*

*On the insufficiency of the author, with an exhortation of the fraternity.*

Although from the beginning I had proposed to write more concerning the praises of the most holy mother Anne, nevertheless from this point it has now pleased me to stop, lest, if I extended the treatise without measure, I should smother the reader’s devotion by excessive length. For those who desire to persuade others must be found brief in speech and abundant in the majesty of their thoughts. From the first point I do not at all trust that I have gone astray; but what I have accomplished in the second will be judged by those who are going to read this labor of mine, such as it is. I have satisfied my promise; I have also satisfied my friends; but neither my devotion nor the dignity of the subject has been satisfied. I wished more than I could do, and I did less than I wished. Finally, who is sufficient to venerate with worthy praises Anne, the parent of the most blessed mother of God? I confess, therefore, that I am unworthy to speak of the most holy mother, since I am a sinful man and wrapped in offenses from my youth. Yet I could not keep silent from her praise, because necessity lay upon me, and devotion, strongly fortified by the prayers of friends, pressed me on. But after I had come thus far, the excellence of the subject overcame my impatient heat of summer, so that henceforth I ought to honor in silence her whom I am not sufficient to praise. Whatever, however, has been said and written by me in this work, I submit to the holy Roman Church and to all learned Catholic men, from whose definitive judgment I never intend to dissent. Whatever the most holy Roman Church determines is to be believed will be for me the firmest faith. Finally we turn our speech to you, friends, worshipers, brothers and sisters of the most holy mother Anne, asking that you receive in good part what has been written by us out of great devotion to the praise of so great a mother, and that with devout prayers you knock at the door of the holy patroness, so that with her accustomed loving-kindness she may come to our aid, take away our faults, obtain virtues for us, and secure eternal life. We also exhort you to take care to persevere in the love and fraternity of the most holy mother, honoring her with continual praises, so that, absolved from faults by her merits, you may lead a tranquil life here and an eternal life after this life.

*Here ends the book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, On the Praises of Saint Anne.*

*There follows a tetrastich on the most holy mother Anne.*

*Tetrastich of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the most holy mother Anne.*

Venerable Anne, mother of the life-giving Virgin,
Excellent in merits and mighty in loving-kindness:
No one goes away sad who worships and venerates you;
For you bring to all the gentle aid of salvation.

*Distich of the same, which he placed by the image of Saint Anne.*

Whoever in adversities has appealed to Saint Anne
With devout prayers, he will be well protected.

*Tetrastich of Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate, on Saint Anne.*

Anne has never dismissed anyone’s prayers empty,
But, radiant, has always brought the desired aid.
Therefore let us heap great honor upon the pious mother,
Who allows no one’s prayers to be empty.

*Tetrastich of Theodoricus Gresmundus the Younger of Mainz to Saint Anne.*

Hail, Anne, parent of the Virgin Mary,
Auspicious, worthy to begin the work of our salvation.
Since human redemption was begun through you, I pray,
Continue it by your prayers, covering our crimes.
