Let vain arguments cease; let words of excuse depart far away: he is proved to love the daughter less, who, that is, does not venerate Saint Anne. Let the man hardened in heart, and reprobate in piety, blush, who says that he loves the Mother of God and is proved not to love Anne, her mother. It is a foolish and ruinous presumption that he should take confidence from the daughter's patronage who scorns to honor her venerable mother. For whoever has the mother hostile will not be able to find the daughter appeased; for the daughter loves her mother, and the whole host of the heavenly court venerates Anne. If anyone has Anne as advocate, he will have all the saints in heaven as patrons. Therefore let us love the mother, dearest brothers, who can bring us into the sight of God; for she is pleasing and nearest to God.

*On the feast and commemoration of the most holy mother Anne, always to be held by us in honor. Chapter 12.*

Finally, if we love Anne, the most blessed parent of the Mother of God, if we wish to attend her with due honor, we must celebrate with yearly devotion her honorable feast, which is on the seventh day before the Kalends of August [26 July]. Nor should we believe this sufficient for our devotion, unless we take care to frequent her commemoration more often, as that of a most singular patroness. But if an adversary should falsely wish to object to us the novelty of this feast, let us reply: We are by no means introducing new feasts, but renewing ancient ones. And on this matter I believe I have disputed sufficiently above; I do not doubt that the devout venerators of Anne have been satisfied by those things. Moreover, to wish to satisfy the curious and the proud is superfluous, since piety does not move them, devotion does not stir them, charity does not inflame them. Therefore let the devout venerators of blessed Anne believe, and hold without any hesitation, that her feast is ancient and was customarily celebrated by the ancients with the greatest reverence, as was fitting for the mother of the most holy Mother of God. For in Judea and the regions overseas her commemoration was most celebrated among faithful Christians, until at last, when the land had been seized by unbelievers, it was for a long time [unclear] neglected. But in the regions of Europe her feast was not so celebrated, because many of them came to the faith rather late. Yet in certain places of Europe there was always a solemn remembrance of Anne, which had been instituted by the faithful coming from Palestine. The altars erected in her honor from ancient times in various churches prove this. The very ancient oratories also prove this, and various [unclear] of the fathers concerning the praises of Anne.
