*Poem of Adewernherus of Themar on most holy Anne, to the Reverend Father and Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot*

In praise of the renowned grandmother we sing;
Jesus, dear grandson, move the plectra of the heart,
and you, the daughter, chaste mother,
do not be absent from the choir as it sings.

Anne, offspring of kings, long remains
childless with her husband; she laments herself barren;
weeping, she prays for offspring,
and her tears do not flow in vain.

Heavenly messengers promise offspring;
pregnant, joined to her husband, then in childbirth,
she is proved a mother by the child
through whom our salvation came.

Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green;
a fruitful rod comes forth with fertile shoot,
by whose flower, made perfect,
we live again through death.

By this birth Joachim is made worthy
to stand in the shrines, removing the pontiff’s reproaches.
Thus, Anne, prepare us to bring pure prayers
to your grandson.

Commend us to the boy whom your pious chastity
bore as a mother; by prayer we ask this.
O blessed matron,
Christ denies you nothing.

To Christ, who is consort with the Father and the Spirit,
who reigns, be glory through all ages,
whom they give as reconciled.
Amen.

This hymn, however, is sung to the tune of that one which, on the Nativity of Saint Mary, is used by many churches, and begins “Rejoice in your inmost bowels, mother,” etc. From Heidelberg Castle, the third kalends of February, 1493.

*Poem or hymn of Johannes [Herbsluterburgensis?] on Saint Anne, to the Reverend Father and Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot*

Rejoice, Anne, refuge for the wretched;
we pray, we sing, we bring forth
praises now to you on the feast;
be gracious with your offspring.

Rejoice, earth shining, fertile with grass,
and wet with abundant deifying dew,
from which was born the Virgin,
purer than ether, knowing no sin.

Rejoice, you who were of royal seed;
now, conceived, given to a husband, you had led
long in marriage without offspring;
a mother in childbed, your vows were fulfilled.

Rejoice, you who blushed at the pontiff’s reproach
and were confounded; you call upon God with tears.
Heard, you receive your husband as he comes to meet you
at the gate called Golden.

Rejoice, you who, joyful, embraced
Joachim your husband as he met you, taught by deity,
and thus the mother of heaven, and of your Jesus,
is conceived in the womb.

Rejoice, simple wisdom, discerning with angelic insight,
which praises in choir, and, divided threefold,
you distribute your goods to the temple and to the poor,
and a part to your servants.

Rejoice: the people of the faithful celebrates your feasts,
as their patroness; and with the Father, the Spirit,
and Christ the Son, by your petitions,
Anne, may you render them propitious. Amen.

This hymn takes the melody of that which is sung concerning the Ascension of the Lord, namely “Now the solemn feast,” etc., and that one concerning martyrs, “Renowned by the merit of the saints,” etc. From Heidelberg, [unclear] nones of February, in the year 1493.

*Elegiac poem of Rutger Venray, canon regular in Haina, of the diocese of Worms, on Saint Anne*

Hail, bright mother of the Virgin Mother,
of the Father’s splendor, of the brightness of untouched light.
You bore for the Lord the divine, holy maiden,
from whom Jesus, kindly light of the world, came forth.

Speak steadfastly to your grandson the groaning of your daughter,
although he himself made the heavens and the ground.
Your grandson in the flesh, Jesus himself, joined supreme deity
to flesh, for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always existed, nevertheless he chooses
and deigns to be called your grandson.
Do not hesitate to strike such a grandson with prayers.
Anne, we ask you, and you will bear all things asked.

If he has not bent ready ears at the first petitions,
Anne, return more quickly to your daughter.
Let your daughter show her breasts to her son Christ;
straightway the kindly mother will obtain what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to obtain by prayer,
let the daughter seek aid together with her mother.
My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that straightway
the Lord will hear both as they pray with gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers one must seek
the aid of Anne and of Mary alike.
It is certain that, if you honor the mother of the mother of the Lord,
you yourself worship her under the title of mother of the Virgin.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it becomes clear
what praises you prepare for the Lord in his mother.
To the daughter, to the son, be praise, power, and honor of Anne,
and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*Sapphic poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the day of Saint Anne*

Let us celebrate the sacred feasts of Anne,
of the exalted mother, rich in merits,
who brought forth into the world the parent
of God the Father’s Son.

Her life, always to be venerated by us,
is deservedly praised as blessed;
by the gift of signs she increases relief
for wretched minds.

She heals the languid, purges all evils,
brightens the hearts of the sorrowful with rejoicing,
orders what is fitting, rules and governs,
smooths rough things.
