I shall sing the praises of the most blessed mother Anne with what devotion I can. For if we are commanded to praise God generally in his saints, we are without doubt urged to honor his parents more especially. For he who commanded us to honor our parents by no means excludes his own according to the flesh. It is therefore holy, dearest brothers, to bestow diligent praises of devotion upon those from whose blood we believe the Son of God took flesh. Among them the most blessed Mother of God rightly holds the primacy; so great are the merits of her holiness that she has been exalted above the choirs of angels in the holy heavens. After her, her most holy mother Anne, of whom we have undertaken to speak, just as she is nearest in the flesh to the Son of God, so by the merits of her virtues she is believed to be acceptable before the other saints. Nor may we doubt concerning her holiness, since we believe that she gave birth to the Mother of God without the stain of original guilt. For she could not have been the mother of so great a virgin unless she was holy, through whose virginal birth we have received the remedies of eternal salvation. Anne is the holy root; Mary the virgin is the most holy tree; the fruit is the Son of God, the medicine of souls. A good tree, a holy tree, a tree most fruitful without human cultivation, which took its beginning from a holy root and its growth from the richness of heavenly rain. A good root never produces a bad tree, nor does a good tree bear bad fruits. A good tree cannot stand without a root, nor bear good fruit unless it is good. As a tree is known from its fruit, so what sort the mother is is declared in the daughter. In the most holy Mother of God we understand what we ought to think concerning the holiness of her mother. It was not fitting that any mother but a holy one should conceive and bear the Mother of God, a child without the guilt of original stain; it was not fitting except for one most pleasing to God and devoted above all then living in Israel. For that she had loved God above all things is shown by the first instruction of her daughter. As a little child she is read to have been entrusted to the Lord; she is recorded to have served God in the temple; she is believed to have been conceived without any fault and always to have lived holy in the world. What more do you require? Surely so excellent a life of the daughter from infancy is sufficient to declare the merits of the mother. If Anne had not been most holy, she would never have instructed her daughter so diligently and devoutly for the honor of God. As the mothers are, such also we know their daughters to have been brought up. But the most blessed mother, just as in merit and holiness she surpasses all women, so she took care to instruct her daughter from youth more diligently than all the saints in the love of God. If therefore we love the Son of God, if we honor his most pure Mother, why do we, lukewarm, keep silent from the praise of his most sacred grandmother and mother Anne? If one despises the offspring of an emperor, he does injury to the king.
