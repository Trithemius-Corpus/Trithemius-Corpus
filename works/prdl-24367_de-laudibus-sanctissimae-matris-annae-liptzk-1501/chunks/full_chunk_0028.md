Who could enumerate how many have been healed from the ruinous sadness of mind and melancholy by the aid of the most pious mother Anne? How many also, secure amid adversities, fearless among enemies and robbers, safe amid the perils of the sea, have escaped? Anne, most holy mother, has been accustomed to rescue many devoted to her from various temptations, to free them from the contagion of pestilence, and to cure them from various illnesses. Who can recount how many she has brought back from the worst despair to hope of mercy; how many she has recalled from the hardest habit of sin to the paths of penitence; how many living lukewarmly in religion and outside it she has inflamed with the fire of divine love? By her merits and suffrages women in childbirth have been freed from dangers otherwise close to death. Many also, as we read, have been snatched from the sentence of perpetual damnation. Finally, it is said that she has guarded some of her devotees from the disgrace of infamy; it is recorded that she has freed others from desperate sickness, some from chains and prisons, and certain ones even from the imminent dangers of death. We read also that by her merits some have been called back from death to life; others, at the point of death, have been most mercifully visited by her and, before they left the world, made already secure concerning heaven. Often too she is said to have enlightened the dull wits of certain persons, so that those who before had been despised as without learning afterwards became objects of admiration to many. She is said at times to have strengthened orators when invoked, so that they not only found themselves constant but also made readier in speaking. Things lost, therefore, we read, have finally been wonderfully found through the invocation of Anne. But why do I strive into immensity? For if I had a voice of iron and a hundred tongues, I could not run through all Anne’s benefits by which she is accustomed to come to the aid of her devotees. Indeed, as many benefits of hers are found as there are inconveniences of human life. From whatever tribulation anyone cries to her with devotion, he is able to obtain help. She extinguishes the flames of carnal concupiscence. She turns the ruinous sadness of mind into saving joy. She gives the necessities of life. She preserves a good and fair reputation. She frees women who are in peril in childbirth. She lessens pains. She puts demons to flight. Invoked, she comes to the aid of those laboring in tribulation; she brings travelers back to their own country; she hears the wretched who cry to her. She knows how to give all things which it is lawful for a Christian person to ask, provided that devoted faith commends the one who prays. But concerning all the things which we have touched upon only briefly in commemorating them generally, we have at hand particular examples that have actually occurred, by which we could recount not uncertain things in writing, but deeds in truth.
