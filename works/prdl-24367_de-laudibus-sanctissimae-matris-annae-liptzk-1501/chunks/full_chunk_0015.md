She did not seek to be seen outside by people. She strove to observe concord in what is good and peace with her neighbors. She did injury to no one. By word or deed she disturbed no one. She was rarely seen in public. She did not sit in the streets with little women gossiping, and she did not stand in the conversation of slanderers. For that most prudent woman knew that sin is not absent from much speaking. Therefore with all watchfulness she guarded her heart, lest she should ever offend in speech.

Thus, intent upon holy studies and exercises, she merited to be made a mother from one barren, as almighty God had ordained from the beginning. Therefore the holy woman conceived from a holy husband. She conceived without original stain; she gave birth without fault. O holy conception, and previously unheard of. She conceived from a man, but this conception was never subject to any charge of crime. She conceived according to the order of nature, but beyond the custom of nature. O holy conception, to be celebrated with continual praises, which prepares for the Son of God a mother without stain, concerning which the choir of angels rejoices, and the whole world throughout the earth exults. Truly a conception to be honored above all, which was pleasing to almighty God before it was fulfilled. Truly worthy conception, which, with the Holy Spirit preserving it, took its beginning from such purity that it was disposed to conceive and bear God.

Therefore Anne, the most holy parent, is to be revered by us with the greatest devotion, since she conceived the mother of God without any stain. How worthy an offering she offered to God, when she presented her most holy daughter in the temple. Finally, it pertains to declaring the merits of the holy mother that the daughter whom she conceived and bore she consecrated to the Lord at once from infancy. As was the mother's manner of life, so was the daughter's instruction. No one can teach another what he himself has not learned; no one badly instructed instructs children well. Children frequently advance according to the habits of their parents, and what they have seen practiced by them they embrace more gladly. A mother worthy of her most holy daughter, so that from her from whom she had drawn the material of her flesh without crime, from her she might suck in the teachings of piety. The grace of God had anticipated both; it had filled both. But it was earlier in the mother, more abundant in the daughter. The holy mother taught her daughter what she herself had first learned from her parents, although she had no need of human instruction, whom the Holy Spirit filled from conception. For neither did she need maternal teaching, who was full of heavenly grace.

Learn, O mothers, to instruct your daughters for the honor of God, not to love the pomps of the world, to flee the public gaze of the crowd, to remain at home. For the Holy Spirit hates what is public; he loves the secret place of a pure mind. Why do you run about through streets and squares, if you love virginal honor? The archangel came to Mary not in public but in secret; not as she was speaking in the street, but as she was silent in her chamber.
