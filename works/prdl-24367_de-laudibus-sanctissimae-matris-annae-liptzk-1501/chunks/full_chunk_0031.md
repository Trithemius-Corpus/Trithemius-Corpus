This fraternity of the most blessed mother Anne is not superstitious, as you say, O impious slanderers, but is true, upright, holy, in which the worship of God is not diminished but increased; obedience is not despised but commended; even the catholic faith is not weakened by this holy fraternity, as you think, but renewed and defended, while through the exercises of holy charity it is illumined, that it may be formed. But as for your lament that the parish churches are deserted, that must be imputed in no small measure to your own offenses: you demand milk and wool from the sheep of the Lord, yet you do not feed the sheep themselves as first of all you ought to have done, nor do you guard them. Why do you pretend that offerings are being denied to you, when you abound in many riches? The Lord Christ is shepherd of shepherds and vicar. If he wished the faithful to be free in hearing masses, there is no one who can prevent him. You have not seen all the privileges of monks, nor will you see them. Who does not know that for your purses you wage war against holy fraternities, and that you tear at monks not from love of God, but of gold? Behold, the supreme pontiff has confirmed this fraternity; behold, Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, than whom there is no other more excellent, approved it by ordinary authority, and you foolish men still murmur. Cease, I beseech you, cease from this folly, and do not censure what you know such great pontiffs to have approved. Believe me, it is not good to stretch out the tongue against the saints of God; it is not good to wish to prohibit the devotion of so many people toward Saint Anne. He draws very near to confusion who opens his mouth against Saint Anne. If you do not believe my words, observe those who are such, and by experience you will know that I have spoken the truth. It is rash to censure a good which is seen to come to many for salvation. Many benefits come to devout people from the celebration of this most happy fraternity; those who are strangers to it are for the most part without them. It also draws its worshippers to charity toward God and neighbor, creates the concord of brotherly love, calls them back from vices and sins, and inflames desire for the love of heavenly happiness. I pass over how many masses, how many prayers, how many alms, how many sacred works are done daily in this fraternity, which otherwise would be entirely omitted. Would that the Almighty might illumine the hearts of all Christians toward similar exercises of devotion; would that he might inflame the minds of all to duly honor this fraternity. How much devotion, how much peace, and how much concord could abound in the catholic Church! Indeed, if all were to come together in this fraternity, it could happen that, through love of the holy mother Anne, they would cease from mutual injuries and be made more eager in the observance of God’s commandments. For true fraternity cultivates and nourishes brotherly love, nor does devotion allow those to be unlike whom the observance of so great a congregation has made concordant in love of most holy Anne.
