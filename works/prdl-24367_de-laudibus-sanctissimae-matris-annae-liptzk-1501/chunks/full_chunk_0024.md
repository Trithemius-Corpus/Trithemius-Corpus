Yet this must be our task: that it be celebrated by faithful and devout Christians with all reverence. For if we observe the feasts of other saints of Christ with yearly devotion, why should we not celebrate the feast of Anne, most beloved of God? For she is not inferior to the other saints in merits and glory, since, while still mortal, she was of such dignity that she gave birth to the mother of him who, immortal, lives forever in heaven. And just as we make some daily commemoration of our most special patrons, so too, concerning Saint Anne, as right devotion shall suggest, let us offer something of praise to God every day.

And although devout worshippers ought always to be ready for her honor, nevertheless on her birthday especially they ought to be solicitous, since her commemoration is performed not only on earth but is also honored in heaven with the votive praises of the saints. It also accords with devotion and befits piety that, just as the Sabbath day is observed in honor of the most holy mother of God by clergy and people with masses and prayers, so the commemoration of her mother should be celebrated at least once in the week. For by anxiety over household affairs we are quickly drawn away from devotion; to it we must necessarily be restored at least once in the week, lest, if we put off the honor of venerable Anne too long, we lose her protection through sluggishness.

For celebrating this weekly memorial, Tuesday seems sufficiently fitting, because she is read to have been both born and died on that day. Finally, in many places a praiseworthy custom has prevailed, that the commemoration of Saint Anne is celebrated on Tuesday with no less reverence than the Sabbath day is kept in memory of the mother of God in the church of the faithful. We have seen among the Sicambrians and the Grunes that on Tuesday masses are always sung in honor of blessed Anne, lamps are kindled, offerings are made, and the poor are more generously refreshed. We have also known among them certain devout worshippers of Anne who, for the honor of Jesus, Mary, and Anne, every Tuesday would light three candles on her altar, make three special alms, and receive as many pardons or disciplines.

This custom, moreover, took its origin from a miracle of Saint Anne which happened in Pannonia, where she advanced a desolate and very poor young man to great honors and riches. If anyone desires to have knowledge of this matter, let him read the said miracle, which is now so widespread among the worshippers of blessed Anne that it cannot be hidden from anyone. And we have learned that this is done by many not only on Tuesday, but every day, as the devotion of each person requires. Therefore priests of the Lord who desire to be devout worshippers of the most holy mother Anne should take care to celebrate devoutly in her commemoration at least once in the week. Whoever has taken up that devotion as his custom, we believe, will have Anne as a pious advocate in every necessity. Therefore let no one be silent from the praise of so great a patroness.
