Look to both: mothers, to the mother; virgins, to the virgin. Believe me, it does not befit your honor to take pleasure in public; it is not expedient for virgins to be greeted in the marketplace, because it lessens reverence for honor when someone frequently goes into public. Virginity with modesty flees the public. Without modesty there is no sincere virginity, nor can what is too public remain pure.

*Chapter 7.*

*That the holy mother conceived her daughter, the most blessed Mother of God, without original stain.*

Since the matter has come to declaring the merits of holiness of the most blessed mother Anne, by no means must the prerogative of so great an honor be passed over by us in silence. And indeed we know that this purest conception has been set up as a sign, one which many have contradicted down to the present day; while they wish to defend it by the custom of nature, they are not afraid to attack the grace of God. Why do fools bark? Why do the inexperienced murmur? Is it not lawful for God to do what he wills? Behold, the Church venerates the conception of the Mother of God without stain; behold, it celebrates the feast with annual devotion, and captious men strive to stain it with rash presumption. Learned and most learned men, past and present, in various kinds of writing, proclaim in their lucubrations that this conception was never subject to original stain; and still the sons of Adam rashly attack it. Men of the religious orders of almost every kind confess, believe, and proclaim that she was always most pure; to wish to condemn them as erring is extreme madness. Behold, secular men, religious, devout and learned monks celebrate in verse and prose a conception always most pure, and lukewarm and unlearned men, for whom it seems enough to know the name of philosophy, attempt to defile the holiness of the Mother of God. Many, I say, most learned men have published various works in its defense. What learned man ever wrote the contrary? But you, [unclear], unworthy even of your name, who, setting your mouth against heaven, impiously blasphemed against the most pure Mother of God, will not have done this without grave chastisement from God, since you were moved to write not by piety but by impiety. Finally, we know that an answer has been given more than abundantly to your foolish rashness by learned men in various styles. For you depart confounded not by our weapons, but by the power of God; nor do I condemn your error, but your arrogance, your rashness, your presumption.
