If you love God, if you love Jesus Christ, the Savior of all, do not do such injury to his most holy mother, concerning whom no one is permitted to think anything except what is most holy. To believe that her conception was always most pure is not a matter of necessity but of piety; not to believe it is a mark of human slowness. But not believing, to attack it, and to wish to defend the contrary opinion, not only is not free from fault, but is also a matter of great rashness and folly. For rashness is rightly blamed where piety is rewarded. Let no one think it a small sin which is committed against God and his mother. But he who loves the Son of God does not open his mouth against the honor of his mother, does not disparage her purity, does not obstinately defend an uncertain opinion; and because where the devotion of the humble receives reward, the presumption of the proud will find torment. For the mother of God is believed to have been worthy to be conceived without original stain, she who, as a virgin, was to bring forth him who would wipe away the crimes of the whole world. Worthy, too, of such a conception was the blessed mother Anna, whose noble manner of life was most pleasing to God. Do not be surprised that the mother of God, contrary to the custom of nature, was preserved from original sin, she whom God, the giver of grace, had prepared as a dwelling for himself. Consider the offspring, the mother of God, and you will not be surprised at the conception without stain. The things that are said are great, but true, but holy, but fitting for the mother whose conception grace forestalled; and at this nature marvels. We too marvel, because we faithfully believe. But the adversaries, not believing, do not marvel, but are confounded. If you can grasp these things, believe; if you do not grasp them, do not examine them, if you do not wish to sin. For it is better to keep reverent silence concerning the purity of the most blessed mother of God than to say unworthy things. Therefore take care always to honor with humble silence those things which you cannot understand.

*How the most blessed mother Anna presented her sweetest daughter to the Lord in the temple. Chapter VIII.*

In the beginning of the book of Kings we read that Anna, the wife of Helchana, offered Samuel her son, whom she had asked from the Lord, after the years of nursing, to the temple to serve God. So pleasing to God was Samuel's offering that he not only chose him as priest, after Heli had been rejected, but also filled him with the spirit of prophecy and wisdom. What, brothers, did the offering of Samuel signify other than that the most blessed mother of God is presented by her mother to the Lord in the temple? O how pleasing to almighty God was that offering, in which even then she was preparing a dwelling most worthy for the Only-begotten.
