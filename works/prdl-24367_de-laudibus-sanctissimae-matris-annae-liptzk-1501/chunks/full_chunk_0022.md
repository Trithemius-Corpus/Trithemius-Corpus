Believe me: there is no tribulation, no distress, no necessity that can withstand the prayers of Anne. Whatever weighs upon you, whatever troubles you, whatever is opposed to a good will, refer it to Anne, and for the glory of God she will free you. It is impossible for her not to obtain everything that she wishes. The King of heaven is her grandson; he will not be able to deny anything to his sweetest grandmother. The Mother of God is her daughter; she cannot pour out prayers to God in vain. The whole court of the heavenly fatherland loves Anne as a mother, venerates her as a lady; if she asks anything of God, the prayers of all the saints will come together for the desired end. Blessed is the man who has Anne as his advocate with continual devotion and diligent prayers.

*Why holy mother Anne, as a most pious advocate, is to be held by us in the greatest honor. Chapter 11.*

How great the honor and how great the reverence with which most blessed Anne is to be honored by us, we sufficiently perceive from what has already been said, since in the heavenly fatherland she is praised and honored with such, and indeed ineffable, praises by the most high God himself and by all the saints. For if those citizens of the heavenly fatherland, now established in the greatest tranquility, venerate and love her as a most powerful lady, how much more ought we wretched ones, still placed amid many tribulations, to invoke and honor her as a pious patroness. Indeed she is to be worshiped by us with the greatest reverence, through whose patronage we can safely escape all the evils of the passing world. And truly the mother is always to be venerated with worthy praises, who at her birth carried enclosed beneath her breast the beginning of our redemption. Deservedly, I say, she is to be honored, who bore for us the Mother of God without stain, through whom the Son of God came to the world as Savior. Therefore, just as we venerate and worship the most blessed parent of God because, an immaculate virgin, she bore the Redeemer for us, so also it is fitting for us to honor Anne because she poured forth for us the mother of the Savior without fault. If we did not have the mother, whence would the daughter have come? Let no one doubt, let no one waver, let no one be inflamed with wicked thoughts, because if Anne had not been most holy, she would not have been chosen as the mother of the Mother of God. For it was fitting that she who was to bear the mother of God should be adorned with the splendor of every holiness, because she was to become the mother not of just any virgin, but of that one than whom, among the pure human creatures of God, none purer or holier could have been found. Therefore most blessed Anne was worthy for this office by the gift of God; she was worthy by the merit of her life; she was also worthy by the testimony of the law. Worthy, I say, was the mother of the Mother of God, who had learned to love God above all things, who had learned to keep his law without offense. There was no one holier than she in Israel, who is believed to have been honored by God with such great gifts. A barren woman conceived from seed, but without the stain of seed.
