*This Sapphic hymn is sung to the melody of “Ut queant laxis,” “Vita sanctorum,” “Virginis proles,” “Quo chorus vatum,” “Iste confessor,” and similar hymns, which are Sapphic only. From Sponheim, on the Nones of July, in the year of the Lord 1494.*

*An elegiac poem by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, concerning the holy mother Anne.*

How great the rewards Anne gives back to those who worship her,
no one grasps in mind nor can speak with the mouth.
She drives away hardships, obtains pardon, increases
ornaments of honor for her own, and makes them be in heaven.
Too many bitter things, by contrast, accompany those who despise
mother Anne; look out for yourself, wicked one.
She crushes the proud, casts the powerful down from honor,
and allows them often to suffer great scandals.
This is plain and remains fixed: Anne offers joyful things
to those who honor her, but brings many sorrows to her enemies.
Therefore, men, I advise you: serve so great a patroness;
and let the choir of women also be present together in love.
It is also clearly established by examples that she helps pious hearts.
*From Sponheim, the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August, 1493.*

*A Sapphic poem by Rutgerus Siccamber, as a hymn in praise of Saint Anne.*

We sing your praises, venerable
Anne; favor the melodies of those who praise you,
obtaining pardon for all, and giving
the rewards of salvation.

You, Anne, parent of Christ’s mother,
dwelling in the highest citadel of heaven,
obtain whatever you seek from the begotten son
of the Almighty.

You heal the sick, cleanse what is stained,
make your faithful servants attain their vow,
and finally make them climb to the heavenly kingdom
in the threefold realm.

By your approved conduct, Anne,
you bent the highest Lord, and by many prayers,
that he should grant you to become in the world
parent of so great an offspring.

To the highest Father, to the heavenly Son,
to the sacred Spirit be honor and power,
brightness, virtue, piety, and vigor
through all ages.

*Another by the same.*

Let us all resound the diligent praises
of Anne, mother of the Virgin Mother,
with all our strength, from the marrow of the soul,
with ringing voice.

She brought forth for our wretched race,
stained with the defilement of mother Eve,
a noble offspring, and gave to the world
a radiant beam.

This light of the world drove away
the shadows of the darkened world,
radiating all with heavenly light,
as holy John proclaims in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine girl,
whose begotten son you call your grandson
in the flesh only, do not disdain
the people praying to you.

Virgin, favor the earnest prayers
of your mother Anne, who asks for us.
You, grandson Jesus, readily fulfill
her prayer.

To the Son of Anne’s daughter and mother,
with God most high, be honor and power;
equal honor to the blessed Spirit
for all time.

*Another iambic dimeter poem by the same, which is sung to the melody of Ambrose’s hymns.*

Let us, bowed down, honor
Anne’s blessed feast,
singing due thanks with our voices
and from our inmost hearts.

She is the mother of the kindly Virgin,
who brought forth the radiance of the world;
she is the one who, by virtues,
made herself most pleasing to God.

She repels harms and grants
grace by her prayers;
she relieves grievous diseases,
of the body as well as of the spirit.

In praise of [unclear], king of heaven,
to you with ringing songs,
and at the same time to the Son and the Spirit,
we utter pious praises. Amen.

*An Alcmanian poem by the same, which is sung to the melody “Crux fidelis” or “Pange lingua.”*

This company, with voice and vows
and pious hearts,
is eager, mother Anne,
to proclaim your feasts so splendidly.

Be present kindly, we pray,
to those who praise you together.
You are the parent of Mary’s mother;
you are most pleasing to God.

You preserved the beginnings of the Law
which Moses once bestowed
on the ancient fathers
and on the Hebrew peoples.

You obtain wondrous, gracious gifts
for the faithful
by pleasing intercession
with the Son of the high Father.

You heal the sick, and grant
that hearts be chaste.
