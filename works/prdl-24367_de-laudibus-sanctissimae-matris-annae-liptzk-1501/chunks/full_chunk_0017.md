To attack, and rashly to define what you do not know, is an unheard-of crime. If we are instructed that those things which we do not know, with what intention they are done, are to be interpreted in the better part, how much more ought we to receive with piety the hidden works of God, whose reasons we do not know? None of us was present at God’s counsel; no one knew the manner of the conception. But, led by many reasons, we, out of pious belief, believe that she always existed most pure; the adversaries, led away by consideration of nature, with rash presumption defend the contrary opinion. But it is better to render an account for piety toward the parent of God than for rashness. If we err, piety, indeed God himself, is the cause; but if you err, O adversaries, say for what cause. What consideration moves you to say so boldly that the most pure conception of the mother of God was subject to original guilt? I know what he will answer: love of truth. But show that your opinion is true, and you will be believed lovers of truth. Yet you will not show it, because you do not understand, because you do not believe. You do not defend the truth, which you do not know. For no one properly defends what he does not know. Consideration of human nature deceives you; while you carefully turn over its custom, you do not weigh God, the establisher of that same nature. He who made nature is above nature; God, the founder of the law, is not restricted under the condition of human law, but he who willed that a virgin should give birth, even contrary to the order of nature, also preserved her from sin. Therefore the cause of the opposing opinion takes its beginning from rashness, and for that reason it deserves not reward from God but vengeance; for just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so the error concerning the conception does not damn, but obstinacy does. Finally, if examples and memorials of the ancients are to be believed, we read that many have perished most miserably who attacked the purity of the glorious conception, but we find that no defender of it was ever struck by God. For does not the greatest dignity of the mother of God, when considered, admonish us to the defense of the most holy conception, which shone forth such and so great that she was most worthy of every honor? If we are deceived, we are excused by holy piety. But you do not have the bowels of mercy; therefore will the Virgin Mary deliver you? But you make light of her honor. Do you not love the Son of God, but not his mother? The prayers of the saints, but you disparage their queen? Therefore you have no excuse, because you offend God and all his saints. You arm yourselves with scholastic arguments and are naked of piety.
