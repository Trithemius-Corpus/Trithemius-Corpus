Therefore I admonish you, O men distinguished in learning and knowledge of letters, not to despise the devotion of the simple, nor to censure the cult of the most holy mother Anne as though it were new, but rather, as far as you can, to imitate it. For I was not persuaded in vain to say these things.

There are indeed among you, if I may say so without offense to good men, some who, puffed up with the conceit of pride, despise all the exercises of the simple in devotion. They scorn the miracles and examples of the saints as delusions, and think that nothing holy should be admitted which they do not prove by the arguments of philosophers. All revelations shown by God to devout persons they regard as lies or women’s dreams. They call the legends of the saints fables, and while they rashly proclaim their own learning, they shamelessly attack the great works of God. The treatises, too, of the holy fathers and of devout men, because they do not display Ciceronian eloquence, they reject as lacking learning; and they despise the words of heavenly instruction because they are the instruction of the simple.

O fools and subverters, why do you deceive yourselves? Why, though you are Christians, do you despise ecclesiastical simplicity? The grace of God is not known in eloquence, but is acquired in holy simplicity. At the final judgment of God, Quintilian eloquence will not be required of you, but a Christian life. Ornate speech is not rewarded by God, but humble devotion. There sophistical arguments will cease, where the secrets of hearts will be manifest. Then the devout simple will appear in glory, and the learned proud in punishment. Behold, the unlearned simple rise up and receive the kingdom of God in their devout simplicity, and you, with your doctrines, descend in pride and arrogance to hell.

For you the fame of your learning is enough, by which, after the example of the ancients, you may acquire immortality of name among posterity. But so be it. Let what you seek come to pass: let your name and reputation be proclaimed with praise in the world as long as it shall stand. What will it profit you? Truly foolish consolation, if you are praised where you will never be, and are tormented where you will remain forever without end. Therefore I warn and counsel you: lay aside your haughtiness, drive away your madness, cast off your swelling pride, so that you may be able to escape the torment of hell. For God resists the proud. He regards not learning but humble devotion; he rewards not words but works, not knowledge but life, not doctrine but conscience.

Rather, change your purpose, and do not despise the devotion of the simple, but imitate it. No one understands the gift of God except the one who receives it. With what rashness, then, do you tear at what is not understood? The gift of God is not enclosed under the reasoning of a philosopher. Devotion is rare among the learned, because while they trust in the depth of their acquired doctrine, they withdraw farther from simple devotion.
