Now therefore, if we believe these and similar confraternities of the saints to be useful for obtaining God’s mercy for the people, we trust that those which are established in memory of the most blessed Mother of God and of Anne, her most holy mother, are better. For by as much as the Mother of God, together with her venerable mother, surpasses the other saints in heaven in merits and holiness, by so much does a confraternity dedicated to her name hold a more eminent place. Hence the faithful, moved by pious devotion, in order that they might deserve to have her as a ready advocate with God, established in her honor a confraternity under the name of the Rosary, which has now become so widespread through various regions of the world that there is scarcely any province in which several are not found.

But the devout worshippers of the most blessed Anne, considering that from these confraternities the devotion of the faithful could greatly profit, thought to erect, after the example of the Rosary, a praiseworthy congregation in honor of the holy mother Anne, so that the name and praises of so great a patroness might be able to come to the knowledge of many. And so this holy confraternity began in the diocese of Osnaburg, and, having been confirmed by Conrad, bishop of the said church, almost a decade before this under Frederick III, it was greatly multiplied. Then Rumuldus de Laupach, prior of the convent of the Carmelites at Frankfurt, a very great worshipper and lover of the holy mother Anne, at the urging of certain nobles established the same confraternity in his aforesaid convent; Berthold of Henneberg, most reverend archbishop of the church of Mainz, confirmed it, and the supreme pontiff Innocent VIII, in the eighth year of his pontificate, approved the same by apostolic authority. And indeed it is a worthy institution, that the Carmelite friars, who are especially distinguished by the title of the most blessed Mother of God, should also be more ready in the praises of her mother.

Today there is a great and abundant number of persons of both sexes in this confraternity, who daily do and expend wonderful and many things for the honor of Saint Anne. Moreover, in various places this same venerable confraternity is erected by the faithful and celebrated with fitting devotion. Yet detractors of every good thing are found, as from the beginning, also of this honorable confraternity, who tear it with impious tooth and assail it with ruinous presumption. What displeases you in this most holy confraternity, O profane detractors? What will you have against it? Bring it forth, that we may hear. You say: These confraternities are superstitious, because they render the Catholic faith weak, withdraw due worship from God, and despise obedience to the parish churches. These are the heads of the opposing party, the crimes of an unjust accusation, the charges of wicked detraction. To these we shall briefly respond in order.
