Let no one be sluggish in devotion to her. Let no one omit her commemoration. For Anna is interpreted as “grace” or “gift.” A name fitting so great a parent, which we believe was not given to her at birth by chance, but was foreordained by God. For she is rightly interpreted as “grace,” since she was to bear the mother of grace. Rightly “grace,” through whose intercession the grace of God is obtained for the faithful. She is also interpreted as “one who gives,” or “gift,” because those whom she finds devout in her honor she makes rich with many gifts of grace. Rightly “gift,” through whom the remission of sins is given. Therefore whoever is weighed down by the burden of sins, whoever is struck by the darts of temptations, let him confidently flee to the bosom of mother Anne, let him offer prayers and gifts of devotion, and there will be nothing that can prevail against him unto evil. For Anne is called “grace”; according to her name she does not cease to obtain graces for the one who asks, if she has found devout faith. Therefore, faithful of God, celebrate the feasts of the most holy mother; rejoice in her commemoration, and pour out diligent prayers to her, for she is able to free you from every tribulation. Sing joyfully to the Lord our God, all you who love Saint Anne; praise the name of the Lord, all faithful peoples, who love the mother of the God-bearing Virgin. Take Anne for yourselves as patroness, and honor her with devout prayers, and you will always find her a faithful advocate.

*By what exercises and devotions we ought to honor the most blessed mother Anne. Chapter 14.*

[unclear] We have heard how greatly the most blessed mother Anne avails by her merits before almighty God; we know how worthy she is of honor. It remains now that we briefly say how she is to be honored by us. And although, because of the variety of devotion, a general rule concerning the manner of veneration cannot be set down by us, nevertheless we shall try, as far as we can, to persuade all, so that no one may be able to excuse himself through ignorance from the praise of so great a mother. And indeed, first of all, devotion will establish for each person the manner of praising her; the greater it is, the more useful the manner will be, just as we are accustomed to venerate the other saints with various ceremonies. If perhaps I seem too brief to you, take a fuller manner. Kings, princes, and prelates of churches, if they desire to honor blessed Anne, should erect chapels and oratories in her memory, establish prebends, found monasteries. They should also make diligent prayers to her, celebrate her memory, and feed the poor and needy out of reverence for her. With whatever zeal they venerate the other saints special to themselves, let them know that the holy mother Anne is to be honored not with less but with more abundant zeal. Likewise the prelates of the faithful themselves should have the same manner of veneration, and should institute that the feast of the most holy mother Anne be celebrated by all in their dioceses.
