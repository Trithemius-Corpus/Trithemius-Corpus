If you wish to please God, the supreme governor, believe me, brothers, for I am going to speak the truth. If you love and honor the venerable mother with your whole heart, you will know by experience how much the heavenly emperor defers to her. She has such power with God that whatever she asks of him she is able to obtain without delay. Just as the king of heaven is believed to honor his mother before all, so after her he venerates mother Anne before all the saints. And very often Anne obtains for her faithful what her daughter denies: not because the latter does not know how to grant what is requested, but so that, deferring to her mother, she may be seen to summon us to veneration of her. Thus also the supreme emperor is bent to mercy by the prayers of the saints, not because he begins to will what before he did not will, but so that, deferring to his elect, he might instruct us in honoring them. For God is not bent in human fashion, nor is he ever moved to anger by disturbance, but, always steadfast, he wills what he wills; and when, through the prayers of the saints, he shows mercy, he does not change his counsel, but mitigates the sentence. Therefore, to obtain God’s mercy, since our own merits do not suffice, the suffrages of the saints must be implored by us, so that what we cannot obtain by our own merits we may attain through their prayers. But although the citizens of the heavenly fatherland are unlike in the quality of their merits, nevertheless, for aiding us in our miseries, according to the variety of those who supplicate, if devotion demands it, they are believed to be concordant in deed and will. For when the faith of the petitioner aids the advocate, it renders the merit for obtaining twice as great. And this we gain not by conjecture but by experience, when we marvel that one is heard and another is disregarded by the saints. Two blind men come to supplicate the merit of some saint: one is given sight, the other is left in his blindness. It is believed that no contempt of the saint is present, but the distrust of the petitioner is reproved. Without faith, devotion merits nothing. I have set these things forth with certain consideration, so that if anyone does not feel the most blessed Anne present to him with such readiness as we promise, he should not charge us with falsehood, but should recognize his own distrust as blameworthy. For the greatest confidence before God and the saints is steadfast faith in aid. Moreover, no Christian is unaware that those who are more excellent in merits have greater power with God; and we know that her life was most holy. There was no other woman in Israel more holy than she who, in the conception of the mother of God, fashioned for the Holy Spirit a vessel without stain. Finally, if Elizabeth, the mother of the Lord’s forerunner, is believed to have been filled with the Holy Spirit and with ineffable joy, let it be considered how great an exultation, joy, and grace befitted the most holy mother of the parent of God.
