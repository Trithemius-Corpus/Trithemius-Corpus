I, who am not worthy to lift my face to heaven because of the multitude of my iniquity: how much less should I dare to touch the praises of most blessed Anne with impure lips? But by your grace I am what I am, and all the good that I will and can do. For you, Lord, by your grace enlighten the will so that it may will good things and be good. You also are he who grants us to work for good will. In all things we acknowledge your grace going before our will; in the good things that we will and do, we proclaim your gifts, without merits of our own. Nothing proceeds from us except evil; nothing from you except good. Since, therefore, I have no good from myself without your grace, I flee devoutly to you; I appeal to you in humble subjection, that what my merits hinder, you may mercifully bestow by the gift of your grace.

Give me, Lord, wisdom, the attendant at your thrones, that she may be with me and labor with me at every time, and especially in this work, in which with scant powers I have resolved to praise your name with holy mother Anne. You also, Anne, most holy mother, whose memory is being treated, deign with your accustomed kindness to be present with me, so that I may be made fit to describe your praises and may be found a faithful [serophantes?] by your merits. My lady, my hope, my advocate in every tribulation, now be graciously present to one who longs to praise you; and since you have stirred my will to write by manifold benefit, obtain by your merits ability for my poor intellect, so that it may suffice to praise you worthily. I seek nothing except your honor; I desire nothing except to please you. I intend to write nothing except what is right and true concerning your praises. Therefore, I pray, bring help, give the hand of assistance, obtain grace.

You also, immaculate mother of God, who taught your servants to honor Anne, your venerable mother, be present to me, who desire to describe her praises, by your merits, so that the devotion of your servant may obtain in the work the due effect which the grace of God conceived in the mind. You too, blessed spirits of the heavenly fatherland, who exult in the most sacred fruit of the mother, the daughter, the virgin, our Lord Jesus Christ, by your prayers make me worthy to be a praiser of Anne, and obtain for me the grace of the Holy Spirit. If you honor mother and daughter in heavenly joy, it is fitting that you assist their praisers in this pilgrimage. You also, most blessed devoted worshippers of Anne, do not disparage our pious studies; but if in some matters through weakness we have failed, excuse us through charity. Nothing is written by me out of contempt or empty glory. Nothing of apocryphal uncertainty is alleged; nothing is taken up unless proved. I receive nothing that opposes divine ordinances. I write nothing that stains the Christian faith. I introduce nothing that does not accord with holy morals.
