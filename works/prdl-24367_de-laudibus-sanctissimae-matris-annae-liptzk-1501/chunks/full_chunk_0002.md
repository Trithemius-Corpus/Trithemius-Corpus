*Here begins the treatise of Dom Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of Saint Benedict, on the praises of the venerable and most blessed Anne, mother of the Mother of God.*

I shall write, according to the powers of my understanding, the praises of Anne, the most blessed mother of the Mother of God, for the edification of the faithful, and with a truthful account I shall declare with how great compassion she has been accustomed to come to the aid of the wretched. But when I consider the poverty of my understanding, unclean as I am I fear to speak of the most holy woman. For with how great praise is she worthy, who bore the Mother of God? Who will set it forth in speech? And so I wish to fashion something for the honor of so great a mother, and I am afraid, because whatever I shall have said is less than her praises; whatever I shall have written is far beneath the majesty of the matter.

Indeed, when I recall to mind her benefits toward the wretched, I cannot keep silent from her praises, even if I wish to. Here the greatest kindness has earned a vow, has stirred devotion, and requires the showing of honor. The devout also, not to say importunate, request of my friends has no less impelled me to dare, from whose faith and prayer our weakness has received confidence. Although, therefore, I know myself a sinner, although I am not able worthily to praise the most holy parent of the queen of heaven, yet I ought by no means to withdraw from the proposed praise, lest I seem to have incurred the fault of ingratitude. I shall try, according to my devotion, what I can, for no one is required to do what he cannot; no one is judged empty of good will before the eternal judge.

Therefore I, a man, shall praise her, a woman; a servant, my lady; a sinner, the most holy one. For neither did he who said in the psalm, “Praise the Lord, all nations; praise him, all peoples,” restrain sinners from the praise of God. Indeed, when he summons the nations to the praise of the Lord, what else does he do but call sinners? For they were far from justice, because they worshiped not God but idols. Therefore no one is permitted to keep silent from the praises of God, since even idolatrous nations are called to penitence. Let all therefore praise the Lord who receive his mercy; let all who live in the flesh praise him, because he himself is the one who made us all, he himself who gave life. Let the just man and the sinner praise him together: the former because by the Lord’s mercy he was preserved lest he fall; the latter because even after his fall he was raised up lest he perish forever.

And if praise is not beautiful in the mouth of a sinner, nevertheless a guilty conscience excuses no one from the praises of God, since by his mercy all are reserved for repentance. Let the just man bear praise; the sinner, confession. Let the former praise because, by preserving grace, he stood firm; let the latter confess because, with mercy following after, he rose again. “Praise the Lord, my soul; I shall praise my God in my life; I shall sing to the Lord as long as I shall be.” I shall open my mouth to confess to the Lord; I shall likewise make ready my mind and my pen.
