Therefore the most blessed Anna, so far as concerns her generation according to the flesh, was of the tribe of David, born from Bethlehem, her father being Stollanus, and her mother Emerentia, parents religious and devout according to the decrees of the law, who nurtured her, together with her sister Esmeria, from infancy in the fear of the Lord. Moreover Esmeria, her sister, bore Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist. Joachim, from Nazareth, of the tribe of Judah, took Anna as his wife; she bore the daughter who afterward was the mother of God, the ever-virgin Mary.

O Joachim and Anna, parents worthy of eternal remembrance, who, serving the Lord God in simplicity, offered an inestimable treasure. Who could suffice to praise these parents worthily? Indeed, unless their life had been most holy, they would never have brought forth offspring so pleasing to the Lord. They were, moreover, of moderate or sufficient means, obtaining necessities from the pasturing of sheep and from agriculture; from these, they are said to have made three equal portions, of which they gave one to the temple, another to the poor, and kept the third for themselves for domestic uses.

But so far as concerns morals, they were most zealous in observance of the law, approved in the fear of the Lord, as pleasing in the virtues; in all these they made such progress that they pleased God and men. In concord, innocence, and piety they were of one mind, and rich in every virtue. Both derived their origin from the house of David; both were perfect before the Lord; both awaited the redemption of Israel with longing. Both had been instructed from early age in the fear of the Lord; both were dear to God; both were chosen to be the parents of the mother of God. Yet Anna is believed, without injury to her husband, to have been filled with greater grace; in her venerable womb the most holy mother of God was formed without any stain. In her breast the charity of God found for itself a pleasing dwelling; from her blood the Holy Spirit built the most pure tabernacle of the daughter of God. Worthy parent of the most worthy parent, who first ripened the coming of our Savior.

In her, O brothers, direct the eyes of your mind; attend to the rule of her life, and impress your morals upon her footprints. Do you wish to know what sort she was? Turn over the writings of the aforesaid fathers. She was grave in gait, calm with a face shining with grace, mature in counsel, composed in speech, most holy in worship and action. She meditated on the law of the Lord by day and by night, firm in faith, strong in hope, filled with twin charity. She had learned always to be devoted to prayers, and awaited the coming of the Lord with the greatest longing. She was honorable in work, sober in conduct, humble in mind.
