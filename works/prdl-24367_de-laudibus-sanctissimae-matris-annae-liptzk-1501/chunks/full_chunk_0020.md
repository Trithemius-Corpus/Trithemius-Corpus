*The divine mother Anna is to be held in the greatest honor before God and all the saints. Chapter IX.*

Anna, the parent of the Mother of God, is of singular merit and honor before God, so that she is sufficient not only for her own glory but also fit and powerful for our aid. For if we trust that the apostles and the other saints are powerful before God, we must believe that the parents of God are most powerful. For Anna, a most holy woman, so pleased almighty God that he willed the mother of his only-begotten Son to be born from her; he would never have dedicated her womb for such a merit unless he had found in her the merit of great holiness. For Scripture says: The Holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit, nor will he dwell in a body subject to sins. In the most blessed mother Anna there was nothing false, nothing that could have offended God, because all things were upright, pure, and holy, and worthy of the habitation of the Holy Spirit. Rightly, therefore, after her daughter she is believed to be nearest to God in glory, since she was able to please him so greatly in this life.

Let no sound disputant object to me that the saints of the Old Testament were of lesser merit than those of the New, since time does not bestow holiness, but the grace of faith does. I except Anna, whom I recognize as a lover not so much of the Old as of the New Testament. For she herself fashioned the ark of the New Testament, whom she bore for nine months in her own womb. Nor does time diminish the holiness that God’s special grace grants. So great a grace had come upon most blessed Anna that she had no equal in virtue and holiness in Israel in her time. It therefore seems fitting that she should have power before God above the rest in glory, she who shone before others in life. Indeed, just as the Son of God honors and loves his mother above all, so also he endowed his beloved grandmother after her with singular glory.

But perhaps someone will say: carnal affection ceases where there is no generation of flesh. Granted. But love does not cease where the spirit abounds; affection does not come to an end where love is continued; the love of kinship does not perish where glory is owed to merits. I admit that carnal affection ceases in the blessed; the generation of the flesh also ceases. But the love of kinship does not cease, since among them it is common to all. For no one ever hated his own flesh. Nor is the Son of God so harsh that he would not love his holy parents, his flesh, his own bowels. Or how would he not love his own parents, who loves all his saints and his servants? For he says: I love those who love me. Who could ever have loved God above his parents according to the flesh? From their blood he was incarnate without stain, fed with milk, nourished with care. Far be it, therefore, that we should think that most blessed Anna, the grandmother of the Son of God, is without the greatest honor in the homeland, she who was so near to God on earth.

If the Lord Jesus, king of heaven, loves those who love him, we must rightly believe that he loves his parents above all; nor can they be in the heavenly homeland without special glory, whom almighty God on earth had filled with such extraordinary grace. Therefore we believe that Anna, after the Virgin Mother, is nearest to the Son of God, and a powerful advocate for the wretched, to whom the king of heaven denies nothing, but in all things [unclear] honors her as his most beloved parent. The whole Trinity also loves her; the angelic choir reveres her; all the citizens of the heavenly homeland venerate her with the greatest reverence as the honorable mother of their queen. And not undeservedly. For if they love the king, they rightly venerate his grandmother. For honor paid to parents is shown to their children. He is proved to love the king less who does not venerate his father and mother and his whole lineage.

We are also accustomed to grieve more over injuries to our parents than over our own. Why is this, unless because the insult inflicted on them is doubled in us? For we believe not only our parents but also ourselves to be marked by a grave injury. In the same way, just as we believe an insult to our parents to be ours, so we reckon honor shown to them. Whatever honor, therefore, we pay to the divine mother Anna, we should believe without doubt to be shown to God. For the Lord says to us in the Gospel: Whatever you did to one of my least, you did to me. If he asserts that everything bestowed on the least who believe in Christ is done to himself, how much more must we believe this to be done in those who are not merely least before him, but greatest and most powerful? His parents are not last before the Son of God; they are not least but greatest, not small but most powerful. Therefore we should by no means doubt that whatever veneration we pay to them is doubled before God.

And indeed, if revelations are to be believed, the most blessed Virgin appeared to a certain devout man and, among other things, said to him: If you love me, honor my mother, and whatever reverence you pay to her will be doubly pleasing to me. Now therefore, brothers, if we love the Mother of God, if we desire to please the queen of heaven, let us honor her venerable mother with diligent devotion, and in every tribulation we shall find them both pious helpers. Behold, the angels of God and all the saints venerate and love Anna, and we, mute, keep silence from her honor.
