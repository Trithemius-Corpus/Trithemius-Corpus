The sounds have filled [the air], music and every rejoicing: Hail.
Anne, you bear the veil, fair beneath the image of the fathers: Hail.
Holding within itself the deeds, because it may cover the lofty things of God: Hail.
Anne, you bear the offspring whose head the golden flame of Phoebus clothes: Hail.
And the bright moon clothes her snowy feet: Hail.
Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, now made possessor of the longed-for Mary: Hail.
You scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have arisen: Hail.
Anne, all things applauded the newly born Mary: Hail.
The stars, and an unwonted radiance flowed from the pole: Hail.
Anne, it is right to believe that angelic choirs celebrated around you: Hail.
The birthday of your offspring: Hail.
Anne, the shades of the deep felt your childbirth: Hail.
Then that new light terrified the Stygian pools: Hail.
Anne, as mother you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands: Hail.
More ruddy than the sun and than the lights of heaven: Hail.
Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl? Hail.
She smiles at her mother; she causes no sadness: Hail.
Anne, take courage; behold, the daughter reaches toward your milky breasts: Hail.
And seeks with tender hands your neck: Hail.
Anne, you break off delays, baring breasts and bosom: Hail.
You receive her tender little limbs in your tender lap: Hail.
Anne, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast she draws sweet juices: Hail.
With her rosy mouth, making pleasing murmurs: Hail.
Anne, if you knew how many sufferings your daughter will bear: Hail.
And how many deaths she will bear, you would now be nothing: Hail.
Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, it is your concern to adorn your daughter with approved: Hail.
Manners, and to make her fit for the highest God: Hail.
Anne, you prepare to bring your daughter into the lofty temple: Hail.
But of her own accord the kindly one reached the step of those above: Hail.
Anne, you follow the swift one with slow pace and toil: Hail.
The ready little virgin consoles her weary mother: Hail.
Anne, why do you wonder at strength in the tender virgin? Hail.
You see not human things, but the holy signs of God: Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to give gifts to the needy and to sacred temples: Hail.
Now you give that than which no woman ever gave more: Hail.
Anne, when you wished your consecrated daughter to live: Hail.
You gladly entered the cloister, and the virginal house: Hail.
Anne, about to send away the pious one, you [unclear] cheeks and eyes with weeping: Hail.
Deep love holds you unmoved: Hail.
Anne, return home; send your daughter to the Thunderer: Hail.
Let her think of nothing except the Jesus who is to come: Hail.
Anne, let your daughter fly through the ethereal recesses: Hail.
Let love sent from the highest summit possess her: Hail.
Anne, it is enough for you to serve two Marys: Hail.
They also will honor me, not with small devotion, and you: Hail.
Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, by your own weeping you have been taught to succor one who weeps: Hail.
Hence you too readily favor the wretched man with his vows: Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to overturn the fixed laws of nature: Hail.
And thus you will desire it: let the barren woman now be a joyful mother: Hail.
Anne, you also halt the gifts of fleeting fortune: Hail.
Lest, slipping suddenly, they flee from our bosoms: Hail.
You relieve mourning, and love yields to you: Hail.
Anne, you help whether hostile force terrifies someone, or whether: Hail.
The savage chains of a dark prison hold him: Hail.
Anne, be present as relief for sick mortals: Hail.
With you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing: Hail.
Anne, you provide things always to be feared for the timid tyrant: Hail.
If the winds and wave toss a battered ship: Hail.
Anne, mortal hearts cannot ask from you in vain: Hail.
And whatever you will, your daughter and God will will: Hail.
Anne, therefore blessed in merits and in so great an offspring: Hail.
Anne, great mother, look upon our prayers: Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary*

Almighty and most merciful God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as the mother of the mother of your Son, graciously grant that we, who honor her memory with faithful devotion, may through her merits and prayers obtain the helps of eternal life. Through the same.

Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beissel, professor of both laws, patrician of Aachen, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1493.
