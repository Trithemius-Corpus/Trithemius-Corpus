Likewise let the leaders and brothers of the other religious orders do the same as far as they can, and let them never at any time grow lukewarm in the worship of so great a mother. But you, professors of the Carmelite order, who by the title of the most blessed Mother of God and Virgin Mary have been specially distinguished with a most ancient privilege, you must venerate and embrace most holy Anne with singular devotion, from whose daughter you have been called venerable. Take care, therefore, that you are never lukewarm in the worship of the mother, you who from her daughter have obtained the name of so great an honor. You also, all the rest, chosen into the lot [unclear], stretch out your souls to the honor of the most holy mother of the Mother of God. Bestow upon her whatever praise you can, and instruct those subject to you, as far as possible, to do the same. You also, O men distinguished by learning in letters, in whom the vein of nocturnal study abounds, dedicate your talents to the blessed mother Anne. Play in verse to her honor, and praise her also in prose as much as you can. But let the simpler, whose talents are slower and not sufficiently suited for composition, copy out with devotion the things that have been published by others; let them read them more often, and for the edification of others also let them affix them to the altars of the holy mother. Let them read prayers suited to her honor. Let them read due praises. Let them read hymns, poems, and proses. Let no one be idle from the praises of Anne; let no one be silent; let no one grow lukewarm. Let all nations redeemed by the blood of Christ celebrate the memory of Anne, from whose merits a great hope of salvation is promised to us. But lest matter for prayer be lacking to the devout worshipers of Anne, three years ago we made, under her honor, a brief prayer in the manner of the angelic salutation, which is now both possessed and read by many. But it seems to us sufficiently fitting that, just as in the rosary of the Mother of God we pay five Lord’s Prayers and fifty angelic salutations, so too we should do to the honor of Saint Anne, yet in such a way that in place of the Ave Maria our prayer which follows is said.

*Prayer.*

Hail, venerable Anne, mother of the Mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women. Blessed are you by the Lord, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, [from whom?] the virgin-born Jesus. Amen.

Many appoint this prayer to the honor of Saint Anne in place of the angelic salutation in the rosary, because it is brief and by no means useless. But if our prayer for the rosary of Saint Anne should please anyone, let him read the angelic salutation as is customary, and at the end, after he has said “Jesus Christ,” let him always add this clause: “And blessed be your most honorable mother, Saint Anne, from whom your virginal flesh proceeded without stain. Amen.”
