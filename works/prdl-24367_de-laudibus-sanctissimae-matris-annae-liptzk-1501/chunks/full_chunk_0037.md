*Poem of Rutger, a venerable German regular canon of Heyn[?], on the book On the Praises of Saint Anne*

Whoever you shall be, reader of this excellent book, put aside empty fear: it contains no trifles. Abbot Trithemius, by his art, has published this book, pregnant with wondrous meaning and sweet with charm: he who watches over books, guardian of the Sponheim flock, who cultivates the sacred writings of the threefold tongue. Here you will find titles of Anne full of reason and art; only turn them over in a pious heart. He does not follow vain and everywhere doubtful little tales; he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence. Therefore, I pray, read the worthy praises of venerable Anne, which the learned man presents to you, beloved one.

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, sends greeting to the reader. Since recently, at the urging of friends, I had resolved to write something on the praises of the most holy mother Anne, certain fathers of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel asked that I not omit the defense of the most pure conception of Mary. And so, moved by their prayers and otherwise by just reasoning, I designated the seventh chapter of this work for the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin. Lest anyone take it in a sense other than mine, I thought the reader should be warned. For I know, and it has not escaped me, that many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the stain of original sin, men who flourished long before the Council of Basel. Granting this opinion to its own time, I pass over it wholly untouched, and I do not intend to say or write anything at all against them. But indeed, if anything is seen to have been said by me rather harshly, let it be referred not to those who thought otherwise than we do, but only to the obstinate, who, prattling without learning, do not shrink from calling the defenders of the other opinion heretics. And my words show this, because they indicate the present time and not the past. For when I say, “Why do fools bark? Why do the inexperienced murmur?” it belongs to the present time, not to the past; and today the obstinate are reproved, not those in former times who thought otherwise. But when you ask who ever wrote the contrary, I do not bring charges against the ancient fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the aforesaid council, hold another opinion. And so I reproach only the obstinacy of certain unlearned men of this time, not the opinion of learned men, whoever they may be, who think otherwise, especially since I am not ignorant of the declaration of the supreme pontiff Sixtus IV. It has seemed to me, however, by no means alien from the holy Roman Church to venerate the faith of the most pure conception, which she believes and venerates, as is shown today by many documents. Finally, with the doctors of the universities of Paris, Cologne, Heidelberg, Erfurt, Vienna, Leipzig, Tübingen, Ingolstadt, Basel, Mainz, and many others, and with nearly all the orders and the faithful of Christ spread throughout the whole Christian world, I do not fear to venerate the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary as pure. Yet I do not admit obstinacy, but I wish to obey the Roman Church in all things as the most reverend mother of all churches. This is the pure and sincere meaning of my aforesaid chapter, in which I think no one who holds an opinion soberly and without obstinacy is disparaged. Farewell, reader.

This letter of the aforesaid lord Johannes the abbot is missing from certain copies through carelessness and negligence; yet he himself ordered it to be appended to all copies, and decreed that it be regarded as appended to each one.
