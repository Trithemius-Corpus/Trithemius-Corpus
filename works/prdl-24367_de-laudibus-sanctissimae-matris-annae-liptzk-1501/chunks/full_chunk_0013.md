Therefore, dearest brothers, let us choose as our advocate her whom we have known from eternity to have pleased God as supremely holy, and, venerating her daily, let us honor her with fitting praises. Let married persons honor her, because by her most holy childbirth she consecrated marriage to the Lord, for whom she bore a mother without stain. Let widows and the continent praise her, because she prefigured the standard of that continence. Let virgins love her, because she bore a virgin of such holiness that no one more holy ever shone forth. Let the just honor her, since by her most holy conduct she showed them the rule of justice. Let sinners venerate her, because she bore the mother of him who makes the just out of sinners. Let all venerate her with the greatest love, since she brought forth the mother of him who came as redeemer to all. Let no one grow sluggish in her praises, let no one fall away from love of her, let no one become lukewarm in her worship. Let all love her as their common mother; let all honor her as a public advocate. From her gift all received the common savior. For she herself will obtain pardon for us sinners, from whose blood the fountain of mercy flowed forth bodily. Rise up before her, O brothers, for she does not forsake those who hope in her; she relieves the oppressed, sustains those relieved, protects widows, mercifully defends orphans and wards, refreshes the weak, raises up the feeble, and strengthens them in holy virtue. It is impossible that she, praying for her devotees, should not be heard, she who constructed so precious a bridal chamber from her own breast for almighty God.

*Chapter VI.*

*How just, holy, and acceptable to God was the life and conduct of the most blessed mother Anne.*

Whoever has read through the monuments of the holy fathers with diligent devotion can discover with how great holiness the life of the most blessed mother Anne shone forth. Concerning her, the holy bishop Epiphanius composed certain things; he records that her life was bright with such justice, innocence, and purity that she had no equal in Israel. Honorius also, priest of Autun, a holy and learned man, in the Mirror of the Church published certain things in a lucid discourse on the praises of holy mother Anne, and showed in brief compendium that she always lived holily. Saint Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, once a cultivator of the mountain and order of Carmel, when he was writing to Pope Celestine I concerning the birth of the blessed mother of the ever-virgin Mary, inserted many proclamations of praise concerning the life and conduct of holy Anne.
