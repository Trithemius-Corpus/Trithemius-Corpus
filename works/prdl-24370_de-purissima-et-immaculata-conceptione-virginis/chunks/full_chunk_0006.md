Therefore the most blessed Anna was worthy of this office by the gift of God; she was worthy by the merit of her life; she was also worthy by the testimony of the law. Worthy, I say, was she to bear the Mother of God: she who had learned to love God above all things, who had learned to keep his law without offense. There was no woman holier than she in Israel who is believed to have been honored by God with such great gifts. Being barren, she conceived from seed, but without the stain of seed. She conceived, I say, without the stain of guilt, and Anna bore Mary, the most devout, most holy, blessed Mother of God. Who loves the king and does not honor his parent? Who loves Mary and does not venerate Anna? Let the arguments of vanity cease; let words excusing reason depart far away: he is convicted of loving the Son of God less who knowingly does not venerate Anna. Let the man hard of heart and reprobate in devotion blush, who says that he loves the Mother of God and is convicted of not loving her mother Anna. Foolish and lost presumption: to take confidence from the patronage of the daughter when one scorns to honor her venerable mother. For he who has the mother offended will not be able to find the daughter appeased. For the daughter loves her mother, and the whole host of the heavenly court venerates Anna. If anyone has Anna as advocate, he will have all the saints in heaven as patrons. Therefore let us love the mother, dearest sons, who can introduce us to the sight of God, for she is both pleasing and near to God. Thus John, abbot, as above.

*There follows the statute and determination of the sacred faculty of theology of the University of Paris concerning the immaculate conception of the glorious [Virgin]. And first, the recantation made at Paris in the year of the Lord 1496 by brother Johannes [Verijs?] of the Order of Preachers concerning the aforesaid matter.*

I, brother Johannes [Verijs?], religious of the Order of Friars Preachers, priest and master in theology, was accused and denounced before my mother, the University of Paris, and the faculty of theology, as having preached in the town of Dieppe, in the diocese of Rouen, on the feast of the conception of the most pure and most glorious Virgin Mary, mother of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the year just past, the following three propositions. First: the blessed Virgin Mary was cleansed from original guilt. Second, by way of question: how Mary could say the Lord’s Prayer, and especially as to that part, “forgive us our [unclear],” not completing the statement. Third: those asserting that the greatest Virgin was conceived in original sin do not sin mortally and are not heretics; rather, all who say that they, by speaking thus, sin mortally or are heretics are excommunicated by Sixtus in a certain bull.
