...we know that an answer has been given more than abundantly, in a varied kind of style. For you go away confounded not by our weapons, but by the power of God. Nor do I condemn your error, but your arrogance, your rashness, your presumption. For I know that it is not a sin to hesitate about the conception of the Virgin, which God wished to be hidden; but to attack it, and rashly to define what you do not know, is a crime unheard of. If we are commanded to interpret for the better those things which we do not know with what intention they are done, how much more ought we to receive with piety the hidden works of God, whose reasons we do not know. None of us was present at the counsel of God; no one has known the manner of the conception. But, led by many reasons, we, from pious belief, believe that she always existed most pure. The adversaries, led away by consideration of nature, defend the contrary opinion with rash presumption. But it is better to render an account for piety toward the parent of God than for rashness. If we err, piety, indeed God himself, is the cause. But if you err, O adversaries, say, for what cause? What consideration moves you to say so boldly that the most pure conception of the Mother of God was subject to original fault? You will answer, I know: love of truth. But show that your opinion is true, and I shall believe you lovers of truth. But you will not show it, because you do not understand. You will not understand, because you do not believe. You do not defend truth, which you do not know. For no one properly defends what he is ignorant of. Consideration of human nature deceives you; while you examine and turn over its custom in detail, you do not weigh that God is the establisher of nature. He who made nature is above nature. God, the founder of the law, is not restricted under the guidance of human law; but he who wished a virgin to give birth, even contrary to the order of nature, preserved her also from sin. Therefore the cause of the adverse opinion takes its beginning from rashness, and for that reason it deserves from God not reward but vengeance. For just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so error about the conception does not condemn, but obstinacy does. Finally, if examples and records of the ancients are to be believed, we read that many have perished most miserably who attacked the purity of the glorious conception; but never anyone who was its defender, and she shone forth so greatly that she was most worthy of every honor. If we are deceived, we are excused by holy piety. But if you err, who will be your defender?

Truth? But you do not know it. Justice? But it condemns errors; it does not excuse them. Mercy? But you do not have the bowels of mercy. Who, then, will deliver you? The Virgin Mary? But you make light of her honors. The Son of God? But you do not love his mother. The prayers [of the saints]? But you disparage their queen. Therefore you will have no excuse, you who offend God and all his saints. You arm yourselves with scholastic arguments, and you are naked of piety. Whatever you bring forward in defense of your opinion is alien from the fear of God. For you do not seek the honor of God, you who attack the purity of his mother. But hear my counsel, and change your purpose. Return at last to yourselves, for it is time, and do not presumptuously defend an uncertain opinion which, left undiscussed, you can pass over without sin. If you love God, if you love Jesus Christ, the savior of all, do not do so great an injury to his most holy mother, concerning whom it is lawful to think nothing except what is most seemly. To believe that her conception was always most pure is not a matter of necessity, but of piety; not to believe it is a matter of human slowness, but in not believing either to attack it and to wish to defend the contrary opinion lacks not only the crime [unclear], but is also of great rashness and folly. For truly rashness is rightly reproved where piety is rewarded. Nor should it seem to anyone a small sin which is committed against God and his mother. But he who loves the Son of God does not open his mouth against the honor of the mother; he does not detract from her purity; he does not obstinately defend an uncertain opinion, because where humble devotion receives a reward, the presumption of the proud will find torment. For the Mother of God is believed to have been worthy to be conceived without original stain, she who was to bear as a virgin him who would blot out the crimes of the whole world. Consider also what sort of mother blessed Anna was by such a conception, whose illustrious way of life was most pleasing to God. I do not want you to marvel that the Mother of God was preserved from original sin contrary to the custom of nature, whom God, the infuser of grace, had prepared as a dwelling for himself. Consider that the Mother of God was born, and you will not marvel at a conception without stain. Great are the things that are said, but true, but [unclear], but befitting the Mother of God, whose conception grace preceded; and at this nature marvels. We too marvel because we faithfully believe. But the adversaries, not believing, do not marvel, but are confounded. If you can grasp these things, believe; if you do not grasp them, do not examine them if you do not wish to sin. For it is better to keep silence with reverence about the purity of the most blessed Mother of God than to speak unworthy things.
