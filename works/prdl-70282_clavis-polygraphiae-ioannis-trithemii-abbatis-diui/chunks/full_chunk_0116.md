We have sufficiently shown the method of numbering by Latin letters, after the manner of the Hebrews and Greeks; we have reasonably inserted it here by way of digression, although it is also found earlier in the sixth book. But by the fact that, as we proceed, we have here concluded our numbering at thirty times one hundred thousand, we plainly declare that it can be extended to infinity. If anyone dislikes this method of numbers, he will easily be able to devise a better one. Also, in imitation of the Northmen, from the aforesaid numeral letters we have arranged a new alphabet, by means of which you will be able safely to write and announce to an absent friend whatever secret you wish. Indeed, this method of writing is so secret, and so new, never seen or heard before us, that it seems to me utterly impossible that it can be understood by anyone without the service of a teacher. But now let us set down its alphabet.

Behold, you now have here an alphabet from Roman numeral letters, in imitation of the Northmen, with which you will be able most safely to write and announce to an absent friend whatever you wish; and no one will be able to penetrate your hidden secret except him who knows the secret of the art. For all who see the writing will suppose that the letters have been transposed, and they will labor in vain in calculating them. Let us set down an example: Iaaibid ifeib, ieifeigeiccbkib, cakcebhid, id et amp;kahk, gidsbid, cigib, iceigihg ibkgig.&kif, iaiahibifid,ibeicdaka.eib.ieiei et amp;kaib. The hidden meaning of this writing is as follows: Beware of the bearer of these presents, because the man is very wicked, a thief, a robber, a liar, and worthless. From the demonstration of this example it is easily given to be understood how safe and secure this method of writing is universally in every language known to the writer, and in every cause or business, however difficult, secret, great, or lengthy. Nor do I think there is any man living in the world who, by his own ingenuity and without a teacher, could read this very writing. For all who see it will suppose it to be nothing other than that the letters have been transposed; and when they have been unable to obtain an understanding of them, at last, worn out by despair, they will cease from investigation, and whatever you have written will remain hidden. For no one, as I think, will examine this technique of writing secretly in its own powers, just as it is, unless he has at hand either the Polygraphia, or this key to it, or has been instructed by another. From these numeral letters, too, many other alphabets can be produced according to the order of numbering, when the beginning for writing alphabetically is taken not from unity but from any other following number. But in that progression you should know that one must take care that no more than two numerable signs be taken to represent one letter. Hence, when you have reached ww, that is, ten times one hundred thousand, stop your step and do not wander further, lest the multitude of signs confuse the beauty of writing by spoiling it. For after unity has been excluded, ten times one hundred thousand, with so many signs included, suffice for nearly thirty separate alphabets. Nevertheless, the method of the first alphabet, beginning with unity, is the more beautiful for wrapping secrets in writings, in proportion as it is shorter and simpler.
