With the demonstration of the possibility of our new invention having been shown briefly, and no less sufficiently, I do not think that I myself should be wearied any longer over the same matter on this occasion, since the method of the journey extends to infinity, if anyone should need an example for further matters. Nor do I by any means think it necessary to recall to memory the things that have been recited in the preceding sections, since a sharp reader, if he is studious, can also, with the Lord's help, discover the greater things that lie hidden in these. We have handed over the material, and at the same time the cause, to the wise student; if he wishes to use them prudently, by his own talent he will surpass ours. But whoever desires this paved road more beautiful in the shade should join hand and mind to talent, and himself make a path more beautifully adorned by paraphrase. Let him hold it fixed in memory, if these things delight anyone, that he must always remain within the prescribed circle; if he passes beyond it in any way, the whole operation vanishes uselessly. There are many names of men; indeed, there are almost infinite words for things, towns, cities, provinces, herbs, trees, stones, and verbs, all of which serve our purposes. From these it is clear that it is not impossible for a book of this kind to be made, if talent is present with diligence, which may surpass our inventions without limit. But the man of such dullness that he cannot understand these childish things of ours, when will he penetrate the subtler matters that remain? We put an end to the fourth book of the Polygraphia, to which we join the fifth in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Here is the end of the fourth book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot, on the fourteenth day of March, in the year 1508.
