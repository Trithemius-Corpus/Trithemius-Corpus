The eighth, on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus: “Let us adore Jesus Christ; let us bewail him crucified.” The ninth, on the compassion of the blessed Mary ever virgin; and it begins thus: “Come, let us weep with devout mind, sharing the suffering of the blessed Virgin Mary,” under the melody, namely, “Ave praeclara.” The tenth, on Saint Joachim, father of Our Lady, which begins thus: “Let us adore the Trinity, let us praise together the unity, of Father, Son, and Spirit.” The eleventh, on the good proper angel of every person. The twelfth, in the proper Mass of each priest [sacerdotis?]. The thirteenth, on Saint Maximus, bishop of Mainz. The fourteenth, on Saint Martha, virgin, Christ’s hostess. The fifteenth, on Saint Mary Magdalene. The sixteenth, on Saint Mary of Egypt. The seventeenth, on Saint James the Greater, apostle. The eighteenth, on Saint Andrew, apostle. The nineteenth, on Saint Benedict, abbot. The twentieth, on All Saints. And for each of the named memorials and saints he composed individual new offices of the Mass, conformable to the divine Scriptures. He wrote besides a little work of meditations and prayers on the Lord’s incarnation, nativity, life, passion, and death, suited to devotion, in two books. The Mirror of Heavenly Doctrine, serving not a little for compunction of mind, one book, which begins thus: “The beginning of human salvation.” He also composed many very devout meditations and prayers on our Lord Jesus Christ, on his most holy mother the Virgin Mary, and on various saints, composed with beautiful variety, together with very many other things, which all devout posterity will see and marvel at. These, dearest Nicolaus, are the works of Trithemius our abbot that are known to me, which he has produced in almost thirty-two years of continuous nightly study, besides those which, not yet finished, are in his hands and still under the hammer. Farewell, from my house at [Duraclusa?], on the fifth day of the month of October, in the year of our salvation one thousand five hundred and fifteen. Johannes Duraclusius.

*Here ends the index of the works of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James at Würzburg, from the letter of Johannes Duraclusius, his disciple, to Nicolaus Hamerius of Emel.*
