A way is prepared for beginners, in which, as they advance, it will not be difficult in the course of time to find many things, both rare and very great. Here one who did not know how will learn to write in Latin, and prudently to wrap all the mysteries of his will in a foreign speech. But that an unlearned man may be able to understand not only his own mysteries which he wraps up, but also open speech, of which until now he has always been ignorant, is a matter of no great labor, if the paraphrast has fully attained our intention in this volume. For beneath the bark lie hidden the kernels of our *Steganographia*, buried for good reason, lest they lie open to unworthy babblers. Finally, it had been worth the effort that one work should not prejudice the other, but that its own right should be distributed inviolate to each. Wearied after a long voyage, we here cast anchor in the deep under the name of JESUS CHRIST, savior of the faithful, on the twenty-first day of the month of March, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1508, in the eleventh Roman indiction, at Paeapolis, the city of the East Franks, which they also call Würzburg and Herbipolis.

*Preface to the Explanation of the Whole Work of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Paeopolis, that is, Würzburg, at St. James.*

No one who has read these things, as I trust, will judge me either proud or superfluous, because I myself have commented on the *Polygraphia* recently composed by me, especially if he has been able to weigh precisely what cause induced us to speak obscurely. For whatever can do harm when made public had reasonably to be hidden in mystical speech. But the subject which we treat in the *Polygraphia* is, for the advantage of the commonwealth among good men, very good, useful, and honorable; and for this reason, persuaded by a reasonable cause, we wrote it, so that by its service we might render good men safe and secure from the investigation of the wicked in communicating their secrets to one another. But lest an occasion of deceiving the simpler and less cautious in their judgment of things be offered to wicked and perverse men, who very often abuse even the inventions of the good, we handed down in it mystical or hidden modes of writing, and covered the truth hidden in the marrow with certain coverings of figures. Yet we do not in the least doubt that any industrious man, desirous of these things, will easily grasp everything, if he applies even moderate perseverance in searching them out. For the things I have handed down are neither so difficult nor wrapped in such great mysteries that they cannot be thoroughly penetrated by a studious man. For everywhere we have so tempered the enigmas of our words that they may easily be passable to learned and studious men, while nevertheless remaining inaccessible to lazy men of darkness. But after it pleased us to offer the work of the *Polygraphia* itself, dedicated, to the most serene and unconquered Caesar of the Romans, Maximilian, ever august emperor of the world, I judged that it would be worthwhile if I made it clear with brief and few interpretations.
