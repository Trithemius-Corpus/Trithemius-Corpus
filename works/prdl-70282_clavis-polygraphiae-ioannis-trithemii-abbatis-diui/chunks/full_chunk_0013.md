I have prefixed the letters of the Latin alphabet in order to the individual words with this consideration, so that the operator may have an easy method in the proposed matter, and, reciprocally, may be relieved in the relation from excessive labor. The paraphrasmata that are placed outside the straight order of the lines are not devoid of the mysteries of the art, since they serve [unclear] propositions for the necessary completion of perfection, not obscure ones. Therefore they are also taken up in the former; they are neglected in the latter. For they themselves are certain means by which every suspicion of an adversary is removed. All things have been set down with reason, so that not even one proposition is introduced idly. By as much, for example, as the sun advances farther from the equinoctial circle, by so much the day is prolonged for those dwelling in those regions. Therefore, in order that unity may advance straight, by means of the ternary, into the binary, the paradigm that we hand down must be observed rather carefully. And first, indeed, once the operator, understanding his intention, has had prior consideration, let him observe what, by means of the binary, he will be about to grant to the ternary; let him divide the day with the night by minutes, and distinguish the small divisions of the winds, not by an alien supposition of phrase, but by its own. In any mystical business let him diligently beware lest he overturn the established order of the art, or substitute one paraphrasma for another, or grant to one what is owed to another; otherwise unity will not release the binary, and the whole operation will be confused and useless. Let the wandering night not be of excessive length; you will not press on, advancing beyond the limits established for it by the art; let curiosity not change water and snow, nor let him sleep in the day who keeps watch through the night. Let the mind, anxious in its ingenuity, use the art with watchful correction, and care must be taken lest the wind be [unclear]. After this a rule is prescribed for approaching the practice of the art: namely, that, so far as it can by mystical art, it imitate one human person consisting of two natures, that is, the soul surpassing visible things while the flesh remains visible. For just as the soul is not seen, although it has been placed in a visible body, so let the secret of the mind go as a secret; nevertheless let it operate at will, requiring the guidance of light, remaining in its own workshop of shadows. Therefore, whenever the body serves the soul, the servant the master, the will reason, see that the principal virtue of the power in the light of the paraphrasma be preserved in its natural vigor. For by this teaching the first of the Peripatetics will duly enter the second binary through [unclear]. Next likewise we advise the willing man that, whenever a leaf is moved by the wind, he should discern the property of each nature and compare the night itself with the day by degrees and minutes, and conform the darkness to the light. But if Phoebus has prejudiced the night, or the night the sun, through which a confluence of eclipse would be born, either let unity take back the binary, which is dismissed in the light, or let the shadow of night be brought back to the first line of the sun. Hold fixed in memory whatever has anywhere been produced in number, weight, and measure. Therefore, when you encounter the knot of the difficulty mentioned, number the small divisions of the night, note also the paraphrasmata of the daylight, and so temper one from the other that the necessary proportion may follow the action. Beware lest, while an eclipse of the sun lasts, you interrupt the necessary labor. No less take care that you be mindful of the ancient fathers, Thuysco, who, the grandson of Noah, is the father of the Teutons, and Marcomer, who is read to have first led the people of the Franks from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine. For the Franks are brothers of the Greeks and, intermingled with the Germans, once made two languages from one. Hence, according to the oracle, g.g.g. receive those who have lost one g. Finally, studious inquirer into polygraphy, you are advised that, after you have obtained the true method of working for these intentions, eagerly sought, you should prudently guard all things and not pour them out indiscriminately into the knowledge of all men, especially filthy ones. For this reason do not ask, since you know that there is nothing in the world so good and holy that the reprobate do not rather often abuse it for evil.
