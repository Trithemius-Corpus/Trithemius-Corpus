We judge it to be of little usefulness. We fear that someday we shall be ashamed to have turned our mind to these trifles, more useful matters having been set aside. But I pray that readers will spare me, since my own will did indeed draw me back from these childish studies, not to say at least partly vain ones; but the insistence of certain friends compelled me to write them, and indeed I could not refuse them this without injury to the bond of obligation contracted. Nevertheless, induced by their entreaty, I also had to connect the following chapter with the preceding matters, and to assign numbers for letters. For the ancients arranged various and manifold methods of writing under the figures of numbers, by which, through subtle invention, they safely transmitted their secrets to one another. Therefore, persuaded by the prayers of friends, we have placed a few methods out of many at the end of this sixth book, by which a path to knowledge of the others may lie open to those who do not know them. For if anyone delights to know these things, once these are known he will easily be able to ascend to higher matters, and to vary the methods discovered with manifold interchange at his own discretion. As the beginning of this matter, we have placed a table under the orchema, which, when the reading is varied in the manner of a cross, will contain ninety-six alphabets in itself.
