But so that this institution might remain forever unshaken, he gave the same men power to substitute also others, fit under certain conditions, who, enjoying the aforesaid faculty, might with impunity exercise the office of inquisition and death against the guilty. Finally he prescribed for them secret laws, hidden signs, and also the form of an oath, by which they might proceed justly in judging and punishing, and, known to one another, might remain hidden from others, and might forever preserve the necessary, more secret tribunal in Saxon land. They also used certain alphabets among themselves for a time, which nevertheless in the end fell wholly out of use. Yet the office of an inquisition of this kind remains down to the present time, whose ministers are commonly called Feimers.

If anyone according to his own judgment wishes to devise a new alphabet for himself, he should know that two things in particular must be observed, which, however, I do not always find [observed?] in the foregoing. The first is that he form characters easy to draw; and the simpler they are, the better they will be. The second is that he arrange the individual letters so that, joined into words, they present a beautiful and elegant appearance. I add a third, not promised: if you wish the writing to remain secret, make some letters of the alphabet double, after the manner of the Hebrews.

This Frank [Hicbus?] came with Marcomer from the borders of Scythia to the mouths of the Rhine in Germany, and is said to have invented these characters as a supplement to the German language; they seem to agree with it fairly well in many respects. Pharamund also, king of the Franks in Germany, the forty-third after Marcomer, who had brought in the people, died in the year of the Lord’s nativity 426; when he had sent his scouts through Gaul for the purpose of invasion, he ordered them to write with such characters.

From Latin letters also manifold alphabets are made, when, either reduced to number according to the pattern that we handed down in the preceding fifth book, or otherwise, in imitation of the Northmen, together also with Greek letters, they furnish us a very secure method of announcing whatever we wish. Let us accordingly set the letters themselves, unusually changed, according to the number and order of our customary alphabet, and we shall have a secret and mystical method of writing, quite beautiful and elegant, confirmed by practice. This method of numbering, however, is different from that which we placed in the fifth book, and more suitable to our purpose.
