Let us also set down an example of this alphabet, so that we may prove that a secret can lie hidden in it.

Gquog, oʒuqi, ykar, din, uc, qacquxua, riqdiku99i, aqqurixrk3, skti, idta, ykoc9aqukg. uc, coddotacuo3, ofoqui, iukg, ci, hidiog.

This writing contains in itself the following sense: Know, friend, that the king in council has decreed that you are to be killed. Flee therefore as quickly as possible into [Aragon?] from his face, lest you perish.

This manner of writing too is tortuous, and is subject to no regular rule, since it can be varied and changed at the writer’s pleasure by chance transposition as much as anyone wishes.

Next it must be noted that at the end of the sixth book of the Polygraphia we handed down another method of writing by numerical signs, which people commonly call ciphers; under its covering you may safely write to an absent friend whatever you wish, and no one unskilled in the art will be able either to discover or suspect that a secret lies hidden there, especially if the rules that we have given concerning these matters are observed with careful caution. This is that the paper be formed in the manner of an astronomical table, as though it contained something pertaining to the reckoning of the stars, in such a way, namely, that the characters of the signs or of the planets are inserted, so that anyone into whose hands it may perhaps fall by chance, led by the appearance of a table, may think that no secret lies hidden in it, but may suppose that everything pertains to some astronomical computation.

We have arranged twelve alphabets according to the number of the twelve signs of the zodiac, assigning to each a number of twenty-four in place of letters; the first of these, of Aries, begins from one and ends at twenty-four.

In weighty and difficult matters one must beware of beginning from one, because that very method of writing can easily be suspect on account of it. But whoever wishes prudently to use his own natural industry will find subtler things from ours, and what we have handed down without sufficient polish he will easily make better.

In the numerical dance great secrets lie hidden; if anyone properly understands it, he will penetrate more mysteries of numbers. For we had neither the time nor the reason on this occasion to pour everything forth into public view; but in our judgment what we have done will suffice for the instruction of those to whom our discoveries will one day be pleasing, so that they may have many methods not only of secretly writing whatever they wish to absent sharers in their skill, but also one method of producing from one several better and subtler methods, according to each person’s capacity of understanding.

Likewise, since the dances of numbers have been sufficiently opened in their proper places, and at the same time their tables laid out, it was not necessary here to repeat what has once been said, lest excessive prolixity create tedium for the reader. But I did not think it necessary to set down an example of writing more secretly by means of the figures of numbers, since there is no difficulty in knowing the method for all who possess even moderate ability. With like ease the very ancient signs for numbering which we have brought back to light can be understood and comprehended; by them the knowledge of numbers is extended to infinity. For whenever a stroke is placed over a smaller number, a thousandfold is denoted. For V̄ means ten thousand; etc. means one hundred thousand. M̄ means one thousand thousands. Xͨ means ten times one thousand thousands; etc. means one hundred times one thousand thousands. Mͫ means a thousand times one thousand thousands, and so on successively, as far as you may wish to advance in numbering, however much, without limit.
