But because this method of writing secretly, under a long narrative of open discourse, comprises rather few statements of hidden intention, it will perhaps be usable only in great and difficult matters, especially among those to whom leisure is pleasing and labor displeasing. We shall therefore give, in the four following books, easier methods of writing, although they are by no means free from the suspicion of observers, because they are far removed from understanding and [unclear].

In the *Steganographia*, however, the methods of writing very secretly without any suspicion are more abundant, where a word does not represent a letter, but each word represents another whole word suitable to the mystery. Let us set down an example of this: if you wish to forewarn a friend about some imminent danger, under such a narrative of words: Melancius, Tyberius, Ioannes Petrus, servants of the count de Asoto, have conspired to kill you; on the fourth day after Lawrence, at night, they will surround your house; consider what you should do. Steganography conceals this secret as follows: I beg you, dearest friend, to send me by the bearer of the present letter ten florins as a loan, because they are very necessary to me for the construction of a certain building; I shall faithfully repay them to you. Who would seek or suspect that this secret lay hidden in the discourse written above? Let us set down the same thing also in another narrative. Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who for our sake wished to become man, to suffer, and to die, have mercy on me, an unworthy sinner, now in the perishable hour of my death. Behold, now you have one hidden thought of your heart, veiled under the bounds of steganography in a faithful brevity of speech, in a twofold narrative; the whole world will not even be able to suspect it, nor, if it sought for a hundred years, to find it in any way, even if all the learned came together. Finally, steganography teaches you to hide this very secret thought of your heart not only in one or two of the narratives written above, but in almost infinitely many, by the method now described, and to announce it to whomever you wish most safely, most secretly, and most securely. All methods of this kind of invention can also be changed by metathesis in infinite variations, and by them everything can be written and announced. But why the work on steganography remains buried in silence has been sufficiently said at the beginning of our *Polygraphia*; therefore it was not to be repeated here. Let it lie hidden, then, lest it be seen by the ungrateful.

*An explanation of the obscure matters contained in the third book of our Polygraphia.*

In the third book of the *Polygraphia* we have handed down a newly devised method of secret writing, sufficiently safe and secure indeed, but suspect to all observers of containing a hidden secret. For no one, however learned, will discover the hidden mystery without knowledge of the instruction, since he cannot even understand the open writing. We arrange fabricated words, representing nothing in themselves, but only the letters of the alphabet set before them; by means of these you will be able to write and make known all the secrets of your mind to a friend, however far absent, with the greatest safety. But the unskilled and ignorant of the art, when they see the writing, will think it is some foreign language, and will be utterly ignorant of what it contains. When you wish to write or announce something to an absent person according to the method of this book, take care first of all that you properly separate every single word from the others, so that each one can be most clearly distinguished from another, lest the person to whom you write take one word for two, or two for one, because that would produce a great error. Let us set down an example of this: Abrin madon badis, cadulur pasu, adin loroff masara damis, boda omo dromi drus mastron pha lares medas raffut ma debor tara, mesen baiu phofat, galos, prosol fane lemoros, aroso habilneso, hamol heliet rhamil fesia doron hubur rodex. In this narrative of fictitious speech there is contained this brief hidden statement of a friend warning a friend of danger: Do not come to court, because the king is angry with you. You see that this method of writing and announcing whatever you wish to a friend who understands it is easy, safe, and secure.
