*Table or Index of the Lucubrations of Johannes Trithemius, first abbot of Spanheim, and afterward of St. James at Würzburg.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot, was born in the village of Trittenheim, in the diocese of Trier, on the banks of the river Moselle, on the first day of the month of February, at the eleventh hour, in the thirtieth minute after midday, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1462, in the tenth indiction of the Romans, his father being Johannes de Heidenberg, and his mother called Elisabeth de Longouico: namely, in that year in which the city of Mainz, in the dissension of two pontiffs, was captured, lost its liberty, and found servitude, many of the citizens having been driven into exile; in the fourth year running of Pope Pius, the second of that name, and the twenty-second of Emperor Frederick III. But in the year of the Lord 1482, in the monastery of Spanheim in the diocese of Mainz, namely on the first day of February, on which he had once been born, he laid aside the secular habit and there assumed the monastic one under the rule of the holy father Benedict, according to the observance newly introduced. In the following year, which was the eighty-third above the four-hundredth and thousandth, after Johannes de Colenhusen, the abbot, had been transferred by canonical postulation to [Segenia?], Johannes Trithemius, by the common election of the brothers, was promoted and ordained abbot of the aforesaid monastery of Spanheim on the fourth day before the Kalends of July, and presided for twenty-three years, one month, and nineteen days. When these had passed, not without many labors, provoked by the rashness of certain rivals, he accepted the abbacy of St. James at Würzburg, which was offered to him, and of his own free will resigned Spanheim, full of useless cares. In both places he wrote a number of little works, which he recently listed for me with his own hand on a small sheet, as subscribed below. Since you, my friend, ask me for their titles, I gladly agree to your wishes, so that, from consideration of so many labors of our common friend and teacher, you may hold his memory more closely. And first, indeed, I shall record the titles of those lucubrations which he laboriously composed while he was abbot at Spanheim. First he wrote the constitutions of his order which were made in the provincial chapters by command of the presidents, one book. On the manner of holding a provincial chapter, one book. On the Rule of Saint Benedict, two books. Homilies and exhortatory sermons to his monks, two books, which he dedicated to his predecessor. On the temptations of cloistered persons and the remedies for the same, two books.
