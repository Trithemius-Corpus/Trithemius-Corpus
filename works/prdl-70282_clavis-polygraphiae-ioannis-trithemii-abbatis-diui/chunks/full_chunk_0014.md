*An index of the things contained in the third book of the Polygraphia.*

In the third book of the Polygraphia, having overstepped the prescribed bounds of our nation by rational design, we are led to foreign matters; placed in the midst of the essential [unclear] of the Franks, we wander farther abroad through images and likenesses of barbarians. For with nearly four thousand mythological paraphrases artfully arranged in order, we show a very easy path for an artificial unity, through which it will more securely [unclear] symbolic notions with its binary at all times. Here the binary is harder for unity, unless our philologist be sharp-sighted with prudence; he will render the binary contrary to the monad. For only the ternary, which proceeds from unity and the binary, like a palindrome connects both in itself. But if the necessary utility of the [reason?] extends beyond the bounds of the proposed matter, then as often as needed let the paraphrases be numbered by minutes from the beginning of the day, until the minutes establish the degree in which night equals day. The face of this image knows perpetual silence, and does not fear the deceits of these blind men. It is extended to as great an abundance as you wish; it is varied without number.

*Brief index, or table, of the things treated in the fourth book.*

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia we consider the binary very much; when all obstacles have been removed, without the labor of difficulty, at first sight we reduce it to unity. Hence we use a circle here in great things, a line or point in the smallest. Nor does it matter if night exceeds day, or if light does not agree with darkness, because the circle receives no end, and emptiness does not admit fullness. For they are contraries: whenever they succeed one another, one is destroyed by the other. A singular mystery, leading the binary onward, returns in this fourth book of the Polygraphia, since the airy [unclear] itself, more composite than the old base, is never designated in anything other than a binary. But in that part the lightness of owls is always suspect to us in difficult matters. The construction of this matter makes paraphrases with infinite atoms.

*Index, or table, of the things treated in the fifth book.*

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have written in order, on two tables, the letters of the Latins which we Germans use; one of these is called direct, while the other is named reversed. We have placed dances beneath them, so that what we seek with labor in foreign things we may see in our own without difficulty. For besides those things that have been set down openly by us, there still lie hidden many secrets in the inner parts; through understanding them, the worker’s path to greater things is foretold, though they are by no means proposed to all. We have subjected writings to numbers, by which, according to our will, hidden things become manifest. Hence through the skillful operation of this book, all messages are delivered securely to the cabalists, although the mysteries are not free from suspicion of the glutton Bacchae. For every transposition of the form of signs produces a sigh in the beholder, although it brings no harm from the owl. But you, who shall have attained understanding of this work, will easily penetrate every transposition of letters among our Latins.
