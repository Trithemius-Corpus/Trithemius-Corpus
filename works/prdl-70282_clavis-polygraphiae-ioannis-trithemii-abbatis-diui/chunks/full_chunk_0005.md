He wrote a history or annals on the origin, kings, and deeds of the people of the Franks and Germans, in three large volumes, beginning the narrative with King Marcomer, who in the 433rd year before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine, down to the twenty-eighth and ninth year of Emperor Maximilian. To Lawrence, bishop of Würzburg and duke of East Francia, he wrote an epitome or abridgment of the histories of the same three volumes, in three books. To the same man, on the dukes and bishops of the Franks of Würzburg, one book. On the miracles shown at the memorial of our Lady Saint Mary at Dettelbach, six books. An Apologetic against Charles de Bovelles, who slandered the things he did not understand, one book. On the origin and kings of the Franks, one book. On the miracles of the same [unclear] at Urticetum near Heilbronn, shown, six books. An Apologetic against Charles de Bovelles, who slandered the things he did not understand, one book. On the origin of the miracles at the memorial [unclear].

A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, Saint James at Würzburg, one book. To Albert, of the margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, a life of the divine Rabanus, archbishop, three books. On the translation of the same man from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To the same man, a life of Saint Maximus, bishop, one book. He also wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene. On Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, a rosary. On Saint Peter the Apostle, a rosary. On Saint John the Evangelist, a rosary. At the request of John, archbishop of Trier, a sequence on Anne, which begins, “Jesu, king of the heavens,” to the melody “Ave praeclara.” Another, to be sung on her feast, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day.” A third, to the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, and it begins, “Let us rejoice in honor.” A fourth, on Saint Hildegard, virgin, which begins thus: “Praise be to thee, Lord of eternal glory.” A fifth, on Saint Rupert, duke of Bingen, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day.” A sixth, on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, and it begins thus: “Let us sing, renowned [unclear].” A seventh, on Saint Rabanus, archbishop.
