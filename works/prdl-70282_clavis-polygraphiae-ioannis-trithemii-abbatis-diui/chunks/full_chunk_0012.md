But whenever the discourse becomes rather obscure, this is done for this reason: lest a madman should misuse the sword of defense for destruction, which a sane man in soul and mind bears for his own protection. Yet nothing evil is contained in it on that account, although perverse men misuse even good things for evil. Whoever presumes to condemn this edition of ours is himself conscious and witness against himself that he has never truly understood it. Mysteries are concealed under enigmas, lest the secrets of the Bubones be made manifest to the Bacuceans in any fashion one likes. If anyone takes pleasure in knowing these things, let him approach as a reader, with envy set far away. For I am a Christian; I am a priest and a monk under the rule of the blessed father Benedict. I love Christ, and with what sincerity of mind I can, I devoutly adore him always. I have no dealings with demons, have had none, and, with God as protector, shall have none; no studies in magical, necromantic, or profane arts; no occupations, no teachings. He who thinks otherwise of me thinks wrongly, does me injury, and defends the open lie of Bouillus. But now let us dismiss these matters, and let us set forth in order what is contained in each book of our Polygraphy.

*Register or Index of the Things Treated in the First Book*

In the first book of this Polygraphy of ours we arrange and distribute, according to the order of our ternary, the words of the Latin language, as many as are found. By their use and service all princes throughout the world will find safe assistance in any secrets whatever, which, without danger to the mystery, without mark, and without suspicion of the Bacuceans, will remain perpetually hidden. For the Bubones this path is established as safe, and not ever to be violated by any investigation whatsoever. Knowledge of the hidden matter is granted to only two, if the binary is skilfully reduced to the monad. Ignorance is the mother of error, and he who scorns learning a good thing which he does not know loves blindness. The periphrases themselves, moreover, are extended in the manner either of one admonishing or of one supplicating; their subject matter can be varied for every visible thing of this world. For they are suited to individual mysteries, and are set before the secrets of the Greeks no less than before our own. When you have understood the things you see, do not rashly despise the hidden things you do not see. You perceive the accidents; the substance remains invisible. Hence the greater things lie hidden; the lesser lie open to those who look upon them. Since this has been done rationally, let no good man take it for evil, since every suitable person can from these things advance to higher matters, provided he wishes to do so.
