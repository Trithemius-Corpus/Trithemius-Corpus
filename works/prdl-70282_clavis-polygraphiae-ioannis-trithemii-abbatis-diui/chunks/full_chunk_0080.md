What we promised at the beginning of the third book of our work, the Polygraphia, for obtaining before the inexperienced a shadowy likeness of the new gloss, we have sufficiently fulfilled by way of example, with an abridgment of labor. We entrusted night, safely serving secrets, to be led then by shadows as a periphrasis; we have inserted numbers in the order of four lines [unclear], adapted for the use of those who wish. There are three thousand one hundred fifty places of minutiae, having fixed degrees; and, if you wish, however many moments of the hours remain are infinite, for making secret, in every nation and language of the world, whatever secret matter may occur. The circle, arising linearly from a point, as often as night exceeds the image of light, as much as you wish: if there is need, you will be able to bring it back to the path by which you came. If much of night remains, bring back the tail of the dragon to the original tail-piece in the head. But let the binary be wholly assured to us concerning the known signs of unity. He who hungers much should labor much, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is it a matter of great labor to lead whither you wish that which advances of its own accord. Here, therefore, we set an end to the third book of our Polygraphia, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell. From Peapolium, on the seventh day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ our Savior 1508, in the eleventh indiction.

*Here is the end of the third book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Peapolium, of the Order of Saint Benedict.*

*The fourth book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Saint James the Greater, apostle, at Wurzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, begins.*

The order of reasoning required that the fourth book of our Polygraphia be made different from the preceding ones in quality, invention, and ease, so that those wishing to share in the secrets of our matters may attain this not by one means but by various and different ones. We have extended the magpie, comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that night, always joined through the minutia to the monad in any order, may be recognized in the light of day. Here no difference is found between unity and the binary; nor will one wishing to perform the art need to have the instrument at hand, when, as the first things follow upon the last without a middle, the hidden mystery reveals itself. Hence the prudent, understanding man, experienced in the science, will at every time and place sufficiently perform the operation. The binary receives from unity, whence it produces the ternary. The first knowledge of the face of this cryptology is easy, after the moon has been illuminated by the sun, even at a single point. Yet it can also happen that an industrious gnostic may come forth without the guidance of a master. This, however, I think should be understood only in this book, but in none of the preceding ones. Concerning the following books, judgment will be given in its proper place. Among all the cabalistic methods of secrets which we have given up to the present, the procedure of this fourth book can more easily be extended to infinity. Therefore it suffices that I have handed down to those desiring more both the form and the reason of the example. The light of understanding easily penetrates all these things. I wrote at Peapolium, on the ninth day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
