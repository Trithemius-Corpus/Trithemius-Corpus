[page 479:] *The Sixth Book of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Saint James the Greater the Apostle, of Würzburg, takes its beginning.*

Before every invention and use of letters, in the beginning of the world, when men were still rude in talent [and] uncultivated in morals—under the rule of the alternating communication of the three [intelligences], as some suppose, the intelligences succeeding one another in order through one thousand and sixty-three years—the influences of the planets were present in the world, in their own manner excelling [over] bodies on earth. The ancient speculators of this matter say that Saturn, from the beginning of the world, presided over the [things] germinating [and] being born on earth, by influence, his intelligence principally moderating [it]—which intelligence they named Orifiel, that is, the life-giving [intelligence] of God—for three hundred and fifty-four lunar years, four months, and as many days and hours. These [things] we recount as poetical, [and] do not confirm. After Saturn, Venus; after Venus, Jupiter is set down to have assumed the dominion. But after Jupiter, Mercury, fourth in order, as ruler, adorned the talents of men with diverse arts. But under the three intelligences mentioned—Saturn, namely, Venus, and Jupiter—ruling, they assert that men had not even the knowledge of letters. Whence, as often as one wished to announce something secret to another, he did this not by writings—since the office of letters was lacking—but by certain similitudes of animals, trees, plants, or of other sensible [things], symbolically, according as each had agreed among themselves. The time of Saturn gave to men, as they say, morals properly agreeing with [his] influence. For it made [them] farmers, hunters, founders of cities, rude, wild, sad, simple, uncultivated in life [and] morals, living long in the flesh. After Orifiel of Saturn, as those so opining [say], they say the intelligence Anael, the mover of Venus, [received] the governance of the world by influence, taking three hundred and fifty-four years…
