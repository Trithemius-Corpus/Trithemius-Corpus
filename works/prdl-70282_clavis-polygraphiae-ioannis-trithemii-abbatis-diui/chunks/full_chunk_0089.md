*Straight table of transposition.*

[letter table]

In this canonical, or straight, table of letters, from our single usual alphabet of the Latin letters themselves, by change or transposition, you have as many alphabets as there are monograms in it throughout, namely twenty-four times twenty-four, which make in number 576; and multiplied again by the same number, they make a little less than fourteen thousand.

*Table of reversed transposition.*

[letter table]

In this second reversed table you have as many alphabets by transposition of letters as we also set down in the preceding straight one. For, as you see, under metathesis there are twenty-four toward the right; likewise as many toward the left; twenty-four downward, and twenty-four upward. These, multiplied by ninety-eight twenty-four times and reduced in the form of a cross to the four sides, make not fewer than 112 alphabets.

The same table expanded follows.

*First figure of the expansion of the straight table.*

a b c d e f g h i k l m n o p q r s t u v x y z w a b c d e f g h j k l m n o p q

This is the first figure of the expansion of the straight table: five alphabets by metathesis, the letters transposed as is seen. For in the first alphabet b is taken for a, c is taken for b, and so on, as is clearer to one looking at it. In the second alphabet c is taken for a, in the third d, in the fourth e, in the fifth f, as the placement of the order shows.

The expansion is continued.
