...read and write in his mother tongue, you will teach him, in the Latin language, as you wish, to write ornately or elegantly, and to conceal and hide all the secret mysteries of his will, and safely, secretly, and without suspicion to communicate very widely whatever has pleased one who knows the art. Again I warn you beforehand not to understand us otherwise than as having promised what we do promise.

The third benefit is that, in a few hours, you will teach a man ignorant of the Roman tongue not only what we promise, namely to write and read, but also to understand, if you prudently apply the duty of the office; this we have thought should not be [considered?] without reasonable cause. For the pupil of the new art will write in a language he had not known, Latin speech composed under regulated instruction, and he will understand and interpret it, if you order him, adequately word for word. Finally, this instruction can be extended to all the affairs and business of the world. If anyone wishes to know from me how these things can be done, he should first remember to show himself such that he deserves to receive it. For not all things are owed to everyone, but one man is to be preferred to another.

The fourth benefit is that, by extending the likeness of the thing received, all these matters can be extended into any language whatever, once the binary of the instruction has been reduced to unity. Yet not just any man at random can do this, but only he whom, with God going before by his special grace, art and nature have dignified.

The fifth benefit is that, by imitation of the likeness, we can communicate to one who knows the art the inventions of our knowledge, and whatever secrets of our mind, as abundantly as we wish, in many ways, very widely, by various methods which could not be written or noted by anyone else. Here is given a manifold speech without voice, through which safely, securely, very widely, with every suspicion removed, we will be able to indicate most fully to those who know the art all the conceptions of our mind, however difficult they may be. And this wondrous invention has innumerable advances, capable of all mysteries.

The sixth benefit is that, through understanding this work, you will be able to find any regulated metathesis or transposition of letters whatever, and nevertheless to form them in such a way that they serve only those who know the art, and absolutely no one else in the world. And there is great force in metathesis of that form, and at the same time of the operation, which has several modes and is varied in many ways.

The seventh benefit is that swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor laden with precious jewels. All things are unseasonable that do not accord with the manners of the time.
