This secret is rightly preferred to all inventions thus far, through which the hidden things of mysteries take on strength and power; yet reason is pleased to hide a great thing from [little ones?], and by no means to entrust pearls to swine. From nothing, nothing is brought back. The known multitude of visible things is great; but invisible things remain remote from us. If these are prudently adapted to those, the enlightened mind of man will [know?] and behold wondrous things. But to treat more fully of these matters is not the purpose of the present work; since they are greater and weightier, they require another work and another time.

Matthias, most powerful king of Hungary and hammer of the Turks harder than adamant, who in our age fought many victorious battles against the enemies of the cross of Christ, discovered many things by wondrous ingenuity and art, adorned owls with periphrases, kept his mysteries, [made them?] with manifold variety, and by manifest diligence destroyed the plans of his enemies. He preferred wisdom to gold; therefore, always victorious, he triumphed gloriously.

In the wise man there is a sound mind in a well-mortified body; borne upward by desire for a higher understanding, when through the likeness of the binary it has returned from the ternary into unity, thereafter it will easily rise to the denary. Pythagoras of Samos, that philosopher, warned his disciples not to forget the quaternary, in which the first perfection of numbering is contained. For thus we count: one, two, three, four. Stop; take thought, for you have ten. For the denary is the head of perfection. What is eleven, if not ten and unity? What is thirty? Three times ten. What is forty? Four times ten. You have a hundred, and you have not departed at all from the quaternary. And what is a hundred, if not ten times ten? Profound mysteries lie hidden in these things, and they are manifest to few.

For love of brevity we pass over in silence many who, aiding nature by art, adhered prudently to secrets, and what seemed impossible to many who did not know, they brought by keen study into easy use. A cultivated mind can do great things, and he who cleaves to God becomes, in understanding, higher than the world. There are many mysteries of kings, and counsels full of cares, in which confidence and trust are required; [unclear] harms the one who trusts, and [unclear], even when it is present, more often admits [unclear]. Therefore it will have been worthwhile for all princes to be philosophers and endowed with singular wisdom, just, gentle, benevolent, and distinguished by munificence, so that they themselves may neither wish to deceive others, nor easily be overthrown by others. Justice alone, the companion of prudence, makes a prince glorious.
