In this figure we have placed, for each of the remaining six signs, individual alphabets by numbers, just as they are seen set beneath the Latin letters in order, so that anyone who wishes may more safely conceal his secrets by means of them.

We have, by God’s mercy, brought the sixth and last book of our *Polygraphy* to its close; in it, ending our discourse with the wandering stars, we give the reader advance warning on certain points.

First, whenever he uses the alphabet of any given [sign?], he should prudently attach it, for the sake of a double advantage. For by this excessive labor is removed from the friendly reader, and the secret will remain hidden, since anyone ignorant of the craft will suppose that a table written with numbers and marked beforehand with the characters of signs or planets pertains to the reckoning of astronomy, and will not at all suspect that any secret lies hidden. For that reason we have not taken up the official matter of the letters from unity, but from a distance, lest we give anyone any mark of suspicion. Nevertheless prudence must be used by writers, since a clever reader who is touched by suspicion of something hidden, if he discovers the function of one number, will easily calculate the whole alphabet.

Second, let anyone who desires to be a shrewd practitioner of this matter remember that whenever a letter is doubled in any word, as often happens, it should be written only once and marked above with a point. For example, I wish to write *Ioannes* from the alphabet of Aries: I do not double 18, which is *n*, but I write thus: 14. 19. 6. 13. 10. 23.; or *abbas*: 6 [unclear]. 6. 23.; *Ioanes*, *abas*, so that every mark of suspension may be removed. In similar cases, similar caution must be employed; for unless the practitioner is endowed with diligence and knows how to find greater things from small ones, he will not be fit to have these least things of ours disclosed to him.

Not all things are to be communicated indiscriminately to everyone, especially those whose use can be turned to good and evil. Moved reasonably by that consideration, we have plainly handed down many things in these six books of the *Polygraphy* to those wishing to know, but we have allowed far more secrets to lie hidden beneath manifest things. Finally, we have written out a key that unfolds the whole work, explaining how each metaphor is to be understood; nevertheless, by the advice of certain persons, we have ordered it to remain hidden at home, so that students may not lack material for exercising their memory, and the wicked may be deprived of the means of knowing those things which they are not willing to use well.
