A most powerful king of the Franks in Germany, when, in the four hundred and twenty-fourth year of the Lord’s nativity, he powerfully entered the borders of the Gauls, in order that everything might be done safely, bound foreign minutiae to his secrets.

Clodius also, called the Long-haired, the elder son of Pharamond, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, and afterward having violently subjected all Gaul to his rule, ordered all the Gauls, as conquered slaves, to be stripped of their hair and shaved as to the beard; but he commanded the Franks, the victorious lords of the Gauls, to let the hair and beard grow. Hence he was called Long-haired. In imitation of his father, he wrapped the mysteries of counsel in signs of his own and foreign signs.

Charles, surnamed the Great, the sixty-third king of the Franks after Marcomer, who first among the Franks ruled over the Romans, with Nanno the grammarian as his teacher, desiring to adorn the German tongue, which was his paternal language, with grammatical rules in imitation of Latin, began an arduous work, which nevertheless, being entangled in many affairs and forestalled by death, he did not complete. He attached owls of mystery in various and many ways; than him Brabant, or Namur, Germany had no greater native. To each prefect of the kingdom he assigned individual owls by art.

The Northmen, having gone out from the borders of Gothia, when once they were cruelly devastating Gaul, summoned owls from Greece and prepared a safe hiding-place for their secrets, which today is known to very few. Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the deed of the Northmen, described many inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made the bright path of mystery dark. He himself first commended artrology to later memory; he invented dactylology. There were those who distinguished minutiae by the hissings of [unclear], whose prudence turned night into day; without the service of owls, light shone in darkness.

Concerning these matters, moreover, the illustrious count palatine, prince-elector, was also concerned about our Steganography, and received from us a manifest trial of a proven document. Others, multiplying minutiae by regulated division, after the manner of barbarians overturned the forms of nature, concealing in darkness the dances of the shadows of things being born; where the secret of counsel was required, they supplied cabala to the absent through diapyramata. Great labor in these matters is required, and long exercise of the mind over years; in it no one suddenly becomes skilled for use. Necessity endures all things.

There were also those whom celestial harmony taught to compose foreign sounds, of which there was a twofold use for those who understood: while one suspended the sense, the other lifted the mind into the ether. God can do all things. Many attempted to seek light in shadow without light, but they were by no means able, because the fire lies buried in ashes.
