But whoever wishes prudently to use his own natural industry will find subtler things from ours, and will easily make better what we have handed down not sufficiently polished.

Great secrets lie hidden in the numerical orchema; if anyone properly understands it, he will penetrate many mysteries of numbers. For we had neither the time nor the reason on this occasion to pour everything forth to the public. In our judgment it will have been enough that we have done this for the instruction of those to whom our discoveries will one day be pleasing, so that they may have several methods not only of secretly writing whatever they wish to absent sharers in the art, but also one method of extending it into several better and subtler ones, according to each person's capacity of understanding. Likewise, since the orchemata of numbers have been sufficiently opened and made manifest in their proper places, and their tables at the same time set out, it was not necessary here to repeat what has once been said, lest excessive prolixity create weariness for the reader. But I did not think it necessary to set down, by means of figures of numbers, an example of writing more secretly, since there is no difficulty in knowing the method among all who are vigorous in even a moderate degree of intelligence. With like ease the most ancient signs of numbering, which we have brought back to light, can be understood and comprehended; by them the knowledge of numbers is extended to infinity. For whenever a stroke is placed above a smaller number, a thousand is designated. Thus [unclear] means ten thousand, etc.; [unclear] one hundred thousand; M [with mark] one thousand thousand; X [with mark] ten times one thousand thousand, etc.; [unclear] one hundred times one thousand thousand; M [with mark] signifies a thousand times one thousand thousand, and so consequently as far as, and however far, you wish to proceed in numbering, without limit.
