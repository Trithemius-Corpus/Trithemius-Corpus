Let us set down an example of this alphabet also, that we may prove that a secret can lie hidden in it: [enciphered line, as printed: "Gquog, ozuqi, ykar, din, uc, qacquxua…"]. But this writing contains in itself such a sense: "Know, friend, that the king in council has decreed that you are to be killed. Flee therefore, as quickly as possible, into Narragonia, from his face, lest you perish." This mode of writing, too, is tortuous, and is subjected to no regular rule; for it can be varied and changed by a fortuitous transposition, at the writer's pleasure, as much as anyone shall wish. Consequently it is to be noted that, at the end of the sixth book of the Polygraphy, we handed down another mode of writing by numeral notes, which they commonly call ciphers, under whose veil you will safely write to an absent friend whatever you wish, and no one unskilled in the art will be able either to discover or to suspect that a secret lies hidden—especially if the rules which we have given upon these [matters] be observed with diligent caution. This is, that the paper be formed in the manner of an astronomical table, as if it contained something pertaining to the reckoning of the stars—namely, so that the characters of the signs or planets be interposed—so that he into whose hands it perhaps fell by chance, led by the tabular similitude, would judge that nothing of a secret lies hidden in it, but would think that the whole pertains to some astronomical computation. And we have arranged twelve alphabets according to the number of the twelve signs of the zodiac, assigning to each a number of twenty-four in place of letters, of which the first, [that] of Aries, begins from unity and ends in twenty-four.
