*Clavis Polygraphiae* — the "Key to the Polygraphia" — is Trithemius's own commentary on his *Polygraphiae libri sex*, explaining how the six books of cipher tables are constructed and how a correspondent is meant to use them in practice. A cipher manual is of little use without its key, and the *Clavis* is what made the *Polygraphiae* operational: it walks through the *modi* of enciphering and deciphering, the Ave Maria alphabets, and the polyalphabetic tables, tying the abstract apparatus of the six books to the concrete business of sending and recovering a hidden message. It was composed alongside the *Polygraphiae* in 1508 and printed with it.

This witness is the **1518 Basel edition** — printed by Adam Petri for Johann Haselberg, the edition in which the *Clavis* and the *Polygraphiae* first appeared together (the companion *Polygraphiae* witness is `prdl-24390` in this corpus). The Latin was re-OCR'd from the page scans and translated by GPT-5.5. For the cipher tables themselves see the three *Polygraphiae* witnesses (`prdl-24389`, `prdl-24390`, `prdl-24391`); for the decoding of the *Steganographia*'s separate cipher system, see the [cipher solutions page](../cipher-solutions.html).
