*In the chapter room after Prime.*

On entering the chapter room after Prime, the devout monk should remember to give thanks to God for the victory of the holy martyrs, whom he knows to have fought for the Lord, in the greatest fervor of spirit, despising their own torments, through the testimony of death. Therefore, when you have heard the brave deeds of the saints read, pray the Lord that by their merits he may deign to remit your sins, and mercifully strengthen you to bear the yoke of monastic discipline, so that you may obtain fellowship with them. And for this desire he invokes aid [unclear] from all, so that, when their passions have been read, strengthened to patience by divine examples, we may fear no adversities throughout the whole day. But when you hear that some martyr stood firm bravely in the struggle of combat, you will pray to him with these or similar words: O holy soldier of the eternal King, I ask you, by the love of our Lord Jesus Christ, which made you overcome the torments of the flesh and the snares of this world: pray for me, that I may deserve to come as quickly as possible to fellowship with you. If the name of a confessor or virgin, or of a saint of another condition, has been recited, a devout lover of God will easily vary, according to the ardor of his own affection, an extempore prayer to any saint. For affective prayers are effective above all.

*At the reading of the Rule.*

When the reader recites one passage from the Rule according to custom, let the devout monk say in his heart: Help me, sweetest Lord Jesus Christ, so that according to this standard of Saint Benedict I may be able to live religiously, and remain faithfully at all times in your most holy service.

Then let him consider that, just as the Rule is read daily for the good and devout unto perfection, so it is recited for the lukewarm, the bad, or the negligent so that they may be inexcusable, to the increase of condemnation. Finally, let him know that he is bound to faithful effort in all things that the Rule contains, just as the tenor of his profession has it. For thus he said in profession: I promise obedience and conversion of my ways according to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

*When the names of the dead are recited.*

When the names of the dead have been recited to you after the reading of the Rule, for these and for all other faithful people, entrusted and not entrusted, no less from charity than from obligation, the monk will pray thus.

*Prayer.*

I beseech you, sweetest Lord Jesus, through the pain of your wounds and through the shedding of your most holy blood, and through the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on these souls, and on all those entrusted to me in prayer, and on all the faithful departed, and grant them eternal rest.
