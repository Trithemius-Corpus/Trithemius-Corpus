*On Manual Labor. Consideration X.*

Since our most holy father Benedict, in the Rule, commending monks who live from the labors of their own hands, declares them blessed, it seemed fitting to us that after the end of the day we should say something about this most holy exercise. For we read that this work is commended in many ways by the holy fathers, and we know that in it they made no small progress before God and men. For the holy Apostle says: He who does not work shall not eat. But what Augustine, more learned among the most learned, thought about this manual exercise, he shows most clearly in the book *On the Work of Monks*. We also read that those ancient and most holy fathers dwelling in solitude labored diligently in the work of their hands, so that they would not incur the danger of idleness, and, living from their own labors after the example of the most blessed teacher of the Gentiles, would burden no one. Would that we too, brothers, might deign to undertake this holy exercise of laboring with the same intention and goodwill as they did, so that after the end of our labor we may deserve to rest with them without end in the heavenly homeland.

*Four Things Are [to Be Observed?] by a Monk in Labor*

When called to labor, the monk should strive to show himself such in the things to be observed that he does not lose the merit of bodily labor through dissolution of mind. For we see many among monks go out to labor wrongly, whom we see seeking nothing other than comforts of the flesh. Among these, some, so that at the time of the regular fast they may have an occasion for eating, take up useless and less necessary labors. For they labor in order to eat; whether it benefits or harms the monastery they do not care. Others, so that they may run about here and there without the restraint of discipline, as though by occasion, have been accustomed to arrange various labors. Others, so that they may freely indulge in vain talk and frivolities, procure labors useless to the monastery. Others love external labors so that they may be able, as though by opportunity and without suspicion, to see and speak with people, I will not say secular persons, known to them and friends according to the flesh, not according to God.
