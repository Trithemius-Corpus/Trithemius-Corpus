The love of God voluntarily restrains good cenobites from sins, and fear restrains the weak. But where neither the love of God nor fear of correction penetrates the monk, then the mind, through rebellion and the habit of sinning, draws near to hell. For Scripture says: When the impious man has come into the depth of evils, he shows contempt. What does he contemn? He contemns God, despises men, and does not know how to seek the salvation of his own soul.

Therefore he falls into the twelfth degree of pride who, through boldness in sinning, runs into an evil habit. This is the nearest descent to hell; this is the path of hell; this is the swift road to the region covered with the darkness of death. Whoever has once fallen into this degree of the habit of sinning rises again only by the strong hand of the merciful God, because when he does not resist habit, the necessity of sinning is born. For wickedness always advances toward the worse, and as it is always falling it falls more deeply.

Behold, these are the twelve degrees of pride, through which one descends to hell; whoever desires to escape them should always take care to guard himself in the observance of humility. In these twelve evils every unworthiness of vices darkens the cenobite, causes the disordered monk to turn toward himself, and in his morals changes a man into a most savage beast. The end of these things is destruction, and unless before the death of the flesh the remedy of penitence intervenes, the progression of evil degrees condemns the monk to burn without end. But whoever, disordered toward himself, has once fallen into the first degree of pride, unless he strives to rise through penitence from all of it, soon casts himself headlong into the second and consequently into all the rest in order. The sum of the disorder of carnal monks toward themselves is expressed in these degrees of exaltation, through which they show themselves to be disordered citizens of the lowest region. But what we have said about the disordered and proud monk, we must also understand concerning the prelates of the lowest region.

*In what manner the citizens of the lowest region [are disordered]. Article VI.*

Whoever, established in a monastery, neglects to order his inner affections to the purity of his holy purpose frequently also exceeds measure enormously in outward things. For monks disordered toward God are consequently deemed disordered toward all things. For just as a good cenobite, united to the divine will through love, considers all things according to the right rule of the order with a simple eye, so one turned away from God through disordered affections sins even in the good things which he uses badly. Therefore carnal monks, cultivators of the lowest region, are not only badly disordered toward the soul, but also toward the substance of the monastery
