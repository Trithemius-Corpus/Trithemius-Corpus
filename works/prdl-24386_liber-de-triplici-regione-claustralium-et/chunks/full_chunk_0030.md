Let us therefore now at last enter upon the middle region of the soul, through which one comes by stages to the highest. This region of the soul is quite fittingly called rational, in which continual labor is undertaken for the investigation of the highest truth. The fervor of mind of the citizens of this middle region always strives to raise them to higher things, but the weakness of the flesh, through the motions of a raging storm, tries to drag them back to the lowest things. Hence continual labor; hence sighs and groans arise, because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and earthly habitation depresses the thinking mind. This is the narrow way that leads to life, and few walk by it. In this region force is imposed upon the concupiscences of the flesh until the will is subjected to the spirit at its nod. For the Lord says in the Gospel: The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent seize it. In that region the war of contest is proclaimed to those who are to serve God. Thus the wise man testifies: Son, he says, when you come to the service of God, stand in fear, and prepare your soul for temptation. How great the temptations are in this middle region we learn daily through the difficulty of spiritual exercise, while, driven in various ways, we cannot remain long in fervor of spirit. Now the weakness of the flesh recalls us from zeal for advancement; now the temptation of the ancient enemy vexes us; now the company of neighbors hinders us. There is need of the greatest fortitude, because no one can resist his temptations unto the end unless he studies daily to do violence to himself. But whoever thinks that he has come to this region should not presume upon his own powers, because just as the grace of God, in order to work good in us, requires the choice of our will, so the will necessarily requires grace. For no one is holy by his own powers. Therefore let no one dare presume upon himself, but let him who stands take care lest he fall. The life of man upon earth is warfare, because one comes to the kingdom through many tribulations, and heaven is acquired by the arduous labors of virtues. The prophet says: Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming, they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves. Indeed, those who sow in tears shall reap in joy. But if anyone fails in sowing, certainly he will not rejoice in the harvest. Therefore, since through the continual labors of spiritual exercise the mind must be cleansed from the dust of the first region, do not seek leisure in this middle region; do not love the quiet of negligence, because no one is able to stand here except the one who strongly girds himself for continual labor. Whoever in this middle region does not always advance toward better things soon falls back into the negligences of his former life, and there falls into the danger of failing where he has lost the desire of advancing. This is the place of continual contest, in which he who neglects to fight retreats, to be conquered. *The preface ends.*
