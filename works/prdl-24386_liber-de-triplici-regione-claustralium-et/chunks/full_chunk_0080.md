But we know the citizens of this most holy region infallibly by the fruits of the Spirit, concerning which we know that the holy Apostle spoke. “The fruit of the Spirit,” moreover, “is charity, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, long-suffering, meekness, faith, modesty, continence, chastity.” These fruits the citizens of this third region bring forth, since, filled with God and united to him, they walk always joyful in the Lord and most fervent in charity. For whatever befalls the just man dwelling on this mountain of love will not sadden him. For the soul, always peaceful and tranquil in the love of God, is lifted up by no prosperities and disturbed by no adversities.

*That this highest region of the Spirit is called a pleasant and most true treasuring-up. Article VI.*

Sixth, this most beautiful region of the Spirit is called a pleasant and most true treasuring-up, since whatever is acquired through the exercise of good work is possessed in simplicity of spirit. The treasure of the soul is the love of God; when the mind, purified, is kindled by its fervor, it is raised to this region as though to love’s own storehouse. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “Where your treasure is, there also will your heart be.” This is the treasure hidden in the field of the Scriptures; when a man has found it, he hides it, and going away sells all things, so that he may buy the field with the treasure. The field is this highest region of the Spirit; the treasure is divine love. When a man finds it, he ought to despise all other things, so that he may be able joyfully to possess the field with the treasure. There is no possession in the world more joyful than divine love; there is no greater beauty in this life, since whoever has had this treasure of love seeks nothing more. And just as fire is naturally driven upward, so love is raised into this region of the Spirit as though to its own proper dwelling. For the mind, set aflame by divine love with burning affections, is always lifted up toward God, and beyond every thought it is moved wonderfully, sweetly, day by day, toward its own beginning. For, purified in the exercise of the second region, it prepares itself for the ascent of this third region through a loving affection; it pants to come as quickly as possible to him whom it loves; it thinks of nothing other than Jesus, the treasure of its heart; it adores him with burning sighs because of the greatness of its love. But when the most sweet office of contemplation has been completed, suddenly the beloved slips away; the mind, languishing with love, is called back to the exercise of the second region, in which the true lover of God is indeed seen to dwell bodily among men, but in affection and desire with his most beloved Jesus [unclear]
