But hurry, and do not put it off from day to day, since he who receives penitents with humble confession has not promised tomorrow to the one who delays. For where haste finds pardon, delay often cuts off life. The hope of a longer life drags many to destruction, because, while they trust that they can live longer in this world, they make the time for penitence pass by without penitence. But you, man, if you accept my counsel, do not put off penitence, since you are utterly ignorant of what tomorrow will bring you. For he deserves to lose the time for penitence who so often is not afraid to postpone the zeal for repenting.

*On the Vanity of Present Things and the Eternity of Future Things*

[unclear]: if the vanity of visible things and the eternity of invisible things are pondered with diligent attention. For the things that we perceive in this world are wholly subject to vanity and corruption, and indeed will endure for a very brief time. All mortal things hang under an uncertain chance, and by their own mutability promise flight. Whatever you have today will perhaps leave you tomorrow; or while you are speaking it will cease to be yours. For we perceive nothing in this world as lasting and stable, but see all things changing with time.

Therefore ascend the [unclear] watchtower of your mind, O faithful cenobite, and consider that all things which time contains are nothing, since whatever is now seen as visible will shortly be corrupted. Take from past things a consideration of present things, and see that what has passed away does not exist, and what now seems to exist will shortly not exist. Where are the kings of the nations, where the princes, where the leaders, where the men once powerful in the world? Where are all the lovers of the world, where the men who despised the arduous way of heaven? Finally, where are all those once known to you in the world, whom death has meanwhile snatched away while you were in the cloister? Behold, from the beginning of the world, both those who lived for many times and those who lived for few have all died in their own generation, and according to the diversity of their merits they have also received different rewards, with justice dictating. The lovers of God receive bright everlasting dwellings, filled with inestimable joys, associated with the Creator of all things; and the worshippers of the world, scorning God’s commandments, once swollen here with honors, puffed up with knowledge, immersed in riches, given over to pleasures and vices, have descended to hell to be tormented without end. What, I ask, does the once laborious contempt of the world, contrary to the flesh, now harm the saints in heaven, for which they have eternal [unclear], compared with the consolation of the flesh once enjoyed, for which they now receive the torments of eternal damnation?
