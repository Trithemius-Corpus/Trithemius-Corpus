*Here begins the third region of the soul, which is called spiritual, in which the highest tranquillity of the mind is held.*

The third, or highest, region is called spiritual, in which the highest [repose] of the mind and tranquillity is engaged. And this region is compared to the empyrean heaven, in which is the true rest of all and the true felicity of souls. Dwelling in this region, the spirit deserts the rational [faculty] beneath it, and, raised up by love, marvelously ascends into God. Whatever it does in this most knowing region is judged wholly divine. Here the human spirit, caught up above itself, receives the divine inflowing, and, drowsing even unto the darkness of divine contemplation, abstracted from all creatures, sweetly comes to rest. In this region the omnipotent God directs the contemplating spirit to himself; he simplifies the intellectual powers of the soul, recalled from every phantasm into the inner chamber; he perfects them according to their objected tendencies; and the mind, stripped of all multiplicity, leading it to himself as the one [object] of perception, he makes also one in himself—not by nature, but by gracious participation. The men of this highest region are truly spiritual, for in contemplation, [set apart] from all things and affections of the age, they love nothing in this world except God; they desire nothing inordinately; but, absorbed by inner affection into the divine spirit, they vehemently thirst for God himself, and are kindled with ineffable longing into his love. With so great a fervor of love do they act toward God that, raised above their very selves, they desert all the intellectual operations [of the soul], and [the mind] is caught up into the abyss of divine contemplation, [souls] becoming one [with God] while the property of their natures is preserved, by the gift of participation. The apostle of Jesus Christ says it more knowingly: "He who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit [with him]." And again he says: "Those who are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God." The same apostle also shows himself a citizen of this most knowing region, where he cries out to the Philippians: "But our conversation is in heaven." For as long as we are in the body we are pilgrims away from the Lord; but [it is] as though through a dream that we are sweetly led to our homeland—which we understand by a remote comparison. For in dreams we go forth by the images of things [reserved] to the spirits in the imagination; in contemplation, beyond all imagination, pure, we raise ourselves to God with mind and spirit. And therefore this highest region of the soul is called spiritual, from which all the religious [take] the name of spirituality. Woe to us! We are called spiritual from this most happy region; we religious are named its due lovers—who yet, save by small [measures], do not ascend the height of this summit of contemplation, but with the weak [we hope]…
