Again, descending more deeply, with the gaze of the mind [if opportunity is present], try, as far as you can, to fix yourself in the abyss of infinite depth, and to consider how long that which is eternal is. Eternal is the time which is closed by no end, bounded by no space. The saints of God will exult forever in blessed glory. Sinners in hell will burn in misery. O eternal name, terrible without end: who will be able to think of you or speak of you without fear? Truly, he who thinks of you never sins forever. The eternal has no end. Hear a comparison; and since it will not be sufficient for eternity, receive fear. If a heap of sand were set up in the size of Mount Olympus, stretched up to heaven, from which, after ten thousand years ten times over, a little bird were to consume only one grain, that mountain would by no means be said to be destined to last forever, but would be subject to an end; and although after innumerable years, nevertheless sometime it would cease. Yet all that time during which something of the mountain remained, however very long it might be, would nevertheless, in comparison, seem nothing beside eternity. That which is eternal admits neither an imagining of an end, nor hope of completion, nor any boundary.

O therefore how foolish, how mad is the man who casts away this consideration of eternity, and uselessly spends the acceptable time granted to him for earning eternal goods. The hour will come, and now hastens near, in which the wretch, when he is no longer able to do better, will learn what the eternal is. But you, my monk, who desire to be faithful, cast away from yourself all concern for the world; set all your care upon the love of God. Do nothing, think nothing, except what can lead you to the love of your Creator, or what you know pertains to the edification of others or to pure bodily necessity. Separate, I pray, from the lowest things, so that you may be able to delight in eternal goods. Let no solicitude of the flesh remove you from the inward tranquility of the mind; let no pleasure corrupt you. Strive always to persist in prayers with devotion as much as you are able, and more often to raise yourself upward through the affection of love.

*On Always Having Zeal for God.*

The seventh root of the fear of God is zeal for God, in which the monk is commanded to exercise himself not a little. For no one tolerates calmly the injuries of his lord if he truly loves him. Therefore you, monk, who desire to be a faithful servant of Jesus Christ, do not neglect his zeal in yourself; when you see an injury to God, do not pretend not to notice it, because whoever does not at once resist those who disparage him is proved to love his Lord less. Let men know you as such a lover of God and enemy of vices that in your presence they do not dare to speak evil, nor to do anything evil while you look on [unclear].
