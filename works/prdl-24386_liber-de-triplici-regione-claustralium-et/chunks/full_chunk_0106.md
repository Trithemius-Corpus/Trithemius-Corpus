*At “Ite, missa est.”*

*When at the end “Ite, missa est” is sung,* see that your mind is lifted up with the holy angels returning into heaven, so that through longing you may deserve to behold the Lord Jesus presiding in majesty, whom, now sent to the Father in the sacrament, you can no longer see on earth. Therefore kindle your longing by the image of desolation, in the manner of those who are unwillingly separated from their friends. Say, then, in your heart:

*Meditation*

Behold, my Lord has returned to the heavens; he has left me desolate on earth. What am I doing here with mortals, I who have an immortal Lord? Why do I not follow him? Alas for me, because my sojourning has been prolonged; I have dwelt with the dwellers in darkness; my soul has long been a sojourner. I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ; but who will free me, unhappy man, from the body of this death? Most beloved Lord Jesus, O my hope, O God and savior of my soul, draw me after you; I shall run in the fragrance of your ointments.

O my sweet love, do not forget me unto the end, but deign often to lift up my mind to you, so that, since while set in this mortality I cannot see you bodily, at least, lifted up in affection and desire in your most holy love, I may deserve more often to behold you in spirit in glory. O my salvation, O heart of my heart, most beloved Jesus, do not abandon me or despise me; be my gracious helper and direct me always in your most holy will, so that I may be able to please you [unclear].

*After high mass and the following hour*

After the end of the hour which immediately follows high mass, return to your cell with the customary reverence described above; pour out prayers to the Lord with fervor of spirit, as we taught after Matins; devote what remains of the time to meditations. Consider the foulness of sin: how it separates from God and condemns forever the one who is turned away and does not repent. Therefore take care to turn your will wholly to God with the firmest resolve, and to avoid the contagion of sin at all times with all your strength.

*On the regular refectory meal. Consideration V.*

When the signal for the meal is rung, feel heartfelt compassion for the sick, the needy, and the poor who suffer want, and, when you can do nothing else, pray to the Lord for them. Consider that among Christians there are many far better than you whom harsh poverty oppresses in this life; at this hour neither the food necessary for sustenance is offered to them, nor are even the thinnest garments supplied for bodily clothing.
