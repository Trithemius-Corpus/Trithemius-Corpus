First, therefore, let the faithful man consider the beginning of his own creation, and he will find that, when he did not exist, he was created from the goodness of God alone. Who could have asked God, when no one existed? That highest and utterly ineffable Good created you in his own image, O man, when you did not exist; from goodness alone he made you a sharer in his goodness. What did you deserve? Or what advantage could your creation bring to almighty God, who, always subsisting unchangeable in himself, admits no accident? Nothing is new to God; nothing can be old to the highest and unchangeable Good. He is always what he can be, for neither does the best become better, nor the greatest become greater. God created you by his goodness, not because your creation was necessary to him, but because it was advantageous to you alone. Whoever, by continual meditation, considers this goodness of God deservedly praises his Creator.

Secondly, lest you be lifted up into pride, O man, consider the vileness of your creation: how you were made from the mud of the earth, and after a little while, consumed by worms, you will return into the earth as dust. Why then are you proud, earth and ashes? Your conception contains guilt, your birth misery, your life punishment, the dissolution of death anguish. For a man born of woman lives a short time, is filled with many miseries, and then is most miserable when, miserable as he is in the flesh, he does not recognize himself.

Thirdly, consider the origin of your conception, and you will find your soul defiled by the foulness of original sin. For we are born children of wrath, but, receiving the grace of baptism in the blood of Christ Jesus, we are cleansed from the original stain. Deservedly, through this consideration, we love our Savior, by whose mercy we have been cleansed from the original stain.

Fourthly, faithful soul, consider how exceedingly vile you have made yourself by repeating your ways in the act of sin: how you have shown yourself an adulteress to Christ your spouse, when he had joined you to himself through his blood, wholly unstained. What are you doing, O wretched one? Why do you desert your most loving spouse, or why, foolish one, do you withdraw from your beginning? Know yourself to be placed in the flesh, not so that you may consent to its vices and concupiscences, but rather so that you may resist by the command of reason. For as often as you have, by dissembling, so to speak, surrendered your consent to the disordered motions of the flesh and those contrary to divine rectitude, so often, turned away from the highest Good and from your spouse, you show yourself an adulteress. Beware, beware, O soul redeemed by the blood of Christ, lest the love of the flesh deceive you; for just as the first man, deceived by love of his woman companion, fell into death, so every soul that consents to the pleasures of its flesh shamefully withdraws from its beginning, which is God.
