[At whose majesty?] the angels tremble; at whose might all the powers of heaven marvel. To the highest and immutable good, God, the beginning and end of every creature, the rational spirit ought to devote love and the desire for a cleaving that is beyond human understanding; and, despising all phantasms, it ought to raise itself higher, wholly, to that which is truly one, as to its own center. It can lift itself toward this the more easily, the more, leaving all other things, it has striven to unite itself to itself. For by the power of that One itself union comes about everywhere; nor are like things more easily united with one another for any other reason than that in quality they are one. Therefore the mind is then most rightly said to be united with the One itself, the principle of things, when it itself, having especially been made one in itself, separated from every multitude, and above all imagination, reason, and understanding, escapes as its own unity alone, enjoying through loving affection the unity of God, joining, so to speak, its own center with the center of the universe. But when the human mind itself, ill at ease in its quasi-marriage with the flesh, is turned toward any animal passions whatever, then that unity itself is badly changed into a multitude contrary to the sole One, and it becomes the more removed from its principle, the more it has been divided from itself. Finally the mind itself contracts great difficulty in returning to that ineffable divine One whenever, confused in itself, it falls into the sin of apostasy. Think now how great and ineffable is he against whose honor sin is committed; and since you cannot understand the greatness of his majesty, learn humbly to fear his power.

*Sixth, consider what, wretched one, you have lost by sinning, so that, when the magnitude of the loss is known, repentance may hasten and restore through tears what was lost.* What then, wretched one, have you lost by sinning? Hear what you have lost. You had been made a son of God by grace, a companion of angels, a member of Christ, and chosen as heir of eternal happiness; you ought to have lived and rejoiced without end in the good things of the Lord with all the saints. But what became of you when you sinned? You were compared to foolish beasts and made like them. For when you were in honor, you refused to understand; you abandoned the fountain of life; by sinning you were made a companion and member of the devil, a harlot of Antichrist, an heir of hell. You abandoned life; you chose everlasting death. If you fear the sentence, change your will, and you will find forgiveness.

*Seventh, consider what you have deserved by sinning; for if eternal death is inflicted for one sin, think what one who has committed many, not to say innumerable, deserves.* For the soul that has sinned shall itself die. Moreover, even through one mortal sin you have deserved to become a companion of the devil, to be deprived of all the joys of heaven, to be burned forever in the fires of hell. These are the rewards that sin has brought you; these are the gifts it promised.

Now therefore, from these seven considerations, make a plaster for the [soul?].
