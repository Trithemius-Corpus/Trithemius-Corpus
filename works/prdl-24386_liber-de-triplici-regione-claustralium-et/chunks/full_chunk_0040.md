The greatest virtue of a monk is humility, and the gravest crime in a cenobite is pride. For a monk who is proud with a swollen mind becomes an imitator and servant of the devil; and he is stripped of all virtues, if he had any, because no virtue can subsist in a mind in which pride has happened to dwell. Therefore the cenobite who desires to make progress in this middle region should learn to order himself toward the true advancement of monastic discipline through humility, because no one truly attains humility except one who dwells upon spiritual studies. But true advancement in the regular discipline, excluding all adulterous Muses, arises from the degrees of humility, which are set down as twelve in the Rule through their roots. I say that the roots of this advancement come from the study of sacred reading, which lead a man little by little to the fear of the Lord. From reading arises spiritual meditation; from this holy compunction is born. But the more useful manner of cenobitic meditation consists in the remembrance of the Lord’s Passion and in the consideration of God’s benefits. Further, those who, passing beyond this middle region, come to tranquility of spirit, make their meditations more sublime through contemplation of the divine majesty and excellence. And concerning these we shall speak in their own place. These two roots, moreover, receive their increase from the watering of constant prayer, and grow strong into the strength of a very firm plant. For no meditation profits without prayer, and study without it will not save.

From these three arises spiritual advancement, the fear of the Lord, which is the first degree of humility; from it the other degrees, succeeding in order, are brought forth. But monks who neglect the salutary study of the Scriptures and holy meditations together with constant prayers cannot ascend to this first degree of humility; when will they be able to come to the others? For the second is never ascended if the first is neglected. Therefore he who neglects one ascends none.

The study of sacred reading, however, must not be undertaken by them for the vanity of the world, nor for the pleasure of a seduced mind, because reading never profits toward compunction when the delight of the flesh or of vanity has corrupted it. But a monk cannot attain devotion through the study of sacred reading unless he strives to change all curiosity into a simple spirit. Therefore whoever desires to advance from the reading of the Scriptures to compunction, whoever longs to ascend to humility through the roots of fear, ought not to choose just any subject matter for reading, but only that which can more fully stir the mind to the love of God. For this purpose the reading of the conduct of the saints seems to me sufficiently suitable, because often the mind, as it lies low, is raised up to the love of God when the life of his servants is recalled to memory.
