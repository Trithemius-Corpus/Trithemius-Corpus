For the publican, terrified by the guilt of his sins, feared the sentence of the just judge, and therefore, being pierced with compunction, said: “God, be merciful to me, a sinner.” But Jerome is shaken by fear not from the guilt of conscience, but, grounded in charity and reckoning himself an unprofitable servant, reveres the abyss of divine justice.

The second degree is to relinquish one’s own will for the love of God, and in no respect to love one’s own willing contrary to the rule. This degree arises from the first ascent of humility and is raised into the third. For truly, he who fears God and fears to be separated from him, and truly dreads undergoing punishments, hates nothing more than his own will. For the faithful cenobite knows that every sin arises from one’s own will alone, and therefore detests it as the gate of hell. For that blessed man truly said: “Take away one’s own will, and there will no longer be hell.” For nothing burns in hell except one’s own will; nothing loses the grace of God except voluntary fault. Therefore whoever truly fears God and fears losing his grace is afraid of his own will as of an ambusher of his soul. Consideration of the danger of one’s own will makes the cenobite despise his own willing and deny himself to himself. And this denial of one’s own will and of oneself is accomplished in the manner that will be stated in what follows.

First, then, he who denies himself to himself studies to live according to the teaching of eternal wisdom and not according to his own impulses, and to obey Christ in all things as he persuades him by humility. Therefore let the Savior say to the cenobite: “If you wish to deny yourself and follow me, turn your eyes away lest they see harmful things, your ears lest they hear vain things; and in place of the sweet things of this world take bitter things. Learn to despise the pleasures of your body, to love tranquillity and peace of heart, to receive all tribulations for love of me patiently and gladly, and desire to be seen by all as worthless and contemptible. Consequently, learn to break your wills in all things, to mortify your desires, to despise all earthly things, and to conform your life, as far as possible, to the conduct of your Savior according to your strength. For he who says that he remains in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked.” Whoever has done these things has well denied himself.

Second, the denial of one’s own will is then rightly accomplished when the advantages of others are set before one’s own. For the holy apostle says: “Not seeking the things that are their own, but the things that are others’.” For according to the saying of Bernard, the soul is laid down for the brethren as often as their advantages are preferred to one’s own benefits for the love of God.
