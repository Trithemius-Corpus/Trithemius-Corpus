For indeed, he who loves you forgets himself. He meditates on nothing other than your most sweet presence. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves you; nowhere is anything found that the soul desires besides you. O, with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is distressed; with what abundant tears the face is watered in secret, because as often as it thinks of the face of the beloved presiding in heaven, so often it bewails itself as desolate on earth. Meanwhile the mind, languishing with love, attempts to say something of its most beloved Jesus; it strives also to stir others to love of him. But as soon as it has begun to utter the most sweet name of Jesus, because of the greatness of love it falls silent against its will. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, but is felt with sweetness in the secret of the mind. I know who said: “My love has been crucified.” “Who is your love, O servant of God? Why do you weep? Why do you groan?” “Ignatius. My love has been crucified, and shall I not mourn? My Lord is dead, and shall I be able to restrain my tears?” Do not weep, O faithful soul; for although your love died in order to redeem you from death, nevertheless he has now risen from the dead in order to crown you in heaven. Be faithful unto death, and he will give you the crown of life.

*On the manner and seven degrees of divine love, comprising the conclusion of this second region.*

Since no one is suddenly made highest, nor can anyone by his own powers suddenly ascend to that very summit of divine love, it is useful now briefly to note the fixed modes or degrees themselves of loving God. Therefore, for moving the human heart to divine love, the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is in my judgment more effective than all other things, if it has been purely, sweetly, fervently, perseveringly, and diligently pondered in the mind of a human being. For indeed, whoever recalls to memory, with compassion and thanksgiving, the life, Passion, and death of the Savior according to the principal and especially important articles, prepares a swift ascent of the heart to divine love. But we shall treat this most holy occupation of the mind concerning the Lord’s Passion appropriately in the second principal part of this work, where we shall set forth more fully the manner of spiritual exercise. But insofar as the account of what has been said pertains to this place, I find three modes of loving God, which I shall briefly explain in order.

The first mode commands that you love him who loves you sweetly; join yourself wholly to him delightfully, so that your heart loves nothing apart from God and seeks nothing elsewhere. For since God is the whole, he alone desires to possess the whole heart of the lover; he withdraws himself from scattered thoughts. Therefore, if you wish to love sweetly, love totally.
