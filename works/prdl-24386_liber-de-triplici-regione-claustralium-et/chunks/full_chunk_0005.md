Therefore, approaching as best we can the subject proposed for our consideration, we divide the first treatise into three parts, according to the threefold state of cloistered men, of whom we may call the first sensual, the second rational, the third spiritual. From this threefold state of men we take a fitting title for this work, which we call *On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Men*. In these three regions the whole multitude of monks is comprised, as will be said in what follows.

*On the first region of the soul, in which carnal monks live with beasts: disordered by many passions, living sensually in the lowest region*

The first region of the soul, then, is called sensual, lowest, and also animal; in it the roots of all evils are contained. Men placed in this dark region share, in their kind, with beasts, since they observe, so to speak, bestial habits, while they give either nothing or little to reason, but, like beasts, yield everything to sensuality. For in that lowest region three sensual powers of the soul preside: the first, the sensitive power, is spread about the objects of the five senses; the second, the concupiscible power, delights in carnal things; the third, the irascible power, flees things contrary to the pleasures of the flesh.

And this lowest region is compared to the airy heaven, because, just as hailstorms, snows, lightning, and various tempests are generated in the air, so too in the minds of carnal men inhabiting this dark region various tempests of vices rage. Woe to carnal monks who inhabit this dark region, in which the motion of crimes is frequent and the storm of pleasures continual. These are monks given over to animal passions, who subject reason to sensuality, who live according to the flesh in bestial desires. Manifest, moreover, are the works of the flesh among those dwelling in this lowest region: fornication, uncleanness, shamelessness, luxury, service of idols, sorceries, enmities, rivalries, angers, quarrels, dissensions, sects, envies, murders, drunkenness, feasting, and things similar to these. Concerning them the apostle cries out directly that those who do such things will not obtain the kingdom of God. These are those living in the lowest work of religion, who despise the traditions of the holy fathers, who presumptuously contemn the precepts handed down by their elders. These are the unclean worshippers of the lowest region, given over to vices and carnal desires, who do not care to have God in their knowledge, who serve the pleasures of the flesh, who [unclear] the vow of holy religion which they have professed, who, despising the salvation of their souls, scorn to follow the grace of God calling them.

Therefore he handed them over to a reprobate sense and to false judgment, so that they judge evil to be good, and falsely suppose that the things which in truth are worst are themselves best. These are those of whom the holy apostle prophesied: carnal monks, whisperers, detractors, hateful to God, insulting, proud, puffed up, inventors of evils, disobedient to superiors, foolish, disorderly, without affection, without covenant, without mercy, filled with every iniquity, malice, wickedness, contention, deceit, and malignity; although they know the justice of God, they do not wish to understand that those who do such things are worthy of eternal death, and not only those who do them, but also those who consent to those who do them, and those who by duty of office by no means oppose the pursuits of the wicked. For indeed the ruler of souls who does not correct his offending subjects involves himself in damnation for the crimes of others, and by keeping silent becomes their associate, whose faults condemnation will follow. For whoever is appointed shepherd of souls and neglects to correct sinners according to the canonical rule, even if he is free of his own offense, nevertheless will be condemned before the strict judge for another’s merit.

For the supreme shepherd says to the prophet: Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel, and you shall hear the word from my mouth, and you shall announce it to them from me. If, therefore, when I say to the wicked man, You shall surely die, you have not announced it to him, nor have spoken so that he may turn away from his wicked way and live, then that wicked man indeed shall die in his iniquity, but I will require his blood from your hand. And Benedict our father says in the Rule: Let the abbot know that the fault lies upon the shepherd if he shall have been able to find anything of less profit for the father of the household in his sheep. O sentence always to be feared by all shepherds: because not only is the depraved conduct of subjects ascribed to the ruler, but he is also held guilty before the inward judge for their lesser progress. O what shall we wretched shepherds say in that strict examination, we who neglect to correct according to the Rule the monks entrusted to us when they offend in many ways; who do not call those lying in the lowest region to higher things, but, carnal with the carnal, grow numb in the shadow of death with harmful idleness.
