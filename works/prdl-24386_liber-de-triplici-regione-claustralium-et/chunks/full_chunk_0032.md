The highest truth and unchangeable good is God, who said to Moses: “No man shall see me and live.” And the disciple of the highest truth says: “No one has ever seen God.” But the prophet too, addressing the Lord, says: “Truly you are a hidden God.” Although the aforesaid three powers of the soul cannot perfectly comprehend their object in this life, nevertheless none of them ought to cease from its office, lest it incur the danger of failing precisely where it has laid aside the practice of striving. Therefore the citizens of this middle region mortify evil affections in themselves; they adorn themselves with constant virtues; they direct all their works with right intention; they depart not at all from the office of reason.

But the citizens of this region are scholastic men, speculative and active, devoted to various and indeed honorable arts, who subject the sensual or carnal powers to reason; who do not cease to cleanse these three powers of the soul from all unlawful objects; who always lift their intention toward the end of honorable usefulness; who reform the will, in act or in habit, toward its due end. Therefore the monk who loves purification and desires to ascend in spirit to the third region ought to observe the following principal articles, which stand in the middle region.

*The summary of the truth of this middle region is expressed in seven principal articles.*

Whoever in this middle region desires to advance according to the vow of his profession should take diligent care to observe the following articles. If he neglects to keep them, he shows that he is still not a citizen of the middle region, but of the lowest. For, as we said above, no one can remain in this middle region without a continual effort to make progress; in it, not to have advanced is to fail, and not to have ascended is to descend. Therefore the monk who, having overcome the dark region, has at last, with the Lord’s help, deserved to reach this middle one, should know that he must take care not to abandon, even for a little, the effort to make progress, but always stretch himself toward further things; and let him remember the admonition of the wise man, who says: “Unless you hold yourself constantly in the fear of the Lord, your house,” that is, your conscience, “will quickly be overthrown.” For it is impossible for anyone to advance in holy conduct if he neglects daily to renew the effort of progress through the fear of the Lord. The effect of this renewal is then shown to be ordered when the truth of the articles of the middle region is duly observed. The first article, therefore, is diligent attention to the cenobitic end.
