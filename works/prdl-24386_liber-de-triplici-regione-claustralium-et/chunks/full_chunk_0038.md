But severity is by no means pleasing to the weaker, unless kindness has been tempered somewhat with sweetness. Therefore, accordingly, kindness itself must be taken up by our soldier, so that when, through the weakness of the flesh, tempted in battle or even sometimes wounded, he falters, he may at once rise again through penitence, to fight more strongly anew. For the soldier of Christ, if he has fallen in battle, must rise again as often as necessary, through contrition and the other remedies of penitence, to fight once more, until at last, by God’s mercy, he is able to overcome the adversary. If the just man falls seven times in a day and rises again, how shall the sinner, but now penitent, not hope for pardon? Rise again to fight as often, O soldiers, as you have fallen when the adversary conquered; always renew the warfare afresh more bravely, so that the enemy whom you cannot destroy by strength you may at last overcome by perseverance. Do not despair of victory, O faithful cenobite, because, often conquered by the temptation of vices, you have fallen into the snare. Do not lie prostrate any longer because you feel yourself weak, but rise again at once as often as you have slipped, and trust how merciful a Lord you have; by his help you will at last be victor against the adversary. For no one becomes supreme all at once, nor is victory over the enemy granted immediately to incautious soldiers, lest, to their greater ruin, they be lifted up into pride. The possession of a thing acquired with difficulty is always dearer, and a good that is acquired laboriously is guarded with greater diligence. For so long does God, the kindly Father, permit us, conquered by the temptations of the adversary, to fall by turns into the pit of sin, until, conscious of our weakness, we learn to hope in nothing in our own powers, but only in his grace. But you, man, who have often broken your resolve by sinning, never delay to rise again; for even if you cannot perfectly subject the enemy to yourself, nevertheless it will be good for you if the last day can find you standing and fighting, even after an often repeated fall. But for those to whom the mercy of God has granted strength and grace to conquer the adversary, this counsel does not loosen discipline. Nevertheless, let him who stands take heed lest he fall.
