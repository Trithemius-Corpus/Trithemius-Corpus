For, as a certain lover of the wisdom of the world said: God is a sphere whose center is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere. He is within all things and is not enclosed; he is outside all things and is not excluded; he is beneath all things and is not oppressed; he is above all things and is not borne up. He himself contains all things, who, though wholly everywhere, is enclosed in no place; great and immeasurable, he is bounded by no quantity. He orders all things as he wills; stable in himself always and immovable, he changes the vicissitudes of things; for the good he mercifully prepares eternal rewards without passion of mind; angry without disturbance, he condemns the unjust. Great without magnitude, immeasurable without quantity, he has set a limit to things; governing the world, he disposes all things.

Third, the merciful kindness of God is praiseworthy in our redemption, because the man whom he created in his own image, fallen so miserably into the pit of damnation, he restored so mercifully. O what shall I, a little worm, indeed nothing in comparison with my maker, render to the supreme and incomprehensible majesty, who, when I did not exist, created me by his own power; when I had perished, became man for my sake and, dying on the cross, redeemed me? What shall I repay you, mediator of God and men, Jesus, savior of the world, who, though you are God most high and inestimable, became man for me, most vile, and willed to undergo things so vile and unworthy? Behold, my heart speaks to you; all my inward parts incline toward you, since no voice can express what you give your faithful to feel within, in the heart, concerning your most innocent passion.

Fourth, in the work of our justification the kindly patience of God is praiseworthy, because he not only mercifully tolerates sinners but also calls them, to be justified, to repentance with promises of eternal joys. Who could suffice to praise this kindness of the most indulgent Father, who so mercifully justifies sinners returning to him, grants after a fall the grace of meriting, orders manifold opportunities for living well, and supplies his sacraments to the penitent as certain vessels of grace? Finally, for compunction, instruction, consolation, and thanksgiving, he pours the Holy Spirit into his faithful, making friends and sons out of enemies. He does not consider what sort of sinner a person previously was; he seeks only that henceforth he remain faithful to him. Great is the kindness of divine compassion, which so mercifully receives the penitent to pardon, and even promises them great rewards after guilt.

Fifth, the profound justice of God is praiseworthy in the damnation of the reprobate, because he allows no evil to pass unpunished, depriving the sinner of the good which the fool is not afraid to lose by sinning.
