We by no means thrust our childish efforts upon these men, whom we know to be higher than the stages of the present exercise. For we, imperfect as we are, write for beginners who are making progress; we are not teaching the perfect, but rather are humbly prepared to listen to them as teachers.

*Here begins the second principal part of the little work on the threefold region of cloistered persons, which contains the manner and form of the daily exercise of monks.*

He who is about to serve as a soldier under the true king, our Lord Jesus Christ, according to the monastic rule, should humbly and perseveringly strive to take up, first, the very strong arms of holy obedience in the Lord; then he should accustom himself to be continually exercised in holy works, to persist in prayers without interruption and with devotion, and to love God above all things. Whatever he does, let him set before himself the honor of God alone; whatever he begins, let him always first offer prayer to the Lord. For he truly speaks with the Lord God who is always occupied in devout prayers. Therefore, so that a good cenobite may continually have ready at hand a manner of praying with elevation of the mind, he should not fail to read the present little work rather often and with attention. For in it we have arranged for the several hours and offices certain brief little prayers, through which the mind is more often raised to the love of heavenly things, and the fervor of holy purpose is more often renewed.

*At Matins.*

Therefore, when the devout cenobite has been awakened for Matins, let him at once shake off from himself all the sluggishness of sleepiness; let him believe that he has been called to serve as a soldier for his Lord, to whom he has made profession. Let him therefore hasten to meet his king with cheerfulness, and humbly prepare himself for his immortal service with fervor of spirit. Accordingly, let him immediately rise with the greatest propriety; and sitting on his bed, and fortifying himself with the sign of the cross, let him say:

*Prayer.*

In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. Blessed be the name of the Lord, from this time forth and forevermore.

Then, getting down from his bed, let him say on bended knees:

I give thanks to you, almighty God, who have deigned to guard me, an unworthy sinner, this night. I ask you through your mercy that you direct me this day and at all times in your praise, guard me pure from sin, and sweetly kindle me in your most holy love.
