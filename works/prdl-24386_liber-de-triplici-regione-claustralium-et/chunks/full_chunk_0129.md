*So that I may not be more prolix than is fitting, I here set an end to the Compendium. For that venerable company of abbots of our annual Chapter instructed me to avoid prolixity in this place, lest lukewarm and worldly cloisterers make weariness their excuse. If the Lord Jesus gives me a life free from cares, which I have always desired, I shall try to satisfy both my own intention and another’s request. For, at the request of certain virgins dedicated to Christ, I have resolved to hand down a rule by which every devout cenobite may exercise himself, in the Lord’s passion, against all temptations. If, in this, they remain for a few months exercising themselves as they ought, he will by degrees sweetly fly from the humanity of Christ to his divinity through the ray of love. For to those who desire to come to the love of God, continual meditation on the Lord’s passion opens an easy entrance, because, while the mind through compassion is pierced by the memory of the benefits of so great a lover, burning with tears and desire it is lifted up to love. You, therefore, cloisterers dedicated to God, who have no leisure to attend to many things, receive these few things: in them, although there may not be a continual fire of love, yet for those who persevere it will be able to be renewed.*

*Praise to God and to his mother for ever and ever. Amen.*

*Here ends this Compendium, in which the business of the whole spiritual exercise is contained briefly and selectively. By Johannes Trithemius, Abbot. In the year from the reconciled divinity 1516. From the workshop of Melchior Lotter.*
