He suffered also in his possessions, because, naked and stripped, he was hung upon the wood, and his garments are divided by lot among the soldiers. He suffers in his soul, because, forefeeling the bitterness of the passion, he was sorrowful even unto death. He suffered in his whole body, since from the sole of the foot to the crown of the head there was no soundness in him.

He suffers in his friends, since he is betrayed by a disciple, deserted by all, and even publicly denied by Peter. He is afflicted also in the compassion of his mother, whose most holy soul the sword of sorrow is recorded to have pierced. He suffered from the ingratitude of many, because he foreknew that the death which he bore in suffering for all would be lost in so many of the reprobate. Finally, he suffered in all his senses, as we are taught from the Gospel. He suffers in his eyes, because after many tears had been shed they are covered with a cloth. He suffers in his hearing, when amid many insults and outrages he is mocked and blasphemed. He suffers in his smell, since he is defiled by the foul spittle of the Jews. He suffers in touch, because, thirsting, he is given gall and vinegar to drink. Sixth, because of the cause of the passion, which was solely the envy of the Jews, although he was plainly without any fault. For truly, whoever suffers unjustly is tormented all the more in his spirit. But the innocent Son of God dies for our sins; he is tormented for our crimes, as is read in Isaiah: Truly he himself bore our infirmities. Seventh, because of the lack of all consolation, since Christ found no one consoling him in his passion. For he says in the Psalm: You have put friend and neighbor far from me. Indeed, his acquaintances stood far off.

Fourth, how fruitful the passion of Christ was; this is chiefly to be considered in three things. First, the remission of sins was accomplished; second, the bestowal of grace; third, the display of glory. And these three things are indicated by the title fixed to the cross, which, as the evangelist says, contained the cause of Jesus’ passion: Jesus of Nazareth, king of the Jews. Jesus, saving sinners, with respect to the first; Nazarene, bestowing the flower of grace, with respect to the second; king of the Jews, showing the heavenly kingdom to those who confess him, with respect to the third.

Moreover, in this second root we have arranged meditations on the passion of the Lord for the several hours of the seven days of the week. We have therefore placed them after the end of the third region, lest at this point, by their excessive length, we disturb the order of the things to be said. For what we have now set forth we see to pertain rather to reading than to meditation, and therefore we postpone them.
