But if you are accused unjustly, remember that you are a monk, and you will find that you are bound to suffer wrongs. For when you are justly accused, know that you are to be cleansed through humble patience; but when unjustly, you show yourself to be a monk and earn still more. Therefore, when you are accused, call to mind the fourth and sixth degrees of humility; and if you are a true monk, pray even for those who accuse you. For the true lover of God loves still more the one who accuses him, and on that same day [offers?] him either some prayer or some special service. If you are good and wise, accusation cannot displease you; but if you are bad and foolish, you are wholly worthy of being accused. For Scripture says concerning the wise man: Rebuke a wise man, and he will love you. But concerning the bad and foolish man the prophet says: They hated him who rebuked in the gate, and abhorred the one who spoke perfectly.

*The Second Active Chapter, in which one person accuses another.*

We call the second chapter active, in which we no longer accuse ourselves, but another. For the act of this accusation, charity is supremely necessary; compassion too must be applied no less than discretion. Therefore, when you wish to accuse your brother by reason of charity, you should apply the fear of God, and, mindful of the fellowship of eternal life, say at the beginning: Our beloved brother has committed this or that; I ask that he be willing to amend himself for the Lord’s sake. Whatever must be said, let him set it forth in few words, with compassion; let him state the fact simply; let him not increase the fault of the accused by making it heavier; but let him silently leave to the president the judgment whether what was done is good or bad. For one who alone among many aggravates a brother’s fault by his own judgment very often carries away the suspicion of a zeal of bitterness and displeasure. Therefore, he who strives to accuse another should take care that he is not moved against his brother by bitter zeal, because if charity does not intervene in those who accuse, resentment does not correct the accused. Never does the accusation of the accuser bear fruit in the offender unless the love of God and brotherly charity commend it. For indeed, since almighty God is supremely good, he will not grant the purpose of compunction in your brother’s heart to your accusation when he finds that you have been moved to accuse not from charity but from displeasure. For where he finds the root of his charity, he will not deny good fruit. Therefore, if you feel yourself moved by any indignation against your brother, it is better to omit the accusation for a time than to wound yourself so cruelly by another’s wound.
