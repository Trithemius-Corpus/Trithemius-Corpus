But if it has fallen into sensual passions, then it descends again to the lowest region. Yet the soul of the true lover of God, although because of the frailty of the flesh it cannot always abide in the pleasantness of contemplation, nevertheless strives more often, through a fiery affection, to lift itself up to it, so that it may so fulfill the exercise of reason as not wholly to omit the sweetness of inward delight. O how good it is to cleave to the Lord; how sweet it is to attend to God alone, so that the mind thirsts for the Lord Jesus above all things and longs to embrace him with exceedingly fiery desires. For while it abides in this region it is very near to the empyrean heaven, and therefore is inflamed toward it with the greatest desires of love. O how many groans and tears the mind burning with love is accustomed to pour forth; how ardently it sighs for heavenly things; for the nearer it is to the empyrean heaven while abiding, through the secret of contemplation, in this supreme region, the more ardent is known to be its affection for leaving the prison of the body. Hence also, with the holy Apostle, the mind, sick with love, says in its fiery sighs: “I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ.”

*That This Region of the Soul Is by Divine Instruction*

*Article III*

It is learned by divine instruction, in which the mind, fervent with divine love, is taught not by man but by the Holy Spirit. For Christ said to his lovers in the holy Gospel: “When that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth.” And again that saying of the prophet: “And they shall all be taught by God.” For the Holy Spirit inhabits the heart of one who loves Jesus, and illumines it by his invisible teaching.

In this region the true wisdom of the spirit, Mary’s best part, is chosen in the tranquillity of silence; mystical theology is taught in the secret of the divine darkness with ineffable sweetness; the mind, disposed by the fervor of most holy love, speaks to its beloved in secret, and by desire is continually lifted up into him. This wisdom, or mystical theology, can indeed be commended by a human being, but it cannot be taught except in divine instruction. For the work of the Holy Spirit is being done when the mind is illumined in truth through contemplation.

But we must consider that mystical theology is acquired through the exercise of purified affection; it is taught through the burning of love. Speculative theology, however, which we call scholastic, is learned through the exercise of the intellect, while the mind is bound over to continual study.
