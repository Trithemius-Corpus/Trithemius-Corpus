For just as monks who, according to the Rule, walk in the spirit are to be happily crowned without end in heaven with the holy angels, so, conversely, those who, contrary to the norm of monastic institution, persevere in the flesh in this lowest region even unto death, being worse than pagans, are to be tortured eternally in hell with the demons. For that blessed man truly said: “As I have never seen better men than those who have made progress in the cenobium, so I have not found worse men than those who have failed in the monastery.”

For the monastic profession requires the perfection of the spiritual life, and tends toward the summit of the virtues as toward its proper due end. But by however much anyone, placed in the monastery, has fallen away from this effort of making progress, by so much the farther does he withdraw from the purpose of a monk.

*In what manner the carnal monks, citizens of this lowest region, are disordered in many articles.*

Although the description of this lowest region applies generally to all carnal men who dwell with beasts in sins and crimes, nevertheless, so that we may attend to our own affairs, we intend to speak of lukewarm and carnal cloistered men, doing no injury at all to the good and devout. For always and in all things we wish the conduct of good cloistered men to remain untouched and unharmed whenever we discuss the evil pursuits of the wicked. Thus we rebuke evil cloistered men and depraved conduct; honoring the good, we praise them everywhere, as is fitting. And what else than evil can we say about the evil, in whom we find no good things to say? In a holy order they live disorderly lives; they despise the traditions of the elders; they do not admit words of salvation. Indeed, the disorder of the monks of the lowest region is so great that, in my judgment, it would be easier to convert a publican than to recall a disordered monk to repentance. We comprise this disorder of carnal monks in eight articles, although we are not unaware that they are disordered in many other things.

And first, indeed, the citizens of this lowest region, beastly monks, are disordered toward God; then toward their own prelate; third, the prelate toward his subjects; fourth, the members toward one another; again, fifth, they are disordered toward themselves; then toward the property of the monastery; seventh, toward contempt of the world; consequently, toward repentance for sin. These are the principal disorders of the citizens of the lowest region; these are the beastly dispositions of carnal monks, who dwell in the lowest things in mind and body, who do not hasten to make progress toward the summit. Therefore, to set out briefly, in order, these disordered conditions of the lowest region, and to show, as far as we can, how far carnal monks are from God. For, as the Prophet says, salvation is far from sinners.
