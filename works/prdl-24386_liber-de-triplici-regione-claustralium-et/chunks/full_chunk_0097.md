For thus the Son prays to the Father in the holy Gospel: “Father, I will that where I am, there also may my minister be.” For in this venerable sacrament the condescension of divine loving-kindness is great, because, as the same Hugh says, God seems to do nothing less in this, that daily he deigns to descend from heaven upon the altar for our salvation, than when, descending from heaven, he became man and assumed human nature. And just as our Lord Jesus Christ bestowed this venerable sacrament upon us in memory of his most bitter Passion, as a sign of the greatest love, so he loves more fully and singularly those who prepare themselves with humble devotion and love to receive it or to minister to those receiving it; and, when they ask through it in faith, he comes to their aid in every necessity.

For Jesus, presiding in heaven, will not be able to be hard toward us when we pray with full faith, he who was so merciful and kind to those asking mercy while he walked on earth. Who ever came to his feet and was rejected? What sick person asked to be healed whom he did not cure while he walked among men? Therefore the Lord Jesus will never be able to deny mercy to us when we ask in faith, he who, still mortal, was so merciful to those praying to him. For he says: “All things are possible to one who believes.”

Therefore he who is about to minister at Mass should before all things strive to cleanse himself from every evil temptation and disordered affection, and, so far as he can, should bring himself back to himself through desire for the love of Jesus, so that, purified, he may be able to attend upon the Lord and pray effectively both for himself and for the whole Church, the living and the dead. Standing therefore during Mass behind the priest, at a suitable time, let him pray in these or similar words.

*Prayer of the Minister*

O sweetest and most beloved Lord Jesus, I beseech thee by all the anguish of thy most holy heart which thou didst endure in thy Passion; I beseech thee by the outpouring of thy most holy blood and by the power of thy most innocent death; I beseech thee by the most holy mystery of thy body and blood, at whose immolation to thee I, an unprofitable minister, am present: have mercy on me and purify my heart from every unlawful affection, so that I may be able to minister worthily to thee. Give me true contrition and forgiveness of all my sins, the grace of holy compunction, continual memory of thy Passion, pure devotion of heart, a fountain of tears, purity of serene conscience, and perpetual fervor of thy most holy love. Give me, I beseech thee, most merciful lover of men, through the pain of thy wounds, a firm purpose in holy fervor, and also true contempt of all worldly things, continual desire for the heavenly homeland, and sure progress in holy religion.
