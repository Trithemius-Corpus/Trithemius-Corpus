Let us therefore always say from the heart with the prophet: “It is good for me to cleave to God, to place my hope in the Lord God.”

Third, almighty God gave us the craft of uprightness and the teachings of truth. For these we are bound to our creator in faithful execution and perpetual service without deceit. We are therefore commanded above all to do all things to the praise of God, and in everything that we do chiefly to raise our intention toward his honor. For only a good intention directed toward God distinguishes works; love alone makes them fruitful. Behold, the Lord bestowed upon us the craft of nature for praising him; he gave, besides, the teachings of salvation through the law; he requires from us praise and love above all things. Let us therefore exercise, to the honor of God, the craft bestowed upon us, and let the pure eye of intention be formed through the teaching of the law.

Fourth, the merciful Father bestowed upon us the world with its necessary elements; for these he requires from us due and sincere contemplation of himself. Whatever we discern in this world ought to lead us to the memory of God and continually stir us to love its creator above all things. Hence Bernard, [unclear] in sacred matters, says: “The whole and true lover of God, wherever he turns, has before him a familiar reminder of his creator, and uses things as a mirror, and in such thinking is raised up from the creature to the creator. For concerning each thing he says: If those things seem good, beautiful, and sweet which, having been created, change and pass away with time, what goodness, beauty, and sweetness has God, their author, who remains forever, who has reserved for himself things incomparably better than these?” By this consideration of things the devout man looks upon all human beings with the simple eye of the mind, as if upon the likeness of the image of God, and does not curiously attend in them to the body’s appearance, pleasing or displeasing. For in a human being he considers not what he sees outwardly, but the image of the creator, which he knows to be a sharer in eternal happiness.

Fifth, the Son of God offered himself in torments for us, and redeemed us, lost as we were, from the ruin of everlasting death by his blood. We are commanded to render to this love of our savior a willing imitation, if we desire to arrive at his kingdom. For the prince of the apostles says to the faithful: “Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow in his footsteps.” What shall I render to you, O good Jesus, for your benefits toward me, so great and so immeasurable? I shall suffer with you in heart, I shall suffer with you in deed; I shall imitate you in the affections of the mind according to my ability.
