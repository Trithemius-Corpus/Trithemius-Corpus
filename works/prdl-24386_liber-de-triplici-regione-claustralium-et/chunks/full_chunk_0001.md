*Book on the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons and on the Spiritual Exercise of Monks: no less useful than necessary for all religious.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, revising the little work.*

*Hermann Tulichus to the pious and religious brother, professor Christian [unclear].*

Ethereal Olympus yields to no one after death
who passes the swift days without toil.
Whatever in this life you say and do freely,
an account must be rendered before God as witness.
Not he who gives himself to Venus, to downy beds, and to devouring Ceres
enjoys the starry heaven after his death.
The man devoted to enticements is barred from the realm of the gods;
the stars indeed must be sought by a narrow way.
While, unharmed, we feed upon the living air of heaven,
the kingdoms of the paradisal soil must be earned.
After death we cast belated cries into the ether,
lamenting that we have gone down into the Stygian pools.
But because the strict Judge after our final fate
pays no heed to complaints, now, lover of piety,
the sentence after death must be considered with watchful mind:
that rewards await each person according to his own merit.
As soon as you ponder this with yourself in a thoughtful heart,
if you yourself are wise, you will set a limit to sin.

Do not sin; God sees.
