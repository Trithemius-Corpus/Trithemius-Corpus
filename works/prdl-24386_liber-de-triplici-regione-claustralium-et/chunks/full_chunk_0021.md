But if his vain presumption has been detected through the diligence of the confessor, he at once seeks another confessor. If the abbot begins to oppose him, even gently, the monk, lifted up, falls through disquiet of mind into a new degree of pride.

*The ninth degree of pride is described as murmuring or impatience*, which very often dominates carnal monks. For the man whose confession is feigned, touched by one slight insult or penance, at once rises up into open pride; the deceit of simulated humility is disclosed. Nor can a feigned mind hide itself any longer, but it is compelled to bring out in public whatever lies hidden within. From then on, the proud cenobite, having lost control of himself, murmurs at everything; he obstinately accuses the judgment of his pastor, however just it may be; he gnashes his teeth. Moved, he grows angry; without reason, like a madman, he is thrown into confusion. But the proud monk given to murmuring, having despised the fourth degree of humility, falls into the tenth descent of pride, through which he runs toward hell.

*The tenth degree of pride follows murmuring*, and is called rebellion. Proud and carnal monks fall into this, those who, still dwelling in the lowest region, devote themselves to murmurings. Hence Bernard: “Unless [unclear] looks upon the murmurer, so that at last, being converted, which is very difficult for such men, he silently acquiesces in the judgments of others, he soon, becoming brazen and shameless, will fall the more ruinously into this degree, the more desperately he fails to restrain the movements of his mind.” He always advances toward worse things; and he who before had arrogantly despised his brothers in secret now also openly shows, what is more serious, that he despises his master. But the chastisement of divine judgment does not allow this degree of pride to pass long unpunished, since monks rebellious against their pastors are frequently consumed quickly and by a miserable death. For some, withdrawing from the order, become apostates; others, because of grave crimes, foul prisons; some, expelled from monasteries, wander as vagrants and wretched men in exile. Many, struck horribly by the Lord, in despair [unclear] to the lower regions. For it is written: “He who resists authority contradicts the ordinance of God.” And again the Lord says concerning the prelates of the faithful: “He who despises you despises me.” What will you say to these things, rebellious monks? Will you cite laws to me, whose truth you corrupt? But I dare to say what I wish you were ashamed to hear. The presumption of law will not save you, rebellious monk, in the strict examination of God, you who now proudly despise the obedience of subjection. Law commands obedience; it requires humble subjection in the order.
