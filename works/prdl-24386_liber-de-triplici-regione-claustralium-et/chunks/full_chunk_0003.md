*Hermannus Tulichus to the religious brothers of the order of Saint Benedict: greeting in the Lord.*

Everyone knows with how great care the ancient Romans, and other peoples alien to Christian piety, acted not only to preserve but also to enlarge their superstition, if he has merely handled the ancient monuments of that nation. Indeed, the slightest fault in religion, either insufficiently diligently observed or neglected through carelessness, they punished with great severity. Since this is so, with how great effort must you, professors of the Christian faith and religion, strive, not only to observe the worship of the true God, but also, according to your strength, equally to confirm and increase it?

This is what the noble and most zealous defender of the Christian name and religion, Heinrich von Schleinitz, abbot in Chemnitz, and the most watchful propagator and champion of divine worship, Johannes Schreiger, abbot in Marienzell, carefully considering, procure all things which awaken and enlarge love for Jesus Christ, the vindicator of the human race from diabolical servitude. Of this matter, among many other things, this above all is evidence: that they are the chief authors in ensuring that, with the present little book copied in a thousand examples, the opportunity of reading it should be available to all those who, according to their profession, wish to exercise themselves in Christian piety.

Therefore all who wish both to be called Christians and to be regarded as such, and especially the brothers who have entered the religion of Saint Benedict, must strive that you not merely read through this little book often, but constantly exercise yourselves in those things which are most holily prescribed in it. This is certainly worth the labor, provided that after death you are to go happily into the kingdom of heaven, which, as it is in the sacred writings, suffers violence and the violent seize it. Let us strive earnestly and with all, as they say, our strength, that through the grace of God, best and greatest, we may all be led after death from this valley of tears to that place. Farewell. Given at Leipzig, in the house of Melchior Lotter, in the year from the reconciled divinity 1516.

*Here begins the book on the threefold region of cloistered persons, and on the spiritual exercise of novices.*

When I recall to mind the state of monastic life in this time, I grieve deeply over its calamity, because the fervor of spiritual exercise which once produced very abundant fruits in the order of our holy father Benedict has now, for the most part, departed from monks. For we see cloistered persons returning in their morals to the world; we see the institution of regular observance despised by many; and religion, which at the beginning had been instituted for contempt of the world, is again recalled to the pomp of the world through wicked monks.

The crown of our head has fallen; the integrity of regular discipline has been corrupted, and the former fervor of conduct has perished. We see very many monasteries of our order lying buried in the worst deformity; very few do we see reformed to the purity of the Rule. But the cause of this fall of regular institution is negligence of spiritual exercises, because as soon as the spirit of fervor in the minds of cloistered persons lost its savor, the flesh, previously dead through denial of itself, revived, and the world, which before had appeared dry and hopeless to devout hearts, began again to flourish in carnal minds.

For truly the thought of the human heart, inclined to evil from youth, unless it has been daily stirred to fervor of spirit by the bridle of spiritual exercise, soon falling back into its former negligences, grows lukewarm from its holy purpose. For just as newly broken land, deprived of the diligent care of the cultivator, with thorns growing up, quickly returns into the solitude of its former state, so the human mind, however pure or holy it may be, is quickly reduced to the brambles of vices unless it is renewed to fervor of spirit by the daily exercise of devotion.

Led by this consideration, the fathers of our Bursfeld observance, devout cultivators, entrusted to us and to posterity by word and example that we, who for our frailty cannot remain constant in fervor of spirit, should at least by evening recollection after compline, accusing our daily negligences, renew the state of our mind to the fervor of our holy purpose. But whoever has neglected the daily exercise of this institution falls into the depth of torpor, just as the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “If you do not hold yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown”; by this is understood good confidence.
