Finally, let us review the whole history of the Old and New Testament, and we shall find that what was known to have been rashly committed against the justice of God was often severely punished. The gravity of the crime is therefore easily recognized if the strictness of the vengeance that followed is considered.

Third, consider how grave a scandal arises from sin, especially when one sins before others. For he who by his own evil example provokes someone to sin shows himself to be an adversary of God. For God, of his mercy, resolved by his own will to save souls redeemed by the blood of his only-begotten Son; this man, through evil example, desires to destroy them through aversion from God. The sin of scandal, with respect to guilt, is for this reason called very grave, because, as we have said, it is opposed to God. But if one who gives occasion for harm is to be thought to have done the harm, the sinner subjects himself to condemnation by as many sentences of death as the people whom by his own evil example he made fall into mortal crime. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: Woe to the man through whom scandal comes. And again he says: Whoever scandalizes one of these little ones believing in me, it is expedient for him that a millstone turned by a donkey be hung around his neck and that he be sunk in the depth of the sea. True repentance is rare in one who scandalizes little ones, because, when he neglects to restore another’s loss, he by no means attains the fruit of saving penitence.

Fourth, consider the negligence of your ungrateful heart: since you, created in the image of God, ought to have loved your maker above all things, yet by your sins you dishonor him in many ways. You ought to devote continual service of love to Christ the Son of God, by whose blood you have been redeemed; but you do not fear to love the flesh more than Jesus your savior. Finally, with a compassionate mind you ought daily to turn over in your mind his most holy Passion; and you are accustomed to think of nothing less than that. Adopted among the sons of God by mercy alone, you ought by imitation to show yourself worthy of the heavenly calling; and still you do not dread to lie in the lowest things through sloth. And why do I say many things? Behold, you who are marked with the Holy Spirit are reckoned with the angels; you are called to the inheritance of the sons of God; you are invited by all the saints to the feasts of paradise; you daily partake of the body and blood of our savior Jesus Christ; and nevertheless you do not observe the commandments of God and of the Rule which you promised, that you might be blessed. Examine your conscience diligently, therefore, and you will find that you ought to do many good things which you omit, and to omit many more evil things which you commit against God’s good pleasure.
