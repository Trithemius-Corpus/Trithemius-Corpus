Who would believe that the thief crucified with the Lord was chosen from eternity, if he judged him according to his works? Divine election anticipates what is good; for where sin is seen to have abounded, often, by the will of divine election, greater grace is found. For divine love regards and embraces its chosen ones from eternity, and beholds them as chosen not according to the misery in which they now are, but according to their future glory. Therefore let the cenobite beware of disparaging others; let him beware of judging others, if he desires to escape eternal death. For who are you, to judge another’s servant? He stands to be judged by his own master alone.

But the disposition of this seventh degree, because of that clause “from the inmost affection of the heart,” possesses a threefold root of its origin: namely truth, hope, and charity. For indeed, when the faithful cenobite sees that he does not have his being from himself, he truly considers himself as nothing from himself; and consequently, in the same respect, he regards his works as nothing from himself, since he himself is nothing, for he knows that nothing comes to be from nothing. Therefore the monk rejoices with the truth, because in this respect he and his works are accounted as nothing, since he fears to incur the vice of falsehood if he believed himself to be something great. And therefore, withdrawing from himself, he fails, and places his being, life, and power in God alone. Secondly, this disposition arises from hope of receiving and seeking greater grace, because when a man through humility fails from himself, he approaches nearer to the grace of God. Thirdly, this disposition of humility takes its beginning from the love of God and receives continual increase in it. For whoever perfectly loves God seeks nothing in all things except his honor, nor knows how to exalt himself in anything, but desires for God’s sake to be subject to all. The more he is cast down through reproaches and injuries, the more, rejoicing, he is glad in the Lord. He rejoices that he is miserable, in order that he may proclaim the Lord glorious in himself. He who truly loves his Lord seeks nothing other than his glory.

It is clear from what we have said that the perfect charity promised to those ascending through the degrees of humility is displayed in this seventh degree. For the other five degrees that follow in the Rule add nothing to the inward perfection of the man who is in the seventh degree, but rather order the outward man, lest he dissipate the inward. Finally, from the seventh degree we could erect certain new ascents of humility, and in them raise all the other degrees higher. For a more fervent filial fear resides in him who can more fully perfect the breadth of the others.

Now that the ascents of the heart up to the degree of charity have been known, let the cenobite consider with what zeal, what effort, what perseverance he ought to exercise himself in these things, so that he may reach this seventh degree of love.
