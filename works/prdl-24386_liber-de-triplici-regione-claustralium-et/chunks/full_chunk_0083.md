*By what modes or degrees one must ascend to this supreme summit of this region. Article 8.*

Although in the preceding parts of this little work I have briefly set forth the manner of ascending to this supreme region of the spirit, nevertheless, as I think, it will not be useless often to repeat things well said, so that we may be able to retain them more firmly. For it seems to me of little profit to describe the beauty of contemplation unless we also know how to show those who desire it the way of coming to it. Therefore, let the one who wishes to ascend by desire of love to the pleasantness of this supreme region take care to exercise himself diligently in the labors of the middle region; for unless in that region he has been purged from sensual passions by the practice of holy compunction, he does not approach this one without danger. For the consummation of this exercise let him often recall to memory the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ; let him weep from delight in divine love; and let him learn frequently to run through the text of that same passion with attention as mediator, and to stir himself to compassion for the Savior. And because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, so that the mind may be able to raise itself vigorously in this meditation on the Lord’s passion, let him take care, if need be, to present to his thoughts images that are truly sensible. Therefore, withdrawing from crowds into silence, let him begin to turn over in memory the order of the passion; let him see himself with the eyes of the mind as present with the suffering Lord at each event; let him hear Jesus speaking in imagination; let him go with him as he goes through all the places of the passion.

Then at each article let him bring his affections out of the desire of compassion; let him look with faithful eyes upon Jesus suffering, set before him; weeping because of the anguish of his heart, let him grow accustomed in spirit to address him as though bodily present, and through the desire of love to embrace him wounded at the pillar and on the cross. For the mind will quickly be able to be kindled into the love of the Lord Jesus if it truly grows accustomed, through the tears of meditation, to suffer with him. But I remember that elsewhere I have written more fully on the most effective exercise of the Lord’s passion; therefore I have passed over it briefly here. For it is enough to have given the beginnings of meditating on it to those who do not know, since those who know have higher things.
