But he is called obstinate if he has not given opportunely what ought to be given, or stubbornly withholds necessities from his subjects. He is also to be called obstinate who casts aside the fear of the Lord, who trusts too much in his own judgment, who in any matters to be done admits no counsels of the wise. And indeed among pastors of souls there are very many disordered by this sickness of mind, who either spend the property of the monastery too improvidently or preserve it with excessive tenacity. They prepare very sumptuous banquets for their flatterers, but to others, from whom they are averse, they deny even the smallest things.

In the guest-house, or in their own abbey, pastors depraved by disordered conduct sometimes eat to the point of drunken excess; they always drink the best wine. But to poor monks, who by watching and singing bear the burden of the day and the heat, they grant necessary things scarcely and meagerly.

Sixth, a prelate of monks is shown to be very disordered if, bold against God, he is lifted up into pride by making light of his judgments. For since a pastor cannot be corrected by his subjects, he is referred by the holy father Benedict to the judgment of God, as is found in many places of the Rule. “Let the abbot know,” he says, “that for all his judgments he must render account to God.” And again: “Let the ordained abbot always consider what sort of burden he has taken up, and to whom he is to render account of his stewardship.” And again he says: “As soon as faults begin to arise, let him cut them off with charity, not with tyranny.” And in another place he warns, saying: “Let the abbot himself, however, do all things with the fear of God and observance of the holy Rule, knowing beyond doubt that he will render account for all his judgments to God, the most just judge.”

But the disordered ruler of monks, like an asp with stopped ears, despises hearing this threat of the most holy lawgiver; he scorns the patient long-suffering of God; he thinks nothing of the examination to come, until at last the most holy lawgiver, angered, says to him: “Restore to me without delay the souls once committed to you in the body. Return their number whole; for if even one of them has perished through your negligence, your soul shall not see my face forever.” What then will you answer to the highest pastor, useless ruler, when not only the blood of those perishing is required from you, but also an account of those making too little progress is demanded? Behold, now you walk in the worst security, despising the examination of God; you think that all things that please you in the body are lawful for you, and you do not think to whom you are one day to render account for all things. Soon the Lord of all will come, before whom you do not fear to show yourself an unfaithful servant, and whose honor you do not fear to arrogate to yourself. Unless meanwhile you do penance, unless you amend your life for the better, you will burn without end with the demons in hell.
