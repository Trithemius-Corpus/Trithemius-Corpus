*To the Son.*

My body and my soul I commend to you, Lord Jesus Christ, this night and at all times, in the power and union of that love by which, hanging on the cross, you commended your beloved mother, the Virgin Mary, to the disciple. In that love, I beseech you, guard me from all snares of the ancient serpent, suppress the illicit motions of the flesh, drive far away foul and evil dreams, and repel the phantasms of the nights, so that in your peace I may sleep securely and, at the hours appointed for me, rise gladly to your praises. Amen.

*To the Father.*

Lord, holy Father, almighty eternal God, who graciously received us into your grace in the passion of your only-begotten Son: receive mercifully, I beseech you, all the good works that, by your gift, I have done on this day, in words, deeds, or thoughts, in the power and union of that charity and love by which you received the same your Son hanging and dying on the cross for us. To you, holy Father, in the faith and union of that charity, I commend myself this night and at all times, by which your Son, our Lord, now about to die, commended his spirit to you on the cross.

*To Saint Mary.*

Holy and immaculate Mother of God, Virgin Mary, I beg you, by the bitter passion and death of your sweetest Son: do not despise me, a wretched sinner, because of my unclean conscience, but remember that you are the mother of mercy, and pray for me to the God whom you bore, that he may mercifully forgive me my sins, make me his fervent lover, guard me this night from all snares and evils, and after the death of the flesh lead my soul to eternal life. To your protection, most blessed Virgin Mary, I commit myself this night and at all times, in the power and union of that committal by which your beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, now about to die on the cross, commended you to John his disciple.

*On the Memory of the Lord’s Passion.*

Since the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is the gate of our salvation, through which the kingdom of heaven is opened to us, we ought, with whatever devotion we can, to exercise ourselves in it more frequently, because we shall easily obtain the effect of our prayers if, through the love of compassion, in the power of his wounds, we adore the Lord. Therefore, faithful cenobite, with what diligence I can I warn, exhort, and ask you that, mindful of the benefits of God, you wholly neglect no day on which you do not give thanks to your Savior for the bitterness of his passion and death. And if, for any reason, you are unable to pray at greater length, at least do not refuse to show yourself grateful to your Redeemer with one word. Thus the Lord Jesus himself revealed to his lovers, to whom he wished, that nothing is more pleasing to him, and nothing more useful to us, than to recall with thanksgiving the memory of his passion and death, and to admonish others to do the same. Receive, therefore, an abbreviated word, lest a longer prayer make you weary.

With knees bent to the ground and hands joined together, say:

I give thanks to you, sweetest Lord Jesus, for all the wounds, pains, and injuries of your most innocent passion and death. I beg you, by that same death of yours, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death. Or thus:

For your most bitter passion and most innocent death, Lord Jesus Christ, I give thanks with a compassionate soul. I beg you, have mercy on me. Or thus:

I praise, bless, and adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, King of heaven and earth, and I give thanks to you for your holy passion, by which you delivered wretched me from perpetual death. I beg you, by the power of that same passion of yours: make me eternally a participant in your most innocent death.

*Conclusion of this Compendium by Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim.*
