For truly the devout mind of a man, through communion of this most sacred sacrament, is kindled by its effective operation into the fervor of divine love; and, having utterly denied itself, it is transformed into Christ, united to him by grace. Therefore, the more each person, having denied himself through true resignation, offers himself to the Lord as a living victim in remembrance of the Lord’s passion, the more disposed he will be to celebrate worthily.

*Returning to the cell after private masses*

When the celebration of masses is finished and the thanksgivings have been completed according to custom, let the monk return to his cell; and, after first saying the customary prayer, as above at matins, let him recollect himself for a little while and grow more fully in the holy purpose of perseverance. Then, turning to the Lord, let him say:

¶ *Prayer.*

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, by the power of your most holy body and blood, which I have received, though unworthy: grant me the sweetness of your love, sincerity of brotherly affection, strength in temptation, purity in heart, and discipline in conduct, so that, by your bounty, I may satisfy my profession and continually advance in your most holy love. Then, until the first signal for choir, let him read something good, meditate, or pray; or certainly let him write, and fervently apply himself to the work enjoined on him by obedience.

*At the first signal for choir before [unclear]*

When the first signal has sounded for the hour that is customarily put before high mass, the monk of the community, immediately putting aside everything he has in his hands, should read the preparatory prayers for choir, just as they were set out in order above before matins. Afterward let him go to choir with reverence and maturity, bowing and praying according to the rite set forth above.

*At high mass. Consideration 4.*

At the ringing for high mass you will be able to read the due hour concerning the Holy Cross, and afterward, as much as time allows, to recall to memory something from the prologue placed before the private masses. For the sum of our salvation is placed in the merit of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ; nor is any good work believed meritorious for us unless it is offered in its power. Now the remembrance of this passion is performed in the celebration of masses, where the innocent Lamb, slain for us, is offered to God the Father in an ineffable mystery. Therefore from our only Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, whom we believe to have suffered for us, we truly hope for salvation.
