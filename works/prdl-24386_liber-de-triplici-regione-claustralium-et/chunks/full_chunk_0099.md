The priest who is about to approach to offer this most holy sacrament of the Lord’s body and blood should first examine himself diligently, and should most carefully investigate of which region he is a citizen. If he still finds himself a dweller at the boundary of the region, he must not approach the altar unless he first strives to cleanse himself through contrition and confession, and a firm purpose of amending his life. But if he finds himself a citizen of the second region, if he has found his powers reformed and ordered toward the cenobitic end, he can readily dispose himself to celebrate, since by emptying himself daily of his own affection he has learned more often to lift himself upward through desire. For divine goodness quickly enters the affection of a man who is stained by no desire of his own.

Therefore, wishing to perform this most holy priestly office with devotion and the fervor of divine love, let him first say some ardent prayers, by which he may be able to stir himself to fuller compunction. For devotion is rarely granted at the altar if the mind has not beforehand prepared itself for fervor. For this reason our fathers, desiring each one of us to advance in his own free devotion, did not wish to occupy us, before the approach to the altar, with many psalms after the custom of certain persons; not because anyone ought on that account to approach without some recollection, but so that each might more freely devote himself, in secret, to exercises suited to his devotion, or to prayers.

Therefore, wishing to recommend the elevation of the mind in the Lord Jesus to those celebrating before the approach to the altar, we have arranged the following prayer from that which is attributed to Saint Ambrose of Milan, which we believe to be sufficiently effective by the merit of the Lord’s passion. Accordingly, the devout cenobite who is about to approach the altar, if he has leisure and is freely able, may first say this or a similar prayer, or may prepare himself in whatever ways he wishes.

*Prayer Before the Approach to the Altar*

Highest priest and true pontiff, Jesus Christ, who offered yourself to God the Father as a pure, holy, and immaculate victim on the altar of the cross for us miserable sinners; and who gave us your flesh to eat and your blood to drink, and established that mystery in the power of the Holy Spirit, saying, “As often as you do these things, you shall do them in memory of me.”

I beseech you through the pain of your wounds and through the shedding of your most precious blood, and the power of your most innocent death, and through your very body and blood, the great price of our salvation; I beseech you through this wondrous and inexpressible charity with which you so loved us, miserable and unworthy, that you washed us from our sins in your blood: have mercy on me and forgive me all my sins, and teach me, your unworthy servant, whom among your other gifts you have deigned to call to the priestly office, not by any merits of mine but by the sole graciousness of your mercy, to handle this mystery through your Holy Spirit with that reverence and honor, devotion and fear, with which it ought and is fitting to be handled.
