Third in the recollection of a good cenobite is the devout asking of suffrages, since he understands his own weakness very well and seeks help from those stronger. Therefore, as soon as he has completed his recollection, he turns to the holy and undivided Trinity to seek mercy; then he invokes the most blessed Mother of God; afterward he implores the prayers and assistance of all the saints. And here he is mindful not only of his own, but pours out prayers for the whole order and the Church.

*Blessing for Resting*

Fourth in the recollection is the cenobite's blessing for resting, by which he arms himself with prayers and entrusts himself to the divine goodness to be guarded through the night. Therefore, at the sound of the dormitory signal, or at the appointed time, as he goes to the place of rest, let him fortify himself with the sign of the holy cross and say with devotion:

The peace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the merit and integrity of the blessed Virgin, the sign of the cross over each one, the power of the Lord's Passion, the blessing, guardianship, and merit of all the holy angels and of God's elect, be between me and all enemies, visible and invisible, here and forever. Amen.

*Prayer*

I commend myself this night and at all times into your wounds, most loving Lord Jesus, and I ask you, through the merit [unclear], have mercy on me and guard me from all the snares and illusions of the enemy, so that with pure mind and clean body I may be able to rise at the hours appointed for me and praise you with fervor of spirit.

*Discretion for Obtaining Rest*

The fifth and final thing to be commemorated in recollection is [unclear] for rest: just as penance should never be omitted [unclear], so also one should not wish to prolong it too indiscreetly to the destruction of one's own head. Therefore, when all things have been completed with discretion, let desire and fervor toward God raise the mind with tears, if it can; let it not confound the mind. But as you ascend your bed, carry something in memory concerning the Lord's Passion, or concerning the love of Jesus, or certainly concerning the glory of the heavenly kingdom, according as [unclear], and let something help you as you are carried into sleep, which may also receive you when you wake and restore you to the state of yesterday's [interruption?]. Thus the night will be illuminated for you like the day, and the night will be your illumination in its delights, so that you may fall asleep peacefully, rest in peace, and wake easily; and rising, you will be ready to return to that from which you had not wholly departed. Yet in all these things discretion must always be applied, lest either excessive meditation, hindering sleep, destroy the brain, or immoderate sleep, [unclear].
