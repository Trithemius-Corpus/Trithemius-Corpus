*Letter of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, on the little work concerning the threefold region of cloistered persons.*

To the reverend fathers in Christ, the lord presidents, definitors, and the other abbots of the annual chapter of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the Bursfelde Union and Observance: Brother Johannes Trithemius, called abbot in Sponheim, of the same order and congregation, of the diocese of Mainz, sends reverence and charity.

Most honored fathers, I have complied with your commands, and, as you instructed me, I undertook to correct the little work on the threefold region of cloistered persons, together with the spiritual exercise of monks, recently issued by the venerable father, lord Johannes, abbot of Bursfelde. And indeed, unless your authority had compelled me, I would never have dared to lay my hand upon the distinguished work of so great a father, who, it is agreed, being far more learned and holier than I, wrote most excellently. For what would I correct, where everything is found most approved? A good and devout man wrote no otherwise than he lived and than was fitting.

Nevertheless, in order to satisfy both your command and the prayers of the author himself, I rendered the whole little work in briefer, though not fewer, sentences; and, attending to [unclear], I brought it, as far as I could, into my own manner and style of writing. But I kept the order and matter of the earlier volume; I completely changed the form and habit of expression. I added many things that seemed useful to me in almost every place; I removed a few; in individual passages I altered the words, not the sense. For both your command gave me warrant to dare this, and the insistent request of the aforesaid father persuaded me.

For you wished that I should make a new work out of old material, should add whatever seemed suitable to me, should cut away superfluities, should alter [everything?] according to the powers of my talent, which are slight; this I completed by rewriting. Receive now, therefore, most beloved fathers, a work not mine but yours, not composed by me, but rather rewritten. If the labor of the aforesaid father pleases you, praise his talent; but if it displeases because of its meanness, correct my imperfection, since I, who had excellent material for spiritual building from another’s expenditure, have through my inexperience fashioned an unsuitable structure. If I have become a fool, you compelled me.
