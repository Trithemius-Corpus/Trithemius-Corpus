Although between the first region of evils and the second of goods there is, properly speaking, no middle place, nevertheless there are some who often move between the two: at one time, through weakness, they are lukewarm; at another, from contrition of mind, they seem to be devout.

These are weak human beings: although they are not submerged in the lowest region, yet through negligence they are very near it, and indeed frequently fall into it through bad habit, since they do not withdraw, as far as they can, from the affections of the flesh. Concerning these Bernard says in a certain sermon: “Lukewarmness is ruinous, because it is dangerous, because it is most troublesome and full of misery and grief; and, being plainly very near to hell, it is rightly reckoned the shadow of death.”

Indeed one may find faint-hearted and slack persons failing under the burden, needing rod and spurs: their joy is slack, their sadness faint-hearted; their compunction is brief and rare. Their thought is animal, their way of life lukewarm; their obedience is without devotion, their speech without circumspection, their prayer without attention of heart, their reading without edification. These, finally, as we see, are scarcely restrained by fear of Gehenna; shame holds them back; reason scarcely bridles them, discipline scarcely checks them. If persons of this sort remain long in this way under the burden, they are either crushed and sink down, or are in some manner in hell, so that they never breathe fully into the light of the Lord’s mercies, nor into the liberty of the spirit, which alone makes the yoke sweet and the burden light.

From this, moreover, such ruinous lukewarmness flows: because their will has not yet been purified, nor do they will the good in the way they know it, being drawn away and enticed by their own concupiscence. For they love in the flesh their earthly little consolations, whether in word, or in sign, or in deed, or in some other thing; and although they sometimes interrupt these things, nevertheless they do not break them off completely. Hence it is that they rarely direct their affections toward God, and their compunction is not continual, but only for an hour. A soul that is subject to these distractions cannot be filled with the visitations of the Lord; and the more it is emptied by the former, the more fully it will be filled by the latter. If much, much; if little, little. Or perhaps, if you approve it more, those former things will never be able to be mingled with these latter for all eternity. For spirit and flesh, fire and lukewarmness, do not dwell in one abode, especially since lukewarmness itself is wont to provoke the Lord to vomit.

Therefore whoever desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the region of the spirit, should take care always to drive torpor of mind away from himself, and, once placed in holiness, to burn continually through the fire of divine love. Nothing is so [unclear] to those who wish to serve as lukewarmness of mind, which always arises at the beginning from a certain small negligence, but, unless it is immediately treated, increases successively in a wonderful way. One established in the neighborhood of the lowest region often slips back into the same, because the dregs of his former fleshly way of life still subtly occupy his will and disturb the weak fervor of devotion on an easy occasion.
