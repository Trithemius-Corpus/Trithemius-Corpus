Therefore, since the love of carnal persons is carnal, it is easily changeable on occasion. For the citizens of this lowest region venerate similar ways, and monks always embrace monks like themselves with affection. Him whom God has loved from eternity the disordered cenobite persecutes; him whom God spurns because of his demerits, this man venerates as a saint.

Secondly, cloistered men are disordered toward one another by reason of dissimilarity, when either the seniors, stirred up by austerity and pride, despise their juniors, or the juniors obstinately set themselves against their seniors. And this disorder is proved to be very contrary to the holy Rule, which commands seniors to love juniors with paternal affection, and enjoins juniors humbly to obey their seniors for God's sake. But when the seniors, citizens of this lowest region, withdraw paternal affection from the juniors, the lesser men blaze up into grave hatred; and when the young consider themselves unjustly despised by them, they daily accuse their depraved ways. Therefore weak young men learn the wrong ways of their seniors, and, as the proverb says, just as they see the greater men sing, so too the lesser men dance.

Thirdly, monks who cast away good zeal and mutual obedience, and take up the kind of zeal that is evil and forbidden by the Rule, are badly ordered toward one another. By words and deeds they commit various injuries against one another. One strives, out of eagerness to rival, to watch another for evil; one is provoked by another to inflict insult. They mutually hold their weaknesses ready against each other; they are always panting for vengeance. Zeal for regular discipline is despised; the passion of indignation and bitterness rules. They meet one another with faces turned away; by fitting signs they make manifest how much hatred lies hidden in the breast. Not so the impious, not so: for whoever pursues his brother with hatred is proved a murderer and unworthy of the kingdom of God. O wretched condition, always, of carnal monks, which is the nearer to hell the more alien it has been from the fear of God. For whoever, through evil zeal, delights to be discordant in the school of peace imitates not angels but demons. For indeed, just as a brute animal does not abandon the savagery of its nature, so a bestial monk does not withdraw from the worst zeal.

*How monks, citizens of the lowest region, are disordered toward themselves*

Carnal monks, citizens of the lowest region, are disordered in nothing more than toward their own selves. For they cast away every consideration of themselves; to what end they were created they do not attend. *Article V.*
