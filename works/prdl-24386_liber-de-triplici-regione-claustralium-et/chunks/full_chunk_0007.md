*In what way the citizens of this lowest region are disordered toward God. Article I.*

Therefore the carnal monks of this lowest region, mad worshippers, are in many ways disordered toward God; they never feel the sweetness of his love; they admit no compunction of divine fear; in their affections they are beastly and swollen with pride; in their carnal pleasures they are at all times unclean. And first, indeed, their disorder toward God takes its origin from ignorance: for, being given over to animal passions, they do not care to have God in their knowledge, nor do they consent to ponder in their mind those things that lead to fear of him. But, blinded by evil desires, they become vain in their thoughts. But what does Scripture say? “He who is ignorant shall be ignored.” They do not wish to understand, that they may do well; they do not tremble before the face of the strict judge; without repentance they continually persevere in evils.

Indeed, like beasts of burden, they do not fear to rot away in their pleasures; they pay no heed to the patience of God calling them to repentance; they seek nothing other than the comforts of their flesh. At all times they walk in the greatest sins without any fear; they stubbornly refuse to know the ways of the Lord. Of the future judgment they think either nothing or little; they do not fear the punishments prepared in hell for sinners; the grace of compunction never pierces their hearts, whose mind the love of God does not inflame. They have no care for God; no dread of death. They obey the belly rather than God; they desire at all times to live according to the flesh; and while they shamefully neglect their own salvation, they descend to hell with the demons. Indeed, those who, forgetful of God, keep badly the vow of holy monastic conduct do not attain to the heavenly rewards of life to be bestowed upon the just.

Secondly, carnal monks are disordered toward God through foolish profession, because they promise to live according to the Rule, yet they do not truly care to fulfill this. And what is more disordered than to promise to the Lord God strictness of life, and to remain in the foulness of one’s former life? He is a mocker of almighty God, not a monk, whoever lives in a monastery otherwise than the monastic Rule teaches. O how many today do we see living secularly within the cloister; how many do we see neglecting the purpose of their profession; who violate the observance of regular discipline in various ways; who knowingly ignore the goodness of God; who refuse to do penance.
