And, lest we be too lengthy: whoever desires to exercise this reformation in himself should take up, in this region, works contrary to the eight articles of the first. For, as the tradition of physicians teaches and prescribes: opposites are cured by opposites.

Therefore the citizens of this middle region, faithful monks, are commanded first to consider this: that they should strive before all things to order themselves toward God. This ordering is said to be well made when God is loved with the whole heart, according to the law, above all things.

Next, the devout cenobite should show himself ordered toward his prelate in all things: he should show humility and render reverence; he should strive to love him with sincere charity, and to keep all the things that he commands according to the Rule.

The rector also of souls should take care to walk in ordered fashion toward the cenobites, and to show himself a shepherd to the sheep, not a hireling: he should not neglect the salvation of the souls committed to him; he should not put temporal things before spiritual things. Let him hate vices, love the brothers, and show himself exemplary in all good things.

Then the brothers should cleave to one another like members of one head; in the true charity of God they should love one another mutually and not depart from good zeal. For since we trust that, by the faith of Christ and by applying ourselves to good works, we shall come to eternal life, we are members of Christ, the supreme head. But whoever persecutes his brother with evil zeal or hatred and the other evil affections first divides himself from the head, and then does injury to Christ.

Consequently each person shows himself ordered toward himself: for the love of Christ he should humbly subject himself to all; by humility he should prefer others to himself, and believe himself more unworthy than all the monks.

In the use of the things of the monastery they should always beware of exceeding the institution of the Rule, but should strive to show themselves ordered in all things and faithful everywhere. They should desire to have nothing against the will of the Abbot; they should admit no pleasure of the mind in the use of things.

Next, faithful cenobites will take care to order themselves, according to the regular institution, to contempt of the world, so that they love nothing in this world, but despise all things sincerely for the love of God.

They should not neglect to order themselves most diligently to penance; they should often have recourse to humble confession of their transgressions, because just as no one is believed to live in a place in the world without sin, so penance is judged necessary for all, even for monks. For the good cenobite should always learn, through the study of compunction, to have recourse to penance, and humbly to accuse himself of his transgressions in chapter, gladly to perform the penance enjoined, and faithfully to amend himself in all things.
