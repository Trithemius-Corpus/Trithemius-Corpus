Therefore it is necessary for the cenobite, the cultivator of this middle region, to correct his inner powers from every disorder, if he wishes to remain continually in the pursuit of making progress. These intellectual powers, moreover, are to be amended in this way.

First, the intellect must be purged of every imagination of vain likenesses, so that, tearing itself away from all material things, it may raise itself upward, without any imagination of likeness, to its principle; and, prudently intent upon its object, may turn itself wholly toward the ray of supreme truth.

Then let our faithful cenobite not neglect to cleanse his memory from every occupation with illicit things; let him admit no forms of shameful or less useful thoughts; and let him strive to repel the phantasms of irrational passions through holy meditations, and, as far as can be done, to think nothing foolish.

The will also must be purged of every disordered affection, so that it may wish nothing except what right reason suggests is to be wished, and reject nothing except what that reason, according to the judgment of truth, forbids it.

But this restraint of the powers and cleansing of the faculties, as we have said, is granted to no one at all except through great, discreet, and continual diligence in strong combat, knowing that the kingdom of God suffers violence, and that no one can subject the enemy to himself unless he has wished to fight against him fiercely and perseveringly. For, blessed Bishop Augustine bearing witness, we have learned that a man does not die well unless he has first died to vices and desires. But no one truly dies to these among men unless he resists his own wills through continual labor, sowing in tears. But although the weapons of spiritual warfare are many in its progress, nevertheless three are chiefly to be assumed by the monk.

And first, indeed, vigor is necessary for the cenobite in all that he does, so that he may dare courageously to undertake things contrary to the flesh, and may never at any time grow lukewarm in his holy purpose. For the exercises of the spirit are hard and wholly contrary to the flesh; in them he is not able to persevere constantly who disdains to put on the weapons of the strongest vigor. Therefore, if you desire to subject all your affections to the spirit, if you wish to fight successfully against the vices of the flesh, take care to do yourself a most prudent violence, and to arm yourself strongly against all things.
