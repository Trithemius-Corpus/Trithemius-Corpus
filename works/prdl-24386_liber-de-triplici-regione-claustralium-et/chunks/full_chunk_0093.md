*On the chapter of faults.*

Since nothing in human acts is perfect in every respect, let the devout cenobite rejoice that he has found a remedy for his imperfection, because whatever is done amiss in well-reformed monasteries through human frailty is corrected daily. For the chapter of faults, continued on each day, washes away public offenses. Sacramental confession, repeated daily, through the grace of contrition remits hidden negligences. We discern four chapters in the monastery, of which we shall speak in order.

The first passive chapter is when a brother proclaims himself or is proclaimed by another.

The first chapter is called passive, in which our devout cenobite proclaims himself for his negligences, or is proclaimed by another. In this chapter the lover of God, who has denied himself to himself, both humbly accuses his negligences and allows himself to be accused of them by others, lest he be judged more harshly by the Lord after this life. For the holy apostle of God says: If we judged ourselves, we should certainly not be judged. And the prophet Nahum says: Affliction shall not rise up a second time. For indeed he who here, for love of God, is now voluntarily afflicted for his sins, is worthy, as one so well purified, to be freed from future affliction through the mercy of the Creator. Would that all cloistered persons knew how greatly, by voluntarily enduring capitular proclamations for their sins, they would advance toward the merit of everlasting life; they would endure all accusations and injuries for the love of God with inestimable joy. For we merit more when we patiently suffer injuries and adversities for love of God and fraternal charity: [unclear].

Therefore the devout monk standing in judgment should not fear any proclamations whatever, but should desire to be accused by others in remembrance of the Lord’s passion, knowing that before him is set the loftiness of glory, to be bestowed in heaven upon humble patience. O brother, why would you not endure the proclamations of the brothers, you who await the fellowship of angels?
