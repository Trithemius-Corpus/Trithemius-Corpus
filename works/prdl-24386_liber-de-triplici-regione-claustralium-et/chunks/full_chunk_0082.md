Ascending into this region, the mind, kindled by love, utterly abandons thoughts and images; it escapes alone, naked, and wholly free, because thoughts, by reason of the office of memory, are compelled to remain in the middle region, and they can in no way approach the supreme region of the spirit, which is above all human understanding. For one ascends hither by love through affection and desire alone; far from here the phantasms of thoughts are driven back. For as soon as the mind begins to ascend by means of thoughts, it is repelled and falls into the middle region. Yet the ascent itself is begun through the operation of the intellect; when that soon fails, it is completed through affection. For the intellect stirs up desire toward the love of the highest good; as much as it can, it commends its beauty. But when affection has begun to ascend by love to the contemplation of such great beauty, the intellect, failing through amazement, falls silent. But the mind, unable to bear the company of the intellect, alone and naked, wholly free from every image of thought, enters the dark mountain, shining beyond measure; it contemplates the beloved, not indeed face to face, but in an enigma; it fails before the greatness of love; caught up, it rests in silence.

Once Moses, leader of the people of Israel, called by God, is read to have ascended the dark mountain alone; he is recorded to have forbidden the people access to the mountain under threat of death; in the darkness the Lord appeared; through Moses himself he gave the law to the people. For thus it is written in Exodus: But the whole of Mount Sinai was smoking, because the Lord had descended upon it in fire, and smoke went up from it as from a furnace. And the whole mountain was terrible, and the sound of the trumpet gradually grew louder and extended longer. But all the people saw the lamps and the smoking mountain, and, terrified and shaken with fear, they stood far off, saying to Moses: Speak to us yourself and we will hear; let not the Lord speak to us, lest perhaps we die.

These divine things, full of mysteries, if they are examined with careful consideration, will magnificently commend this supreme region of the spirit. For Mount Sinai signifies the height of this region; the smoking darkness represents the object of contemplation; Moses expresses affection burning in love; the people shows the crowd of thoughts and phantasms. But Moses alone, the leader of the people, is permitted to ascend the mountain and enter the darkness, because affection alone, inflamed by divine love, is raised to the summit of highest contemplation. But the people are forbidden, under threat of death, to approach the mountain, because no intellect, no thought, no imagination, or reasoning will ever be able in any way to penetrate the secret of the living majesty. The people stand far from the mountain, terrified with fear, because while the mind alone enters, through the affection of love, into the abyss of contemplation, the whole crowd of thoughts, utterly failing, is thrown into confusion.
