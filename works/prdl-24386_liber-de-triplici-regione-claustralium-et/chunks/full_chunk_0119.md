But if, being now about to die, you would not do what you now do, you are most foolish, who, being about to die shortly, so boldly permit yourself works of death unto death. But if, since it is necessary, you desire to die well, do at no time what you would not do if death were already at hand. For whatever you now do, whether it is good or evil, you will find after death.

*Inwardly the monk becomes humble and*

Secondly, at the time of labor the monk ought inwardly to be devout, devout and humble; for if the fervor of inward progress leaves him, what he acquires outwardly through labor is of no help. Therefore, so that the soul may truly make progress in laboring, let it often recall to memory the degrees of good will written in the first region. Let it order its meditations on the Lord’s passion according to the fervor of devotion; in these, as far as possible, let it conform its own labors to the labors of Christ. Let him always be humble in heart through self-denial; let him be devout in affection through the fervor of holy compunction, so that the outward labor of the hands may contribute to the advancement and usefulness of the community, and the mind may not withdraw from the inward sweetness of divine love. If labor wearies the working cenobite, if heat burns, if untimely thirst presses him hard, let him call to mind how many labors the Lord Jesus endured for us, and he will bear all things more lightly. Drawing breath, therefore, let him say to the Lord:

*Most sweet and most beloved Lord Jesus. Prayer of one laboring.*

O King of heaven and earth, who for my redemption, born of the most pure Virgin Mary, lived among men for thirty-three years as God and man; who had great labors in walking, teaching, and preaching; who for my sake willingly and patiently endured thirst, hunger, heat, and cold: for all these things I give thanks to you. Receive, I beseech you, these very small labors of mine, however useless, which I humbly offer to your most holy majesty in memory and honor of all your labors and of your most holy passion; and may they be pleasing to you in that union in which your most holy labors were pleasing to God the Father. Amen.

Let our cenobite, moreover, under labor, if time permits, turn over in his mind the principal articles of the Lord’s passion: first by commemorating them as though Christ were present in the passion; then by giving thanks on his knees; third, by asking mercy in them according to their likeness.

Let him also recall to memory the ways of life of the holy fathers or the passions of the martyrs, and consider how great a reward and glory they now receive in their homeland for their labors.
