For no one can sweetly love God in this life so long as he is proved to love anything in the world. For the love of the world is wholly contrary to the divine love, because where the one is admitted into the inner chambers of the heart, the other is forbidden to be present. But how great a sweetness, how great a delight, and how great a gladness of mind the love of God begets in the heart of the lover, no one at all understands except him who, having despised all the things and events of the world, has loved God alone for a long time. To a heart loving God, none of the things that are in the world is to be loved: it despises riches, honors, and the favors of the age; it does not fear to undergo any adversities for the love of its Lord. The love of God is never idle; it always desires to come to its beginning; it refuses to endure no labors in this world. Therefore let him who does not wish to become slothful love.

The neighbor also is to be sweetly loved on account of God, and is in no way to be offended against God or justice. Let each person always show himself to his brother, in words and deeds, such as he expects others to be toward himself. For he truly loves his neighbor who for him exposes property, body, and soul. This Christ taught; this the Son of God did for us. Indeed, whoever says that he truly believes in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked. He does not truly love Christ who does not keep his commandments. For he says: This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you. We are members of one head, Jesus Christ, in whom we then believe rightly when we observe his commandment concerning love toward God and toward neighbor. Let us therefore love our neighbor, because he is a member in Christ with us, so that, just as one member in the body suffers together with another, so the brother may humbly be helped for the love of Christ.

The second manner commands you to love wisely, and to seek nothing else in divine love than God. Stretch forth your affection toward that Word which was in the beginning with God; lift your mind above everything that is created, because unless you abandon yourself and all things that are in the world, you will not be able to come to the God whom you love. Finally, if you desire to love God wisely, do not esteem yourself to be anything, because where a man judges himself to be something, he already loves foolishly.

The third manner enjoins that you love strongly, and that you never fear to expose body and soul for the love of God. He is not a strong lover of God who fears to undergo death for him. For love is strong as death. The neighbor is loved wisely if the man is not hated, but the vice. But he is loved strongly if every injury is remitted to him from a soul disposed to do good.

*The second region ends.*

*Preface to the third treatise of the first part, which describes the highest region of the spirit of cloistered persons.*

Writing at last, with the Lord’s help, of that highest region of the spirit, I am struck at once by fear and shame, because, being small, I fear to speak of the greatest matters, and I am greatly ashamed to teach what I have not sufficiently experienced. For whose intellect, however exercised a man may be, would recognize the state of a mind sweetly resting through love in God? Or whose discourse, even that of the most eloquent orator, would explain the secret sweetness of inward contemplation? Indeed, the exercise of this third region for the greater part surpasses human understanding; in it the mind of a purified man, through the fervor of divine love, is carried above itself, and, while its natural essence remains, is, as it were, lulled and absorbed in the abyss of divine contemplation with a wondrous sweetness. For it is oppressed by so great a delight of inward majesty that it seems able neither to understand nor to express its state. For, forsaking the operation of the senses, it wholly goes beyond even the intellective operation, and, what is more, it is lifted above itself by affection to that deserted summit of divine darkness. When, therefore, it has almost wholly failed from itself, it does not act but rests; nor, when returned to itself, can it understand what it has undergone, nor reveal to anyone the manner of its elevation. For this elevation of the mind into God is so profound and so sublime that not only can the inexperienced not understand it, but even the experienced cannot fully explain it in words as it is in itself.

But what shall I be able to say about the mountain of divine darkness, which I have not yet deserved to penetrate? For the well of inward contemplation is deep, but I am weak, since I have neither brought a jar fit for these waters nor prepared steps of ascent suitable for lofty things. Composed words do not befit a fool; nor ought an untried and rigid man to speak of the secrets of divine contemplation, one who still stands with the weak in the valleys. For only he speaks fruitfully about profound matters who is illumined by the ray of a twofold spirit. Nevertheless, since I am a man under obedience, I am compelled by the statutes of the annual chapter and of that synod itself to undertake it and to bring to an end, according to my powers, the work proposed to me. And so I enter upon the third region of cloistered persons under the guidance of the venerable father, the lord of Bursfeld, so that, by reducing, as far as I can, to my own manner of speaking what he described in scholastic and truthful discourse, I may seem to have obeyed the chapter.
