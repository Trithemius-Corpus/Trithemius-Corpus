Flesh and blood, however, shall not possess the kingdom of God. But what [does] the Savior [say] to us? A man’s enemies are those of his own household. And who are these household members, if not the sensual powers that are opposed to the spirit? For concupiscibility and irascibility, enemies of our [unclear], stir up in us all evils against the spirit, and continually disturb the house of conscience with various passions. As long as the [unclear] sensual powers carry out their natural movements and courses in a man, they do not permit him to ascend to the liberty of the spirit. Concupiscibility sensually enjoys the delights of the flesh; and, on the contrary, the irascible power detests bodily pains. Meanwhile, when this war of the flesh is long waged in a man, he is regarded as a restless monk, a cultivator of the flesh and of the lowest region. Therefore whoever desires to come to eternal life must strongly restrain in all things these disordered powers of the senses.

Therefore let the faithful cenobite strive to direct the concupiscible power toward the painful things which are [unclear] acceptable to God, and to reform all his affections according to God’s good pleasure. If he does this, he will pray confidently to the Lord: “My soul has longed to desire your justifications at all times.” And often, weeping sweetly, he will cry out that other verse: “As the hart longs for the springs of waters, so my soul longs for you, O God.” Then, cleaving to God, let him prudently set the irascible power against sensual delights, and diligently reform whatever in it is ordered toward vice. Thus he will not fear the soul’s hard return to its first ordering for the love of God, and by means of the irascible power he will begin strongly to resist all carnal things that stir up desire. And then the devout cenobite, raising himself in spirit, will cry out: “My soul refused to be comforted.” And that saying of the Apostle: “I have judged [unclear] as dung.” The amended soul that truly loves God despises all things except him.

From these two powers, however, every disordered affection arises, and from this the [unclear] oppositions of [unclear] are generated. For from the concupiscible power joy and hope take their highest beginning, and the irascible power gives birth to sorrow and fear. Of these the shield [unclear] Bernard says thus: “The whole heart of man is perverted by four affections; if we bring these back to God, the whole heart of man is converted.”

*On faithful cleansing. Article 3.* But it is by no means enough to restrain the sensual powers unless one also faithfully cleanses the intellectual powers. For this cleansing great and continual diligence is needed. For because by negligence [unclear] is dissolved, it is never cleansed from vicious corruptions. For he who sows in corrupted powers reports back nothing other than corruption from his errors.
