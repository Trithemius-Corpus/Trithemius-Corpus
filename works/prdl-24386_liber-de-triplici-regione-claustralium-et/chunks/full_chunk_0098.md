Let him also pray for his pastor; let him pray for the whole congregation, that the Lord may mercifully direct him in his will, and, preserving this congregation under regular observance, may defend it from every adversity.

Furthermore, let him pray for the universal Church; let him pray for the faithful and the unbelievers, that almighty God may keep believers in the faith and convert non-believers to the Catholic faith. Let him also pray for the conversion of useful persons to the order, through whom both his own monastery may advance in each state and the whole observance of the order may grow for the better. Let him not forget the souls of the faithful in the pains of purgatory, awaiting the mercy of the Creator; but, praying for them, let him know that after a little while he himself will pass to them, where the prayer now poured forth will return into the bosom of the one who prays.

But let the devout minister of Christ beware lest he neglect, on any occasion whatever, anything that belongs to his office, or lest through his prayers or disturbances he make the celebrant distracted. When there is leisure, he may meditate or pray in silence; when service is to be rendered, he ought to interrupt prayers, at least verbal ones, and answer the priest by serving him diligently in all things.

At the elevation of the most sacred body of Christ, following the example of Saint Thomas Aquinas, he may say with attention directed to Christ: “You are the King of glory, O Christ; you are the everlasting Son of the Father,” up to the end of that hymn.

After the elevation, let the devout monk turn himself to the Lord Jesus, desiring from the depths of his heart to have a share in so great a mystery, and let him prepare his affection as though he were about to communicate; for as often as a devout lover of God, from fervor of heart, should desire to receive the body of Christ, he believes that he has received the power of the sacrament. For the Lord Jesus said to a certain soul dear to him: If a man fervent in my love, out of desire, has prayed to receive my body a thousand times in a day, if it were possible, so many times before me, through the power of my body itself, he truly obtains his desire in his soul. And therefore, when this desire has been conceived in the mind of one who loves Jesus, thus, or in this manner, let the monk pray to the Lord:

*Prayer of the minister*

Would that, most beloved Lord Jesus, I were so well and fervently disposed that it were permitted me now to receive your most holy body in this venerable sacrament; would that it were permitted me to repeat this holy communion often in a day. I pray, my sweetness, O only delight of my heart, O my hope, Lord Jesus: you know the ardor of my mind toward you, and before you is all my desire. I pray you, by your divine goodness, grant me the power and grace to receive this most holy sacrament now and at every time into my soul, so that I may be able to remain continually in you.

*The diligent preparation of a priest about to celebrate.*
