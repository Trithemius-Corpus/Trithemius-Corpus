Here the soul directs its gaze through love toward that highest, unchangeable good; it utterly rejects from itself every created and creatable multiplicity; the soul gathers itself into one, so that, united to itself, it may be able to rise without the impediment of multiplicity to its own principle, which is one. For only unity can attain to the one principle of all things, because unity flees multiplicity; unity seeks the one. For insofar as you yield to multiplicity, so far will you withdraw from unity. Therefore the mind of a person dwelling in this highest region of the spirit, so that it may be able through love to raise itself to the contemplation of its principle, is accustomed to cast off from itself every multiplicity clinging in imagination or understanding, so that, gathered in its unity, it may be able ardently to direct itself, with the simple affection of love, toward him whom it knows to be above every affirmation.

*That this highest region of the spirit is pleasant and lovable by reason of its nearness.*

*Secondly, this highest region of the spirit. Article II.*

It is sweet and most pleasant in solitude and in singular nearness. For the citizens of this most holy region are separated from the citizens above by only one earthen wall, so to speak, because as long as the spirit inhabits flesh, it cries to heaven as though through a wall. But the earthen wall is the prison-house of the flesh, from which the free spirit, about to migrate to heaven, goes forth when it is separated from the body by death. But the spirit wounded by sins, which cannot raise itself to this region through the pure affection of love, is said still to stand farther from the empyrean heaven. Those, however, who dwell in that most quiet region are nearest to the empyrean heaven, because, weighed down by no stains of sins, they despise all earthly and carnal things and always long to dwell in heavenly things in mind and spirit. For those who inhabit the empyrean heaven and those who dwell in the third region have one and the same object. But this is the difference: these contemplate God in an enigma, while those do so in clear vision. Wondrous on both sides is the sweetness of those who contemplate: here it is great, there greatest; here in the desire of fervent love, there in the possession of the most blessed vision.

In this highest region of divine contemplation the rational spirit calls back its powers and capacities into its own chamber; there it too, free from every phantasm, is at leisure; it raises itself above itself into the dense cloud of divine darkness, where, lulled by ecstatic love, it sweetly rests. Here there is no troublesome confusion of thoughts, no imagination of phantasms; rather, the naked mind is raised upward, contemplating without any thought or imagination at all. Indeed, when the mind itself opens an entrance to thoughts, it again [unclear] to descend to the middle region, which is that of reason.
