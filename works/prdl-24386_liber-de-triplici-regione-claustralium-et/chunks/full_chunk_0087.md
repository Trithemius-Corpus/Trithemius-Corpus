*At the first signal.*

When the first signal sounds, the cenobite should immediately go to the privacy of an inner chamber; he should diligently examine his conscience, cleansing it, and compose himself by preparing himself for prayer in the three following ways.

First, he should strive to draw his mind back from outward things, vain and perishable, and from contrary spirits, to inward things, and to think absolutely nothing about secular or temporal business. Just as Abraham is read to have stood firm and driven the birds away from the sacrifice, so it seems necessary for us to act against worldly thoughts flying importunately around us during divine services. The cenobite can more easily bring his mind to this forgetting of passing things if he is willing often to recall to memory the sixth root, placed above in the second region, concerning the vanity of present things.

Second, he should direct the eyes of his mind with humility to the knowledge of himself, knowing that by his own strength he cannot persist in the holy fervor of the spirit unless divine compassion deigns to assist him. Finally, the humble monk should understand that he is not worthy to praise his creator, and from this he should ask help from the Lord and the merit of worthy praise. But if the monk desires to know more about self-knowledge, let him devoutly read the fourth root.

Third, for one wishing to stand devoutly before the Lord God in prayers, it is necessary to raise the mind with fervor of spirit to heavenly things. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: God is spirit, and those who adore him must adore in spirit and truth.
