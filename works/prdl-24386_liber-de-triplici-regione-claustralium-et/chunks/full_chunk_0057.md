Sixth, the generous munificence of God is praiseworthy in the glorification of the blessed, because for a small merit he rewards his elect with so ineffable a recompense, as though he gives immortal gifts together with the holy angels.

Seventh, the incomprehensible excellence of God is praiseworthy in itself, because he orders [unclear] according to his providence and, most tranquil in himself, always remains immovable, without motion or time. The contemplation of this seventh consideration belongs to the sabbath of eternal happiness, in which there is now no work of laborious activity, but the blessedness of a happy eternal dwelling and rest. For just as God, the creator of all things, is read to have completed all his work and rested on the seventh day, so not work but eternal rest will be laid upon the righteous in the heavenly sabbath. Therefore, if we desire happily to arrive at those heavenly mansions, let us rest in God alone, in whom the rational creature is perfected by resting; having been perfected through the adherence of love, it rejoices with the Lord in ineffable union and joy. Henceforth, for praising God, let pure devotion seek nothing outside God, because God himself, almighty, perfect, and most wise, suffices for all as the material of his own praise, and exceeds all praise by his infinite perfections. For Scripture says: Praise the Lord as much as you can; he will still surpass it. For he is greater than all praise, and therefore he is to be praised by us most humbly in himself and for himself rather than from creatures.

From the aforesaid considerations concerning God, a manifold and varied affection of devotion arises; through it also a worthier effect of prayer is generated according to the desire of the affection. For when meditation of the heart is taken up concerning the majesty or power of God, reverent admiration is produced. But when we begin to meditate on his inexpressible wisdom, confounded by our foolish imperfection, we accuse ourselves. Thus consequently, from consideration of the mercy of God, hope of pardon is received; from his patience, love of the heart is generated; from justice, humiliation is born; from meditation on munificence, gratitude. For just as fire, once kindled, increases in magnitude according to the quality of the wood, so according to various meditations on God the ardor of devotion rises up in prayers among those who love God.
