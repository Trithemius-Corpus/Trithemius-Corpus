*Secondly, the pastor of souls is rendered disordered toward his subjects* if his mind is occupied too greedily with temporal affairs, if he bears greater anxiety for secular business than for the souls committed to him. This excessive care for temporal things holy father Benedict forbids in the Rule, and rightly judges rational souls to be preferred to all things of the world.

“Before all things,” he says, “let the abbot not, by dissembling or making little of the salvation of the souls committed to him, bear more anxiety for transitory, earthly, and perishable things; but let him always consider that he has undertaken to govern souls, for which he will also render account.” Consequently the pious father assures him concerning the necessities of the body, if, loving God, he worships him with a pure mind. “And lest perhaps,” he says, “he complain of scantier substance, let him remember what is written: ‘Seek first the kingdom of God and his justice, and all these things shall be added to you.’ And again: ‘Nothing is lacking to those who fear God.’”

But the disordered pastor of monks is led astray by the spirit of avarice; blinded in mind as though without feeling, he casts away from himself the fear of the Lord; sold under sin with Ahab, he runs to the aid of wickedness; he lacks confidence in the promises made through the holy father concerning the attainment of necessities; he is occupied with external matters more than befits a monk. Driven now thereafter by the goads of avarice, he despises the commands of God and of holy mother Church; he does not keep feast days; he plunges wholly into earthly affairs; he thinks on nothing but temporal things; he detests the observance of regular discipline; and when he fears being restrained from evil pursuits by the authority of the Chapter, he seeks the dissolution of the holy union.

Then, having become reprobate with Saul, he calls upon the counsel of the pythoness; he declares the obedience of reform damnable for his monastery; he desires to be released [as quickly as possible?] from submission to the confederation; to this end he relates many things falsely and mendaciously. But when the authority of the annual Chapter is despised with impunity, it remains that monastic observance suffer shipwreck; for love of God keeps the good regularly in discipline, and fear of correction restrains the depraved from evil works. Whoever strives to evade the unity of the Chapter imitates the devil.
