In this way he will never be able to prepare himself better for celebrating than if, before approaching the altar, he always runs through at least one point of the Lord's Passion in meditation and with tears. For indeed, since the celebration of Mass was instituted by our Redeemer himself in memory of the Lord's Passion, a man never prepares himself for it better or more fruitfully than if he is more fervently set aflame through meditation on that most bitter Passion.

*The Elevation of the Priest's Mind to God During the Celebration*

With how great reverence, devotion, and purity of mind we ought to stand before almighty God at the altar, what speech could express, or what understanding could penetrate by grasping it? For there the true body and true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are confected in an ineffable manner. There the Lord of majesty, truly present, is adored. There the invisible host of angels, attending upon its king, rejoices together. There the whole heavenly court adores Jesus, God and man. Therefore the greatest purity of mind, the greatest reverence and humility, are needed when we must stand before so great a majesty, lest the Lord Jesus, searching the reins and the heart, be able to find anything in us that offends him. Let all worldly anxieties withdraw far from here; let all thoughts depart. Let nothing be thought here except what pertains to the completion of so great a ministry. Let nothing move in the mind except what belongs to the office in the presence of Majesty: the miracle of divine majesty is being performed; the Son of God is sacrificed to the Father; the body of our Redeemer is truly received; the mystery of our restoration is celebrated. Therefore, standing at the altar, the priest should remember that he is attending upon invisible majesty, and should take care not to think of anything from elsewhere.

*After the Celebration, Diligent [unclear] of the Mind*

After the communion of the body and blood, the devout cenobite should give thanks to almighty God, and should guard himself throughout the whole day, by spiritual exercise, in the purity and devotion of mind he has conceived. He should direct his affections toward God with fiery rays, and not grow lukewarm in his holy purpose. Above all he should strive to love nothing in this world, but to raise his heart continually toward the fire of divine love, wholly stripped of all earthly affections. He should always consider how, from now on, he may order his life as if anew in the holy fervor of his way of life, and humbly dispose himself for the often repeated reception of so great a guest. Finally, during the day, amid the labors enjoined upon him, he should often offer some prayer to God the Father in the power and union of that sacrament, so that through the exercises of holy communion and regular obedience he may kindle himself more fully in divine love.
