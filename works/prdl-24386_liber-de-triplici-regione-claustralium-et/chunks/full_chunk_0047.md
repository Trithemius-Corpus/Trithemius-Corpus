But in three things the virtue of patience is especially necessary, and is rewarded with the highest recompenses. In insults of words, the patience of a prudent man and lover of God preserves the purpose of humility; in the taking away of temporal goods and advantages it receives increase; and in bodily injury the silent conscience of virtue [unclear] the discipline. Consider now, O faithful soul, how your most beloved Savior Jesus sustained these three things most patiently for your redemption; and if you believe that you love him, do not think it burdensome, for love of him, to endure similar things with patience.

From the three steps immediately preceding we establish one consummate end of the cenobite, which, as seems reasonable to us, we call the threefold step. For by three steps, namely the second, third, and fourth, the end of the cenobitic institution is perfected, and the renunciation of one’s own will is completed. For where patience, the preserver of virtues, is joined to the renunciation of one’s own will and to obedience, every pleasure and care of the body is mortified. Whoever has deserved to ascend this threefold step has fulfilled the Lord’s commandment, which is stated in the Gospel: “If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself” through the second step of humility, “and take up his cross” through the third, “and follow me” through the fourth.

The fifth step of humility is true confession of sins. The faithful cenobite is said to ascend into this if he confesses to his abbot, with humble trust, all evil thoughts that come into his heart, or any evils committed in secret. For if anything is committed through impatience, at the adversary’s prompting, in the preceding threefold step, it is restored in this one through contrition and humble confession. For no one is found so perfect that he does not contract the stain of earthly life, as Scripture says: “In many things we all offend.” For no one is clean from sin, not even an infant whose life upon the earth is one day. Therefore this step necessarily follows the fourth immediately; in it all the negligences of the preceding steps are corrected. True confession raises up the soldier falling in battle, heals the one wounded by the enemy, and comforts the weak and faint-hearted. Whoever, therefore, desires to stand in battle and bravely conquer the enemy should often run back to the safeguards of true confession. For it is impossible for one wounded by the enemy to recover health if he neglects to reveal the wound itself to the physician. Therefore you, faithful cenobite, who desire to save your soul and to be a good soldier of Jesus Christ, do not omit humble confession, because you will never be able to conquer the devil better than if you take care faithfully to reveal to your abbot all the thoughts and sins that he suggests to you. It is certain to me, and proved by the destruction of many, that a cenobite commits himself to being overcome by many temptations of the enemy if he refuses to keep this step.
