For if spiritual exercise has failed in all who are established in holy religion, little by little one falls into a deep torpor. For just as this visible fire of ours cannot continue in its operation without fuel suited to it, so the love of inward compunction cannot remain in a person’s mind without the continual exercise of devotion. For as soon as the labor of spiritual study has been interrupted through negligence, every compunction of devotion gradually vanishes. Indeed, no one can remain stable and firm in a holy purpose unless he strives daily to renew the fervor of his mind by some exercise. For as soon as the fuel of divine love is withdrawn, devotion is changed into torpor. For Scripture says: Every idle person is in desires. Therefore, lest the mind gradually slip away from the holy fervor of the spirit through sloth, it must learn to renew itself daily by some spiritual exercise, so that, while fuel is supplied to love on each single day, the desire of making progress may be kindled further. Thus we read that the most holy fathers once made progress in the order; thus we know that they remained in devotion, because they knew very well the instability of the human mind, and therefore they were accustomed daily to rouse themselves to a new fervor of spirit. One, through the study of sacred reading, drove his mind toward compunction of devotion; another stirred himself by meditation on the Lord’s Passion; another, for the continuation of his holy purpose, placed before his inward eyes each day the image of death close at hand; another, by a certain method in prayers, daily offered himself to the Lord with tears. No one among them is believed to have kept his mind without daily spiritual exercise; no one is found to have been given over to idleness, because the kingdom of God is promised to those laboring in the Lord’s vineyard, but is denied to the idle. Considering this, the devout fathers of the annual chapter unanimously resolved to adopt the present exercise, and to offer it for practice to their subjects who have nothing better. And although so great a multitude of monks subject to the aforesaid chapter neither can nor ought to be restricted to the formula of a single exercise, nevertheless it is proposed to all in common that anyone who does not have a better exercise should use this one.
