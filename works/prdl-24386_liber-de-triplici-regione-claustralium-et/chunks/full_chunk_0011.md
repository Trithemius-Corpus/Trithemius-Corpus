Since, however, we have shown that very many monks of the lowest region are disordered, and likewise their pastors, shall we not also show the disorders of prelates toward their subjects, as though sparing ourselves? Neither does the name of abbot sanctify anyone, nor does the preeminence of pastoral care save the undeserving. Therefore, since many among the prelates inhabit the mountain of the lowest region with the beasts of the earth, they must by no means be passed over by us in silence. For very many rulers of cloistered persons show themselves disordered toward their subjects: they not only neglect to feed according to the rule the souls committed to them, but tear and kill them in various ways. But whenever someone from the lowest region is taken up as a pastor, the pursuits of the wicked are strengthened for the worse. Therefore a good man, one who loves God, is to be promoted as abbot: one who has long since overcome this lowest region; one who has learned to subdue the passions of the flesh; one who both wishes and is able to ascend to that highest region of the spirit. For the abbot of monks must be of higher merit than the monks, since whoever is appointed ruler of souls must necessarily be adorned with many virtues. For if a weak pastor still dwells in the lowest region with the sheep, he kills the feeble by neglecting them; he does not strengthen them. For if a blind man offers guidance to a blind man, he must necessarily fall with him into the pit. But the pastor who does not show himself well ordered toward his subjects according to the rule is to be called not a pastor but a hireling, and he will not remain in the house of the Lord forever.

Many, moreover, are the disorders of carnal prelates, which do harm so much the more widely as they are corrected the more rarely.

And first, indeed, a ruler is disordered toward his subjects if he has been a hireling: if, with the sheep neglected, he seeks his own advantage of flesh or soul; if, so that he may be able to obtain his own will, he denies nothing contrary to the rule to those who ask. For truly the one who is a hireling does not truly love the sheep: he seeks his own glory, desires to be over others, does not care to be of service according to the rule, rejoices to be called abbot, does not know how to show himself a pastor. Therefore he receives the empty reward of his vanity: he who seeks not God's glory but his own; who would more readily permit the souls of his subjects to perish than resign the abbacy to another, better man. He is a hireling, and the sheep do not belong to him. He is not a true monk; he is not a good pastor; he is not a sincere lover of God, who, presiding over monks, is unwilling to step down if, by the counsel of those who fear God, it should seem expedient to do so. The care of governing souls despises the proud man; it requires one humble and resigned to God in all things. Therefore, if you wish to be found a good pastor and a true monk, remember that your intention must be formed in this way at all times: that you be ready, out of love for God, humbly to feed, according to your strength, the souls committed to you; that you also be ready to resign the pastoral office, at the judgment of God and of good men, from integrity of heart. If you have kept this intention always abiding in your mind, you will be a good pastor both to yourself and to others
