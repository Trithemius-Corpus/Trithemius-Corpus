You usurp another's right for yourselves. Is this the virtue of obedience: to be unwilling to yield to the command of a superior, but stubbornly to cling to one’s own judgment? The holy father did not lay down in the Rule that you should live in this way, since he ordered you in all things to follow the will of the abbot. For obedience is the greatest virtue of a monk, which our divine father wished to commend so highly that he said that a good work done without the abbot’s will lacks merit. Hence, giving instruction, he concludes thus: “Therefore let all things be done with the abbot’s will, and let no one in the monastery follow his own will.” But if a discretion sufficient for the ordering of the monastery does not appear to be present in the ruler, that must be imputed to carnal monks, who voluntarily set over themselves one who was not suitable. Folly always hates prudence, and wicked monks detest a wise abbot. Therefore those who knowingly set a fool over themselves will necessarily be subject to his ordering and will. Indeed, no incapacity of the pastor ought to extinguish the obedience of subjects, since the Rule commands us to obey even an unprofitable one. For it says: “The precepts of the abbot are to be obeyed in all things, even if he himself, which God forbid, acts otherwise.” But carnal monks, reproving us for disobedience, object: “We do these things rightly and with a good intention, because we see the substance of the monastery perishing through bad government.” But the apostle contradicts you, since evils are not to be done so that good things may come, nor on account of the loss of a temporal thing is the subjection of humility to be abandoned. For where, between loss of temporal substance and danger to regular obedience, something must be undergone, it is better that the substance perish than that the purity of obedience be harmed. For God will not require money from our hands, but souls. Truly, he who is a true monk prefers the virtue of obedience to all the things of the world. But the citizens of this lowest region, carnal monks, tear apart the virtue of obedience in various ways; they attack the weapons of holy humility, so that it seems easier to me to establish a new monk from the world than to be able to amend the sloth of those men. But from their fruits we clearly know what sort they are, namely those who violate peace and charity in the monastery, disturb the concord of unity, sow discord between the brothers and the abbot, and stubbornly refuse to obey the commands of the Rule. These are the works of evil cloisterers; these are the marks of the citizens of the lowest region, since, as most wicked defenders of their own will, they cast aside the weapons of holy obedience; they live according to the flesh, not the spirit; they show either no deference or little to the statutes of the fathers; they do not acquiesce in sound counsels. Their damnation is just. For just as it is impossible for a blazing fire not to ascend upward, so it is not possible for a monk without obedience to penetrate the secrets of heaven.
