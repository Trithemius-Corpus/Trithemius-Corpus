This disorder, moreover, is seen especially in those who, on any pretext, resign their office, to whom, as the reward of their stewardship, as if they had been bishops, an annual pension is assigned from the goods of the monastery. This is not the manner of those who seek the Lord; this is not the way of the monks of old. When you resign the pastoral office for God’s sake, resign it simply; do not demand anything besides food and clothing, if you do not wish to sin with those who have private property. You are a monk; you were a monk before you became abbot; you will remain a monk after the office. Why, then, should you demand a pension, since you know that you are a monk? Just as the fault of simony is committed by buying prelacies, so by demanding a pension for the resignation of an abbacy one sins against the Rule. What then? Do we condemn those who, resigning the office of pastoral care without fault, have received annual provisions from the goods of the monastery? Far be it. We do not forbid necessities, since we deny superfluities. Yet the man who is a true monk and a lover of God is content with the provision of the Rule even after an abbacy.

*How monks, citizens of the lowest region, are disordered toward one another. Article 4.*

Carnal [unclear] are disordered toward one another in many and various ways, because those who, stirred up by animal passions after the manner of beasts, do not direct themselves according to the right judgment of reason. It is no wonder if they overturn the order of fraternal conversation. For an unruly and disturbed conscience always presumes, and an evil mind never has the joys of peace, but is continually shaken by the sorrow of vices and is inwardly tormented by the everlasting fires of crimes. Thus monks, citizens of the dark region, blinded by evil and disordered affections, do not consider the end of the monastic profession; they love nothing but the advantage of the present life; they neglect their own salvation; they do not rise up to the love of eternity.

First, therefore, they are very disordered toward one another when they cast aside the intellectual regard that ought to be had for their brothers; when they do not notice the image of God in man; when they do not think of the common share of grace and glory infused in human beings and to be granted in the future; nor do they take care to show one another the love of fraternal fellowship. They live with one another like horse and mule, concordant in bodily appearance, because whatever is pleasing to sensuality they love; whatever accords with reason they do not admit.
