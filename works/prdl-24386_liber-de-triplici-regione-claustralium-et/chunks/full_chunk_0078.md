Therefore, one who stands in this region should prudently return at times to the degrees of humility described in the second; he should humbly learn to remain in the deep resignation of his own self, because as soon as the mind has despised self-denial, it rushes from the highest mountain into a dreadful abyss.

For many, trusting in the jubilation of their sensual devotion, have been deceived, who climbed this very high mountain without the foundation of humility. Others, their heads injured and their senses destroyed, became foolish, because, when their passions had not yet been mortified, they presumed to enter the darkness of mystical silence. Others, lifted up into pride while they were still given over to sensuality, descended through the fault of their own will into the depth of hell. For Scripture says concerning the ascent of this summit: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” But a beast is then said to touch the mountain when a man, not yet purified through the exercise of the second region, attempts to ascend the third. For as long as the affection has not been purified from the passions of its sensuality, the beast still lives in him. But if the mind of a man with its beastliness presumes to approach the mountain of God, it is overwhelmed by as many stones as it is wounded by passions. For the wise man says: “He will not be stoned with a muddy stone, and all will speak in contempt of him.” By the muddy stone carnal affection is expressed, because man is compared to mud. For in Job it is written: “Remember, I ask, that you have made me like mud, and you will bring me back into dust.” And again he says: “I have been compared to mud, and I have been likened to embers and ashes.” Thus the slothful man is overwhelmed by the muddy stone when the mind, still unclean with carnal and sensual passions, tries to ascend to the mountain of contemplation; on it, as unworthy, it is compelled to suffer rejection and ruin through two stones set against it. For no one will be able to reach this most quiet region of the spirit who has not first cleansed himself by long exercise in the furnace of the middle region. Whoever, still devoted to carnal passions, attempts to approach this supreme region of the spirit is striving, as it were, to draw water in a sieve. For in truth this region does not admit carnal minds into itself, since it is prepared for hearts purified from every affection.
