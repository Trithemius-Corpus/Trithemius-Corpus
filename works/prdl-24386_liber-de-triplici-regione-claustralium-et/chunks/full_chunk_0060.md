Hence he loves God above all things with burning desire and with the pure fervor of the mind, and longs to cleave continually to him alone, without intermediary. And although the merciful God has bestowed many things specially upon each one of us, nevertheless, because we write these our tokens not for one but for many, we must briefly bring forward not particular benefits assigned to individuals, but general benefits set before all. Therefore we include the more important ones under the number seven, so that through the seven days of the week, or even in the canonical hours themselves, we may have ready at hand material for daily meditation. But if anyone already dwells in higher things through the elevation of the mind toward God, let him not require these things, proposed as they are for novices. But he who, because of the weakness of his mind, cannot ascend to that supreme summit of contemplation, should strive to exercise himself more fervently in these meditations on the benefits of God, so that, while he diligently continues in the lower things, he may, purified and stronger, ascend to the higher. Let the devout monk therefore remember what he has received from the Lord God, so that he may be able to show himself grateful. Let him render to God what he has received, if he is good [unclear] he desires [unclear]. with his ornaments, and those whom he created intellectual beings in his own image he also mercifully called to the heavenly kingdoms. What more could he have given us, who, when we did not exist, created us and gave us himself? But what can we render to our Creator, we who by our own merit are sinners and weak? Let us render what we are; let us render what we owe: namely, faithful service for the body which we have received from the Lord, and sincere love for the soul by which we were created in the image of God. For thus the prophet admonishes us in the psalm: Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice before him with trembling. Serve faithfully; love reverently. On the part of the body, offer faithful service to your Creator, and on the part of the soul love him with all your strength. Serve him because he is powerful and a great Lord; love him because he is good. His power is shown in your creation; his goodness is declared in your restoration. Since he created you by his power when you did not exist, he deserves faithful service; since he restored you when you had fallen by his goodness, he deserves the affection of sincere love. For truly he is our Father through love; he is Lord through incomprehensible power.

Secondly, he gave us faith with the sacraments, since, just as he created us without our will, so without our merit he called us to the faith. For Christ says to his disciples in the holy Gospel: You did not choose me; rather, I chose you. Through faith the Christian soul is led back to its principle; through the sacraments it is renewed. Therefore, incorporated into Christ through the faith and the sacraments which he gave, we are bound to offer perpetual adherence, and to remain continually in his holy covenant.
