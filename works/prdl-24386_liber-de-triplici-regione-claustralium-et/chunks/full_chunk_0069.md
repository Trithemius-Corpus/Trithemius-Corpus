...and let them not presume [unclear]. Be conspicuous for uprightness of mind, renowned for the merit of your life, revered for zeal. Let the righteous love you as a servant of God; let the wicked dread your face as that of a judge. Let no one in your sight presume to do anything alien to good morals, since all know that you cannot tolerate injury done to God. Thus Phinehas earned the everlasting priesthood, when by zealously defending the law he powerfully appeased the wrath of the Lord.

If you see order perishing in the monastery, if your brothers have begun in any way to corrupt regular observance, do not, if you are a servant of God, keep silence about their vices. Set yourself like a wall, bravely, before the house of the Lord; exercise zeal for God as far as you are able; destroy the counsels of the malicious; resist the rashness of the proud. If you are a true lover of God, by your face alone you will scatter the assemblies of the depraved. For no one will disclose his plan before you if he suspects your zeal to be against it. But if the wicked and reprobate do not fear your presence, they plainly recognize no zeal for God in you.

Therefore, to show yourself a true servant of Christ, you must willingly forgive your brothers, for the love of God, the wrongs done to you, and pray to God for those very persons who wrong you. But wrongs done to the Lord are not yours to forgive without his will; rather, out of zeal for uprightness, you must avenge them. In these matters let love of blood-kin cease; let the bond of friendship withdraw far away: zeal for God is rightly to be preferred to all things. For human friendship must be observed in such a way that it is never preferred to zeal for God.

Woe to the monks and abbots who so boldly cast aside zeal for God; who put human familiarities before the divine command; who conceal the vices of their subjects through foolish compassion; who do not observe the traditions of the holy Rule even in others. And what shall I say? We see among cloistered monks obedience to commands despised, zeal for uprightness scorned, observance of the Rule contemned. Two or more contract among themselves a bond of fleshly friendship under the name of charity. The infirm entrust the diseases of their soul to those more infirm, and they hardly seek friendship with God through purity of mind. Each thinks his own companion better than the rest, and each, deceived, loves the other not spiritually but carnally, since they do not lift their affections to God; he loves nothing but the consolations of the flesh.

But the monk whom you see seeking the friendship of infirm brothers you must judge not a servant of God but a lover of the flesh. For the man who truly occupies his whole heart in the love of God cares nothing for human friendship. The love of Jesus suffices him for all things; beyond him he seeks nothing more. But whoever has sought the consolation of his mind in outward things shows by his deed that he does not love God with his whole heart.

The love of God loves solitude, hates the tumult of men, and does not seek conversations with friends. O good Jesus, how sweet is your love, how it pierces the heart that loves you; seeing you, it desires to see you; having you, the soul longs to embrace you.
