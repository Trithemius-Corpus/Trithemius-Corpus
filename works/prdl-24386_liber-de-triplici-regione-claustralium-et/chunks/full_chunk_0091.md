Then, after matins and the several hours of the day, having returned to his cell, the devout lover of Christ should fall on his knees before his bed and humbly give thanks to almighty God, because he has deigned to admit him, an inadequate servant, to his service, and there to number him among his sons. Therefore let him say this or a similar prayer.

*Prayer*

I give thanks to you, who have deigned to call me to your service, not by any merits of mine, but by the sole condescension of your mercy. I beseech you, through the pain of the wounds of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, and through the shedding of his most holy blood, and by the power of his most innocent death: receive this sacrifice of my service, for the glory of your majesty, for my sins, for your holy Church, and for the souls of all the faithful departed who are placed in the pains of purgatory, in that union of charity in which [unclear] the same our Lord Jesus Christ dying for us, having been received on the cross; and grant me, through the merit of that same Passion, to love you in truth and to remain continually faithful in your service. Amen.

Then enter briefly into an examination of your conscience, and see whether you still have in yourself the conditions of a good cloistered religious, described in the seventh article of the second [rule?]. If you know that anything is lacking, do not neglect to reform it as soon as possible, lest little by little through sloth you vanish away. For it is written: “He who neglects small things gradually falls away.” And the holy lover of God Augustine says: “No sin is so small that it does not grow when neglected.” Therefore, when the conscience has been examined and reformed in holy fervor, let him say to the Lord:

Grant us, almighty God, through the merit of the Passion of the Lord, and through the intercession of blessed Mary ever Virgin, and of all the holy heavenly virtues, and of the blessed patriarchs, prophets, apostles, martyrs, confessors, virgins, and all your elect, perseverance in your will, so that we may continually be able to offer you service pleasing to you. Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

*Ascending the Bed*

In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I intend to lay myself down, so that, strengthened by his divine majesty, I may be able to praise him further through the day.

Sitting upon [unclear]. May the sign of the holy cross, the power of the Lord’s Passion, and the intercession of all the saints guard me now and always. In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*The Exercise of the Cenobite at Prime*

*Consideration II*

When our devout cenobite has been awakened for prime in the customary way, let him observe the order and manner of recollecting himself and praying set down above before matins; and, arming himself with the sign of the life-giving cross, let him say: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Spirit. Amen. Then, descending from his bed and fervent in his holy purpose, let him again fall upon his knees and, with hands clasped, say:

*Prayer*

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love, you who through the diversity of tongues gathered the nations of all peoples into the unity of faith. Send forth your spirit, and they shall be created. And you shall renew the face of the earth.

God, to whom every heart is open, and every will speaks, and from whom no secret is hidden: purify the thoughts of our heart by the infusion of the Holy Spirit, so that we may deserve to love you perfectly and praise you worthily. Through Christ.

Let him add “O rose of flowers,” and if he wishes to add anything else out of devotion, let him do so.

At the first signal of the hour of the day, or also at the striking of the clock, if he has not been occupied with loftier meditations, let him say: Blessed be the name of the Lord from this time forth and for evermore. And let him add some brief little prayer formed from the ardor of his mind, in this or a similar manner:

Receive me, sweetest Lord Jesus, as a perpetual servant, so that I may always have you as my only good.

I beseech you, most merciful God, grant me to live so innocently in holy religion that I may be able to come to you, the beginning and end of every good.

Would that my ways were directed to keeping your justifications at all times.

Most loving Lord Jesus, I beseech you through the merit of your most holy Passion: teach me to contemn all things, so that I may continually deserve to find you, the highest good of the soul.

Then let the general preparation for the fervor of prayer, set down above before matins, be observed; and let the conscience be cleansed if anything has been committed against its purity. Nevertheless, if the mind has been raised in contemplation of divine love by continual desire, it will have less need of the exercises of little ones.

When the second signal sounds, both for prime and for the remaining hours of the day, let the manner set down above for matins be observed, insofar as the time allowed to each person permits him to pray. And when our cenobite enters or leaves the church, let him not neglect to show reverence to God before the altars and the crucifix, but, humbly bowing, let him say the appropriate prayers according to custom. For the modest inclination of the body is held to be a sign of the inner man well ordered toward God.
