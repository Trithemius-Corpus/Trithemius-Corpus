But if it has cast off sensual passions, if the will of man has been conformed to the divine will, the mind is raised upward through fiery love and is lifted, by anagogical motions, into the immensity of the divine darkness. No one understands the sweetness of this theology unless he receives it; no one receives it unless, from among all things, he seeks it alone through the sighs of love. The psalmist was seeking this when he prayed to the Lord: May my ways be directed to keeping your statutes at all times. For by such movements of desire the holy man was borne upward to obedience to the divine will, so that in that same Psalm 118 he said almost no verse in which he did not set down the divine word, or [law?], judgment. The exercise of this purgation begins in the second region and is completed in this one. For no small labor is required for the affection to be cleansed from the concupiscences of the world. Once the exercise of spiritual reformation has been undertaken, the mind gradually advances in love; it desires to ascend to this region by certain steps; by desire it hastens toward the invisible school of this theology; sighing with continual groans, it knocks at the door of divine mercy. But the mind is not admitted into the secret of this mystical wisdom unless, after every kind of purgation, the affection is stripped of every creature and unites itself to itself, and withdraws far from every reasoning or imagining of thinkable things. For unless it has withdrawn from every creature, it does not approach the creator, who is above all things. In this divine school of learning the Holy Spirit is believed to preside over the students; he teaches man to love nothing in this world and to conform himself in all things to the will of God. Happy is he who has deserved to be a disciple of this master; happy is he who has entered this school through the desire of love, since in mystical silence he perceives wondrous things which God hides from those who do not love.

*That this supreme region of the spirit is founded on simplicity. Article 4.*

Fourth, this most holy region of the spirit is said to be founded on simplicity, in which the mind is united to the incommutable principle, not through essence but through the love of adhesion. For, as we have already said above, that highest good, God, is one; our soul cannot approach it unless, stripped of every multiplicity that pertains to reason, imagination, and understanding, it strives to present itself wholly united.
