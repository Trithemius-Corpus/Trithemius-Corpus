Grant me, I beseech you, Lord God, to make progress in the observance of your commandments according to the good pleasure of your most holy will, and continually to fulfill the obligation of my profession, according to the rule set before me, with fervor of spirit. Through Christ the Lord.

*At private Masses. Consideration II.*

Since human nature cannot of itself receive strength for entry into the kingdom of heaven, it pleased God the most merciful Father, through the death of our Lord Jesus Christ, his only-begotten Son, mercifully to come to our aid, so that we, who because of our weakness cannot approach the invisible majesty, might have a mediator, who is God and man, approaching on our behalf. For the merit of the most holy passion of our mediator opened for us the entrance to the heavenly kingdom; it gave the nourishment of spiritual life. And as the branch draws nourishment from the vine, so our mind truly lives in Christ. But this sacrament we perceive with singular sweetness in the sacrament of the body and blood of this same mediator of ours, whenever that [unclear] of the body and blood of the Lord not only profits us for the pardon of sins, but also wonderfully aids the souls of the dead who are placed in purgatory. Therefore so noble a sacrifice must be offered with great and continual devotion, since our good works, which in themselves are very base, through this most holy oblation become most pleasing to God the Father. Therefore, so that our cenobite may be able to attend with fervor to the devotion of offering this precious sacrifice, let him note four things in order.

*Concerning the one serving at Mass.*

A devout brother who is going to serve at Mass ought to come with the greatest cheerfulness and fervor, and prepare himself most diligently to minister to the priest. For Saint Hugh says: Serve willingly at Masses, because it is the office of angels; and when you serve at that sacrifice, you make them familiar to yourself, as whose fellow-worker you stand by. If, according to the promise of the Savior, he who receives a righteous man in the name of a righteous man receives the reward of a righteous man, who will deny a reward to one who devoutly ministers to the celebrant at Mass? For whoever has been accustomed, with the fervor of divine love, devoutly to stand by in order to minister to those celebrating, divine mercy is wont to reward him, even in this life, with some special grace; and when he is dying, the holy angels are believed to come to meet him as one familiarly known, and the soul of the servant of Jesus to his sight with [joy?]
