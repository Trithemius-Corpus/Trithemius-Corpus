But when, with the understanding lifting it up, it raises itself through wonder to the knowledge of it, immediately the operation of the understanding vanishes; nor can it follow further with pure affection, which it sees can be raised above all things in no other way than alone and wholly naked. So long as the mind has not been duly purified for the Lord, it does not attain to the true darkness of this affection.

Contemplation, moreover, is sometimes achieved by an enlargement of the mind, because when the soul, by its own effort, stretches itself more vehemently in holy meditation, meditation is changed into contemplation. And this mode of contemplation is accustomed to rise by three stages: by art, through which we learn what must be done by us; by exercise, through which we carry out what has been learned; by attention, through which we fulfill our office.

Sometimes contemplation is taken up into God by an uplifting of the mind, when the mind, divinely irradiated beyond human effort, as if carried above itself by accustomed things, passes into the contemplation of divine things. This mode too is raised on high by three gradual steps of contemplation: for sometimes the understanding of a person, divinely inspired, receives something that wholly exceeds the measure of its own knowledge. The second step raises the mind above effort, when, divinely enlightened, it perceives something to which no human effort attains. In the third step the mind is raised on high, when beyond the limits of human nature and effort it is divinely illuminated in contemplation.

At times also contemplation directs itself, by the alienation of the mind, to the mountain of divine darkness, when, the memory of present things lulled to rest, the soul passes into an unknown state of human knowledge by the illumination of divine prayer. And this mode belongs especially to this highest region of the spirit, in which the mind of the true lover of God is seen to be kindled with so great a fervor of love that it is often proved to be estranged from itself. For sometimes, by the greatness of devotion, it is carried outside itself; sometimes, by exceeding wonder, it is lifted above itself; at times, on account of inexpressible exultation, it abandons itself and passes by the ray of love into the highest elevation of contemplation. But the pleasantness of this most lofty and highest region the mortal man does not understand who has not ascended to it, purified by experience.
