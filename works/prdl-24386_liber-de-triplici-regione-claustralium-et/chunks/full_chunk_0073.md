Let us therefore take care with all our strength, through the continual exercise of the middle region, to ascend to this height of the supreme summit, if we truly desire to obtain the name of spiritual conduct. In this region charity holds sovereignty, wisdom the consulship; in it nothing is loved except God, nothing disorderly is perpetrated. This most holy region, moreover, belongs to the empyrean heaven, because those whom it spiritually receives as citizens it sends on, without intermediary, to the joys of heaven. For just as the lowest region of the flesh sends its dying citizens to hell, and the middle region of reason to purgatory, so this one sends its perfect worshippers, at their departure, to heaven. The dignity of this third and supreme region is expressed by many prerogatives, and is especially commended in seven articles, as will be said in order.

*That this supreme region of the spirit of cloistered persons is pleasing by comparison. Article I.*

And first, indeed, this third region of the soul is said to be pleasing by comparison, because it is compared to the highest empyrean heaven. For just as in the empyrean heaven all things are tranquil, so on this summit of the supreme region the mind of a man contemplating God is disturbed by no agitations. Here is the highest rest of the soul that contemplates; here no crowd of vices dwells, no bitterness of mind at all approaches. In the empyrean heaven, indeed, the majesty of God is beheld by the holy spirits through an ineffable gift; here, in darkness, through the desire of love, it is seen above understanding. There God is all in all; here nothing from among all things is pleasing except God. There it is granted to the saints to rest from their labors without end; here the mind is allowed, by resting, to taste beforehand the unspeakable sweetness of inward delight. There is no happiness greater than that in heaven, and there is in the world no tranquillity so sweet as that in this region. Blessed indeed are those who have deserved to ascend this mountain of divine contemplation, who love absolutely nothing in this world, who desire nothing other than to cleave ever to God. O how secure is the conscience of these citizens, how joyful their manner of life, where nothing evil is committed, nothing other than that highest, unchangeable good is desired. For the mind purified by divine love, which has truly and perseveringly ascended this region, desires nothing carnal, nothing disorderly; it raises itself wholly into contemplation of the highest majesty through desire; it sweetly rests in God alone.
