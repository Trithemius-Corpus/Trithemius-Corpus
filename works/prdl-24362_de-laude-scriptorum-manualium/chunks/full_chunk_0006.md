Finally, if we carefully inspect the histories of time past, we shall both find and marvel with what great care the ancient and holy fathers devoted themselves to writing books, even with their own hands. For they knew the twofold usefulness of this art: that it was beneficial to themselves in many ways, for body and soul, and that it served posterity for the honorable conduct of life. Saint Pamphilus the martyr, as we have already said a little earlier, was so great a lover of this art that he copied out with his own hand the greater part of the books of Origen. If so great and worthy a man, a martyr of Christ, did not refuse to undertake the office of a scribe with such fervent desire, what shall we little worms say, who, dwelling in a monastery without care for temporal affairs, do not deign to set our hand to writings, neither by the example of the ancients nor by the command of prelates? That holy martyr will stand against us at the judgment as a witness of our sloth and laziness: we who neglect to imitate in peace what he performed with great desire in persecution. We read of Saint Ambrose, bishop of Milan, that he copied out with his own hand all the books published by him; of these I myself, lest you think them few, have now found more than a hundred. Who among us would dare to boast that he has copied out a hundred books published by others in the leisure of the monastery, when this most blessed bishop, placed in ecclesiastical care and occupied with many things, even recopied more books, which he himself had previously composed? Love of the Scriptures does not allow its devotees to be idle. But because we neglect to have this love in ourselves, we grow sluggish in inert leisure, so that things which are very easy for the studious are made difficult for us to accomplish. O how happy our order would be, brothers, if by the examples of the ancients we devoted ourselves earnestly to the study of the Scriptures. Let us look upon the ancient abbots and monks of our order, pillars of our religious life: Cassiodorus, Bede, Alcuin, Regino, Peter Damian, and the rest; with what great diligence they copied out the books of the ancients, for whom no labor seemed hard, no expenses burdensome, by which they could increase the adornment of their libraries.
