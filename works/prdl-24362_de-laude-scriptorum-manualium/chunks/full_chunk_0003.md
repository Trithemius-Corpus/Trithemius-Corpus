What is more salutary than this art, what more commendable in piety, which God loves, the angels cultivate, and all the citizens of heaven venerate? For this is what forges weapons for the faithful against heretics, casts down the proud, despoils demons by its strength, and sets a rule of living for Christians. This is what teaches the ignorant, strengthens the faint-hearted, cherishes the devout, and embraces the peaceable in love. What can be said by me worthily in praise of the writer? Whatever I may say falls below his praises, because my narrow spirit will be straitened within me before material worthy of so great an honor is lacking. I confess that it happens to me as usually happens to unskilled orators, who, terrified by the majesty of the subjects about which they had begun to speak, can neither say what they feel nor, because of shame, presume to be silent. And so it comes about that, while they fear to transgress the order and rules of speaking, they fall away from the useful purpose proposed. For just as the spirit of prophecy sometimes profitably deserts even prophets for a time, so too customary eloquence is not always present to orators. How much more easily, then, is trembling instilled in me, who am neither a rhetorician nor fit to adorn a broad subject with speech. My tongue, now dry, clings to my palate; my breath presses upon my dry breast; my sight trembles; likewise my pen; in my heart alone desire and love for the writer flourish. Therefore, when I have failed, let no one know that the subject-matter was lacking, but rather words for the speaker. Yet with Christ’s help I shall do what I can, since charity alone is the cause of our labor, which is rightly proclaimed able to do all things.

*Commendation of the usefulness of sacred Scripture.*

*Chapter 2.*

But before we pursue further the praises and usefulness of writers, it is pleasing to loosen the pen for a little in commendation of sacred Scripture, so that the brothers who are to be urged on to the study of writing may first be urged on to love of sacred knowledge. For unless the usefulness of the work is evident, persuasion to labor will not bend the mind.

Sacred Scripture, therefore, dearest brothers, is useful to us for many things; like a little boat on this great sea, it carries its lovers across to the shore without danger, while it raises minds from earthly things to heavenly ones, while it softens tribulations, while it lightens labors, while, uprooting vices, it plants virtues. For it kindles charity, increases faith, stirs devotion, and nourishes truth. Blessed is he who has loved it, provided that he has remained in its paths even to death. Better is its acquisition than the trade of gold and silver; its first and purest fruits are more precious than all riches. Knowledge of the Scriptures is more precious than all resources, and all things that are desired by human beings in this life cannot be compared with it. Length of days is in its right hand, and in its left hand are riches and glory. Its ways are beautiful ways, and all its paths are peaceful. It is a tree of life to those who have taken hold of it, and those who have held it fast are blessed. For it purifies the human heart, enlightens the understanding of its lover, and, sweetly inflaming the affection, makes it drunk. This I loved and sought out from my youth; I chose it as a bride, and became a lover of its beauty. Having fellowship with God glorifies its nobility; indeed, the Lord of all loved it.

For it is the teacher of the discipline of God and the chooser of his works. It is more beautiful than the sun, and above every arrangement of the stars; compared with light, it is found to be prior, because its radiance is never extinguished by darkness. He who loves knowledge of the Scriptures, his dwelling will be established in peace. For its conversation has no bitterness, nor does living with it have weariness, but gladness and joy. O how wonderful and sweet is the order of sayings present in the Scriptures, where the desire of the lover always increases, where satiety never produces disgust, where there is the delightful [unclear] of the righteous, where excess is not reproved, but rather frequent importunity is more praised. In this the knowledge of things pertaining to salvation is learned; in this eternal life is promised to those who believe rightly. For the Scriptures describe past things without falsehood, show present things otherwise than they appear, and announce future things before us as if from certainty.
