For what usefulness and sweetness is not contained in the holy letters, if they are read with pure intention? There is what the strong man may eat; there what the little child may suck. There history proclaims the truth of the deed; there allegory strengthens faith; there tropology forms morals; there anagogy raises the mind to the heavenly homeland. It is wholly full of virtues, if it is read with devout and pure intention. Nothing there has been set down idly; nothing fashioned without cause of mystery. All things are full of mysteries and piety. For human reason did not discover these letters; rather, heavenly power once infused them into holy men. And they are then granted to be well understood when the devout mind has believed that they proclaim true and useful things. But the carnal man, however much he may be puffed up with knowledge, cannot understand the sacred scriptures, because the true knowledge of the scriptures is the devout humility of minds.

The truly studious lover of sacred knowledge easily obtains quiet of heart, because while he is intent on the teaching of the scriptures, he is less assailed by the vanity of the world. For that blessed man truly said: Love the knowledge of the scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh! O, if the human mind did not grow lukewarm from such studies, how happy a state of the inner man would flourish. If the restless mind of mortals had no time of idleness, so easy an entrance would not lie open to the filth of vices. O, if our cloistered men now truly applied themselves to the studies of the scriptures as they once did, how useful they would be both to themselves and to the catholic Church. For the knowledge of the scriptures is what profitably sets the humble on high; enriches the poor; glorifies the despised; helps the devout; adorns prelates; separates monks from the world; purges vices; exalts by the steps of virtues. It is she indeed who forms faith, orders charity, raises hope, cultivates justice, instructs temperance, and directs prudence to the rule of piety.

Sacred scripture is the fountain of wisdom, the path of justice, the form of discipline, the storehouse of salvation, the mistress of truth. If you truly love her, you will find her; and if you remain in her company, she will delight your heart with ineffable joy, so that in comparison with her you will despise all the honors and riches of this world as a little dust. For she will teach you to overcome all the adversities of the world, to spurn the enticements of pleasures, to esteem the beauties of this life as foul things. If you have this beloved bride, you will never be ruinously sad, but always joyful, always cheerful, always advancing in the love of God. Moreover she will exalt you before God and men, and you will be admirable in the mouth of all, and your superiors will venerate you. There will be no one to contradict your words; you will have kings and princes as friends, and from remote parts of the earth they will send blessings to you. You will be glorious without ambition, and revered without fear. You will obtain an ineffable gift from her, and you will be venerable before all men. She will free you from the bond of this corrupt flesh, and will lead you before the throne of the most high King, where you will be clothed with the robe of joyful immortality. This mother is strong and very honorable; with her going before, you will pass safely through the camps of enemies. For she has the knowledge of God as her friend. Whoever you are, therefore, who love the salvation of your soul, do not depart from this mistress; do not neglect her precepts, so that you may be able to come to the love of God. To continue her friendship, an abundance of holy volumes is necessary for you. For without books you will not come to her familiarity.

*On the diligence and love of the ancients toward books.*

*Chapter 3.*

Now, therefore, by the example of the ancients, even of the gentiles, I shall try, as far as possible, to stir you to the love of books. For examples sometimes profit more than words, because when we see that others have already done what we are commanded to do, we fear less the hardness of the labor. But what is proposed as singular is judged more burdensome by the one undertaking it. Human weakness rejoices in the company of many, and what is more frequently practiced by many frightens less. Therefore, if we consider how great a diligence the ancients had in collecting books, our studies will appear as nothing, like children’s play.
