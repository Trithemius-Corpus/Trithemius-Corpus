*Concerning those who cannot write. Chapter IX.*

But if any brothers should be so untrained and dull that they know absolutely nothing of writing, we advise first of all that they learn, for nothing is so difficult that it does not yield to good will. But if they are wholly unable to learn this most sacred art, we shall give ready counsel, lest they be altogether deprived of the fruit of so holy a work.

Therefore assist the scribes in other things, if you yourselves cannot devote yourselves to writing volumes. Let one correct what another has written; let another adorn in red what the former has emended; let this one divide it with marks and figures; let that one glue and bind the codex with boards. You will fit the boards; that one the leather; let another prepare the plates for ornament. Let no service be lacking here by which the devoted scribe, relieved, may be able to continue his office. In short, many things pertain to the practice of writing, each of which individuals can prepare.

Let one cut the parchment, another clean it; let a third make it ready for the scribes by ruling lines; let another supply ink, another pens. For you always have means by which you ought to support your scribes. Nor should it displease you if a useful scribe, by the abbot’s judgment, is sometimes relieved from conventual burdens.

I am addressing you, brothers, who for some reason are hindered and cannot write; to you I have set forth these teachings with special intent. Look at the distinguished monks of our order in former times, such as the contemporaries of Bede, Rabanus, Haimo, and the other venerable abbots: with what great cheerfulness they served the scribes. For, burning with an incredible love of the Scriptures, they considered themselves blessed and happy if they had been able to help their teachers and scribes in anything. This zeal of the monks, together with holiness of morals, once magnificently exalted our venerable order; when it failed, the excellence of the order collapsed and perished. Now therefore, brothers, for the salvation of your souls and of the order, devote yourselves fervently to the sacred writings, so that with joy you may be able to reach those who laudably preceded you in this.
