*Exhortation to the Study and Love of the Scriptures*

*Chapter 16*

Now therefore, dearest brothers, receive our exhortation with goodwill, and diligently prepare yourselves for writing books of sacred doctrine. For from those things which we have set forth in commendation of writings, you perceive what you ought to think about such an office. Therefore do not neglect so great a good; do not cease from the study of writing. For writing is what in a certain way makes you immortal, and survivors even after death. Let individuals do what they will; let them glory in an office [unclear]; they quickly come to [unclear]; but your memory, from the books which you write, will pass with glory to posterity.

Nor do we say that you ought to write with this intention, since this can happen apart from intention. Far be such foolish desire from a holy work: that it should covet empty fame as the reward for labor. But if you wish to imprint your names on the books written by you, it must be done with this intention: that you aim to summon posterity either to intercession or to imitation. For with such a view you can safely and lawfully subscribe title-headings of your names to your books.

It is also not undeservedly believed that for you, O venerable writers, who write useful things from a good intention, accidental merit comes to your aid after death, whenever someone is summoned to repentance by your writing. You poor men have nothing from which you can give alms, nor do you possess anything by which you might validate a testament for your remembrance. Hear therefore my counsel, lest you depart from this life without a notable testament. Write with diligence the books and little works of the holy fathers, and you will have made a glorious testament. For if a small alms of bread and water so effectively extinguishes sin, what will so great a piety of writers not be able to do?

If he who here feeds the poor who are subject to death will be richly rewarded before God, with how great a reward must you be endowed, who feed immortal souls? For spiritual gifts are rightly preferred to carnal ones. May those hands live and rest happily, which by their labors wrote out for us so many volumes of the holy fathers. Blessed are the labors of the venerable writer, by which the mother Church of the faithful is so magnificently sustained. May their memory be with the Most High, and may the names of the writers remain written without end in the book of life. May the Lord render to them the inestimable reward of their labors, and may they never be separated from his sight. May they drink there from the fountains of the Savior, who by their offices offered us the drink of salvation. May they live, live, and live happily forever, who handed down to us such timely counsels for living well.

Make for yourselves also testaments of this kind of holiness, which will never perish through any oblivion. Write books now while you live, and after death you will gather abundant fruit. Let no labor frighten you away from writing; let no pleasure hinder you. It will conduce more to your salvation to write by renewing the volumes of the saints than to enrich the poor with perishable riches. And indeed that is not so much difficult for you as impossible. Let worldly men follow their own studies; let dissolute monks be idle; let foolish abbots take delight in horses; let some wanderers sport with the birds of heaven; let others run about with hunting dogs. You, meditating on the law of the Lord, attend to sacred letters, and by your labors make provision for the Church in the future.

Although very many thirst without measure for gold, although they join field to field, although they build houses destined to fall high into the air, know that the fame of your name rests in higher things, for whom it is not expedient to think similar thoughts about the world. You are higher than the world; do not disgracefully submit yourselves to what is lower. Strongly despise what you cannot love without fault. Write the books of the saints, and look upon them to be read with devotion, because nothing profits you so much for contempt of the world as if with love you devote study to the Scriptures.
