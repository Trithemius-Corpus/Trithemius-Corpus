*Here begins the book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot in Sponheim, On the Praise of Scribes, addressed to lord Gerlac, abbot of Deutz.*

Since we are about to discourse on the praise of scribes, we invoke the aid of him who promised to sincere scribes the brightness of eternal life as a reward. For those who instruct many unto righteousness shall shine like stars for everlasting eternities. That this must be understood not only of those who forge new things from their own vein, but also of those who transcribe old things, we shall show in what follows, the Lord granting it. For however useful the teaching of a doctor may be, without the ministry of the scribe it is by no means brought down to the knowledge of posterity. Whatever good we do, whatever we teach fruitfully, is quickly snatched into oblivion unless it is committed to letters by the zeal of scribes. Scribes, therefore, give power to words, memory to things, vigor to times. If you remove them from the Church, faith wavers, charity grows cold, hope falls away, right perishes, law is confounded, the Gospel passes into oblivion. In short, if writing fails, the people will be scattered, devotion will be extinguished, the peace of Catholic unity, confounded, will be disturbed. But without scribes, writing cannot long remain safe: it is both broken by accident and corrupted by age. For printing is a thing of paper, and in a short time it is wholly consumed. But the scribe, entrusting letters to parchment, extends both himself and what he writes into a long time. Hence he enriches the Church, preserves faith, destroys heresies, drives away vices, instructs morals, and gives increase to virtues. The devout scribe, such as we intend to describe, praises God, gladdens angels, strengthens righteous men, corrects sinners, commends the humble, preserves the good, overthrows the proud, condemns the obstinate. The scribe distinguished by piety is God’s herald, because he announces God’s will both to those present and to those to come, promising eternal life to the good, pardon to the penitent, punishment to the negligent, damnation to those who despise him.
