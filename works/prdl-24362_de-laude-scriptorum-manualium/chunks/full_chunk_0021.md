Nor do we say that teachers were lacking to those who were regarded as instructors of others. They certainly had teachers, but monks, and companions in the regular life, who instructed them no less by life than by doctrine. We too might perhaps find teachers, if we were willing to be disciples. For unless humility and love of the Scriptures agree with one another, the studies of monks neither benefit the order nor make progress.

For love of the Scriptures without humility destroys the discipline of the regular life; humility without love does not attain to knowledge of them. Therefore whoever desires to become learned in a monastery should preserve these two things inviolate. Let us imitate the holy fathers, applying themselves to the divine Scriptures with humble love; and what divine grace bestowed on them, it will not deny to us as we pray and read diligently. For the more constant each person has been in reading, the richer the fruit he receives from them.

But the fruit that we seek in the sacred Scriptures is the love of God and neighbor, and the knowledge of our most gracious Savior.

The desired end is auspiciously placed upon this little work, elegant beyond measure, by Friedberg, the Mainz printer, in the year of the Virgin birth 1493.
