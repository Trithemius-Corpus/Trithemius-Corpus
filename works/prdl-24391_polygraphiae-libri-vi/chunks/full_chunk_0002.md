## Dedication to Emperor Maximilian

*The letter of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Saint James at Würzburg, on the Polygraphia*

I was wavering, most unconquered Maximilian Caesar, with an uncertain mind, and for some little while I doubted within myself whether I would be doing something worthwhile or not if I dedicated the codex of my *Polygraphia* to Your Caesarean Majesty. For among the great you are greatest, among the more powerful most powerful, and among all wise men by far the wisest; and for that reason nothing is to be inscribed to you unless it is great, nothing dedicated unless it has been composed with the highest learning. But I, having attained no, or only little, literary learning, not without reason hesitated rather long as to whether I ought to bring trifles before so great a Majesty, since I am neither fit by talent to compose anything nor adorned with the necessary charm of expression. For I know, and cannot deny, how scanty is my equipment, how rough my talent, and that I have no vein of eloquence in speech; for which reason, also with due consideration, I held back for some time, and feared to appear before your glorious Majesty as an inept writer.

For since after God you are by right lord of the world, it is no wonder if I, unlearned and the least of monks, fear the judgment of so great a Majesty of yours, and dread to offer those compositions of mine which I know are not worthy of your hands. And when I was turning these things over in my mind, there arrived one sent by your Highness with letters, by which you summoned me to Cologne, the expenses necessary for the journey having already some time before been assigned through another. But when the letters of your Serenity had been reread, confidence soon overcame shame, and the consideration of your innate gentleness drove away fear. For I know that your disposition is most mild: since you are the most modest of all, you are accustomed to be gentle to the humble and to resist the proud. You do not know how to be vainly puffed up, nor, in the manner of tyrants, to despise the humble who are devoted to Your Highness; for just as you have no room to grow on earth, so no envy will penetrate your mind. You are the greatest, and you have also accomplished many things, and great things, no less bravely in peace and in war; by these you have attained an eternal name, and with the highest reverence you are honored, with the love and favor of all your subjects. These are the riches of princes: the love and favor of the people, and to do justice to each person according to the commandment of the love of God.

Since, therefore, all carry your name to the stars with praises and all proclaim you most gentle and most humane, there was henceforth no reason why I in particular should suspect you to be more severe, especially when in your letter you deign to entice me to be enrolled among yours. Therefore, at last encouraged by the weighing of these matters, I laid aside all fear and assumed great confidence in the gentleness innate in you. Indeed, since all draw confidence from the fountain of your piety, why should I alone be cruel [unclear]. Therefore, with that humility which befits me, and with which I ought and am able, I approach the throne of your most excellent Majesty, prostrate on the ground; devotedly I offer the said work of *Polygraphia*, and humbly commend my littleness to Your Serenity as a servant to his lord. Now therefore, most merciful Caesar, accept the very small little gift which I bring, in which may you deign to weigh my mind, most subject to you, not its value.

There are, moreover, in the appended system of the *Polygraphia*, in certain places, some mysteries wrapped in enigmas, lest the meaning of the more inward art, which lies hidden, should become manifest to the understanding of all. Just as not all things befit all persons, so not all things everywhere and always suit everyone. Therefore certain mysteries are enclosed there, which in my judgment can be more useful and more necessary to none than to princely men. For the more matters they attend to, the greater caution of secrecy must be applied in their counsels. For this book teaches princes to command secrecy over their secrets. But because Your Caesarean Majesty, occupied with many affairs, does not have time to search out the things that lie hidden, wrapped in mysteries, lest you be deprived of the fruit of my labor, I have appended a little book that extracts the kernel of the whole work, which I have entitled *The Key of the Polygraphia*; with it as guide you will more easily understand everything shut up here in figures. May God preserve you unharmed for our salvation. I wrote on the twenty-sixth day of the month of April, in the year of Christ one thousand five hundred eight.
