For the moment it did not please me to bring higher matters into the light, since an inconvenient time seems to stand in the way. I have prefixed the letters of the Latin alphabet in order to the individual words with this consideration: that the operator may have an easy method in the proposed task, and that the reciprocal party in the relation may be relieved of excessive labor. The paraphrases that are set opposite, outside the straight order of the lines, are not without a mystery of the art, since they serve the light-bearing propositions for the completion of the necessary perfection, not the obscure ones. For this reason, too, they are employed in the former and neglected in the latter. For they themselves are fixed means by which every suspicion of opposition is removed. All things have been set down according to reason, so that not even one proposition is introduced idly. For example, the farther the sun advances from the equinoctial circle, the more the days are lengthened for those who live in those regions.

Therefore, so that unity may proceed in the proper order, by means of the ternary, into the binary, the paradigm that we hand down must be observed with rather great care. And first, after he has taken prior account with intelligent consideration of his intention, let the operator observe what he is going to grant to the ternary through the binary; let him divide the day with the night by minutes, and distinguish the minute particulars of the winds, not by an alien but by the proper supposition of the phrase. In any mystical business let him diligently beware lest he overturn the established order of the art, or substitute one paraphrase for another, or grant to one what is owed to another; otherwise unity will not admit the binary, and the whole operation will be confused and useless. Let the night not be wandering through excessive length; not advancing, it will stand beyond the limits appointed to it by the art; let curiosity not change water into snow, nor let him sleep by day who keeps watch at night. Let the mind, solicitous in its ingenuity, also use art, lest the hand by its own levity commit an error which, though slight and small in the monad, is always accustomed to appear large and very great in the binary.

Only one paraphrase from any given order is admitted to the service of the secret; and, following the example of a servant, as often as the little particle of light has required it, one phrase similarly attached is taken up, not several. These things must be commended to a watchful mind, and care must be taken that the wind not be animated. After this a rule is prescribed for one approaching the practice of the art: that, as far as he can, by mystical art he imitate the single person of a man consisting of two natures, namely the soul surpassing visible things and the flesh remaining visible. For just as the soul is not seen, although it is established in a visible body, so the secret of the mind, in order that it may remain secret and nevertheless operate at will, requires the guidance of light, enduring in its workshop of darkness. Therefore, as often as the body ministers to the soul, the servant to the master, the will to reason, see that the principal virtue of the paraphrastic power in the light is preserved in its natural vigor. For by this instruction, the first of the Peripatetics will duly enter into the second, and the monad into the binary.

Likewise, consequently, we warn the one who wishes it that, as often as the leaf is moved by the wind, he should discern the property of each nature, compare the very night of [unclear] by degrees and minutes, and conform the light-darknesses. But if Phoebus should prejudice the night, or the night the sun, through which a confusion of eclipse would arise, either let unity resume the binary which it sent away in the light, or let the shadow of night be brought back to the first line of the sun. Keep fixed in memory whatever has anywhere been produced in number, weight, and measure. Therefore, when you have encountered the knot of the difficulty mentioned, count the minute particulars of the night, and also mark the paraphrases of the daylight, and so [unclear] the one from [unclear], so that the necessary proportion may follow the action. Beware lest, while the eclipse of the sun lasts, you interrupt the necessary labor.

No less attend to this: that you be mindful of the ancient fathers, Thuysco, who, the grandson of Noah, is the father of the Teutons, and Marcomer, who is read to have first led the people of the Franks from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine. For the Franks are brothers of the Greeks and intermixed with the Germans, once making two languages from one. Hence, according to the oracle g.g., those who have lost one g. receive [unclear]. Finally, diligent investigator of Polygraphy, you are warned that, after you, eager for these discoveries, have attained the true method of operating, you should guard everything prudently and not pour it out everywhere into the knowledge of all men, especially filthy men. For this reason do not ask, since you know that there is nothing in the world so good and holy that the wicked do not rather often abuse it.
