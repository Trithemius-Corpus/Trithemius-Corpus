*Book I.*

We rejoice that the end of the first book of our Polygraphy is at hand; made more ready for what remains, we judge that we must attempt what the powers of our talent may be. For newly invented things sometimes trouble the beginner, because he whom all follow has no one going before him. The first beginning of any invention is more difficult; after it has been made firm, it brings greater confidence to the author. Therefore, completing the first book of our invention with God’s favor and help, we have divided the whole into three hundred eighty-four small parts, placing beneath each, in order as is seen, the proper degrees of paraphrases. And these form a sufficiently beautiful Christian harmony, sixteen varied stations, with more than two hundred nine thousand phrases duly joined together in every respect. Nothing is lacking here for students of these inventions of ours, so that they may have the fullest knowledge and capacity in mystical, secret, and hidden matters, even those to be reported more remotely with the greatest safety.
