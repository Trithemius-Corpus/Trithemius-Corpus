What, at the beginning of the third book of our work, the Polygraphia, we promised, namely to provide for the sake of the semblance of a [unclear] gloss before the inexperienced, we have fulfilled sufficiently, by a concise labor, as an example. We have entrusted the night, safely serving hidden things, to be guided by the shadows of periphrases; we have inserted numbers in the order of four lines all the way to the end, adapted for the use of those who wish to employ them. The places of minutes, having fixed degrees, are three thousand one hundred and fifty, although, if you wish, infinite moments of hours remain for concealing, in every language of every nation of the world, whatever secret matter may arise. The circle, arising linearly from a point, as far as you wish and as the work requires, you will be able to lead back to the path by which you came. If much of the night remains, lead back the dragon's tail to the original tail-place at the head. But the binary is made altogether certain from the known signs of unity. Let him who rages greatly labor greatly, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is there need of great labor to lead wherever you wish that which proceeds of its own accord. Here, therefore, we set an end to the third book of our Polygraphia, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell.

From Peapoli, on the seventh day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ our Savior 1508, in the eleventh indiction.

*The Fourth Book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James the Greater, apostle, at Wuerzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, begins.*

The order of reasoning demanded that our fourth book of the Polygraphia be made unlike the preceding ones in quality, invention, and facility, so that those wishing to share in secret matters may attain this not by one method but by various and different ones. We have extended a system easily comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that the night, always connected through the minute to the monad in whatever order, may be recognized in the light of day.
