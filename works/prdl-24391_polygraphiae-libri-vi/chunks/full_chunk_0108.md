*563. The Second Figure of the Expansion of the Straight Table.*

[expansion grid of letter-pairs, as printed]

In this second figure of the expansion of the reverse table the alphabets are six in number, in the first of which k, in the third i, in the fifth h, are not changed. And since the norm has so given it, it makes no difference; for in so great a multitude of variety no one, even the most expert, will be able to weigh out the constancy of a single mark or character.

*564. The Third Figure of the Expansion of the Reverse Table.*

[expansion grid, as printed]

In this third figure of the expansion of the reverse table there is nothing new of its kind except the metathesis, which is common to all in degrees almost infinite. Six alphabets are transposed. In the first t is eclipsed, in the same g, in the third s, and c, r, and e—the cause of which thing the norm teaches.

Continuation of the table.

*565. The Fourth Figure of the Expansion of the Reverse Table.*

[expansion grid, as printed]

In this fourth figure of the expansion of the table several errors of the defect of letters concur according to the canonical reckoning; which, since they can easily be emended, I did not wish to depart from the norm of the system. In the first q and d, in the fifth c, in the fifth also b, are not changed.

Continuation of the table.

*566. The Fifth Figure of the Expansion of the Reverse Table.*

[expansion grid, as printed]

In this fifth figure of the expansion of the reverse table there are only two alphabets—the first in order—looking upon it[self], in the number of which a is eclipsed in the first. But the remaining four [are left] for forging new ones at will.
