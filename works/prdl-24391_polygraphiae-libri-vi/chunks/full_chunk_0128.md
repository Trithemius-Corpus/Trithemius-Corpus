After Gabriel, Michael, spirit of the Sun, again assumed the fourteenth rule of the world; it began on the first day of the month of May, in the year of the world 4606, in the fourth month, and he ruled the world in his order for 354 years, until the year 4960 from the creation of the world, and 8 months. In the time of this governance Evil-Merodach, king of Babylon, restored liberty and a king to the people of the Hebrews, under the direction of Michael, who, Daniel writes, stood for the people of the Jews, to whom they had been committed by God. Likewise in these times the monarchy of the kingdom of the Persians began; its first king was Darius, and the second Cyrus, who utterly destroyed that most powerful kingdom of Babylon in the days of Belshazzar, as Daniel and the prophets had foretold. In these times the Cumaean Sibyl flourished; she offered nine books to King Tarquinius Priscus to be bought for a price, in which was contained the sequence of future warnings and the rationale of the whole commonwealth of the Romans. But when the king refused the price, the Sibyl burned the first three books in his sight; for the remaining six she asked the same price. When he again refused it, she also handed three others over to the fire to be burned,

which she would have done with the last ones as well, had not the king, persuaded by the counsel of his men, redeemed them from destruction by paying the price that had been demanded for all. Likewise, after abolishing kings, the Romans established two consuls for each year. Phalaris the tyrant occupied Sicily in those times. Magic too at those times was held in great esteem among the kings of the Persians. Pythagoras the philosopher and many others among the Greeks then flourished. The temple and city at Jerusalem were rebuilt. Ezra the prophet restored from memory, using it as his archetype, the books of Moses burned by the Chaldeans, who were also called Babylonians. Xerxes, king of the Persians, led an army against the Greeks, but accomplished little. Rome was taken, burned, and destroyed by the Gauls, except only the Capitol, guarded by a goose, which roused the weary fighters. The very famous wars of the Athenians also took place in those times. Socrates and Plato the philosophers lived at that time. The Romans, again abolishing the consuls, established tribunes and aediles, and in these times were involved in many calamities. Alexander the Great also, after the governance of Michael had been completed, reigned in Macedonia. He destroyed the monarchy of the Persians in Darius, and subjected all Asia, with part of Europe, to his rule. He lived 125 years and five months; after his death endless wars and many evils followed, and the monarchy was divided into four. Among the Hebrews contention began over the high priesthood.
