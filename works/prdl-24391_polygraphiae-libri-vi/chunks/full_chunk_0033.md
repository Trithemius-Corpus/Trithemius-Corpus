Such is catoptrics, which teaches how to present visible signs of things to the sight in an instant, by means of certain images projected far from us outside mirrors into the air, devised to signify some speech; these are easily understood by one who has knowledge of the signs. But in what manner it can be brought about that all things are transferred to the use of signs is not the subject of this undertaking, but belongs to the *Steganographia*, the contents of which work the most learned man Johannes Trithemius sets out in a certain letter to Bostius in these words, etc.

I now have in hand a great work which, if it is ever published, which may it not be, the whole world will marvel at; the title of its first book is *Steganographia*. There will be four books, and each will be divided into at least one hundred chapters. I began this work at the urging of a certain great prince, whom there is no need to name. You ask what this work teaches: things astounding and incredible to all who do not know them, which have never been heard since the beginning of the world. For the first book contains more than one hundred methods of writing secretly whatever you wish, without any suspicion, without transposition of letters, without any fear, so that there is no man in the world who by natural skill could know, or even suspect, what is contained in my letters, except the one who knows the art from me, or from him whom I have taught. The words are plain and familiar, free from every suspicion; but [no one?] will ever be able to perceive my intention without the art, however learned he may be. It is a truly astonishing thing. The second book will contain many more wonderful things. By fire, namely, in this art I can make the conception of my mind known to one who knows my art at any distance whatever, at one hundred miles or more, safely, without words, without writings, without signs, through any messenger whatever. If he is seized on the road, if he is questioned even under the harshest tortures, he cannot confess anything about my message, because absolutely nothing is known to him about it. Whatever may happen, my message will always remain hidden, nor could all the men of the whole world, if they were gathered together, trace it out by natural power. I can also do this without a messenger whenever I wish. I can also make my will known to one who knows the art, even if he is silent in prison, though I am far absent, however closely he is guarded, even if he were sitting three thousand [miles?] under the earth. And all these things, most broadly and universally, whenever and as often as I wish, I can do naturally, without any superstition or aid of any spirits whatsoever. I admit they are wonderful, but hear things still more wonderful. The third book teaches an art by which I can teach an unlearned man who knows only his mother tongue, who has never known a word of the Latin language, in two hours to write, read, and understand Latin quite elegantly and eloquently, as much as he wishes, so that whoever sees his letters will praise the words and understand that they are composed in Latin. The fourth book contains many astonishing experiments, but purely natural ones. Namely, I can make my mind known to one who knows my art while eating or sitting with others, without words, without nods, as extensively as I wish, even while speaking, preaching, playing on the organ, or singing, without hindering any other action whatever, so that while preaching good things to one who knows, with eyes closed, [unclear]. The fourth book will contain many other secrets which are not to be brought out in public. All who hear these things marvel at them; among them are many noble and very learned men, who think that these things are either impossible or wholly supernatural. To them and to you I answer what I have answered many: that many things are possible naturally which, to those who do not know the powers of nature, seem impossible or even supernatural.
