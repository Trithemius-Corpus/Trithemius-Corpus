To these the same author immediately adds: From the very computation that I have set out above, a certain manual speech can be practiced, both for exercising the wit and for playing a game; by it, when the letters are expressed one by one, a man may convey to another, who also knows this technique, even if he is stationed far away, the words contained in those letters, to be read and understood; or he may, through these signs, indicate necessary matters more secretly, or mock the unskilled as though by divination. The order of this game or speech is as follows. When you wish to indicate the first letter of the alphabet, hold one with the hand; when the second, two; when the third, three; and so the rest in order. For example, if you desire to warn a friend placed among ambushers to act cautiously, show three, one, twenty, nineteen, five fingers; for the letters of this order signify “to act cautiously.”

Thus far the example of dactylogy according to Bede, which is varied in many other ways. For, as was also said before, let the sign of unity be taken to signify the letter A, and so thereafter in order. Let there be assigned on the left hand: one on the thumb, two on the index finger, three on the middle finger, four on the ring finger, or the third from the thumb, and five on the little finger. Likewise on the right hand there will be understood: two on the thumb, three on the index finger, four on the middle finger, five on the third from the thumb, and six on the little finger. With these things so established, let us observe which numbers arise from the addition, multiplication, or division of which numbers among themselves. For example, twelve arises from the addition of one to eleven, of two to ten, of three to nine, of four to eight, of six to six, of seven to five. In multiplication, however, twelve is produced by the leading of four into three, or of two into six. In division we have twelve if twenty-four is divided by two.

Furthermore, when the fingers are merely raised, it signifies addition; but when we touch one with another, multiplication. But if we bend one and raise the other, it must be division. Therefore, wishing to show the letter M, which occupies the twelfth place, I indicate the number twelve, either by addition, or by multiplication, or by division. First, by raising the two little fingers of both hands together with the thumb of the left hand; for the thumb signifies one, while one of the other fingers designates five, the other six, and this number shows the letter M. By multiplication in this way: let us touch the third finger from the thumb of the left hand with the index finger of the right, since three multiplied by four makes twelve; or let us touch the little finger of the right hand with the index finger of the left, which is the same thing, for twice six gives twelve. It could also be done in another way, namely by the contact of the index finger of the right hand and the middle finger of the left, which show six. These again, by the contact of both thumbs, or of the index finger of the right hand with the thumb of the left, signify multiplication of the prior number by two; but twice six makes twelve.

By division thus: first we shall indicate twenty-four, the division of which number by two yields twelve. The gesture about which I was speaking above represents division by two, namely if two thumbs are shown, one raised and the other bent; likewise the thumb and little finger of the left hand, and the index finger of the right bent. For they signify the partition of six by three, that is, two; and twenty-four divided by these shows twelve.

So much for dactylogy, or the speech of the fingers, which can be handled otherwise in infinite ways, by assigning to fixed places vowels, consonants, semivowels, double letters, mutes, and liquids, according as it has seemed good to each person to handle such things. Although these matters appear ridiculous, nevertheless they have very great use, if by a certain analogy they are transferred to other matters.
