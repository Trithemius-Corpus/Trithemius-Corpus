You will complete this second sentence on the second membrane, in such a way that the words of the sentence seem to cohere; on the third also you will fit it so that, with the first letters unharmed, the whole sentence, the number and size of the words, may stand firm and retain a certain neatness. When this has been done, place the exemplar which you cut out over a membrane of equal size, and at the ends of the openings mark the very finest points as far as the letters extend which you desire to write. Then take the third membrane, and write the letter in continuous words, with proper order and size of spaces, and also of letters, so that the first sentence and its words are contained within the limits of the points. Therefore no suspicion of trickery then remains: the other person, when he receives it with the exemplar laid over it, immediately understands the meaning of your mind; nor can anything equal to this device, though it is no slight labor, be found for communicating one’s meaning to one’s friends in dangerous times.

*Marcus Tullius.* The meaning is that Cicero devised individual particular characters for individual Latin words; with these, if necessity demanded, he wrote an oration however lengthy with very few signs and very secretly. And in this way, they report, Cato’s speech, that the conspirators should be punished, was preserved and committed to writing, when Cicero as consul appointed very swift writers to capture spoken words by notes of this kind. That the ancients also followed a certain similar method for writing words quickly and concisely, so that often a single letter signified a word, is attested by Valerius Probus, Petrus Crinitus, Angelus Decembrius, and also by the very ancient monuments most diligently collected by Petrus Apianus and Bartholomaeus Amantius. But this artifice of writing will be treated more carefully hereafter.

*Our Bede in England.* On reckoning and speech by the fingers. This invention is found in Bede, concerning the divisions of times. When you say one, bending the little finger on the left hand, you will set the joint in the middle of the palm. When you say two, you will place there the second from the little finger, bent. When you say three, you will likewise bend down the third. When you say four, you will likewise raise the little finger similarly. When you say six, you will nevertheless lift the third, with only the middle finger, which is called the ring finger, fixed in the middle of the palm. When you say seven, you will place only the little finger, the others being lifted, upon the root of the palm. Next to it, when you say eight, the ring finger. When you say ten, you will set the nail of the index finger in the middle joint of the thumb. When you say twenty, you will put the tip of the thumb between the middle joints of the index and middle fingers. When you say thirty, you will join the nails of the index finger and thumb in a gentle embrace. When you say forty, you will draw the inner parts of the thumb over the side or back of the index finger, both only being upright. When you say fifty, you will bend the thumb, curved like the Greek letter called [unclear], toward the palm. When you say sixty, you will carefully gird from the front the thumb, bent around as above. When you say seventy, you will fill the index finger, bent around as above, with the thumb inserted, with only its nail standing upright across the middle joint of the index finger. When you say eighty, you will fill the index finger, bent around as above, with the thumb stretched lengthwise, namely with its nail fixed in the middle joint of the index finger. When you say ninety, you will fix the little nail of the bent index finger into the root of the thumb; thus far on the left hand. But one hundred you will make on the right hand as you make ten on the left; two hundred on the right as twenty on the left. In the same way also the rest up to nine hundred. Likewise one thousand on the right as two on the left, and the rest up to nine thousand. Two thousand on the right as three on the left. When you say the rest, you will place the left hand highest on the middle of the breast, with only the fingers raised toward the neck. When you say twenty thousand, you will place the same hand spread broadly over the breast. When you say thirty thousand, with the same hand prone, but raised, you will put the thumb into the cartilage of the middle of the breast. When you say fifty thousand, with the same hand prone but raised, you will place the thumb on the navel. When you say sixty thousand, with the same hand prone, you will grasp the left thigh from above. When you say seventy thousand, you will place it upon the thigh.
