This method of writing, too, is tortuous and is subject to no regular norm, since it can be varied and changed at the writer’s pleasure by a chance transposition as much as anyone wishes.

Next it should be noted that at the end of the sixth book of the *Polygraphy* we have handed down another method of writing by numerical signs, which they commonly call ciphers; under its covering you may safely write to an absent friend whatever you wish, and no one unskilled in the art will be able either to discover or to suspect that a secret lies hidden, especially if the rules we have given on these matters are observed with diligent caution. This is that the sheet be formed in the manner of an astronomical table, as though it contained something pertaining to the reckoning of the stars, namely in such a way that characters of the signs or of the planets are interposed, so that anyone into whose hands it should perhaps fall by chance, led on by its table-like appearance, may think that no secret lies hidden in it, but may suppose that everything pertains to some computation of astronomy.

We have arranged twelve alphabets according to the number twenty-four, assigning them in place of letters; the first of these, that of Aries, begins from one and ends at twenty-four. We advise, however, in great

and difficult matters, that one should beware of beginning from one, because that method of writing can easily be suspect for that very reason. But whoever wishes prudently to use his own natural diligence will find subtler things from ours, and what we have handed down not very polished he will easily make better. Great secrets lie hidden in the numerical scheme; if anyone properly understands it, he will penetrate more mysteries of numbers. For we had neither time nor reason on this occasion to pour everything out publicly, but in our judgment what we have done will be enough for the instruction of those to whom the inventions will some day be pleasing: so that they may have several methods not only of writing secretly whatever they wish to absent sharers in the art, but also one method of producing several better and subtler ones, according to each person’s capacity of understanding.

Likewise, since the schemes of the numbers are sufficiently open and clear in their own places, and their tables unfolded at the same time, it was not necessary here to repeat them together, lest excessive length produce an example. But to set things down more secretly by figures of numbers I did not think necessary, since there is no difficulty in knowing the method among all who possess even moderate intelligence. With similar ease the very ancient signs of numbering which we have brought back to light can be understood and comprehended; through them knowledge of numbers is extended to infinity. For whenever a small stroke is placed above a smaller number, a thousandfold number is designated. For X̄ means ten thousand; C̄, one hundred thousand; M̄, one thousand thousands; X̄, ten times one thousand thousands; C̄, one hundred times one thousand thousands; M̄, one thousand times one thousand thousands; and so consequently as far and as much as you wish to advance in counting, without limit.

*End of the Key to the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of [Paeapolis?], to Maximilian I, Caesar.*
