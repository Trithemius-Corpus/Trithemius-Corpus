*How we can safely write any secrets whatever, both with numbers and in many other varied ways.*

These things are childish, I admit, since anyone, according to his own capacity and judgment, can devise many alphabets more elegant than those we have recorded in this volume. For that reason we knowingly omit many things which, by the same reasoning, we judge to be childish, common, and of little use. I fear that someday I shall be ashamed to have directed my mind to these trifles, while more useful matters were left aside. But let the readers spare me, I pray: my own will indeed drew me back from these childish studies, not to say at least in part vain ones, but the insistence of certain friends compelled me to write them; to them I neither could nor should deny this without injury to the bond contracted between us. Nevertheless, induced by their entreaty, I also had to connect the following chapter with what was prescribed above: to set numbers in place of letters. For the ancients established various and manifold methods of writing by means of numerical figures, by which, through subtle invention, they safely transmitted their secrets to one another. And so, persuaded by the prayers of my friends, we have placed, at the end of this sixth book, a few methods out of many, by which a path may lie open for the ignorant toward knowledge of the others. For if anyone takes pleasure in knowing these things, he will easily be able, from those once known, to climb to higher things, and to vary the methods discovered by manifold interchange according to his own judgment. As a beginning for this matter we have placed a table under the orchema, which, when the reading is varied in the manner of a caterpillar, will contain in itself ninety-six alphabets.
