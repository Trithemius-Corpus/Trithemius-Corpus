We have arranged the fabricated words so that they contain nothing in themselves, but merely represent the letters of the alphabet placed before them; through them you will be able to write and make known all the secrets of your mind to a friend, however far absent, with the greatest security. But those unskilled in and ignorant of the art, when they see the writing, will think it is some foreign language, and will be wholly ignorant of what it contains. But when you wish to write or report something to an absent person by the method of this book, take care first of all that you properly distinguish each and every word from the others, so that each can be very clearly discerned from another, lest the person to whom you write take one for two, or two for one, because that would produce a great error. Let us set down an example of this matter: Abrin madon badis, cadulur pasu, adin loroff masara damis, boda omo dromidrus vares mastron phosat, galos, prosol fane lemoros, aro soca bilneso, hamol heliet rhamil festa doron hubor rodex. In this narration of an invented discourse there is contained this brief secret sentence of a friend warning a friend about danger: Do not come to court, because the king is angry with you. You see how easy, safe, and secure this method is of writing and reporting whatever you wish to a friend who understands it. In all things, however, you must know that care is to be taken, as was said before, that you take no more than one word from each alphabet, and again that you skip no alphabet.

*Of Book IV*

Whenever you have brought the narration of the invented discourse to the end of the book, if the secret of your mind has not yet been completed, begin again from the beginning, and do this as often as will be necessary. Nevertheless, each person will be able at his own discretion to change these words of ours, improve them, and add more, provided, however, that you avoid a consonance that breaks out disgracefully into sounds.

*Explanation of the Fourth Book of Our Polygraphy*

In the fourth book of the Polygraphy we have placed words fabricated by us in alphabetical order by a similar method, but in one point alone we deliberately differ from the preceding book. For in the preceding book the words contain nothing else of any proper meaning in themselves, except that they represent the letters placed before them at the head. But in the fourth book, which we now have in hand, the individual words designate the letters placed before them. They have within what they display without, because the second letter of each word always serves the hidden mystery, as we shall make clear in the following example. Ecorach hanasar dulay semalas, chorion suriel anephor scabaras busarat vimalo tropen tumelech amasar. For understanding this mystery, take only the second letter from each word, and you will find this hidden meaning: Beware this man. For the second letter in Ecorach is C, in hanasar A, in dulay V, in semalas E; join these four letters and they will make this word: Beware. And so let one proceed in the rest as far as the end of the book. If any hidden mystery remains to be written, one must return to the beginning as often as necessary, until the secret has been completed in full. In this very secret method of writing, if someone has been trained by long practice, he does not need the book, but will readily compose on the spot words suitable for his purpose. But one who is less practiced in composing necessarily requires a copy of the book from which to write. A copy of the volume will not be necessary for the reader, since without the book he can easily find the hidden secret everywhere through the second letters. Yet in great, difficult, or dangerous matters, this method of writing would not be trusted as sufficiently safe, because the skill of an experienced man could by chance find out the hidden mystery. But this method of writing can be changed by an intelligent man, so that the signifying letters are placed in the third or fourth position of the word, with the words altered.
