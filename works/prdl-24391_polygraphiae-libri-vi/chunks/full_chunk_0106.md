*554. Polygraphy, Book V.*

…[such] a lapse that you yourself can no longer read what you had written. For the transposition of letters is possible in as many ways as—according to the estimation of the human intellect, which, enclosed in a mortal body by temporal operation, does not properly grasp the infinite—a mortal but innumerable number rises up. Whence whoever shall wish to have more modes of writing by metathesis, and ones remote from our system, if he be but a little ingenious and diligent, will easily achieve the effect in his own working. For the talent of man can [accomplish] the greatest things, if it be aided by study and art. But he who spurns the least things will never attain to the greater. I know there will be very many who will deride these our studies as vain and childish; to whom we give for answer that we shall be entirely content, provided they please those for whom they were composed.

There follows the Straight Table.

*555. The Straight Table of Transposition.*

[transposition grid, as printed]

In this canonical or straight table of letters you have, from our one and usual [alphabet] of Latin letters themselves, by their permutation or transposition, as many alphabets as there are monograms throughout it—namely four-and-twenty times twenty-four, which make in number 576; and multiplied by as many again, they yield a little less than fourteen thousand.

*556. The Reverse Table of Transposition.*

[reverse transposition grid, as printed]

In this reverse table you have, by the transposition of letters, as many alphabets as we marked in the preceding straight [table]. For, as you see, under the metathesis there are 24 to the right; to the left likewise as many; downward 23; and upward 23. Which, multiplied 24 times by 93, reduced in the form of a cross to the four sides, make alphabets not fewer than 112.

There follows the same table expanded.

*557. The First Figure of the Expansion of the Straight Table.*

[expansion grid, as printed]

This is the first figure of the expansion of the straight table, having five alphabets by metathesis, with the letters transposed as is seen. For in the first alphabet, for a is taken b, for b is taken e, and so on consequently, as appears more clearly to one looking. In the second alphabet, for a let c be taken. In the third, d; in the fourth, e—as the placement of the order teaches.

Let the expansion be continued.
