*J. Trithemius.*

For the presidents of the annual chapter concerning the Bursfeld observance, he abridged the statutes of that same union, one book. The Spiritual Exercise of Monks, one book. An Epitome or Breviary of the same, one book. On Ecclesiastical Computation, one book. On the Illustrious Writers of Germany, addressed to Jakob Wimpfeling of [unclear], one book. Spanheim letters to various persons, four books. Orations delivered in chapters and elsewhere are twenty-four in number, making one book. For his brothers of the monastery of Spanheim he wrote forty very beautiful sermons, one book. Sermons to the people on feast days, one book. Several supplicatory prayers to God and the saints, one book. From the Greek tongue he translated into Latin Maximus the Abbot on the incarnation of the Lord; also the anathemas of St. Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria, promulgated against heretics in the third synod of Ephesus, and certain letters of that same synod. He also wrote for the edification of monks a notable work, which he entitled On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons, three books. He wrote also certain other smaller works, the titles of which he did not send on to me. These, dearest brother Nicholas, are the Trithemian works which, during the twenty-three years in which he was abbot, he composed at Spanheim for the honor of God, amid cares and the continual occupation of domestic business, reading or writing day and night whenever he was able to steal some leisure for himself.

*Lucubrations II.*

During the years in which he was in charge he brought into the library of Spanheim a very great treasury of books in every field, not only through the art of printing but also through manuscripts. For when I was with him for the space of one year, to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, I counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eighty had been decently written with the pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his mouth that he had spent more than two thousand florins for books, which he had not received from the substance of the monastery, which was always very meager; but since he was both known and dear to many princes, the gifts with which he was honored by them he very often spent on books. For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books beyond all whom I know living in the flesh today. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot in Spanheim, has alienated very many volumes.

In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had arisen between the interregnum and Philip, Count Palatine, Trithemius, fearing the enemies, withdrew with his library and the monastery’s possessions to Kreuznach, the nearest town, and I likewise returned to my own affairs. In the year 1506, in the month of October, Trithemius himself, spurred by the envy of certain persons, accepted the abbacy of St. James at Würzburg and resigned that of Spanheim. There, in quiet, he writes various works, the titles of which, when asked, he recently sent me as follows.

*Of Trithemius.*

To the emperor Maximilian he published a remarkable work, which he entitled Polygraphy, six books. To the same man, the key of that same Polygraphy, one book. Replies to eight questions of the same emperor, one book. To the same man, On the Seven Intelligences Moving the Spheres, one book. To Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg and prince-elector, twenty books of various questions, orations, and propositions. To the same man, On Prognostications Permitted and Not Permitted to a Christian, two books. To the same man he wrote that remarkable and laborious work of Steganography, eight books, which that rash Bouillus, not understanding it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely. To the same prince, one book of letters. To the same man, Against Sorcerers and All Arts and Superstitions Forbidden by the Church, five books. To the same man, On the Causes and Cure of the Falling Sickness and of Sorcerers, three books. To Jakob Trithemius, his brother, an Itinerary of His Life, two books. To Burkhard von Horneck, physician, On the Peculium of Nuns, one book. He wrote a History or Annals on the Origin, Kings, and Deeds of the People of the Franks and Germans, three large volumes; he brought the narrative from the incarnation of the Lord, in the year 433, from the borders of Scythia into Germany at the mouths of the Rhine, down to the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth year of the emperor Maximilian.
