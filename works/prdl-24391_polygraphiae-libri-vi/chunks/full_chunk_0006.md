*Preface of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of St James at Würzburg, to the Polygraphia, addressed to Maximilian I, Caesar.*

We read that very many wise men of the ancients, philosophers, kings, and princes once devised various and manifold methods by which they might safely entrust to their messengers, when they were sent to more distant places, the secrets of counsel, and whatever of secret mystery occurred that had to be confided, so that they might become fit for carrying them out and secure from every assault of the wicked, lest either the simplicity of the cabalists should lay bare the mystery, or the inborn curiosity of the Bacucei should bring harm to apostolic matters. Great among these, Augustus Caesar of the Romans, wishing in difficult affairs to use the service of messengers in obscurity, changed the breathings of conceived speech from clear forms into dark ones; and by the dance of metathesis, with a fine invention, he made their barbarousness, though resisting his wishes, accord at his command. And that receptive and, as it were, perpetual order is nevertheless subject to will and to the reckoning of time; although it keeps messengers safe on the road from the assault of the Bacucei, still, since it does not keep the regulated bounds of itself, it will make all who look on anxious with suspicion of the matter.

Archimedes of Syracuse, easily the chief mathematician, fastened round about a white bird suited to his designs, bare about the head and feet, to a staff cut back into the form of a tetragon; and when the servants had been arranged in due proportion, the work of the white bird appeared changed. When this had been duly performed, he loosed with his own hand what had been fastened; and when he had allowed it to fly away, there suddenly arose among the familiars of the inner chambers a great separation of appearances, and soon there arose a monster of a tortuous monastic image, formerly far too wandering, at the sight of which no one could think what it was. Nor did the dissension of the owls come to rest before the image of the master had skillfully bound the magpie again with a similar tetragon. Marcus Tullius Cicero, the eloquent orator, using talent and art, changed the minutiae of phrase into signs, and put the diligence, or character, of his will in place of the customary phrase. In this very novelty, indeed, he used such abundance that his invention could suffice for almost all things in the world. But upon these heralds of mysteries the institution of the art bestowed a wonderful agility, so that, beyond the nature of all, with solid steps of the feet, and not by minutiae or moments, but by whole degrees, they serve the good will of the master. A king can always use their service most safely, although not without great labor and manifest suspicion on the part of the Bacucei. Pharamund also, the forty-third after Marcomer, most powerful king of the Franks in Germany, when in the four hundred twenty-fourth year of the Lord's nativity he was forcefully entering the borders of the Gauls, in order that all things might be done safely, bound foreign minutiae to his secrets.

Chlodio Crinitus too, Pharamund's elder son, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, after he had violently subjected all Gaul to his rule, commanded that all the Gauls, as conquered men and slaves, be shorn and their beards shaved, but ordered the Franks, conquerors and lords of the Gauls, to grow their hair and beard; whence he was also called Crinitus. In imitation of his father, he wrapped the mysteries of counsel in signs both proper and foreign.

Charles, surnamed the Great, the sixty-third king of the Franks after Marcomer, who was the first of the Franks to rule the Romans, with Nanno the grammarian as his teacher, wishing to adorn the German language, which was his paternal tongue, with grammarians after the manner of Latin, began an arduous work, which, however, involved in many affairs and overtaken by death, he did not complete. He bound owls with varied and many minutiae; greater than whom Brabant, or Namur in Germany, had not produced. To each prefect of the kingdom he gave single owls by art.
