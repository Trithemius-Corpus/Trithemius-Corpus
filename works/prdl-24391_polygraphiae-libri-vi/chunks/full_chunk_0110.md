Behold, reader, you have the method by which every number capable of being written can sufficiently be marked and written with a few letters; their extension, as you see, proceeds to infinity. But, as it was once customary to say in a proverb, “the weary ox plants his foot firmly”: whoever wishes to be content with former things should follow the ancient method of numbering. So that those who do not know it may understand it more easily, it will not be tedious for me to give, in what follows, the form which Hunibald, historian of the Franks, and also Bede, Rabanus, the monks Helpericus, and several of the ancients used in chronicles and ecclesiastical calculations, as follows.

We have set out thus far, by way of example, the method of the ancient Germans and Franks of writing numbers with Latin letters, so that we might also provide knowledge of it to those who do not know it. But the fact that, in the placement of the figures of the algorism, we have departed from the common rule in computation was done not from error but for a reasonable cause, namely so that we should not be burdened in an excessive progression.
