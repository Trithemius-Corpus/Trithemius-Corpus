*Here begins the sixth book of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Saint James the Greater the Apostle at Würzburg.*

Before every invention and use of letters, in the beginning of the world, when human beings were still rude in intellect and uncultivated in morals, under the rule, shared by alternation, of three intelligences, as certain persons think, succeeding one another in order through one thousand sixty-three years, the influences of the planets in the sphere are said to have presided in their own way over bodies on earth by their influence, chiefly under the governance of their intelligence, whom they called Orisiel, that is, the life-giver of God, for three hundred fifty-four lunar years, likewise four months, and as many days and hours. These things we recount as poetic, not as affirming them.

After Saturn, Venus is set down as having assumed dominion; after Venus, Jupiter. But after Jupiter, Mercury, the fourth ruler in order, adorned human talents with various arts. Under the three intelligences already mentioned, namely while Saturn, Venus, and Jupiter were ruling, they assert that human beings did not have the use or knowledge of letters. Hence, whenever one person wished to announce something secret to another, he did it not by writings, since the function of letters was lacking, but symbolically, by certain likenesses of animals, trees, plants, or of some other sensible things, just as they had agreed among themselves.

The time of Saturn, as they say, gave to human beings morals properly suited to his influence. For it made them farmers, hunters, founders of cities, rude, woodland-dwelling, gloomy, simple, uncultivated in life and morals, living for a long time in the flesh. After Orisiel of Saturn, as those who hold this opinion say, the intelligence Anael, mover of Venus, receiving the government of the world by influence, continued it for three hundred fifty-four years, and for four months, days, and hours; under him human beings began to be more cultivated, to build houses and cities more frequently, and also to discover various manual arts, namely weaving, wool-working, and many other things similar to these. They also began to flow away much more into pleasures of the flesh, and to take beautiful wives, and, many children having been begotten by them from these wives, they notably fell away from natural simplicity. They invented games and songs, began to play the cithara, and more eagerly devised whatever they affirm to pertain to the influence of Venus, following the motion of the flesh.

They named the third intelligence of Jupiter Zachariel; when it received dominion of the world after Anael, it made human beings restless with desire for ruling, to carry on hunts, to make tents, and to adorn themselves with various garments. While he was ruling, in accordance with the quality of Jupiter, who deprived his father of the kingdom, a great division arose between the good and the bad: the former calling upon God, but the latter given over to the flesh and to the world. Under his rule Adam, the first man, died, leaving to all posterity the testament of dying once.

But the fourth mover or angel of Mercury, Raphael, influencing worldly things after Zachariel, began in the one thousand fifty-fifth year of the world at the end of the month of February, and lasted until the one thousand four hundred tenth year of the world; under him the use of letters is said first to have been invented. But the letters then, as they report, were very different from those which the world has today, devised in the form and likeness of trees, plants, and animals; yet with time afterward these were changed in many ways, and took on an easier manner of formation and a more cultivated ornament. For the fictions of the poets said that Mercury was the presiding deity of all literature, promising that great secrets of things lay hidden beneath fables. After this, when the human race had multiplied and had been spread everywhere over all the earth, the unity of letters did not remain long; rather, the separation of bodies and minds far from one another discovered different and various modes of writing, about which our narration is long. Plato the Philosopher
