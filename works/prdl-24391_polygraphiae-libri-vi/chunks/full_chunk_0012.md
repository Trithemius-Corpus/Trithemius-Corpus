*Index or table of the things contained in the sixth book.*

In the sixth book, which is the last of our *Polygraphia*, after introducing a mental narration, we have set out in order the minute details of almost all the preceding signs and degrees, and, with arithmetical distributions, have drawn the functions of letters into the service of cabalistic operation. One thing lies hidden here in the bark, and another is found in the common operation of signs. For just as the spirit in the blood gives life to the human body, so the vigor hidden in the art commends the operation. Therefore whoever needs knowledge of this invention should open the eyes of his understanding, so that he may comprehend what is the proper character of each operation. For we desire all things to be open to good men in such a way that, if it were possible, they would nevertheless never become manifest to the wicked. A reason compelled us to adapt the letters of the Latins to numbers, lest, if we were too obscure, the intention of our mind should be clear to no one. Yet this novelty, so that it might not be made public without reasonable cause, had to be mystically concealed under unknown signs and marks of numbering. For the winds of the night are always stronger than those of the day, and just as night intercepts day, so light shudders at darkness. What nature denies, no one ventures upon successfully. We ask the reader not to despise this laborious work of ours before he understands it, but, so that he may be a judge without rashness, prudently to remove the bark, in order to arrive at the hidden kernel. Good and natural is the work that can be turned to good use or to evil, according as the desire of each person's will is moved.

A sword is a good instrument, and is turned also to good use whenever it is carried for defense without injury to one's neighbor. But if it is unlawfully extended to the harming of anyone, through abuse a good thing is loosened into evil. Therefore, so that the good may have means by which they may prudently provide for their affairs, the *Polygraphia* is written by us; and so that occasion for wrongdoing may not be furnished to the wicked, the kernel is concealed in the bark under various riddles. He who is good does not abuse good for evil; rather, if he can, he turns evil into good.

*What are the causes, and at the same time the uses, of this work of ours, the Polygraphia.*

We undertook the labor of the present work, both rare and arduous, for no other reason than to provide for the usefulness of those in need. I say those in need, not of food, but of aid in preserving mysteries without danger. Therefore we judge that it will be worth the trouble if we briefly note the uses and advantages that can come to those who use these inventions of ours. Indeed, since in our private affairs we have no business such that we need these teachings for concealing secrets, we have certainly labored for those whose command had to be obeyed. The first use is that the complete understanding of this work gives an easy method by which you will safely, secretly, and without any suspicion on the part of any observers make known the secret of your mind, through the industry of the art, as widely as there may be need, to any distance of places, in regulated speech. What lies hidden in these things the work of *Steganographia* brings forth, which requires neither letters nor a messenger aware of the secrets he carries. It would be otherwise in matters admitting the light, according to the common custom of all our people. Do not take the matter otherwise than it is. The second use is that, by the service of this work, in a short space of time, you will teach a man ignorant of the Latin tongue, provided only that he knows how to read and write letters in his mother tongue, to write in Latin as you wish, elegantly or neatly, and to conceal and hide all the secret mysteries of his will, and to announce them, to one who knows the art, as widely as he pleases, safely, secretly, and without suspicion. Again I warn you beforehand not to understand us as promising otherwise than we promise.
