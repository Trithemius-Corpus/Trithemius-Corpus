For the pupil of the new art will write a language that he did not know, and will understand and interpret Latin speech composed under regulated instruction, if you command it, adequately word for word. Finally, this instruction can be extended to all the affairs and business of the world. If anyone wishes to know from me how these things can be done, let him remember first of all to show himself such that he deserves to receive it. For not all things are owed to all men, but one is to be preferred to another. The fourth advantage is that, through the extension of the likeness of the thing received, all these things can be extended into any language whatever, when the binary of the instructor has been reduced to unity. Yet not just any man can do this indiscriminately, but only he whom, God preceding him with his special grace, art and nature have made worthy. The fifth advantage is that, by imitation of likeness, we can communicate to one who knows the art of our invention any secrets whatever of our mind, as abundantly, variously, and widely as you wish, in diverse ways that cannot be known or noted by anyone else. Here is granted a manifold speech without voice, by which safely, securely, most widely, with all suspicion removed, we shall be able to indicate most fully to those who know the art all the conceptions of our mind, however difficult. And this wonderful invention has innumerable progressions, capable of all mysteries.

*The Causes and Advantages of the Work.*

The sixth advantage is that, through the understanding of this work, you will be able to discover any regulated metathesis or transposition of letters, and nevertheless to form them in such a way that they serve only those who know the art, and absolutely no one else in the world. And great is the power of metathesis in form and also in operation; it has several modes and is varied in many ways. The seventh advantage is that, through the understanding of this volume, very great capacity is given to those who desire to enlarge the memory without labor, whenever the seeds of minute things are set in their proper degrees. All things, for the sake of the cause of goodness, lie hidden wrapped in the secrets of words and figures; if anyone wishes to understand these more fully, let him recall to mind that unremitting labor conquers all things. For it will have been enough for me to have found the principles of the art and to have handed down a rough and deliberately unformed manner of writing symbolically. For there was neither time nor reason for our discoveries to be formed in a more polished way, lest honey produce vomiting in those who are nauseated, or truth produce hatred in those who are indignant. Every time must be valued according to its own merits, and swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor burdened with precious jewels. All things are untimely which do not agree with the manners and times of the mortals upon whom they are pressed. Many most noble talents of men have existed in unsuitable times, and almost every day more new things are found in times not their own. What did the wondrous invention of nautical art profit the excellent versifier Bartolus under Pope Innocent VIII, which in a certain little book he offered for sale for one hundred thousand ducats, when neither the Supreme Pontiff himself nor the Genoese, to whom he proposed that his matter be bought, deigned to come to terms with him even for a small sum? For he promised that by his art he could keep a ship unharmed in the middle of the sea for thirty months, repair it when broken, and recover without danger anything thrown into the deep. But no one cared. Nor does it escape me that many, with Naso, have perished by their own genius. It is therefore better to set a limit to knowledge than fruitlessly to expose oneself to the hatred and detraction of many, for whom it is more familiar to despise any knowledge whatever than to have learned it.
