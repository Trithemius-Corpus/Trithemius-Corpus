Indeed, so far as I am concerned, I would rather appease rivals by silence than irritate them by letters, or provoke them to defamation by writings. For I judged it better to condemn to perpetual silence all things wondrously discovered, than to incur, even through men’s false estimation, the stigma of magic or of the pernicious superstition of necromancy. Let the *Steganographia*, therefore, lie hidden in darkness, and let it not become common to the lying Bovillan company, which is accustomed to judge matters unknown to it, and to corrupt the reputation of a good man out of the mere lust of depravity. Nevertheless, at the urging of friends, so that our rivals, together with Bovillus, may understand what a reckless and fabricated injury he has done us, while the *Steganographia* remains within its own bounds, we consent to bring into the light the present work, which we call the *Polygraphia*, divided into six books. Let those who wish read it, and let friends read this volume without ill feeling; when they have understood all its hidden mysteries, let them themselves, without envy, be judges whether they are natural or superstitious. For I know, and am certain, that all things are sincere, pure, natural, and removed from every pursuit of evil superstition.

Whenever, however, the discourse proceeds rather obscurely, this is done for the reason that a madman should not misuse for destruction the sword of defense which a man sound in spirit and mind carries for his own protection. Yet nothing evil is contained in it on that account, since perverse men misuse even good things for evil. Whoever presumes to condemn this edition of ours is himself conscious and witness that he has never truly understood it. Mysteries are concealed beneath enigmas, lest the secrets of the Bubones become in any way manifest to the Bacuceans. If it delights anyone to know these things, let the reader approach with envy far removed.

For I am a Christian; I am a priest; I am also a monk under the rule of the holy father Benedict. I love Christ, and with whatever sincerity of mind I can, I always devoutly adore him. I have no dealings with demons; I have had none; and, with God as protector, I shall have none. I have no pursuits, no occupations, no teachings in magical, necromantic, or profane arts. Whoever thinks otherwise of me [unclear], and defends the open lie of Bovillus. But now let us leave these things aside, and let us set forth in order what is contained in each book of our *Polygraphia*.

*Table or Index of the Things Treated in the First Book.*

In the first book of this *Polygraphia* of ours we place and distribute, according to the order of our ternary, the words of the Latin language, as many as are seen; by their use and service all princes throughout the world will find safe assistance in any secrets whatever, which, without danger to the mystery, without mark, and without suspicion of the Bacuceans, will remain forever hidden. For the Bubones, by these means the path is known to be safe, and never to be violated by any investigation of anyone. Knowledge of the hidden matter is granted to two only, if the binary is skillfully brought back to the monad. Ignorance is the mother of error, and he who scorns to learn the good he does not know loves blindness. The periphrases themselves are extended in the manner either of one admonishing or of one supplicating, and they can be varied for every visible subject of this world. For they are suited to individual mysteries, and are set before the secrets of the Greeks no less than before our own; when you have understood the things you see, do not rashly despise the hidden things you do not see. You perceive accidents; substance remains invisible. Hence the greater things lie hidden, the lesser things are open to sight. Since this has been done with reason, let no good man take it in a bad sense, since from these things every capable man could, if only he wished, advance to higher matters.
