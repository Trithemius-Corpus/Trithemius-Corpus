*A brief explanation of the fifth book of our Polygraphia*

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have presented various methods of writing secretly by transposition of ordinary letters, arranged in such an order that, under their covering, we shall be able safely to write and communicate everything to an absent friend. Now, to speak in our own manner, the progression of the transposition of letters is almost infinite, since from one alphabet by metathesis countless forms arise. First we described a straight table containing twenty-four alphabets; by understanding it, as many alphabets will be able to be composed as there are stars counted in the firmament of heaven. For as many characters as there are in that table, so many times, by the art, hundreds of thousands of alphabets arise in order. After this we set out a reversed table distributively, which will arise so many times into another as often as we have changed the first letter at the head. In the first straight table the [unclear] letter is B, and in the reversed one Z. As often as you put any other changed letter in their place, so often you will find a table new in every respect, and so on to infinity. Then we expanded the first straight table, placing at the head of each transposed black letter, in minium, the letter which it represents, so that we might furnish the reader with an easier method of writing.

*Explanation*

This, then, is the method of writing: in the first black alphabet take one letter of the hidden sentence, from the second another, from the third a third, and so on consecutively to the end. When you have reached it, remember to return each time to the first order, until by concealing you have completed the secret mystery of your mind. But so that you may see the order, let us set down an example. Hxpf gfhmczfueib gmbt gx hsr egerbd qopma wvz. fxeqk ak tnr qxyz. The meaning of this mystic speech is: Beware this man, because he is evil, a thief, deceiver, liar, and unjust. You now perceive what a wondrous transposition of the letters of the alphabet this table provides, since there is no one who, without knowledge of it, can penetrate this secret. For this method of writing surpasses every common transposition of letters, since each letter is always changed from one series of the alphabet into another. From the reversed table also, which we have expanded in order by a similar distribution, let us set down such an example by way of introduction. Rdkt, ftz nyb, teuqz, fu zf, fd. drgh, vfd. Its hidden sense is this: Beware this man, because he is evil. And note that under the example of the straight table already set down, we make the hidden series from the beginning through the reversed table, and again the circle, as you see, to the beginning of the straight table. Hence it must be considered that one series of writing can be drawn through both tables, just as we have already done in the demonstration of the first example, or, if you prefer, through one alone, as we have shown sufficiently in the reversed table. Nor will it create difficulty for the reader to whom you send the hidden message; he will easily understand everything if he has had even a little experience in the art. The strongest proof, moreover, which increases knowledge, is practice repeatedly exercised in any art.

After the expansion of the tables, certain orchemata follow, in which there is not only a transposition of all the letters, but also a passage of one ordered over another. For orchema in Greek is called transilitio in Latin. And first we marked the leap of the letters in the expanded form; afterward we reduced it into an orchema. In the operation of this orchema, however, a wondrous leap is made. There are twenty-four alphabets in it. In the first no letter stands between. In the sixth, three. In the fifth, two. In the seventh, six. In the eighth, seven. In the ninth, eight. Finally, in the tenth, ten, and so consequently up to the twenty-fourth alphabet, in which twenty-three letters stand between; afterward a return by likeness is made to the beginning. The method in the orchemata for writing a mystery is similar to that which is observed in the tables of transposition of letters: namely, when one is going to write according to the rule of the orchema, from any black alphabet one is to take only one letter, which, once written, the minium shows at the head.
