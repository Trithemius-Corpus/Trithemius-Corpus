*Table or Index of the Works of Johannes Trithemius, first Abbot of Spanheim and afterward of St. James at Würzburg.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot, was born in the village of Trittenheim, in the diocese of Trier, on the banks of the river Moselle, on the first day of the month of February, at the eleventh hour and thirtieth minute after noon, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1462, in the tenth Roman indiction: his father was Johannes de Heydenberg, and his mother was called Elisabeth de Longouico. This was the very year in which the city of Mainz, in the dissension of two pontiffs, was captured, lost its liberty, and found servitude, many of the citizens being driven into exile; in the fourth current year of Pope Pius II, and the twenty-second of Emperor Frederick III.

In the year of the Lord 1482, however, in the monastery of Spanheim, in the diocese of Mainz, on the first day of February, that is, the day on which he had once been born, he laid aside the secular habit and there took up the monastic one under the rule of the blessed father Benedict, according to the observance newly introduced. In the following year, which was the eighty-third above the four-hundredth and the thousandth, after Abbot Johannes de Colenhusen had been transferred by canonical postulation to Seligenstadt, Johannes Trithemius, by the common election of the brothers, was promoted and adorned as abbot of the aforesaid monastery of Spanheim on the fourth day before the Kalends of July, and presided for twenty-three years, one month, ten days, and nine. When these had passed, not without many labors, provoked by the rashness of certain rivals, he accepted the abbacy of St. James at Würzburg, which was offered to him, and of his own free will resigned Spanheim, full of useless cares.

In both places he wrote a number of little works, from which recently he set down for me in his own hand the items written below on a sheet. Since you, as a friend, ask me for their titles, I gladly yield to your wishes, so that from consideration of such great labors of his you may hold the memory of our common friend and teacher more closely. And first indeed I shall set down the titles of those works which, while he was abbot at Spanheim, [unclear] of his order, which were made in provincial chapters by order of the presiding officers: one book. *On the Visitation of Monks*, one book. *On the Manner of Holding a Provincial Chapter*, one book. *On the Rule of St. Benedict*, two books.
