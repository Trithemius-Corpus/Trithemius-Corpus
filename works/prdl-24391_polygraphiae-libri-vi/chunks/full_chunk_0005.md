*Lucubrations* He wrote, for the bishop and duke of eastern Francia, an epitome or abridgment of the same volumes, three books of histories. To the same man, on the dukes and bishops of the Franks of Wurzburg, one book. On the miracles shown at the memorial of Our Lady, Saint Mary, at Dettelbach, two books. On the miracles of the same shown in the nettle-bed at [Helprunnam], six books. An Apologetic against Charles Bovillus, who slandered things he did not understand, one book. On the origin and kings of the Franks, one book. A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, of Saint James at Wurzburg, one book. To Albert, of the Margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, the life of the divine chief-prelate, three books. On the transfer of the same from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To John, Count Palatine, Duke of Bavaria, and Count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, on the origin, kings, and dukes of the Bavarians, together with the Counts Palatine and of Sponheim, four books. On demons and profane arts, he attacks, confounds, and overturns sorcerers and superstitious [unclear]. Of Wurzburg letters, which he wrote in that abbacy, four books. He also wrote many other smaller works, suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as courses, a rosary, a missal office, and various prayers, likewise proses or sequences on Saint Anne, grandmother of our Savior. He wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene. A rosary on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord. A rosary on Saint Peter the Apostle. A rosary on Saint John the Evangelist. At the request of John, archbishop of Trier, a sequence on Anne, which begins, “Jesus, king of the heavens,” to the melody “Ave praeclara.” Another, to be sung on her feast, which begins: “Let them exult on this day.” A third, to the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, begins, “Let us rejoice in honor.” A fourth on Saint Hildegard the virgin, which begins thus: “Praise be to you, Lord, king of eternal glory.” A fifth on Saint Rupert, duke of Bingen, which begins: “Let them exult on this day.” A sixth on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, begins thus: “Let us sing, renowned men.” A seventh on Saint Rabanus, archbishop. An eighth on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus: “Let us adore Jesus Christ, let us mourn the crucified one.” A ninth on the compassion of Blessed Mary, ever Virgin, begins thus: “Come, let us weep with devout mind, sharing compassion with the blessed Virgin Mary,” namely to the melody “Ave praeclara.” A tenth on Saint Joachim, father of Our Lady, which begins thus: “Let us adore the Trinity, let us together praise the unity of Father, Son, and Spirit.” An eleventh on each man’s own good angel. A twelfth for the proper mass of each priest. A thirteenth on Saint Maximus, bishop of Mainz. A fourteenth on Saint Martha the virgin, Christ’s hostess. A fifteenth on Saint Mary Magdalene. A sixteenth on Saint Mary of Egypt. A seventeenth on Saint James the Greater, apostle. An eighteenth on Saint Andrew, apostle. A nineteenth on Saint Benedict, abbot. A twentieth on All Saints. And for each of the named memorials and saints he composed individual new offices of the mass, conformable to the divine scriptures. He wrote besides a little work of meditations and prayers on the Lord’s incarnation, nativity, life, Passion, and death, suited to devotion, in two books. The Mirror of Heavenly Doctrine, serving in no small measure for compunction of mind, one book, which begins thus: “The beginning of human salvation.” He also composed many very devout meditations and prayers on our Lord Jesus Christ, on his most holy mother the Virgin Mary, and on various saints, with beautiful variety; together with many great other works, which all devout posterity will see and admire. These, my dearest Nicholas, are the volumes of our abbot Trithemius, which over almost thirty-two years he has composed in continuous study, apart from those which, not yet completed, are in hand on the anvil, unknown to me. Farewell, from my house at Durach, on the fifth day of the month of October, in the year of our salvation 1515. Johannes Duraclusius.

From the letter of Johannes Duraclusius, his disciple, to Nicolaus Hamerius of Emel.
