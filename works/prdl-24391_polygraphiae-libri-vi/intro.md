*Polygraphiae libri VI* — "Six Books of Polygraphy" — is Trithemius's openly technical cryptographic manual, the first printed book on cryptography in the West. Dedicated to Emperor Maximilian I in 1508, it appeared in print in 1518, two years after his death. Its method is stated plainly rather than veiled as in the *Steganographia*: substitution alphabets, the "Ave Maria" tableau by which any plaintext is recast as a Latin invocation, and the long combinatory tables of the later books, culminating in the polyalphabetic *tabula recta*.

This is the **1600 Strasbourg edition** — the third and longest-paginated of the three *Polygraphiae* witnesses in the corpus (1518, 1550, 1600), the same six-book text in a different print. The Latin was re-OCR'd from the page scans and translated by GPT-5.5; the running prose renders to the top tier (S). The dense cipher grids are only approximately captured by the source scan and are preserved as honestly as the OCR allows — for the tables themselves readers should work from a facsimile of an original edition, or from the dedicated cipher renderings on the 1518 and 1550 witnesses in this corpus. No complete English translation of the *Polygraphiae* exists; it circulated mainly through Gabriel de Collange's French *Polygraphie* (Paris, 1561), and in English only as Philip Neal's partial translation of the preface.
