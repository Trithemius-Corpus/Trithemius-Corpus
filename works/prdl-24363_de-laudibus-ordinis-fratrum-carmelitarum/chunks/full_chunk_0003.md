*Letter of Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the work set below, concerning the praises of the order of the Carmelite friars.*

To the reverend father in Christ, Lord Johannes Virici of Neuss, provincial throughout Lower Germany of the order of the friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, and to the other fathers gathered capitularly in the convent at Cologne: Johannes of Trittenheim, abbot of Sponheim, greeting and charity.

Report has it that the kings of the ancient Parthians greeted no one without gifts. I too, most religious fathers, have the desire to greet your company, but gifts worthy of the duty are lacking, by which the sincerity of my love toward you might be declared. What, then, am I to do? Is the bond of charity broken because of poverty of gift? Far from it. Shall I therefore greet you empty-handed? By no means. What, then, shall I bring? Gifts. What gifts, as a poor and needy man? Gifts of charity. Not gold, not silver, not precious stones, but words and letters by which the order of piety may be nourished. Recently, therefore, moved by the prayers and urging of the religious fathers Johannes Lapicida of Cologne, prior of Kreuznach, and Jacobus de Cuba, lector, I wrote in praise of the Carmelite order a brief little work, which I send to you as a small gift for examination. And although it was truly embarrassing enough for me to have wished to praise the excellence of so great an order before men most learned in every branch of learning and doctrine, since I know there are many among you, most eloquent men, who could do this better than I, nevertheless I preferred to be judged unskilled in speech rather than fruitless in charity. For although our little work is far inferior to the praises of so great an order, it will nevertheless be able to bring some usefulness, since he who wished to be a praiser of your religion has incurred no suspicion of his own praise. For I believe that you ought to receive the praises and honor of your most excellent religion from outsiders rather than from your own.

Receive now, therefore, best fathers, the little work sent from my smallness; in it weigh not what I have done, but how much I wished to do for the honor of the Carmelite order. But if you judge anything worthy of correction, correct it as you please, or, if it seems good, entrust it to me to be corrected. Various occupations of household affairs did not allow me to apply the file of every kind of elegance, nor to arrange each point according to my training. If there is error anywhere, negligence, not will, furnished the cause. But you, venerable fathers, will readily accept the apology, if you weigh my love toward you and the solicitude of pastoral care. I shall count it among the greatest delights if I come to know that my affection toward the order has not displeased you. I desire you always to be well in the Lord and mindful of me. Given very hastily from the monastery of Sponheim, the ninth day before the Kalends of May, in the year of the Lord 1492.

*Here begins the preface of Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, to the book on the praises of the sacred order of the Carmelite friars.*

At the prayers and urging of certain fathers of the order of the friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, I undertook the work set below. In it I determined to describe briefly the praises of that most sacred religion, so far as the cares of household affairs and the very slight powers of my talent allowed, and I chose to show more compendiously how it has advanced from the beginning of its foundation down to this day; [unclear], lest the dignity of so excellent an order be obscured by my slowness, and there happen to me what is wont to happen to unskilled orators, who by their lack of skill are accustomed to disfigure splendid matters. Indeed, when I seem somewhere to have written too sparingly, I should not be thought to have failed in subject matter, but in words. For the excellence of the Carmelite order is very great, and seems to far exceed not only my fluency and eloquence, but even that of Demosthenes. There are various orders in the Church of God; nevertheless, to this one before all the rest so much is granted, that it is adorned with the title of the most blessed Mother of God and Virgin. Since, therefore, small talents do not suffice for praising the greatest matters, what wonder if I seem to fall short in celebrating a most splendid order? I have indeed satisfied my urging friends, although I have been able to do less than I wished. Still love of the Carmelite order flourishes in my breast; still the material for praising it overflows; but before the excellence of the matter words fail. The institution of this order has deserved great praise, which the Queen of Heaven distinguished by her own name. O truly blessed union, which, fortified by the protection of so great a Mother, stands firm forever.
