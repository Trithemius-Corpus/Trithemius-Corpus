*On the manifold persecution of this most holy order and its victory over its rivals. Chapter ix.*

As the order therefore grew throughout Europe, and the number of the brothers was multiplied, the devil, envying the zeal of the good, did not cease to persecute the brothers; and what he could not do by himself, he arranged through wicked men. He therefore stirred up against them priests, rectors of churches, who tried to bring them under their own power and to subject them, like peasants, to parochial laws. This presumption became so strong that the Carmelites were publicly forbidden to bury their dead in their convents unless they had first paid the parochial rights of the churches. Moreover the envious priests forbade anyone to aid the poverty of the brothers, as though they had moved not for the edification but for the destruction of the faithful.

The afflicted brothers complained to the bishops of the dioceses, but in vain. For all sought their own interests; all were thinking of destroying the order utterly, keeping before their eyes their own advantages, not those of the commonwealth. What now were your brothers to do, virgin lady of the world? Where were they to turn, troubled on every side? Whom were they to call upon as advocate of their calamities, when they saw that among Christians, with each seeking his own, they were held in hatred? Desolate by men, they called upon you, mother of mercy, placing all their hope in you. Nor was it in vain. For how, since you are a spring of loving-kindness, could you have forgotten your brothers, your own bowels?

You therefore called one of them, whom the rest followed as leader, and directed him to the apostolic see, promising good confidence concerning the matter. Nor was the promise empty, but certain. You directed the way of your apostle; you went before him with blessings; you forewarned the vicar of your Son what he should do. O holy and truly worthy reconciliation, which received its origin not from men but from the ordinance of God. When the supreme pontiff heard the needs of the brothers, he restrained their rivals and detractors by apostolic majesty, and provided abundantly for the future tranquillity of the order.

What do you say now, twisted serpent, who hoped that you could destroy the holy order of Carmel? Where is your presumption, by which you tried to overthrow the religion consecrated to the mother of God? You have been confounded, and all your counsellors have been humbled in their iniquities by the power of God. Behold, the holy order has prevailed against all its adversaries and has been strengthened; and, having become one of the four rivers of paradise, it waters the whole earth with the water of learning, while you, with your dogs, pay the punishments owed.

Nor is this rivalry of the perverse a new thing, since from the beginning of the world the wicked have always persecuted the good, so that the elect may be proved through many tribulations. But while the good, afflicted by the adversity of the impious, make progress, the wicked always fall into the worst evils. But you, ornament of Carmel, do not fail; do not cease from the purpose of your most holy institution, so that you may be able to reach without hindrance the rewards prepared for you. If a mother can forget her beloved son, the queen of heaven will be able to abandon you. But even if a mother should forget the fruit of her womb, nevertheless the Virgin Mary will not forget you, but will guard her beloved brothers and make them sharers in her joys.

This, however, is necessarily required of you: that the brothers whom you cherish in your lap with your name and habit you keep immaculate in life and conduct. For it profits nothing to be marked with the name of the parent of God if the conduct of life is stained by contrary morals. Therefore, so that you may truly shine under the title of queen of the heavens, direct your professed members before all else to observe the three substantial things of religion, without the keeping of which monastic profession does not save but condemns. The Lord is with you as long as you have been with him. But if you despise the paths of your first institution, if you give entrance to vices, you will be subject to many evils and troubles. You will be a laughingstock to all the people; and you who now shine in glory will be confounded under reproach and disgrace. Beware therefore that you do not transgress your first institution, nor, neglecting the worship of God, walk after vanity.
