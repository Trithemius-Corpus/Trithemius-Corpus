Now we must answer the calumniator’s second proposition, in which he imprudently assails the Carmelite brethren concerning the succession of Elijah and the prophets.

*How Elijah, prophet of the Lord, was founder of the order of the Carmelites. Chapter 2.*

Elijah, the eminent prophet of the Lord, is rightly and in Catholic fashion called the founder of the Carmelite religious life, if his deeds are examined without malice from the books of Kings. For he himself is read to have been the first to inhabit Mount Carmel, from which the Carmelite brethren are named. It is shown by a threefold reason that Elijah is held to be the chief of this observance: namely from the place, from the habit, and from the manner of life. Since, therefore, the name of the order is taken from the place, Elijah is rightly remembered as chief of the Carmelites, because Mount Carmel, of which he was an inhabitant, gave this order its name. I am not speaking empty things, but bringing forward matters proved by the testimonies of the Scriptures, when I say that Elijah the prophet inhabited that same place long before the birth of Christ, the place which gave the Carmelite brethren the name and beginning of their religious life. Finally, if the testimony of the ancients is to be believed, after Elijah’s rapture and the death of Elisha, the sons of the prophets, inhabiting Mount Carmel through succession of generation, never allowed it to be empty of distinguished worshippers. Citations of testimonies on these matters are not necessary for learned men, and are superfluous for the unlearned.
