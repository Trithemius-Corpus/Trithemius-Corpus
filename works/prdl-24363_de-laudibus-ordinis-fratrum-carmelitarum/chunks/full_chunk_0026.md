[Walterus Hont?], English by nation, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, profound in knowledge, continuous in his study and practice of reading, is said to have written some excellent little works, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity. Of these there survives a distinguished work on the Sentences, three books; On the Poverty of Christ, one book. He is said to have composed many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I do not know whether he is still alive.

Aulus Biscontus, once confessor of Pope Nicholas V, a man exercised and learned in the divine Scriptures by constant study, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, is said to have written many little works on various subjects, by which he made his name known to posterity. I, however, up to this day have been able to find none of them. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1440.

Johannes de Dufeldorp, German by nation, of the convent of Mainz and prior of Strasbourg, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and a very learned doctor, ready in intellect and clear in speech, eloquent and very capable in delivering sermons to the people, devout in life and conduct, and an outstanding guardian and lover of regular discipline, wrote On the Observance of Feast Days, one book; An Itinerary of the Holy Land, which he visited in person, one book; various sermons on the season and on the saints, two books; and many other things. He died in his own convent of Strasbourg and was buried there, in the year of the Lord 1114, in the twelfth indiction.

Johannes Consobrinus, by homeland Portuguese, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, a very celebrated theologian, philosopher, and jurist, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, who did many things bravely against heretics for love of Christ, wrote, at the request of the provost of the royal college of St Mary of [Amhten?] in England, a distinguished tripartite work on contracts and exchanges, which he entitled On Commutative Justice, seven books; various sermons also; and certain other things. He abridged the Conflatus of Franciscus Maronis in a pleasing compendium. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1475, and under Pope Sixtus IV; at Lisbon he underwent death, not without suspicion that poison had been drunk.

Johannes de Arundine, German by nation, of the convent of Bruges, bishop of Us [unclear], a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently taught in secular philosophy, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, subtle in intellect, clear in speech and conduct, wrote many excellent little works, by which he made his name widely known. Of these the following are reported: a notable lecture on the Book of Wisdom, one book; on Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, one book; sermons on the season, one book; sermons on the saints, one book. He is said to have written many other things also, which have not come to my knowledge. He is still living in the city of Utrecht, being the bishop’s vicar there in pontifical matters.

Baptista of Ferrara, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently taught in secular letters, outstanding in verse and prose, skilled in both languages, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote many well-instructed volumes, by which he made his name known far and wide: a most lucid work of histories from the nativity of Christ down to our own times, which he entitled Florida; a chronicle also of his order, one book; On the Fall of the Roman Empire, one book; The Life of Mechtilde, two books; a chronicle of the Ferrarese, one book; various sermons, three books. He composed many epigrams in different kinds of meter. He translated from Greek into Latin several sermons of blessed John Chrysostom, and many other things unknown to us. He is living to this day at Ferrara, and is writing various works.
