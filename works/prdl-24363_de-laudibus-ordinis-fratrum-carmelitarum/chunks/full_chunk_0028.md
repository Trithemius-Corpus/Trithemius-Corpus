He also published various sermons, and many letters both to me and to others, the number of which is uncertain. He also published several poems on various subjects. He composed some other works as well, which have not come to our knowledge. He is still living at Ghent, composing various works and stirring many to the study of letters.

Johannes de Aqua Veteri, German by nation, Brabantine by homeland, of the convent of Mechelen, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and skilled by long practice, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, and a zealous and devout lover of his order, wrote, at the urgent request and for the edification of many brothers, a commendable work on the beginning, progress, and state of his order, which he entitled *A Three-Part Bundle of the Times of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever Virgin of Mount Carmel*, three books. In the first of these he proves that Elijah and the prophets were the founders of his order; in the second he enumerates the saints and canonized persons from the same order; in the third he sets out, according to the times, the successions of the priors general of his whole order. But the other works which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge. He is still living in the convent of Aachen, and strongly perseveres in the study he has begun, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1492, indiction 10.

*Apology of the author for the fact that not all the doctors and writers of this order who wrote have been inserted here.*

From these most learned men, therefore, of whom we have made mention, reader, you can recognize how great you ought to judge the fruits of Carmel to be. But we have extracted a few from many, whose talents and writings we have known. Nor do we doubt that there remain several most learned men of this order, who, flourishing at various times, magnificently adorned it by their talents and writings, but whose memory has not reached us. For the Carmelite religion is older than all orders, and that very great antiquity has easily brought the bright lights of its own into oblivion. Therefore, whoever desires to know more fruitful shoots of Mount Carmel should read the deeds and histories of the same order, and he will be able to find most learned men of whom there has been no mention in this catalogue. For, having obtained neither the time nor the leisure to search out the others who are lacking, we have therefore arranged these in order, so that we might provide the reader with an occasion for investigating the rest. Finally, to you, brothers, cultivators of the Carmelite order, we turn our mind; to your charities we offer the present gift, and, in return for our devoted love, we earnestly beseech you to pray to God for us.

*Elegiac Poem of Lord Johannes Tritemius, Abbot, Addressing His Carmelite Book.*

Go now, dear book; present yourself to the venerable fathers, in whose praise you have been created and are present.
Do not fear to see men learned and honorable in morals, and renowned with a devout mind.
There is no reason for you to be afraid: the men are friendly and sweet and gentle; you may approach them boldly.
Among such men above all, remember to carry my greeting, and do not hesitate; I promise you will be welcome.
If anyone is pricked by the goads of envy, pay no heed. If anyone seeks to gnaw you with his tooth, flee.
Always in adversities the patient man wins glorious trophies; the impatient man carries off shameful reproaches.
The impious and depraved man speaks evils shut up in his heart; such things, my book, I beg you not to fear.
