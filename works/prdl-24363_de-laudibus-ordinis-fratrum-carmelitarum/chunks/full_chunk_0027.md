Johannes Wiric of Nussia, a German by nation, of the convent of Cologne, provincial prior of Lower Germany, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, ready in intellect and clear in speech, a famous preacher of the divine word, is reported by those who knew him to have written on the Sentences, eight books; various sermons and conferences, which in their time will come forth into the light. He is still living in his convent at Cologne, composing various works.

Johannes Placentinus, a man most learned in every respect, a theologian and very celebrated rhetorician, ready in intellect and eloquent in speech, most skilled in the Greek and Latin languages, since he translated many things elegantly, is reported to have written several little works by which he consecrated his name to immortality. Of these I have seen only: a Greek Vocabulary, a work truly useful and necessary, one book. There is also another smaller volume of his, where Latin is set before Greek, one book. To Ludovicus Donatus, bishop of Bergamo, he translated the Psalter from Greek word for word with the greatest exactness; no one can praise this work worthily enough. In it he first set down the Greek translation in Greek letters, and appended his own in Latin letters. He composed many elegant letters to various persons. He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Pope Innocent VIII, and I believe that he is still living.

Johannes Scandeberius, bishop of Erford in England, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, devout also in life and conduct, is reported to have written many distinguished little works by which he made his name known far and wide. But I do not remember that any of them has come to my knowledge. He flourished in England under Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. I do not know whether he is still living.

Baptista Mantuanus, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and, in the knowledge of secular letters, second to none in this age; an eminent doctor of sacred theology, priest, orator, and very celebrated poet; subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, skilled in both languages, namely Greek and Latin; one who equals Virgil in verse and Cicero in prose, not to say surpasses them. He wrote many excellent volumes in both kinds of style, by which he acquired for his name celebrated fame throughout the whole Christian world. Among these the following are reported: Dialogue on the Blessed Life, to his father, one book; Suburbanus, in verse, on bucolic matters, one book; On the Praises of Saint Mary, Parthenices, three books; On Saint Katherine the Martyr, one book; On the Life of Saint Albert of his order, in Sapphic verse, one book; On the Calamity of These Times, or On the Capital Sins, in heroic verse, a most elegant work, one book; Panegyric of Robert, one book; Dream concerning the same, one book; To Falcon, a varied work of epigrams, which he entitled Falcon, one book; Consolation on Death, one book; On Contempt of Death, one book; Against Poets Writing Shameful Things, one book; To Pope Innocent VIII, On the Capture of Granada, four books; On Patience, one book; On the Origin and Diversity of Religious Orders, one book; Apology for Brother Peter, two books; Introduction to the Subtle Scotus, one book; elegant and various Orations, one book. He wrote almost innumerable letters to various persons. He also published countless and most elegant poems of various kinds, which in their time will come forth into the light. Indeed he is so ready and elegant a versifier that he has never had his equal, if you direct your mind to the majesty of his poetry and to his knowledge of all philosophy and learning. He lives to this day in Italy, the unique glory of the Carmelite order, the prince of learned men, and he is composing many works to be published day by day.
