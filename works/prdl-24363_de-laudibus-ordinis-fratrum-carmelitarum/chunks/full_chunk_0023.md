Richard Lavinham, English by nation, doctor of sacred letters at Oxford, a subtle man and of vigorous talent, an acute disputant of questions and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote certain excellent little works by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are extant: Determinations against the Lollards, one book; likewise notable determinations on behalf of the book of the Revelations of Saint Bridget, which he read publicly in the schools of Oxford and London, one book. He also began a distinguished work on the foundation of his order, which, prevented by death, he did not complete. He also composed some things in logic and physics, which have not come into our hands.

Richard Maidescon, English by nation, doctor of sacred Scripture of the Oxford school, excellent in talent and renowned for eloquence, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, is said to have forged certain excellent little works by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there is extant: Against the Lollards, one book; sermons on the season, one book; likewise sermons on the saints, one book; and various questions. In addition he composed several other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

William Talisfordie, English by nation, doctor of holy Scriptures of the Cambridge school, an educated and studious man, subtle in talent, devout in life and conduct, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote very notable sermons on the season, one book; likewise sermons on the saints, one book. He also composed various readings of the Scriptures, which he read in the aforesaid school.

Thomas Lombe, English by nation, a most celebrated doctor of sacred letters of the Oxford school, a man renowned for wisdom and learning, subtle in talent and an acute disputant of scholastic questions, wrote against the Lollards and Wycliffites, one book; On the Incarnation of the Word, one book. He is said also to have written other little works, which I have not yet seen.

Nicholas Ritzonis, of the convent of Catania, Sicilian by nation, and provincial prior of Sicily, doctor of theology of the Bologna school, of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people, wrote, while he was still devoting himself to letters at Bologna, elegant sermons on the season, one book; likewise sermons on the saints, one book. He is said in addition to have composed two Lenten works, the reading of which is judged useful for preachers. He also published several other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

John Campscon, English by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, excellent in talent and renowned for eloquence, a most excellent declaimer of popular sermons, wrote for the benefit of readers sermons on the season, one book; likewise sermons on the saints, one book. The rest that he composed have not come to my knowledge.

John Schonhoven, German by nation, prior of the convent of Mechelen, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and learned by long practice, subtle in talent and ready in eloquence, of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people, wrote a notable work in two volumes according to alphabetical order, which he entitled Polipodion, very suitable for preachers of the word of God, two books. He also published various sermons on the season and on the saints, and certain other things.

Blasius Andernarij, French by nation, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of the secrets of letters, wrote several excellent volumes by which he made known to posterity also the sharpness of his talent. Of these there is extant a celebrated work on the Sentences, four books; likewise various questions disputed in the schools. But the rest that he composed have by no means come to our knowledge.
