I have said these things not because I deny that Elijah’s habit was white, but so that I may cut off from those who hesitate an occasion for murmuring against the order. But after many years, when the Holy Land had fallen into the hands of the infidels, the brothers were compelled to change their habit, the Saracens pressing this, who thought that only their own satraps were permitted to wear white clothing. Therefore, by a public edict of the king, the brothers of Mount Carmel were compelled to lay aside the white habit and to take up any other. Thus, necessarily abandoning the former habit, they assumed striped copes, which were striped in two colors in seven parts; of these the first, around the opening in front, was white on either side; the next was white on both sides; and the last, in the middle of the back, was black. At length, when after not a few years, as the malice of the infidels increased, the brothers were compelled to withdraw from the Holy Land, they were again compelled to change the habit. For, coming by divine ordinance into Europe, lest they make people laugh by their foreign habit, they resumed the old habit, which they use in the order down to the present day. Finally, lest this change should seem frivolous to anyone, it is immediately found to have been approved by the authority of the supreme pontiffs.

*Chapter 7. Why the Carmelite brothers are called brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel.*

But now we must ask why, all the many holy new and ancient orders being passed over, the order of Carmelites alone is adorned with the title of the blessed Mother of God, although she was not its foundress, nor subject to it by any necessary likeness of profession. What, then, was the reason that the Carmelite brothers were called from the beginning brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin? Was it done by some chance accident, or by some empty presumption? Far from it. For, as we have learned from reading the ancients, we know especially that this title befits the Carmelites, whom we do not doubt to have held the Mother of God always in the greatest honor and reverence from the beginning of their institution.

*Chapter 8.*

For not long after the Lord’s Passion, as the Christian faith was increasing throughout Judea, the brothers of Mount Carmel built a chapel on the side of that same mountain in honor of the Mother of God, and they chose her as advocate and patroness of their order; and thereafter they were called her brothers. Therefore, just as they are called Carmelites from Carmel, so from the chapel which they built they are called, down to the present day, brothers of the most blessed Mother of God and Virgin.

And this is indeed a worthy designation for the Carmelites: that those who always venerate the Mother of God with special love should also be more renowned by her title. For just as the most blessed Virgin Mary first of all opened for us the entrance to paradise, so the religion of the Carmelites gave beginning and form to the other orders.

Nor do I doubt that Mary, parent of God, pursues this order with special favor, since she recognizes it as near to her in place, and first in time to have arisen in service to herself and her Son. The Carmelite brothers are indeed worthy to be distinguished by the title of the most holy Virgin, because from the beginning of their institution they were made close to her in their own manner through humility and innocence, and singularly devoted in service to her kinship. Read and turn over all the histories of past time, and nowhere will you find that the order of the Carmelite brothers stirred up schism or scandal in the church of God, or disturbed the faithful people by any novelty, or was ever suspected of any heresy. For it has always served the Lord in humility and tranquillity, and has always remained content with its own innocence. Therefore, the one who was always devoted to the most blessed Mother of God in humility and purity and holy morals was suitably elevated under her title. The habit itself, too, indicates this by its cleanness and purity, for it shows that the Carmelite brothers ought above all to observe continence and chastity. And although the first place itself, which gave the order its name, is now desolate, nevertheless the name of Carmel remains with the brothers, together with the patronage of the Mother of God.
