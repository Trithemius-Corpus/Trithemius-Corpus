The upright also love you, mindful of your beauty. No one truly loves you unless he is good; no one hates your institution unless he is perverse. How beautiful are your steps in the shoes of the Gospel of peace; how lovely your face; how elegant your form, how comely your appearance. Blessed are your worshippers, who walk in the paths of truth, who, despising worldly things, keep your precepts with devout sincerity. Greater are you, O sacred religion of Carmel, than to be proclaimed adequately by my words. I cannot be silent from your praises, since what weakness denies in strength, charity supplies.

Now therefore, you friends, importunate demanders of the present work, receive from the hand of a lover of you and of the Carmelite order the gift long desired, and diligently beseech almighty God for my errors. You have from me the little work requested, in which I have satisfied your petitions, but by no means my own will. For we wish more for the honor of your order than we can do. Then I address you, brothers serving under the protecting mantle of the Virgin; I beseech and pray you that, if our devotion toward your order seems praiseworthy to you, you intercede with the Lord for our faults, and render the Mother of God, patroness of your religion, favorable and propitious to us. This, I say, do, so that the reward of our work may be brotherly love and the prayer of unity.

*Here begins the first book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, on the praises of the order of Carmelites.*

*On the beginning of the order. Chapter 1.*

Intending, with the Lord’s help, to treat of the order of the brothers of the most blessed Mother of God and Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel, let us take the beginning of our discourse from him who holds the primacy of that same religion. For then the knowledge of this most sacred order can be pleasing to the reader, if the eminence of the one who instituted it is not hidden from him. Indeed, individual orders have their teachers and chiefs, founders and governors of life under a rule. Some had Basil as the chief of their order; others Augustine; others Pachomius; we Benedict; the Preachers had Saint Dominic; the Friars Minor Francis. But Elijah, the greatest prophet, is said to have been the institutor of this most sacred order; the sons of the prophets are remembered from the Old Testament as its chiefs; its propagator was the holy prophet Elisha; and the most sacred Mother of God, the Virgin Mary, marked it with her own name and defended it from the envy of rivals.

Let him who glories glory in the Lord. Let each man desire, as far as he can, to imitate the chiefs of his own order; let each follow his own institutors in morals, not in frivolous speeches. Let no one in the Church of God contend over the excellence of his own order; let no one provoke another through zeal for rivalry; let no one disparage another; let no one corrupt another’s reputation by the evil of elation. It is foolish to seek glory from another’s holiness, and to expect reward from another’s virtue rather than from one’s own. There are some who envy the pursuits of good men, who gnaw at and tear the sacred order of the Carmelites with an impious tooth, who are greatly indignant at the prerogative of the Virgin’s title, judging the Carmelites as though unworthy to be called brothers of the most blessed Mother of God and Mother.
