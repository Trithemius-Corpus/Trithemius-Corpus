Aulus of Perugia, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and also excellently trained in secular letters, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, published certain little works of great authority, by which he commended his name to posterity. Of these there is extant an outstanding volume on the Sentences, eight books; also Various Questions, one book; and certain other things. He flourished at Paris under Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1343.

John Balistarii, a Catalan, the sixteenth prior general of the whole order, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and an acute investigator of secular philosophy, excellent in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain very notable volumes, by which he destined his name for posterity. Of these the following are extant: On the Sentences, seven books; Constitutions of His Order, one book; On the Last Times, to the pope, one book; and certain other things. He died in the year of the Lord 1377, in the twelfth indiction, on the seventh day before the Kalends of October, and was buried in the convent of Majorca.

Peter Rimi of Perpignan, a Catalan by homeland, a very learned man, doctor of sacred Scripture, profound in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote an outstanding volume on the Sentences, eight books; also On the Whole Psalter, one book. In addition he composed various and elegant sermons, which have not come into our hands.

Berenus of Boyo, a Breton by nation, a man most skilled in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, an interpreter of the sacred letters and a profound Parisian doctor, is said to have fashioned several very notable volumes, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there is extant an outstanding work on the Sentences, seven books; Various Questions; and other things which are hidden from us.

John de Regno, a Gaul by nation, of the convent of Toulouse, a learned doctor in the holy Scriptures, subtle in intellect and ready in eloquence, wrote on the Sentences, eight books; On the Gospel of Matthew, one book; Sermons on the Season and on the Saints, two books; also a Lenten Book, one book; and certain other things.

Peter Thomas, bishop of Pax and afterward patriarch of Constantinople, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, most holy in life and conduct, since indeed he shone with many miracles both while alive and after death. He is said to have written several very notable little works, by which he usefully exercised his intellect. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, seven books; On the Most Pure Conception of the Blessed Virgin Mary, one book; also Various Sermons, one book; and certain letters to various persons. In 1366, in the third indiction, on the eighth day before the Ides of January, distinguished by miracles.

Osbert, an Englishman by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular philosophy, of acute and forceful intellect, of scholastic eloquence, ready and subtle in debating and resolving questions of the Scriptures, wrote a most elegant volume on the Sentences, four books; Various Disputed Questions, one book; also diverse Sermons, one book. In addition he is said to have written several other things, which we do not know.

Thilmannus, of the convent of Aachen, a German by nation, prior provincial of Lower Germany, an outstanding professor of sacred letters, excellent in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, an excellent declaimer of sermons, wrote on the Sentences, four books; On the Gospel of Matthew, a notable lecture, which he read publicly at Cologne in the convent, one book; also various sermons; and certain other things. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1370.

Heinrich Dolendorp, a German by nation, before Thilmannus prior provincial of Lower Germany, a learned and erudite man and a very celebrated declaimer, excellent in intellect and scholastic in eloquence, a most keen debater of questions, wrote a notable work on the Sentences, seven books; also various and useful sermons; and certain other things. He died and was buried in the convent of Cologne, in the year of the Lord 1356.
