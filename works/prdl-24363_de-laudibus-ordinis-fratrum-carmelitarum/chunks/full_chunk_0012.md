*On the migration of the brothers into Europe. Chapter VIII.*

When, because of the sins of the people, the Holy Land had been brought into the hands of the faithless Saracen race in the six hundred and thirty-ninth year after the Lord’s Passion, the Carmelite brothers, who for a long time had also lived in the cities in increasing numbers, were so greatly afflicted by the unbelievers that they were compelled to return to the hermitage of Mount Carmel. For they could no longer remain in the cities, since the Saracens afflicted them so much that they left them no quiet time for serving God, and utterly denied them the necessities of life.

And so the brothers remained on Mount Carmel, leading a solitary life in no small poverty, until, after many years had passed, the Holy Land was again recovered by the Christians, and the journey to it was made peaceful for pilgrims. This recovery of the Holy Land was accomplished through Duke Godfrey in the year of the Lord 1099. Then, after a few years, when pilgrims were streaming in from every side, and especially from the regions of Europe, the number of brothers on Mount Carmel was greatly multiplied, so much that by their multitude they filled the mountains and the cities. O, if then you had seen the beauty of the Carmelite order in its majesty, you would have cried out that there had never been another like it. It was a delightful sight to see innumerable brothers dwelling around Mount Carmel in cells and caves of the rocks, all of one mind, serving the most high God by day and night as devoutly as possible, and despising the world with its pleasures for the love of Christ. Among them at that time religion was at its height, and the perfection of the regular life was at its height, since the brothers, while remaining in the flesh, had strongly conquered the flesh itself with its vices. Religion flourished in peace, which a little before, disturbed by the incursions of the faithless, was thought to be failing. From all Europe pilgrim brothers came, and humbly submitted their necks to monastic disciplines on Mount Carmel.

But this tranquillity in the Holy Land did not last long. For when John, king of Jerusalem, had died, the Holy Land could not have its accustomed subsidies from the apostolic see, because the emperor Frederick, through his rebellion, was opposing the supreme pontiff, until it was again captured by the Saracens. Now thereafter the Carmelite brothers were afflicted by the unbelievers with such great tribulation that they no longer seemed safe anywhere in Judea. Therefore, in the year of the Lord 1238, when a council of the whole order had been gathered together on Mount Carmel, many, with the prior’s permission and the counsel of the brothers, left Palestine and, with their rule, their ordinal, and their statutes, returned to Europe, whence they had come. Some therefore came to Cyprus, others to France and England, and some to Germany, each to the place where he thought he could find a place for preserving the order. But very many remained on Mount Carmel, who nevertheless were themselves expelled by the Saracens shortly afterward.

O pitiable calamity of a most holy order, which once rejoiced in almost countless sons, and now, under the pressure of necessity, is deserted by its brothers. What man, unless he be stony, would not be softened by this desolation of Carmel, when the religious life not only of one mountain but of many monasteries throughout all Judea was destroyed? But how could the place of Elijah and of the Carmelites remain safe, when that whole land is trampled by the nations, which our Lord, the king of heaven, sanctified by his bodily presence? And so, when the Carmelites came into Europe, by the authority of the supreme pontiff places were appointed for them to live in different regions; and they at once, though with difficulty, established several convents. Great compassion for Christ’s brothers held men, and those whom they grieved to see expelled from their seats they supported with generous munificence. From this time, therefore, the order of brothers was greatly multiplied throughout all Europe, and by its manner of life and teaching brought forth great fruits in the Church of God, and continues to bring them forth to this day.
