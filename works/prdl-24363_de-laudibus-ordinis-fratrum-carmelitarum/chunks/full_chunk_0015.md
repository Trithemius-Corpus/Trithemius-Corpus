[unclear]: O beautiful religious order of Carmel. You are the garden of spices which the right hand of God planted on Mount Carmel; its flowers give forth a most sweet fragrance, since you have brought forth learned and most holy men. You had Elijah, prince of the prophets; you are the school of seers, the norm of discipline, and the herald of monastic unity. Under the Law you preach grace; under grace, piety. Truly these are the camps of God, in which the apostolic life long preceded the apostles themselves. And so the steps of this order have been most beautiful from the beginning, when the brothers, loving God, advanced from virtue to virtue. O ancient glory of Carmel; O truly holy, beautiful, and beloved way of life, which conquers the devil, overcomes the flesh, and despises the world and vices. For the holy life of the Carmelite brothers, if it walks according to the purity of its institution, builds up the Church, instructs the ignorant, calls sinners to repentance, attacks the adversaries of Christ, and chastises offenders. What is more beautiful than this religious order? What order is more useful, in which charity commands, humility obeys, good zeal keeps watch, voluntary poverty reigns, continence fights, and chastity triumphs? A religious order is best preserved when foresight counsels, justice corrects, mercy raises up, kindness instructs, and wisdom guards. O how good and how pleasant it is for brothers so to dwell together in one, who serve God in humility, keep the rights of brotherly charity, flee the world and vices, cultivate virtues, love humility, and withdraw from all evils. These are the men who catch the falling Church upon their shoulders, and both by word and by example instruct faithful peoples in Christian righteousness. Those who show themselves such in the order are worthy to be called brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary. But as for those who pursue the vanities of this world, who, having despised regular observance, are given over to vices and pleasures, I would not call them brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary, but the foulest slaves of the devil. For what is more disgraceful, or more damnable, than to live disorderly under a holy order? He who is a slave of vices has no fellowship in the honor of the Mother of God.

Therefore, Carmelite brothers, you must take great care not to lose by evil conduct the dignity of the name which you receive from the Queen of Heaven. For what will it profit you to be called brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary, if you have been workers of iniquity? Indeed, unless you live religiously according to your rule and first institution, you usurp this title to damnation rather than to glory. Therefore, my brothers, take care to repel every iniquity from yourselves; take care to labor at good conduct, so that by holy exercises you may be able to come to her whom you call the sister of your order. For unless you live in common, without private property, chastely and religiously, you will not reach the Queen of Heaven. If you take care to live thus, you are useful in the Church of God. Indeed, just as we describe good Carmelites, living religiously according to the rule, as worthy of every praise, so we determine that the bad, and those deformed in morals, must be kept far away from this volume. For we intend to praise none but the good. We believe, however, that from the beginning many good and holy men have shone in this order, and still shine today; and therefore we warn the reader not to wish to despise the whole order because of the deformity of a few, for in it there are many good men whom it is certain that he does not know. Let the reader think no one among the Carmelites is to be despised, since he who today is seen to lie in deformity will perhaps tomorrow march first in the battle-line of regular observance.
