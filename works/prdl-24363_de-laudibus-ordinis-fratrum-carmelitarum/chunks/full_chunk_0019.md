John of Alier, of Toulouse, thirteenth prior general of the whole order, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in talent, knowledge, and eloquence, also outstanding in life and conduct, and a notable preserver of regular discipline, wrote a distinguished volume on the Sentences, four books; a notable Ecclesiastical Reading, one book; and certain other things which I have not yet found. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1330, and lies buried in his own convent at Toulouse.

[Petrus?] de Casa, of the province of Aquitaine, after the resignation of John of Alier the fourteenth prior general of the whole order; then bishop of Vaison, and finally patriarch of Jerusalem; a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, left certain splendid volumes of his talent, among which these are reported: On the Sentences, seven books; Sermons for the season and on the saints, two books; also a notable Reading on the book of the Politics; and certain other things which are hidden from us. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1330. He died on the day before the Nones of August, radiant with miracles.

John Walsigram, prior provincial of England, a very learned man in the divine Scriptures, an acute philosopher, and doctor of sacred theology of Paris, wrote notably on the Sentences, seven books; Various Questions and quodlibets. He also composed some other things which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Simon of Speyer, a Teuton by nation, prior provincial of all Germany, doctor of Paris, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote on the Sentences, seven books; also various Sermons; and certain lectures on the divine volumes. In addition he is said to have written some other things, but they have not come into our hands. He died under Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340, in the eighth indiction, on the eighth day before the Ides of January, buried at Cologne in his own convent.

Peter Raymond de Graffa, a Gaul by nation, of the convent of Tarsac in the province of Narbonne, fifteenth prior general, a learned and devout man, wrote many splendid volumes, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are extant: On the Sentences, eight books. He also gathered the various statutes of his order into one volume, and divided the same collections into sixty rubrics. But he also composed certain other things which have not come to our knowledge. He died in the year of the Lord 1357, in the tenth indiction, under Pope Innocent VI, buried at Montpellier, in the sixteenth year of his office as general.

John Baconthorpe, an Englishman by nation, prior provincial of England, doctor of Paris, a man most learned in every respect, a very celebrated philosopher, jurist, and theologian, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote many splendid little works, among which the following are reported: On the Sentences, seven books; On the Gospel of Matthew, one book; On the Gospel of Mark, one book; On the Gospel of Luke, one book; On the Gospel of John, one book; On the Epistles of Paul, fourteen books; On the Acts of the Apostles, one book; Various Disputed Questions, one book; Mirror of His Order, one book; Defense of His Order, one book; On Aristotle’s Metaphysics, in two volumes, twelve books; On the Soul, seven books; also On the books of the Ethics, one book. In addition he composed many other things which have not come to my knowledge. He also produced various explanations on the books of blessed Augustine On the Trinity and The City of God, and on Anselm’s Why God Became Man and On the Incarnation of the Word; in all of these he magnificently exercised his talent. He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1345.
