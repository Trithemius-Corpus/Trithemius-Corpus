Franciscus Martini, a Catalan by nation, of the convent of Barcelona, was a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures. In delivering sermons to the people he was of excellent talent. He is said to have written several little works, in which his profound knowledge is shown. Of these the following are extant: On the conception of blessed Mary, ever virgin, one book; A compendium in honor of his order, seven books; and certain other things. He died in the aforesaid convent in the year of the Lord 1380.

Franciscus de Bachone, of the convent of Gerona, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and an acute disputant, wrote a distinguished volume on the Sentences, three books; also a certain Summa for preachers, one book. He is also said to have composed many other things, which have not come into our hands.

Johannes Deciminetho, lector of the convent of Metz, a Frenchman by nation, a man zealous and very learned in the divine writings, outstanding in talent and clear in eloquence, an eminent declaimer of popular sermons, wrote The Mirror of the Institution of His Order, one book; Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book; also Sermons on the Saints, one book; Sermons through Lent, one book. He is also said to have composed several other things, but they have not yet come to my notice.

Johannes Nobleth, a Frenchman by nation, of the convent of Paris, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular letters, a notable philosopher and physician, wrote certain very distinguished little works of his talent, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of their number the following are reported: a distinguished work which he entitled Centilogium, one book; On the Gospel of John, one book. He is also said to have compiled commentaries and lectures on many other books of the Bible, which have not come to my notice.

Nicolaus Dorhm, an Englishman by nation, a doctor of Oxford, learned in the holy writings and subtly trained in Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect and forceful, a notable disputant in scholastic questions, wrote On the Sentences, three books; also Determinations of Various Questions, one book; a Table of the Originals. Besides these, he is said to have written many things, which have not come to me.

Johannes Marre, an Englishman by nation, a doctor of Oxford, zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures, subtle and forceful in intellect, wrote, among other little works of his exercise, Determinations against the Wycliffite Heretics, one book. He is also said to have written many other things, but they have not yet come to my notice.

Johannes de Gluel, a German by nation, prior of the convent of Cologne, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, clear in intellect, devout in life and conduct, wrote Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book; also Sermons on the Saints, one book; Sermons through Lent, one book. He also published several other things, which have not come to my notice. He flourished in his convent of Cologne, where he was also buried.

Walterus Disse, an Englishman by nation, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and a most celebrated declaimer of popular sermons, subtle in intellect and practiced in eloquence, wrote little works of no contemptible reading, of which the following are reported: Against the Lollards, one book; On the Schism, one book; On Certain Psalms, one book; Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book; also Sermons on the Saints, one book; and certain other things which have not come to my notice. He was the nuncio and legate of Pope Boniface IX in the kingdoms of England, Spain, Portugal, Navarre, Aragon, and in the regions of Gascony, preaching the cross against heretics and schismatics. He flourished under the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1390, and was buried in the convent of Norwich.
