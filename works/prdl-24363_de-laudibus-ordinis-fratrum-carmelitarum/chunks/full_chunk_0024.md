Stephen the Englishman, doctor of Oxford and provincial prior of England, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, adorned no less by life than by learning, wrote Against the Lollards, one book; notable Sermons, both for the season and on the saints, through the cycle of the year, two books; also various Questions, one book; a Repertory of his arguments, one book. He also published certain notable lectures on books of the Bible, along with many other things that are unknown to me.

Arnold of Austria, German by nation, of the convent of Vienna, and doctor of the university of the same city, very learned in the divine Scriptures and notably taught in secular philosophy; subtle in intellect and an outstanding disputant of scholastic questions. He is said to have written some excellent little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there is extant a distinguished volume on the Sentences, four books; also sermons and various questions. He flourished in the aforesaid university of Vienna, where he also publicly read his said lecture on the book of the Sentences.

Johannes Brammart, German by nation, of the convent of Aachen, provincial prior of Lower Germany, doctor of Paris, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures; subtle in intellect and composed in eloquence, devout in life and conduct, wrote a distinguished work on the Sentences, containing four books, which he completed at Paris in the year of the Lord 1380, in the third indiction. It begins thus: “The word of the Lord upon John.” Also sermons and various questions. He died in the convent of Cologne and was buried there, in the year of the Lord 1407, in the fifteenth indiction.

Michael of Bologna, Italian by nation, prior general, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, distinguished in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote many excellent little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, three books; also Questions on the Sentences, one book; Table of the Sentences, one book; on the whole Psalter, a large work, one book; On the Gospel of Matthew, one book; On the Gospel of Luke, one book; Table of the Moralia of Pope Gregory on Job, one book; Table on the Decretum, one book; On the Most Pure Conception of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin, one book; Various Sermons, one book; a notable Lenten collection, one book. He also wrote commentaries on many books of Aristotle; also on Valerius Maximus. In philosophy, in canon law, and in oratory likewise he wrote various works. And many other things that have not come to me. He died at Bologna, buried before the high altar of his convent, in the year of the Lord 1416, on the Kalends of December.

Johannes Grossi, by homeland a Toulousain, prior general of the order, a distinguished doctor of the divine Scriptures and fully trained in secular [letters?], wrote some little works, of which the following are reported: a little work on the institution and progress of his order, which he entitled Viridarium, three books; also sermons and various questions on the Scriptures. He died under Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1424, in the second indiction.

Thomas Walden, English by nation, provincial prior of England, a man studious and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently taught in secular philosophy, an assailant, wrote a distinguished work against the Wycliffites, which he entitled the Doctrinale of the Church, one book. He also made commentaries on many books of Aristotle, which are held in esteem by many. Other works besides have not come to me. He died, not without a reputation for holiness, buried at Rouen, in the year of the Lord 1430.
