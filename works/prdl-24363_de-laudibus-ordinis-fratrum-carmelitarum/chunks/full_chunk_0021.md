In the third indiction, on the fourth day before the Ides of February.

Philip Riboti, provincial prior of Catalonia, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, clear in speech, and a careful moderator of regular discipline. Among other monuments of his talent he wrote a distinguished work on the origin and progress of his order, which he entitled *On the Special Deeds of the Carmelite Brothers*, in ten books. He is also said to have composed some other works, which have not come into our hands.

Robert of Monstrolo, a Gaul by nationality, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, once an ornament of the University of Paris. He wrote on the *Sentences*, in three books; a notable lecture on the epistles of Paul, in fourteen books; various questions, in one book; sermons also on the season and on the saints, in two books. He is also said to have written some other works, which I have not yet seen.

John Fust, lector of the convent of Kreuznach and afterward prior of Strasbourg, German by nationality, born from the county of Sponheim: a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, a very celebrated preacher of sermons. Among other things he wrote elegant sermons on the season, in two books; also on the saints, in two books; sermons for Lent, in two books; and other things which are hidden from us.

John of Hildesheim, born from the city of Saxony, lector of the convent there, devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters also; a distinguished philosopher and poet, practiced in verse and prose, a very famous preacher of sermons to the people. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading, of which the following are reported: to Florentius, bishop of Munster in Westphalia, on the translation of the Three Kings into the city of Cologne, in one book; also a pleasant and useful chronicle, in one book; a defense of his order, in one book; *On the Fountain of Life*, in one book; *Against the One Painting Shameful Things*, in one book; a metrical work on monsters in the church, in one book; letters to various persons, in one book; sermons on the season and on the saints, in two books. He also published some other works, which have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370, and composed various epigrams in verse.

John Golein, a Norman by nationality, prior of Rouen: a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently learned in secular letters; subtle in intellect and clear in speech. He wrote little works of authority not to be spurned, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there survive: on the *Sentences*, in four books; on the office of the Mass, in one book; also various questions; and, as is reported, certain other works.

John Taceffale, English by nationality, of the Norwich convent, a learned man and greatly erudite in the holy Scriptures as well as in law and philosophy, ready in intellect and fit for preaching sermons to the people. He wrote on the *Sentences*, in three books; a notable lecture on the Apocalypse, in one book; also various sermons to the people. He published some other works besides, which have not come to my knowledge. Finally sent to Rome, he died there.

Ronald of Bissalis, [galtetienẜ]: a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures and clear in speech, not ignorant of rhetoric; profound in intellect and [unclear] ... notable, in one book; also various sermons to the people. He published some other works besides, which have not come to my knowledge. Finally sent to Rome, he died there.

John Horuby, English by nationality, a copious and learned doctor in the holy Scriptures, subtle in intellect and a very acute disputant of questions. He is said to have composed some splendid little works, by which the vein of his abundant talent made him known also to posterity. Of these there survives a work defending the antiquity of his order, in one book; also various sermons; and certain other works. He flourished in the university of Cantha[unclear].
