But perhaps some rival of this order may say to me: “Why do you commend the Carmelites so greatly for their multitude of saints, when you are not unaware that no memorial of them is kept in the Roman Church? Show me how many saints from the order of Carmelites you have found in the calendar.” Whoever says such things shows himself to be either envious or wholly ignorant of the deeds that have been done. For what of the fact that the Church venerates few of the saints from the order of Carmelite brothers? Are they therefore to be judged few because the calendar has not received many? Indeed, fifteen thousand saints from it are recorded by their proper names. The ancient orders were not accustomed to boast vainly of the multitude of their saints, since each one strove rather to love holiness in himself than to exalt it in another for the display of his own religion. For the followers of the ancient orders preferred to be holy rather than to be called holy. Hence they did not greatly concern themselves to exalt their most holy brothers to the applause of the crowd, nor were they accustomed to run about soliciting kings and princes for their canonization, nor to weary the supreme pontiffs with importunate prayers. They believed it enough for themselves if their brothers had been placed with the saints in heaven, and therefore they did not concern themselves to have them canonized on earth. But the order of Carmelite brothers is most ancient, and in this matter it has never abandoned the paths of antiquity. For it had saints both many and most excellent, but according to the custom of antiquity it received few into the calendar, since it was always content with its former humility. As to the fact that the Roman Church venerates few of the saints of this order, we believe this happened because, since the greatest part of them had shone forth in Judea before the migration of the order, their holiness was unknown to the Roman Church. Nor was it necessary at that time to run to the Apostolic See for the canonization of saints, since in his own diocese any bishop could proclaim a saint by the testimony of miracles. But after the brothers migrated into Europe, and after various orders had multiplied in the Church, Roman authority decreed that no one should be held as a saint unless thereafter he had been canonized with the consent of the supreme pontiff. Yet a very great number of saints preceded this decree, whom antiquity, led by miracles, canonized. What pope canonized the apostles and the ancient saints? Are they not venerated in the Church of God by antiquity alone, content with their own holiness? And the order of Carmelite brothers had many saints beyond the sea, of whom in Europe there was anciently no memory. Finally, after the coming of the order into Europe they had several saints; if we wished to describe the names of them all, we would be forced to extend this volume to an immense size. We know, however, that the Carmelite brothers were not accustomed to set forth their saints for the admiration of the people, and therefore it was not our purpose to review each one by name. Finally, if anyone desires to know by name the most holy men who shone in this order, let him read the histories and chronicles of the said order, and especially that one which Brother Johannes, of the Mechelen convent, called Oudenwatter, composed with much labor and named the Tripartite Fascicle of the Carmelite Order. In that work he will indeed find so many most holy men of this order that among the other orders it has had few like them. He also set forth the beginning of the order and its progress in a clearly readable manner, and he described by name all the priors general from the first down to Pontius, who now presides over the order. But we, whom concern for household affairs compels to shorten our discourse, make an end here, and to the best of our ability undertake the arranging of a second volume on the praise of the Carmelites, in which we shall make mention of the illustrious and learned men in it who wrote various works for the benefit of the Church, so far as we are able to know them.
