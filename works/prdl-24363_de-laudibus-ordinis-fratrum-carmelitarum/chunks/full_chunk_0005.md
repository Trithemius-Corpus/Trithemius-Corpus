And, with the Virgin Mary’s favor, the speeches of these men must first be blocked; and only then should the discourse be continued to the praise of the order. For unless the false opinions of fools are struck down at the beginning, they will always prepare ambushes for us like certain brigands.

Now then, foolish self-praiser, what do you object against us? What do you lay against the holy order? What do you have against this religious life by which you can be moved? Bring it forth, so that we may hear. Do you think that you are justly indignant against the Carmelite brothers, the servants of the mother of God? What is it that displeases you in their order? Its kind or its form? Its name or its virtue? Its life or its merit?

If its kind, hear Scripture. For we are also the offspring of God. “You,” says the chief apostle, “are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition, that you may announce the virtues of him who called you out of darkness into his admirable light.” It is God’s planting; take care that you do not speak harshly against the saints. God chose the order of the Carmelites for his own honor and that of his mother; if this displeases you, you attack the ordering of the Almighty.

If its form displeases you, hear what the Lord says: “The beauty of the field is with me.” This is the field which the Lord has blessed, in which the lilies of virtues and the flowers of piety grow. No blessed man can curse this field, so holy, so fruitful, and so beloved of God. If the name, splendid with the Virgin’s trophy, displeases you, you are the more foolish for quarreling about a word while setting aside the majesty of the things. The Carmelites possess a blessed name; whoever curses it will be subject to curses.

If the virtue of the brothers moves you to rivalry, you are envious, because you are tormented by another’s goodness. Indeed, whoever hates virtue in his neighbor does injury to God, who bestows merits and rewards. But if the life of the Carmelites displeases you, say whose: that of some, or of all? You are rash if by one sentence you condemn so many brothers, from whose holy way of life you yourself still stand far removed. For to those of us who read the histories of the ancients it is clear that many most holy men have shone in this order. Nor is there any doubt that today very many devout brothers are found who most gloriously adorn that order with their virtues.

But if the weakness of some brothers scandalizes you, you are impious, for you spread the fault of a few over all, and you show that you yourself lie far from the perfection of the saints, while you bear others’ defects impatiently. But I know why you are moved against the holy order of the Carmelites. There are two things in it that displease you: one is that the brothers call themselves brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin; the other is that they proclaim Elijah and the sons of the prophets as the founders and princes of their order. These are the heads of the opposing party; these are the foundations of another’s disturbance.

But, O foolish presumption of the human condition, which cannot stand tranquil in consideration of its own humility, but always strives, through the zeal of depraved rivalry, to surpass one equal to itself. Why do you envy the Carmelite brothers the title of their order, which the queen of heaven conferred upon them? For truly, if the lady of the world wished to show herself a sister to the Carmelites, who can contradict her? Is she not permitted to do what she wishes? Or is your eye wicked because she is good? Do not be troubled; do not be angry; do not gnash your teeth. No one can prevent the queen of heaven from calling her Carmelites her brothers and sons.

I do not speak frivolous things, nor false ones, since the supreme Roman pontiffs, the successors of Peter, have been accustomed to call the Carmelites nothing other than brothers of the Mother of God. Turn over the acts of general councils and the apostolic bulls, and you will never find the Carmelites called apart from the honor of the Marian title. Does this seem little to you, and do you ask greater testimonies from me? There is no greater testimony than the Church of God, since whatever she has approved will no longer need the testimony of another.

But if the excellence of this title also delights you, why are you indignant against the holy brothers? Take it up, display it, preach it: they, unharmed, will by no means forbid you.
