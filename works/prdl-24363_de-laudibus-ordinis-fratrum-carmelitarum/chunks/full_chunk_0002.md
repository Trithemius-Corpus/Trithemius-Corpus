Nor indeed is this unjust. For that book shines with such splendor, blooms with such charm, is strengthened by such gravity and truth, that anyone who thinks it unworthy is mad. The whole work proceeds clause by clause; and if you examine it diligently and keenly, you will find its prose discourse [unclear] in certain feet. In those feet, I say, so that no one may gnaw at us with his tooth, which the most acute [unclear] prescribes as to be observed by an orator. For it is not enough for a most diligent man to delight his readers with truth and the majesty of things, unless he has also moved those same readers by elegance of diction and a most eloquent and [unclear] style. And this indeed justly. For to commit one’s thoughts to writing, and neither to order them nor to make them, by a certain pleasantness and sweetness, soothe one’s hearers, belongs to a man who abuses both leisure and letters. Therefore let rude and unlearned men at last cease to speak ill, who mock what they cannot grasp. For they object to the poetic and oratorical faculty in an eloquent man, just as if the ancient doctors had not used the same style and manner of speaking. Nor indeed do they themselves know what poetry is or what rhetoric is; since answer has been made by me to them in other places, to refute them here would seem both too long and superfluous. But we shall not abandon our undertaking, on account of which we have all good men as friends. This indeed, among many others, the most reverend lord Johannes Lathomus, prior of Crutzen, especially attests by words and deeds. Him, because he is worthy of true praise and does not seek human praises but strives toward the prize above, it is not fitting for us to praise. These things, venerable father, I have written briefly to your charity, knowing that you are not delighted by fleeting praises, since you are wholly turned within. Always revolving in your mind that saying of Persius: Do not seek yourself outside yourself. For the rest, I pray from my heart that, mindful of the purpose conceived and inspired by God, you may pursue your intention, and that you may bring your mind to believe that I love your paternity. If you do this, as you ought to do, you will always find mutual charity. For nothing has ever been more pleasant to me than to be loved by those whom I could rightly and justly love in return. For to displease the wicked is the highest praise. Farewell, honored father. Written very hastily on the Ides of November, in the year of salvation 1493.
