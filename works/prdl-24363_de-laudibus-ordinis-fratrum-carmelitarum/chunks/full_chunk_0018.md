Cyril, another priest of Mount Carmel, the third prior general in this order, a learned and devout man, and illumined by the spirit of prophecy, wrote some small works of his ability. Among these are reported several letters to various persons, and certain revelations addressed to Abbot Joachim; also the Angelic Table, which he is said to have received from the hands of an angel. He is said to have written some other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Henry VI and Pope Innocent III, in the year of the Lord 1200. Those err who think these two Cyrils are one, since neither the time nor the order of deeds agrees for them. For it is certain that the first flourished under Theodosius at the Council of Ephesus, and this one under Henry VI, as we have said.

Nicolaus Gallicus, the seventh prior general of the whole order, a learned and devout man and singularly beloved by God, after he had governed the order energetically for some years, resigned his office and returned to the hermitage of monastic life. Among other things he wrote a distinguished volume which he entitled The Fiery Arrow, in which with many groans he deplores the lamentable state of his order. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1270.

Albertus of Trapani, provincial prior of Sicily, a man zealous and most learned in the divine Scriptures, and also devout in life and conduct, and, while still living, shining with miracles, is said to have written many useful things for the edification of the brethren; these, however, have not come into our hands. He died in the city of Messina in the year of the Lord 1292, in the fifth indiction, on the seventh [before the Ides?] of August, glorious in signs.

William of Samuco, a singular ornament of the Carmelite order, a most learned man and distinguished professor of sacred theology, a Gaul by nation, wrote beautiful and suitable volumes for the edification of simple brethren. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, four books; Chronicle on the Multiplication of His Order, one book; Declarations of His Rule, one book; Decretals of Religious, one book. He is also said to have written some other things, which however have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in 1280.

Gerardus of Bologna, the eleventh prior general of the whole order, a doctor of sacred Scripture at Paris, subtle in intellect, and a most celebrated lover and ruler of his order, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he commended his memory to posterity: On the Sentences, four books; many and various quodlibets, one book; also Questions, one book; various sermons, two books. He began a notable Summa of Theology, which, overtaken by death, he could not finish. At length he died at Avignon and was buried, in the year of the Lord 1317, in the fifteenth indiction, and in the thirty-first year of his generalate.

Sibertus de Beka, a German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, a doctor celebrated by the opinion and praise of all, and a most devout cultivator of his order, wrote certain excellent small works for the edification of the brethren. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, seven books; On the Censures of the New Law, one book; Considerations on the Rule of His Order, one book. He corrected the Ordinal of the Lord’s Sepulchre, which was decreed to be observed throughout the whole order. He also published some other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Guido Perpinianus, a Catalan by nation, the twelfth prior general, then bishop of Majorca and afterward bishop of Elne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, acute in intellect, profound in knowledge, scholastic in speech, who was held in honor and reverence by Pope John XXII, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he commended his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported: addressed to Pope John XXII, a distinguished work in three parts, On the Perfection of the Catholic Life, three books; On the Sentences, seven books; Six Quodlibets, one book; various ordinary disputed questions, one book.
