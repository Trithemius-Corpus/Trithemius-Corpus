In the eighth indiction, on the third day before the Nones of November.

John Bernega, English by nation, a doctor most learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally well taught in secular letters, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people and in disputing questions of the Scriptures, wrote a distinguished work on the Sentences, in three books, and certain other things. He flourished under Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Michael Herbrant of Düren, German by nation, prior of the convent of Kreuznach, a man zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures and renowned for experience in many matters, outstanding in talent and an excellent preacher of the word of God, wrote certain little works not to be despised in reading, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there survive many notable sermons, one book; also many and varied conferences delivered in chapters and synods, one book. The rest that he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Rupert the Bavarian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1410.

John Gauwer, German by nation, lector of the convent of Mainz, zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, wrote certain distinguished little works, of which I have found the following: On the Sentences, four books; On Exodus, one book; One Gospel from the Four, one book; Sermons on the season, one book; also Sermons on the saints, one book; a Lenten book, one book. The other things he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Sigismund in the year of the Lord 1430, and was buried in his convent at Mainz.

Jacob of Reggio, Italian by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and magnificently imbued with secular letters, skilled in the three principal languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, renowned in talent and eloquent in speech, excelling in verse and prose, wrote notable sermons for Lent, one book. He is also said to have written many things in verse, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1460.

John Soreth, Norman by nation, prior general of the whole order, a man learned and trained in the divine Scriptures, subtle in talent, composed in speech, devout and most holy in life and conduct, the greatest and most fervent reformer of his order, for whose establishment he very patiently endured many hard labors, wrote certain distinguished volumes by which he made the vein of his talent known also to his posterity. Of these the following are preserved: On the Sentences, seven books; Various Sermons, one book. On the rule of his order he published a twofold commentary, two books. He also compiled the constitutions of the same order. He died at Angers under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1471, in the fourth indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of August, shining daily with many miracles.

Oswin Hex, from Brabant by homeland, of the convent of Vlissingen, bishop of Hierapolis, vicar in pontificals of the bishop of Utrecht, a man exceptionally learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence, very learned in delivering sermons to the people, wrote On the Ten Commandments, one book; Directory of a Troubled Conscience, one book; Sermons on the season, one book; Sermons on the saints, one book; and certain other things. He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1475, in the seventh indiction, on the Kalends of April.

John Beets, German by nation, of the convent of Tienen, a very celebrated doctor of sacred letters and much learned in secular philosophy, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence, an acute disputant, and an excellent preacher of the word, venerable no less for his conduct than for his learning, wrote for the edification of the brethren certain distinguished volumes, among which the following are preserved: On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, one book; Preceptorium of the Divine Law, ten books; On the Sacrament of the Altar, one book; Sermons on the season, one book; also Sermons on the saints, one book; Various Disputed Questions, one book; and certain other things. He died in the year of the Lord 1486.
