[There is no need of going] to Athens for learning, because at home a sufficient Minerva is available to the diligent. But let us beware lest we become teachers of error to ourselves. Study that is almost solitary, unless it burns with the love of Christ, often begets error. Let us therefore apply the oil of compunction, and the learning of the Scriptures will be useful. Fathers, let us look to our commonwealth, and, as far as is possible for us, let us labor for its just [unclear]. Let each one of us strive to reform himself according to the Rule of Saint Benedict: let him flee vices, spurn pleasures, not love riches, despise the world. Let us be abbots of our brothers according to the Rule: shepherds in solicitude, fathers in charity. Let us feed them by word; let us feed them by example; let us feed them with nourishment. If anyone omits one of these, he will be a useless shepherd. Therefore you shepherds, remember to whom you are going to render account; and so stand watch over your custody solicitously, so that, concerning the sheep committed to you, you may be able to answer securely to the supreme shepherd. Adorn your commonwealth with morals; adorn it with saving knowledge; adorn it with piety; adorn it with love, so that, when in this brief misery you have ministered well, you may obtain an excellent rank in eternal happiness, which may the supreme shepherd Jesus Christ grant us, who lives and reigns without end. Amen.
