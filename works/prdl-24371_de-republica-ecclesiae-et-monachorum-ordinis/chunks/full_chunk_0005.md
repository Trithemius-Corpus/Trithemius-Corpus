“Because,” he says, “iniquity will abound, the charity of many will grow cold.”

Iniquity abounds no less among the people than among the clergy: anger rages, lust storms, pride is mad, discord has broken the bonds of peace. All things are full of crimes, and not even in the monasteries are crimes kept away. Where now is the one heart and one soul of the multitude of believers? And where is the fervor of the Christian faith; where integrity of morals, where purity of actions? And where is zeal for discipline, where the keeping of God’s commandments, where love of the heavenly mansions? O Christians, Christians, where now among you is the concord of brotherly love, where faith, where piety, where sincerity of hearts?

And O holy commonwealth, where now is the appearance of your beauty, with which, once adorned, you shone like a most brilliant star above all the kingdoms of the earth? And where is your honor, where fear, where reverence for authority? It has fallen; it has abandoned its former adornment; it has grown cold from the love of heavenly charity; it has withdrawn from discipline; it has spurned virtues; it has despised observance of the law. Behold, you have fallen among the robbers of crimes, among the swords of wicked deeds, among the arrows of vices. Behold, you have been left despoiled and almost naked and half-alive, while inwardly you are stripped of your former virtues, and outwardly of temporal goods.

O you priests, you Levites, you prelates of the churches and guardians of the Lord’s flock, who see the desolation and ruin of your commonwealth and pretend not to see it: how will you stand before the tribunal of the just judge Jesus Christ, who will require from you the most exact account of the souls of all those committed to you? The mighty arbiter has entrusted to you the care of his sheep; do not doubt that he will require their blood from your hands. Woe to you. Set in place to rebuke vices, you commit vices, and by your wicked morals you slay the souls of those subject to you. O what will you answer to the supreme shepherd at the last judgment, when he begins to require his sheep from your hands? And behold, because of your sins the people has fallen, the commonwealth is destroyed, the treasure of the Church is dissipated, preaching is scorned, the granting of indulgences is despised. But why should I use many words? From the sole of the foot to the crown of the head there is no health in our commonwealth. All seek what is their own, not what belongs to Jesus Christ.

For this reason the people of the Lord has been led into captivity; Constantinople and so much of the empire have been captured by infidels; cities have been destroyed, churches burned, monasteries demolished; Christians, from the child to the elder, have either been slain by a harsh death or sold into slavery with harsher cruelty. Almost all Asia has abandoned the Christian faith; Africa has withdrawn from Christianity; in Europe alone the Catholic faith, though weak, has remained. Alas, how narrow is the ecclesiastical estate, which once, with the greatest honor, had been spread through all the earth. Take up this lament over it, O pontiffs, priests, prelates, and rulers of the Christian commonwealth; and while you bewail the danger of others, fear for your own.

For I fear, and I fear greatly, that iniquity long dissembled may at last come to be punished with a heavier chastisement. If [unclear] God did not spare the sanctified land in which, clothed in human flesh, he dwelt, in what way will he spare ours? Our desolation is nearer than we believe, our destruction closer than we fear. Foolishly we presume upon security, who see so many Christian lands and kingdoms of ours captured by infidels.

But now I have said enough about the general commonwealth, lest the shorter hour compel us to leave our own behind. There is also another commonwealth of the laity, which is called temporal; and although it too, in many respects, seems already quite miserable enough, yet since it does not pertain to us, let it also be left out of our discourse this time. Now at last, reverend fathers, after a long discourse on the common commonwealth, which, as I was able, I have had at length from my elders, let us come to our own commonwealth, which concerns us more. The commonwealth, therefore, about which we intend to speak, is our most sacred order, which follows the ruin of the ecclesiastical commonwealth, to which it is also compared by a sufficiently close likeness. For just as that one, almost in our own times, has lost an empire, kingdoms, and provinces, so also ours, in recent memory, has lost many and very wealthy monasteries in these regions. But before we begin to describe its present state, it is pleasing to set forth a little what kind it once was. And although I have done this to some extent in the preceding conferences, nevertheless I do not think it will be superfluous if we repeat more often what has been well said.
