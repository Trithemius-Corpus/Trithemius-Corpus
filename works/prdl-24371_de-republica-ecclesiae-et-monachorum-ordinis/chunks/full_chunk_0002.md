As I was undertaking, reverend fathers in Christ, for the merit of obedience, to deliver an address before your paternities according to laudable custom, after I had long and much deliberated silently with myself what material I should bring forward that would more effectively stir your minds to devotion for the order, I was finally persuaded by certain venerable men of your council that, by the gift of the Most High, I should discourse on our commonwealth, for love of which, and for its defense and adornment, you have come together into this assembly from distant regions of the earth.

It is holy and salutary, I confess, to speak of your commonwealth, O fathers, both holy and religious; but it belongs to a learned man, an educated man, a holy man, and one fit for persuading, such as I know I am not, since I am poor in talent, bare of merit, uncultivated in speech. How, then, should I dare presume to speak of this most excellent commonwealth, when I am slow at invention, unpolished in speaking, cold in persuading? Another, therefore, fathers, another orator should have been sought for you: one to whom talent would supply powers for speaking, learning understanding for teaching, and for persuading doctrine no less than merit would be fitting. But why do I struggle in vain, since I am compelled to obey? Therefore, although the burden imposed far exceeds the powers of my talent, yet, trusting in the mercy of the Almighty and the aid of obedience, I shall undertake the work enjoined, not so much willingly as cheerfully.

Nor shall I need Plato in ten books, nor Aristotle in eight, nor Tullius in six volumes on the commonwealth, since our Lord Jesus has supplied abundant material for discoursing on this matter according to our intention in today's Gospel, when he said: "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and he fell among robbers," etc. Although this parable is principally to be understood of the perdition of the whole human race, nevertheless nothing prevents us, without prejudice to the faith, from interpreting it also with reference to our commonwealth. For the condition of the commonwealth, not only ours but that of all religious, indeed of the whole Church militant, has fallen away from Jerusalem into Jericho.

For Jericho is interpreted as the moon, or its waning. Therefore he goes down from Jerusalem into Jericho who, falling away from justice, approaches the spring of vices. But before I proceed further to the explanation of our theme, since in all actions, especially theological ones, one must begin from prayer, lest we strive in vain, we must implore divine aid, without which the speech of the speaker profits nothing and the intention of the hearer bears no fruit. Therefore, to obtain grace for speaking and to gain fruit from hearing, let us salute the most holy mother of God, the inviolate Virgin Mary, with devout mind and sincere intention, bending our knees to the ground in prayer, saying: Hail Mary.

I should very much wish, reverend fathers, for the glory of almighty God, that such power of speaking had been bestowed on me from above that, for your edification, compunction, and devotion, I might be able most aptly to develop, according to the condition of our commonwealth, the theme taken from the Gospel of the present Sunday. For just as that man going down from Jerusalem into Jericho, after blows had been inflicted by robbers, was despoiled and left half alive, so the commonwealth of the universal Church, so the condition of almost all religions and orders, so too your commonwealth, O fathers, lies despoiled and half-destroyed, with blows of wicked deeds inflicted everywhere, with wounds of crimes, with punctures of vices.

And to begin from the general commonwealth, which regards all faithful Christians, who through the Catholic faith are reckoned as one: where is the condition of its former splendor, where is faith, where is love of God, where is love of brotherhood? Behold, we see in the commonwealth of Christians that avarice, lust, pride, discord, anger, envy, and the other plagues of the soul abound everywhere; with savage cruelty, like robbers, they lacerate the condition of the Church with very grave blows, despoil it, strip it bare, and slay it, so that, left half alive, it has neither hope nor the beauty of its ancient loveliness.

What will you do, O holy mother Church? What physician for your wounds will you call upon? A priest? But having seen you, he passes by; he spurns your distress, despises your pains, scorns your wounds. A Levite? But the bad minister of a bad priest does not love your honor, casts aside care, hates the healing of wounds. A Samaritan?
