But God’s illumination dwells very far from the slothful, the wicked, gluttons, the envious, the greedy, lechers, the filthy, the lustful, murderers, the impious, the proud, and those estranged from piety, leaving them to the judgment of the avenging demon, who, striking with the sharp point of cruel fire, afflicts sense and mind, and arms and drives the impious still more to commit crimes, so that, guilty of a more shameful fault, they may also become liable to a sharper punishment. Hence, entangled in vices and worldly desires, they not only cannot penetrate the divine Scriptures with understanding, but, neglecting themselves, do not even know themselves.

Since, therefore, sacred Scripture is God’s perfect work, we must so strengthen our mind that we believe it true, right, whole, solid, divinely inspired, and most abundantly sufficient for all who understand it for the confirmation of the Christian faith. For indeed, unless divine Scripture is believed to be true, holy, perfect in all things, and whole, the Christian faith would be deprived of its foundation. Every Christian must therefore take the greatest care lest, by scrutinizing the divine Scriptures more than is fitting, he overstep the bounds of piety; for the author of Scripture is not man, but the Holy Spirit, and whoever struggles against the integrity of God’s Scriptures is convicted of resisting his ordering.

Now Scripture, by God’s ordinance, is beneficially interwoven with parables, riddles, likenesses, and comparisons of figures, so that readers may be exercised toward understanding and, once its inward sweetness has been tasted, the mind may be strengthened toward piety. And so, if with purified minds we attend to the sense of the Scriptures and not to the words alone, we shall understand that all things have been ordered rightly and properly. For it was not fitting that the sacred Scriptures, in which knowledge of the divine majesty is handed down, should be so plain and manifest that they would be understood everywhere by everyone, since they are set forth to be understood only by the good and holy, not also by evil, filthy, and polluted minds.

For this reason it is written: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be stoned.” What is designated here by the beast, except human beings who, in imitation of brute animals, live without the love of God in the pleasures of the flesh? And what is expressed by the mountain, except the height of the divine Scriptures? When the beast approaches this holy mountain, it is overwhelmed with stones, because a person impure in mind, or weak in faith, who attempts to search out the height of the divine Scriptures without a teacher handing it down, is wounded by various and pernicious errors as though by blows of stones. For just as all things are clean to the clean, so all things seem profane to the profane.

God therefore set his hiding place in the height of the Scriptures, which is so inaccessible and closed to the reprobate that it nevertheless always lies plainly open to the saints. But why did almighty God not give holy Scripture as sufficient and manifest for everything that concerns the Christian faith? We answer briefly: there are two things which the Most High provided for believers for the confirmation of the Christian faith, namely the Church and Scripture, so that what is not expressed clearly enough in the one may be declared by the other. Hence, if we find anything in the holy Scriptures expressed less sufficiently concerning the rules of faith, we at once have recourse to the Church; and where the Church has need of saving instruction for piety, it immediately turns to Scripture. From this it follows that neither would the Church have authority without Scripture, nor Scripture faith without the Church, so that from the two one judgment may be made.
