There is, most wise Caesar, no one who can express it. For, first of all, denying God, the faith of Christ, and holy baptism, they offer themselves in mind and body to demons; by their evil arts they weaken, injure, and kill men and beasts. There is no disease which, if God permits it, they cannot bring on, because, when they present themselves as servants to demons in observance of the covenant contracted with them, in comparison with those spirits they dare whatever they wish.

Therefore they are not to be tolerated at all, but rather are to be utterly exterminated in every place, as God, the creator of all, commands: “You shall not suffer sorcerers to live.” Some witches call upon demons with spells; others entice them with prayers and sacrifices; the rest, provoking them with unspeakable things and ceremonies, draw them into the service of their impiety.

It is therefore not surprising that witches seem to command demons, since through the impiety of a perverse will they have become their servants, and by mutual association are united for the harm of the human race.

The second kind of wicked men who wish to command demons is that of those commonly called necromancers, who profess arts full of abominations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly visible, enclosed in a crystal or some other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of lies and superstitions, commit such evils that no good man could imagine them. For in order to entice demons into their service, they commonly use the foulest sacrifices and enter into an explicit pact with them.

They circulate many various little books full of filth, impiety, and falsehood, deceitfully inscribed with the names of ancient philosophers and sages; by displaying these they deceive many unwary and curious people and drag them with themselves into the pit of demons. They promise great things, indeed things wholly incredible, and by hearing of them they deceitfully hold men suspended in amazement. They say that by rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations they can compel malignant demons to appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal, or in some other receptacle, and to answer distinctly all questions put to them.

All of them indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found whom malignant spirits, even evil ones, obey in this way, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it, lest human curiosity, prone to novelties, should despise the religion of Christ and in time of need seek [unclear] assistance from demons. Yet all those whom malignant spirits obey when summoned by the conjurations of necromancers have without doubt passed into their fellowship through an explicit or implicit pact. But the number of the others is greater, whom malignant spirits disdain to obey, although they are not afraid to busy themselves at all times with the profane arts of necromancers.

And because iniquity has begun to abound especially in our times, and the charity of many has grown cold, these accursed and most lying men have become too powerful; and although they are despised by demons, nevertheless, persisting in their pernicious doctrines, they deceive many. Believe me, Caesar, this most deceitful class of men is very harmful to your holy empire, and therefore, no less than the brood of witches, must be utterly exterminated.

They promise great things, and they lie in everything. For in truth all the things promised in their books are empty and false, and they offer those who believe them nothing except tricks, frauds, and the most subtle deceptions. But if at times they seem to present what they have promised, it should be clear to all that this happens only by the cooperation of demons, from whose fellowship one must flee no less than from the most savage [unclear].
