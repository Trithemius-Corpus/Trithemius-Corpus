QUESTION SEVEN

It is manifest that the weak enemy of the human race, the devil, is in all things subject to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing by himself, but everything he does he does with God’s permission. Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God permits sorceries to be done, is justice: by it he preserves in inviolate order the freedom that he once bestowed upon the creature from the beginning.

But because the power of every creature dependent on its Creator is restricted and limited, it is manifest that neither the devil can do everything he wishes, nor can a man, however depraved in will. The will indeed is free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wish, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would shake everything. The story of holy Job shows by manifest example that the power of demons is bound: Satan did not dare to tempt him any further than had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in this present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons, if they could always use a free power to harm according to their own choice.

Before the Lord’s Passion they used a greater freedom of power than afterward; but when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, their power was thereafter greatly diminished, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of this world will be cast out.” Indeed, Christ’s humility and the perseverance of the saints have greatly restrained the boldness of demons, so that they can rightly be called inciters of evils, but by no means compellers. Nor would the devil grow strong against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” And again: “Do not give place to the devil.”

Therefore, to conclude the discussion for the understanding of the question proposed, namely why God permits so many sorceries to be done, I believe the reasons have been made sufficiently clear. For although the will of demons is always wicked, nevertheless their power can never be unjust, since what they wickedly will they have from their own choice, but what they are able wickedly to do they have only from God.

And these things have been said insofar as they concern those who do evil. But why does almighty God permit demons to harm, through the agency of witches, people who have been baptized in the name of the holy Trinity and faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ? For if they could harm only the wicked, the perverse, and unbelievers in body and possessions, but were restrained from approaching and injuring the good and faithful, the judgment of this permission would rightly seem to be just. But now the good and innocent are more often weakened by the sorcery of demons, and sometimes are even deprived of life, to pass over in silence the loss of temporal possessions that those bewitched often suffer.

It comes as a scruple to many why the Almighty permits these things, since he permits nothing unjustly. For the purpose of this question proposed, I shall briefly set forth in order, as far as I can, the necessary reasons, which I have received from none other than the ancient and Catholic teachers.

The first reason is the testing of Christians’ faith and the proving of their integrity, so that those scourged by the sorcery of demons may show, in the sufferings of the flesh, what faith and love toward God they have in the hidden part of the mind. It is not difficult to be patient in prosperity, but in adversity to await with humble patience help from the Savior of the faithful belongs not to the weak, but to the strong and truly faithful Christians. In his tribulation most holy Job learned how great a constancy of faith he had toward God, which before in tranquility he had preserved without [unclear].
