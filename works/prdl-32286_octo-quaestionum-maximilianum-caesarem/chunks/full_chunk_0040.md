What, finally, concerning the savior of all, our Lord Jesus Christ, except that he will reckon the whole thing an empty fable and a fiction, if there is no providence of God caring for mortal things? Therefore whatever good we do in this life, whatever evil we endure for the love of God, is wholly vain and useless to us, if he has no care at all for us. Let those now see and shudder who deny the providence of God, when they behold the most evil end of their opinion: the despair of all. Therefore let no one, clothed in the vain talk of secular philosophers, train himself to contempt of God; let no one take away the providence of the highest and immutable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of and ungrateful for the benefits of the most merciful savior. For it must be firmly believed and in no way doubted that this whole world, visible and invisible, and all things and each thing that are either in heaven or on earth and in the sea, are ruled and governed by divine providence, and that absolutely nothing in the whole universe is alien to his care. For both the crops and all the rest that the earth brings forth, and also storms and the varieties of time and changes of the sky,

by which all things that the earth brings forth grow to their natural maturity, divine providence furnishes and provides for the human race, all things and each thing; not only great things, but also the very least, so much so that not even the very slightest leaf of a tree falls to the ground without the will of almighty God the Father, who created, rules, and cares for them. But if anyone thinks that this so great magnitude of the whole universe cannot be ruled and governed by the eternal God, who alone exists, he is greatly mistaken, and is proved not to know how great is the power of divine might. Indeed, he who was able to bring the whole world from nothing into being is also able most wisely to govern forever what he made. For he knows all things, understands all things, can do all things, who alone, omnipotent, created all things. Therefore we, believing the holy Gospel, should above all venerate divine providence, and lift up our minds, with whatever virtue we can, from these perishable things, and, brought back to our very selves, be united: so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God present everywhere, which, since it is altogether uncircumscribable, holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, surpasses all things, and sustains all things. surpass from another, nor fill from one part and encompass from another; rather, by encompassing it fills, and by filling it encompasses; by sustaining and by surpassing it sustains all things. He is nowhere by the mass of a body, and by his uncircumscribable substance he is lacking nowhere; he is not seen by the eyes of flesh, but he is seen through faith in purity of heart. O unfathomable power, O incomprehensible compassion, O ineffable wisdom, O uncircumscribable divinity: which is not bounded by parts as a body is, not distinguished by affections as a soul is, not subject to forms as all other things are which have been made by you, you who are in all things and above all things, ruling and governing the universe most wisely. Thus by these testimonies, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, it has been sufficiently proved, as I think, that this whole world is governed by the providence of God: he, since he is pure intellect, simple, uncreated, and eternal, knows all things, understands all things, recognizes all things, and because he is omnipotent by nature, he can do all things, for whom nothing is difficult, nothing impossible; who by his power created things so wonderful, established them in truth, perfected them by reason, sustains them by strength, illuminates them by goodness. To him be praise, to him honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, glorious and exalted God, forever and ever. Amen.
