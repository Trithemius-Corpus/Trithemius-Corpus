It is useful and very profitable because it brings forth many true meanings and brings them to light for human beings; when one person draws out this sense, and another draws out another, while expounding it in a catholic way, it becomes known how lofty and deep it is. Therefore no man swollen with secular learning should think that he understands sacred Scripture unless he first lays aside the veil of pride and of every disordered desire. For secular learning is more effective for exercising the intellect than for enlightening the understanding toward true wisdom.

Therefore divine Scripture is, without doubt, clear and bright to minds purified by the continual exercise of the fear of God and by frequent zeal for reading; but to filthy minds, and to those puffed up with secular wisdom, it seems confused and obscure. Hence he understands the holy Scriptures well and rightly who, with purified affection and frequent in the study of them, expounds them as did the one who wrote them. For if they read them in a spirit other than that by which he is believed to have inspired them to be written, the mind must be wholly shut out from the true understanding. But when we encounter some discordance in them, let us understand that it is only of words, not of meanings; and that this arises not by chance, but by the providence of God and in due order, so that the mind, exercised in the Scriptures, may be strengthened the longer and not grow wholly sluggish. For the counsel of God cannot be mistaken; but the divine Scriptures were written according to the counsel of his will. It necessarily follows that they are true and in no way false.

They were human beings by whom the divine Scriptures were handed down to us; certainly they wrote words used according to human custom, by which they might signify to posterity what that supreme excellence of divinity wished to be known to mortals.

Therefore you now, who, devoted to secular wisdom, attack the divine Scriptures, speak: the Scripture of the New and Old Testament, which the Church of Christians has received and venerates as true, authentic, and holy, from where do you believe it was received? From God, or from human beings? If from God, why do you not believe it? For everyone who attacks Scripture, while believing it to be inspired by God, most plainly resists the divine ordering. But if it is from human beings, why do you hope in it for the promised eternal salvation, which a human being cannot give? Unless perhaps, in your madness, you go on to say that all things are fabricated, and that nothing remains after death except ashes, and that the mind perishes together with the body; this profession would show you no less insane than foolish. For if you truly believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, you will necessarily say that the divine Scriptures were inspired by the Holy Spirit. But if, having become apostates into a reprobate mind, you say they were composed by human beings, you impiously show how far you are from true wisdom, wandering in darkness.

You openly show yourselves to be unskilled and rash judges, since you condemn a Scripture that you do not understand. Truly, because it is more familiar to you to despise wisdom than to learn it, it is not the purpose of this work to weary the mind any longer against the rashness of the unskilled. This one thing I want you to know: no one understands unless he receives; but because you place the filth of your affections in the way, so that you may not receive, you therefore do not have the true understanding of wisdom. For almighty God deigns to enlighten by grace those who, stripped of carnal desires, burn with the fire of inward love, who desire nothing in this world except God. The Spirit of God, by his presence, brings help to those whom he finds rooted in the love of truth, and, purified from every foreign thing, he leads them into the knowledge of all things. For truly, to those who love God, all things are open, all things bare, all things manifest; for whoever, through love, beholds in the faith of Jesus Christ the one who creates and governs all things, [unclear] all things that are necessary for his salvation.
