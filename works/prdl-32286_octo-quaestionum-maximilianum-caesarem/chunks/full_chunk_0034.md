Hence it must also be noted that those whom the sorceries of demons or men have in any way made sick, and who, in order to recover health, approach or consult sorcerers and enchanters, whether by themselves or through others, act against the commandments of God and the Church, and have neither true faith in God nor love for him.

For they prefer the health of mortal flesh to God, and seek salvation not from Christ but from the devil and his minister. For they would rather live healthy but unhappily in the name of the devil than suffer a little here for love of Christ and afterward rejoice with him forever. But the man who does not go to sorcerers because God has forbidden it, preferring to live in observance of God’s commandments in poverty and sickness rather than against God in riches and bodily health, truly loves God and rightly keeps the integrity of the Christian faith.

Among Christians, most sacred Caesar, men corrupt in mind and reprobate concerning the faith are not to be tolerated: those who cure the bewitched by sorceries, who reveal thefts and adulteries through the ministry of demons, who foretell the future by inspecting stones or mirrors. Do not listen, I beg you, to those who, ignorant of the divine laws, think such ministers of demons useful for your rule because they know how to undo sorceries by sorcery; for that ruinous healing of bodies brings death upon souls, and makes men withdraw entirely from God their creator and cling to impious, apostate demons, with whom after death they will be damned forever.

The second reason why almighty God permits the innocent to be harmed by sorcery is the increase of merits: namely, so that their patience, tested in tribulation, may deserve a worthier crown of glory in heaven. For, as holy Augustine says, a man is unknown to himself; sometimes he fears that he cannot bear what he can bear, and yet, when temptation comes, a man is found by himself as though by a questioner, who previously lay hidden and did not know himself.

The blessed chief apostle Peter, before temptation came upon him, was hidden both from himself and from others, thinking himself firm and strong; but that temptation came, and his weakness appeared, which had been hidden. Therefore temptation is the greatest testing of man, as Sacred Scripture testifies: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men.” Patience is easy in prosperity, when no adversities strike with fear. But many are the tribulations of the righteous; yet from them all our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the preservation of humility, so that the faithful and righteous man may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or righteousness. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: “They are not in the labor of men, and with men they shall not be scourged. Therefore pride has held them; they are covered with iniquity; out of their wickedness their iniquity has come forth, as it were from fatness.” It belongs to the mercy of God that he sometimes permits good and righteous men to be shaken by adversities, for he knows that in prosperity they cannot be humbled. For tribulation has made many humble whom good fortune would have made proud. Finally, the fearful imagining of sorcerers makes a good man fear his God and diligently call upon his help, so that, better defended by it, he may trust himself safe against the assaults of demons. But let no proud man, presuming on his own powers, think himself safe from the sorceries of demons; for the man whom God’s protection abandons because he is swollen with pride undoubtedly lies open to the sorceries of demons.
