Sometimes, however, when invoked by conjurations, they disdain to appear, and nevertheless they have led away the minds of those who invoke them.

There is also another kind of people, no less foolish than superstitious: namely those who wish to have no commerce with demons, and nevertheless use, in vain operations, things assigned to demons and therefore wholly unlawful. For they think that great and hidden power resides in characters, and for that reason they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But since this is not the time to recount their madness, let us pursue our subject.

Why good and just men do not obtain command over demons must be briefly learned. Just as likeness of wills makes wicked and impious men equal to demons, so unlikeness of wills makes the good and just entirely opposed to them. But among opposites and things unlike there can be no familiarity; rather, continual resistance and hatred prevail. Where there is no familiarity contracted by likeness of wills, there can be neither confidence of one toward the other, nor mutual cooperation in a marvelous operation. Nor do you, glorious Caesar, a man, the great ruler and prince of men, admit as your familiar anyone wholly contrary and unlike to you in morals and constantly resisting your will.

Good and upright men, because they always resist the most depraved will of demons, can in no way pass into their fellowship, for they neither wish nor consent to be their familiars, but flee and detest their encounter with all their strength. Therefore they do not obtain over them that fictitious command of which we have spoken. For no familiarity is secure where no likeness of wills has preceded it; nor can there be concord among opposites, nor friendship where the will is unlike.

Yet demons do not obey all the wicked and reprobate indiscriminately, but only those with whom they are allied by pacts and services, and whom they cannot otherwise have faithful to them; when invoked, they willingly come, though they falsely claim that they are compelled. Therefore it is not to be wondered at, most excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to obey depraved and perverse men, whom, as we say, malice has made like them and destruction against the human race has made harmonious with them.

But far be it from every Christian to wish to command demons by a depraved likeness of will, since if anyone begins in this life to have fellowship with malignant spirits, in the future he will burn with them without end.

But why can good and just men not command the holy angels of God, into whose likeness of good will they have passed? I think that, from what has already been said, the solution to this question would easily be given, if the difference of understandings is recalled to mind. For although there are many men established in the Church of God whose righteousness benefits many unto salvation, nevertheless in our times very few of the common sort advance so far that, by the merit of holiness, they are either feared by demons or received by angels into the fellowship of familiarity. Hence it happens that men of our age neither command malignant spirits by merit of holiness, nor deserve to be familiar with the holy angels. But if any have advanced to that degree of holiness by which, performing miracles in the Church, the saints once advanced, then they are already able powerfully both to command demons and to have familiar fellowship with the blessed spirits.

For it is certain that holy men beloved by God in the primitive Church of Christians once bestowed greater benefits upon mortals by the cooperation of good spirits than the impiety of depraved men could ever have inflicted harms by the ministry of demons. For whoever has become a true worshiper of God, like the holy angels, can do many wondrous things by prayers.

On the Power of Witches.

QUESTION SIX.

The sixth question of your unconquered Majesty was: Whence comes this power so great in acts of witchcraft, by which they can accomplish so many various and wondrous things, even in one hour such and so great things as none of the good could do in his whole life? To this question, if we wish to answer briefly, we must necessarily say that all power is from the Lord God, without whom no creature can do anything.
