Those who believe are confirmed in their errors by a lie. To illustrate this matter, let it not be irksome to introduce a comparison.

There was a certain man, a keeper of pigs, who had enclosed in a staff a little written slip containing the name of the holy martyr Blasius. He put so much faith in it that, when the staff was fixed in the ground in the midst of the herd, he believed every wolf to be barred from approaching. When this foolish confidence had at last been strengthened by long experience, the man went wherever he wished in security, leaving the pigs in the wood without danger, since no wolf now had any access to them.

It happened, while the shepherd was absent, that a certain man with sight passed by; seeing that a demon had charge of the herd, he said to him, “Wretch, what are you doing here?” He answered, “I am guarding these pigs.” The other said, “Who entrusted you with the care of the pigs?” He answered again, “The shepherd’s foolish confidence. For he thinks that a certain slip enclosed in a staff has great power, by which, through the merits of some Blasius, wolves are kept away from the pigs; he has sinned against his own law and follows our vanities. But because the one invoked does not appear, I, lest the man lose his confidence, guard his pigs, and willingly put myself in Blasius’s place.” “Thus,” he said, “mortals trouble us with vain conjurations, so that, unless we wish to lose our reputation and honor among them, we must very often come to the aid of foolish invocations.”

In a likeness of this fact, demons deceive wretched unbelievers, and the miracles that they ask of God when they pray are quite often obtained by the substitution of a more malignant spirit. For in matters that concern either the worship or the honor of God, demons willingly thrust themselves in; and since they cannot obtain open honors, they are of course delighted with hidden ones.

At the same time it must be noted that, from the beginning of the world, miracles of special excellence have never been lacking to any religion or sect, although not all have been done from one principle. For whatever miracles have been done in the true religion have been done from no other source than God. The law of nature had its miracles; the written law had its miracles; and now grace likewise has its miracles. The almighty God who gave religion also performs miracles. But in false religions, where truth is not worshiped, if any miracles are done, it is not God but the devil who works them. For since God is truth, he does not establish false religions, and therefore he has never adorned a lie with his miracles.

Muhammad, who, filled with an evil demon, established a false religion, adorned it with false miracles not from God but by the cooperation of demons. For the demon who gave the cause of error could also, when the occasion was taken, display a false miracle to the deceived. Hence, if miracles had been wholly lacking to Muhammad as he persuaded an uncultivated people to a new religion, perhaps no one would have put faith in him. For he too, both by the subtlety of his mind and by the cooperation of the devil, showed certain wonders to the peoples, so that he might seem to have confirmed a new doctrine also by new miracles.

Miracles were not lacking even to pagan idolaters, if we do not deny the histories. The Jews, however, before the incarnation of the Son of God, receiving his law through Moses, were frequently illumined by divine miracles. But after the Lord’s resurrection, when the Mosaic law had been made void, those who do not believe in the only-begotten Son of God remain under the wrath of his indignation, and all miracles of truth have ceased among them. Hence, if they perform any wonders today, they are the works of demons and manifest signs of error. For demons keep watch with the greatest zeal so that those men whom they have once made to wander from the way of truth, which is Christ, they may, by continuing their fraud, make obstinate in error even unto death. Indeed, when God for just reason withdraws his grace from unbelievers, their deceiver, the malignant spirit, approaches unrecognized; and when he sees that salvation is far away, he presents himself by false miracles as though he were the Lord present.
