But these men shamefully erred, because the Almighty has providence and care not only of all things and of incorruptible things, but also of particular and corruptible things. Hence Saint Boethius again says: “The high Creator sits upon the earth and bends the reins that govern things, king and lord, source and origin, law and wise arbiter of equity. This is [unclear] continuous love, and good things seek to be held by their end, which cannot otherwise endure unless, by love turned back again, the causes flow back to him who gave them.” But others, unable to bear that they should be neglected, exempted man from the number of corruptible things because of the pre-eminence of his intellect, saying that God has care and providence for man, but despises all the other earthly things. Against their false opinion I think enough has been said, and to bring forward many things is wholly superfluous. For Scripture says: “Men and beasts you will save, O Lord.” And in another place a certain wise man said: “The Lord made the small and the great, and he has equal care for all.” Therefore divine majesty’s providence orders, rules, disposes, and cares for all and each of the lower things, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible; its most powerful will produced all things from nothing, as sacred Scripture again bears witness: “But your providence, Father, governs all things from the beginning.”

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim.*

Let men blush, then, corrupt in mind, impious, and reprobate with regard to the Christian faith, who, puffed up with empty talkativeness, take away the providence of him who knows all things, and do not consider how great an injury they do to God. For what, to use the opinion of Firmianus, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he provided for nothing, if he neglected lower things, if he did not care for mortal things, he has now lost all dignity. But, as holy Anselm attests, it is impossible for God to lose his honor; he, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, nor corruption, nor sin. Therefore he who removes providence removes the whole substance of God; and what does he say except that God does not exist? But now let those who deny that God’s providence cares for lower things tell me: if God neglects lower things, let them consider what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is henceforth no reason why we should either follow him with worship or venerate him with religion, since we obtain no benefit from him; indeed, rather, being neglected and despised, let us neglect him himself also. But if he despises and neglects us unknowingly, there is now utterly no reason that we should worship one who does not know us and for that reason cannot confer any good, or invoke him with prayers. For if God does not care for human things, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every cause of piety ceases, since there is no hope of salvation. What consideration of piety can there be among mortals, what hope for the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human things? Indeed, if providence is taken away, whom do we worship? Whom do we adore? If there is with God no providence for mortals, certainly he neither grants benefits to men, nor imposes punishments on deserters; nor can he help mortals, nor does he wish to, nor does he care at all, nor does he attend to what we do, and he hears neither the prayers nor the petitions of anyone. Why, then, do we offer him any worship? Why honor? Why prayers? Or why do we fear or dread so greatly a God who does not know us, who, since we are neglected and despised by him, can do us neither good nor evil? Therefore, once providence has been removed, neither holiness nor religion will subsist; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disturbance of life and horrible confusion will follow. With piety toward God removed, what now will the commonwealth be except everlasting confusion? Public faith among men will necessarily perish, and the society of the human race, and all justice will vanish together and perish.
