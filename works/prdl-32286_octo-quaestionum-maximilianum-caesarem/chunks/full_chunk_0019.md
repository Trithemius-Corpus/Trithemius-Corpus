FOURTH QUESTION

Whoever does not understand the sacred Scriptures in the spirit in which they were infused into their writers has the veil of ignorance and rashness spread over his heart, after the manner of the Jews, and very often drinks in the poison of pernicious error from the place where the humble, approaching in the spirit of God, have received the cup of salvation.

For heavenly doctrine lies not in the words of Scripture, but in the meaning; not on the surface of the sayings, but in the understanding; not in the mere adornment of polished speech, but in the hidden mystery of divine inspiration.

Hence blessed Pope Gregory says: “Sacred Scripture far surpasses all the sciences of this world in the manner of its speech, because in one and the same discourse, while it narrates the text, it reveals a hidden mystery; and just as it refreshes the simple on the surface, so it exercises the wise by its mystery. For it has in public what may nourish little ones, and it preserves in secret what may hold the minds of the lofty suspended in wonder at its sweetness, like some wonderful river in which both the simple little lamb may walk and the great elephant may swim.”

And Saint Augustine says on Genesis: “Divine Scripture speaks in such a way that by its height it mocks the proud, by its depth it terrifies the attentive, by its power it feeds the great, and by its affability it nourishes little ones.”

For the authority of sacred Scripture is greater than all the insight of human intelligence. Therefore, in order to arrive at its true understanding, the mind must first be cleansed of every carnal affection, and the help of divine goodness must be earnestly implored with humility, that by his Spirit he may enlighten the understanding of the learner. For to the authority of the divine Scriptures the necks of all powers must be bowed. And as blessed Jerome says: “No man must ever be flattered in such a way that the truth of the holy Scriptures is neglected”; for in both Testaments it is wholly true in its narration of deeds, right in its admonitions, certain in its promises, and terribly to be feared in its threats.

Therefore it is not given to all to be able to penetrate the mysteries of the divine Scriptures, but this is permitted only to those in whom love makes study continual, and to whose purified minds the Spirit of God grants true understanding. But whoever is soiled by the darkness of vices, whoever walks proudly with a swollen mind, whoever doubts the truth of God’s Scriptures either in part or in whole, should never trust that he can reach an understanding of them. For the authority of Scripture itself says: “Unless you believe, you will not understand.”

It is the slowness of our intelligence, and the impurity of our desire, whenever we do not rightly understand the divine Scriptures; since the veil of pernicious occupation remains spread over our hearts and does not allow us to arrive at true understanding. Hence it comes about that we do not understand the meaning which the Holy Spirit infused into the divine Scriptures, but, grasping only the bare words of the writers, we twist them into another sense.

Indeed, if our minds were cleansed from carnal vices; if, through the continual practice of sobriety, our intelligence were exercised in the Scriptures; if faith and reverence were shown to the holy Scriptures as their dignity requires, every studious person would understand more clearly than light that there is nothing confused in the sacred readings, nothing discordant, nothing unfitting, nothing absurd, nothing inserted without the most just reason.

For every passage of divine Scripture is full of mysteries; and whenever anything in them seems to be set down obscurely, it was done by divine providence, so that the studious mind might have the necessary exercise of devotion, and the faith of the simple might not lose the place of due reverence. Therefore it was not by chance, but by eternal providence, that God wished the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that they might abound in as many mysteries as meanings, to be explained in a Catholic manner by teachers under the inspiration of the Spirit of God. For, as Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture is very useful.
