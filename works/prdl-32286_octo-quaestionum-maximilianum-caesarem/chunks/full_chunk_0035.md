Question Seven.

The holy lover of Christ, Paul, says in a certain epistle: All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution. Likewise the most wise king Solomon says in Proverbs: Whom the Lord loves he corrects, and he takes pleasure in him as a father in a son. Is it not a very glorious Christian thing, for the love of God, patiently to endure adversities, whether they are brought upon us by wicked men or by demons? For power is granted to the devil and his ministers to harm good and just people, not for their ruin, as their persecutors suppose, but rather for the greatest and sure consolation of the soul.

For truly, when a just man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeping his commandments with all care, and nevertheless is afflicted by sorceries, whether in body, or in possessions, or in both, that man is truly blessed, because when he has been tested he will receive the crown of life which God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of faith of the Catholic Church of Christians, and who for the love of God patiently endures the evils he receives in this world, has a sure sign that he will persevere in the grace of God and be saved.

On the contrary, concerning the wicked and the reprobate we read written thus: Why do the impious live, raised up and strengthened with riches? Their houses are secure and at peace, and the rod of God is not upon them. They spend their days in good things, and in a moment they descend to hell. For it is a manifest sign of damnation if here you live badly in sins and vices, contrary to God, and prosper in the goods of fortune, bodily strength, and health.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and just people to be afflicted by the wicked and reprobate is for the complete purification of the mind. For, as Scripture says, there is no just man on earth who does good and does not sin. But since God lets no sin pass unpunished, he ordains temporal punishments for just people, through mercy, for their offenses, but eternal punishments for the impious and reprobate, through justice. Therefore, out of mercy, he commutes eternal punishment for the penitent into temporal punishment, which they must necessarily suffer either in this world or after death in purgatory. But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and very grave, the merciful God has been accustomed to purify in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the hardships of diverse tribulation in body and possessions. For the sorceries of the impious profit good and just people as a remedy, but those who inflict them they profit toward damnation.

The sixth reason is that a man, harmed by the attacks of demons, may be recalled to repentance and may set in his heart the necessary amendment of life. For almighty God does not send vengeance immediately when we sin, but waits until the appointed time for our amendment. But if we neglect to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not expect it; and he whom we have scorned to acknowledge by repentance while we sinners were in tranquility, we shall acknowledge in tribulation by his severity. Hence the holy prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: If his sons forsake my law, and do not walk in my judgments, and do not keep my commandments, I will visit their iniquities with a rod, and their sins with scourges. But my mercy I will not take away from them, nor will I harm my truth.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits the demonic sorceries to prevail against good and innocent people is that he may cut off from them too unrestrained an occasion of sinning.
