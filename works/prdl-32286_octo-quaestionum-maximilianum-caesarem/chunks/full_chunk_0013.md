They raise them, but they deceive human senses in various ways: for either they themselves enter dead corpses and move those very bodies about as though alive, or, after carrying them off to a more remote place, they present to the beholders something to be seen in the appearance of a likeness of the dead person as living, as the experience of certain people has informed us. We believe it manifest that demons can show, in a certain false likeness, everything whatever that the saints do as miracles in truth.

All the miracles of demons are either phantasms or things done by a certain natural skill. For if men experienced in the more hidden mysteries of nature, by a certain application of matter to form unknown to others, produce unusual and marvelous effects, such as forming natural eels from the dew of heaven, bees from the flesh of calves, serpents from certain roots of a plant, and many things similar to these, not to mention greater things, as we have [unclear] in very many cases, who would deny that demons, being of a subtler nature and greater experience, can perform more marvelous natural things which are manifest to no man?

The fourth worker of miracle is man, who works marvelous things in four ways, things which, to those who know neither the cause nor the manner, do not seem human. And first, indeed, by invocation of the divine name, when the merit of accepted holiness concurs in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ. In this way the holy apostles and their imitators worked miracles by the power of God and at the same time by the merit of holiness in the unity of faith. But unbelievers cannot work miracles in this way, for they do not walk the necessary path of faith toward salvation. For although very many of them believe in God, yet because they refuse to believe in the mediator between God and men, they have gone very far away from the mercy of God.

But, according to the promise of the holy Gospel, all things are possible to those who believe in God and our Lord Jesus Christ, that is, to those who adorn right faith also with holy works. For truly, just as without faith it is impossible to please God, so also faith without works is dead. Almighty God would grant us all things that we ask in the faith of Christ, if we did all things according to the faith we owe. Believe firmly, and act faithfully, and you will always obtain all things.

Second, man works miracles by likeness to angelic purity. For the purer our mind, confirmed in the faith of Jesus Christ, has become through love, the more like the holy angels it is made, and the greater familiarity with them it will attain. But whoever has attained every kind of familiarity with the holy angels will, whenever he wishes, shine forth in miracles, by God’s gift. For the holy angels reveal to pure men who burn with the love of God secrets hidden from others, and by command of the Creator they make many things manifest to them. For the Lord is near to those who call upon him in truth. The Lord will do the will of those who fear him, and he will hear their prayers and save them. Whoever is united with Christ through grace is refreshed by the frequent visitation of angels; and as often as necessity demands, he is made illustrious by signs and powers through their ministry.

This is why those oppressed by various illnesses also betake themselves to the aid of the saints; and, turning over in their minds the promises of the Lord, they obtain by the merits of others what they cannot obtain by their own. Their holy devotion in the faith of Christ receives salvation from the Lord, because, aided by the purity of their own conscience and supported by the merit of another’s holiness, it rises, not rashly, in sure hope.
