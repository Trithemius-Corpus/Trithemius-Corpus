On the faith necessary for salvation.

The Second Question.

Since today only a small part of the world is subject to the law of the Christian faith, whether that opinion of very many people seems admissible without injury to the truth: that each person, in his own religion, which he believes to be true and saving, as a worshipper of the one God, outside the Christian faith and without baptism, can be saved, provided that nothing is known to him of the religion of Christ.

To answer this question simply according to the common understanding I have judged superfluous, since I know that today there are many among Christians whose impiety, in favor of the salvation of lost men, makes God out to be cruel. But because a cause of just consideration stirred your Most Serene Highness to ask these things, not because you do not know what must be held in this position according to the Catholic faith, but so that you might have something true to set against those who attack the truth, I shall answer the questions briefly, as best I can.

If anyone could be saved outside the Christian faith, Christ, the Son of almighty God, would seem to have become man superfluously and without cause. For if there is salvation outside Christ, then Christ is not the Savior of all. But because it was necessary that the only-begotten Son of God, for the salvation of the human race, be born as man in the world, suffer, die, and rise again on the third day according to the Scriptures, he did not come into this world in vain, nor superfluously, nor without cause. Since this is so, whoever asserts that anyone can be saved outside the faith of Christ shows himself not a Christian, but an adversary of the faith and a deserter of Christ. For whoever does not believe in Christ, the Son of God, promises himself eternal salvation in vain. For he who is the truth, Jesus Christ, says in the holy Gospel: He who does not believe will be condemned. And again: He who does not believe is already judged, because he does not believe in the name of the only-begotten Son of God. And again, a little later, he adds, saying: He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. The holy Apostle also says: Without faith it is impossible to please God.

The solution of the proposed question is now clear to all who truly believe in Christ: salvation is through him, the Savior of the world, and whoever does not believe in him cannot be saved. Nor is it lawful for a Christian in any way to go beyond the boundaries of his religion, so as to presume to explain this question otherwise than Christ has taught. Whoever goes outside this rule of faith by asserting the contrary is plainly shown not to think rightly about Christ, the Savior of the faithful. For whoever promises salvation to anyone outside Christ wishes to declare Christ a liar. But we, confirmed by the most sacred ordinances of the Gospel, firmly believe and without doubt pronounce that no one can be saved outside the faith of Christ, however much he worships one God, however holy or just his life, however much he trusts in his paternal religion or in another.
