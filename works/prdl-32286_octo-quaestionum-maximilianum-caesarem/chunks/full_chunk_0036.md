It is not expedient for everyone’s salvation to have honors and riches together with health; nor does a longer life always make a person better. Therefore almighty God, wishing to show his mercy in those whom he has chosen, permits good, just, and innocent people to be subject to various calamities. And sometimes through demons, sometimes through wicked people, and sometimes by various other ways and means, he turns away riches and honors from such people, which he foreknew they would use badly, so that they may preserve humility in poverty and abasement, which, if placed in wealth and high rank, they would by no means have kept. He also very often permits them to fall into many and great illnesses, because glad health of the flesh would have brought the greatest sickness upon the mind. Finally, he permits some to be driven to death by witchcraft, so that the freedom to sin may be cut off from them, which, had they been allowed to live longer, they would have abused. Hence Scripture says of the just man: “He was taken away, lest malice should change his understanding, and lest deceit should beguile his soul.” The eighth reason for divine permission is that people may venerate the sacraments of the Church, which have great power against all the witchcrafts of malignant spirits. For one who venerates the sacraments and rites of the Catholic Church with due honor is rarely harmed by the witchcrafts of demons. And by the very confession of witches we have often heard that the hearing of Masses and the use of things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons. For things are not blessed in the Church of God in vain, but so that they may be defenses of divine protection and may show demons signs to be feared. But concerning boys and infants who, reborn in Christ, are in some way killed by the witchcrafts of demons or of human beings, we do not doubt that divine prudence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things for the better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish through witchcraft because of the sins of their parents.

On the Providence of God.

Question Eight.

This was the eighth question of your Serenity: whether it can be proved by natural reason, as also by the holy Scriptures, that God has care for human affairs and actions, and a certain and infallible providence over all and each of the things that he does in the world. I remember answering this question as follows. The whole world, heaven and earth, and all things that are in them, are subject to divine providence, by whose will and command they were brought into being from the beginning. For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us prove this opinion briefly, first by the Scriptures and then, as far as is permitted, by reasons. In the book of Judith we read: “All your ways are peaceful, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the most wise king Solomon speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth to make your soul sin, nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest the Lord, angered against your words, destroy all the works of your hands.” The clearest testimonies of providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to his disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? And not one of them falls upon the earth before God.” This is a great commendation of divine providence: it has care not only for human beings, but also for tiny birds. Indeed, if the Almighty cares for such small things, who would deny that he has care for great things?
