The Church also, and in like manner, confirms this from Scripture. The Church abounds whenever Scripture falls short; and if anyone has doubts about the Church, he has recourse to the counsel of Scripture. For there is one Holy Spirit of God, who founded the Church in the faith of Jesus Christ, the very same almighty Spirit who alone inspired Scripture. And this is the threefold cord that is not easily broken, where divine Scripture, through the same Spirit, is joined to the authority of the Church, and so prevails that the Church commends Scripture, and Scripture in turn commends the Church.

Therefore, as it seems to me, O Caesar, we have sufficiently assigned the reason for the question proposed by your unconquered Majesty: because, if everything that concerns the piety of faith were plainly expressed in the Holy Scriptures, holy mother Church would not appear to have such great authority, and the merit of saving obedience would in large part be emptied out. At the same time I think enough has been answered to the main question, where certain people suppose that sacred Scripture is in many places confused and entangled; but if they read it with due purity of mind, they would certainly find it perfect and solid. Great is the authority of the Catholic Church, which alone, in doubtful matters concerning the faith, has the right to interpret the Scriptures; to it the necks of all powers must be subjected, and if anyone, with rash daring, departs from its definition, he has denied the Gospel of Christ.

There is one congregation of the faithful, holy Church, the mother of Christians; outside its unity no one will be saved. And although in conduct it may sometimes seem weak, nevertheless, constant always in faith, it has never erred, the loving-kindness of Christ preserving it.

Concerning the reprobate and evildoers

Question Five

The fifth question of your most serene Highness was this: Why can impious, evil, and reprobate people in matters concerning faith, such as those women whom we commonly call witches, command malignant spirits, while good men and the most zealous observers of the Christian faith can command neither good spirits nor always malignant ones?

I remember that I answered this question before in this way. In all the works of the divine Majesty there is a sequence and order of beauty, an unchanging equity, by which each thing, suitably disposed, perseveres in the rank it has received. For without its proper order nothing subsists in the world. In this order the freedom of the will, which the Creator of all gave to all human beings, cannot be compelled by any violence of power not to remain in the order that the first human being received from the beginning of his creation. Hence no one among mortals is good except by the counsel of his own will, aided by the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil except by his own will, deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin.

By rectitude of will we are made like the good spirits, if we prove by holy and necessary works the piety of faith that we have in Christ. And just as the perfection of a good will makes good people equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious makes them like demons. Hence evil and perverse people, the more they have been depraved by the malice of their own will, become so much the more like demons in affection. Through likeness, moreover, friendship and familiarity are introduced, from which little by little mutual confidence is generated. From the lesser’s trust in the greater arises authority, whether it be true or simulated by cunning. Therefore, if there seems to be any authority of wicked people over demons, it arises from the greatness of depravity, namely when their affection is so far depraved, and by their reprobate will they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons that join with them. For demons are not all in malice [unclear].
