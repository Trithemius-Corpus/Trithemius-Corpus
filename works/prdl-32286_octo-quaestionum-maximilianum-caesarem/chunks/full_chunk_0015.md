Such, for instance, is the thing that those afflicted with spasms commonly use. On the holy day of Parasceve they make a ring of some metal during the office, and thereafter they always wear it on the heart-finger. These are utterly vain things, having no root at all in truth. Yet I cannot deny that I have seen some people cured of spasm by that same vanity. Likewise, I know that certain people have opened the strongest locks by words and herbs, which I do not think was done either by the power of God or by the force of the things applied.

I could bring forward countless similar things that I have seen and heard; their manifest effects are nothing other than certain marvels of demons. Finally, there are some overly curious people who stubbornly put faith and confidence in characters, little written sheets, rings, figures, necklaces, and unknown words, and often obtain astonishing effects. We do not doubt at all that the devil supplied these, not through any merit of holiness, nor by God’s merciful grant, but rather through faithless credulity.

For demons, having the greatest envy of the human race, gladly assist the vain works of the impious in order to destroy the souls of mortals. For what else were characters and interpretations of hidden names but certain foods and drinks of demons? Whoever puts faith in characters that promise marvelous effects has implicitly denied both the faith of Christ and the Church of the saints. Alas, how many Christians today, how many clerics, how many priests, to say nothing of their superiors, use many and various superstitions in their daily actions, by which implicit pacts with demons are entered into, to the insult of the Christian faith and to their own condemnation.

And because these evils have now multiplied too much among Christians, the Almighty has withdrawn his hand and handed them over to the power of the faithless, so that they may feel them as exactors of punishments, since by imitation they were stubbornly serving not the living and true God but vain superstitions.

That marvelous things are done by the substitution of demons we shall clearly recognize if we inquire more diligently into the truth. Jews, Saracens, pagans, or gentiles, who do not believe in our God Jesus Christ, if they perform any miracles, we believe that they are done not by the power of God, not by the ministry of angels, not by the merit of holiness, but by the substitution of demons. For whenever they invoke almighty God, in whose only-begotten Son they do not believe, for the merciful granting of some marvelous effect, the devil, knowing that there is no salvation for sinners, and that the Most High does not hear unbelieving sinners, secretly applies himself in place of the God invoked, so that he may keep them stubborn in their errors; and, if he can, with the Lord permitting it, he grants to their prayers what is asked.

The devil uses this substitution all the more freely, the less he is hindered by the faith of Christ and the sacraments of the Church. For the wrath of God remains upon all unbelievers who do not believe in his only-begotten Son; and they are in the power of the devil, and therefore he thrusts himself upon those who invoke them in place of God. Hence, blinded in their errors, they invoke God, in whose Son they do not believe; and when he disdains to hear them while they remain under wrath, the devil, the false worker of miracles, comes near. Thus they believe the devil’s deceptions, while they scorn to believe the true miracles of God. From demons they receive false miracles suited to their errors, because they have refused to believe the truth, that is, Christ.

For there are many among the unbelievers who worship one God, the creator of all things, do not adore idols, and firmly believe that they will be saved in their own faith. But because they disdain to believe the mysteries of the Lord’s incarnation, the wrath of God remains upon them; and they vanish away in their follies, thinking God near to them, while, since they do not believe rightly, they are subjected to demons.
