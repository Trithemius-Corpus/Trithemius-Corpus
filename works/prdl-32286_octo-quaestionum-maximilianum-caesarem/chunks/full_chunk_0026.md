QUESTION SIX.

For he says in the holy Gospel of Christ: “Without me you can do nothing.” Hence it is clear that every power of doing anything is granted to human beings by the Lord God, and from nowhere else, whether people do good or evil. Thus the most holy Father Augustine says: “The will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily forms and motions; and nothing happens visibly in this whole vast and immense commonwealth of creation unless it is either commanded or permitted from the inner, invisible, and intelligible court of the supreme Emperor, according to the ineffable justice of rewards and punishments.”

Therefore, Caesar, it must not be doubted that that power of witches by which, through the cooperation of demons, they produce wondrous and dreadful effects is granted by the Lord God, whose will no one can resist and without whose influence a person cannot even move his hand. For the holy doctors conclude that neither demons nor witches can do anything, whether harming a human being or an animal, unless almighty God wills it, consents to it, and grants the power, so that they may do what he has decreed by his will [unclear]. Unless God consents and wills, no creature has any power; otherwise, if something could be done when he did not will or consent, it would necessarily follow that God was weak, since he could not prevent what he would be forced unwillingly to permit. But since every human being, insofar as he is a creature, depends on God, he can do nothing without the will of him from whom he has both the will and the power.

Whatever witches do, therefore, is done by God’s permission; without his will they can do nothing. And unless God had granted that they could do what they wish, they could obtain it neither from themselves nor from demons. Thus all power is from the Lord God, in whom all things exist and are moved, as in the first cause of all things; for from him from whom we have received freedom of choice, from him also we have the power to do what we can.

But since the understanding of witches is very confused, they do not perpetrate the evils they do by human art, but by the cooperation of demons, whom nature has made subtle and long experience has made swift in producing marvels. For the causes of the wondrous effects of witches are not such means as they use for witchcraft, such as owls’ heads, toads, serpents, characters, images, and things like these, but the devil who instituted these signs; for since he is most proud, he desires in all things to be seen as comparable to God.

Indeed, just as almighty God instituted certain sacraments in his Church, which, when worthily administered, signify and produce a saving effect through his own assistance, he who instituted them working salvation, so also the devil, who desires nothing more than to be considered equal to God, instituted certain signs as sacraments. When he is invoked by these by those with whom he has been joined in covenants, the malignant worker assists them, and as often as God permits, he brings about the evil that is requested; if God does not permit it, he does absolutely nothing.

For the devil is the worker of all the effects of all necromancers and witches: by applying the means which he himself first instituted to the subject matter, he produces the desired work, sometimes in reality, sometimes only in appearance. But witch-women, who have now passed over with depraved will and consent into the fellowship of demons through pacts, receive from them certain marks and symbolic signs, by which the demons, once summoned, immediately appear. In these destructive operations of demons nothing is stronger than the foolish credulity of little witches, which is increased by pacts and vows; for the firmer the faith they have placed either in demons or in their signs, the more swiftly they obtain [unclear].
