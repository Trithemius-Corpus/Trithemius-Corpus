For the exceedingly savage corruption of the witches’ will in a certain way turns them into frenzy; when this has seized their mind, demons at once attach themselves to the raging women, and, summoned by pacts and signs, by God’s permission accomplish for the madwomen the effect of destruction.

Plato relates something similar in his book on poetic frenzy, saying: just as Bacchic women, when their mind is not sound, draw honey and milk from rivers, but when their mind is sound cannot draw them. This is brought about by their spirit, carried outside itself into fellowship with an evil demon. We know by experience that witches have drawn butter from rushing rivers at certain times, which cannot in any way happen otherwise than by the ministry of a demon.

In like manner, when they have been led into hatred of some man or beast, sighing for its destruction, they are driven by a most grievous frenzy, thinking of nothing in their mind except revenge. They take things known and familiar to them for witchcraft, invoke demons, show the signs appointed by them, and also the characters; and what they cannot accomplish by their own power, they obtain by the demons’ ministry.

But another person, who is not subject to the demons by pact and surrender, even if he knows the things suited to witchcraft and also the manner of operating, and although he displays the signs, nevertheless rarely obtains the effect, because he is not counted among the demons’ familiars. For the devil, swollen with pride, imitates the Church of Christ to such a degree that, just as an unordained man cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he may utter the words of consecration, so also a demon either never or rarely comes to the aid of one who invokes him and shows signs or characters, if he does not know that person to be subject to him by pact.

For if he came indiscriminately to all who invoked him without pacts having been made, he would not possess so many witches dedicated to him by right of ownership. Hence many who are given over to profane arts, striving by dreadful conjurations to call demons to a circle, a mirror, or any other receptacles, obtain no effect, because either God’s command stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man who has not been dedicated to him by homage.

For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is agreed that none is perpetrated: fury in the mind and a depraved will in the witch, the cooperation of a familiar demon, and before all else divine permission. If one of these does not concur, witchcraft cannot be perpetrated. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not meet the will of the wicked woman, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch is lacking, the devil will by no means bring the witchcraft to completion.

For the devil uses the will of the raging witch as an artisan uses an instrument for working; without it he could not inflict witchcraft. And just as demons differ from one another in power and keenness of subtlety, so the power of a witch is not one and the same in all, but differs in each according as demons of a subtler or grosser nature concur with them. For there are many kinds of demons, and they stand apart from one another by fixed degrees, according to the places into which they were cast down when they fell in the beginning.
