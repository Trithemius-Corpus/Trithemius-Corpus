This kind of witch sometimes differs greatly from the rest: for what the others make diseased by witchcraft, these promise to cure. But because they very often repel witchcraft by witchcraft, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, because, plunging itself into watery places, it dwells around lakes and rivers. It is full of anger, troubled, restless, and deceitful; it raises storms at sea, sinks ships into the deep, and takes life from many in the waters. Whenever these demons assume a visible body, they appear more often in the female sex, and are more rarely seen in the male, because they remain in damp places and follow a softer manner of life, and cannot easily assume a male form in the manner of the others. Hence antiquity long ago named Naiads, Nereids, and nymphs of the waters not in the male sex but in the female. Among our people they are commonly called aquatic women, that is, wasser frauwen. But those who dwell in drier and somewhat arid places, whenever they appear bodily and visibly to human beings, transform themselves into men.

They also assume different forms of beasts, according as they are touched by various affections. But the holy angels, because they are not varied by affection, always appear uniformly in male form. For nowhere do we read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast, but always in a male form. Demons, however, have very often been seen around rivers and springs in the shape of women, sometimes dancing, sometimes spreading out their hair after the manner of women, and sometimes speaking with human beings and carrying out various mockeries. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have dealings with acts of witchcraft.

The fifth kind is called subterranean, because it dwells in caves and caverns and in the remote hollows of mountains. These demons are the worst in disposition, and they especially attack those who dig wells and mines and those who seek treasures hidden in the earth, being most ready for the destruction of the human race. They make openings in the earth, stir up flame-breathing winds, and shake the foundations of buildings. Sometimes by night they come out from the mountains in troops, lead wonderful and astonishing dances in the fields, and, as though fearing the command of one leader, suddenly vanish at a signal and return to their hiding places. At times the sound of our people is heard among them, and sometimes they falsely claim that they are the spirits of human beings who have departed this life. They seek nothing more than the fear and amazement of human beings.

Hence we have ascertained that they have sometimes led some of the simpler sort of men into their hiding places in the mountains, showing astonishing sights to those who marveled, and falsely claiming that the dwellings of the blessed are there and that they are friends of those who see them. Some of these demons also brood over the treasures which the greed of mortals has hidden in the earth; and lest they come again into human use, they carry them off, steal them, guard them, and sometimes transfer them from place to place. This kind of demon is not accustomed to have any dealings with women’s witchcraft.

The sixth kind of demons is called light-fleeing, because they especially dread and detest the light: for they never appear by day, nor can they assume a body in any other way than by night. This is the business that walks about in darkness, an inscrutable and utterly dark kind of demons, driven by cold passions, malicious, restless, and disturbed. It violently oppresses people who meet it in the hours of night, and more often, when God permits, kills some either by its breath or by its touch. This kind of demon does not associate with witches, nor can it be constrained by incantations, because it flees both light and the voices of human beings, and likewise every disturbance of the air.

The aerial kind is especially accustomed to come to the witchcraft of women, because it is subtler than all the rest and of the boldest rashness. Some of the terrestrial demons also, as has been said, serve acts of witchcraft; but since they are held to be of a lower rank, they cannot bring about those greater acts of witchcraft. It is therefore plainly established that the whole power of witches, by which they do such astonishing things, is granted to demons within limits set by the Lord God; and when he does not permit it, they accomplish nothing.
