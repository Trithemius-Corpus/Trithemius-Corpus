They are not equal in wickedness or intelligence; rather, one is much craftier than another in quickness of understanding, and far worse in corrupt disposition. For those who, having fallen from their high seats, are more closely joined to the earth and inhabit caverns and caves are certainly duller than the rest, to such a degree that they even fear swords and rods, and seem to be moved by the threats of men who terrify them, as may be seen in those whom we call the possessed. But demons who dwell farther from the earth are subtler by nature and encounter human beings more rarely.

Men full of wickedness, however, the more they advance in perversity of will, become so much the more like demons; and, once admitted by them into familiarity, they thereafter grow bold, and acquire a simulated, not truly coercive, authority over demons, which they use by that familiarity. For demons, in order to keep in their own depravity those men whom they have once received into surrender as willing subjects, gladly submit themselves to them, pretending to be moved by their entreaties; and what they do of their own accord they pretend to do as though compelled to obedience.

Indeed no one obtains true authority over demons unless faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made him acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.” Therefore evil men command demons because malevolence has made them like them, likeness has made them familiar, and familiarity has made them their own and subject to them.

It is like the case of a great prince, as often happens, if one of his servants, born in a humble station and joined to him by long familiarity, is said to command him, because from him he obtains without contradiction everything he has asked, so that he seems rather to rule his master than to be ruled by him. According to this likeness, evil and reprobate men, who are admitted by demons into close familiarity and become very bold through prolonged confidence, receive over them a simulated, not to say familiar, authority, which they have deserved through the likeness of a most depraved will.

Hence whatever they request from demons, who cooperate with them by an implicit or explicit pact, they receive, unless at times the demons, prevented by divine ordinance, are unable to provide what is asked. Yet demons do not thus cooperate indiscriminately with all reprobate and evil men, but only with those with whom they have entered into alliances through the intervention of some pact: such as witch-women, who have offered homage to the devil, and those invokers of demons whom the common people call by the usual name necromancers.

There are two kinds of men who can lawfully command demons. The first is that of those men who, beloved by God through the merit of holiness, have received from him the power of treading down the head of the ancient serpent, such as in the primitive Church were the apostles and their followers and disciples, of whom the Lord speaks in the holy Gospel: “In my name they shall cast out demons,” and “they shall take up serpents.”

The second kind is that of those men to whom, by reason of order in the Church of God, it is granted to drive demons from the bodies of men by the power of the ministry they have received. And this ministry is committed to exorcists in the Church of God when they are ordained by the bishop.

Again, there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons; and the wicked conditions they accept show how unlawfully they presume to do so. The first and most pernicious kind is that of witches, who enter into pacts with demons and, after making a solemn profession, hand themselves over to some demon as his property and for perpetual service.
