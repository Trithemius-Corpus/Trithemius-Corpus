*That Saint Anne, the mother, conceived her daughter, the most blessed Mother of God, without original stain. Chapter vii.*

Since, for declaring the merits of the holiness of the most blessed mother Anne, it contributes much that she bore the Mother of God without the stain of original guilt, so great a prerogative of honor is by no means to be passed over by us in silence. And indeed we know that this most pure conception has been set up as a sign, which many contradict even to the present day: while they wish to defend the custom of nature, they do not fear to attack the grace of God. Why do fools bark? Why do the unlearned murmur? Is God not permitted to do what he wills? Behold, the Church venerates the conception of the Mother of God as pure, without stain; behold, she celebrates its feast with yearly devotion; and headstrong men labor to stain it with rash presumption. Holy and most learned men, past and present, in various kinds of writing, proclaim in their lucubrations that this conception was never subject to original stain; and still the sons of Adam rashly attack it. Nearly all religious of diverse orders confess, believe, and preach that it was always most pure; to wish to condemn them, or to say that they err, is the mark of extreme madness. Behold, secular men, religious men, devout and learned monks celebrate the conception as always most pure, in verse and prose; and lukewarm men, without learning, for whom to know the name of philosopher seems enough, strive to defile the holiness of the Mother of God. Many, I say, most learned men have published various treatises in its defense; what learned man has ever written the contrary? But you, unworthy even of your own name, [unclear], who, setting your mouth against heaven, have impiously blasphemed against the most pure Mother of God, will not have done this without God’s grave notice, since you are to be judged as moved not by piety but by impiety. Finally, we know that reply has been made more than abundantly to your foolish rashness by learned men in various styles. And you depart confounded, not by our weapons, but by the power of God. I condemn not your error, but your arrogance, your rashness, your presumption. For I know that to hesitate about the conception of the Virgin Mary is not a sin, since God wished it to be hidden; but to attack it and rashly define what you do not know is a crime unheard of.
