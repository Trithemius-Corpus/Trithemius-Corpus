But, led by many reasons, we, out of pious belief, hold that she always existed most pure; the adversaries, led away by consideration of nature, defend the contrary opinion with rash presumption. But it is better to render account for piety toward the parent of God than for rashness. If we err, piety, indeed God himself, is the cause. But if you err, O adversaries, say for what cause. What consideration moves you to say so boldly that the most pure conception of the mother of God was subject to original guilt? You will answer, I know: love of truth. But show that your opinion is true, and I shall believe you lovers of truth; yet you will not show it, because you do not understand. You do not understand because you do not believe. You do not defend the truth, which you do not know. For no one properly defends what he does not know. Consideration of human nature deceives you; while you examine its custom minutely, you do not weigh that God is the establisher of nature. He who made nature is above nature; God, the maker of the law, is not confined under the condition of human law, but he who willed that a virgin should give birth even beyond the order of nature preserved her from sin. Therefore the cause of the adverse opinion takes its beginning from rashness, and for that reason it will deserve not reward from God, but vengeance. For just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so error concerning the conception does not condemn, but obstinacy does. Finally, if examples and memorials of the ancients are to be believed, we read that many perished most wretchedly who attacked the purity of the glorious conception; but we find no defender of it ever struck by God. For the very great dignity, duly considered, of the mother of God urges us to the defense of the most holy conception; she shone forth such and so great that she was most worthy of every honor. If we are deceived, we are excused by holy piety. But if you err, who will defend you? Truth? But you do not know it. Justice? But that condemns errors, it does not excuse them. Mercy? But you do not have the bowels of mercy. Who then will deliver you? The Virgin Mary? But you make light of her honor. The Son of God? But you do not love his mother. The prayers of the saints? But you detract from their queen. Therefore you will have no excuse, you who offend God and all his saints. You arm yourselves with scholastic arguments and are bare of piety; whatever you bring forward in defense of your opinion is alien from the fear of God.
