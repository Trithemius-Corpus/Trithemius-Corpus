Therefore I admonish you, O men distinguished by learning and knowledge of letters, not to despise the devotion of the simple, not to censure the cult of the most holy mother Anne as though it were new, but rather, as far as you can, to imitate it. For I was not persuaded in vain to say these things. There are indeed among you, if I may say it without offence to the good, some who, swollen with the stamp of pride, despise all the devotional exercises of the simple, contemn the miracles and examples of the saints as ravings, and think that nothing holy should be admitted which they do not prove by the arguments of philosophers. All revelations shown by God to devout people they regard as lies or women’s dreams; they call the legends of the saints fables; and while they rashly proclaim their own learning, they shamelessly attack the works of the great God. They also reject the treatises of the holy fathers and of devout people which do not display Ciceronian eloquence, as though lacking learning, and they despise the words of heavenly instruction because of their simple style.

O fools and subverters, why do you deceive yourselves? Why, when you are Christians, do you despise ecclesiastical simplicity? The grace of God is not recognized in eloquence, but is obtained in holy simplicity. At the final judgment of God, Quintilianic eloquence will not be required of you, but Christian life. Ornate speech is not rewarded by God, but humble devotion. There sophistical arguments will cease, where the secrets of hearts will be made manifest. Then the simple devout will appear in glory, and the proud learned in punishment. Behold, the simple unlearned rise up and receive the kingdom of God in their devout simplicity, and you with your doctrines descend in pride and arrogance to hell.

Do you believe that the fame of your learning is sufficient for you, because, after the example of the ancients, you acquire immortality of name among posterity? But so be it. Let what you seek come to pass: let your name and fame be proclaimed with praise in the world for as long as it shall stand. What will it profit you? Truly a foolish consolation, if you are praised where you will never be, and are tormented where you will always remain without end. Therefore I warn and advise you: lay aside your haughtiness, drive out folly, cast away fear, so that you may be able to escape the torments of hell. For God resists the proud; he looks not to learning but to humble devotion; he rewards not words but works, not knowledge but life, not doctrine but conscience. Rather, change your purpose, and do not despise the devotion of the simple, but imitate it. No one understands the gift of God except the one who receives it.
