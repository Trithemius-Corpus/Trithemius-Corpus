For they thought that this insult pertained to our God Jesus Christ, since what was imposed upon and falsely attributed to his parent, most worthy of all praise, seemed to concern him. For what Ecclesiasticus writes in chapter 3 concerning the father must certainly be interpreted most truly also concerning the mother: “Do not glory,” he says, “in the dishonor of your father. For it is not glory for you, but confusion. For the glory of a man comes from the honor of his father, and a father without honor is the disgrace of the son.” Therefore, when in the last century the question of the purity of the conception of Mary, the most blessed Mother of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, had begun to be debated more often than usual, the Holy Spirit, namely, willing that the [unclear] of that matter should at some time be brought to light, after the arguments of both sides had been more diligently weighed, they first for a time most prudently suspended their judgment. Then, inclining to that side which seemed to support the glory and purity of the Virgin, they so confuted by disputation the utterly vain rashness of those who obstinately contended, with regard to an effective proof, that the Virgin had been conceived in original sin, that they affirmed the contrary side of the question, namely that the Virgin, when she was conceived, by a special gift of God had been free from original stain, as very much more consonant with the piety of faith, with right reason, and with the divine Scriptures. This opinion, a few years later, so pleased the holy Council of Basel that, after long disputation of that question and very weighty deliberation, the Holy Spirit inspiring it, they at last defined that doctrine which asserts and teaches that the most blessed Mother of God, Mary, when she was conceived, was by the special and singular grace of God entirely free from every stain of original sin, as pious and consonant with ecclesiastical worship, the catholic faith, right reason, and Sacred Scripture, and as to be approved, embraced, and defended by all Catholics. And they forbade the contrary assertion to be taught henceforth or defended, under threat of divine judgment.
