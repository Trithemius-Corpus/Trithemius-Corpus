Devotion is rare among the learned, because, while they trust in the profundity of their acquired doctrine, they withdraw rather far from simple devotion. For the grace of God is not well received when it is examined by human curiosity. While savor is sought in speech, inward sweetness is lost. The Holy Spirit despises a proud dwelling.

Therefore block up rash mouths, restrain ill-spoken tongues, and do not speak falsehood against the Lord. Do not tear apart the devotion of the simple, lest it be reckoned heavily against you as a scandal. Let no one suppose me, in saying these things, to be an enemy of the good arts; let no one believe me a slanderer of the learned, since I have always cultivated and loved letters, and have always been accustomed to honor learned men, and have sometimes defended them even to the point of hatred and persecution from the unskilled. But let those who seem learned in their own eyes beware, lest, despising the devotion of the simple, they think people should be called back from reverence for Saint Anne. But if any such men are to exist, I would believe no learned man to be among their number, since true learning is accustomed to assist devotion, not to rebuke it. Many also of the most learned men living today, exercising their talents magnificently in verse and prose, have dedicated them to the merits of the holy woman. Therefore Anne, the most holy parent, is to be venerated by us with the highest devotion, she who conceived the Mother of God herself free from every stain. O how worthy a victim she offered when she presented her most holy daughter in the temple.

Finally, it pertains to declaring the merits of the holy mother that the daughter whom she conceived and bore she immediately consecrated from infancy to the Lord. As was the mother’s manner of life, so was the daughter’s instruction. No one can teach another what he himself has not learned; no one poorly instructed instructs children well. Children frequently advance according to the habits of their parents, and what they see being done by them they embrace more willingly. A mother worthy of her most holy daughter, so that, as she had drawn from her without fault the material of the flesh, she might suck from her the principles of piety. The grace of God had anticipated each; it had filled each; but it was earlier in the mother, more abundant in the daughter. The holy mother taught her daughter what she herself had first learned from her parents, although she had no need of human instruction, since the Holy Spirit had filled her from conception. For she scarcely needed maternal training, since she was full of heavenly grace.

Learn, O mothers, to instruct your daughters for the honor of God, not to love the pomps of the world, to flee the confused gaze of the public, to remain at home. For the Holy Spirit hates the public gaze; he loves the secrecy of a pure mind. Why do you run about through streets and squares if you love virginal modesty? The archangel came to Mary not in public but in secret, not speaking in the square, but in her chamber, loving secrecy.
