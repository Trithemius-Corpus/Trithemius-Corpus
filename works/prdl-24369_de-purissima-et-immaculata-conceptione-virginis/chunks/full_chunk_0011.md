In order that we may meet and resist their fury more eagerly and more strongly, according to our profession, order, and rank, following the footsteps of our elders, all thirty of us, assembled after much serious and mature deliberation, have sworn by a special oath to defend and champion that most pious doctrine which affirms that the most blessed Mother of God, by God’s singular gift, was preserved from original sin, and which we long ago believed true and still believe; and we have given ourselves to the same.

We decree that henceforth no one shall be enrolled in this sacred college of ours unless by a like oath he professes that he will always, to the best of his powers, be an advocate and vigorous champion of this religious doctrine. But if anyone of ours, which God forbid, should wish to flee to the enemies of the Virgin, and, rejecting the protection not only of us but of the synod of the Church, which without doubt is of the highest authority, should dare for any reason to take up the assertion of the contrary opinion, which we have judged false, impious, and erroneous, we decree that he is to be deprived of all our honors and excluded from our fellowship, and cast far away as a heathen and a publican.

Therefore we appeal to you and summon you, most reverend fathers, pontiffs, and pastors and leaders of the churches of Christ: believe and proclaim with us that the most holy Virgin, Mother of God, most worthy of all praise, was preserved from all original sin by God’s singular gift; and help those who preach this with your blessing and resources. And by the bowels of mercy of Jesus Christ and by the charity of his mother, the undefiled Virgin, we beseech you: thus, by your pious office and divine authority, resist bravely our enemies, or rather the enemies of holy Mary; drive them from your territories and, having punished them, expel them, so that the religious devotion of the Christian people toward the most glorious God-bearer, the unique special patroness and mother of the human race, and devotion of piety and charity, may be more and more fostered, increased, and enkindled. This, without doubt, is most pious and most pleasant, and also most fruitful for this people whose care you bear.

Given at Paris, in the general congregation at Saint-Mathurin so solemnly celebrated, in the year of the Lord 1497, on the sixteenth day of the month of September.
