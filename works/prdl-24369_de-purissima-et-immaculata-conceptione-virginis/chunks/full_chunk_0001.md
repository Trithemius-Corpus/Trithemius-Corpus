*Johannes Trithemius, abbot, On the Most Pure and Immaculate Conception of the Virgin Mary, and on the Feast of Saint Anne, Her Mother.*

Blessed be holy mother Anne, from whom, without original stain, came forth undefiled virginal flesh.

*Letter of the venerable lord Johannes, abbot of Sponheim, on the praise of Saint Anne and on the conception of the immaculate Virgin, mother of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, to the reader: greetings. Since recently, at the urging of friends, I had resolved to write something concerning the praises of the most holy mother Anne, certain fathers of the order of brothers of the Order of Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel asked that I not omit a defense of the most pure conception of the Virgin Mary. And so, persuaded by their prayers and also by just reason, I entitled the seventh chapter of the same work “On the Conception of the Mother of God and Virgin”; and, lest anyone take this otherwise than my intention holds, I thought the reader should be advised.

For I know, and it does not escape me, that many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the stain of original sin, men who flourished long before the Council of Basel. Yielding their opinion to its own time, I pass over it wholly untouched, and I have no intention at all of saying or writing anything against them. But if anyone sees that something has been said by me rather harshly, let him understand that it is not directed against those who hold otherwise than we do, but only against the obstinate, who, without learning, do not fear to call the supporters of the other opinion heretics; and my words indicate this, because they mark the present time and not the past. For when I say, “What do the foolish bark? What do the ignorant murmur?” this belongs to the present time, not the past, and reproves the obstinate today, not the ancients who held otherwise.

But you ask what learned man ever wrote the contrary. I am not speaking of the ancient fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the aforesaid council, hold otherwise. And so I reproach only the obstinacy of certain unskilled men of this time, not the opinion of any learned men whatsoever who think otherwise, since I am certainly not ignorant of the declarations of Pope Sixtus IV. It seemed to me, moreover, that the belief concerning the most pure conception, which the holy Roman Church believes and venerates, in no way departs from that Church; and this is shown today by many proofs. Finally, with the doctors of the universities, and with almost all the orders and the faithful of Christ spread throughout the whole Christian world, I do not fear to venerate the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary as pure. Yet I do not admit obstinacy, but I wish to obey the Roman Church in all things, as the most reverend mother of all churches.
