The apostles received the Holy Spirit not in the marketplace, but shut up in a house. What the most blessed Virgin learned from her mother, indeed from the Holy Spirit, she carefully kept. She had been accustomed to avoid wandering abroad; Mary remained alone in the house. In both look, mothers to the mother, virgins to the virgin. Believe me: it does not befit your honor to take pleasure in public; it is not expedient for virgins to be greeted in the marketplace, because whoever goes often into public diminishes reverence for honor. Virginity, when it is truly modest, flees the public place. Without modesty there is no sincere virginity, nor can what is too public remain pure. Anna offered her sweetest daughter, three years old, to the Lord; she taught the little child with devout fear, and presented her in the temple to be instructed in the fear of the Lord. But you do not do this; instead you instruct your daughters in dances and the vanities of the world and its love. You say nothing to them about the fear of the Lord; you do not urge eternal life upon them, since indeed you yourselves learned nothing good from your parents. A woman is not glorified in a multitude of children if she has neglected to train them uprightly in the love of God, for it is not the multitude of children that commends women, but probity. Therefore Saint Anna is deservedly to be honored, who bore for us, without blemish, the mother of God, through whom the Son of God came into the world as Savior. Just as, then, we venerate and worship the most blessed parent of God because, an immaculate virgin, she bore our Redeemer, so too it is fitting that we honor Anna because she brought forth for us, without fault, the mother of the Savior. [unclear] that we should have a mother whence the daughter came forth. Let no one doubt, let no one waver, let no one seethe with wicked thoughts, because if Anna had not been most holy she would not have been chosen as mother of the mother of God. But it was fitting that she who was to bear the mother of God should shine adorned with every holiness, because she was to be the mother not of just any virgin, but of her than whom, among God's pure human creatures, none more pure or more holy could be found. Therefore the most blessed Anna was worthy of this office by the gift of God. She was worthy by the merit of her life; she was also worthy by the testimony of the law; worthy, I say, was she to bear the Mother of God, who knew how to love God above all things, who had learned to keep his law without offense. There was no woman holier than she in Israel, who is believed to have been honored by God with such great gifts. Being barren, she conceived from seed, but without the blemish of seed. She conceived, I say, without the blemish of fault, and Anna, devout, most holy, and blessed, bore Mary, the Mother of God. Who loves the king and does not honor his parent? Who loves Mary and does not venerate Anna? Let the arguments of vanity cease; let words of excuse depart far away. He is proved to love the Son of God less who does not venerate Saint Anna.
