For the authority of Scripture itself says that unless you believe, you will not understand. It is the slowness of our intellect, and also the [unclear] of our desire, whenever we do not rightly understand the divine Scriptures: namely, when the veil of harmful occupation remains spread over our hearts, which does not permit us to come to true understanding. Hence it happens that we do not understand the sense which the Spirit has infused into the divine Scriptures, but, grasping the bare words of the writers, twist them into another sense. Indeed, if our minds were purged of carnal vices; if, through the continual pursuit of sobriety, the intellect were exercised in the Scriptures; if that faith and reverence were applied to the holy Scriptures which their dignity requires, every diligent person would understand more clearly than daylight that there is nothing confused in the sacred readings, nothing discordant, nothing unsuitable, nothing absurd, or introduced without the most just reason. For all the passages of divine Scripture are full of mystery; whenever something in them seems to have been placed obscurely, it has been done by divine providence, so that the diligent mind may have the necessary exercise of piety, and the faith of the simple may not lose the place of due reverence. Therefore it was not by chance, but by eternal providence, that he willed the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that they might abound in as many mysteries as senses in which, by the inspiration of the Spirit of God, they are expounded in Catholic fashion by the doctors. For, as holy Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture is useful and very profitable, because it begets many judgments of truth and brings them forth into the light for human beings; for when one person, and another another, draws out this sense or that by expounding it in Catholic fashion, how lofty and profound it is becomes known. Hence let no man, puffed up with secular learning, suppose that he understands sacred Scripture unless he first lays aside the veil of pride and of every disordered concupiscence.

For secular learning is of greater value for exercising the intellect than for illuminating the understanding toward true wisdom. Therefore, without doubt, divine Scripture is both manifest and clear to minds purified through the continual exercise of the fear of God and frequent study in reading; to minds that are filthy and swollen with secular wisdom it seems confused and obscure. Hence he understands the holy Scriptures well and rightly who, with purified affection and frequent in their study, expounds them just as he who wrote did. For if it is read in a spirit other than that by which he is believed to have inspired them to be written, the mind must be altogether shut off from the true understanding. But when we encounter some discordance in them, let us understand that it is only of words, not of senses; and this arises in an ordered way, not by chance, but by the providence of God, so that the mind, exercised longer in the Scriptures lest it become wholly sluggish, may be strengthened. For the counsel of God cannot be deceived; but the divine Scriptures were written by the counsel of his will; it follows necessarily that they are true and in no way false. They were human beings by whom the divine writings were handed down to us; certainly they wrote words used according to human custom, by which they might signify to posterity what that highest excellence of divinity willed to be known to mortals. Therefore now, you who, devoted to secular wisdom, attack the divine Scriptures, say: the Scripture of the New and Old Testament, which the Church has received and venerates as true, authoritative, and holy Scripture of Christians, from where do you believe it was received? From God, or from human beings? If from God, why do you not believe it? For everyone who attacks Scripture which he believes to have been inspired by God most plainly resists the divine ordinance. But if it is from human beings, why do you hope for the eternal salvation promised in it, which man cannot give? Unless perhaps you madmen go on to say that all things are inventions, and that nothing remains after the fates but ashes, and that the mind undergoes destruction together with the body; such a profession would show you to be no less insane than foolish. For if you truly believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, you will necessarily say that the divine Scriptures were inspired by the Holy Spirit. But if, having fallen with apostates into a reprobate sense, you say that they were fabricated by human beings, you impiously show how far you are from true wisdom, wandering in darkness. You plainly show yourselves to be ignorant and rash judges, since you condemn Scripture which you do not understand. But because it is more familiar to you to despise wisdom than to learn it, it is not the purpose of this work to weary the mind any longer against the rashness of the ignorant. I want you to know this one thing: that no one understands except the one who receives; but because you put the filth of your affections in the way, so that you do not receive, therefore the understanding of true wisdom is not possessed.
