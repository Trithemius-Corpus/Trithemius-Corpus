And again, a little later, he adds, saying: “He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him.” Saint [Christophylus?] also says: “Without faith it is impossible to please God.”

The solution of the proposed question is now clear to all who truly believe in Christ: that through him, the Savior of the world, there is salvation, and whoever does not believe in him cannot be saved. Nor is it permitted for a Christian in any way to pass beyond the bounds of his religion, so as to presume to explain this question otherwise than Christ taught. But whoever departs from this rule of faith by bringing forward the contrary plainly shows that he does not think rightly concerning Christ, the Savior of the faithful. For whoever promises salvation to anyone outside Christ wishes to declare Christ a liar. But we, confirmed by the most sacred ordinances of the Gospel, firmly and indubitably believe and declare that no one outside the faith of Christ can be saved, however much he worships one God, however holy or just a life he lives, however much he trusts in his paternal or other religion. *Christ is salvation, and outside the Christian faith no one will be saved.* For why did Christ, the Son of God, wish to die for the sins of the world, if each person could have been saved outside faith in him, in his own paternal tradition? From the beginning of the world, after the fall of the human race, it was impossible for anyone to be saved unto eternal life otherwise than in the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, he says.

For what of the fact that the greater part of mankind remains outside the faith of Jesus today? Did not the Savior of the world himself long ago foretell in the holy Gospel that many are called, but few chosen? And again he says: “Enter through the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and spacious is the way that leads to perdition, and many are they who enter through it.” What, we ask, can our salvation contribute to God, who has no need of our goods? For whether many are saved, God himself is the immutable Lord of all; nothing is added to him if all are saved, nothing is lacking if the whole world perishes.

The order of divine providence and majesty is infallible; it embraces those who are to be damned no less than those who are to be saved, each and all. Certainly no one of the damned suffers injury, since, just as in the first man we all fell, so no one is saved except in the blood of Jesus Christ through mercy, and no one is damned except in his own unbelief and by desert through justice.

From what has been set forth, another question of no small difficulty arises, which the curiosity of men is accustomed to raise against those who think rightly concerning the faith of Christ: Why does almighty God, since he is just and merciful, not cease to create so many thousands of men whom he most certainly foreknew were to be damned? Why does he not spare the wretched, and refrain from bringing forth into destruction in such a multitude those whom he foreknew would turn aside to evil? For it would seem better not to be than to be evilly. To this question let him answer who thinks himself God’s counsellor. Who am I, that I should explain the cause of the divine will? For all things whatsoever the Lord willed, he made in heaven and on earth; there is no one who can resist his will. Hence a certain wise man says to God: “Who will charge it to you if the nations that you made perish?” The almighty has established for things an order of alternation, which he will never revoke by any fullness of change. For just as he permits his sun to rise upon the good and the evil, so until the end of the world he allows men to be conceived, born, and grow, the pious and the impious. The thoughts of men are not in God, nor does he change his counsel according to time; but since he is immutable and eternal, willing all things together and at once and always, he has from eternity unchangeably preordained them. God speaks once, as Scripture bears witness, and does not add; since whatever he wills, he wills immovably and admits no change.
