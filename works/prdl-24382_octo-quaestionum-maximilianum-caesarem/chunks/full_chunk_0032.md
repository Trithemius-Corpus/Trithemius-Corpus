But there are many reasons, long since assigned by the holy doctors, why the Almighty permits such great evil deeds to be done through the cooperation of demons; of these, in due order, we shall briefly make mention.

*The first* is that the excellence of divine power may be manifested, for he alone is unchangeable and above every positive law. But every mutable creature has no comparison with the unchangeable Creator. Hence one reads in Numbers: God is not like a man, that he should lie, nor like a son of man, that he should be changed. In Isaiah too it is said: God is faithful, without iniquity, just and upright. In the book of Tobit also it is written: You are just, Lord, and all your judgments are true, and all your ways are mercy, truth, and judgment. And in the book of holy Job we read: Far be impiety from God, and iniquity from the Almighty. For he will render to a man his work, and according to the ways of each he will repay them. Therefore every man who rightly understands his own ignorance and weakness openly acknowledges that he is as nothing in comparison with his Creator. For God justly permits man to sin, both so that freedom of choice may be continued, and so that the excellence of the great God may become known to men.

*The second reason* is that the inscrutable wisdom of God may become known to mortals: that he knows how to draw good from our evils, when he makes the sin of one person become the merit of another for the increase of virtue. For if he did not permit evil to be done, which of us would understand the good? For God makes and ordains the good; evils, however, he does not make, but he himself disposes that those committed by the impious should not come about in a disorderly way. Nor does the malice of men impede the will of God, nor can man do anything that God does not permit.

*The third cause* is that the Almighty permits evil deeds to be done in order to manifest his most merciful goodness toward those who have sinned and have done penance; this he could in no way show if he did not permit the creature to sin. For if there were no sinner, God could not be one who has mercy. But since in many things we all offend, everywhere God finds something to remit; everywhere the guilty person presents himself, in whom the kindly Creator may show his mercy. Hence the holy bishop Augustine: If the disease were slight, the physician would be despised and would not be sought; if the physician were not sought, the disease would not be ended. Therefore where sin abounded, grace also abounded, which alone distinguishes the redeemed from the lost, whom the mass of perdition, drawn from the origin of creation, gathers into one. Therefore the mercy of the Lord is over all his works.

*The fourth reason* for divine permission is that the justice of the Creator may be declared toward those who have sinned and have not done penance, since if all did good and no one sinned, God’s justice would have nothing to punish. Therefore, so that justice may have a place, by divine permission the creature sins. For the Lord is just, who will render to each according to the work of his will.

*The fifth reason* is the dignity of the human condition, because if God did not permit man to sin, men would seem worse than brute beasts, which, with no violence driving them, freely use the motions of nature. For even if brute beasts lack reason, nevertheless they have a free motion of nature which they follow; in comparison with this man would seem of a lower condition, if he did not have free choice of the will. But God permitted free will to man, over which neither kings nor princes rule.

*The sixth reason* is the praise of the merit of steadfastness in human virtue, since we praise a just man for this reason, that although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, with the grace of God assisting him, remained in the steadfastness of a good will.

*The seventh reason* why almighty God permits demons to do such great evil deeds through the agency of witches is that those who, scorning truth, delight in falsehood may, seduced by the devil, the inventor of falsehood, be utterly deceived. For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who despise the saving teachings of Christ, should all, deceived by demons, be plunged down to hell to be punished forever. They seek aid from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have adored. For it is just that he who, deserting truth by his own spontaneous will, adheres to falsehood should be cast headlong by the devil into destruction.

*The eighth cause* is the manifestation of divine power, whose might and virtue are over every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits great things to the devil and denies certain small things, it is apparent how weak the enemy of the human race is, and subject to the divine majesty in all things; whatever he does, he does with God permitting it.
