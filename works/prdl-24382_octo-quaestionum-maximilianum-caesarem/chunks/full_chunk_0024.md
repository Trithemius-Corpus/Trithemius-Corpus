Hence no one among mortals is good unless aided, properly speaking, by the counsel of his will through the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil unless, of his own accord, deprived of grace, [he has] the obstacle of sin. By uprightness of will, however, we are made like the good spirits, if we confirm the piety of the faith which we have in Christ by the necessary holy works. And just as the consummation of a good will makes good men equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious renders them like demons. Hence evil and perverse men, the more they have been depraved by the malice of their own will, the more they become like demons in disposition.

But likeness introduces the intimacy of friendship, from which mutual confidence is gradually generated. From the confidence of the lesser toward the greater there arises authority, whether true or feigned by cunning. Thus if any authority of depraved men is seen to exist over demons, it arises from the greatness of their depravity: namely, when their disposition is so far depraved that by a reprobate will they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons who join themselves to them. For demons are not all equal in malice or intelligence; rather one is much more cunning than another in agility of understanding, and far worse in depraved affection. For those who inhabit [unclear] and caves are assuredly duller than the rest, to such an extent that they fear swords and rods and seem to be admonished by the threats of men who terrify them, as may be observed in those whom we call energumens. But demons who dwell farther removed from the earth are subtler in nature and meet with men more rarely.

Men full of malice, however, the more they have advanced in perversity of will, the more they become like demons; and once admitted by them into familiarity, they thereafter become bold, and they receive authority over the demons whose familiarity they use, not by violent power but by feigned pretense. For demons, in order to keep in their depravity those men whom they have once received into surrender as willing subjects, gladly submit themselves to them as if moved by a simulated dominion; and what they do voluntarily, they pretend they are compelled to do in obedience. For no one obtains true authority over demons except him whom faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.”

Evil men, therefore, command demons, whom malevolence has made like to them, likeness has made familiar, and familiarity has made their own and subject. Just as, if some servant of a great prince, lowered in rank, yet joined to him by long familiarity, is said to command him, from whom he obtains without contradiction everything he has asked, so that he himself seems rather to rule his lord than to be ruled by him. According to this likeness, evil and reprobate men, because they are admitted into the demons’ familiarity and by more prolonged confidence become very bold, receive [unclear] they have deserved by likeness. Hence whatever they have demanded from demons joining with them by an implicit or explicit compact, they receive, unless at times, being forbidden by divine ordinance, they cannot provide what is sought. Yet they do not thus everywhere join themselves to all reprobate and evil persons, but only to those with whom, by the intervention of a compact, they have entered into certain agreements: such as women who are witches, who have rendered homage to the devil, and those invokers of demons whom the common people call by the usual word necromancers.
