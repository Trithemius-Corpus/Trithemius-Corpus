But after our mind, at last released from the flesh, has [unclear] escaped to its own center, made equal to the angels, we shall know God perfectly, and all things, just as the holy Apostle says to the Corinthians: “Now we see through a mirror and in an enigma; but then face to face.” Therefore we said that the angelic intellect, by the guidance of its nature, adheres immovably to its own principle, because it is pure act and habit; and since it is naturally simple in condition, it can be hindered by no traffic of composition. But the human intellect, as it is now sufficiently established has been declared, since it is potentiality and privation, is impure because of its commerce with its body; indeed it is so far held back from perfect act until at last, released from the flesh, it shall have passed into the fellowship of likeness with the angels, then for the first time to be admitted to the true knowledge of God. But if meanwhile the affection of love, necessary for salvation, is joined to present Christian faith and future divine knowledge, the same eternal blessedness prepared for God’s worshippers will be granted with the holy angels. But if a depraved mind, through too much commerce with its flesh, has scorned the love of God, then indeed the intellect, released from its prison-house, will escape to the knowledge sublime in the measure belonging to it by nature; but it will be the more wretched, the more by evil will it is found to be like the demons. Therefore, to conclude as I may the solution of the question proposed, I say that it is not possible that almighty God be perfectly understood by us so long as, mortal, we dwell in the flesh.

*On Faith Necessary for Salvation: The Second Question.*

The second question of your unconquered majesty was this. Since today only a small part of the world is subject to the laws of the Christian faith: whether that opinion of very many people seems admissible without injury to the truth, who think that each person, in his own religion which he believes to be true and saving, as a worshipper of one God, can be saved outside the Christian faith without baptism, provided that nothing is known of the religion of Christ.

To respond to this question simply, according to the common intention, I judged superfluous, since I know that today many among Christians, through what is as it were an impious piety for the salvation of lost men, make themselves cruel toward God. But because the cause of just consideration moved your most serene highness to ask these things, not because you do not know what must be held as Catholic in this position, but so that you might have something true to set against those who attack it, I shall answer the questions, as briefly as I can. If anyone could be saved outside the Christian faith, Christ, the Son of almighty God, would seem to have been made man superfluously and without cause. For if there is salvation outside Christ, Christ is not the savior of all. But because it was necessary, according to the Scriptures, that the only-begotten Son of God, for the salvation of the human race, be born a man in the world, suffer, die, and rise again on the third day, he did not come into this world in vain, nor superfluously, nor without cause. Since this is so, whoever asserts that anyone can be saved outside the faith of Christ shows himself not a Christian but an adversary of the faith and a deserter of Christ. For whoever does not believe in Christ the Son of God promises himself eternal salvation in vain.
