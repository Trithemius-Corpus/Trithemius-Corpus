But the second kind is of those persons to whom, by reason of their order in the Church of God, it is granted that, having received the power of the ministry, they may drive demons from human bodies. And this ministry is committed to exorcists in the Church of God, namely when they are ordained in apostolic fashion.

Again, there are two kinds of persons who desire to command demons; and they show, by their most wicked conditions, that they presume to do this unlawfully.

The first, most pernicious kind is that of witches, who enter into pacts with demons, and, after a solemn profession has first been made, hand themselves over to some demon as his property and for perpetual service. How great are the evils which this most pernicious kind of persons works in your empire, most wise Caesar, there is no one who can express. For first of all, denying God, the faith of Christ, and holy baptism, they offer themselves, in mind and body, to demons; by their sorceries they make men and beasts sick, harm them, and kill them. There is no disease which, God permitting, they cannot bring on, because, since they show themselves servants to demons in the observance of the covenant that has been contracted, in comparison with those demons they dare whatever they wish. Hence they are nowhere to be tolerated, but rather in every place to be utterly exterminated, as God, creator of all, commands thus: “You shall not suffer sorcerers to live.” Of witches, some call upon demons with incantations; others entice them with prayers and sacrifices; the rest, provoking them with unspeakable things and ceremonies, draw them into the aid of their impiety. It is therefore not to be wondered at that witches seem to command demons, to whom, through the impiety of a perverse will, they have become like, and with whom they are in mutual concord.

The second kind of wicked persons who wish to command demons is that of those whom we commonly call necromancers. They profess arts full of execrations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly visible, enclosed in a crystal or some other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of falsehood and superstitions, commit such great evils as no good man could imagine. For in order to entice demons into obedience to themselves, they very often use the foulest sacrifices and enter into an explicit pact with them. They carry about many various little books full of filth, impiety, and falsehood, falsely inscribed with the names of ancient philosophers and sages; by displaying these they deceive many unwary and curious people and draw them with themselves into the demons’ pit. They promise great and utterly incredible things; by the hearing of these, they hold men suspended in wonder at the falsehood. By rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations, they say that demons, though malignant, can be constrained, so that they appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal, or in some other receptacle, and answer distinctly to all questions asked. All indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found whom the malignant spirits, even in evil matters, obey in this way, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it: lest human curiosity, prone to novelties, after despising the religion of Christ, should in necessity seek help from demons. But all those whom malignant spirits, summoned by the conjurations of necromancers, obey have without doubt passed into fellowship with them by an explicit or implicit pact. Yet the number of the rest is greater: those whom the malignant spirits disdain to obey, although in profane matters they do not fear to occupy themselves always with the arts of necromancers. And because especially in our times iniquity has begun to abound, and charity has grown cold, these accursed and most deceitful men have gained too much strength; and although they are despised by demons, nevertheless, persevering in their most pernicious doctrines, they deceive many.
