For he is an Antichrist, not a Christian, who says that one who does not believe in the Lord Jesus is to be saved without the Savior. Therefore, most wise Caesar, let no one appear in the sight of your majesty, let no one feel that you adheres to him, who thinks that men are to be saved outside the faith of Jesus Christ. For everyone who has believed that anyone is to be saved outside the faith of Christ has denied both Christ and his Gospel. But when we say that all who are not Christians are to be damned, this is a sign not of cruelty but of true piety. For it is better to believe firmly in the truth than to assent falsely to a man. That all Jews, pagans, gentiles, Saracens, schismatics, heretics, and unbelievers are without doubt to be damned eternally with the demons is attested by the holy Church, which took and confirmed this judgment from no other source than from the words of Christ. For thus he said: “He who believes in the Son has eternal life,” by which the faith and work of a good Christian are signified. “But he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him.” Here are designated all Jews, pagans, Saracens, gentiles, and all who do not believe in our Lord Jesus Christ. Concerning schismatics and heretics, however, the judgment of Christ is manifest in the Gospel, where he says: “If he refuses to hear the Church, let him be to you as a heathen and a publican.” Heathens are gentiles; publicans are manifest and public sinners, of whose damnation the most certain holy Church does not doubt. For who among Christians would doubt that gentiles and monstrous sinners, and manifest ones, are to be damned? Since, therefore, all schismatics and heretics despise the holy Church, they are most rightly said to be like the gentiles and publicans who are to be damned for their crimes and deserts, and they will justly undergo with them equal punishments of everlasting damnation.

Behold those whom the just sentence of Christ the Savior has damned: does the foolish commiseration of men, not to say impious cruelty, presume that they are to be saved? They desire to be merciful toward lost men, and do not fear to show themselves cruel toward God. Those whom he by justice has assigned to death, they assert, with the worst presumption, are to be recalled to life. It is not good or useful for a Christian, whether in earnest or in jest, to contradict Christ. He is impious, not a Christian, whoever says that unbelievers are to be saved. When Adam, the first man, perished, we all sinned, and the wrath of God remained upon the whole human race from the beginning of the world until Christ. Christ came, born of a virgin; being God and man, innocent of sin, he voluntarily underwent the death of the cross, and, having become mediator between God and man, removed the wrath of original guilt from those who believe in him. For thus he says: “He who believes in the only-begotten Son of God has eternal life. But he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life; rather, the wrath of God remains upon him.” Indeed, without faith in Jesus Christ no one will be saved, however justly and holily he may seem to live in his own religion. For almighty God can save a believer without material baptism, but he cannot make safe one who denies the faith without faith. No one is saved unwillingly, because no one believes unless willingly. For the Lord knows who are his, since he saves those who believe in the Son through repentance, but condemns unbelievers by the equity of justice.

Hence, while these matters were enclosed, a new question struck the mind. What is to be thought about the infants and fools of unbelieving pagans and Jews? To this one must answer briefly. Just as the infants of Christians, baptized in the faith of their parents, are saved, so the unbaptized children of all unbelievers will be damned in the perfidy of their parents. The judgment concerning fools of either religion is similar: just as we believe without doubt that baptized fools of Christians are to be saved, so we should say without doubt that those of unbelievers are to be damned.
