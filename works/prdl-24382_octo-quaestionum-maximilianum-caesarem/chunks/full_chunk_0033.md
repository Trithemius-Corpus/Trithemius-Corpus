But because the power of every creature, dependent upon its creator, is restricted and limited, it is plain that neither can the devil do everything he wishes, nor can a man, however depraved in will. The will indeed is free, but power is limited by certain and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wish, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would undermine everything. The history of Saint Job shows by a manifest example that the power of demons is bound: Satan did not dare to tempt him beyond what had been permitted him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could withstand the fury of demons, if they could always freely use the power of doing harm at their own discretion. Before the Lord’s Passion they used a greater liberty of power than afterwards; but when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, from then on their power was greatly diminished, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of the world will be cast out.”

For indeed the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints have greatly restrained the boldness of demons, so that they can rightly be called kindlers of evils, but by no means compellers. Nor would the devil prevail against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” And again: “Do not give place to the devil.” Therefore, to bring the discourse to a conclusion for the understanding of the proposed question, why God permits so many evil deeds to be done, I believe the reasons have been sufficiently made plain. For if the will of demons is always wicked, nevertheless their power can never be unjust, because what it wickedly wills it has from its own choice, but what it can wickedly do it has from God alone. And these things have been said as far as concerns those who do evil.

But why does almighty God permit demons to injure men, through the agency of evildoers, who have been baptized in the name of the holy Trinity and faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ? For if they could injure only evil, perverse unbelievers in body and possessions, but were restrained from approach to and injury of the good and faithful, his permission would rightly seem to be a just judgment. But now good and innocent people are more frequently made ill by the evil deeds of demons, and sometimes are even deprived of life, to pass over in silence the loss of goods that those subjected to sorcery very often suffer. It troubles many why the Almighty permits these things, he who permits nothing unjustly.

For this proposed question, I shall assign in order, as briefly as I can, the necessary reasons, received from no others than the ancient and Catholic doctors. And the first reason is the testing of the faith of Christians and the experience of integrity: so that, when scourged by the evil deeds of demons, they may learn in the sufferings of the flesh what faith and love toward God they have hidden in the mind.
