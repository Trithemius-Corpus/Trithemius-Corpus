For divine providence supplies to the human race both crops and all the other things that the earth brings forth: also storms and variations, the changes of times, by which all things that the earth produces, being endowed with nature, grow to maturity. And it provides for all things collectively and individually: not only the great, but even the very smallest, so far indeed that not even the most slender leaf of a tree falls to the ground without the will of almighty God the Father, who created it and rules and cares for it. But if anyone thinks that this so great magnitude of the whole universe cannot be directed and governed by one God, who alone is, he is greatly mistaken, and is shown not to know how great is the power of divine might. For indeed he who was able to bring the whole world from nothing into being can also govern forever, with the greatest wisdom, what he has made. For he knows all things, understands all things, can do all things: he alone, omnipotent, created all things.

We therefore, believing the holy Gospel, should above all venerate divine providence; and we should raise our minds upward, with whatever virtue we can, from these things that are perishable, and, being brought back to ourselves, be united, so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God present everywhere. Since it is altogether uncircumscribable, it holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, exceeds all things, and sustains all things. It does not sustain from one part and exceed from another; nor does it fill from one part and embrace from another; but by embracing it fills, by filling it super-embraces, by sustaining it exceeds, and by exceeding it sustains all things. He is nowhere by the mass of a body, and by his uncircumscribable substance he is nowhere absent; he is not seen by the eyes of flesh; he is seen through faith in purity of heart. O inexplicable power. O incomprehensible goodness. O ineffable wisdom. O uncircumscribable divinity: you do not consist of parts as a body does; you are not divided by affections as a soul is; you do not underlie forms as do all the other things that have been made through you; you are in all things and above all things, ruling and governing the universe with the greatest wisdom. By these testimonies, therefore, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, it has been sufficiently proved, as I think, that this whole universe is governed by the providence of God. Since he is pure intellect, simple, uncreated, and eternal, he knows all things, understands all things, foreknows all things; and since he is omnipotent by nature, he can do all things. To him nothing is difficult, nothing impossible; he, with his power, created such wondrous things; in truth he established them; by reason he perfected them; by strength he sustains them; by goodness he illumines them. To him be honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, God glorious and exalted, forever and ever. Amen.

*The Author’s Declaration to Caesar*

These, most unconquered Caesar Maximilian, are the things that seemed to me should be said in reply to the Eight Questions proposed by your majesty. For you wished to have even my judgment on them in writing, making an estimate of me above the truth, since I am not to be compared in talent with those whose opinion you heard before me. Let pardon make me worthy if I have said anything less proper; for, at the command of your highness, while set as a traveler on a journey, I dictated as I could, at intervals, whatever occurred to me to answer. But I say from my mind and openly declare that I am a sincere confessor of the Christian faith, and that I wish to remain until death in the union of the sacrosanct Roman Church. Therefore, if anything either in this lucubration of mine or in others should be found said or written otherwise than that Church holds, thinks, believes, and teaches, as a true son of obedience, by the attestation of these presents I now simply revoke it, and wish it to be and to be held perpetually revoked.

Wherefore, most wise Caesar, I both humbly ask and earnestly admonish your majesty that, before you cause my responses to come to the knowledge of those for whom they are kept destined, you have them approved by the judgment of many. For it must be carefully ensured that nothing be proposed to those for whose salvation your serenity keeps watch except what is solid, approved, manifest, and lawfully confirmed in the Catholic faith by the opinion of very many wise men. May almighty God by his providence direct and guard you always to the praise of his name, Maximilian, greatest of emperors. From the city of Neometana, on the tenth day of the month of July, in the year of Christians one thousand five hundred and eight.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot.
