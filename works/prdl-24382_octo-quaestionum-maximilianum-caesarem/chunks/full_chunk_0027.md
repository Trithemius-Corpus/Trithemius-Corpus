*On the Power of Witches. Sixth Question.*

The sixth question of your most invincible majesty was: whence comes this so great power to witches, by which they can accomplish so many various wonders even in a single hour, such and so great as no one of the good could accomplish in his whole life?

To this question, if we wish to answer briefly, we must necessarily say that all power is from the Lord God, without whom no creature can do anything. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Without me you can do nothing.” Hence it is manifest that all power of doing anything is granted to human beings by the Lord God and from no other source, whether men do good or evil. Hence the most holy prelate Augustine says: “The will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily forms and motions; and nothing happens in this most vast and immense commonwealth of the whole visible creation which is not either commanded or permitted from the inner, invisible, and intelligible court of the supreme emperor, according to the ineffable justice of rewards and punishments.”

Therefore, Caesar, it must not be doubted that that power of witches, in which, with the cooperation of demons, they produce wondrous and horrible effects, is granted by the Lord God, whose will no one can resist, and without whose influence a man cannot even move his hand. For the holy doctors conclude that neither demons nor witches can do anything, whether to harm a man or a beast, unless God himself wills it, consents, and grants the power, so that they may do what they have already resolved upon with a perverse will. Unless God consents and wills, no creature can do anything at all; otherwise, if something could happen without his willing and consenting to it, it would necessarily follow that God is weak, since he could not prevent what, unconquered, he would be forced to permit to happen. But because every man, insofar as he is a creature, depends on God, he can do nothing without the will of him from whom he has both the willing and the power.

Whatever, therefore, witches do is done by God’s permission; without his will they can do nothing, because, unless God has granted it, they obtain neither from themselves nor from demons the ability to do what they wish. And so all power is from the Lord God, in whom all things are moved as in the first cause of all things; for from him from whom we have received freedom of choice, from him also we have the power to do what we will. But since the understanding of witches is much confused, they do not perpetrate the evils they do by human art, but by the cooperation of demons, whom nature has made subtle and long experience has made swift in producing wonders. For the effects of witches are not caused by such means as they use for witchcraft, such as heads of the dead, toads, serpents, characters, images, and things similar to these, but by the devil who established these signs; since he is most proud, he desires in all things to be seen as comparable to God. For just as God once instituted sacraments in his Church, which, when worthily administered, signify and produce a saving effect, while he himself assists and works the salvation which he instituted, so the devil, who desires nothing more than to be esteemed equal to God, instituted certain signs as if sacraments; and when he is invoked by them by those to whom he is bound by covenant pacts, the malignant operator assists, and as often as God has permitted it, he effects the evil that is asked; if God has not permitted it, he does nothing at all.
