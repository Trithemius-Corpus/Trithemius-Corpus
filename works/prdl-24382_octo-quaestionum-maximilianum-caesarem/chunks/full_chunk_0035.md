... to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution. The most wise king Solomon also says in Proverbs: Whom the Lord loves, he corrects, and as a father he takes pleasure in his son. For a Christian it is a very glorious thing, for love of God, to bear adversities patiently, whether they are inflicted by evil men or by demons. For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and just men, not to their confounding, as [unclear] suppose, but rather to the greatest and sure consolation of the soul. For when a just man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves his God with his whole heart, fears and venerates him, keeping his commandments with all solicitude, and nevertheless is afflicted by evildoers, whether in body, in possessions, or in both, that man is truly blessed, since, when he has been tested, he will receive the crown of life which God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the catholic Church of Christians, and patiently bears for love of God the evils he receives in this world, is a sure sign that he will persevere in the grace of God, to be saved. On the contrary, concerning the wicked reprobate we read it written thus: Why do the wicked live? They have been raised up and strengthened with riches. Their houses are secure; the rod of God is not upon them. They spend their days in good things; in a moment they go down to hell. For it is a manifest sign of damnation if here you live wickedly in sins and vices, contrary to God, while prospered in the goods of fortune and in bodily health. The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and just men to be afflicted by the wicked reprobate is for the complete purification of merits. For, as Scripture says, there is no just man on earth who does good and does not sin. But since God lets no sin pass unpunished, he appoints temporal punishments for just men, through mercy, for their offenses, but eternal punishments for the impious reprobate, through justice. Therefore, out of mercy, he changes eternal punishment into temporal punishment for the penitent, which it is necessary that they suffer either in this world or after death in purgatory. But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and very grave, the merciful God is accustomed to purify in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the inconveniences of diverse tribulations in body and in possessions.

For the evil deeds of the impious benefit good and just men as a remedy, but bring damnation upon those who inflict them. The sixth reason is that a man, harmed through the assaults of demons, may be recalled to penitence and may resolve in his heart upon the necessary amendment of his life. For Almighty God does not immediately send vengeance when we sin, but waits for our amendment until a predetermined time. But if we neglect to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not think it; and [unclear]. Hence the holy prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: If his sons abandon my law and do not walk in my judgments and do not keep my commandments, I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their sins with blows; but my mercy I will not scatter from them, nor will I harm my truth.

The seventh reason why Almighty God permits the evil deeds of demons to prevail over the good and innocent is that too licentious an occasion of sinning may be cut away from them. For there are many poor and infirm people, pleasing to God, good, devout, and humble, whom poverty and bodily sickness preserve in the love of God; if they were rich and raised high with honors, they would quickly flow away from the resolve of holiness into the depth of vices. It is not expedient for all, for their salvation, to have honors and riches together with health; nor does a longer life always make a man better. Therefore almighty God, wishing to show his mercy toward those whom he has chosen, permits the good, just, and innocent to be subject to various calamities.
