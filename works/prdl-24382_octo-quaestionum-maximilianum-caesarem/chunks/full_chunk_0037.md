But the providence of the Creator cares for all things: for he who feeds the birds and gives the beasts their food, and fills every living creature with blessing, and clothes the grass of the field, adorning violets and lilies with beautiful comeliness, cannot forget man, whom he created from the beginning in his own image. He cares for all things who governs all things; and he exercises providence not only over man but also over all beasts. Since all things are placed under his dominion, there is no one who can resist his will in anything.

Let us now therefore consider this mighty structure of the whole universe, how in its original arrangement and motion it always persists most orderly: unless we shamefully depart from reason, we shall understand by the clearest reasoning that that divine and unchangeable providence preserves, directs, and governs these things. Then let us contemplate all earthly things, each and every one of which arises and proceeds most orderly in its natural disposition. This cannot happen by chance; rather they must be moved by the will of him by whose power they were made and created. Next, the earth placed in the middle of the world, like a point or center that unfolds the circle of the whole universe at equal distance: than this nothing more beautiful, nothing more adorned, nothing, finally, more delightful to the sense could offer itself for [unclear]. In this point of immense mass, wonderfully enclosed by the mass itself, we discern all things ordered, and nothing could be left unordered; so that all things which sometimes seem to us somehow to drift, we should know certainly, in view of the order of the universe, not to lack a certain order of their own. For by the simple and utterly unchangeable nod of God the whole structure of the entire universe, gathered from so many diverse and movable things, is most orderly governed in harmony. Certainly we see, in those things which present themselves to our senses, that the greater the intelligence and power that arises with time in any governor, the less can anything disordered happen by chance. Since, therefore, that supreme intellect, the creator, preserver, ruler, almighty God, governs the world by his unchangeable providence, holding all things under his command, certainly no place is left anywhere for rash chance, which might impede the will of so great a majesty.

Therefore the universe, whatever exists anywhere or can exist, is comprehended under the rule of one divine mind, whose infallible judgment nothing can escape. But unexpected events happen to us, because we are changeable and are changed almost daily, having no determined knowledge of future things. But that best maker of all things, God, since he is altogether unchangeable, knows all things, beholds all things, understands all things at once and simultaneously. His providence is not deceived; his will is never varied; his power is not broken; his ordering is not violated; his counsel is never interrupted, even if his decision sometimes seems to be changed. Hence, if we attend carefully, we shall plainly understand, since the ends of things and of motions will make it clear, that all these lower things are made, preserved, disposed, and moved by divine providence; to its command nothing at all is alien. For the Lord God of the universe himself understands all things individually, not stretched out after our fashion among many things, but abiding in himself as an indivisible one. By looking upon him, as upon the pattern of the whole universe, the cause of causes, and through causes the cause of all things, he beholds all things; in beholding he cares and provides, directing each thing to a good end by an everlasting order. Come now, most wise Caesar, in order that we may understand divine providence governing all things, let us employ arguments, as briefly as we can, according to our purpose.
