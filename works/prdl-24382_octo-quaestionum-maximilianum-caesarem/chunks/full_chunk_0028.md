But women who are witches, who with their will already depraved and with consent have passed by pacts into fellowship with demons, receive from them certain marks and symbolic signs; when these are summoned by them, the demons at once come. In these pernicious operations of demons nothing is stronger than the foolish credulity of witches, which is increased by pacts and vows, since the firmer the faith they have placed either in the demons or in their signs, the more swiftly they obtain the operation, with those demons cooperating. For the excessively savage depravity of the witches’ will in a certain way turns them into madness; when this has preoccupied the mind, the demons at once apply themselves to those who are raging, and, invited by pacts and signs, God permitting, they grant to the madwomen the effect of their petition. For Plato related something similar in the book On Poetic Frenzy, saying: just as Bacchic women, when not of sound mind, draw honey and milk from rivers, but when of sound mind cannot draw them. This, however, is effected by their spirit, carried outside itself into fellowship with an evil demon. We have established that witches have drawn butter from stirred-up rivers at times, which can in no way happen otherwise than by the ministry of a demon. In a similar way, when women have been brought into hatred of some man or beast, sighing for his destruction, they are driven by a very grievous frenzy, thinking of nothing in their mind except revenge. They take things known and familiar to them for witchcraft, invoke the demons, show the signs instituted by them together with the characters; and what they cannot accomplish by their own power, they obtain by the ministry of those demons. But another person, who is not subject to demons by pact and surrender, even if he knows the things suitable for witchcraft as well as the method of working, and although he displays the signs, nevertheless rarely obtains the effect, because he is not reckoned among the familiars of demons. For to such a degree the devil, puffed up with pride, imitates the church of Christ, that just as one who has not been ordained cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he may pronounce the words of consecration, so also a demon, however much he is invoked and signs or characters are shown to him, either never or rarely comes to the aid of one whom he does not know to be subject to himself by pact. For if he came indiscriminately to all who invoked him, without pacts being interposed, he would not procure so many witches, subject to him by right of ownership. Every day we see demons being summoned to a circle, to a mirror, or to any other receptacles by dreadful conjurations, with men laboring, and they achieve no effect, because either God’s command stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man not dedicated to himself by homage. For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is agreed that none is perpetrated: a witch raging in mind and depraved in will; also the cooperation of a familiar demon; and before all things divine permission. But if one of these does not concur, the witchcraft will not be able to be perpetrated. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not come to the aid of the wicked woman’s will, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch is lacking, the devil will in no way complete the witchcraft. For he uses the will of the raging witch as an artisan uses an instrument for working; without it he could not inflict witchcraft. And just as demons differ from one another in power and in the keenness of their subtlety, so the power of witches is not one and the same in all of them, but differs in individuals according as demons of subtler or grosser nature come together with them. For there are many kinds of demons, and they stand apart from one another by fixed degrees, by reason of the places into which, falling from the beginning, they were thrust down.
