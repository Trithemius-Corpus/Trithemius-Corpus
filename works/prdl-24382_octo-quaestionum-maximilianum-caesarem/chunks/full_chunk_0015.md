But in this way unbelievers cannot work miracles, for they do not walk the necessary path of faith unto salvation. For even if many of them believe in one God, nevertheless, because they refuse to believe in the mediator between God and men, they have gone very far away from the mercy of God.

But, as the holy Gospel promises, all things are possible for those who believe in God and our Lord Jesus Christ, that is, for those who also adorn right faith with holy works. For indeed, just as without faith it is impossible to please God, so also faith without works is dead. For almighty God would grant us all the things that we ask in the faith of Christ, if we did all things according to the faith that we owe. Believe firmly and act faithfully, and you will always obtain all things.

*Second*, a man works miracles by likeness to angelic purity. For the purer our mind becomes in love, confirmed in the faith of Jesus Christ, so much the more, made like to the holy angels, does it attain greater familiarity with them. Whoever, however, has attained complete familiarity with the holy angels, when he wishes, by God’s bounty, he will shine forth in miracles. For the holy angels reveal to pure men, and to those fervent in the love of God, secrets hidden from others, and by command of the Creator they make many things manifest to them. For the Lord is near to those who call upon him in truth. The Lord will do the will of those who fear him; and he will hear their prayers and save them. For whoever through grace is one with Christ is refreshed by the frequent visitation of angels; and, as often as necessity requires it, he is made illustrious by signs and powers through their ministry.

Hence it is that those also who are weighed down by various illnesses betake themselves to the intercessions of the saints; and, calling to mind the promises of the Lord, they obtain by another’s merits what they cannot obtain by their own. Their holy devotion in the faith of Christ receives salvation from the Lord; for, aided by the purity of its own conscience and supported by the merit of another’s holiness, it rises, not rashly, into sure hope.

Miracles, however, occur in the Church of God by divine power with a threefold regard or means. For sometimes almighty God works a miracle by the ministry of angels, solely by the merits of the worker himself, as Saint Martin healed a blind man and a lame man against their will. Sometimes miracles occur by the merit of the sufferer alone, as we read that many were cured through bad Christians whom the sick believed to be very good in the faith. Third, that healings of this kind occur by the merits of both is to be held, since Christ says to a certain person in the holy Gospel: If you can believe, all things are possible to the believer. For faith is always required for a miracle, so that our Lord Jesus Christ, coming into his own country, wondered that he could not do any mighty work there because of their unbelief. But none of these things is done among unbelievers, where neither holiness in the worker nor orthodox faith in the sufferer is found.
