To them the Spirit of God brings help by his presence; those whom he has found rooted in the love of truth, purged of every extraneous thing, he leads into the knowledge of all things. For truly, to those who love God all things are open, all things naked, all things manifest, since whoever through love beholds in the faith of Jesus Christ the one who creates and governs all things knows everything that is necessary for his salvation. But, on the contrary, the illumination of God dwells very far from the slothful, wicked feasters, the envious, the greedy, incubi, the foul, the lustful, murderers, the impious and proud, those alien to piety; it leaves them to the judgment of the avenging demon, who, driving in the sharp point of savage fire, afflicts the senses and the mind, and all the more arms and drives the impious to perpetrate crimes, so that, guilty of a more shameful fault, they may also become liable to a more bitter punishment. Hence those entangled in worldly vices and desires are not only unable to penetrate the divine Scriptures with understanding, but, neglecting themselves, do not even know themselves.

Since, therefore, sacred Scripture is the perfect work of God, Caesar, we must so establish our mind that we believe it to be true, right, complete, solid, divinely inspired, and most abundantly sufficient for all who understand it for the confirmation of the Christian faith. For indeed, unless divine Scripture is believed to be true, holy, perfect in all respects, and complete, the Christian faith would be deprived of its foundation. Therefore every Christian must take the greatest care not to overstep the bounds of piety by examining the divine Scriptures more than is fitting, because the author of the Scriptures is not man but the Holy Spirit; and whoever resists the integrity of the Scriptures of God is proved to resist his ordering. Scripture, moreover, by God’s ordinance, is usefully interwoven in its text of figures with parables, riddles, likenesses, and comparisons, so that readers may be exercised toward understanding, and, once its inward sweetness has been tasted, the mind may be strengthened toward piety. And so, if with purified minds we attend to the sense of the Scriptures, not the words, we shall understand how rightly and properly all things have been ordered. For it was not fitting that the sacred Scriptures, in which the knowledge of the divine majesty is handed down, should be so plain and manifest that they would everywhere be understood by all; for this knowledge is set forth to be understood only by the good and holy, not also by evil, foul, and polluted minds. Hence it is written: The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones. What is designated here by the beast, if not human beings who, in imitation of brute animals, live without the love of God in the pleasures of the flesh? And what is expressed by the mountain, if not the loftiness of the divine Scriptures?
