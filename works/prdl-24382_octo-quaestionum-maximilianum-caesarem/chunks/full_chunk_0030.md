Hence Saint John Chrysostom, in the third book of *On Providence* to the monk Stagyrius, says: “The greatness of grief is more powerful for doing harm than any diabolical action, because the devil conquers whomever he conquers through grief. If you remove it, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon.”

This kind of demons, however, is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy of its affections and the frivolity by which they delight in terrifying many rather than being subject to the command of one wretched little woman. Yet sometimes, as they promise to madwomen, they dwell in glass or in crystal or in a mirror, and, stirred up by charms, give answers to women. And if someone who is about to come has spoken ill of them on the road, they foretell it. And this kind of witch sometimes differs greatly from the rest, since those whom the former make sick by sorcery, the latter promise to cure. But because for the most part they repel sorcery by sorcery, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, because, immersing itself in waters, it dwells around lakes and rivers, full of anger, troubled, restless, and deceitful. In the sea it stirs up storms, and, sinking ships into the deep, takes life from many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, they more often appear in the female sex, and are seen more rarely in the male, because they live in damp places and follow a softer course of life, nor can they easily assume a manly form according to the custom of the others. Hence antiquity long ago named the Naiads and Nereids, nymphs of the waters, not with the male sex but with the female. Among our people, moreover, aquatic women, that is, Wasserfrauen, are so called by the common term.

But those who dwell in drier, somewhat arid places, whenever they appear visibly to human beings in bodily form, transform themselves into men. They also assume the forms of different beasts, according as they are touched by various affections. But the holy angels, since they are never changed in affection, always appear uniformly in male form. For nowhere do we read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast, but always in male appearance. Demons, however, have very often been seen around rivers and springs in the appearance of women: sometimes dancing, sometimes letting down their hair in the manner of women, and sometimes speaking with human beings and carrying out various mockeries. And this kind of demons has not customarily had commerce with witches.

The fifth kind is called subterranean, because it dwells in caves, caverns, and remote hollows of mountains. And these demons are very evil in disposition, and they especially attack those who dig wells and mines, and those who seek treasures hidden in the earth; they are most ready for the destruction of the human race. They make openings in the earth and stir up flame-vomiting winds; they shake the foundations of buildings. Sometimes at night, coming out of the mountains in troops, they lead marvelous and astonishing dances in the fields, as though fearing the command of a single leader. At a sign they suddenly vanish and return to their hiding places. At times the sound of little bells is heard among them; sometimes they falsely claim that they are the spirits of men departed from life. They seek nothing more than human fear and wonder. Hence we have found that they have sometimes led certain simpler people into their hiding places in the mountains, showing astonishing spectacles to those who marveled, and falsely claim that there are dwellings of the blessed there, and that they are friends of the living.

Among these demons, too, some are brooding guardians of treasures, which the avarice of mortals has hidden in the earth; lest they again come into human use, they remove them, steal them, guard them, and sometimes transfer them from place to place. And this kind of demons has not customarily had any [unclear] with witch-women.
