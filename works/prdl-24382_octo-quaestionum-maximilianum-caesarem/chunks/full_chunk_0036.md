And sometimes through demons, sometimes through evil men, sometimes by various other ways and means, he takes away from men of this sort the riches and honors which he foresaw they would use badly, so that in poverty and abasement they may preserve the humility which, placed in wealth and high estate, they would by no means have preserved. He also quite often permits them to fall into many great illnesses, when cheerful health of the flesh would have brought upon them the greatest illness of mind. Finally, he permits certain persons to be driven to death by sorcery, so that their opportunity for sinning may be cut off, an opportunity which they would have abused had they been allowed to live longer. Hence Scripture says of the just man: “He was snatched away lest malice change his understanding, or deceit beguile his soul.” The eighth reason for divine permission is that men may venerate the sacraments of the Church, which have great power against all the sorceries of malignant spirits. For he is rarely harmed by the sorceries of demons who venerates with due honor the sacraments and rites of the Catholic Church. And from the own confession of witches we have rather often heard that attendance at masses and the use of things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons. For they are not blessed in the Church of God in vain, but so that they may be defenses of divine protection, tokens showing demons signs to be feared. As for infant children, who, reborn in Christ, are sometimes killed by the sorceries of demons or of men, we do not doubt that divine providence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things for the better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish by sorceries because of the offenses of their parents.

*The eighth question of your Serenity was this: Whether it can be proved by natural reason, as also from the Holy Scriptures, that God has care for human affairs and actions, and a sure and infallible providence over all and each of the things that are done in the world.*

To this question I remember that I answered as follows. The whole world, heaven and earth and all things that are in them, are subject to divine providence, by whose will and command they were brought forth into being from the beginning. For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us confirm this opinion first by the Scriptures, and afterwards by reasons, as briefly as possible. In the book of Judith we read: “All your ways are prepared, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the most wise king Solomon speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth so as to make your soul sin; nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest perhaps the Lord, angered at your words, destroy all the works of your hands.” Most manifest testimonies of providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to the disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for an assarius? And one of them does not fall to the ground before God?” A great commendation of divine providence, which has care not only for men but also for tiny birds. Indeed, if he cares for such small things, who would not believe that he has care for great things? “Look,” he says, “at the birds of heaven: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them.” And a little later he adds, saying: “If God so clothes the grass of the field, which today is and tomorrow is cast into the oven, how much more you, of little faith?” [Almighty?] God, because he created all things and brought them into being when they did not exist, rules and governs the universe lest it fall and pass away; and this could in no way happen if he did not have providence and care for all things.
