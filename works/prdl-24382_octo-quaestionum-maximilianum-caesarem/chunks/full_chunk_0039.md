Therefore all and each of the lower things, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible, the providence of the divine majesty orders, rules, disposes, and cares for; his most powerful will produced all things from nothing, as Sacred Scripture again bears witness: “But your providence, Father, governs all things from the beginning.”

Therefore let men blush who are corrupt in mind, impious and reprobate with regard to the Christian faith, who, puffed up with empty loquacity, remove the providence of him who knows all things, and do not attend to how great an injury they do to God. For what, to use the judgment of Firmianus, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he provides for nothing, if he neglects lower things, if he does not care for mortal things, he has now lost all dignity. But it is impossible, as holy Anselm attests, for God to lose his honor; for, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, nor corruption, nor sin. He therefore who takes away providence takes away the whole substance of God. And what does he say, except that God does not exist?

But now let those who deny that the providence of God cares for lower things tell me: if God neglects lower things, let them attend to what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is henceforth no reason why we should either honor him with worship or venerate him with religion, since from him we obtain no benefit; rather, being neglected and despised, let us ourselves also neglect him. But if, not knowing us, he despises and wholly neglects us, there is now no reason for us to worship one who does not know us, and for that reason can confer no good, or to invoke him with prayers. For if God does not care for human affairs, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every reason for piety ceases, since there is no hope of salvation. What regard for piety can there be among mortals, what hope for the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human affairs? Indeed, if providence is taken away, whom do we worship, whom do we adore? If there is with God no providence for mortals, certainly he neither grants benefits to human beings nor imposes punishments on deserters; he can neither help mortals, nor wishes to, nor cares at all; he does not notice what we do, and he hears neither the prayers nor the supplications of anyone. Why do we offer him any worship; why honor, why prayers? Or why do we so greatly fear or dread a God who does not know us, who, with us neglected and despised, can neither do good to us nor harm?

For once providence is removed, neither holiness nor religion will remain; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disorder of life and a dreadful confusion will follow. Once piety toward God is removed, what will the commonwealth be except everlasting confusion? Public faith among human beings will necessarily perish, and the fellowship of the human race, and all justice together with it, will vanish and perish. What must be thought of the Christian faith if God does not care for human affairs? What, finally, of the Savior of all, our Lord Jesus Christ, except that he will be reckoned an empty fable, wholly fictitious, if there is no providence of God caring for mortal things? Therefore whatever good we do, whatever evil we suffer for the love of God, is wholly vain and useless to us if he has no care for us.

Let those who deny the providence of God now see and shudder, when they perceive that the end of their opinion is the worst despair of all. Therefore let no one, induced by the vain-speaking of secular philosophers, train himself to contempt of God; let no one take away the providence of the highest and unchangeable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of the benefits of the most merciful Savior and ungrateful.

For it must be firmly believed, and in no way doubted, that this whole universe, both visible and invisible, and all things individually, whether they are in heaven or on earth or in the sea, are ruled and governed by divine providence, and that absolutely nothing in the whole universe is alien to his care.
