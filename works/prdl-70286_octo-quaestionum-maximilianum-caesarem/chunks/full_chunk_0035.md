For nowhere do we read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast whatsoever, but always in male appearance. Yet demons have often been seen around rivers and springs in the appearance of women, sometimes dancing, sometimes letting down their hair in the manner of women, and sometimes indeed speaking with human beings and practicing various deceptions. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have dealings with witches.

The fifth kind is called subterranean, because it dwells in caves and caverns and in the remote hollows of mountains. And these demons are the worst in disposition, and they especially attack those who dig wells and mines and who seek treasures hidden in the earth, being most ready for the destruction of the human race. They make chasms in the earth, and [unclear] flame-spewing winds; suddenly they vanish at a sign and return to their hiding places. At times the sound of little bells is heard among them, and they sometimes falsely claim that they are spirits of men deceased from life. They seek nothing more than the fear and wonder of human beings. Hence we have found by experience that they sometimes led certain of the simpler sort of men into their mountain hiding places.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

They led them, showing astonishing spectacles to those who marveled, and they falsely claim to be friends of the living, as if the dwellings of the blessed were there. Among these demons also some are incubones of treasures which the avarice of mortals has hidden in the earth; and lest they come again into the use of men, they withdraw them, steal them, guard them, and sometimes transport them from place to place. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have any dealings with female witches.

The sixth kind of demons is called light-fleeing, because they especially dread and detest the light, since they never appear by day, nor can they assume a body in any way except by night. This is the business that walks about in darkness, an unsearchable and utterly dark kind of demon, agitated by cold passions; malicious, restless, and disturbed, which violently presses upon people it meets in the hours of night, and more often, God permitting, kills certain persons either by breath or by touch. This kind is not suited to witches, nor can it be driven by incantations, because it flees light and the voices of human beings, and at the same time every disturbance of the air. It is especially accustomed to attend upon the witchcraft of women, since it is more subtle than all the rest and of the most audacious rashness. Some of the terrestrial demons also, as has been said, serve witches, who nevertheless, being held in a lower rank, are by no means able to bring about those greater acts of witchcraft. It is therefore plainly evident that the whole power of witches, by which they do such wondrous things, is granted to demons under limitation by the Lord God; and when he does not permit it, they accomplish nothing.
