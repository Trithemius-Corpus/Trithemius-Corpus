*12. Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

...and those whom he foreknew would turn aside to evil, he would not bring forth, in so great a multitude, to destruction. For it would seem better not to be than to be badly.

To this question let him answer, if anyone thinks himself God’s counselor. Who am I, that I should expound the cause of the divine will? For all things whatsoever the Lord willed he did in heaven and on earth, and there is no one who can resist his will. Hence a certain wise man says to God: Who will charge it against you if the nations that you made have perished? The Almighty established for things an order of vicissitude, which he will never revoke by any fullness of change. For just as he permits his sun to rise upon the good and the wicked, so also until the end of the age he has caused human beings to be conceived, born, and to grow, both pious and impious. In God there are not the thoughts of human beings, nor does he change his counsel according to time; but since he is unchangeable and [unclear], willing all things together and at once and always, he has from eternity preordained them invariably. God speaks once, as sacred Scripture bears witness, and adds that whatever he wills, he wills immovably, and admits no change. Therefore that human beings should be born good and evil, pious and impious, faithful and faithless, sinners and saints, to be saved and to be damned, God permits by a most just reason, he who assigned this order to all things. Nothing perishes to God, who is Lord of all, and he everywhere holds firm dominion no less over wicked human beings and spirits than over good ones.
