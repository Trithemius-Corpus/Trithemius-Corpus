*To Maximilian Caesar.*

*On Faith Necessary for Salvation.*

*The Second Question.*

This was the second question of your unconquered Majesty: since only a small part of the world today is subject to the laws of the Christian faith, whether that opinion of very many persons seems admissible without injury to the truth, who think that each person, in the religion which he believes to be true and salutary, as a worshipper of the one God, can be saved outside the Christian faith and without Baptism, provided nothing is known concerning the religion of Christ.

I judged it superfluous to answer this question simply according to the common intention, since I know that today there are many among Christians whom an impious piety, for the salvation of lost men, makes cruel toward God. But because a reason of just consideration moved your most serene Highness to ask these things, not because you do not know what must be held in this Catholic proposition, but so that you might have something with which to set the truth against those who attack it, I shall answer the questions asked as briefly as I can. If anyone could be saved outside the Christian faith, Christ, the Son of almighty God, would seem to have been made man superfluously and without cause. For if there is salvation outside Christ, Christ is not the savior of all. But because it was necessary, according to the Scriptures, that the only-begotten Son of God, for the salvation of the human race, be born as a man in the world, suffer, die, and rise again on the third day, he did not come into this world in vain, nor superfluously, nor without cause. Since this is so, whoever asserts that anyone can be saved outside the faith of Christ shows himself to be not a Christian but an adversary of the faith and a deserter of Christ. For whoever does not believe in Christ, the Son of God, promises himself eternal salvation in vain. For he who is Truth, Jesus Christ, says in the holy Gospel: “He who has not believed will be condemned.” And again: “He who does not believe has already been judged, because he does not believe in the name of the only-begotten Son of God.” And again, a little later, he says, adding: “He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him.” Saint [Christophilus?] also says: “Without faith it is impossible to please God.”

The solution to the proposed question is now plain to all who truly believe in Christ: because salvation is through him, the Savior of the world, and whoever does not believe in him cannot be saved. Nor is it lawful for a Christian in any way to overstep the boundaries of his religion, so as to presume to correct this question otherwise than Christ taught. But whoever goes beyond this norm of faith by asserting the contrary plainly demonstrates that he does not think rightly concerning Christ, the savior of the faithful. For whoever promises salvation to anyone outside Christ wishes to declare Christ a liar. But we, confirmed by the most sacred ordinances of the Gospel, firmly believe and pronounce without doubt that no one can be saved outside the faith of Christ, however holy or justly he may live, however much he may trust in his paternal or another religion.
