The third kind, however, we call terrestrial demons, whom we do not at all doubt to have fallen from heaven to earth for their deserts. Concerning these the most holy bishop and martyr of Christ, Theophorus, once Ignatius, wrote thus in a certain letter to the Ephesians: “Nothing is better than to have peace, in which every weapon of the aerial and terrestrial spirits alike is made void.” Of these demons, some dwell in woods and groves, and lay snares for hunters. Others live in open fields, and make travelers wander at night; some abide in hidden places and caves; the rest, less furious and disturbed than the others, delight to dwell with human beings in darkness. They are not led by one disposition but by a varied one, since some are less depraved than others, although all are full of evil disturbances. Of these, some rejoice in terrifying human beings by illusions alone; others desire, by predictions of future events, to draw their hearers into admiration of themselves. Some, however, labor with the greatest effort to this end: that they may terrify, harm, or kill people disturbed by irrational madness or raging with melancholy, which we have recognized by experience to have happened rather often. Hence Saint John Chrysostom, in the third book *On Providence* to the monk Stagirius, says: “More powerful than every diabolical action for doing no harm is the greatness of sorrow, because the demon overcomes whomever he overcomes through sorrow; if you take this away, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon.” This kind of demons, however, is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy and lightness of its affections, whereby they rejoice in terrifying many rather than in being subject to the command of one little woman. Yet they sometimes dwell with, or make promises to, mad women, in glass or in crystal or in a mirror; and when stirred up by incantations they give replies to women, and if someone who is to come has cursed them on the road, they announce it beforehand. And this kind of witches differs in some respects greatly from the rest, since what the former make sick by witchcraft, these promise to cure. But because for the most part they repel witchcraft by witchcraft, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, since, immersing itself in liquids, it dwells about lakes and rivers, full of anger, disturbed, restless, and deceitful. In the sea it stirs up storms, and, sinking ships into the deep, takes life from many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, they more often appear in the female sex, and are more rarely seen in the male, because they live in damp places and follow a softer course of life; nor can they easily assume a base form by the custom of the others. Hence antiquity long ago named the Naiads and Nereids, nymphs of the waters, not in the male but in the female sex. By our people, however, they are called by the common term aquatic women, that is, *Wasser Frawen*. Those, however, who dwell in drier and rather arid places, whenever they appear bodily visible to human beings, transform themselves into men.
