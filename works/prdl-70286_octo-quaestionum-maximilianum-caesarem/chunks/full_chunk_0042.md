*To Maximilian Caesar.*

As for infants who, reborn in Christ, are sometimes killed by the evil arts of demons or of men, we do not doubt that divine providence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things for the better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish by evil arts because of the sins of their parents.

*On the Providence of God.*
*Question Eight.*

This was the eighth question of Your Serenity: whether it can be proved by natural reason, as also by the holy Scriptures, that God has care for human affairs and actions, and a certain and infallible providence over all and each of those things which are done in the world.

I remember that I answered this question as follows. The whole world, heaven and earth and all things that are in them, are subject to divine providence, by whose will and command they were brought forth into being from the beginning. For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us prove this opinion first, indeed, from the Scriptures, and afterward by arguments, as briefly as may be allowed. In the book of Judith we read: “All your ways are peaceful, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the most wise King Solomon speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth so as to cause your soul to sin, nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest perhaps the Lord, angered against your words, scatter all the works of your hands.” The clearest testimonies to providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to his disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for an as, and one of them does not fall upon the earth before God?” A great commendation of divine providence, which has care not only for men, but also for tiny birds. Indeed, if the Almighty cares for such small things, who would not believe that he has care for great things? “Look,” he says, “at the birds of heaven, which neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them.” And a little later he adds, saying: “If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is today and tomorrow is cast into the oven, how much more you, of little faith?”

Almighty God, because he created all things and brought them forth into being when they did not exist, rules and governs the universe, lest it slip away and flow into dissolution. This could in no way happen if he did not have providence and care for all things. For if providence is removed, it is necessary that the ordered continuity of the whole universe be thrown into confusion. But the providence of the Creator cares for all things, since he who feeds the birds and gives their food to the beasts of burden, and fills every living creature with blessing, and clothes the grass of the fields, adorning violets and lilies with beautiful comeliness, cannot forget man, whom he created from the beginning in his own image. He cares for all things, who governs all things; and he bears providence not only for man, but also for all beasts of burden, in whose dominion all things are placed, and there is no one who can resist his will in anything.

Let us now, therefore, consider this immense structure of the whole universe, how in its original arrangement and motion it always persists, ordered in the highest degree, and how that divine and unchangeable providence, unless we stray shamefully in mind, preserves, directs, and governs these things by the clearest reason [unclear].
