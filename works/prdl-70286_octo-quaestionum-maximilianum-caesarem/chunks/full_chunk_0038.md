For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who disdain to follow the saving teachings of Christ, deceived by demons, should all be plunged down to hell to be punished forever: they seek help from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have worshiped. For it is just that he who, abandoning the truth of his own free will, clings to falsehood should be cast headlong by the devil into destruction.

The eighth cause is the manifestation of divine power, whose mighty virtue is above every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits the devil great things and denies him certain small things, he makes manifest how weak the enemy of the human race is, subject in all things to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing of himself, but everything that he does, he does by God’s permission. Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God has permitted acts of witchcraft to be done, is justice, by which he preserves in inviolable order the freedom that he once bestowed upon the creature from the beginning. But since the power of every creature dependent on its creator is restricted and limited, it is clear that neither can the devil do everything he wills, nor can man, however depraved in will. The will indeed is free, but power is limited by certain necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wish, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would overthrow everything. Satan did not dare to tempt him further than had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if they could always use a free power to harm according to their own judgment. Before the Lord’s passion, however, they used a greater freedom of power than afterward. But when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, their power was thereafter greatly diminished, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of the world will be cast out.” For the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints greatly restrained the audacity of demons, so that they can rightly be called instigators of evils, but by no means drivers. Nor would the Devil prevail against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you”; again, “Do not give place to the devil.” Therefore, in concluding the discourse for the understanding of the proposed question, why God permits so many acts of witchcraft to be done, I believe the reasons have been sufficiently made clear. For although the will of demons is always wicked, nevertheless their power can never be unjust, since what they wickedly will they have from their own choice, but what they can do wickedly they have only from God. And these things have been said as far as concerns those who do evil.

But why does almighty God permit demons to injure, through the agency of witches, human beings who have been baptized in the name of the holy Trinity and faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ?
