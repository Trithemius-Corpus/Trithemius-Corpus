Therefore let no one, led by the vain talk of secular philosophers, train himself to contempt of God; let no one take away the providence of the supreme and unchangeable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of, and ungrateful for, the benefits of the most merciful Savior.

For it must be firmly believed, and in no way doubted, that this whole world, both visible and invisible, and all and each of the things that are either in heaven or on earth and in the sea, are ruled and governed by divine providence, and that absolutely nothing in the whole universe is foreign to his care. For the crops and all the other things that the earth brings forth, and also the storms and the variations of times and the changes of the sky, by which all things that the earth naturally produces grow to maturity, divine providence supplies to the human race; and it provides for all things and each thing, not only the great, but even the very least, to such a degree that not even the slightest leaf of a tree falls to the ground without the will of almighty God the Father, who created it and rules and cares for it. But if anyone thinks that this so great magnitude of the whole universe cannot be ruled and governed by the one God, who alone exists, he is greatly deceived, and is shown not to know how great is the power of divine might. For indeed, he who was able to bring the whole world from nothing into being can also govern forever, most wisely, what he made. For he knows all things, understands all things, can do all things, who alone, omnipotent, created all things.

We therefore, believing the holy Gospel, should above all venerate divine providence, and should also lift up our minds, with whatever strength we can, from those things that are perishable; and, brought back to ourselves, we should be united, so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God, present everywhere. Since it is altogether uncircumscribable, it holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, surpasses all things, and sustains all things. Yet it does not sustain on one side and surpass on another, nor fill on one side and encompass on another; rather, by encompassing it fills, and by filling it encompasses; by sustaining it surpasses, and by surpassing it sustains all things. He is nowhere through the bulk of a body, nowhere absent through his uncircumscribable substance; he is not seen by the eyes of flesh, but is seen through faith in purity of heart. O inexplicable power, O incomprehensible loving-kindness, O ineffable wisdom, O uncircumscribable divinity, which does not consist of parts like a body, is not separated by affections like a soul, does not underlie forms like all other things that were made through you, who are in all things, above all things, ruling and governing the universe most wisely.

By these testimonies, therefore, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, it has been sufficiently proved, as I think, that this whole world is governed by the providence of God. Since he is pure intellect, simple, uncreated, and everlasting, he knows all things, understands all things, foreknows all things; and since he is of an omnipotent nature, being all-powerful, he can do all things. Nothing is difficult for him, nothing impossible; he created such wondrous things by his power, established them in truth, perfected them by reason, sustains them by virtue, illumines them by goodness. To him be praise, to him honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, God glorious and exalted, forever and ever. Amen.

*The Author’s Protestation to the Emperor.*

These are the things, most unconquered Caesar Maximilian, that seemed to me necessary to say in response to the eight questions proposed by your majesty. For you wished to have my judgment too, in writing, concerning them, making an estimate of me beyond the truth; for I am not to be compared in talent with those whose opinion you heard before me. Let pardon make me worthy, if I have said anything less appropriate, because, a traveler set upon the road, at the command of your loftiness, I dictated, as I could, at intervals, whatever occurred to me that should be answered. But I say from my heart and openly declare that I, a sincere confessor of the Christian faith, wish to remain in union with the most holy Roman Church until death.
