There was a certain swineherd who had enclosed in his staff a little slip containing the name of the holy martyr Blasius; he ascribed so much trust to it that, when the staff was fixed in the ground in the midst of the herd, he believed every wolf was kept from coming near. When this foolish confidence had at last been strengthened by long experience, the man went safely wherever he wished, leaving the pigs in the wood without danger, since no wolf now had any access. It happened that one passing by saw this in the shepherd’s absence; and, observing that demons were keeping guard over the herd, he said to one of them: “What are you doing here, wretch?” He answered: “I am guarding these pigs.” But the other said: “Who entrusted you with the care of the pigs?” Again he answered: “The shepherd’s foolish confidence. For, thinking a certain slip enclosed in a staff to be of great power, by which through the merits of some Blasius wolves are kept from the gates, he has sinned against his own law and follows our vanities. But since the one invoked does not appear, I, lest the man lose his confidence, guard his pigs, and willingly put myself in the place of Blasius.” Thus, he says, mortals trouble us with vain conjurations, so that, unless we wish to lose all esteem and honor for ourselves among them, it often becomes necessary for us to answer their foolish invocations. By the likeness of this event demons deceive wretched unbelievers: the miracles which they ask of God in prayer they very often obtain through the substitution of malignant spirits. For in those things which concern either the worship or the honor of God, demons readily insert themselves; since they cannot obtain open honors, they plainly take delight in hidden ones.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

At the same time it must be noted that, from the beginning of the world, miracles have never been lacking to any religion or sect of principal excellence, although not all have been done from one principle. For whatever miracles have been done in the true religion have been done from no other source than from God. The law of nature had its miracles; the written law had them; grace now has them likewise. Almighty God, who gave religion, himself also works miracles. But in false religions, where truth is not worshiped, if any miracles are done, not God but the devil works them. For since God is truth, he did not institute false religions, and therefore he has never adorned a lie with his miracles. Muhammad, who was full of an evil demon and instituted a false religion, adorned it with false miracles not from God, but by the cooperation of demons. For the demon who gave the cause of error could also, when the occasion had been accepted, display a false miracle to the deceived. Hence, if miracles had been wholly lacking to Muhammad when he was persuading a rude people of a new religion, perhaps no one would have given him credence. For he too showed the peoples certain marvels, both by subtlety of intellect and by the cooperation of the devil, so that he might seem to have confirmed a new doctrine with new miracles as well. Miracles were not lacking even to the pagan idolaters, if we do not deny credence to the histories. The Jews, however, before the incarnation of the Son of God, receiving his law through Moses, were frequently illumined by divine miracles. But after the Lord’s resurrection, when the Mosaic law had been emptied of force, those who do not believe in the only-begotten Son of God remain under the wrath of his indignation, and all miracles of truth have ceased among them.
