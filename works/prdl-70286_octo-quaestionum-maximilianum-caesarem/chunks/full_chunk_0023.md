Just as, if you show to a boy learning the first elements of letters any written volume whose character is unlike that in his own little book, when he cannot read the writing he will at once cry out that it is bad and not properly formed. In the same way, men who approach the reading of the divine Scriptures, wise only in a fleshly manner, and who do not grasp those things that are of the divine Spirit, burdened by vices and rashness, when they ought to have blamed their own ignorance and unworthiness, reproach as holy the Scriptures which they do not understand. For it is not lawful for everyone everywhere to interpret the divine Scriptures, lest the beast, touching the mountain, be overwhelmed with stones.

Many now arise in a reversed order, who have spent the whole time of their life on humane letters; having become older men, they wrongly interpret the divine Scriptures, which they have never learned, and do not explain them but confuse them, examining them according to their own sense. Hence all who do not understand the sacred Scriptures by that Spirit by which they were breathed into those who wrote them have over their hearts the veil of ignorance and rashness, spread out after the manner of the Jews; from there they very often drink the poison of destructive error, from which those who approach humbly in the Spirit of God have received the cup of salvation.

For heavenly doctrine is not contained in the words of the Scriptures, but in their meaning; not on the surface of the words, but in the understanding; not in the leaves of ornate speech, but in the hidden place of mystical inspiration. Hence the divine Pope Gregory says: Sacred Scripture far surpasses all the sciences of this world in the manner of its speech, since in one and the same discourse, while it recounts the text, it reveals a hidden mystery; just as it refreshes the simple on the surface, so it exercises the wise with mystery. For it has in public that from which it may nourish little ones; it preserves in secret that by which it may suspend the minds of the lofty in wonder at its sweetness, like a certain marvelous river in which both the simple little lamb may walk and the great elephant may swim.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

And St. Augustine says on Genesis: Divine Scripture speaks in such a way that by its height it mocks the proud, by its depth it terrifies the attentive, by its strength it feeds the great, and by its affability it nourishes little ones. For the authority of sacred Scripture is greater than all the perspicacity of human talent. Therefore, in order that one may come to their true understanding, above all the mind must be cleansed of every carnal affection, and the aid of divine goodness must be diligently implored with humility, so that by his Spirit he may illumine the understanding of the learner.

For all necks of powers must be subjected to the authority of the divine Scriptures. And, as the divine Jerome says, one must never flatter a man in such a way that the truth of the holy Scriptures is neglected; in the whole of both Testaments it is true in its narration of deeds, right in its admonitions, certain in its promises, and terrifyingly to be feared in its threats. Therefore it has not been given to all to be able to penetrate the mysteries of the divine Scriptures, but it is permitted in some measure only to those in whom love makes study continuous, and to whose purified minds the Spirit of God grants true understanding.

But whoever is foul with the darkness of vices, whoever walks with a swollen mind, proud; whoever doubts the truth of the Scriptures of God, whether in part or in whole, let him never trust that he can attain to their understanding. For the authority of Scripture itself says that unless you have believed, you will not understand.
