*To Maximilian Caesar.*

... for those who minister worthily, with him assisting, they signify and produce a saving effect, he who instituted them working salvation. Thus the devil, who desires nothing more than to be thought equal to God, has instituted certain signs as if they were sacraments; when he is invoked by those with whom he has entered into covenants by pacts, the malignant agent assists and, as often as God has permitted it, brings about the evil that is asked; if he has not permitted it, he does absolutely nothing. For the devil is the agent of all the necromantic effects of witches; by applying the means that he himself first instituted, he produces the desired work upon the subject matter, sometimes in reality, sometimes only in appearance.

But women witches, who, with their will now depraved, have passed by consent into the fellowship of demons through pacts, receive from them certain symbolic marks; when summoned by these, the demons immediately appear. In these pernicious operations of demons nothing is stronger than the foolish credulity of witches, which is increased by pacts and vows; when this has preoccupied the mind, the more firmly they have put faith at once in the demons’ signs, the more swiftly, with those demons cooperating, they obtain the operation. For the excessively savage depravity of the witches’ will in a certain way transforms them into [unclear], and when the mind permits this, they serve those who are raving. Plato related something similar in the book *On the Poetic*, saying: just as bacchant women, with unsound mind, are not able to draw [unclear]. Their spirit, carried outside itself into the fellowship of an evil demon, brings this about.

We have ascertained that witches, in times of swollen rivers, have drawn out butter, which can in no way happen otherwise than by the ministry of a demon. In like manner, when they have been brought into hatred of some man or beast and desire his destruction, they are driven by the gravest fury, thinking of nothing in their mind except vengeance. They take things familiar to them, known for witchcraft; they invoke demons; they show the signs instituted by them, together with characters; what they cannot accomplish by their own power, they achieve by the ministry of those demons.

But another person, who is not subjected to demons by a pact of surrender, even if he knows the things suitable for witchcraft and likewise the manner of working, although he displays the signs, rarely obtains the effect, because he is not counted among the familiars of the demons. For the devil is so lifted up with pride that he emulates the Church of Christ: just as one who has not been ordained cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he may utter the words of consecration, so also the demon, however much signs or characters are shown to him by one who invokes him, if he does not know that person to be subject to himself by pact, either never or rarely cooperates. For if he came everywhere to all who invoked him, without pacts having been interposed, he would not possess so many witches dedicated to him by right of ownership.

Hence it is that many who are given over to profane arts, laboring with dreadful conjurations to summon demons to a circle, to a mirror, or to any other receptacles, obtain no effect, because either the command of God stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man not dedicated to him by homage. For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is clear that none is perpetrated: a witch raging in spirit and depraved in will; also the cooperation of a familiar devil; [unclear].
