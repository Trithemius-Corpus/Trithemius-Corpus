*To Emperor Maximilian.*

The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men. Patience is easy in prosperity, when no adversity strikes the mind. But many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the guarding of humility, so that the faithful and righteous man may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or righteousness. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: “They are not in the labor of men, and with men they shall not be scourged.” Therefore pride held them; they were covered with their iniquity and impiety; their iniquity comes forth as though from fat. It belongs to God’s mercy that he sometimes permits good and righteous men to be shaken by adversities, whom he knows cannot be humbled in prosperity. For tribulation has made many humble whom favorable circumstances would have made proud. Finally, the fearful imagining of evildoers makes the good man fear his God and diligently invoke his help, strengthened by which he may trust that he is safe against the assaults of demons. But let no proud man, presuming on his own powers, think himself safe from the harmful arts of demons, since the man whom, swollen with pride, God’s protection abandons, without doubt lies open to the harmful arts of demons.

The fourth reason is that there may be a distinction between the good and the wicked. For Scripture says of good men, beloved by God, in the Acts of the Apostles, that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. And Saint Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: “All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution.” The most wise King Solomon also says in Proverbs: “The Lord corrects the one whom he loves, and as a father in the son [unclear].”

For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and righteous men, not for their confusion, as the petitioners suppose, but rather for the greatest and sure consolation of the soul. Indeed, when a righteous man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeping his commandments with all care, and nevertheless is troubled by evildoers, whether in body or in possessions or in both, that man is truly blessed, because when he has been tested he will receive the crown of life, which God promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the Catholic faith of Christians and patiently endures the evils he receives in this world for the love of God has a sure sign that he has persevered in the grace of God to be saved. On the contrary, we read this written of the wicked and reprobate: “Why do the impious live, exalted and strengthened by riches? Their houses are secure and at peace, and the rod of God is not upon them; they pass their days in good things, and in a moment descend to hell.” For it is a manifest sign of damnation if here you live wickedly in sins and vices, opposed to God, and prospered in the goods of fortune and the health of the body.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and righteous men to be troubled by the wicked and reprobate is for the complete purification of the mind. For, as Scripture says, there is no righteous man on earth who does good and does not sin; but since God lets no sin pass unpunished, through mercy he ordains temporal punishments for righteous men for their offenses, but through justice eternal punishments for the impious and reprobate.
