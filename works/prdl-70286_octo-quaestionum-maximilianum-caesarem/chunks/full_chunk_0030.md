*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

Demons obey, but only those with whom they are allied by pacts and acts of service; and when invoked by those whom they cannot otherwise keep faithful, they gladly come, although they falsely claim that they are compelled. It is therefore no wonder, most excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to obey depraved and perverse men, whom, as we have said, both likeness in malice and the destruction of the human race has made to be in agreement. But far be it from every Christian to wish, through a depraved likeness of will, to command demons. For if anyone in this life has begun to have fellowship with malignant spirits, in the future he will burn with them without end.

But why good and righteous men cannot command the holy angels of God, though they have passed into their likeness of good will, I think the solution of this question would easily be given from what has been said above, if the difference of intelligences is recalled to mind. For although in the Church of God there are many men established whose righteousness benefits many unto salvation, yet in our times very few make such progress that, by reason of the merit of holiness, they are either feared by demons or received by angels into the fellowship of familiarity. Hence it comes about that men of our age neither command malignant spirits by merit of holiness nor deserve to be familiar with the holy angels. But if any have advanced to that degree of holiness by which, working miracles in the Church, they have advanced as the saints once did, then they can both powerfully command demons and have the familiar fellowship of blessed spirits.
