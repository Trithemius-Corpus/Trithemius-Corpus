When a beast approaches this holy mountain, it is overwhelmed with stones, because a man impure in mind, or weak in regard to the faith, who strives to search out the height of the divine Scriptures without a teacher handing it down, is wounded by various and pernicious errors as by blows of stones. For just as all things are clean to the clean, so all things seem profane to the profane. Therefore God placed his hiding-place in the height of the Scriptures, which is so inaccessible and closed to the reprobate that nevertheless it always lies manifestly open to the saints.

But why almighty God did not give Holy Scripture as sufficient and manifest for all things that concern the Christian faith, we answer briefly. There are two things that the Most High provided for believers for the confirmation of the Christian faith, namely the Church and Scripture, so that what is declared in one may be supplied in the other. Hence if we find anything in the Holy Scriptures concerning the rules of faith expressed less sufficiently, we at once have recourse to the Church; and where the Church, in saving instruction, has need of help for piety, it immediately betakes itself to Scripture. Nor would the Church have authority without Scripture, nor Scripture faith without the Church, so that from the two there may be one judgment. For Augustine says: I would not believe the Gospel unless the authority of the Church compelled me. But Johannes Gersonensis says: I would not believe the Church unless the Gospel compelled me. The Church confirms Holy Scripture, and it likewise is confirmed by Scripture. The Church abounds whenever Scripture is lacking. And if at any time the Church is in doubt, it has recourse to the counsel of Scripture. For there is one Holy Spirit of God, who founded the Church in the faith of Jesus Christ, the very same almighty one who alone inspired Scripture. And this is the threefold cord, which is not easily broken, where divine Scripture is joined by the Holy Spirit to the authority of the Church to such a degree that the Church commends Scripture, and Scripture in turn commends the Church.

Thus, as it seems to me, O Caesar of unconquered majesty, we have assigned a sufficient reason for the question proposed. For if all things that concern the piety of faith were manifestly expressed in the Holy Scriptures, holy mother Church would not appear to be of such great authority, and the merit of saving obedience would in large part be emptied away. At the same time I think a sufficient answer has been given to the principal question, where certain people suppose Sacred Scripture in very many places to be confused and imperfect; yet if they read it with due purity of mind, they would indeed find it perfect and solid. Great is the authority of the Catholic Church, which alone, in doubtful matters concerning the faith, has the right to interpret the Scriptures; to it the necks of all powers must be subjected. If anyone by rash daring departs from its definition, he has denied the Gospel of Christ. There is one congregation of the faithful, the mother of Christians, the holy Church; outside its unity no one will be saved. And although in morals it may sometimes seem weak, nevertheless in faith it has always remained constant and has never erred, by the preserving mercy of Christ.
