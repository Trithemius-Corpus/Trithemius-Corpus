*To Maximilian Caesar.*

The fourth worker of miracles, however, is man, who works marvelous things in four ways, which to those who know neither their cause nor their manner do not seem human.

And first, by invocation of the divine name, when, in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the merit of accepted holiness is joined with it. In this way the holy apostles and their imitators, by the power of God and at the same time by the merit of holiness in the unity of faith, performed miracles. But unbelievers cannot perform miracles in this way, because they do not walk the necessary path of faith unto salvation. For although many of them believe in one Lord, yet because they refuse to believe in the mediator between God and men, they have gone very far away from God’s mercy. But, as the holy Gospel promises, all things are possible to those who believe in God and our Lord Jesus Christ: namely, to those who adorn right faith also with holy works. For just as without faith it is impossible to please God, so also faith without works is dead. Almighty God would grant us all things that we ask in the faith of Christ, if we did all things according to the faith as we ought. Believe firmly and work faithfully, and you will always obtain all things.

Secondly, man performs miracles by likeness to angelic purity. For the purer our mind, confirmed in the faith of Jesus Christ through love, has become, the more, having been made like the holy angels, it attains their greater familiarity. For whoever has altogether attained the familiarity of the holy angels, when he wishes, by God’s bounty, he will shine forth in miracles. For the holy angels reveal to pure men, fervent in the love of God, secrets hidden from others, and by the Creator’s command they make many things manifest to him. For the Lord is near to those who call upon him in truth. The Lord will do the will of those who fear him, and he will hear their prayers and save them. For whoever has been united with Christ through grace is refreshed by the frequent visitation of angels, and often, when necessity demands it, is illumined by their ministry, and often also by their powers. Hence it is that even those weighed down by various illnesses betake themselves to the intercessions of the saints and, recalling the Lord’s promises to a friend, obtain by the merits of others what they cannot by their own; their holy devotion in the faith of Christ receives health from the Lord, when, aided both by the purity of its own conscience and supported by the merit of another’s holiness, it rises not rashly into certain hope.

In the Church of God, however, miracles are performed by divine power with a threefold consideration or means. For sometimes almighty God works a miracle by the ministry of angels, solely through the merits of the one working it, as Saint Martin healed a blind man and a lame man against their will. Sometimes miracles are performed through the merit of the sufferers alone, as we read that many were cured by bad Christians, because the sick believed them to be excellent in the faith. Thirdly, there is no doubt that healings of this kind are performed through the merit of both, namely of the sufferer and the worker together, as Christ says to someone in the holy Gospel: “If you can believe, all things are possible to one who believes.” For faith is required for a miracle to such an extent that our Lord Jesus Christ, coming into his own country, wondered that he could not do any mighty work there because of their unbelief. But none of these things is done among unbelievers, where neither holiness is found in the worker nor orthodox faith in the sufferer.
