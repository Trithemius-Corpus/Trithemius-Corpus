Demons of this kind, indeed, more fastened to the earth and those who move about among human beings, are very restless; the ancients called them [Bacuaeos?]. They terrify people at night, and seek nothing more from mortals than the esteem of divinity. Hence, in the presence of those with whom they are permitted to be by reason of likeness, they sometimes perform various mockeries, and, as chance allows, present themselves in minds as terrifying. As often as they can, they gladly assume the likeness of a human body, but they do not always find suitable material of air with which to be clothed; for this reason they rather often appear monstrous and horrible, because they have not had suitable [unclear] or vapors for the perfection of the appearance desired. Moreover, the more they are fastened to earthly dwellings, the more by chance they have become dull with respect to the material of their making. Hence, because of the ignorance which they contracted through disobedience, the predictions of demons are frequently false, although by divine permission they are sometimes even verified. For, as that most holy John Chrysostom says, it has been granted to the devil sometimes to speak truth, so that he may commend his lie by a rare truth. For if he never spoke truth, he could deceive no one, and his seduction would by no means suffice for tempting. It is therefore no wonder if demons sometimes seem to foretell true things to unbelieving people, since they once were able to present prophecies in the idols of the gentiles. Nor is it any wonder if they sometimes provide care to the sick, since they know the natures of diseases, and can remove diseases which they had previously brought on, or withdraw for a time from the senses those which chance has inflicted.

But almighty God, by the right of his sovereign majesty, uses the ministry of all beings, even demons, both for the testing of the faithful and for the punishment of the impious. Since they are perverse in affection, obstinate in their rebellion, and proud, they especially desire divine estimation and honors; and for that reason they gladly come to the invocations of unbelievers, which they are accustomed to pour forth to their gods, as though they themselves were gods. Among unbelievers they sometimes seem to raise the dead back to life, and they show marvelous things to curious people, so that by what are like miracles they render them, confirmed in their former error, stubborn against the Lord. Yet they do not truly raise the dead, but deceive human senses in various ways, since either they themselves enter the corpses of the dead and carry them around there for a time as though alive, or, after those bodies have been removed to a more distant place, they present to the spectators for viewing some image in the semblance of the dead person’s life. Just as the experience of certain people has informed us, we believe it evident that demons can show in a certain false likeness all the things whatever that the saints do as miracles in truth. All the miracles of demons are either phantasms or things done by a certain natural skill. For if people experienced in the more hidden mysteries of nature, by some combination of matter concealed from others, bring forth unusual and marvelous effects into form, just as [unclear] from the dew of heaven, bees from the flesh of [unclear], serpents from certain roots of herbs, and many other things of this kind, if I do not wish to mention greater ones, as proof in many cases has made you experienced, who would deny that demons, of a subtler nature and greater experience, can work naturally more marvelous things which are manifest to no human beings?
