*To Emperor Maximilian*

I cannot deny that [they were freed?] from a spasm. Likewise, I know that someone opened [locks?] by words and very powerful herbs; this I think was done neither by the power of God nor by the force of the things applied. I could bring forward countless similar things that I have seen and heard, whose effects, if they are manifest, are surely nothing other than certain miracles of demons. For some men, excessively curious, stubbornly placing faith and trust in characters, slips of paper, rings, figures, necklaces, and unknown words, often obtain astonishing effects; these, we do not at all doubt, the devil has supplied, not by merit of holiness nor granted by God’s mercy, but rather through treacherous credulity. For demons, having the greatest envy of the human race, readily come to meet the vain operations of the impious, in order to destroy the souls of mortals. What else are characters and interpretations of hidden names than certain foods and drinks of demons? Whoever places faith in characters promising marvelous effects has implicitly denied both the faith of Christ and the Church of the saints. Alas, how many Christians today, how many clerics, how many priests, to spare greater men, use many and various superstitions in their daily actions, by which they follow the entangled passions of demons, to the disgrace of the Christian faith and to their own condemnation. And because these evils have now multiplied too greatly among Christians, the Almighty has withdrawn his hand and handed them over into the power of destroyers, so that they may feel as exactors of punishments those whom, by imitation, they will stubbornly have served, not the living and true God, but vain superstitions.

*3: The Eight Questions of Johannes Trithemius*

By the substitution of demons, moreover, we know plainly, if we inquire more diligently into the truth, that astonishing things are done. For the Jews, Saracens, pagans, or gentiles, who do not believe in God our Lord Jesus Christ, if they perform any miracles, we believe that these are done not by the power of God, not by the ministry of angels, not by merit of holiness, but by the substitution of demons. For whenever they invoke almighty God, in whose only-begotten Son they do not believe, for mercy in regard to some marvelous effect, then the devil, coming in, who is not false to sinners, and because the Most High does not hear unbelieving sinners, in order that they may persist in errors, secretly applies himself in place of the God invoked and, if he can, the Lord permitting, grants to [their] prayers what is requested. The devil uses this substitution the more freely, the less he is hindered by the faith of Christ and the sacraments of the Church. For the wrath of God remains upon all unbelievers who do not believe in his only-begotten Son, and [he] was in the power of the devil. And therefore he enters, for those invoking him, in the place of God. Hence, blinded in their errors, while the innocent God, in whose Son they do not believe, fails to hear those remaining in wrath, the devil comes as a false worker of miracles. Therefore those who scorn believing the true miracles of God believe the deceits of the devil. They receive from demons false miracles suited to their errors, those who, by believing, have not chosen to follow the truth, that is, Christ. There have been many among the unbelievers who, worshiping the one God, creator of all things, do not adore idols and firmly believe that they will be saved in their own faith. But because they fail to believe the mystery of the Lord’s incarnation, the wrath of God remains upon them; and in their follies they vanish away, thinking that God is near, in whom they do not rightly believe; [they are] subjected to the demon.
