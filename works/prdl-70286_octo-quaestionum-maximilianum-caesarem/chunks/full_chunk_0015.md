Indeed, since they are obstinate in the error of false religion, if in their own estimation they have obtained anything miraculous, there is no doubt whatever that it is done through the ministry of demons. For since they are wholly unlike the good angels, they obtain no familiarity with them. For no despiser of the Christian faith can please God; but whoever is proved displeasing to almighty God is without doubt deprived of the familiarity of the holy angels. Yet by this we do not withdraw from anyone every good spirit assigned to him, by whose ministry, even if he is unbelieving toward the Son of God, he may receive the benefits of nature, although he does not attain to the gifts of grace. But upon those who believe in our Lord Christ they confer not only the benefits of nature, but also minister the gifts of grace. It is therefore manifest that, if any miracles are done among unbelievers, they cannot be done by the gift of God or by the ministry of the holy angels, since all who are placed outside the unity of the Church cannot work signs by public justice when they are impious. For God, as has been said, does not remove his original wrath from man except through faith in Jesus Christ; but everyone who is unbelieving toward the Son of God will not see eternal life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. Therefore everyone upon whom the wrath of God remains will not be able to please God; and so the wonders that are done at his prayers should be believed to be performed not by the power of God nor by the ministry of angels, but rather by the intervention of demons. The third worker of wonders is the devil, who, like an ape, tries to imitate whatever he sees, and deceives the minds of men, especially unbelievers, with various illusions,

and seduces those who believe him by a certain likeness of miracles. For I do not judge it difficult for demons to be able to delude the senses of men, since it is agreed that by nature they were created more subtle than all mortals. But they cannot know the thoughts of mortals except by a certain conjecture, since of course by nature we make our inward matters manifest by certain signs. Demons foretell future things, and sometimes speak the truth, sometimes are deceived in conjecturing and deceive. From the beginning of the world they have known certain signs antecedent to events; by experience and use of these they sometimes foretell truly what is to come. And when they have seen signs known to them that precede an event, they immediately conjecture what is in a man’s thought; yet very often they deceive, because they themselves are deceived. For when they are asked about some matter which they do not know, as, for example, whether a messenger sent far away is going to return soon, or on what day, they immediately, in the blink of an eye, reach the place where they know him to be. When they have seen that he has already begun the journey to return, they carefully count the days, and, having suddenly returned to the one who asked, confidently affirm that the absent man will come on a fixed day. But if he has delayed on the road or has turned aside elsewhere, then certainly the demons, already deceived in their judgment, must be mistaken. They gladly insert themselves into prophecies, and are ready enough to foretell future things to men for the sake of the honor and esteem which they most greatly desire to have, as being worthy of admiration. They seek divine honors, and greatly long to be feared by mortals.
