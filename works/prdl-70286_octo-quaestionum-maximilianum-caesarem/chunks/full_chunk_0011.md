…who has affirmed that a man in any sect outside the faith of Jesus Christ, or has believed it in his heart, is not a good Christian, but rather an apostate and a disciple of Antichrist; and unless he comes to his senses, let him know that he will be tormented forever in the lower world with him. For he is Antichrist, not a Christian, who says that one who does not believe in the Lord Jesus is to be saved without the Savior. Therefore, most wise Caesar, let no one appear in the sight of Your Majesty, let no one perceive you as adhering to him, who thinks that men outside the faith of Jesus Christ are to be saved. For everyone who has believed that anyone is to be saved outside the faith of Christ has denied both Christ and his Gospel. But that we say that all who are not Christians are to be condemned is an indication not of cruelty but of truthful piety. For it is better to believe firmly in the truth than falsely to agree with a man. That all Jews, pagans, gentiles, Saracens, schismatics, heretics, and unbelievers are without doubt to be condemned with the demons is attested by the Church of the saints, which took and confirmed this judgment from nowhere other than from the words of Christ. For thus he said: He who believes in the Son has eternal life; by this are designated Christians who are good in faith and work. But he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. Here are denoted all Jews, pagans, Saracens, gentiles, and whoever do not believe in our Lord Jesus Christ. Concerning schismatics and heretics, however, the judgment of Christ is clear, when he says in the Gospel: Whoever refuses to hear the Church, let him be to you as a heathen and a publican. Heathens are gentiles, and publicans are manifest and public sinners; concerning their condemnation, most certain among the saints, the Church does not doubt. For who among Christians would have doubted that gentiles and enormous sinners, manifest and public, are to be condemned? Why then do schismatics

and heretics, all of whom despise the Church of the saints, being most rightly said on account of their offenses to be like the gentiles and publicans who are to be condemned for their crimes, justly undergo punishments of eternal damnation equal with them? Behold, those whom the just sentence of Christ the Savior condemned, the foolish compassion of men, not to say impious cruelty, presumes are to be saved. They desire to be merciful toward lost men; they do not fear to show themselves cruel toward God, whom he through justice assigned to death: these men, with the worst presumption, by recalling them assert that they are to be brought to life. It is neither good nor useful for a Christian, whether seriously or in jest, to contradict Christ; he is impious, not a Christian, whoever says that unbelievers are to be saved. When Adam, the first man, perished, we all sinned; the wrath of God remained upon the whole human race from the beginning of the world until Christ. Christ came, born of a virgin, who, God and man, innocent of sin, voluntarily underwent the death of the cross; having been made mediator between God and man, he removed the wrath due to original guilt from those who believe in him. For thus he says: He who believes in the only-begotten Son of God has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. Indeed, without faith in Jesus Christ no one will be saved, however much he may seem to live justly and holily in his own religion. For almighty God can save a believer without material baptism, but he cannot make a denier saved without faith. Let no one be saved unwillingly, because no one believes except willingly; for God knows who are his, since those who believe in the Son he has through repentance, but unbelievers he condemns through the equity of justice.
