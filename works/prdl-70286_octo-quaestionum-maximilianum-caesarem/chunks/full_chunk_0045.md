For Scripture says: “Men and beasts you will save, O Lord.” And in another place a certain wise man said: “The Lord made the small and the great, and he has care equally for all.” Therefore the providence of the divine majesty orders, rules, disposes, and cares for all and each of the lower things, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible; its most powerful will produces all things from nothing, as sacred Scripture again declares: “But your providence, Father, governs all things from the beginning.” Therefore let men corrupted in mind, impious and reprobate concerning the Christian faith, blush: men who, puffed up with vain talkativeness, take away the providence of him who knows all things, and do not consider how great an injury they do to God. For what, to use the judgment of Firmianus, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he foresees nothing, if he neglects lower things, if he does not care for mortal things, he has already lost all dignity. But it is impossible, as holy Anselm attests, for God to lose his honor, since, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, nor corruption, nor sin. Therefore he who removes providence removes the whole substance of God; and what does he say except that God does not exist?

But now let those who deny that God’s providence cares for lower things tell me. If God neglects lower things, let them consider what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is thereafter no reason why we should pursue him with worship or venerate him with religious devotion as a benefactor; indeed rather, being neglected and despised, let us ourselves neglect him too. But if, not knowing, he despises and neglects us, there is now utterly no reason why we should worship one who does not know us and for that reason is unable to confer any good, or invoke him with prayers.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

For if God does not care for human affairs, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every cause of piety ceases, since there is no hope of salvation. What consideration of piety can there be among mortals, what hope of the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human affairs? Indeed, if providence is removed, whom do we worship, whom do we adore? If there is no providence for mortals with God, certainly he neither bestows benefits on human beings nor imposes punishments on deserters, nor can or will he help mortals, nor does he care at all, nor notice what we do, and he hears neither the entreaties nor the prayers of anyone. But why do we offer him any worship, why honor, why prayers, or why do we so greatly fear or dread a God who does not know us, who, with us neglected and despised, can do us neither good nor ill? For with providence removed, neither holiness nor religion will remain; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disturbance of life and a dreadful confusion will follow. With piety toward God removed, what will the commonwealth then be, except everlasting confusion? Public trust among human beings will necessarily perish, and the fellowship of the human race, and all justice will vanish and perish at once. What is to be thought of the Christian faith, if God does not care for human affairs? What, finally, of the Savior of all, our Lord Jesus Christ, except that the whole will be regarded as an empty fable and a fiction, if there is no providence of God caring for mortal things? Surely whatever good we do in this life, whatever evil we endure for the love of God, is wholly vain and useless to us, if he has no care for us at all.
