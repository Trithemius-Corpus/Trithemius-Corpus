For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not meet the wicked woman’s will, she would perpetrate no evil deed. Again, if the witch is lacking, the devil will in no way consummate the evil deed. For the devil uses the will of the raving witch, like a craftsman using an instrument for working, without which he cannot inflict the evil deed. And just as demons differ from one another in power and in keenness of subtlety, so the power of witches is not one and the same in all, but is diverse in each individual, according as demons of a subtler or grosser nature concur with them. For there are many kinds of demons, and they are separated from one another by fixed degrees, by reason of the places into which, when they fell at the beginning, they were thrust down.

The first kind of demons is called fiery; it wanders about the upper air and is never cast down to the lower regions before the day of judgment, but, remaining continually beneath the lunar regions, has no intercourse on earth with human beings. That it remains beneath the moon, Saint Augustine bears witness in the work *On the Christian Combat*, saying thus: demons do not dwell in heaven, where the sun and stars are, but all move about beneath the lunar region. Moved by this, as I think, Apuleius said that demons are fiery animals, because Aristotle confirmed that in burning furnaces certain small animals, furnished with little wings, have rather often been seen flying about; they spend their whole life in fire, since they are born with fire and are extinguished with it, and, vanishing, return to the place from which they had come. They have no associations with witches, because, since they dwell in fire, on account of their subtlety they cannot put on a gross or aerial body.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions*

The second kind is called aerial, which, wandering in the air, dwells near us. Indeed they can descend to the lower regions and, having assumed bodies from the thicker air, sometimes appear visibly to human beings. With God permitting, they quite often disturb the air, stir up thunder and storms; all together they conspire for the destruction of the human race. They are moved, in the manner of human beings, by the affections of the passions; they are touched especially by pride and envy and by continual disturbances. They are not clothed with a solid body, nor do they remain in one place, nor do they all have one form, but many, and they change these rather often according to the variety of the affections by which, either summoned by the incantations of witches, they come to meet them, or, driven by disturbance, they move to do harm. For they have an altogether violent, frenzied character, full of disturbances; hence, when greatly agitated by evil-working, they very often devise sudden ambushes. While making their attacks, they partly wish to lie hidden, partly they inflict violence. Witches, supported by the cooperation of these demons, are so much the more powerful for evil-working, the higher in that order the one who assists them is whom they have obtained. In the life of Saint Anthony, Saint Athanasius said that the air is full of demons, which Mercury, thrice-greatest, had said before: namely, that no part of the world is devoid of the presence of demons.
