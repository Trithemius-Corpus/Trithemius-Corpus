*To Maximilian Caesar.*

... has made our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, acceptable and a friend to God; for thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.” Evil men therefore command demons, whom malice has made like to them; likeness made them familiar, and familiarity made them their own and subject.

It is as if some servant, born in a humble station, were said to command a great prince, which often happens, because he has been joined to him by long familiarity; through this he obtains without contradiction everything he has asked, so that he seems rather to rule his lord than to be ruled by him. According to this likeness, evil and reprobate men, who are admitted by demons into their familiarity and become very bold through more prolonged [unclear] confidence, receive a counterfeit, not to say familiar, command over them, which they have deserved by the likeness of a most depraved will. Hence whatever they have sought to receive from demons for themselves by an explicit compact, with [unclear] concurring, they receive, except whenever, being forbidden by divine ordinance, they are unable to furnish what is asked. Yet they do not thus present themselves indiscriminately to all reprobate and evil persons, but only to those with whom, through the intervention of some compact, they have entered into treaties, as are sorcerous women who have done homage to the Devil, and invokers of those same demons, whom the common people call by the customary term necromancers.

*There are two kinds of men who may command lawfully.*

The first indeed is of those men who, beloved of God by the merit of holiness, have received from him the power of trampling the head of the ancient serpent, such as in the primitive Church were the Apostles and their followers and disciples, concerning whom the Lord speaks in the holy Gospel: “In my name they will cast out demons,” and “they will take up serpents.”

The second kind, however, is of those men to whom, by reason of their order in the Church of God, it is granted that they drive demons out of men’s bodies by the power of the ministry received. And this ministry is entrusted to exorcists in the Church of God, namely when they are ordained by the bishop.

Again there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons; with what unlawful presumption they do this, they show by their most wicked conditions.

The first and most pernicious kind is that of sorceresses, who enter into pacts with demons and, after a solemn profession has first been made, hand themselves over to some demon as property and for perpetual service. How many evils this most pernicious kind of human beings commits in your empire, most wise Caesar, there is no one who can express. For first of all, denying God and the faith of Christ and holy Baptism, they offer themselves, in mind and body, to demons; by their sorcery they make human beings and beasts sick, harm them, and kill them. There is no disease which, if God permits it, they cannot bring on, because, when they present themselves as servants to demons in observance of the compacted treaty, by comparison with them they dare whatever they wish. Hence they are nowhere to be tolerated, but rather in every place are to be utterly exterminated, as God, creator of all, so commands: “You shall not suffer sorcerers to live.” Of sorceresses, some summon demons by spells, others entice them by prayers and sacrifices, the rest by unspeakable things and ceremonies
