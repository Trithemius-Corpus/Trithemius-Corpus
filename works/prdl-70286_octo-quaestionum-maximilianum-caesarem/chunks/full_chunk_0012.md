To this one must reply: just as the infants of Christians, baptized in the faith of their parents, are saved, so the children of all unbelievers, not baptized, will be damned in the unbelief of their parents. The judgment concerning the simple-minded of either religion is similar: just as we believe without doubt that baptized simple-minded Christians are to be saved, so we say without doubt that those of unbelievers are to be damned.

Again it is asked here what is to be held in Catholic belief concerning those people who inhabit the islands newly discovered in the great sea, of which no mention is made among the ancient cosmographers, and who, as we have been informed, have never heard anything at all of the Christian religion. A brief answer, conformable in all respects to what has been set forth, is given: since Truth exempts no unbeliever at all from damnation, neither [unclear] of the islands, nor one contiguous to our world, nor anyone else dwelling either near or far, but has concluded all without distinction, without defining persons, saying: “He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned”; “the wrath of God remains upon him.” Who removes the wrath of God from man, except our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the mediator of God and men? By what means, except through faith formed in him, who, though innocent, tasted death? Since, therefore, without faith no one can [attain?] salvation, those who are known not to have the piety of faith in him [unclear]. Accordingly we must necessarily say that all are to be damned who do not believe in the Son of God, whether they have heard of him or are wholly ignorant of him.

One thing, however, we grant: that if there were any among those who are ignorant of Christ who lived well according to the law of nature, invoking the one God, even if they sometimes sinned, provided they had done penance before death, they were damned, or are to be damned, less gravely than those who either hear of Christ and despise him, or in either case have lived wickedly and foully. For God is just; he does injury to no one, but renders to each according to his works. Yet the wrath of God remains upon everyone who does not believe in his only-begotten Son, on account of the original sin of the first man, from which no one is justified except through faith and baptism of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

If original sin alone is present in a person, as in little children, unless it is removed through Christ, it condemns the one who has it only to the punishment of loss, whereby, namely, he is barred forever from the vision of God and the enjoyment of the blessedness of the saints. But an adult who sins mortally will be punished after death with a twofold punishment, namely of loss and of sense, so that he both does not see God and, feeling the pains of Gehenna without end, never escapes. Jews, Saracens, and all the rest who, hearing the faith of Christ, despise it are without doubt damned to the punishment both of loss and of sense, whether otherwise they live innocently according to the law of nature or the contrary. But among those who have never heard of Christ, if any should live innocent according to the law of nature up to death, I do not think that they have furnished cause for being said or believed to be damned to the punishment of sense, although they are deprived forever of the vision of God, which theologians have called the punishment of loss; for no one will behold the face of God the Father almighty unless he has been reborn through faith in Christ.

Now let us conclude with a judgment against those who, when we assert these things in Catholic fashion according to Christ’s doctrine, object with weighty consideration.
