But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and most grievous, merciful God is accustomed to purge in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the hardships of diverse tribulations in body and in possessions. For the malice of the impious benefits good and just men as a remedy, but for those who inflict it, it leads to damnation.

The sixth reason is that a man bewitched through the assaults of demons may be called back to repentance and may set in his heart the necessary amendment of life. For almighty God does not immediately send vengeance when we sin, but awaits our amendment until the appointed time. But if we neglect to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not expect it; we sinners who have disdained to acknowledge him by repenting in tranquillity shall at last acknowledge him, late, in tribulation. Hence the holy Prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: If his sons forsake my law and do not walk in my judgments, if they do not keep my commandments, I shall visit their iniquities with a rod and their sins with scourges; but my mercy I shall not scatter from them, nor shall I do harm in my truth.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits the witchcrafts of demons to prevail against good and innocent persons is so that an excessively licentious occasion for sinning may be cut off from them. For there are many poor, sick, good, devout, humble persons pleasing to God, whom poverty and bodily illness preserve in the love of God, and who, if they were rich and raised to honors, would quickly fall away from the purpose of holiness into the abyss of vices. It is not beneficial for everyone’s salvation to have honors and riches together with health, nor does a longer life always make a person better. Therefore almighty God, wishing to show his mercy toward those whom he has chosen, permits the good, the just, and the innocent to be subject to various calamities; sometimes through demons, sometimes through evil men, sometimes in diverse other ways and by other means, he takes away from such persons riches and honors, which he foreknew they would use badly, so that they may keep humility in poverty and abasement, which they would certainly not have preserved if placed in wealth and exaltation. He also often permits them to fall into many great illnesses, for whom glad health of the flesh would have brought the greatest sickness of mind.

Finally, he permits certain persons to be driven to death by witchcraft, so that the license for sinning may be cut off from them, which they would have abused had they been allowed to remain longer in life. Hence Scripture says of the just man: He was snatched away, lest malice change his understanding, or deceit deceive his soul.

The eighth reason for divine permission is so that men may venerate the sacraments of the Church, which have great power against all witchcrafts of malignant spirits. For one who venerates with due honor the sacraments and rites of the Catholic Church is rarely harmed by the witchcrafts of demons. And especially the hearing of witches and the use of things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons.
