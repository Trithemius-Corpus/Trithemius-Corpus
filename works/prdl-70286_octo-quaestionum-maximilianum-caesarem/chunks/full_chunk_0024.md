*To Maximilian Caesar.*

... remains spread over our hearts, which does not permit us to arrive at true understanding. Hence it happens that we do not understand the sense which the Holy Spirit has poured into the divine Scriptures, but, seizing the bare words of the writers, we twist them into another sense. Indeed, if our minds were purged of carnal vices, if through the continual pursuit of sobriety the intellect were stirred up in the Scriptures, if belief and reverence were applied as their dignity requires, every student would understand more clearly than light that in the sacred readings there is nothing confused, nothing misshapen, nothing unsuitable, nothing absurd, or inserted without the most just reason. For all the passages of divine Scripture are full of mysteries; and whenever anything in them seems to have been set down obscurely, this has been done by divine providence, so that the studious mind may have the necessary exercise of piety, and the faith of the simple may not lose the place of due reverence. Therefore not by chance, but by eternal providence, he willed the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that it might abound in as many mysteries as senses in which, by the inspiration of the Spirit of God, it is explained in Catholic fashion by the doctors. For, as holy Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture is useful and very profitable, because it begets many meanings of truth and brings them forth into the light for human beings; for when one man draws out this sense, and another that sense, while expounding it in Catholic fashion, it becomes known how lofty and deep it is. Therefore let no man swollen with secular learning suppose that he understands sacred Scripture, unless he first lays aside the veil of pride and of every disordered desire. For secular learning is of more value for exercising the intellect than for illuminating the understanding toward true wisdom. Therefore without doubt divine Scripture is, to minds purified through the continual exercise of the fear of God and frequent study of reading, both manifest and clear, though to filthy minds, confused by secular wisdom, it seems obscure.

*Johannes Trithemius, Eight Questions.*

Hence he understands the holy Scriptures well and rightly who, with purified affection, being frequent in the study of them, expounds them as did the one who wrote. For if they are read in another spirit than that by which he is believed to have inspired them to be written, the mind must necessarily be wholly shut away from true understanding. But when we encounter some discordance in them, let us understand that it is only of words, not of meanings; and it arises not by chance but by the ordering providence of God, so that the mind, exercised longer in the Scriptures, may be strengthened and not become altogether sluggish. For the counsel of God cannot be deceived; but the divine Scriptures were written by the counsel of his will. It follows necessarily that they are true and in no way false. They were men by whom the divine [Scriptures] came to us. You who are devoted to secular wisdom, say of the divine Scriptures of the New and Old Testament, which the Church of Christians has received and venerates as true, authentic, and holy Scripture: whence do you believe it was received, from God or from men? If from God, why do you not believe it? For everyone who attacks the Scripture which he believes to be inspired most openly resists the divine ordering. But if it is from men, why do you hope for the eternal salvation promised in it, which man cannot give?
