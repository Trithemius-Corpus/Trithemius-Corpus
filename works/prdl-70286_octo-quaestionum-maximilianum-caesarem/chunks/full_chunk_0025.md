For if you truly believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, you will necessarily say that the divine Scriptures were inspired by the Holy Spirit; but if, having fallen with the apostates into a reprobate sense, you say that they were [composed?] by men, you impiously show how far you are from true wisdom, wandering in darkness, and you openly reveal yourselves to be unskilled and rash judges, since you condemn Scripture which you do not understand. But because it is more familiar to you to despise wisdom than to learn it, it is not the purpose of this work to weary the mind longer against the rashness of emperors. This one thing I want you to know: that no one understands unless he receives; but because you set the filth of your affections in the way, so that you may not receive, therefore you do not have the understanding of true wisdom. For almighty God deigns, through grace, to be present as illuminator to those who, stripped of carnal desires, burn with the fire of inward love, who desire nothing in this world except for God. The Spirit of God brings help by his presence to those whom he has found rooted in the love of truth, and, having been cleansed from every extraneous thing, he leads them into the knowledge of all things. For to those who truly love God all things are open, all things naked, all things manifest, since whoever, through love, beholds in the faith of Jesus Christ him who creates and governs all things, knows all things that are necessary for his salvation. But, on the contrary, the illumination of God dwells very far from the slothful, the wicked, gluttons, the envious, the avaricious, [incubonibus], the filthy, the lustful, murderers, the impious, the proud, and those alien to piety, leaving them to the discretion of the avenging demon, who, driving in the sharp point of savage fire, afflicts sense and mind, and more and more arms and drives the impious to perpetrate crimes, so that, guilty of a more shameful fault, they may also be liable to a sharper punishment.

Whence, entangled in vices and worldly desires, they not only cannot penetrate the divine Scriptures with understanding, but, neglecting themselves, do not even know themselves. Since, therefore, sacred Scripture is the perfect work of God, we must so confirm the mind, Caesar, that we believe it true, right, complete, solid, divinely inspired, and most abundantly sufficient for all who understand it for the confirmation of the Christian faith. Indeed, unless divine Scripture is believed to be true, holy, perfect, and complete in all things, the Christian faith would be deprived of its foundation. Therefore every Christian must take the greatest care that, by scrutinizing the divine Scriptures more than is expedient, he not go beyond the bounds of piety, because the author of the Scriptures is not man, but the Holy Spirit; and whoever resists the integrity of God’s Scriptures is proved to resist his ordinance. Moreover Scripture, by God’s ordaining, is usefully interwoven with parables, enigmas, likenesses, and comparisons of figures, so that readers may be exercised toward understanding and, once the inward sweetness has been tasted, the mind may be confirmed in piety. And so, if with purified minds we attend to the sense of Scripture, not the words, we shall understand how rightly and properly all things have been ordered. For it was not fitting that the sacred Scriptures, in which the knowledge of the divine majesty is handed down, should be so completely full and manifest that they would everywhere be understood by all; what is proposed to be understood is only for the good and holy, not also for evil, filthy, and polluted minds.
