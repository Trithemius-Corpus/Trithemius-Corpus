*An elegiac poem by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on holy mother Anne.*

What great rewards Anne brings to those who honor her,
no one grasps in mind, nor can he speak with his mouth.
She drives away hardships, obtains pardon, increases
adornments of honor for her own, and makes them be in heaven.
On the other hand, many very bitter things attend
those who scorn mother Anne. She casts the powerful down from honor,
crushes the proud, [unclear]
and permits that great scandals often [unclear].
Therefore, men, I advise you: serve so great a patroness,
and let the chorus of women be present likewise in love.
It is clear also from examples that what we write is plain; let open ears
take in what aids devout hearts.

*From Sponheim, the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August, 1493.*

*A Sapphic poem by Rutgerus Sicamber, for a hymn in praise of Saint Anne.*

We sing your praises, venerable
Anne; favor those who praise you with measures,
obtaining pardon for all, bestowing
rewards of salvation.

You, Anne, parent of the mother of Christ,
shining in the highest citadel of heaven,
obtain whatever you ask of the Son
of the Almighty.

You heal the sick, you wipe clean the stained;
you make faithful servants attain their vow;
at last you grant them to climb
the heavenly kingdom.

By your approved ways, Anne,
you bent the highest Lord, and by many prayers,
that he should grant you to become the mother
of so great a child in the world.

May honor and power be to the highest Father,
to the heavenly Son,
to the holy Spirit; brightness, virtue, devotion, and strength
through all ages.

*Another by the same.*

Let us all resound the diligent praises
of Anne, mother of the Virgin Mother,
with all our strength, from the marrow of the soul,
with sonorous voice.

She brought forth for the wretched race,
stained with the blemish of mother Eve,
a noble offspring, a radiant beam, which
she gave to the world.

This light drove out the darkness
of the blackening world, shining on all with heavenly
light, as holy John resounds
in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine maiden,
whose Son you call your grandson,
according to the flesh only: do not despise
the people that prays to you.

Virgin, through the prayers of Anne your mother,
who begs for us with intent prayers,
you, grandson Jesus, kindly fulfill
her petition.

To the Son born of the mother Anne,
with the highest God, be honor and power;
to the blessed Spirit equal honor
for all time.

*An iambic dimeter poem by the same, which is sung to the melody of the hymns of Ambrose.*

Bowing down, let us honor
blessed Anne’s feast,
singing due thanks with our voices
and with our inmost hearts.

She is the mother of the nourishing Virgin,
who brought forth the light of the world;
she who, by virtues, made herself
most pleasing to God.

She repels injuries; she bestows
grace by her prayers.
She relieves the most dreadful diseases,
of body as well as of spirit.

In praise of her, king of heaven,
to you with sonorous songs,
together with the Son and the Spirit,
we bring forth devout praises. Amen.

*An Alcaic poem by the same, which is sung to the melody Crux fidelis, or Pange lingua.*

This company, with voice and vows,
and also with hearts,
is eager to proclaim the feasts of Anne,
so illustrious a mother.
Be gentle, we beseech you,
to those who praise you.

You are the parent of Mary’s mother;
you are most pleasing to God.
You preserved the ordinances of the law
which Moses once delivered
to the ancient fathers
and to the Hebrew peoples.

You obtain wondrous,
gracious gifts for the faithful
by prayer pleasing
to the Son of the high Father.
You heal the sick and grant
hearts to be chaste.

We ask you, Father, and
Son, with the Spirit,
that you grant joyful things to us
singing the praises
of the renowned, very sweet Anne,
the nourishing mother of the Virgin.
Amen.

*A poem by Rutgerus Venral, a German regular canon of Heinsberg, on the book concerning the praises of Saint Anne.*

Whoever you are, reader of this excellent book,
lay aside empty fear; it contains nothing frivolous.
This book, pregnant with wondrous meaning and sweet with charm,
Abbot Trithemius has brought forth by his art.
Keeping watch over books, guardian of the Sponheim sheepfold,
he cultivates the sacred writings of the threefold tongue.
You will find [unclear]; only turn it over in a devout heart.
He does not follow empty and everywhere doubtful little tales;
he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence.
Therefore, I pray, read with love the worthy praises
of venerable Anne, which a learned man has brought forth for you.

*Printed in the noble city of Mainz by Peter Friedberg, in the year of the Virgin birth 1494, the twelfth day before the Kalends of August.*
