Anne, pregnant, you bear [the garden?], which the flaming sword,
Glowing night and day, a cherub guards.
Hail.
Anne, you bear a flower that surpasses white lilies,
And lowly violets and the scarlet rose.
Hail.
Anne, in you the Mosaic ark is found,
And the golden urn of heavenly bread lies there.
Hail.
Anne, you bear the bride of the true Solomon, and the house
Adorned with every jewel within and without.
Hail.
Anne, you bear the temple whose inner sanctuaries
Sweet sounds have filled, and all music rejoicing.
Hail.
Anne, you bear the veil under a beautiful image of the fathers,
Holding in itself deeds that cover the high things of God.
Hail.
Anne, you bear offspring: golden Phoebus clothes her head
With flame, the bright moon her snowy feet.
Our Father. Hail, of God.
Anne, now made possessor of the longed-for Mary,
You can scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have arisen.
Hail.
Anne, at Mary newly born all the stars
Applauded, and an unwonted radiance flowed from the axis of heaven.
Hail.
Anne, it is right to believe that at the birthday of your child
You celebrated, surrounded by angelic choirs.
Hail.
Anne, the deep shades felt your new birth;
Then a new light itself terrified the Stygian pools.
Hail.
Anne, parent, you hesitate to touch your child with your hands,
More ruddy than the sun and the lights of heaven.
Hail.
Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl?
She smiles at her mother; she makes no sorrow.
Hail.
Anne, take courage: see, your daughter reaches toward your milky breasts;
She seeks them with tender hands, and seeks your neck.
You receive her tender limbs in your tender bosom.
Hail.
Anne, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast she draws sweet juices
With rosy mouth, making pleasing murmurs.
Whatever deaths she may bear, you would now be none.
Our Father. Hail, of God.

Anne, it is fitting for you to adorn your worthy daughter
With approved morals, and to unite her to the highest God.
Hail.
Anne, you prepare to bring your daughter to the lofty temple,
But of her own will the gracious one reached the step above.
Hail.
Anne, you follow her, swift, with slow step and labor;
The ready little virgin comforts her weary mother.
Hail.
Anne, why do you marvel at strength in the tender virgin?
You see not human things, but the holy signs of God.
Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to give gifts to the poor and to sacred temples;
Now you give that than which no woman ever gave more.
Hail.
Anne, where you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell,
She gladly entered the cloisters and the virginal house.
Hail.
Anne, about to send away the pious one, you wear out your cheeks and eyes
With weeping; a deep love holds you unmoved.
Hail.
Anne, return home; leave your daughter to the Thunderer.
Let her think of nothing except the Jesus who is to come.
Hail.
Anne, it is enough for you to serve two Marys;
They too will honor you with no small devotion.
Our Father. Hail, of God.
Hail.
Anne, by your own weeping you have learned to come to the aid of one who weeps;
Hence you too readily favor the wretched man in his vows.
Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to turn the fixed laws of nature;
And if you wish, let a barren woman now be a joyful mother.
Hail.
Anne, you also halt the gifts of fleeting fortune,
So that, having slipped away suddenly, they do not flee from our bosoms.
Hail.
Anne, you put disgrace to flight; you give the gifts of fair fame.
You relieve mourning, and love yields to you.
Hail.
Anne, you help either him whom hostile force terrifies, or him whom
The cruel bonds of a dark prison hold.
Hail.
Anne, be present as a faithful relief to sick mortals;
With you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing.
Hail.
Anne, parent, the sailor calls upon you amid the swollen wave,
If winds and water toss his torn vessel.
Hail.
Anne, parent, you must always be feared by the black tyrant;
As ice flees fire, that harmful one flees you.
From you mortal hearts cannot seek in vain,
And whatever you will, your daughter and God will will.
Hail.
Anne, therefore, blessed by merits and by so great an offspring,
Anne, great parent, look upon our prayers.
Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary.*
Only-begotten and most loving God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as the mother of the begetter of your Son: mercifully grant that we who honor her memory with faithful devotion may, by her merits and prayers, obtain the aids of eternal life. Through the same our Lord.

Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beissel, professor of both laws, patrician of Aachen, to the reverend father lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim of the order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1494.

*The poem of Ada Wernher of Themar on most holy Anne. To the reverend father lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.*

We sing in praise of the renowned grandmother. Move the plectra of [unclear], dear grandson, nor you, daughter, fail the singer, chaste parent of the choir. Anne, offspring of kings, is given to a husband. Long childless, she laments herself barren; with weeping she prays for offspring, nor do the tears flow in vain. Heavenly messengers promise a child. Pregnant, joined to her husband, then in childbirth, she is proved by the child born, through whom our salvation came. Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green; the fertile rod came forth with fruitful shoot; refreshed by its flower, we live again, death conquered.
