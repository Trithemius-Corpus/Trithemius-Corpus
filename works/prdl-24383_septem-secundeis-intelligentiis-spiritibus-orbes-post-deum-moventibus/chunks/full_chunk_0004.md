You have a great store of these things: you revere him so devoutly that you could not do so more holily, and you imitate his learning and integrity of morals so closely that no one alive is more like him than you. This, though I had heard it celebrated in the speech of many concerning your excellence, I have found to be greater by I know not how many degrees. It is two years now since, fleeing on account of the plague in the Odenwald, and thus lying hidden in the court of the most splendid lord Schenck Eberhard of Erbach, the elder baron, I was summoned into the number of the friends of your highness by letters most learnedly written in your own hand, and at the same time by an honorable little gift. And you thought even these things too little, unless you invited a very small man, and one perhaps beneath your opinion of him; you received him when invited, and indeed most liberally, and with more than human kindness you wiped away the hardships of his whole exile. To what mortal has such favor ever come from so great an abbot as came to Marquard from that Jacob, most richly endowed with every ornament truly worthy of an abbot? Nor is it for me here to recount by what and how great virtues you have climbed from a man of middling fortune to this summit, so that, chosen by the most ardent wishes of your brothers as their father, as sons are safe in their father’s spirit, vigorous when he is vigorous, strong when he is firm, blessed when he is safe, collapsed when he is weak, so all have embraced your gentle rule, even now for many years, so that they have regretted no day less than the one that first saw you as abbot. These things may be small, however great they are. Yet I admire still more the ready munificence of your spirit toward those who are devoted to you, and your manners, in no way harsh even toward the lowest; by these you easily reconcile even enemies to yourself and carry them away into admiration of you. You live a blameless life, such as could have been sketched by Saint Paul to Timothy. Besides this, who will sufficiently admire in you eloquence joined with prudence, ease seasoned with gravity, beneficence moderated by frugality, and, finally, a versatile intellect, by which you administer human and divine affairs not without incredible toil, but with great and certain praise? But of these things I shall speak elsewhere, among others, at a more fitting time, since I have long understood that your modesty seeks nothing less than immoderate praises, although none are to be considered immoderate in view of your greatness. Meanwhile, however, I ask this one thing: receive with a cheerful face Trithemius brought back to life, publicly restored to light and liberty, and, most worthy abbot, most worthily protect him for this most worthy abbot. May Christ Jesus, the savior of all, keep your reverend fatherhood safe and unharmed for a very long time. Amen.

The book of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James at Würzburg, on the celestial intelligences that govern the world after God.

It is the opinion of very many of the ancients, most wise Caesar, that this lower world is governed, by the ordinance of the first intellect, who is God, through secondary intelligences; agreeing with their opinion, the Conciliator of Physicians says that seven planetary spirits were appointed from the beginning of the creation of heaven and earth, each of whom governs the world in order for 354 years and 4 months. Very many most learned men up to now have given assent to this position. I make it known to your most sacred majesty not asserting it, but merely reporting it.

The first angel, or spirit of Saturn, is called Orifiel; to him God entrusted the world to be governed from the beginning of creation. His rule began on the fifteenth day of the month of March, in the first year of the world, and lasted 354 years and 4 months. Orifiel, however, is a name of office, not of nature, attributed to the spirit by reason of his action. Under his rule humans were rough and rustic, living in solitude after the manner of beasts. This matter needs no proof from me, since it is clear to everyone from the text of Genesis.

The second governor of the world is Anael, the spirit of Venus, who after Orifiel began to rule by the influence of his planet in the year of the world 354, the fourth month, that is, on the fourteenth day of the month of June; and he governed the world for 354 years and 4 months, up to the year 708 from the creation of the world, as is plain to anyone calculating the age. Under the rule of this Anael, human beings began to be more cultivated, to build houses and cities, to discover manual arts, the work of weaving, wool-working, and things of this kind, and also to indulge more abundantly in the pleasures of the flesh. They took beautiful wives for themselves, forgot God, departed in many ways from natural simplicity, invented games and songs, played the lyre, and devised whatever belongs to the manners and cult of Venus. This wantonness of life endured among human beings up to the flood, taking the proof of its own depravity.

The third, Zachariel, angel of Jupiter, began to govern the world in the year 708 from the creation of heaven and earth, the eighth month, that is, on the sixteenth day of the month of October; and he governed the world for 354 years and 4 months, up to the year 1063 from the creation of the world, inclusive. Under his direction human beings first [unclear] dominion over another.
