Justinian built the church of Saint Sophia [unclear] at Constantinople. The empire was often divided, confused, and full of disturbances. Many signs appeared in these times, and are found in the histories. Cosdrai, king of the Persians, took Jerusalem; Heraclius later killed him. Muhammad the Arab introduced the sect of the Saracens, in these times, around the year of Christ 600, after the Roman empire in Asia was already seen to have been entirely taken away. Dagobert, king of Francia, overcame and killed the Angles, who were then called Saxons. Note, finally, that from this point the Christian faith began gradually to fail in Asia and Africa, under the entry of the sect of the Saracens, which has now defiled the whole world, [unclear] Europe, in which the religion of Christ was multiplied and spread widely through the monks of the order of Saint Benedict. Around the year of the Lord 774 crosses appeared on people’s clothing, and not long afterward the Roman empire was divided, the transfer of the monarchy to the Franks being made in Charles, who rescued the Church, and at the same time the empire, from destruction, and performed many deeds. Under him, after his victory, the name of the Westphalians began in Saxony.

In the eighteenth place after Zachariel, Louis, angel Raphael, spirit of Mercury, undertook the governance of the world on the third [?] day, the second day of the month of November, in the year 6023 from the creation of the universe, in the eighth month, but in the year 817 from the Lord’s nativity; and he presided over the world for 354 years and 4 months, that is, until the year of the world 6378, and of the Lord 1171. At the beginning of this revolution of Mercury, the monarchy of the Roman empire, as we have said, was transferred to Charles the Great. After Charles, his son Louis held the empire for 25 years; when he died, his sons fought among themselves and in turn weakened the strength of the empire. The Normans devastated Gaul. Rome was twice defiled by the Saracens. Under Louis II, blood rained from the sky in Italy for three days. In Saxony a certain village, with all its buildings and people, was swallowed in a moment by a terrible opening of the earth. Around the year of the Lord 910 great disturbances took place in Italy, and Italy withdrew from the Frankish empire and established kings of its own for itself. The first of these was Berengar, duke of Friuli, to whom seven succeeded in order for nearly fifty years, until the transfer of the empire to the Germans. To the first Otto, that is, in the year of Christ 962, the empire was transferred from the Franks to the Germans; Otto was made the first emperor, and from this point the monarchy began to be reformed. His son Otto and his grandson Otto succeeded him in the empire. Under them the Hungarians were converted to the Christian faith. Otto III, about to die without children, afterward established the electors of the empire, in the year of the Christians 1002, as they remain until the present day.
