The world began to apply itself still further to this; among these men were, last of all, the most illustrious men Mercury, Bacchus, Omogyrus, Isis, Inachus, Argus, Apollo, Cecrops, and many others who by their inventions benefited both the world then and posterity. In these times also various superstitions were instituted in the worship of idols; incantations and arts of diabolical images were wonderfully increased; and whatever subtlety and ingenuity is customarily attributed to Mercury was increasing in these times.

Moses, the most wise leader of the Hebrews, skilled in many matters and arts, a worshiper of the true and one God, in these times delivered the people of the Hebrews from the slavery of the Egyptians into freedom. In these times also Janus first reigned in Italy, and after him Saturn, who taught that fields should be enriched with manure and was regarded as a god. In these times also Cadmus invented the Greek letters, and Carmentis, daughter of Evander, the Latin. Under the rule of this Raphael, angel of Mercury, almighty God gave his people the law in writing through Moses, which gives clear testimony to the future birth of Christ in the flesh. A wonderful diversity of religions also arose in the world. In these times many Sibyls, prophets, soothsayers, haruspices, magi, seers, and prophets flourished, such as the Erythraean Sibyl, the Delphic, the Phrygian, and the others.

Twelfth in order, Samael, angel of Mars, again began to govern the world on the second day of the month of October, in the year of the world 3897, month 8. His rule lasted 354 years and 4 months, until the year 4152. Under his rule occurred that great and most famous destruction of Troy in Asia Minor, and there was an astonishing change in the monarchy of many kingdoms, and the founding anew of many cities, such as Paris, Mainz, Carthage, Naples, and many others. Many kingdoms also arose anew, such as those of the Lacedaemonians, Corinthians, Hebrews, and many others. In these times there were very great wars and battles of kings and nations throughout the whole world, and various changes of empires.

From this time the Venetians reckon the beginning both of their people and of their city from the Trojans. And it should be noted that very many other nations, both in Europe and in Asia, claim that their origin was taken from the Trojans; I have decided to grant them only as much credit as they themselves can persuade me of by sufficient testimony of the truth. Frivolous are the things they bring forward about their nobility and antiquity, wishing openly to boast, as though there had not been peoples in Europe before the destruction of the Trojans, and as though no Trojan among them had been ignoble.
