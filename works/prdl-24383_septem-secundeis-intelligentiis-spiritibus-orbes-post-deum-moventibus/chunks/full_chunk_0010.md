Its first king, Darius, and its second, Cyrus, utterly destroyed that most powerful kingdom of Babylon in the days of Belshazzar, as Daniel and the prophets had foretold.

In these times the Cumaean Sibyl was renowned. She offered nine books to King Tarquinius Priscus to be bought for a price; in them was contained the sequence of future warnings and events of the whole Roman commonwealth. But when the king refused the price she demanded, the Sibyl burned the first three books before his eyes. For the remaining six she demanded the same price; and when he again refused it, she likewise gave three others to the fire to be burned. She would have done the same with the last ones, had not the king, persuaded by the counsel of his men, redeemed them from destruction by paying the price that had been demanded for all.

Likewise, after abolishing kings, the Romans established two consuls each year. Phalaris the tyrant occupied Sicily in those times. In those times magic was held in the highest value among the kings of the Persians. Pythagoras the philosopher and many others then flourished among the Greeks. The temple at Jerusalem and the city were rebuilt. Ezra the prophet, from memory, restored as an archetype the books of Moses that had been burned by the Chaldeans, who were also called Babylonians.

Xerxes, king of the Persians, led an army against the Greeks and accomplished little. Rome was taken, burned, and destroyed by the Gauls, with only the Capitol excepted, protected by a goose; [unclear]. The very famous wars of the Athenians also occurred in these times. Socrates and Plato the philosophers were renowned. The Romans, again abolishing the consuls, instituted tribunes and aediles, and in these times they were involved in many calamities.

Alexander the Great also, after the rule of Michael had been completed, reigned in Macedonia. He destroyed the monarchy of the Persians under Darius, and subjected all Asia, with part of Europe, to his empire. He lived thirty-three years, and ruled twelve years and five months. After his death endless wars followed, and many evils, and the monarchy was divided into four. Among the Hebrews contention began over the high priesthood. The kingdom of Syria was begun.

After Michael, in the fifteenth order, the spirit of Saturn, Orifiel, now for the third time assumed the rule of the world on the last day of September, in the year from the creation of the universe 4960, in the eighth month; and he presided for 354 years and four months, until the year of the world 5315. Under his rule the Punic War began between the Romans and the Carthaginians. The city of Rome was almost entirely consumed by fire and water.
