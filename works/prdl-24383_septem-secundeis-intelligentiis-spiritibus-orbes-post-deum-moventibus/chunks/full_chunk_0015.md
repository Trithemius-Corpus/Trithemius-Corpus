He ruled for twenty years; he founded the church of Bamberg, and, dying a virgin together with his wife Kunigunda, shone with miracles. After him Conrad, the first duke of the Franks, was elected, and he ruled for twenty years. Likewise Godfrey, count of Bouillon, recovered the Holy Land and Jerusalem from the hands of the infidels.

Before the end of the revolution, many signs and portents were seen, and not long afterward the race of the Tartars, having gone out from its own borders, did much harm to the Roman Empire. Famines, pestilences, earthquakes, and many evils followed in the Empire. Three suns were seen in the east, and as many moons.

In the year of the Lord 1153, Frederick I, called Barbarossa, began to rule, and he ruled for thirty-three years. He began in the 336th year of Raphael, and did many things magnificently, enlarged the strength of the empire, and successfully waged many wars. In his time the [unclear] and the [Lithuanians?] were converted to the faith of Christ.

In the nineteenth order of the empire of the world, Samael, the spirit of Mars, took it up for the third time on the third day of March, in the year of the world 6378, and governed it for 354 years and four months, until the year of the world 6732, the fourth month, but of the Lord 1525. Under his rule there were many wars throughout the world, in which countless thousands of men perished, and very many kingdoms lost their former boundaries.

Between Frederick I, emperor, and the Roman nobles there were many discords and great battles, and many thousands of Romans perished. He also utterly overthrew Milan; he destroyed [Leodium?]. Jerusalem was again captured by the Saracens. The empire of the Tartars, the greatest of all in the world at this time, had its beginning in these times; they made a very great scourge in the world, and they do not cease.

After Frederick, Henry his son was elected emperor. When he had died, a schism threw the empire into confusion under Philip and Otto. And many battles followed in the borders of Germany: at Strasbourg, Cologne, [Leodium?], Worms, Speyer, and throughout the whole kingdom. The order of the mendicants was founded in these times, in the fortieth year of Samael, or thereabout; whence it is certain that all things happen by providence.

There were many battles of the Saracens against the Christians in Asia and Africa. Constantinople was captured by the Germans, and Baldwin, count of Flanders, was made emperor. From Germany more than 20,000 boys were drowned at sea by pirates; seduced by a vain spirit, they said that they would recover the Holy Land. From Spain, many shepherds of flocks, gathered together and advancing in common, seized the goods of clerics, while the people rejoiced with them; but when they stretched out their hands to the goods of laymen, they were killed.

In the year of the Christians 1212, Frederick II was elected, and ruled for thirty-three years; he did many things against the Church. In the year 1238, eclipses and continual earthquakes crushed many thousands of men; Frisia also was almost entirely submerged by sea waves.
