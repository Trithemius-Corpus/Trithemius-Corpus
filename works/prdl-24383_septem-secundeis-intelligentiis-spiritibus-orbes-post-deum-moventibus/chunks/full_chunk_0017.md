[Under?] Wenceslaus the German, Wenceslaus was deposed. Leopold, duke of Austria, with eight counts and more than 4,000 men, was killed by the Swiss while fighting in battle. Under Wenceslaus, king of the Bohemians and emperor, the sect of the Hussites took its beginning; after he had been deposed, Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, was elected and ruled for ten years. In the year of the Lord 1396, the Christians declared war on the Saracens, but it turned out badly because of the arrogance of the French, since more than 100,000 of our men fell, apart from the captives, among whom was John, duke of the Burgundians. There were many wars in these times. In the year [1411?] Sigismund became emperor for twenty-seven years. He laid waste Bohemia for the extirpation of heresies, but accomplished little. The kingdom of France was cruelly ravaged and afflicted by the English and the Burgundians. When Sigismund had died, Albert, duke of Austria, his son-in-law, succeeded him, in the year of the Christians 1438. He ruled the kingdom only two years: a most excellent man and worthy of empire. When he died, Frederick, duke of Austria, son of Ernest, was made emperor by the election of the princes, and ruled for fifty-six years, a man divine in talent and peaceful in spirit. He began, however, in the year of the Lord 1440. In the year of the Christians 1453, Constantinople was taken by the Turks through the betrayal of a certain [unclear], and gradually all Greece fell away from the Christian faith; for in a short time many kingdoms and provinces of the Christians were laid waste and captured by the Turks. The Christians had among themselves many very serious wars in these times: in Gaul, England, Saxony, Westphalia, Prussia, Flanders, Swabia, and other places. In these times the art of printing was newly invented at Mainz, the metropolis of Germany, with marvellous industry, by the gift of divinity. In the year of the Christians 1456, the Turks were overthrown in Hungary; through the faith many of the faithful perished. The pilgrimage of boys to Saint Michael was marvellous. There were earthquakes in the kingdom of Naples, and more than 40,000 men perished. In the year of the Lord 1462, Mainz was captured and the metropolis of the Franks plundered. Charles, duke of Burgundy, defeated the French in the year 1465; afterward, in the year 1467, he destroyed Dinant and Liege. In the year 1473, he entered Guelders and obtained it by force; likewise also the whole duchy of Lorraine. A comet appeared through the month of January in the year of the Lord 1472. Charles, duke of Burgundy, afterward besieged the town of Neuss for the space of a year, in the year 1474; he was later killed in battle in the year 1477, a magnanimous prince. The Turks captured many cities of the Christians in these times: Negroponte in Euboea, the kingdom of the Bosnians, the duchy of [spetis?], Achaea, Mytilene, and several kingdoms in the East. In the year 1476 a gathering of fools took place in the France of Germany at Niklashausen, full of errors. In 1480 the Turks with a great army besieged the Rhodians, but did not prevail.
