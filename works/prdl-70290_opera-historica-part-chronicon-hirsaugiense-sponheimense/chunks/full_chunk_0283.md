*1329.*

In the twelfth year of Abbot Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1329, Cuno of Schoneck, bishop of Worms, died. After his death, the canons of the cathedral unanimously took Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, as administrator, against Gerlach Schenk of Erbach, who had ambitiously invaded the bishopric, which he also afterward obtained.

In the same year also Berthold, bishop of Speyer, was transferred by the pope's authority as bishop to the church of Strasbourg; and the aforesaid Archbishop Baldwin was taken up into the administration of the church of Speyer, now ruler of four churches.

*1330.*

In the thirteenth year of Abbot Sigismund, which was the year 1330 from the Lord's nativity, Walram, from the counts of Veldenz, by the promotion of Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, was made bishop of Speyer, and presided for six years, a good and merciful man.

In the same year, when Archbishop Baldwin resigned the administration of the church of Worms, Gerlach Schenk, about whom we spoke in the preceding year, secured the bishopric and presided for two years, not elected nor postulated, but violently intruded with the help and favor of friends, and, as was said, not confirmed by anyone.

In this same year, after a long contest held in the papal curia, Henry of Virneburg was sent to Mainz by the authority of Pope John XXII, and, having been ordained archbishop, was received by the clergy and people at the apostolic command, in the presence of two legates; and he presided for almost fifteen years, Baldwin resigning the administration by order of the pope and the legates of the apostolic see. This Archbishop Henry completed the castle of Eltville below Mainz beside the Rhine, which had been begun by Baldwin the administrator.

*1331.*

In the fourteenth year of Abbot Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1331, Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, purchased for a price the chapel of St. Alban, with a very broad garden adjoining it, from the monastery of St. Matthias outside the walls of Trier, and began to build a monastery for Carthusian monks. Completing it in eight years and endowing it magnificently, he consecrated the new foundation. The same archbishop also built anew at this time, at great expense, a stone bridge over the Moselle near Koblenz.

In the same year John, formerly count of Luxembourg, king of Bohemia, entered Italy with a great army and received Brescia, Parma, Reggio, and Modena into his power.

In this same year also a great dissension arose between Henry, duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Upright, on one side, and his brother Otto and the younger Henry, Otto's son, from the dukes of Bavaria and king of Hungary, on the other side, over the duchy of Lower Bavaria. Emperor Louis, on behalf of Otto and the younger Henry, having summoned the Hungarians to his aid, was preparing an army against the elder Henry. When this became known, the king of Bohemia, father-in-law of the elder Henry, with certain other princes joined to him, proclaimed a meeting for the disputants at Rambingen, Scharding, and other surrounding castles, fortifications, and villages.
