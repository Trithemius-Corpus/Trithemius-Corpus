In the same year Conrad, bishop of Würzburg, chancellor of the imperial court, when he was rather steadfastly reproving the vices of the wicked, was cruelly killed by Bodo and Henry, knights of Rabensburg, and by their assailants Herald and Conrad, in the city of Würzburg, in the public street, on the fourth Nones of December. Henry, called Käse, succeeded him, because he held cheese among his delicacies. The murderers, going to Rome, received a very severe penance from Pope Innocent III, which is contained in the bull of the same pope.

*1203.*

Adelbert, count of Sponheim, returned from the parts beyond the sea and brought to us relics of many saints; and he gave many gifts to Abbot Rupert, whom meanwhile he had appointed as steward. Through his munificence he gave in perpetual right and ownership to this monastery the little village of Auwen, not far from Monzingen, with its household, land, jurisdiction, justice, pastures, woods, waters, fisheries, hunts, and all its revenues or appurtenances, together with the Schultheiss, the scabini, and the leading men.

*1206.*

In this year, on the day before the Kalends of March, at the tenth hour of the day, there was a quite dreadful eclipse of the sun. For several testified that they had then seen a human head in the sun. Many other signs also appeared at this time in the sun, the stars, and the air. Abbot Rupert, considering that a great danger of souls was arising for the monks, [unclear] that no sister thereafter should be received into the enclosure beside this present monastery of ours, lest by any occasion they become a ruin of souls for posterity; and so none was received thereafter. For at that time there were still six in the enclosure. Two of them died after some years, and the four remaining transferred to Mount Saint Rupert under Abbot Iwan, as we shall say below in its proper place, when we come to the eleventh year of Abbot Iwan.

*1211.*

In this year Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, by the command and authority of Pope Innocent III, everywhere throughout all Germany denounced Emperor Otto as excommunicated. Hence Henry, count palatine of the Rhine, the emperor’s brother, was moved, and with the duke of Brabant, the count in Sponheim, and the other nobles of Lotharingia and of these regions, he devastated the whole bishopric of Mainz around the feast of Saint Michael with fire and plunder, leaving nothing untouched except cities and castles, but destroying everything.
