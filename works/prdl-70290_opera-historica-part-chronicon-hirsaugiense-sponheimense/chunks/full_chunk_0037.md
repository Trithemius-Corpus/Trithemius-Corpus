But only in the more famous monasteries, which had both a more abundant supply of temporal goods and a larger number of monks, the most learned monks of all were appointed to the office of teaching others; and they were educated not only in sacred but also in secular letters. To them monks were sent from monasteries of lower rank to be instructed in the higher disciplines. Whoever among them had acquired in the order the reputation of greater learning also had more pupils sent to him for the instruction of monks.

Among the monasteries having this custom of studies, the principal at this time were the monastery of Fulda, of Saint Gall, of Reichenau, of Hersfeld, our Hirsau, of Saint Alban at Mainz, of Corvey, of Prum, of Mediolacense, of Saint-Denis at Paris, of Saint Alban at Mainz, of Reims, of Auxerre, of Tours, of Stavelot, and of Wissembourg; of Saint Maximin at Trier, of Reims, of Auxerre, of Tours, of Stavelot, from whose community in each [unclear], in all of which there were many very studious monks, from whom the more excellent was appointed scholasticus. *Concerning Siger, the seventh abbot of this monastery, who presided for almost 30 years, his character, and certain deeds of that time.*

When Diethmar, the most reverend abbot of this monastery, had died, as we said before, Siger, a monk, who had held the office of dean, was immediately elected in his place: a man reverend in conduct and life, excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters. He presided over this monastery for almost 30 years in all holiness of religion. He was elected abbot on the same day on which his predecessor had died, namely the fourth nones of March, in the year of the Lord's nativity 912, which was the sixteenth year of the emperor Otto I. This Siger, born at Mainz, when he was twenty-four years old and was powerful in the world with many riches and honors, spurned and despised them all for the love of God and became a monk under Abbot Diethmar, his predecessor. He advanced so far in the holy life of religion, in wisdom, and in knowledge of the Scriptures, that when Diethmar had entered the way of all flesh, he himself, by the unanimous consent of all, was appointed shepherd of the sheep of Christ.

What kind of man he showed himself to his subjects, how zealous for regular discipline, how kind to the good, how severe to the negligent, no one could write better than one who deserved to see him and live with him in the monastery. In all things, indeed, he was solicitous always to present himself to his subjects as a living example of good works and virtues, lest either, preaching good things, he should neglect himself in deed, if he lived otherwise, or, withholding the teaching of the word, he should seem a useless pastor to his subjects and a mute dog unable to bark. Hence whatever he did not teach by example, no one else was able to perform.

*853.* In the first year of Abbot Siger, Emperor Otto I surrounded the city of Mainz with a siege. In the second year, which was the year of the Lord 954, Wifrid, archbishop of Cologne, died in the twenty-ninth year of his pontificate. Then the bereft clergy and people, by unanimous deliberation, agreed upon the man of God Bruno, brother of the emperor Otto I; and, after the leading men of that church had been sent, they obtained him as bishop. The monuments of his watchful [unclear], with buildings and the adornment of churches, are seen in many forms at Cologne down to the present day.
