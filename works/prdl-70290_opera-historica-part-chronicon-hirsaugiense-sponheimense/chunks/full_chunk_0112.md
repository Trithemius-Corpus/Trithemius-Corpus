*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

Restored to health, since he lived religiously in all things, he was appointed prior of this monastery, on the counsel of the brothers, by the holy father William. In that office he showed great diligence, and always zealously went before the brothers in all humility, patience, charity, and regular observance.

Afterwards, when the holy father William was much occupied in the construction of the new monastery of Hirsau, and saw that the completion of the labor he had undertaken was drawing near, and wished to appoint Saint Peter, prince of the Apostles, as patron of his church, he desired with great longing to have some relics from his most sacred body, which he might place in the new church founded by him. Therefore he sent his venerable prior Gebhard, with letters and commendatory epistles, to Rome to the supreme pontiff Urban II, who had once been a monk at Cluny, asking that something of the relics of the most blessed prince of the Apostles be sent to him.

When Prior Gebhard came to the pope, he was indeed kindly received by him, but he could not obtain the relics requested. Gebhard heard from the supreme pontiff himself that true relics of the hairs of Saint Peter were kept in the monastery of Cluny; and the pope advised that Abbot William, as a friend of Saint Hugh, should ask for something from them, which was later done. But the pope, lest he send the envoy back empty, destined relics of certain others for the blessed Abbot William by the hands of the same Gebhard, as a gift of charity.

When Prior Gebhard returned to Hirsau, he reported the words of the supreme pontiff to the holy father, and with joy presented to the abbot the particles of relics delivered to him by the same. When the holy Abbot William heard that something of the hairs of the blessed prince of the Apostles Peter was held at Cluny by Abbot Hugh, who was most loving toward him, he rejoiced greatly, trusting that absolutely nothing could be denied him by one whom he had found, through many benefits, to be charitable toward him.
