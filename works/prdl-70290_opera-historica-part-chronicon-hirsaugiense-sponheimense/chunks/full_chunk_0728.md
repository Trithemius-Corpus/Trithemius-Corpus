*XXX.*

*John Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James in the suburb of the city of Würzburg, but formerly of Sponheim, to John de Woesbruck, master of the Great Customs of Bruges, greeting.*

On the twenty-seventh day of the month of May, dearest friend, we received your letter, together with that of a certain Nicholas of Fair Mountain, doctor of [unclear], through a certain young man; and since he said that he necessarily had to depart at once on the next day, we wrote back more briefly than had been our intention. What you mentioned, that we had been with the most serene prince, the lord Margrave of Brandenburg, and that I had given up the abbey of Sponheim and taken up another, is true; the man who reported it was not mistaken. It was done not without very great cause; so that you may know it, I shall set it down in this letter as briefly as I can. In the year of the Christians one thousand five hundred and five, on the first day of the month of April, I left my monastery of Sponheim, having been summoned to Heidelberg by the most serene Prince Palatine. While I was staying there for some time, certain of my monks, having allied to themselves certain [unclear], arranged a certain hidden conspiracy against me, and made that Duke John, whom you have known very well from long ago, hostile to me, to such a degree that he ordered certain members of our household, who seemed to favor my side, to be put in public chains as if they were wicked criminals. When these things were reported to me in my absence, moved by the rashness of the deed, I had resolved not to return to the monastery unless the authors of so great an evil had first expiated the insult by suitable satisfaction. Therefore I remained outside [unclear], in no way doubting the justice of God, since the wicked will fall into the pit which they have made. Accordingly, on the fifteenth day of the month of October just past, I took up this abbey in which I now remain; because of the unfaithfulness of the monks and the hostility of the duke, I resigned Sponheim of my own free will. Here, then, I now live as long as the Most High shall will; I devote myself to my writings, and always await, timid, the final calling which no one can escape. But if that good doctor of yours should wish to approach me, I hope he will find me here, unless perhaps meanwhile I must turn aside somewhere for a time, summoned as before. For you know how necessary it is for us sometimes to obey the commands of princes. Farewell, remembering and loving me. From Würzburg, on the twenty-eighth day of the month of May, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*XXXI.*

*To the most reverend father and lord John Trithemius, abbot of the house of St. James at Würzburg, a man of the first rank among the Germans, Nicholas Gerbellius of Pforzheim wishes greeting and eternal happiness.*

Since indeed, most reverend father, [unclear] in this most happy age, in which so many illustrious men, and men of no obscure fame, have appeared everywhere in Germany, among whom you are one: you who have made Latin, Hebrew, and Greek letters richer among the Germans; among whom you have such power and shine so brightly that I am in doubt, and waver like Terence’s Chremes, whether I should compare you with anyone or prefer you to all. But I do not doubt thoughtlessly, since we behold in you not one virtue or three, but all of them firmly gathered and massed together, as though we were watching a most seemly spectacle.
