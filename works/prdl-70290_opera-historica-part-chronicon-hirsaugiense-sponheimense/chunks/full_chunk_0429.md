*Chronicle of Trithemius*

...the times were more dangerous. For, as we have already said, when pestilence was raging throughout the whole world, it consumed more than half of the human race in three years. In that season there died in the town of Kreuznach, which is quite small, more than sixteen hundred people; in Mainz, more than six thousand people, so that both there and in Erfurt and in other principal cities, sometimes in one day [unclear] people were placed in one pit, in such confused order that they could scarcely be kept to a fixed number. Everywhere there was fear, everywhere lamentation and the image of death; and people went about as if dead and out of their minds, struck by excessive fear of sudden death, which each person saw set before his eyes.

The cause of this calamity was laid upon the Jews, namely that they had poisoned the springs and the air; from this there followed a very severe persecution of them by Christians. For in different parts of the world, when such an opinion against them had been made public among Christians, some were miserably suffocated, others cast headlong into the water, others drowned, others beheaded, others burned, others pierced through with swords and lances, and others killed by various kinds of torments. Some of them, seeing themselves set in evil plight, first killed their children together with their wives, lest they fall into the hands of Christians cruelly raging, and then, setting fire beneath, destroyed themselves by burning along with their houses and dwellings. This persecution of the Jews, beginning in the year noted above, lasted two years. Whether it was carried out justly or unjustly is not for us to judge, although it does not seem likely to us that they could have infected all the springs of the whole world with poison, even if they had wished to. But, mindful of the Greek proverb, [unclear], we leave the judgment to be borne by God, who can neither be deceived nor knows how to deceive. In Cologne also, in the same year, for the same cause that we have mentioned, on the vigil of St. Bartholomew the Apostle, the Jews burned themselves up by setting fire to their houses; all the rest were expelled from that city.

*1350.*

In this year, which was the ninth of our abbot Wilhelm, when the new chapel which he had founded in the ambulatory had already been completed, he had it consecrated in honor of blessed Mary ever virgin, on the 5th day of the month of September, by Rudolph, bishop of Salona, vicar in pontificals of Gerlach, archbishop of Mainz, as appears in his letters, which follow:

To all who shall see these presents, Rudolph, by divine mercy bishop of Salona, vicar of the reverend father and lord Gerlach, by the grace of God archbishop of Mainz, greeting in the Lord. Since in the year of the Lord 1350, on the Nones of September, we dedicated for divine worship to be performed there forever the chapel with the altar of the blessed and glorious Mary ever virgin in the ambulatory of the monastery of St. [Sponheim?], of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, we have granted to all the faithful of Christ, penitent and confessed, who devoutly visit the aforesaid altar with the chapel, indulgences of fourteen days for criminal sins and of one carena once in the year; and as often as the sacrament is brought to the sick, throughout the whole parish, to endure hereafter. Given and enacted in the year of the Lord as above.

And because, as we have said above, all the monks in this monastery at that time were proprietors and openly possessed money as if lawfully, abbot Wilhelm endowed the aforesaid chapel from the goods of the monastery, and with the consent of his brothers assigned it, with all its revenues, to the office of the priorship: that is to say, that the prior for the time being should celebrate two masses in it each week and should receive all its [pectentes?], as appears in the letters drawn up concerning this, whose date is the year of the Lord 1350, on the day of the Epiphany of the Lord. These things were done before his resignation. The consecration of the chapel, however, was carried out afterwards, after the resignation, under lord abbot Philipp, as can be plainly seen from calculation of the days.

Accordingly, in the aforesaid year Wilhelm, abbot of this monastery, weighed down by age and infirmity, voluntarily and freely resigned his abbacy into the hands of the convent on the second feria before the feast of St. Lawrence the Martyr; and in the same year, on the fifth day before the Kalends of October, he died and was buried in the middle of the church which he had founded. He was a pious and good man, who did many good things for this monastery and enlarged its courts, possessions, revenues, and rents. For, as we have said, he purchased with money the court in Blenich and the manor in Bingen as property of the monastery; he also acquired the court in Boekenau, together with certain other goods from his own patrimony, as a perpetual possession for the monastery. In return for these benefactions which he showed to the monastery in his lifetime, the brothers who were then there promised him, while he was still living, a perpetual anniversary to be performed four times a year after his death, on which, after the manner of canons, they were accustomed to divide among themselves portions which they call presences.
