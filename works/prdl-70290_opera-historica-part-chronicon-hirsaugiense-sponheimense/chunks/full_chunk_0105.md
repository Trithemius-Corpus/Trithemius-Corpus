His manner of life was an example to all; in his character the highest religion, true wisdom, and perfect charity shone forth. By the appointment of Abbot William, Diethericus, a monk, a man worthy of every honor, succeeded this holy man in the priorship of this monastery. He was afterward made abbot of the monastery of Petershausen in Constance, as we have said above, where by word and example he called back the flocks entrusted to him by God to the paths of righteousness. After him the holy father William appointed as prior Otto, a monk, a venerable old man, mature in character and conduct and worthy of reverence by all, who was prior for a few months indeed, but cellarer of the monastery for many years. For in arranging temporal affairs and domestic matters he was vigorous with the greatest foresight; hence it came about that, while he wisely disposed all outward things, the holy father was more freely intent on leisure within. And although, by reason of care for the household, he was compelled with Martha to be anxious about many things, nevertheless he did not neglect Mary’s spiritual leisure, but always cared for temporal things in such a way that he did not omit the holy exercises of spiritual life. After him the holy father appointed as prior Gebhard, a man of noble birth and, in all matters to be done, admirable for exceptional prudence; he also succeeded to the abbacy of this monastery, as we shall say later, and of his character and life we shall speak in its proper place.

*1089.*

In the twentieth year of Abbot William, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1089, Sigewinus, archbishop of Cologne, died; Hermann the third, surnamed the Rich, succeeded him in order.

After the death of Dietmar, bishop of Worms, which occurred in the year of the Lord 1085, in the [unclear] indiction, Eppo was ordained bishop and flourished in these times; many wonderful things are told about him, and he presided in his episcopate for not a few years. The emperor celebrated his marriage at Cologne, taking as wife a certain widow of Margrave Udo, named Praxedis, daughter of the king of the Rus’, by whom he received a son named Henry, who after his father ruled, Conrad having been repudiated, and a daughter named Agnes. Then, departing to Aachen, he caused his elder son Conrad to be anointed king; nevertheless he later repudiated him after Henry the fifth had been born.

In this year there was a very great pestilence in the western part of Lotharingia, where many, as a fire arose within and consumed their inward parts, rotted away, their limbs eaten off and blackening like coals, and either died miserably or, their hands or feet having rotted, were left maimed for a still more miserable life. Many others, distorted by contraction of the sinews, were tormented by a miserable kind of torture. There were great earthquakes in different places of Europe.

*1090.*

In the twenty-first year of Abbot William, which was the year of the Lord 1090, the emperor entered Italy again and remained there for almost seven years. He surrounded Florence with a siege and committed many crimes.
