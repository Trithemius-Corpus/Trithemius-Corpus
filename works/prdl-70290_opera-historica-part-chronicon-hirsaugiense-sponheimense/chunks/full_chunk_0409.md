*Concerning Henry, the twelfth abbot of this monastery, who presided for two years, one month, and six days, and concerning his very few deeds.*

*Henry becomes the twelfth abbot of this monastery.*

*Again a division was made between the abbot and the convent.*

*The books of the library are destroyed.*

After the death of Lord Wilicho, the eleventh abbot of this monastery, the brothers, assembling at the appointed place and invoking the grace of the Holy Spirit, elected as abbot Henry, a monk of this convent, born at Kreuznach. Confirmed by Henry, archbishop of Mainz, he received the gift of blessing from his vicar in pontifical matters, Brother Albert, bishop of [Ibingen?], at Mainz in the church of the Friars Preachers, on the first Sunday after the octave of the feast of Easter, and he presided over this monastery for two years, one month, and six days. In his time the discipline of regular observance was very greatly lacking, and the monks again usurped private property for themselves, and a division of revenues was made between the abbot himself and the convent, to the grave loss of this monastery. This lasted until the most recent reform, which was made from the monastery of Saint James near Mainz in the year 1369; and the substance of the monastery, in the course of time greatly diminished, was at last almost entirely dissipated. For when Abbot Wilicho died, who had continued the reform begun by Dietlub, there was no one to care for the integrity of monastic institution; and after the general division the monks began, each one also for himself, to have private property, and to spread out into every license without anyone’s prohibition. They shamefully destroyed the library, from the beginning of the monastery’s foundation filled at different times with many volumes, selling very precious volumes for a low price, so that they could satisfy their [unclear] pleasures. This Henry, moreover, was, as has been said, born at Kreuznach, sprung from humble kin; his father Albert was cupbearer of the counts of Sponheim. Sent to the schools, he devoted himself not to letters but to vanity, and therefore remained an unlearned man. At the petition of the counts, however, he was received into the monastic state of this monastery under Lord Wilicho; and after his death he was ordained abbot, more out of regard for his father than out of fear of God. He did nothing for the utility of this monastery, but on the contrary brought many losses upon it instead.

For first of all, what was the gravest of all losses, he allowed the discipline of regular observance to perish, introduced private property among the monks, and instituted a division of revenues between himself and the convent, contrary to monastic institution. For in the first year of his rule he alienated from the monastery a large part of the groves and woods between Wiler and Argenschwang, and in all his actions he was always incautious and exceedingly negligent.

In the first year of Abbot Henry, Wernher, the nineteenth prior of this monastery, was made provost of the nuns in Ibingen, at the command of Henry, archbishop of Mainz. After him Philip of Sponheim, called Meyswein, was appointed prior; and he was the twentieth in the order of priors of this monastery, and presided in that office of prior for twelve years. Then, after Wilhelm, he was elected abbot, as we shall say in its proper place.
