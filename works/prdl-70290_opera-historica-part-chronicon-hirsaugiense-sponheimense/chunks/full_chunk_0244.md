*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

... took as wife Mary, of the duke of Brabant, a very beautiful woman; by her he had no children, but killed her in the third year of Abbot Volpold, because of suspicion of adultery, as we shall say there in its own place and order. But his brother Henry took as wife Elizabeth, daughter of Hungary, niece through her brother of St. Elizabeth of Marburg; by her he begot three sons, Otto, afterwards king of Hungary, Louis, and Stephen, dukes of Bavaria. We shall mention them below in order; they divided Bavaria among themselves.

*1246.*

In the second current year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year 1246 of the Lord’s Nativity, after Emperor Frederick had been deposed, as we have said, Conrad, the younger son, usurped the empire for himself. Against him Henry Raspe, landgrave of Hesse, son of Saint Elizabeth, elected king by certain princes, went forth to war, defeated him near Frankfurt, and drove him into Italy.

In the same year Ludolph, bishop of Worms, died, and was buried with great pomp and honor before the high altar in the convent of nuns called Nonnenmünster, which he had taken away from our order. After his death the canons of Worms unanimously elected as bishop Cuno, dean of the greater church of Mainz, a good man and worthy of the honor of the episcopate. After he had presided for twenty days, he was taken off by death, perfected in a short time, and fulfilled many times, lest malice should change his understanding. When he had died, Richard, a native of Trier, was elected bishop and presided for nearly fifteen years, a good man and provident governor of his church, who did many good things in his time.

*1247.*

In the third year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year 1247 of the Lord’s Nativity, Louis, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, had his wife Mary, strongly suspected of the crime of adultery, killed at Werd in Swabia; she died slain with remarkable severity, without children. In satisfaction for so great a crime committed, he afterwards had the monastery of Fürstenfeld built in Bavaria, which he magnificently endowed with many goods and revenues.

In this same year the aforesaid Louis, count palatine and duke, built from the foundations the very strong castle called Friedberg, for the defense of the duchy of Bavaria against the bishop of Augsburg. After this he took a second wife, Anna, daughter of the king of Poland; by her he begot three sons, Louis, duke of Bavaria, Rudolph, count palatine of the Rhine, and the other Louis, afterwards Roman emperor. Of these we shall speak below in order.

*1248.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Volpold, and the year 1248 of the Lord’s Nativity, Conrad, archbishop of Cologne, a rich and magnanimous man, began to build that great and distinguished greater church of Cologne, laying the first stone on the vigil of the Assumption of Blessed Mary ever Virgin. Although that work takes increase daily by the labors of craftsmen, and has now for two hundred and fifty-four years been [unclear] without interruption [unclear], he himself also being a good man.

In the same year a very great famine oppressed Germany and killed many.

*1249.*

In the following year, which was the fifth of Abbot Volpold and the year 1249 of the Lord’s Nativity, in the seventh indiction, Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, died on the Ides of March: a man of great works, who magnificently enlarged his church in honor and possessions, and amid so many evils, so many wars, and so many dangers by which the empire was tottering, ruled it most wisely. To him in [unclear] ... as we have said, a few years before, by certain princes, at the command of Pope Innocent, [unclear] came. He died of a flux of the belly and was buried in his town of Eisenach. After Henry’s death, William, count of Holland, son of Count Floris, was elected king of the Romans by certain princes at the village of Wörnich, against Emperor Frederick and his son Conrad, in the presence of Pietro Capocci, cardinal deacon of the Golden Veil and legate of the Apostolic See. When he was elected, he was still a young man of twenty years, and he reigned nearly seven years; although he did not obtain the crown of the empire, nevertheless [unclear].
