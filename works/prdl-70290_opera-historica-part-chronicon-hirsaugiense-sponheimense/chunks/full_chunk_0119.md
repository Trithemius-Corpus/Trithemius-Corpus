*MONASTERY OF HIRSAU*

...having first pursued the Antiochenes and then the Jerusalemites with the remaining cities, with our Lord Jesus Christ assisting them, they took them.

There Godfrey himself was elected king by the people of God; after he died some years later and was buried beside the Sepulchre of the Lord, his brother Baldwin succeeded to the kingdom and greatly enlarged the borders of the Christians round about.

In the same year of the Lord, namely 1066, the abbatial monastery of our order, Reinhausen, in Saxony, in the diocese of Mainz, not far from Gottingen, was founded and suitably endowed for the support of monks.

The first abbot of this monastery, called Reinhard, was a learned and very holy man, who was renowned for many miracles in life and after death; through his merit, down to the present day, Almighty God is said to cure in that same place the disease which they call epilepsy, for those who devoutly invoke him.

This same most holy abbot Reinhard wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he showed the excellence of his talent to those present. Of these the chief are said to be commentaries on the prophet Ezekiel, on the prophet Isaiah, on the Psalms, on the epistles of Paul the Apostle, also a metrical work on Job, and certain other things which do not now come to my memory.

At this time the venerable abbot Gebhard, having compassion on the labors of the brothers in drawing water from the river, after taking counsel with highly experienced architects, caused an aqueduct to be built at no small expense through hidden underground channels, and brought an abundance of water into all the workshops of the monastery.

*1097.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Gebhard, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1097, the emperor Henry IV, on the holy day of the Epiphany of the Lord, made his younger son Henry king at Aachen, after rejecting Conrad, his elder son, whom he had previously caused to be crowned. And unless this Conrad himself, overtaken by an untimely death, had died before his father, Henry V could not have attained the empire.

*1098.*

In the following year Ruthard, archbishop of Mainz, who refused to favor the excommunicated king Henry, lost his favor, and, withdrawing into Thuringia, remained there for some years as though placed in exile. Around this time the Cistercian order began, and its first monastery at Citeaux was founded in this very year; in the course of time it grew greatly, and was wonderfully increased both in number and in wealth. Many writings are found here and there concerning its beginning and progress.

Also around this time, not far from Worms, for many days and nights a very great multitude of armed horsemen and foot-soldiers appeared, as if about to proceed to a meeting for an agreed conference or to war; and now running this way, as if in companies, about the ninth hour of the night they returned to the neighboring mountain from which they were accustomed to come forth.

At length, on a certain night, a monk of the monastery of Limburg, which lay not far from the mountain from which they were accustomed to come out, took certain others with him and went to the place of the mountain; and, fortifying himself with the sign of the holy cross, he stoutly adjured those coming out of the mountain, by the power of the holy and undivided Trinity, to say who they were. One of the company answered him: We are not a phantom nor a living troop of soldiers, but the souls of men once in this world serving a prince of earthly men, slain not many years ago in this place. The arms, dress, and horses, which while we lived were instruments of sin for us, are even now after death certain signs of torment upon us. Everything that you now discern around us is wholly fiery for us, although you see our fire very little. And when the monk asked whether they could be helped by human beings, the spirit replied: We can be helped by fasts and prayers, and especially by the offering of the body and blood of Christ, and we ask for it. When this had been said, all that multitude of spirits cried out as if with one voice three times: Pray for us, pray for us. Soon all dissolved into fire [unclear] emitted a noise.

*1099.*

Also in these times the monastery of Bursfelde in Saxony, in the diocese of Mainz, was founded and built in honor of Saints Thomas the Apostle and Nicholas the Bishop. In the eighth year of Abbot Gebhard, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1099, there was great mortality in Germany.
