Therefore in the same year, in the month of December, the abbot of St. Matthias came, supported by the authority of the Council of Basel and of our whole provincial chapter; discharging his commission, as is customary, he found that the report to the chapter had been true, and compelled the abbot to promise amendment in all things. At Kreuznach, before the friends of Gobelin himself, he heard the accounting for the receipts and expenditures of the three-year period, where he composed peace and concord between the abbot and the convent as best he could. The expenses of this visitation, accounting, and settlement at Kreuznach exceeded the sum of 50 florins, as we find from the registers of the procurators, and yet no profit, or only little, came from it.

*The abbot of St. Matthias visits here by authority of the council.*

In the aforesaid year also, on the day of St. Dionysius the martyr, the abbot Gobelin appointed a certain Johannes, pastor, provider, cellarer, and procurator of the church in Sulm, over this monastery, and entrusted to him the governance of the whole monastery in temporal affairs; and he himself, in his usual fashion, stayed with the count in Trarbach and was very seldom in the monastery.

*A secular provider appointed by the abbot.*

In the aforesaid year also, on the twelfth day of the month of October, Ernest, duke of Bavaria, caused a most beautiful young woman, the daughter of a certain barber of Augsburg, whom his son Albert loved with excessive love and kept as a concubine, to be thrown from the bridge at Straubing and drowned in the Danube, while his son was far away and knew nothing of the matter. When he returned and heard of his father's cruelty committed against the mistress most beloved to him, from excessive grief of heart he first fell into madness; afterward, having returned to himself, he wept most bitterly, and swore that he would spare neither father nor [unclear] until he had avenged the innocent death of his beloved. Great discord therefore followed between the son, the father, and the nobles who had cooperated in the young woman's death, which at last could scarcely be quieted with the greatest difficulty. Over the woman's tomb, at the son's compulsion, the father founded a new chapel, in which one Mass was to be celebrated every day forever for her soul. There were those who said that Duke Albert had contracted a secret marriage with her, and that his father, moved for this reason because he had disgraced the nobility of his lineage with a person of humble stock, had given the order that she be killed. All cried out that the father had acted wickedly in ordering so beautiful, honorable, and innocent a young woman, most beloved to his son above all others, to be killed because of love, since many formerly most noble kings and princes, overcome by virtue or by love, had taken wives for themselves from the humbler class of citizens or peasants without disgrace.

*The miserable deed of Ernest, duke of Bavaria.*

In the same year also the winter was extremely harsh for three months, so that trees, sown crops, and vines perished in many places from the excessive cold. Wine too was at an altogether dear price, because, except in Alsace, little grew anywhere, and it was very sour.

*An extremely harsh winter.*

*1436.*

In the fourth year of Gobelin our abbot, Johannes, the last count of Sponheim, granted the altar of St. Pancratius the martyr in the castle of Sponheim, with all its revenues, rents, and appurtenances, to be incorporated into this monastery with the right of patronage, which he openly renounced, for the increase of the monks' prebends, as is evident from the letters which he gave to the convent concerning this matter. Their date is on the day of St. Martin the bishop, in the year of the Lord 1436. Theoderic, archbishop of Mainz, afterward confirmed this donation, through Johannes de Lysura, doctor of decrees and his vicar in spiritual matters, at the instance of the abbot and convent of this monastery, as is evident in the letters drawn up concerning it, which were dated in the year of the Lord 1439, on Friday in the month of March.

*Count Johannes offers the altar of the castle of Sponheim to this monastery.*

In the aforesaid year of Abbot Gobelin, Albert, duke of Bavaria, of whom mention was made in the previous year, took as wife Anna, daughter of the duke of Brunswick, and celebrated the wedding at Munich.

*Albert, duke of Bavaria, takes a wife.*

In this same year good wine grew in great quantity and was sold for a low price.
