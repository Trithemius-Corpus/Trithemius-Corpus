A pitiable calamity: for as soon as anyone had been infected with this plague, by his very breath alone he also infected others. At this time the human race, especially in Germany, was afflicted by three plagues: namely pestilence, which laid low almost countless thousands of people throughout nearly the whole world. The cause of this calamity was ascribed to the Jews, because they had infected the springs and the air with poison. Hence there followed a very severe persecution of them by the Christians. For in various regions of the world, when this belief of the Christians had been spread abroad against them, some were miserably suffocated, others thrown down, others drowned, others beheaded, others burned, others pierced through with swords and lances, others killed by various kinds of torments. Some of them, seeing that they had fallen into evil, first killed their children and wives, lest they be handed over into the hands of Christians raging cruelly to be tortured, and then, setting fire to their houses, willingly destroyed themselves by burning. This persecution against the Jews, beginning in the year already noted, lasted two years and consumed many thousands of them in all the provinces. The second plague was a flux of blood, which also carried off a huge multitude of people by an unheard-of kind of torment. The third was the burning of the sacred fire, as that disease is commonly called; it raged with such savagery that whomever it attacked, it at once utterly consumed the flesh down to the bones; nor was it hostile only to the living, but, attacking also the unburied corpses of the dead, it consumed the flesh before they could be buried. These were times full of calamities and overflowing with such great dangers that, except for the universal flood, no one has written of worse.

At the same time a certain new heresy arose, taking its beginning in Hungary, which in a short time was striving to infect all Germany with its poison. Ignorant, rustic, and unlearned people, under the pretext of penance, which they imposed on themselves without the judgment of a priest, men and women mixed together, walking half-naked, carried wooden crosses in their hands.
