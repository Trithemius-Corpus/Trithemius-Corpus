*Chronicle of Trithemius*

They were holding Abbot Gobelinus in Winterburgk, for almost the space of a year, in honorable custody, desiring to have from him, as from the secretary of the deceased count, not only an account of the deposit, but also certain treasures, which, as they asserted, had been entrusted to him by the count. Throughout all that time Abbot Gobelinus stayed in a public inn, a prisoner at Winterburgk by command of the princes, with one very strong servant appointed to guard him; and indeed he could walk about within the walls wherever he pleased, his guard always following his steps, but he did not dare go out of the village. At last, after the months of his captivity had passed, he gave a lawful account of the deposit, a sufficient reckoning, to the princes' counsellors who had been sent to him; concerning the suspicion of the treasures, he confidently asserted that he knew nothing of them. Thus at last he was released and returned to the monastery; no execution of the will followed.

In the same year also, on the last day of December, there died the illustrious and most Christian prince, lord Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, son of the late Rupert, king of the Romans, called the Bearded, or also the blind duke, because after his return from the Holy Land he incurred illnesses, among them blindness: a good, pious, holy man, who had been protector and defender of the Council of Constance, and who kept Pope John XXIII, after his deposition, when handed over to him by the council, for three years in his castle at Mannheim. He left behind him three sons, the first of whom was Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, prince-elector, a pious, just, devout man, who had taken as wife Margaret, daughter of Amadeus, Duke of Savoy, afterward Pope Felix, elected in the Council of Basel against Eugenius; by her he begot Philip, the most unconquered prince, ruling far and wide today, of whom we shall make mention afterward in his proper place. The second son of Louis the Bearded was that most powerful Duke Frederick, who after his brother's death was guardian of the most illustrious Philip; he did not take a wife, and of him also we shall speak afterward. The third was Rupert, Archbishop of Cologne after Theodoric; of him we shall make mention below.

Also in the aforesaid year, in the month of December, Sigismund, emperor of the Romans and king of Hungary and Bohemia, died in Moravia: a most Christian prince, lover of God, defender of the clergy, advocate of the poor, buried at Alba Regalis with great honor, not without a reputation for holiness.

Adolf also, Duke of Berg and Jülich, died in the same year, in the month of December. A great famine [unclear]. Matilda, [wife?] of the Count Palatine and Duke of Bavaria, grandmother of Philip our most illustrious prince, died on the twelfth day of May: a devout woman, holy toward God and most pious toward the poor. In the same year, around the middle of Lent, the prince-electors assembled at Frankfurt and unanimously elected to the rule of the Romans Albert, Duke of Austria, son-in-law of the deceased Emperor Sigismund, a vigorous, pious, good, holy man, who reigned only two years. After the death of his father-in-law he obtained many kingdoms, duchies, and counties by hereditary right, peacefully and without any disturbance, such as the kingdom of Hungary, Bohemia, Dalmatia, Croatia, the duchy of Moravia, and many other lands; and among all the princes of Germany he was the more powerful and the wealthier. He was elected, as we have said, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of April, in the middle of Lent: a prince of whom the world was not worthy; therefore, made perfect in a short time, he fulfilled many times.

In the aforesaid year Rabanus of Helmstatt, Archbishop of Trier and Bishop of Speyer, weighed down by old age and seeing that he was less equal to the governance of so great a pontificate, followed the counsel of certain persons and took John, Bishop of Liège, as coadjutor of the church of Trier, against the will of many of the chapter; hence the church of Trier, as many asserted, suffered no small loss, inconvenience, and harm. In the same year also, on the first day of July, Ernest, Duke of Bavaria, died, leaving one son, Albert by name, of whom mention has been made above.
