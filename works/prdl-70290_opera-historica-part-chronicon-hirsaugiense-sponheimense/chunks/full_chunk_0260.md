*Chronicle of Jo. Trithemius* [unclear]. Boniface VIII succeeded him and presided for 8 years, 9 months, and 17 days; because of his scheming Celestine had resigned.

In these times also a great dispute arose between Philip, king of France, and Edward, king of England; on that occasion a fierce war was begun between them. The king of England summoned Adolf of Nassau, king of the Romans, to his aid for one hundred thousand marks of silver, on this condition: that the money be divided among the princes of the empire and that, by common agreement, they assist him against the French. But King Adolf, after receiving the money, held it back for his own uses, despising them. Hence, spurned by them also, and unable to keep his promises to the king of the English, he incurred great disgrace.

*Siegfried.*

*The count of Berg is captured by treachery.*

In the same year Siegfried, archbishop of Cologne, after seven years of his captivity, was freed [unclear] of the whole kingdom. Not long afterward the same archbishop, using craft and industry, against the oath of alliance, seized the same count, who feared no such thing, and consigned him to harsh imprisonment until death. The count, captured and detained in very great affliction, freely and of his own accord promised that he would restore to the archbishop everything that he had extorted from him, and would add greater things for his liberation; but he was not heard by the pontiff. Rather, he received from him this reproachful reply: “St. Peter has the means by which, from your goods and promises, he may maintain his servants; therefore you shall not be released while I live, but you shall undergo death in prison, and you shall learn how you ought to hold an archbishop captive.” And so the count died in custody.

In the same year also John, duke of Brabant, was killed in a tournament.

*1297.*

In the 3rd year of Abbot Gotfrid, the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1296, Manegold, bishop of Würzburg, died. Andrew of Gundelfingen succeeded him and presided for 22 years; he too had many disputes with his citizens, from whom he suffered various injuries.

*Andrew becomes bishop of Würzburg.*

Count Florentius of Holland is killed.

In the same year Florentius, count of Holland, in the 42nd year of his rule, betrayed by his own men, was cruelly killed at the hands of his adversaries on the third day before the Kalends of July. John, his son, succeeded him; he had taken as wife Elizabeth, daughter of Edward, king of the English, by whom he had no children. He ruled Holland only three years and six months, and waged many wars and battles.

In the same year also Pope Boniface VIII transferred John, bishop of Utrecht, to the church of Toul, and appointed in his place as pontiff Wilhelm, born at Mechelen, auditor of causes in the Roman Curia. He presided for 5 years amid many tribulations, and at length was killed in war.

*Frisians killed.*

*1298.*

In the 4th year of Abbot Gotfrid, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1297, John, count of Holland, having gathered a large army of his own men and Zeelanders, entered Frisia, and on the sixth day before the Kalends of April, after battle had been joined with the Frisians near Alkmaar, killed 4,000 of them.

*King Adolf of the Romans.*

*Conspiracy of the princes against Adolf.*

In these times also Adolf, king of the Roman Empire, [brought] many calamities and dissensions into the land among those who had peace. For at his command Hermann, count of Geroldseck, and a certain other count, appointed provincial prefects in Alsace, were exceedingly troublesome to Conrad of Lichtenberg, bishop of Strasbourg, and to all the people of Alsace, and inflicted many injuries, insults, and losses. Hence, provoked to impatience, the aforesaid bishop, the citizens of Strasbourg, the counts of Ochsenstein, the landgrave of Alsace, the count of Freiburg, the count of Zweibrücken, and the count of Hohenlohe, with many other princes, counts, nobles, and peoples, conspired unanimously against King Adolf; and, despising his commands and orders, [unclear], until, when many princes had joined in the same opinion, they at last deposed him from the kingdom and deprived him at once of life and honor, as we shall say in its place.
