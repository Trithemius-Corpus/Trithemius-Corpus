*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*859.*

In the sixth year of Gerung, abbot of Hirsau, Huchertus, formerly a duke, a monk, and abbot of the monastery of Saint Richarius, brother of Queen Thiedberga, flourished: a wise and energetic man, to whom Lotharius entrusted the duchy between Mount Jura and Mount Veneris to govern. But when Lotharius later cast off Queen Thiedberga, Huchertus’s sister, after taking the concubine Wuldrada in addition, he found the abbot his enemy; through his action he was at last compelled to receive his sister back.

*860.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Gerung, which was the year of the Lord 860, and the fourth of Emperor Louis, in the city of Mainz the devil was plainly exercising his wickedness and hatred toward man. He hurled stones from the windows of houses at people, struck the walls strongly by night and by day as if with certain hammers, knocked at the doors of many people, and from there, speaking openly, everywhere disclosed thefts and sowed great discord among men. Then he stirred up the minds of all against one man, as if for that man’s sin the others were suffering such things; he set fire to that man’s crops, heaped together into one place. Wherever that man entered, at once the house was set on fire by that demon, so that now no place remained for him to stay except in the fields. Because of this, while priests were conducting litanies and sprinkling blessed water, the enemy of human nature for some time ceased from throwing stones. When they withdrew, the devil, shouting horribly, said that while the blessed water was being sprinkled he had hidden under the cope of one priest, whose name he stated, as if with a familiar friend; he accused him publicly of having lain with the steward’s daughter. And so it persisted continuously for three years, until in that place it consumed many buildings by fire. It was heard shouting, howling, speaking, knocking, striking the walls, and throwing stones at people, but absolutely nothing was seen. Yet elsewhere we read that these things happened not in the city of Mainz, but in a certain village of its diocese.

In this same seventh year of Gerung, in the eighth indiction, Probus, priest and monk of Saint Alban’s, died at Mainz, a holy and most learned man, whose conduct and zeal wonderfully adorned that same church of Mainz.

In this same year Saint Meginradus, formerly a monk of the monastery of Reichenau and a hermit in the Black Forest, was martyred by night in his cell by two robbers, who thought that they would find a large sum of money with him, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of February. His body, brought back to the monastery of Reichenau and buried, shone for a long time with countless miracles. Moreover, the thieves, betrayed by two ravens which Saint Meginradus had reared, and with these repeatedly pursuing them, were broken on the wheel according to the laws of the country. In these days Egilo flourished, formerly a monk of Sens, afterwards archbishop after Wanlo, a man conspicuous for every goodness, a worthy shepherd of Christ’s sheep.

In the same year Ego, abbot of Prum, voluntarily resigned his abbey, and Ansbald succeeded him. [unclear]

At that time a great famine and mortality raged through the kingdom of the Franks; it converted very many to the love of the heavenly fatherland.

In the same times Mico, monk of Saint Richarius, flourished, a very studious and learned man, who wrote many varied works, excellently composed both in meter and in prose. Among these we have read a book of various epigrams, also excerpts from the poets, a book of riddles, and certain other things. Notgerus, formerly a monk of Saint Gall and bishop of Liege, is held to have been famous in these times among ecclesiastical writers; he first devised the use of sequences, which Pope Nicholas I consented should be sung in the church on this side of the Alps. The disciple of this Notgerus was Salomon, formerly a monk and afterwards bishop of Constance, who himself left to posterity some monuments of a distinguished talent. Wandelbertus also, monk of the monastery of Prum under the venerable abbot Ansbald, flourished at this same time; among other little works of his talent, he comprised in two books the life and miracles of Saint Goar, priest and hermit, resting beside the Rhine in the parish of Trier.

*861.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Gerung, Waldo, abbot of Fulda, was deposed by Emperor Louis, and Thuode succeeded him.

*863.*
