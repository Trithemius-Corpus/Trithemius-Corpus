*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*iii*

In the same year Pope Gelasius died at Cluny and was buried there; [Guido], archbishop of Vienne, succeeded him, was called Calixtus II, and sat for six years. Holding a synod in the city of Rheims at the feast of St. Luke the Evangelist, he, with the bishops and abbots, excommunicated the emperor. Adelgot, archbishop of Magdeburg, died; Rutger succeeded him. The excommunicated emperor, in the archbishop’s absence, was honorably received by the people of Cologne. Therefore the divine offices were interdicted to them by that bishop. An assembly of the emperor and princes was held at Mainz on the feast of Sts. Peter and Paul. In the same year Otbert, bishop of Liège, died; Frederick succeeded him for one year and some months.

It pleased us to insert these matters into the work for the knowledge of posterity. Thus therefore [unclear] between the pope and the emperor:

I, Calixtus, bishop, servant of the servants of God, grant to you, beloved son Henry, by the grace of God august emperor of the Romans, that elections of bishops and abbots of the Teutonic kingdom, which pertain to [unclear], be held in your presence, without simony and without any violence. If any discord should arise between the parties, then by the counsel or judgment of the metropolitan and provincials you are to give assent or aid to the sounder party. The elected man, however, shall receive the regalia from you by the sceptre, and shall do what he rightly owes you from these. But from the other parts of the empire, once consecrated, he shall receive the regalia from you by the sceptre within six months, and what is rightly owed to you from these [unclear], excepting all things which are known to pertain to the Roman Church. Concerning matters about which you make complaint to me and seek aid, I shall give you aid according to the duty of my office. I give true peace to you and to all who are or were on your side in the time of this discord.

When the emperor had received these letters from the pope, he gave him letters of the same tenor:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Henry, by the grace of God august emperor of the Romans, for the love of God and of the holy Roman Church and of lord Pope Calixtus, and for the remedy of my souls, remit to God and to God’s holy apostles Peter and Paul and to the holy catholic churches all investiture by ring and staff, and I concede that in all churches which are in my kingdom or empire canonical election and free consecration ought to take place. The possessions and regalia of Blessed Peter which from the beginning of this discord down to the present day, whether in the time of my father or also in my own, have been taken away, and which I have, I restore to that Roman Church. Those of the churches and princes and others, clerics as well as laymen, which have been lost in this war, [I shall restore] by the counsel of the princes and of others, clerics as well as laymen. And by the counsel or justice of the princes, what I have I shall restore; what I do not have I shall faithfully help to be restored. And I give true peace to lord Pope Calixtus and to the holy Roman Church and to all who are or were on his side. And in matters in which the holy Roman Church seeks aid from me, I shall faithfully help; and concerning matters about which it makes complaint to me, I shall do the justice owed to it.

All these things were done with the consent and counsel of the princes, whose names are written below: Adelbert, archbishop of Mainz; Frederick, archbishop of Cologne; H., bishop of Regensburg; Otto, bishop of Bamberg; Ulrich, bishop of Constance; H., abbot of Fulda; Duke Henry; Duke Frederick; Margrave Diebold; Godfrey, archbishop of [unclear]; H., bishop of Augsburg; Godehard, bishop of Utrecht; Count Ulrich; Margrave Engelbert; Count Berengar; Theodoric Frederick, count palatine; Otto, count palatine; Margrave Engelbert; Count Berengar; Theodoric Frederick, count palatine; Otto, count palatine; Engelbert, margrave-count; Berengar, margrave-counts; and many others.
