For I know that the prayer of one who humbles himself penetrates the clouds and will not return empty, as our Savior promises in the holy Gospel: “Ask,” he says, “and it shall be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives; he who seeks finds; and to him who knocks it shall be opened.” For as James, the apostle of Christ, says: the constant prayer of the righteous man avails much. Nor, when you pour out prayer for us, will you lack benefit, as Pope Gregory says: whoever strives to intercede for others, rather aids himself out of charity, and deserves to be heard more quickly for himself, the more devoutly he intercedes for others. For to pray for oneself, as Chrysostom bears witness, is necessity; but to pray for others belongs to brotherly charity. Sweeter before God is the prayer which not necessity sends forth, but which the charity of brotherhood commends. And the holy bishop Ambrose says: if you ask only for yourself, you alone will pray for yourself; but if you ask for all, all will also ask for you. Prayer, moreover, is then perfect and easily obtains what is desired when cause, tongue, heart, deed, speech, life, and the whole thought cry out, as Menander too expresses in a few words, saying: [unclear]. That is: God is the hearer of righteous prayer. But he cries to the Lord with a great voice who, even if he is silent with his tongue, nevertheless cries out perseveringly by good works. Since we know you to be such an intercessor, distinguished by purity of mind and holy in life, again and again we ask you: pray for us, and farewell.
