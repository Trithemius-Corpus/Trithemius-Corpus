Or from what dangers did he not deliver him, so that the truth of his promises might be established? All these things have this end: that all of us everywhere should display this most beautiful virtue, especially those upon whom hands have duly been laid in the churches for the office of teaching.

Moreover, although he was of a most blameless life, for he was familiarly known to me for many years, and always with the greatest zeal intent upon the divine Scriptures, so that no one could doubt that he proved himself a very pious and faithful abbot of his order, it is remarkable that he attributed so little, or almost nothing, to his own holiness before God, whereas in this age of ours even those are accustomed to boast under this name who are utterly alien from every pious and honorable manner of life, which proceeds only from living faith.

But lest I be burdensome to you by the length of my letter, I now present to you Trithemius himself, abbot of Sponheim. I trust that he will be all the more welcome to all learned and excellent men, not only because he comes forth into the light under your patronage, which is of very great weight with everyone, but also because, through your intercession, he has been granted an imperial privilege and will thus come more freely and with greater commendation into men’s hands. Nor, I think, will it deter anyone from reading so great a man that he seems to have spoken in the familiar language of his own age, and to have said some things in a manner which the more learned men of this century do not accept, since one must look not so much at the words as at the matter itself.

Therefore, since you have bound me by many kindnesses of your humanity, and, after recently having been appointed Count Palatine, have now so obliged me by sending this privilege that I can scarcely ever repay the debt, I judged it most equitable that the fruits of your labors should return first of all to you also. Farewell, most distinguished man, and I pray you to take in good part this devotion of mine toward you. I shall render greater offices of gratitude whenever God offers the occasion; to him I pray and desire that you be most highly commended. From Sélestat, on the third day of March, [1536].
