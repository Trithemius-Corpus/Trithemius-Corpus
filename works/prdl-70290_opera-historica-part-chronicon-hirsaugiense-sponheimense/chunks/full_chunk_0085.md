But he shall acknowledge that no other service, right, or benefice is granted to him for this, except this third part of the customary justice and law which the other advocates have in other free monasteries over thieves, [unclear], censual tenants, and other such matters.

And on those three court-days, on each one, one measure of wheat, one young pig, one [unclear] of wine, and the rest pertaining to this. Furthermore he likewise decreed that, unless the abbot wishes and summons him, he shall by no means approach or touch the good places of the monastery with his reckless assemblies, without cause. Nor shall he have license there, through the most presumptuous [acts?] of sub-advocates, to hold or record any court. Nor shall he [unclear] sub-advocates, nor in any way, without cause, inflict any accusation, exaction, or injury upon the monastery, the abbot, or the household. But if he has been not an advocate but rather an accuser and invader of the monastery, the abbot, with the counsel of the brothers, shall have full power to reject him and choose for himself another, more useful, from wherever he wishes.

The aforesaid count also decreed that if anyone of his posterity, or a man of whatever persons, rashly and unjustly takes away from the aforesaid cell one vineyard, one mansus, one mill, or even one serf or anything of that sort, compelled by the royal power of us and of our successors, he shall pay three talents of gold to the king’s treasury, first restoring to the church the revenues which he had [unclear]. But if any one of them, which God forbid, violently alienates [unclear] or anything in the village from it, or proves to be a manifest invader of its goods, or if he shall most presumptuously attempt to break this testament or any court pertaining to them, he shall likewise pay one hundred pounds of gold to the royal chamber; and according to the laws he shall likewise make composition for what had been taken away, so that his intention may become altogether void. He also grants to the ministers of the household of the sanctuary the law and service which the others freely have in our kingdom, so that thereafter they may serve their [unclear] in all things as before.

Over and above all these things, the often-mentioned count obtained and established an apostolic [privilege?], that one gold coin, which we call a bezant, should be paid under compulsion every year at Easter by the abbot of the aforesaid monastery to Rome, at the altar of Saint Peter, so that the statutes of this freedom and grant may remain the more permanently unshaken in this manner, and so that the aforesaid monastery may always be securely established and defended under the protection and majesty of the Roman church.
