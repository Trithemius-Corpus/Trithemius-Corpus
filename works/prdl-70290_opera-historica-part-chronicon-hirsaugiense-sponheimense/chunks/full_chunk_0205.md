*1181.*

In the following year, on the fifth day before the Nones of July, Adelgerus, the third abbot of my monastery of Sponheim, died, a good and very learned man, who greatly increased and maintained the observance of regular discipline; concerning him I have made fuller mention in the chronicle of that same monastery of mine. Baldemarus, the fourth abbot, succeeded him in the abbacy, and presided for 17 years, 2 months, and 17 days, a sufficiently good man and a lover of piety.

In the same year Pope Alexander III died; [unclear] sat for 4 years and 2 months, and was by nation a Tuscan.

*Pope Alexander died.*

In the same year also Baldwin, king of Jerusalem, defeated Saladin, king of the Egyptians, not far from Ascalon, and put him to flight.

*1182.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Conrad, Emperor Frederick entered Italy with a great army, and committed many evils against the pope and the Roman Church with sword and fire.

*1183.*

In the following year, which was the seventh of Abbot Conrad, Christian, archbishop of Mainz, died; and Conrad, whom the emperor had formerly expelled because of his obedience to the Apostolic See, returned to the see of Mainz and presided for 2 years, and retained, together with the archbishopric of Mainz, the title of bishop of Sabina, which the pope had conferred on him when he was expelled from Mainz.

*1185.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Conrad, which was the year 1185 of the Lord's Nativity, Godfridus, bishop of Wuerzburg, died while on pilgrimage overseas to the sepulchre of our Savior Jesus Christ, and was buried in Antioch. Henry III succeeded him, for 2 years.

In the same year Pope Lucius III died, by nation a Tuscan; Urban III, by nation a Lombard, succeeded him and presided for only 2 years. Emperor Frederick deprived all the cities, except Pistorium, of their agrarian jurisdiction, and accomplished many things in Italy against the rebels of the empire.

*1187.*

In the eleventh and last year of Abbot Conrad, which was the year 1188 of the Lord's Nativity, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of May, there died in the monastery of Schoenau, of the Cistercian order, in the diocese of Worms, not far from Heidelberg, a devout virgin of Christ named Hildegundis, who, having disguised her sex and presenting herself as male, had there taken the habit of a novice among the monks; and, changing her name, she called herself Joseph, and remained unknown until death. Caesarius, monk of Heisterbach, wrote her life at sufficient length in the first distinction of his Dialogues.

In the same year Frederick, emperor of the Romans, and Philip, king of the Franks, together with Richard, king of the English, and Clement, the supreme pontiff, [unclear] for the aid and recovery [unclear]. He went on certain causes and business to the cell of St. Gregory in Reichenbach; when he had arrived there, he began to be struck by frequent burning fevers, and, lying in bed, was ill there for some time. As the disease grew day by day, he turned himself wholly to the Lord with most intent mind; and, after first making humble confession and receiving the sacraments of the Church with great devotion, at last on the fifth day of the month of September he rested in the Lord with a blessed end.
