And although perhaps I could put away every grudge from my heart, nevertheless the brothers who are the authors of these evils would neither believe this very thing, nor would they easily change their depraved habits. For we know well how difficult it is to recall monks depraved by evil habit to the rectitude of newness. For the wise man says: “A young man according to his way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” For habit is likened to nature, which no one resists without difficulty, as blessed Gregory says: “Depraved habits hold him whom they have once seized, and, growing harder every day, are ended only when the sinner’s life is ended.”

There are, besides, many reasons by consideration of which I am moved to yield, but very few by which I could be induced to remain. Most of all, however, I have been moved, as I might say, into hatred of the place by the duke’s inconstancy and the rashness of his people, to such a degree that I can no longer put faith in his promises, nor believe him when he asks pardon, nor accept the favor he offers. Let the men of Sponheim henceforth live in the countryside with peasants, since it had seemed excessively grievous to them to have someone of whom even princes might boast. For the sake of seeing me, learned men, and men most highly educated in every faculty, were accustomed to come to Sponheim from almost all Germany, from Italy, Gaul, and the other adjacent kingdoms. Princes and powerful men of this age sent to me their messengers and envoys in literary matters, as the whole neighborhood knows; and I certainly neither ought nor wished to send them away unheard, or without the honor of hospitality, although several of them lawfully made satisfaction for their expenses. All those around saw these things and praised what was done. Only two monks from among my subjects, and all the lay brothers, whom in the usual word we call donati, could not look on it with an even mind; but they murmured in secret, as is the custom of such men, and by their unjust denunciations gradually stirred up against me the duke himself, who did not sufficiently value students and learned men. I shall therefore give place to wrath, that they may prosper with the peasants, they who have always despised saving doctrine. The Most High, I trust, will provide for me a place more suited to my studies; for the many labors in which I have sweated at Sponheim, he will deign to grant me rest elsewhere.

But you, dearest daughter in Christ, together with the holy virgins committed to you, stand firmly in the purpose of holiness until the end, so that, when the journey of this present life has been completed, you may deserve to be admitted with the holy virgins to the marriage-feast of the bridegroom, the eternal king. Always love with your whole heart your Lord Jesus Christ, to whom you have devoted yourself entirely, and love him above all things, who for love of you became man. The more you have loved him, the more fit you will become for entry into the kingdom of heaven, since he himself is the way by which one goes to the fatherland; he himself is the truth through which the highest good is known; he himself also is the love of God, by which all the plagues of the soul are swiftly healed, the understanding also is wondrously illuminated for knowledge of all things, and the affection is cleansed from every dustiness of worldly attachment. For whoever loves the Lord Jesus with his whole heart leaves in himself no place at all for vices and sins; nor is the devil allowed to enter the soul which is wholly occupied by divine love, because the devil thoroughly considers a soul empty of virtues more suitable for himself. Happy is that conscience in whose secret chamber, besides the love of Christ, which is wisdom, chastity, patience, purity, and justice, no other love has its place; where the heart of one who loves God never sighs even at the recollection of a human being, nor desires to see anything transitory in the world.

Let your love for God be constant and perfect, O virgin betrothed to the company of Christ, so that, if it should be necessary, you may die for love of him who for love of you deigned to die peacefully and kindly. O how sweet, pleasant, strong, and powerful is your love, Lord Jesus, which makes the mind despise eternal [unclear], keeps the conscience pure from every uncleanness, perfectly illuminates the soul of the one who loves, and most robustly strengthens the heart of the weakest human being against all adversities of this world. Therefore love God, O virgin of Christ; love wholly with your whole self, so that you may be able without labor to conquer all things and destroy sins, because charity covers a multitude of sins, makes a human being near to God, and glorious in all the adversities of the world. For it belongs to tender service and delicate combat to carry off victory over all enemies by love alone. Therefore, that you may bravely conquer all the evils of this age, stand bravely constant in the love of God.
