In the year noted above, Nicholas, the thirty-sixth prior of this monastery, was deposed for a certain necessity. For some time afterward John of Fulda was interprior, or president, a man excessively licentious; since he seemed less suitable for the preservation of discipline, he was rejected. John of Bingen was appointed the thirty-seventh prior of this place. He presided in the priorate for one year and six months; afterward, because of bodily infirmity, he had to be released from necessity, as we shall say in the following year.

*1389.*

In this year, which was the sixth of John Trithemius, our abbot, the monastery of our order of Saint Nicholas in Camberg, in the diocese of Wurzburg, with the consent and permission of Pope Innocent VIII, at the instance of apostate monks, was transferred from our order into a college of secular canons. They were made seculars, fit for their prince of perdition; from bad cloisterers they made secular priors, following with their abbot. O patience of God, O times, [unclear], how great is the license of human rashness toward all things.

In the same year Lothar of Bingen, the thirty-seventh prior of this monastery, was deposed because of continual bodily infirmities. John of Cologne was appointed the thirty-eighth prior in his place; after he had presided for two years, he was deposed at his own all too importunate insistence, and afterward was appointed confessor of the nuns in Neuburg near Heidelberg, where he lives today, for how long is uncertain.

The monastery in Ellwangen of our order, in the diocese of Augsburg, exceedingly wealthy, was transferred from our order a few years before these times, at the instance of the perverse abbot and monks, who obtained permission by false accounts, just as all the rest do and had done before.

In the year noted above, John Trithemius the abbot newly composed the following little books: one little book, *The Life of Saint Irmina, Virgin*, whose holy head we possess; one little book, *Praises of Saint Andrew the Apostle*; one little book, *Praises of Saint Joseph, Foster-father of the Lord*; one book, *Praises of Saint Benedict the Abbot*. He did begin certain other things, but did not complete them in that year. For the study which, before his entry into religion, while he was still placed in the world, he had begun, and which he had now almost neglected for seven years while intent on other things, he took up again in this year with all his strength, lest through him it should be led into oblivion; and in it he thereafter persevered with singular diligence up to the present day. He had two teachers in Greek letters, succeeding one another. The first of them was Conrad Celtis Protucius, a Franconian by nation, orator and poet laureate; from him he received the first rudiments of grammar and of Greek instruction. The second was John Capnion, otherwise Reuchlin, a Swabian by nation, born from the town of Pforzheim, a most learned man of singular grace in teaching; from him he had the fortune to be instructed in the higher precepts of the Greek language and at the same time in Hebrew. In Hebrew he had as teacher at Heidelberg a certain poor baptized Jew, whom afterward, when he had become abbot, he summoned here to himself in the monastery and supported for God's sake as long as he lived. In the aforesaid two languages he gathered together many volumes, written and printed, with great sagacity and also with the help of princes, from Italy and more remote lands, in almost every faculty; of these below, in the nineteenth year of his rule, we shall make some mention in general.

*1490.*

In the seventh year of our abbot John Trithemius the twenty-seventh provincial chapter of our order was celebrated, on the first Sunday after the octave of Easter, at Saint Giles in Nuremberg. Presiding in it were George, abbot of Saint Stephen's at Wurzburg; John, abbot of Saint Giles's at Nuremberg; Henry, abbot in Blaubeuren; and Blasius, abbot in Hirsau, monasteries of our order. They made many statutes for reform.
