*Sponheimense.*

... with the consent of the princes who were then governing the Empire, we sought from the church of Mainz that same church of ours as one, especially since our church, without the help of the church of Mainz, which until now has fostered us, could not in any way rise again; since also the said church has reformed our church in many respects, prudently redeeming the castle of that church from the hand of stronger men, in which castle a large part of our church consists; furthermore, by its power it has recovered many other possessions of the same church of ours. Since therefore, pious and merciful father, it is manifest that this was done by the deliberation and providence of prudent men, prostrate at the feet of your holiness we humbly and earnestly beseech you that, regarding our necessity [unclear], and holding our deed and our union with the church of Mainz as valid, you would deign to confirm it by the authority and mercy of the Apostolic See, etc.

When Pope Gregory had received these letters, he wrote back to Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, for this cause as follows.

*Copy of the papal letters to the archbishop of Mainz. The first bull.*

Gregory, servant of the servants of God, to the venerable brother, the archbishop of Mainz, greeting and apostolic blessing. From the representations made to us by our beloved sons, the dean and convent of the church and monastery of Lorsch, it has become known that, when the abbot of Werden [unclear] and his colleague, performing the office of visitation for the same monastery by apostolic mandate, had removed its abbot from its governance, they committed to you, in whose diocese it lies, the charge, defense, and reform of it, since it had fallen so far in spiritual and temporal matters that it was believed it could scarcely rise again except through your help. You, indeed, have devoted such solicitude to its reform that, besides other gracious advantages which it received through your diligence, you bought back a certain castle for no small sum of money. Therefore, commending your prudence in this matter to the Lord, by apostolic writings we command your steadfastness that, watching over the charge, defense, and reform of it committed to you, as you have laudably begun, you so cherish, maintain, and defend it that its disturbed condition may be brought back to its due established state, so that you may deserve to receive recompense from the Lord and to be commended by us. Given at Perugia, May 4, in the sixth year of our pontificate.

When these apostolic letters had been received, Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, not only subjected the principality of the monastery of Lorsch, its lordships, castles, towns, vassals, and dependents to his church of Mainz with authority, but also labored to reform the life of the monks of that monastery to the observance of regular discipline. But when the monks perceived that they had lost all liberty for themselves, and that the monastery itself had been left with all honor as lord not to them but to the church, understanding the mystery too late, they grieved not a little, acknowledging that they had been outmaneuvered, and with the help of certain nobles began to oppose the archbishop's commands. Seeing this, the aforesaid archbishop Sifrid obtained from the supreme pontiff that the office of visitation and inquiry be carried out again through the bishop of Hildesheim, who, after the visitation had been made, informed the Roman pontiff that in the monks of that monastery, who [unclear] by thing or by power repeatedly of friends; for this reason he had labored with every effort that, after the monks had been expelled, men of another profession, who would not care for lordships or principality, should be put in their place.

Gregory, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to the venerable brother, the archbishop of Mainz, greeting and apostolic blessing.
