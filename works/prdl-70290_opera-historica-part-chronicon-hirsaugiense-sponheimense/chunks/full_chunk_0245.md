But on the last day of the month of October he entered the royal city of Aachen with a great multitude of nobles, and on the following day, that is, on the feast of All Saints, having been presented in the church by two bishops, of Minden and of Munster, to the archbishop of Cologne to be blessed, two other bishops, of Utrecht and of Liege, clothed him with a dalmatic in the manner of a deacon; and the aforesaid archbishop Conrad, with great solemnity, anointed and crowned him king of the Romans.

When the consecration had been completed, the same archbishop placed him on the royal seat, saying: May you sit upon the throne of the glorious seat, and do judgment and justice on earth.

Christian, archbishop of Mainz, anointed his right hand outwardly with sanctified oil, saying: May almighty God deign to consecrate you as king of the Romans, who commanded David to be anointed by the hand of Samuel the prophet over the people of the Hebrews.

Arnold, archbishop of Trier, placed his hands upon his head, saying: May the spirit of wisdom, understanding, knowledge, piety, strength, and counsel descend upon you, and may you be filled with the spirit of fear of the Lord.

Ludwig, count palatine of the Rhine, arch-steward of the kingdom, placed the golden orb in his hands, saying: Receive the spherical orb, that you may subject all the nations of the earth to the Roman empire, and may be able to be called a glorious Augustus.

The margrave of Brandenburg, the king's chamberlain, handed the ring into his hand, saying: Receive the seal of the monarchy, that you may preserve the Roman empire in its vigor and defend it with unconquered virtue from every incursion of the barbarians.

*Duke of Saxony.*

*King of Bohemia.*

The duke of Saxony, the king's sword-bearer, gave him the sword, saying: Receive the royal scepter, that you may powerfully afflict rebels with severe reproof, and govern all the well-disposed in tranquil peace.

The king of Bohemia, cupbearer of the empire, with the consent of the archbishop of Cologne, placed the silver crown on the king's head, saying: Receive the splendid diadem, that you may be so radiant on earth with virtuous deeds that you may deserve to have the crown of eternal blessedness in heaven. Amen.

After these things he kept the octave there solemnly. And all the princes and nobles of the lower parts offered him obedience, except for certain castellans still faithful to Frederick.

Among them was the castellan of Kaiserswerth, whom King William, surrounding him with a siege, could not overcome, because he defended himself strongly.

*1250.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1250, in the eighth indiction, Emperor Frederick II died at Taranto, in the fifty-seventh year of his age, leaving two sons: one, named Conrad, king of Jerusalem, whom he had long before designated king of the Romans, although he had been expelled from Germany; the other, named Manfred, by a concubine, who invaded the kingdom of Apulia after his father's death, though his brother resisted him, but he did not reign long.

In the same year Hermann, bishop of Wurzburg, died. Eringius of Reinstein succeeded him and presided for sixteen years, amid much disturbance and discord which he had with his citizens of Wurzburg.

In this same year the winter was very harsh and exceedingly long-lasting, abounding greatly in snow and ice; when these were at length dissolved into water, a very great flood followed, which overthrew many buildings.

*1251.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Volpold, and in the one thousand two hundred [unclear] year of the Lord's Nativity, [Conrad], once the son of Emperor Frederick II, took up the kingdom of Apulia; then in the following year he died, destroyed by poison, as is believed, his brother Manfred, as we have said, having contrived it.

In the same year the archbishop of Mainz died; [unclear] succeeded him and presided for nine years, a magnanimous and very prudent man, named Gerard, who, like the others, supported the party of King William.

*1252.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Volpold, and in the one thousand two hundred fifty-second year of the Lord's Nativity, in the tenth indiction, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of April, the holy virgin Jutta died, formerly attendant of blessed Elisabeth of Marburg, landgravine of Thuringia and Hesse, concerning whom mention was made above. This holy virgin is said to have been renowned for many signs and miracles both in life and after death; she had been privy to all the virtues and works of her lady while she was still living among men, a zealous co-worker in all things, and to the utmost of her strength she most steadfastly took care to imitate her until death.
