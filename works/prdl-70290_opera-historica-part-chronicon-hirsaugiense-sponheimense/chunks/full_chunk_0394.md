*Sponheim.*

... a multitude from all Germany made its way to Rome for indulgences; among them was Dietlibus, abbot of this monastery, who, having set out for Rome with the above-mentioned count and many others, brought back to us, among other spiritual benefits, the following letters of indulgences.

“To all sons of holy mother Church to whom the present letters shall come: We, by divine mercy, Basilius of Jerusalem, Ramitus of Cagliari, Adevulphus of Cosenza, archbishops; Thomas of [Etesia?], Bonifacius of Taranto, Adam of Martirano, Nicolaus of [Neccastro?], Bernhardus of [Pace?], Benedictus of [Tracouar?], Nicolaus of Tortiboli, brother Antonius of [Thunad?], Angelicus of Fiesole, brother Jacobus of Chalcedon, bishops: greeting and sincere charity in the Lord. We do not in the least doubt that the joys bestowed through the merits of the saints belong to Christ’s faithful who earn their patronage by worthy services of devotion, and who also venerate in them him whose glory and recompense of rewards he himself is. Therefore, wishing to give the faithful occasion to obtain the aforesaid things, to all who are truly penitent and confessed, who shall come to the monastery of Sponheim, of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, on whatever days and at whatever hours, for the sake of devotion and prayer, intending to implore from the Lord the pardon of their sins; or who shall extend helping hands for the fabric, lights, vestments, ornaments, or any other necessities of that monastery; or who, laboring at the point of death, shall bequeath, give, or send anything of their means to the aforesaid monastery; or also who shall reverently hear the masses or sermons of the monks of that monastery, as often, wherever, and whenever it shall happen that they hear them or any one of them celebrating divine service or preaching the word of God; or who shall make acceptable offerings to them or to any of them: for each occasion on which they shall do the foregoing, or any of the foregoing, trusting in the mercy of almighty God and in the authority of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, each of us mercifully relaxes in the Lord forty days from the penances enjoined upon them, provided only that the will of the diocesan bishop shall accede to this.”

“In witness whereof seals have been appended to the presents. Given at the Lateran, in the year of the Lord 1300, in the sixth year of the pontificate of the lord Pope Boniface VIII.”

These indulgences, then, Abbot Dietlibus brought with him from Rome, obtained by the labors and expenses of a certain familiar of his, Johannes de Soetern, who was procurator of this monastery, appointed by him, and whom, when he was about to set out for Rome, he had taken with him. But the consent of the diocesan bishop, of which mention is made in the letters, first acceded after twelve years, through Petrus, archbishop of Mainz, as is clear in the charter which he affixed to the letters themselves, secured with his seal; its tenor is as follows:

*Another copy of the indulgences.*

“We, Petrus, by the grace of God [of the holy and Mainzer see?], archchancellor of the Empire throughout Germany, holding as ratified and pleasing the indulgences of the venerable fathers, archbishops and bishops, contained in the letters to which this our charity is affixed, just as they were given, [unclear], each of them forty days, as is clear in the letters which follow; we confirm them for you after the year, so that they are not drawn by us into public questing. Given in the castle of Böckelheim, in the year of the Lord 1312, on the third day before the Nones of November.”

Of the above-mentioned thirteen bishops, three at the same time gave indulgences to this monastery, to the parish church, and to the chapel of St. Pancras the martyr in the castle of Sponheim. “To all sons of holy mother Church to whom the present letters shall come: We, by divine mercy, Basilius, archbishop of Jerusalem, Ramitus, archbishop of Cagliari, and Nicolaus of Tortiboli, gave them, each of them forty days, as is clear in the letters which follow: We, Petrus, by the grace of God archbishop of the holy and Mainzer see, archchancellor of the Empire throughout Germany, holding as ratified and pleasing the indulgences of the venerable fathers, archbishops, contained in the letters to which this our charity is affixed, just as given in the letters, as is clear in the letters which follow: We, Petrus, by the grace of God archchancellor of the holy and Mainzer see of the Empire throughout Germany, holding as ratified and pleasing the indulgences of the venerable fathers, archbishops, contained in the letters to which this our charity is affixed, just as given in the letters, as is clear in the letters which follow:”

“To all sons of holy mother Church to whom the present letters shall come: We, by divine mercy, Basilius, archbishop of Jerusalem, Ramitus, archbishop of Cagliari, and Nicolaus, bishop of Tortiboli, eternal greeting in the Lord. The splendor of the Father’s glory, which illumines the world with its ineffable brightness, then especially pursues with gracious favor the pious vows of the faithful, who hope in the most merciful goodness of his majesty, when their devoted humility is aided by the merits and prayers of the saints. Therefore, wishing that the church of the saints Martin, Giles, Pancras, and Benedict of Sponheim, of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, be frequented with fitting honors and be continually venerated by the people of Christ, to all who are truly penitent and confessed, who [unclear] to the said churches [unclear] by the merits of the aforesaid saints or trusting in their authority, each of us, sealed, mercifully relaxes in the Lord forty days from the penances there enjoined, provided only that the will and consent of the diocesan bishop shall accede to this. In witness whereof we have set our seals to the present writing. Given at Rome, in the year of the Lord 1300.” Its tenor is as follows:

*Consent.*

To these indulgences the lord Gerardus, archbishop of Mainz, at the request of Heinrich, count of Sponheim, above-mentioned, gave consent and confirmed them by the oracle of his living voice.
