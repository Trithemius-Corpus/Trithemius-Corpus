*Familiar Letters, V.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St James at Würzburg, to Johann Damius Curiescius, provost of the nuns in New Castle. Greetings. [unclear]*

Since the reasons, most beloved brother, which move our mind to the resignation of the abbey of Sponheim are not unknown to you, we have thought that few words should be used, signifying this alone first of all: that on the fifteenth day of this month of October we obtained real possession of the abbey of St James by canonical election, in which we hope that we shall live with greater peace than we ever lived at Sponheim. Hence we have sent letters to the convent in Sponheim, in which we both reprove their ingratitude and announce that for that reason we have resigned the abbey of Sponheim. It will be your part to deliver them, and to take care that they find a useful abbot, not one consenting to their vices. Wherefore it would seem advisable to us that your charity, about to announce these matters to the foul abbot of Speciosus Mons, from whom all their counsels depend, and as if from Apollo all responses, should first go down to Mainz, if perhaps it will be necessary to send for visitors, lest the wickedness of the impious prevail.

Wherefore in the bowels of Jesus Christ we exhort you: so far as lies in you, do not omit to provide that a suitable and useful person be set over the poor monastery, just as necessity itself seems to demand. For you know how those two, Prior Melantius and the lying Pingionita, both very foolish and stupid, especially pant after the abbacy; and you know their life and manners. In our judgment each of them will be not only useless to the monastery, but also harmful in no small degree. But although we have suffered an exceedingly grave injury from our own, nevertheless we wish every good for the monastery, for love of him who said in the holy Gospel: Pray for those who persecute and slander you; do good to those who hate you. May almighty God forgive them, who have persecuted us unjustly and without cause, perhaps not knowing what they have done, having a zeal neither good nor according to knowledge, but of bitterness and indiscretion. Farewell, praying continually to God for us. At Würzburg, on the last day of the month of October, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*VI.*

*To the most serene and most unconquered prince, Frederick, duke of Saxony, etc., Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St James in the city of Würzburg, sends greetings.*

*To Fulda, concerning the deeds of the Saxons.*

When I was lately returning from the March of Brandenburg, Conrad Mutianus, doctor, set forth the desire of your serenity, which Henry of Bünau, knight and once your envoy, also explained with no less diligence. Both reported one and the same opinion: that your serenity desires me to complete the history concerning the deeds of the Saxons, which Adam of Fulda once began, but, prevented by death, did not finish. My mind indeed has always been prompt and willing to obey your commands; but with various occupations presenting themselves in the meantime, up to now I have by no means been able to satisfy either your desires or my vows.

Now, however, at last having obtained quiet by a change, I have resigned the abbey of Sponheim and have taken up that of St James at Würzburg, in which I now live poor, in the manner of a philosopher, one suited to my studies and more tranquil by the opportunity for quiet. If therefore there is any strength in me by which I can render pleasing service to your highness, you will always find me prepared, because before many others I have known you, by the mouth of all men, as a prince worthy of praise, since in wisdom, in knowledge of the greatest matters, and in favor toward learned men you have no equal.

I once lent to the aforementioned Henry of Bünau, when he was among the Wangiones suffering from a broken leg, certain volumes, of which I have enclosed the list with these letters, on his pledge that they were to be restored as soon as possible. But he, restoring nothing in his usual manner, entered the way of human dissolution, leaving his brother as heir of all his affairs, with whom those very volumes of mine are said to have remained. Wherefore I beseech your serenity with the humblest submission, that by your command you cause these same volumes of mine to be restored to me. If I shall obtain this by your authority, you will have bound me wholly to your affairs. May almighty God long preserve your excellency unharmed; to him I commend myself with humble diligence. From my aforesaid monastery of St James at Würzburg, on the sixth day of the month of November, in the year of the Christians 1506.
