*MCLXVI.*

In the year noted above, in which the holy virgin Elisabeth departed from the body, which was the first year of Abbot Rupert, the winter was very harsh, and there followed a great famine with pestilence and many evils.

*MCLXVII.*

But in the third year of Abbot Rupert, which was the year of the Lord's nativity 1167, Henry, bishop of Wurzburg, died; Herold, succeeding him, presided for 7 years, 5 months, and 10 days. To this Bishop Herold, Emperor Frederick was the first to confirm the duchy of Franconia under a golden bull, with all ducal power and jurisdiction.

*MCLXVIII.*

In the following year Arnold, archbishop of Mainz, was cruelly killed by his own citizens in the monastery of St. James on the day of St. John the Baptist. The leaders of this conspiracy among the clergy were Godfrey, abbot of St. James, and the provost of the churches of St. Peter, with many others consenting to the same deed against the bishop. When the murder of the venerable prelate had been carried out, the cruel killers elected as archbishop Rudolf, son of Conrad, duke of Zahringen; and so that he might appease the emperor for himself with gifts, he stripped the whole Church of its ornaments. But, being despised by the emperor, he by no means attained to the see of the pontificate. Meanwhile, while Rudolf was going to the emperor, who was staying in Italy, Conrad, count palatine of the Rhine, brother of the emperor himself by one parent, raised a certain Christian to be archbishop. But when both had been rejected, the emperor [unclear] Conrad, brother of Otto, count palatine of Wittelsbach, to be ordained archbishop [unclear], stripped the whole Church [unclear]. We shall say, he expelled him, and ordained the aforesaid Christian in his place. After the murder of Arnold, the venerable bishop, the monastery of St. James was destroyed by his supporters, and the dwellings of the monks were utterly scattered, so that for nearly twelve years no harmony of divine praise was celebrated in that place. The monks were scattered here and there, and the abbot stood [unclear]. But all the citizens who had consented to the murder of the bishop, except the poor and beggars, saved themselves by flight before the emperor's return from Italy.

*MCLXX.*

But in the fifth year of Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, which was the year of the Lord's nativity 1170, Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, because he adhered to Alexander, the third pope of the city of Rome, against the sentence of the emperor, was driven from his church. In his place the emperor caused the above-mentioned Christian to be ordained; he presided for 13 years, and after his death Conrad returned to his church. Meanwhile, fleeing, he turned aside to the pope; received by him with honor, he accepted from him the bishopric of Sabina for the sustenance of his life. Therefore, as long as he lived, even after returning to Mainz, he wrote himself in all letters archbishop of Mainz and bishop of Sabina.

*MCLXXII.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Rupert, Emperor Frederick I subdued Henry, duke of Brunswick, who until then had been rebellious against the empire, both by the forces of the princes and by imperial censure, and compelled him to come to obedience.

In the same year the emperor celebrated Pentecost at Mainz, together with his wife and his son Henry and many princes; and on the same day in the monastery of St. Alban outside the walls he established [unclear] for the benefit of the commonwealth. There [Heminus?], count of Flanders, subjected himself in obedience to the emperor, and promised that same county to the emperor's son, provided he died without children.

In the same year also Louis, the second margrave of Thuringia, died, a man of great deeds and always unconquered in spirit.

*MCLXXIII.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Rupert, which was the year of the Lord's nativity 1173, the head of the holy virgin Irmina, daughter of Dagobert, once king of the Franks, who had been the first abbess of our order in the granary at Trier, was translated, at the petition of Lord [Craston?], the second abbot, to my monastery of Spanheim, with the consent of Hillin, archbishop.
