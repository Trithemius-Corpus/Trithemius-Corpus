*Sponheimense.*

The archbishop of Mainz was being held captive by the duke of Brunswick; Richard himself redeemed him for eight thousand marks, on this condition: that he might deserve to obtain his vote in the election. And this was done. For, released from custody, the same aforesaid archbishop betook himself from Marburg to Frankfurt and elected Richard king of the Romans.

*The archbishop of Mainz taken captive.*

In the year written above, on the sixth day before the Ides of November, Richard, bishop of Worms, died. Conrad of Durkheim, dean of Mainz, succeeded him and presided only about twenty weeks; and he had discord with the citizens.

*Richard, bishop of Worms, dies.*

*Eberhard, bishop of Worms.*

In the same year Louis, count palatine of the Rhine, son of Otto duke of Bavaria, having his wife Maria under suspicion of adultery, as we said above, caused her, while she was staying at Werde in Swabia, to be beheaded on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of February. By her he had no heir. This deed seemed excessively cruel to all good men, and was condemned by many, together with the man who commanded and ordered it to be done.

*MCCLVIII.*

In this year Johannes, abbot of this monastery, removed Petrus the prior and appointed him cellarer of the monastery, because he seemed more fit for governing temporal affairs than spiritual ones. For he was a man, as he himself used to say, born for earthly things; impatient of rest, he was always eager for outward labors. Of him there will be more to say below, where, after the death of Johannes our abbot, he will be recorded as having been substituted in his place. After Petrus, moreover, Nicolaus was appointed prior of this monastery by abbot Johannes, the thirteenth according to the order; he had once been provost of the nuns on Mount Saint Rupert near Bingen. He was a good man, quite zealous for the observance of regular discipline, though he could not restore it to its former fervor. He presided in the office of prior for six years; he died.

In the same year the church of St. Catherine in Oppenheim was erected as a parish church by Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, as appears in the letters that follow.

Gerard, by the grace of God archbishop of the holy see of Mainz, archchancellor of the sacred empire throughout Germany. Since, at the command of our most serene lord, the illustrious king of the Romans, through the older and better inhabitants, the true ancient boundaries having been revealed, which divided the diocese of Mainz from that of Worms, although through negligence they had in part been [unclear], it has been clearly declared that the known city in Oppenheim is established in our diocese; and the people of the same city, numerous and [unclear] in authority, have earnestly represented, with the Lord increasing it, that it ought, indeed that it is expedient for the salvation of souls, to be governed by the rule of a single and proper rector. We, with the counsel and consent of our chapter and of the provost of St. Victor, archdeacon of the place, choose, make, and create the church of St. Catherine, founded in that city, as a parish church, and distinguish it by the use, right, and honor of a parish church; granting to it that it may have the use of its own baptistery for baptizing its little children, and also a special cemetery for the convenience of burial. They shall also have and appoint special [curates?], who shall promote and dispose those things that pertain to spiritual right. In testimony of this deed and for due firmness, we have caused the present writing to be fortified with our seal, that of our chapter, and that of the aforesaid provost of St. Victor. Given at Mainz, on the fifth day before the Nones of July, in the year of the Lord MCCLVIII.

In the same year the canons of the greater church of Metz sold all their goods in Odernheim, where St. Rufus, formerly bishop of Metz, remains buried down to the present day, to the canons of the greater church for 1040 pounds of Metz money, as appears from the letters made and sealed concerning this, whose date is in the year of the Lord MCCLVIII, on the Kalends of June.

*MCCLIX.*

In the sixth year of Johannes our abbot, the monks of our order from the monastery of St. Disibod near Sobernheim, after the death of Otto their abbot, were removed and expelled through Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, and the canons of the greater church of Mainz, as founders of the same place; and that monastery was given to the Cistercian order, as it remains to the present day. Therefore monks and lay brothers coming from Otterberg in the diocese of Worms first began to inhabit that place, appointing for themselves as the first abbot of their order one named Johannes, who bound his monastery to the church of Mainz, as it were in gratitude for the benefit received, in perpetuity, for one hundred malters of rye each year, as appears in the copy that follows:
