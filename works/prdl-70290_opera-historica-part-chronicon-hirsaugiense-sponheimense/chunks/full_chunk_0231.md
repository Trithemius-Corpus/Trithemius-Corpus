*1232.*

In the first year of Abbot Ernest, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1232, the emperor Frederick, staying at Ravenna, prepared war against the Lombards; but he made no progress, since the Lombards blocked the route to the king, Henry his son, and to the German soldiery whom the emperor had summoned to him at Ravenna.

In this same year the Sultan of Babylon sent the emperor a tent constructed with marvelous art, in which images of the sun and moon, moved artificially, completed their course through fixed and proper intervals and indicated the hours of day and night infallibly. The value of this tent was said to have exceeded the price of five thousand florins.

In the same year a great war was made between Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, and Conrad the Landgrave. Taking the town of Fritzlar, he almost utterly destroyed it. In the same place men were captured by the Landgrave himself. At last peace was made between them.

Also in the same year Frederick II, emperor, abolished from the monastery itself the principality of a monastery of our order, not far from Worms, called Lorsch, with every honor, vassals, ministeriales, towns, castles, villages, rights, revenues, and its appurtenances, which the abbot of that place had always been accustomed to hold; and he gave almost all the canonry-rights of the aforesaid town to the archbishop and the church of Mainz forever, whose value exceeds the price of one hundred thousand florins. Pope Gregory IX, at the archbishop’s request, confirmed this donation, commanding him that, after the monks of our order had been expelled from there, he should put Cistercians in their place. When he had done this, our men, grieving that they had been expelled from their monastery so unjustly by the archbishop, returned one night to the monastery, relying on the help of friends; and they attacked the Cistercian monks found there, who feared nothing of the kind, struck them, wounded them, and expelled them from the monastery; and they began to inhabit the place again. But not many days later the archbishop arrived, and with a strong hand expelled them once more from the monastery, and recalled his Cistercians again. And on a certain night, during matins, when the Cistercians believed themselves to be safe, our men again came with their friends to Lorsch, attacked them again, cut them down, struck them, and expelled them once more, declaring that they would kill them all if they presumed to return again to that monastery of Lorsch. Therefore from that day the Cistercians could no longer be induced to return to Lorsch. The monastery was thus vacant for some time, since neither they nor our men [unclear] safely place themselves there. [He] commanded, in the second year of his pontificate, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1246, that he should put monks of whatever order, or certainly secular canons, back into the monastery, lest it remain vacant any longer. Having taken counsel, he [unclear] monks of the Premonstratensian order from the monastery of All Saints in the diocese of Strasbourg for this purpose [unclear] 764, built and magnificently endowed it. In it afterward Saint Ermenold was made the fifth abbot of this monastery, although he resigned the abbacy because of a scruple of conscience; and, translated into Bavaria, he was appointed the first abbot of the monastery of Saint George in Prüfening, where he was renowned for many signs and miracles, as is read in his Life.

This year the summer was dry and very hot; excellent wine grew.

*1233.*

In the second year of Abbot Ernest, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1233, the emperor Frederick violently took Messina, a city of Sicily rebelling against him, and burned all his rebels in it with fire.
