*XIX. Joachim, by the grace of God Margrave of Brandenburg, Prince Elector, etc., sends very many greetings to Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James in the suburb of Wurzburg, with prayer for all good fortune and prosperity.*

Reverend father in Christ, my most beloved abbot, I received the most welcome letters of Your Paternity, and at the same time the messenger whom you sent; by his report I learned that you were sound and unharmed, and that your condition was quiet and fortunate [unclear], for which I congratulate you from affection and give due thanks to the immortal God. Your Paternity gives me very great thanks for the munificence and benefits bestowed on you, which are in no way owed to me, since all the gifts that I have conferred on you are small and cheap; indeed, they do not even deserve to be called gifts, and for that reason they require no acts of thanksgiving. Even if they had been of some value or price, they are nevertheless not such and so great as you deserved to receive from me and as were especially fitting for you. For through them I wished only to show the affection and goodwill of my mind toward you; I ask you again and again to be willing to weigh that rather than the utterly cheap gifts themselves.

I rejoice that Libanius, your teacher, is sound and unharmed; if it could by any means be done, I would desire to see and speak with him. I have also received the letters of our friend the lord of Wurzburg concerning your return to me in the March, promised for next year, which saddens me not a little because of the ordering of my affairs and the nature of the causes to which your long absence can bring harm, since your return to me is not only useful to me, but very necessary. But since it cannot be otherwise, I am compelled to be content and breathlessly await the promised time. Yet I ask that Your Paternity not defer your return to me beyond the promised time, since your coming again is necessary to me. I have also written back to my friend, the lord Bishop of Wurzburg, asking [unclear]; do not be reluctant to present my letters.

I have also given letters to your prince, the Palatine, in which I asked that he assist you in obtaining the books necessary for you. And so that it may not at all be hidden from you by what writings of yours I desire to be delighted, I refer you to the note sent from Tangermunde the previous year, in which I had marked for you the names of certain subjects on which I wish to have splendid treatises from your workshop. Whatever Your Paternity judges conducive to true wisdom and to living blessedly, I shall count as my delights.

I give Your Paternity infinite thanks for that very beautiful and most exquisite method of writing [unclear], which you have again sent me; in my secret affairs I would not wish to lack it for a great deal of money. Let Your Paternity not distrust my person, because I shall never be able to forget the faith and charity conceived between us. For my mind toward you is sufficiently open, sincere, upright, and most loving.

[I send?] one silver cup, gilded inside and out, not as a gift, but as a sign of my love toward you and of the stable friendship long since contracted between us and destined to endure until death; I pray that you receive it with a glad heart and use it rather often in remembrance of me.

I wish to know concerning my letters whether they have reached the hands of Libanius or not. Palestinus, about whom you asked, is still with our King Maximilian, and we do not know when he will come. Let Your Paternity apply diligence in those matters which you know to be useful to me, and you will have me grateful to you in all things as long as I live. Farewell, ornament of Germany and chest of all wisdom, mindful of me before God and loving, we who love you exceedingly. From Berlin, in my own hand, on the twenty-ninth day of the month of May, in the year of the Christians 1507.
