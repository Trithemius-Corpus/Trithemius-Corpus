[Jacobus?] Trithemius, brother of your most reverend Paternity, brought us your letters, from which we understood that you not only live, but live happily. As humbly as I can I give thanks to almighty God, whose mercy has made you stronger than all your rivals, so that those who thought you should be harmed have gravely harmed and confounded themselves, and have made themselves worthy of everlasting reproach. Those most treacherous scoundrels thought that they could extinguish your famous name and the renown of your most holy reputation throughout the whole Christian world; but I trust in him who knows all things, that not only will the unjust persecution of the envious do you no harm, but because of it, after being transferred to Francia, you will shortly shine forth much more brightly than you did before.

No one gave greater occasion for all the evils you endured than your monks, together with those two most treacherous abbots, the one of Mainz and the one of Rheingau, who, as I have heard by truthful report, stirred up the chancellor and the officers of Duke John into hatred of you. For while you were in the March, your monks were running ceaselessly here and there to Mainz, and to the duke himself, who was continually residing at Fortiburgium beside the Moselle, and they tried to provoke against you whomever they could. The unlearned [unclear] of the duke, formerly well disposed toward you, readily believed the reports of the envious, as one who had long regarded your learning and wisdom with suspicion, always fearing, as is now publicly reported, that this duke might take you as his councillor. Wishing therefore to forestall the duke’s plan, he persuaded that overcredulous man of everything that was being fabricated about such matters. But the justice of almighty God will bring all things successively into the light; and certainly it has already begun actually to become plain how very unjustly you have been treated.

Your rivals launched many rash accusations against you when you were stationed in the March, wishing to blacken the most splendid reputation of your holiness, and they spread abroad among the people certain dreadful lies, for which they are now being confounded in the sight of all men. For although we all knew that you were with the most serene prince Joachim in the March, nevertheless certain pestilent and very envious men of your most sacred name were saying: “Where do you think Abbot Trithemius is? With the Margrave of Brandenburg? He is not, they say, with him, but in despair has gone away to Rome, never to return thereafter.” Others dared to say that, touched by excessive desolation of spirit, he had hanged himself with his own noose. Others again falsely declared that he had been consigned to perpetual chains. Many also, giving credit to the words of your enemies, believed that you had perished, saying: “Alas, that splendid light, which was wont to illumine the whole world with its learning, how has it been obscured by the darkness of shadows? Who would have believed that Abbot Trithemius, glorious in nearly the whole Christian world for the fame of his learning and holiness, would come to such dejection of spirit that he would so rashly desert his fatherland and his friends?”

But I, who knew your learning, your prudence, and also the holiness of your life very well, always resisted these blasphemies and curses of the impious with all my strength, and I showed openly by many arguments what a rash injury they had done to your most holy name. For having spent two years in your monastery under your teaching, to learn Greek and Hebrew letters and to draw in the saving knowledge of the Scriptures, I could never see anything in you, never hear anything of you, except what was holy, upright, and religious; indeed, we saw you conducting yourself at all times in such purity that we greatly marveled. While the others slept, you kept watch in the holy study of the Scriptures, always either reading or writing something for the common benefit of many. No one ever found you idle; no one heard that you were given to feasting or drinking; no one saw you dissolved in laughter or any levity; no one ever found you speaking or associating with women in a way from which an evil suspicion could arise.

O my sweetest teacher Trithemius, gracious father, whenever I recall to memory that most holy manner of teaching by which you were accustomed to teach and exhort me, and at the same time your brother Master Jacobus Trithemius, to the knowledge of the divine Scriptures, I cannot keep back my tears for sorrow. For you used to pour forth to us such profound, such mystical, and such ardent words concerning the holy Scriptures that they could have moved hearts of iron to the love of God. Hence I cannot sufficiently wonder by what judgment of God, permitting it at last, your rivals could have invented such unworthy things about you, when you were never harmful to anyone, when you wronged no one by injury, but rather showed yourself pious and generous to all, always seeking all your consolation, without injury to anyone, in the study of the Scriptures alone.

But with firm confidence I believe that almighty God has provided by his disposition that you be placed elsewhere, so that, finally rescued from the midst of a wicked nation, you may persevere in the holy study for the rest of your life with peace and tranquillity, to the common benefit of many. The monks of Sponheim were never worthy of your prelacy; nor did Duke John himself deserve to have in his dominion such a man as you are, excellent Trithemius.
