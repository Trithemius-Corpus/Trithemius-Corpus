*853.*

Lintbert, the first abbot, a very venerable man, after many labors borne in holy religion for the love of God, and worn out with age, sighing for Christ with his whole heart, died on the third day before the Ides of June, under Emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 854, in the second indiction, in the seventy-sixth year of his age, and before the sixteenth year of his administration of the abbacy had yet been completed. He had laudably and usefully presided over this monastery, and through his most holy conduct acquired many goods for it by the donation of the faithful. Gerungus, a monk, succeeded him in the abbacy of this monastery for thirty-two years; concerning him we shall see below.

*The Northmen harass Gaul.*

In this very year in which the most reverend abbot Lintbert died, the Northmen cruelly devastated Brittany, Gaul, and Francia; they overthrew the city of Nantes, and killed the bishop, who on Holy Saturday was intent upon the consecration of baptism, together with all the clergy.
