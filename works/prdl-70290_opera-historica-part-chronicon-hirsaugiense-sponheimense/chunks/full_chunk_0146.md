And because by his works he fulfilled the counsel of a wise man, namely the blessed bishop Prosper, who says, “Wisdom fills a tranquil mind, free from cares, and will be the guest of a peaceful breast,” therefore the spirit of saving wisdom rested upon him; and, as it wondrously illumined his mind, he appeared more learned even among the most learned, especially in those matters that pertained to the way of salvation, the discernment of virtues, and the instruction of monastic life. He was so apt, so elegant, and so persuasive in this place that not only his brothers and subjects, but also outsiders, both religious and secular, whoever heard him, were greatly astonished at his speech. Hence, through his most gentle admonitions, many noble and wealthy men, knights, canons, and men greatly honored in the world, were brought to contempt of the world.

When the Spirit of the Lord rushed upon him to utter the word of exhortation, and he opened his mouth to his hearers, he seemed to speak not human but divine words.

He was therefore buried before the altar of blessed Lawrence, as we have said, and lay under the earth for three hundred thirty-nine years; and at last, when exhumed, he gave sight to a blind man.

*Exhumed in the year 1460.* For in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1460, in the eighth indiction, on the third day before the Kalends of September, when, after the death of Abbot Wolfram, called Meiser, a burial-place was being prepared in that place where the body of Lord Bruno, the aforementioned abbot, lay buried, with an inscription of his name [unclear].
