*242. CHRONICLE OF TRITHEMIUS. ON BERNHELM, THE FIRST ABBOT OF THIS MONASTERY; WHO PRESIDED 26 years, 9 months, [and] 17 days; AND ON THE DEEDS OF THAT TIME.*

In the year, therefore, of the Lord's nativity 1124, in the second Roman indiction, the eighth day of the month of June, Bernhelm, monk of St Alban's near Mainz, sent hither with others, was constituted the first abbot of this monastery, as much by the election of the brothers as by the command of Adalbert, archbishop of Mainz, by whom, ordained, he presided 26 years, 9 months, 17 days. He was ordained at Mainz in the monastery of St Alban, four abbots standing by him—namely [those] of St Alban, St James, St Disibod, and St Ferrutius in Bleidenstadt—with many others, as much of the clergy as of the people, in the aforesaid year, on the nativity of St John the Baptist. This our first abbot Bernhelm was sprung from the county of Sponheim, begotten of honest parents, his father by name Eberhard having strenuously served Stephen, count of Sponheim, for several years. His mother, by name Hiltrudis, was a woman devoted to Christ; whose dwelling was in the village of Sponheim, under the castle. Bernhelm, therefore, a boy prompt and docile in talent, was sent to the schools to be imbued with letters, and, having obtained good teachers, was not only well instructed in the science of the Scriptures, but adorned also with the best morals. In each he greatly advanced, and with time turned out renowned in virtues, and at the same time in erudition: so amiable, and never grievous to anyone even slightly. Whence, his merits advancing, on account of [his] all-round knowledge of the Scriptures, he was made Master of the schools in the same monastery, and at length, made presbyter, was constituted [such]; and he embraced that office of teaching, no less by writings than by daily lessons, magnificently for ten years. He wrote, first of all, On the instruction of boys, 1 book; also Introductions into the seven liberal arts, 2 books; Against a certain Sophist, 1 book; Against the simoniacs, 3 books; on Ecclesiastes of Solomon he published a most beautiful work, [—] book[s]; Against the Jews, 1 book; and some others, which negligence has brought into oblivion.

In this same year Berthold the monk is constituted here the first Prior, a learned and most religious man, who published Commentaries on the Gospel of Mark, 2 books; On the Song of Songs, 2 books; On the humility of monks, 1 book; many sermons and homilies. He presided in the Priorate 13 years, and did many good [things].

In the same year Adelbero was constituted chief Cellarer—a monk, once one of the first sent hither, a good man and of religious conversation, sagacious and much experienced in ordering and disposing temporal matters, who for several years strenuously governed the office committed to him. And straightway the fame of the good and religious conversation of the brothers began to spread round about, and little by little to come to the notice of all the neighbors. Whence, since the new plantation did not yet seem sufficiently abundantly provided for the sustenance of the monks, many, both noble and ignoble, on account of the opinion of [their] good fame and the holy conversation of the brothers serving God, began to donate many alms and benefits.

In this same year a monastery of our order was founded at Ballenstedt, by Otto, count of the same place, in Saxony, and dowered honorably enough by him. [Margin: The monastery of Ballenstedt is founded; today Rudenzhow.]

Bruno, archbishop of Trier, also died, who founded a monastery of our order at Odenheim in the diocese of Speyer, which he dowered with many estates and possessions.

A hail of great mass, too, fell the 9th [day before] the Kalends of August. And the 3rd [day before] the Ides of August there was an eclipse.
