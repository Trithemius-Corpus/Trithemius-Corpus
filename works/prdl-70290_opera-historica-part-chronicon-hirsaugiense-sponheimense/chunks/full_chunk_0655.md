*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

*63.*

*To the most holy father in Christ, Pope Julius II, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, offers the most humble kisses of the blessed feet.*

[unclear]

The fire which our Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the eternal Father, sent upon the earth, and wished to be kindled vehemently in the hearts of men, compels me, most merciful father, the least of your subjects, of no weight before the divine seat of your pontificate except in faith and devotion, merit or consequence, to plead before your most merciful paternity, at the instance of the most Christian Duke George of the Saxons, the cause of blessed Benno, formerly bishop of Meissen, most worthy in merit and holiness, a cause higher than my powers; since I cannot do so with words, I do it at least with mute letters.

He, as we are taught by ancient monuments, was from infancy anticipated by the divine Spirit and devoted himself wholly to the service of God: distinguished in faith, untiring in virtue and constancy, strong in hope, and at all times most fervent in love and charity toward God and neighbor. From being a monk of the rule of Saint Benedict, he was appointed first abbot of the monastery called Goseck, situated in the diocese of Naumburg not far from that city itself, and presided for almost three years in all religion and holiness. When this time had passed, stirred by the envy of his monks and by the assaults of the advocate, a count, he left the monastery together with the abbacy and entered the wilderness to serve Christ in tranquillity. From there, summoned by Pope Victor II, he was made rector of the church of Goslar, while the merits of holiness were always increasing in him; finally he obtained the episcopate of the church of Meissen by the canonical election of the brethren.

And so that he might not seem to have taken on the name of so great a dignity without fruit, he turned himself wholly to the usefulness of all the flock of Christ the highest shepherd, constant in prayer, wondrously devoted in preaching the word of the Lord, both to the Slavs, whom he converted to the orthodox faith, and to his own faithful and those round about, with an [unclear] and fervor. In that persecution also in which Emperor Henry III, disobedient enemy and rebel against the supreme pontiffs of the Roman Church, troubled the whole Church, especially in Germany, Benno, man of God and also a magnificent prelate, formed in faith and love of his Lord, always most faithful to the Apostolic See, resisted the tyrant to his face, rebuked him in his sin, and confidently thundered against him the sentence of anathema which the pope had decreed, publicly proclaiming and denouncing him to all the faithful as schismatic, accursed, rejected by God unless he did penance, to the judgment of the vicar of Christ.

For all these things he was cast into chains and prisons, suffered injuries, insults, reproaches, and blows as well, and always with a cheerful mind sang: “It is good for me to cling to God, since the vicar of Christ must be obeyed rather than a man of evil speech.” O man full of virtues and lofty merits, thrice holy and four times most holy, who, strengthened on the rock of apostolic constancy, confidently set himself as a wall for the house of God at a necessary and dangerous time, and, fighting most bravely for the honor of the most holy Apostolic See, feared no chains of tyrants. O pontiff to be extolled with everlasting praises, whose life, shining with all virtues and every holiness, is rightly set before us as an example; and that it pleased God most high is clearly declared by almost infinite miracles down to the present day, to such an extent that, if the integrity of saints is judged to be proved by the testimony of miracles, no one in these times seems to be judged holier than blessed Benno.

Every day astonishing miracles are done at his tomb, and things still more wondrous always succeed the wondrous. And although, most blessed father Julius, Benno has already received from God, the best and greatest, the eternal reward and glory in the heavenly palace for his merits, and, joined to his ineffable principle of spirit by grace, not by nature, blessed without end, he by no means requires our honors, since he has all things in that highest good, nevertheless provision must be made for us and for the faithful posterity, who need the patronage of the saints, by the authority of your most holy see. Miracles bear witness that Benno is holy; virtues cry out that he is to be honored; the experience of healings promises him as a faithful patron.
