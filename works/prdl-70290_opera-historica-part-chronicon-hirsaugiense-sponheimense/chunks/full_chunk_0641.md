Therefore we exhort you in the Lord Jesus Christ, to whom you have been betrothed, that according to the rule of the cenobitic institution you should always walk worthily, having your conduct in the monastery pure, holy, and immaculate, as befits virgins consecrated to the heavenly king. Keep your minds always occupied, as far as possible, in the law of the Lord, applying yourselves with the sweetness of divine love to the readings of the divine Scriptures, so that while you are working with your hands you may never lack material for holy meditations.

All the delights of this world are forbidden to us; and it is clear that you too renounced them of your own free will, when at the beginning of your profession each one of you sang out in a clear voice: “The kingdom of the world and all the adornment of the age I have despised for the love of my Lord Jesus Christ, whom I have seen through the most certain hope of faith, whom I have loved, in whom I have believed, whom I have cherished.” It is not permitted to you to seek again what once, for love of Christ, you despised; rather you must persevere in contempt of worldly things until death, as our Savior himself says in the holy Gospel: “He who has persevered to the end, he shall be saved.” Therefore, having once despised the world and whatever is beautiful and lovable in it for the love of God, it is not fitting for you to love it any more, unless you wish, with the foolish virgins, to receive rejection from the bridal chamber of the bridegroom and, with the apostate spirits, eternal damnation.

Remember, dearest daughters in the Lord, to whom you have promised faith, and live in such purity of mind and body that, through the good display of works, continually fervent in desire for divine love, after the end of this life you may be able to come to him. Do not wish to love the world, as Saint John says, nor the things that are in the world. For if anyone loves the world, the charity of God the Father is not in him. But the world passes away, and all its concupiscence; he who does the will of God remains forever. Know this, however, [unclear], that the love of God and of the world cannot dwell together in one heart, just as the eye by no means looks at heaven and earth equally at the same time.

This world is rightly compared to the sea, because it is exceedingly bitter with falsehoods, restless with various temptations and adversities, everywhere full of the creeping things of vices. This great sea, dangerous to mortals, you, O daughters, have escaped by fleeing to the monastery through the mercy of God; and by loving God in purity of heart [unclear], you renounced that very sea of the age at the beginning of your profession. Each one of you must take the greatest care not to entangle her mind in the vanities of the age, because it is of no profit to have fled the world in body if the heart is still occupied in love of it. You escaped the world in body for this reason: lest any occasion should bind the mind into love of it. For a person’s own desire is like a tempest of the sea; if by sinning he consents to it, whether he is in a monastery or outside, he is worldly and is overwhelmed by the waves of the sea. If by loving God you resist the desires of the world, you walk above the sea and come to salvation. But if you have walked after your desires, the tempest of the sea will without doubt swallow you up. For the world is accustomed to sink its lovers into the depth of hell, not to carry them; no one ever trusted the world and was not deceived.

Therefore the world must be despised by us, O virgins of Christ; nothing at all must be loved: not riches, not honors, not joys, not pleasures, not conversations with people, not greetings, not little gifts, not praises, not the comforts of the flesh, not any ornament in clothing. Your riches are the love of God and the testimony of a pure conscience; to acquire and preserve these you must always keep watch with every zeal. Nor is it fitting for you, betrothed to the Son of God and attendants of the Virgin Mary, his undefiled mother, to have any care at all for the world and perishable things, but at all times you ought to provide for this alone: that you may be able to please him to whom you have offered yourselves as a holocaust.

Your honors, however, are fasting, abstinence, tears of compunction, and avoiding the sight of all secular outsiders, detesting conversations, spurning greetings, despising little gifts and familiar letters, loving nothing worldly at all. The joys and pleasures of nuns are to apply themselves to the readings of the divine Scriptures, to occupy themselves with holy meditations, to be always intent on prayer, to mortify the flesh, to adorn the mind with virtues, and to spend no time unfruitfully. The adornment of cloistered virgins is humility and purity of heart; without these no one has ever pleased God. For Christ Jesus, the Son of almighty God, justifier of faithful souls, is your bridegroom; to him you have bound yourselves, mind and body, to serve faithfully in perpetual purity. He requires in you your adornment: not of the flesh but of the heart, not of the body but of the mind, not of garments but of virtues, not of outward beauty but of inward purity. Adorn yourselves for him, O virgins, not for the world; strive to please him, not human beings.
