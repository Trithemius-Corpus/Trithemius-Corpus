*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius* 47

In these times Reinhardus, abbot of the monastery of Saint Peter the Apostle at Senones, flourished: a learned and most religious man, who gloriously restored the buildings of his monastery, ruined by excessive age, and, instructing the monks in the regular path, brought them back to the former fervor of their way of life. He also maintained a public school of monks, and fully instructed his monks in all letters and sciences, both secular and divine.

*Reinhardus of Senones, most learned. 1012.*

In the year of the Lord 1012, which was the tenth year while the abbacy was vacant, when Rupertus, priest of the church of Saint Magnus the Martyr in Saxony, had begun the first Mass on the night of the Nativity of the Lord, a certain layman Otbertus, leading a dance in the cemetery with fifteen other men and three women, and making a din with secular songs, caused no small distraction to the celebrant. When the priest ordered them through the sacristan to be silent and to desist from such frivolities on so holy a night, and they did not obey his command, he was moved, and standing at the most holy altar said: “May almighty God make it so that you must remain dancing thus for a whole year.” Wonderful to say, the effect at once followed the voice of the one uttering the curse: thus, for the space of a whole year, they danced without interruption day and night; they did not eat, nor [unclear].

*People dance for an entire year. The curse of the priest Rupertus.*

They seemed to be sunk up to the soles of their feet, trampled beneath them. The son of that priest too, when he tried with violence to pull his sister out from the middle of the dancers by seizing her arm, tore her arm from her body; but she, as if she had suffered nothing, neither felt pain nor uttered a sound, nor did a single drop of blood fall, but continued the dance with the rest.

At last, when the year had passed, Saint Heribertus, archbishop of Cologne, came to the cemetery, absolved the dancers from the curse, and, bringing them into the church, reconciled them to the Lord. Of these the women soon died, and some of the men shortly afterward; they are even said to have been illustrious for miracles after death, since they had undergone a sufficiently hard penance. But the rest, to whom it was permitted to live longer, showed by the trembling of their limbs what they had suffered.

*Saint Heribertus absolves the cursed persons from the bonds.*

When around these times the monks of the monastery of Fulda had become a mockery to all the people because of their most deformed way of life, and, though often warned by the holy emperor, scorned to amend their wicked habits, since he knew that they had become insolent through an excessive abundance of riches, he ordered many goods and possessions to be taken from them and distributed them to other monasteries.
