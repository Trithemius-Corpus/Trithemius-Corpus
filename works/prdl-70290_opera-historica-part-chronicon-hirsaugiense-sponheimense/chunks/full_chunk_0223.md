In this same year also Lupold, bishop of Worms, through the intervention of King Frederick and with the pope’s permission, returned to his see and was received into the grace of the apostolic see: out of hatred, as it seemed to most, of Otto, who, after King Philip had died, with the pope’s consent had proscribed him into exile. Meanwhile Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, for as long as he stood in Otto’s favor, also governed the church of Worms, especially with regard to ordinations and sacraments.

*The court at Mainz. Lupold. 1213.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Lutfrid, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1213, Frederick held a celebrated assembly of princes at Aachen on the feast of Saint James. There, with the consent of all the princes, he was called king and placed on the throne of the realm. There too, together with many others, he was signed with the cross against the Saracens for the recovery of the Lord’s Sepulchre, Master Conrad preaching the cross, then dean, who afterward was made bishop of Hildesheim in Saxony, a holy and most learned man.

King Frederick first took as wife Maria, daughter of the king of Aragon, who in this same year, while bringing aid to the Albigensians [and?] to the count of Toulouse, was killed. By her he begot Henry, king of the Romans, who died before his father, and Conrad the king. After his first wife died, he took a second, the daughter of John, king of Jerusalem, who had a son named Jordan. After she too died, he took a third.

In the same year Leuthold, bishop of Basel, died; Walter succeeded him.

In the same year also Philip, king of France, entered Flanders with a very great army, to drive out Count Ferrand and [unclear] the land to his own lordship; when [unclear] had been killed, he could not fulfill his desire, for very many of his men were killed there, and with great loss and disgrace he was compelled to return to France.

Around autumn also Duke Henry of Brabant, fighting with the bishop of Liège, was defeated, with almost all his men either captured or killed. Theoderic, archbishop of Cologne, stubbornly disobeying the apostolic mandates, openly supported the party of Otto against Frederick, and by this brought much calamity upon his diocese. It happened in that year that the bishop of Münster came to Cologne; he was seized by certain supporters of the deposed emperor, sent to Werden, and at his order consigned to chains. On account of this the people of Cologne were excommunicated by Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz and legate of the Roman pontiff, and the celebration of divine offices in Cologne was forbidden.

In the same year also, on the seventeenth day before the Kalends of September, Rupert, the fifth abbot of my monastery of Spanheim, died. Juvanus, succeeding him as sixth in the order of abbots, presided for 39 years and 3 months.

*1214.*

In the year of Abbot Lutfrid [unclear], but of the Lord’s Nativity 1214, Otto, formerly emperor, devastated the land of the count of Guelders with fire and sword. In the month of July the same Emperor Otto, together with the king of England, his maternal uncle, and also Henry, duke of Brabant, Ferrand, count of Flanders, of whom mention was made before, and many others, marched to war against Philip, king of the French; when battle was joined, he was defeated by him near Tournai. There Count Ferrand, with many nobles both of Flanders and of Lotharingia, was captured; all of them were sent to Paris and consigned to chains.

At the same time Frederick crossed the Moselle with a very powerful army and by fear of him compelled all the nobles of that land to his obedience. Then, crossing the Meuse, he resolved to attack Henry, the aforesaid duke of Brabant, in war. Terrified by his swift arrival, Henry came to him as a suppliant, swore fidelity, gave his son with many other nobles as hostage, and withdrew from Otto, the deposed emperor.

In this same year, on the day before the Nones of March, a comet-star appeared.

In the same year also Brother Conrad of Marburg, of the Order of Preachers, began to preach in Germany by apostolic authority; he continued for nearly [unclear] years and caused many to be burned as heretics, with no one preventing it. At last, as we shall say, he himself was killed.
