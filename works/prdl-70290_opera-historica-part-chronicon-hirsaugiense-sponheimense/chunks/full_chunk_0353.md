1228.

In this year Ottokar and Wenceslaus, illustrious kings of the Bohemians, gave such a privilege to the church of Mainz: that the archbishop of Mainz, and no one else, must anoint and crown as kings both them and their successors, as is clear in these letters:

In the name of the Lord, Amen. Ottokar and Wenceslaus, illustrious kings, to all the faithful of Christ who shall look upon the present writing, perpetual salvation. We acknowledge and publicly declare that we and all our successors are bound forever to receive royal consecration and the placing of the diadem from the archbishop of the most holy see of Mainz, the metropolitan of our land. But if that archbishop, when requested, should withdraw himself by impulse of his own mind, let him commit his office for carrying this out to another bishop whom we or our successors shall request. And so that this may remain valid and unshaken, we have confirmed this page, with the consent of the venerable bishops of Prague and Olomouc, by the attachment of our seals. Given at Prague in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1228, in the first indiction.

In the same year the emperor Frederick crossed the sea, recovered Jerusalem, together with part of the Holy Land, and made truces there with the Saracens for a fixed time.

1219.

In the sixteenth year of Abbot John, the village called Wimesheim was burned by an accidental fire, almost wholly consumed, and in that fire three infant boys perished by a like misfortune, namely on 21 August; many were impoverished even to beggary.

In the same year the emperor Frederick returned from the lands beyond the sea and, finding his land in Italy harried by troubles through the pope, gravely avenged all the injuries he had suffered.

1230.

In this year a grave controversy and dissension arose among the citizens and the council of the town of Bingen, which went so far that two members of the council were publicly slain in the marketplace through a sedition of the common people; some were wounded; the rest, scarcely escaping by flight, took care to save themselves. But not long afterward a severe vengeance followed against the authors of the sedition. For some were beheaded, others mutilated in their limbs, and the rest, proscribed beyond the borders of the fatherland, endured perpetual exile.

Also in the same year the emperor was absolved by the pope, through the mediation of Leopold, duke of Austria, who, after a treaty of peace had been completed between the pope and the emperor at San Germano in Campania, died; his entrails and flesh were buried in the chapter house of the monastery of our most holy father Benedict on Monte Cassino, while his bones were carried back to Austria.

Also in the same year dissension arose between the archbishop of Cologne and the duke of Limburg over the advocacy of the monastery of Siegburg of our order, in the same said diocese of Cologne.

1231.

In the eighteenth year of our abbot John, Saint Elizabeth died, daughter of the former king of Hungary, widow left by Louis the Pious, fifth landgrave of Thuringia, a woman of many virtues and of most holy conduct, buried at Marburg, which is a town of Hesse; she at once began to shine with countless miracles.

In the same year, in the month of July, in the city of Worms, a fire that arose by chance in the house of Herbord the knight consumed a great part of the city together with the church of Saint Paul.

Also in the same year Louis, duke of Bavaria, when he had gone to the Holy Land with a great army, was killed on his return at Kelheim with a knife by a certain Saracen, a messenger of the Old Man of the Mountains, in the presence of his household, the emperor Frederick having procured this crime, as was afterward confessed. The Saracen himself, however, was immediately cut to pieces by the duke’s attendants. The duke’s body, attended with great honor, was buried in the monastery of Scheyern in Bavaria. His son Otto succeeded him in the duchy; he took as wife Gertrude, the only daughter of Henry, count palatine of the Rhine, who dwelt at Heidelberg, with whom he obtained that whole dominion; thus the duchy of Bavaria and the county palatine were joined into one. That Otto begot from Gertrude, his aforesaid wife, two sons, Henry and Louis, of whom we shall speak in their proper place.

This duke Otto gave the town of Braubach, with the adjacent castle and all its appurtenances, in fief to the count of Katzenelnbogen; the landgrave of Hesse possesses it today, none of those counts now living.

In the same year Ulrich, abbot of Limburg of our order, in the diocese of Speyer, with the consent of his convent, gave to the chapter of the church of Mainz the pastoral church in Heldenberg, with its tithes and revenues, for the increase of the prebends of the church of Mainz, as is clear from their letters, as follows.
