*When, by whom, and in whose presence the dedication and consecration of the first monastery of Hirsau was made.*

Since at that time a grave dispute over the empire was being waged between Emperor Louis and his sons, it happened that the convocation of princes to which we have said Erlafrid was called was transferred from Frankfurt to the city of the Nemetes, which in common speech we call Speyer. Since this is not far from Hirsau, it gave the most Christian count occasion for the dedication of the newly founded monastery to be performed with greater solemnity. For many bishops, abbots, and princes had assembled, summoned to the imperial synod, and, at the invitation of Count Erlafrid, a good number of them took part in the office of dedication. Therefore in the year of the Lord 838, in the first indiction, in the month of September, Otgar, most reverend bishop of the church of Mainz, at the request of Count Erlafrid and his son Ermefrid, consecrated the monastery of Hirsau in honor of Peter, prince of the Apostles, and of Saint Aurelius, bishop, in the presence of many princes, bishops, and abbots who at that time were following the emperor’s court. Present there were Hildebold, archbishop of Cologne; Willeric, bishop of Bremen in Saxony; Ebo, bishop of Hildesheim; Thiagrius, bishop of Halberstadt; Rabanus Maurus, abbot of Fulda; Bruno, abbot of Hersfeld; [unclear] of Lindbert, the first abbot of this Hirsau; Hilduin, abbot of Saint-Denis, [unclear] archchaplain of the palace; Duke Henry; Duke Bernard; Ludolph, his son; Count Erlafrid, founder of the monastery; Ermefrid, his son; Count Ortrung; Count Adelhard; and many others, whose names, together with the foregoing, were not entered in the book of the deeds of Bishop Otgar, or perhaps were omitted through the carelessness of scribes. When the consecration of the church had been solemnly performed, as was the custom, Archbishop Otgar, together with the other bishops, abbots, and princes, [unclear] the body of the most holy prelate Aurelius, brought with great devotion from the chapel of Saint Nazarius, honorably [unclear] at the high altar; through his merit many miracles were performed. But when, after fifty years, under Emperor Arnulf, the Northmen were devastating Worms, Abbot Hardrad, fearing that such holy relics might by some fraud or violence come into the hands of foreigners, and with the counsel of the brothers added to this, made an underground vault in the church, in which he placed the body of the precious confessor of Christ Aurelius in a stone sarcophagus, with an inscription of his name and dignity, and again covered that chamber with earth in such a way that thereafter no one could suspect that anything was hidden there. Thus so great a treasure of relics remained hidden beneath the earth for almost one hundred and seventy years.
