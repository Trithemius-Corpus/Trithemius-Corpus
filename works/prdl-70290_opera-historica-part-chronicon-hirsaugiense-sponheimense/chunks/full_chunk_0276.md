*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

“If Mary remained a virgin after childbirth, she gave birth not to a man but to an angel; she was not a virgin after childbirth.” Likewise they said that they had twelve apostles, who each year surveyed the whole empire, and that two of them, senior in rank and profession, entered paradise each year and received from Elijah and Enoch the power of binding and loosing, which they communicated to others established in the same sect.

They mocked the sacrament of baptism, saying, “If baptism is a sacrament, then every bath is a sacrament, and consequently every bath-attendant would be God.”

They shamefully corrupted the sacrament of penance, confessing not to priests but to laymen generally, expressing their sins specifically, hoping that from this confession they would receive full remission of all sins, both from penalty and from guilt.

They did not believe in the sacrament of the Lord’s body, calling the consecrated host a fictitious God. They also called the sacrament of lawful marriage “sworn prostitution” in the vernacular.

They mocked the sacrament of extreme unction, and when asked what they thought about it, they answered unanimously: “We believe that vegetables are the better the more they have been seasoned with oil.” They said that the consecrations of churches and cemeteries, and the blessings of palms, salt, water, and the other things that are done in the church, were nothing.

Likewise they said that God neither knew nor punished evils that were done beneath the earth. Hence, gathering in underground caves and caverns, father with daughter, brother with sister, and son with mother indiscriminately practiced the foulness of their flesh.

They said that the Roman Church was not Christ’s but belonged to unbelieving gentiles, and they despised all the statutes of the Church together with its prelates and ministers. They mocked fasting; they ate meat at every season, even on the sixth day of Parasceve. They did not observe feast days, but worked on Easter day. They said that perjury was not a sin.

They denied that the merits and intercessions of any saints could provide assistance to human beings before God. But they also professed many other articles of error, the lengthy insertion of which would, I fear, cause weariness to these readers. There was a great number of them in Austria, Bohemia, and the neighboring lands. For, as a certain one of them burned at Vienna, named Novus Magister, confessed, there were at that time in Austria, Bohemia, and its borderlands more than eighty thousand persons, all sworn and professed into that sect, apart from those who were in other lands.

From this filth Bohemia, then infected, down to the present day abounds in the same errors and foul practices, as we shall say more fully below in the seventeenth year of Abbot Blasius. At that time many in Austria were burned in various places; all of them unanimously remained most obstinately in their errors until death, with great cheerfulness.

Among them was a beautiful girl named Giswa. When she was already about to be burned, she was asked by the judge whether she was still a virgin. She answered before everyone: “Above ground I am still a virgin, but underground by no means.” For the fools believed that virgins could not be deflowered underground, even if they were known by a thousand men, and that such illicit intercourse was not a sin.

In the sixteenth year of Abbot Henry, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1316, Louis, king of the Romans, with all the princes and cities consenting to him, decreed peace throughout the whole kingdom. But Henry, archbishop of Cologne, with those who followed the party of Frederick, did not observe this treaty of peace, and for that reason subjected himself and his diocese to many calamities.

For in the same year King Louis himself, with the assistance of Baldwin of Trier and Peter of Mainz, archbishops, accomplished many things against Frederick’s party. He also encircled with a siege the fortifications of Wiesbaden and Scharfenstein; when he made no progress there because of the strength of the places, he left the matter unfinished and turned to other things.

In the same year, after the apostolic see had been vacant for two years, three months, and seventeen days, the cardinals, at last assembling at Lyon, elected as pope on the fifth day of September James of Cahors, bishop of Porto; called John XXII, he presided over the Roman Church for eighteen years and four months.

In the same year also a battle was fought near Strasbourg between Louis, king of the Romans, and Frederick, duke of Austria, his adversary; in it Frederick’s army was routed and defeated by Louis, and was forced to flee again.
