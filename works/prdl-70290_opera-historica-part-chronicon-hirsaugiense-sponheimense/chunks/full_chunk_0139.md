For mountains were split apart; rivers, swallowed by the earth, were completely dried up, so that those who wished could have crossed on dry feet. The Po also, one of the four greater rivers of Europe, lifting itself high from its place, stood raised from the earth like an arch, so that between the earth and the water a path lay open for those passing through. After the water had hung in the air in this way for some time, at last it fell back upon itself from on high with such a crash that the sound of it was heard for many miles. When these dreadful signs had been reported throughout all Europe, everyone believed nothing other than that the last day was at hand. But by fasts and three days of prayers, performed throughout all Italy by all the people, they sought to mitigate the sentence of the Lord.

Meanwhile, while these proclaimed fasts were being carried out with great fear, at Cremona a little infant lying in a cradle, wrapped in swaddling-clothes, opened his mouth, contrary to nature, in his first act of speech: to his elder brother, who was tearfully asking for bread, and to his mother, who was preparing food for him, he said to both: “Cease, mortals, to prepare foods, O you who are soon to perish: behold, the holy Virgin Mary, Mother of God, before the tribunal of Christ, with most urgent prayers, is beseeching concerning the judgment of the dread sentence which the most just Judge himself had intended against the world because of the sins of men.” And although these signs terrified all who saw and heard them, nevertheless they corrected few. For in the same year many evils were committed by men, especially in Gaul and Germany: unjust conspiracies, burnings, murders, burnings of churches, seizures of the goods of those serving God in them. Citizens rise up against citizens, despoil one another, plunder goods, destroy and cut down cities, castles, villages, and houses; they tear apart and kill men not in human fashion but like cattle. There was an eclipse of the moon in the same year, namely on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of July; on the fourth day before the Ides of December.

A wondrous sign also, mixed with various colors, appeared in the sky on the seventeenth day before the Kalends of January.

1117.

In the twelfth year of Abbot Bruno, which was the 1118th year of the Lord’s Nativity, Buggo, the most reverend bishop of the church of Worms, who had succeeded Eppo, consecrated that church of his at Worms, renewed with handsome stone paneling, on the Ides of June, many bishops being present there: Bruno of Trier, Eberhard of Eichstätt, Albuin of Merseburg, Hartwig of Meissen. In the same year, on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of February, Pope Paschal II died, who had formerly been of our order. John succeeded him, a monk of the Cassinese monastery of our order, cardinal deacon and archchancellor, a man most renowned for learning in letters and integrity of conduct; he was called Pope Gelasius II and presided over the Church for only one year. He had first been a monk of the monastery of Cassino under Subabbot Oderisius; then, because of his admirable knowledge and the probity of his life, he received from Urban II the office of secretary in the Roman Church. Afterwards, made cardinal deacon and chancellor by Paschal, he was at last, as we have said, ordained pope. Cuno, legate of the apostolic see and bishop of Palestrina, sent as legate from Paschal to the kingdom of the Teutons, and confirmed by Gelasius through the bishop of [unclear], came to Cologne and celebrated there a glorious synod of bishops and abbots. Under the just and venerable Abbot Bruno, the substance of the monastery grew quite considerably, both through the offerings of the faithful and through prudent governance.
