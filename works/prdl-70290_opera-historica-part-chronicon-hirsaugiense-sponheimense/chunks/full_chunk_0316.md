*The Abbey of Schönau was a provostship in Liburna.*

And note that the aforesaid monastery had formerly been a provostship, built in that place which is called Lipron, where now there is a parish church with a little village, at a distance of half a mile; and it was subject by right to the monastery and abbot of St Saviour in Schaffhausen, in the diocese of Constance.

*1126.*

In the year 1126, Abbot Bernhelm began to dig and construct the well of this monastery, which still today remains sound, and completed it in a short time. For previously there was no water here in the monastery, but it was brought, not without labor, from the foot of the mountain, where the larger pond now is. He also completed various other buildings, which Count Megenhard had not yet wholly finished.

In this year Henry, the fourth emperor but the fifth king, died without children at Traiectum and was buried at Speyer; Lothar II, duke of Saxony, succeeded him by election of the princes.

*1127.*

In the year 1127, a certain nobleman of Sponheim, a kinsman of Abbot Bernhelm, together with his wife, named Judith, for the remedy of his soul, gave to this same [unclear] all his patrimony which he had in Bockenau in Gensingen, namely the church with tithes and all its appurtenances, serfs, fields, vineyard, woods, cultivated and uncultivated lands, pastures, meadows, mills, fisheries, waters and watercourses, to the altar of St Martin in this monastery at Sponheim, before many witnesses, as is clear in the letter of Lord Adalbert the elder, archbishop of Mainz, who confirmed the same donation, as follows:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Adalbert, by the grace of God archbishop of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, make known to the faithful of Christ’s Church, both future and [present?], that a certain nobleman named Udo, [unclear], and his wife Judith, while they still enjoyed the full powers of body and mind, for the remedy of his soul and those of his parents, delegated all their patrimony in Bockenau in Gensingen, the church with tithes and all its appurtenances, serfs, fields, vineyard, woods, cultivated lands, meadows, pastures, mills, fisheries, waters and watercourses, to the altar of blessed Martin of the monastery of Sponheim, to the abbot and brothers living there under the monastic rule, without any opposition, through the hands of the advocate, Count Megenhard of the castle of Sponheim, [unclear]. It also pleased the two aforesaid persons, namely Udo and his wife, since among the serfs they had some of higher and some of lower condition, that, insofar as nature, their own industry, and uprightness had raised them up, they should appoint them ministeriales for the abbot and brothers, and should grant no advocate any right over them; rather, among the better and foremost of the ministers of the other abbots of our episcopate, they should obtain equality in rank with full justice. The rest of the household, however, should remain under fiscal justice, namely so that it should pay each year to the altar of the aforesaid monastery a census of [nine?] coins. In order that this conveyance may remain firm and unshaken, we have caused this charter to be written, marked with the impression of our seal, and strengthened it with the bond of anathema: so that if anyone by perverse contrivance, which God forbid, should presume to [unclear] or annul this same conveyance, he shall lie under our ban, shall be [unclear] by the testimony of the truth itself, and shall know that he has incurred the offense of almighty God and St Martin. The witnesses of this matter are Abbot Folbert of St Alban’s, Werimbold, abbot of St James’s, Adellun, abbot of St [Disibod?], Bernhelm, abbot of the aforementioned monastery, the whole congregation, Richard, archdeacon of the house of blessed Martin, and many others.

This was done on the eighth day before the Kalends of January, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1127, in the fifth indiction, in the reign of Lothar, august king of the Romans, in the second year of his reign.

After this solemn donation had been made before many witnesses, Abbot Bernhelm peacefully obtained possession of the goods offered in Bockenau and Gensingen; from these, after 138 years, Peter, abbot of this place, conferred the church in Gensingen with the tithes in perpetuity upon the canons of the greater church of Mainz, as an increase of revenues, services, and all dues arising from this donation of Udo. But another estate, which is called the abbot’s, after 205 years William, abbot of Bickelheim, from his own patrimony handed over to blessed Martin and his brothers in the present monastery; concerning this also we shall speak below in its own time.
