*Chronicle of Trithemius. 1373.*

In the twenty-third year of our abbot Philip, Albert, bishop of Wurzburg, died. In his place Albert Heßburg was elected by the chapter; against him Emperor Charles obtained from Pope Gregory XI that Gerard of Schwarzburg be promoted as bishop, against the will of all the clergy and people of Wurzburg. Gerard, ordained bishop by the pope, presided for twenty-[eight?] years, and was hated by everyone because of his greed and the unusual exactions that he imposed.

In the same year the dukes of Bavaria sold the Margraviate of Brandenburg to Charles, emperor and king of Bohemia, for two hundred thousand florins, of which one hundred thousand were paid in cash. For the remaining one hundred thousand certain castles, namely Lauf, Hersbruck, Reichenfels, Sulzbach, Rosenberg, and Hirschau, were left in the hands of the dukes of Bavaria under the title of redemption for the aforesaid sum. But the other castles sold to the king of Bohemia in Bavaria were afterwards recovered by Rupert, king of the Romans, and restored by him to the dukes of Bavaria.

*1374.*

In this year, which was the twenty-fourth and last year of our abbot Philip, Pope Gregory XI, at the request of the same abbot Philip, instructed the scholasticus of St. Peter at Mainz to cause the goods unjustly alienated from the monastery to be restored to the right and ownership of the monastery, as is clear in the bull that follows.

Gregory, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to the beloved son, the scholasticus of St. Peter outside the walls of Mainz, greeting and apostolic blessing. Inclined by the prayers of our beloved sons, the abbot and convent in Sponheim, of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, by the authority of these presents we command you that whatever you find has been unlawfully alienated or disposed of from the goods of that monastery, you take care lawfully to recall to the right and ownership of the same monastery, compelling opponents by ecclesiastical censure, appeal being set aside. And witnesses who have been named, if they withdraw through favor, hatred, or fear, you shall compel by a similar censure, appeal ceasing, to give testimony to the truth. Given at Avignon, the seventh day before the Kalends of March, in the fourth year of our pontificate.

But what the obtaining of this commission accomplished or availed, I do not find, because not long afterwards Abbot Philip died, and Craffto, succeeding him, did not rule very profitably.

In the same year, in the month of February, there was an immense inundation of waters. For the Rhine rose so much that it passed over the walls of the city of Cologne; the town of Bingen too was for the greater part filled with waters, to such an extent that all the gates toward the Rhine and the Nahe were closed, covered by the waters up to the top of the walls. This flooding of the Rhine did many evils and injuries to people dwelling beside the Rhine: for it overturned villages, drowned people, cast down churches and many buildings, and reduced vineyards, gardens, hedges, and roads around the Rhine to nothing.

In the same year also a manic affliction infected many people, which affliction people call the dance of St. John. It attacked many, women and maidens as well as young men, and made them fugitives from their homeland and kin, [unclear]; first, falling to the ground, they foamed, and afterwards, rising, danced until collapse, unless they were restrained by others with a very strong binding. Many deceptions also occurred on this occasion, for many, either so that they might obtain money by begging, or so that they might more freely satisfy their lust, feigned the disease.

In the same year also, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of October, Philip, the fifteenth abbot of this monastery, died, when the twenty-fourth year of his rule had been completed, with one month and twelve days; he was buried in the middle of the choir before the steps of the presbytery. A stone with an inscription was placed over his tomb, which Johannes Colnhausen, the first abbot in the reformation, removed in order to build the refectory.

*Concerning Craffto the Second, the sixteenth abbot of this monastery, who presided for fifteen years, eleven months, and ten days; with various deeds of that time.*
