*1150.*

In the twenty-ninth year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1150, fire and water brought about countless disasters and losses throughout various parts of the world. That whole year, full of miseries, was a portent of the one following. The winter was very severe and excessively long. Fiery battle-lines were seen in the sky. The whole city of Utrecht, with its churches, was consumed by fire; there a certain wooden cross, falling from on high, while the roof of the church with its beams fell down upon it, remained unharmed [unclear]. Even after it had lain for a very long time in the fire, it was afterwards found uncorrupted, except only that the iron nails of the crossbeam, by which the image of the Lord was held, had melted. This cross, in memory of the miracle that occurred, is honorably preserved in the church of St. Boniface.

In these times Pope Eugenius III came to Trier with eighteen cardinals and many bishops and abbots, invited by Adelbero, archbishop of that city, who for three months provided the pope and all the others who were present, at his own expense, most abundantly with food and drink. There Pope Eugenius dedicated the newly repaired church of the monastery of St. Matthew the Apostle on the day before the Kalends of February. He also held a synod there of bishops, cardinals, and abbots; there Henry, archbishop of Mainz, and St. Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux, reported to the supreme pontiff the marvels that God was working in his handmaid Hildegard, abbess of Mount St. Rupert the Confessor.

When he had heard these things, the pope was astonished, and, wishing to investigate the matter more diligently, sent Adelbert, bishop of Verdun, with other trustworthy men to St. Hildegard, so that with humility and gentleness, without noise or curiosity, they might inquire of her what the matter was. When they had come to the monastery in which the holy virgin was living, and, according to the apostolic commission, were humbly inquiring the truth from her, she simply [unclear]. After these things had been examined for the pope, he ordered the volumes of the holy virgin that had been sent to him to be read publicly before the cardinals, archbishops, abbots, and all the clergy; and he himself, performing the office of reader, read no small part, through which the minds of all were turned to amazement, and their voices to praise [unclear]. He gloriously granted her [the authority?] of writing whatever she had learned through the Holy Spirit, and encouraged her to write down without fear the things revealed to her. The tenor of his letter was:

“Eugenius, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to his beloved daughter in the Lord, Hildegard, prioress on Mount St. Rupert, greeting and apostolic blessing. We marvel, O daughter, and we marvel beyond what can be believed, that God in our times is now showing new miracles, since he has so filled you with his spirit that you are said to see, understand, and utter many secrets. This we have learned from certain truthful persons [unclear] having [the keys?] of knowledge, so that we might close and open; and do we neglect, through folly, to do this prudently? We rejoice in the grace of God, and we rejoice also in your love; and we admonish you of this, that you know that God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble. Therefore preserve and guard through humility the grace that is in you, so that the things which you perceive in the spirit should be brought forth you may prudently bring forth, whereby you will hear that saying from the Lord: ‘Open your mouth, and I will fill it.’ What you have signified to us concerning that place which you foresaw there in the spirit, let this be done with our permission and blessing and that of your bishop, namely, that you live there regularly with your sisters according to the Rule of St. Benedict, within the enclosure of that same place.”

*The birth of Blessed Hildegard.*

To these things the virgin of Christ, divinely inspired, sent letters of reply back to the supreme pontiff, in which she admonished him concerning the secrets that he had in his heart, and indicated the divine will and good pleasure concerning these matters. This most blessed virgin Hildegard was born from the county of Spanheim, in the village called Bickelnheim, begotten of her father Hildebert and her mother Mechthild, in the times of Emperor Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1098, in the sixth indiction. Her father Hildebert, a noble man, served as a knight in the court of Count Meginhard of Spanheim, a most noble and at that time very powerful man; he was rich and honored in the world.
