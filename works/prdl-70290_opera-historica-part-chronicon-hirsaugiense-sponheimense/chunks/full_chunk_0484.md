*Of Sponheim.*

Eberhard gave the place the name Clausa Eberhardi. Jacob, archbishop of Trier, confirmed the foundation of this monastery, and consecrated the new church in his own person. The same archbishop of Trier reformed the convent of the Minors at Koblenz, and established the well-reformed canons regular of Windesheim in the new monastery not far from Koblenz, on a certain island of the Rhine near Vallendar.

*1455.*

In this year, which was the tenth of Conrad our abbot, Duke Louis, surnamed the Black, count in Veldenz, son of Stephen, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, and count in Zweibruecken, and brother of Duke Frederick in Simmern, rose against Frederick the Palatine, guardian of Philip our prince, and carried out plundering and burnings here and there in his principality.

Duke Frederick warned him by letters once, a second time, and a third time, in charitable fashion, to desist from his evil undertakings and not rashly to violate another's right by injury. But when he saw that he was being despised by him, judging that the matter must no longer be overlooked, he encircled with a siege the town of Bergzabern, which Duke Louis held as a garrison; after three days and five weeks of assault he finally took it on the day of Saint Tiburtius the martyr. But afterwards, in a certain agreement, at the insistence of friends, he restored the aforesaid town of Bergzabern to the aforesaid Duke Louis, under this condition namely: that he should presume and do nothing thereafter against the Palatine. This, however, as will be clear, he did not keep. Thereafter the high-spirited prince Frederick, count palatine aforesaid, provoked by the insolence of Richard of Hohenburg, besieged the castle called Cleeburg, not far from Weissenburg, and took and acquired it with all its appurtenances in two days.

In the year of the Lord noted above, on Tuesday after Palm Sunday, Gottfried, bishop of Wuerzburg, died. John of Grumbach succeeded him and presided for twelve years, a rather warlike man; although unlearned, nevertheless a lover and defender of the clergy, and he ruled his church well.

In these times also, in the aforesaid diocese of Wuerzburg, not far from Iphofen, in a certain place called Bircklingen, in a wilderness, a certain image of the blessed Mary ever virgin, placed in a certain tree, began to shine with many miracles. To it there was a very great concourse of people, and many offerings of the faithful were expended, through which in the course of time a monastery of regulars of the Windesheim reform was built and sufficiently provided for.

*1456.*

In the eleventh year of Conrad our abbot, the sixteenth provincial chapter of our order was held in the monastery of Saint Peter in Erfurt, on the third Sunday after the feast of the Lord's Resurrection, with continuation, as is customary, on the following days. Presiding in it were Berthold of Saint Stephen's at Wuerzburg, John of Saint Michael's at Hildesheim, Ulrich in Blaubeuren, and Lupert of Saint James's near Mainz, distinguished abbots of monasteries of our order. Many statutes were made in this chapter for the reform and preservation of our order.

In the same year, on the eighth day of the month of May, Jacob of Sierck, archbishop of Trier, died in Palatiolum; his body was brought back to Trier and is buried before the high altar in the church of the blessed Mary ever virgin, in the seventeenth year of his pontificate, a prelate to be celebrated with every praise, who not only kept his own church in peace and tranquility but also often restored peace among princes and the pontiffs of other churches. After his death, the canons of the greater church met on the twenty-first day of the month of June to elect a new pontiff, and, after a long discussion among themselves, were divided into two groups. One part elected Diether of Isenburg, canon of the churches of Mainz and Trier, who afterwards was made archbishop of Mainz, as arch-prelate; the other part, superior in number, elected John, son of Jacob, margrave of Baden, brother of Margrave Charles, then in the twenty-second year of his age. Although Diether tried very hard to obstruct his confirmation at the Apostolic See, nevertheless John prevailed, and was confirmed by Pope Calixtus III, and presides over the church of Trier up to the present day, sound and unharmed, in the forty-sixth year of his pontificate and the sixty-eighth of his age.

In the same year also, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of April, Reinhard of Helmstatt, bishop of Speyer, died, and is buried in the middle of the church. Sifrid of Venningen succeeded him, elected by the chapter on the twenty-ninth day of the month of March, and he presided for three years, five months, and four days; he did many good things for the church in so short a time.
