At this time there also flourished in England St. Elphegus, formerly abbot of the monastery of our order called Bath, first bishop of Winchester and afterward archbishop of Canterbury, a learned and most holy man, who, after many labors undertaken for the love of Christ, was crowned with martyrdom. His feast is observed on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of May.

In these days there also flourished in the city of Trier St. Simeon, monk of Mount Sinai, a Greek by nation. Poppo, archbishop of Trier, returning from overseas regions, brought him with him and, for the love of Christ, enclosed him in the aforesaid city, in a certain tower, to lead the life of a hermit. For he was a man of many virtues, and one who endured much from demons and wicked men. After his death he was immediately enrolled in the catalogue of the saints, and began to shine with countless miracles. Everwin, abbot of Dolcia, of whom we have already spoken, described his life in a brilliant style. I have in my possession three leaves of the Greek Psalter of this most holy man Simeon; because of his merit I value them as highly as if I possessed a precious treasure of gold and silver. His feast is kept on the Kalends of June.

Around these times there also flourished St. Gerard, formerly a monk of our order, bishop of Morisena in Hungary. After he had preached the word of God to the nations for a long time, he was afflicted with very severe torments by the impious and pagans, and suffered martyrdom for our faith.

Saint William also, abbot of Dijon, of our order, flourished in this same age: a learned man and of most holy life, burning with the greatest zeal for regular discipline. In life and after death he was renowned for many miracles in the diocese of Liege and throughout all Gaul, distinguished also for the performance of many miracles; in his time he magnificently restored monastic discipline, which had almost collapsed. His feast is kept on the eighth day before the Kalends of February.

Richard also, abbot of Verdun, [unclear] of Stavelot. In these same times there also flourished Meginfrid, formerly a monk of a monastery of our order, renowned in these times, whose most holy life was marked by many virtues; by his learning and his religion he brought many back to the path of a more corrected life.

Around these times there also flourished Brando, formerly abbot and then bishop of the aforesaid church for twenty-seven years. Around these times there also flourished St. Burchard, near Wurzburg, sixth archbishop of the church of Magdeburg, appointed by St. Henry, a learned and very religious man, who presided over the aforesaid church for sixteen years. He built a monastery of our order near the city of Hildesheim in honor of St. Bartholomew the Apostle, which in our times is called St. Gotthard after his name, since he was buried in it.

In these days Poppo, son of Liudolf, duke of Austria, archbishop of Trier, of whom we spoke before, who brought St. Simeon the monk with him from the monastery of Blessed Mary in Bethlehem to Trier, is held to have been very renowned. He is said to have converted the Danes to the faith of Christ, as is contained in the deeds of the Treveri, and he obtained from Pope Benedict the canonization of the same St. Simeon on account of the very great performance of miracles. He gloriously presided over the church committed to him by God for forty-one years. He died in the year of the Lord 1057, in the tenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Kalends of July.
