Saint Anselm wrote back to him from England in this manner:

To the lord and reverend father William, abbot of the brothers of Hirsau, Anselm, unworthy minister of the church of Canterbury: in this present life lasting prosperity, in the life to come eternal happiness. From the time when your [unclear] deigned to grant me knowledge of itself, I conceived so great an affection that your memory is always sweet to me; I have learned that this has not happened undeservedly, since your life is worthy to exist in love and reverence, and your fatherhood is not without a feeling of sweetness toward my littleness. For often brothers coming from you have not only edified me by report of your holiness, but have honored me by the greeting of your condescension. In this matter, although whatever is in my whole self can scarcely answer to such merits, it is nevertheless mine to show what love and reverence I can. But committing these things to be preserved and increased by God in us, I shall answer, not presumptuously but rather obediently, to those matters on which your prudence has deigned to consult my insignificance: perhaps not what suffices for the subject, but what seems right to me.

Concerning priests who, by open commerce of lust, show themselves reprobate and rejected by God, what apostolic providence has established with ecclesiastical and just rigor must be held altogether.
