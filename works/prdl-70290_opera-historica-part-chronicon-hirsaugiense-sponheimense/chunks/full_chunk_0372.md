*1273.*

In this year, which was the ninth of Peter our abbot, the princes of the Roman Empire, assembled at Frankfurt, after many deliberations at last unanimously elected as king of the Romans Rudolf, count of Habsburg, then absent and besieging Basel with a great army. When he heard that he had been elected king of the Romans, he at once raised the siege and came to Frankfurt; then, proceeding to Aachen, he received the office of consecration according to custom. There the princes, having sworn obedience, received their feudal holdings from his hand, and they stood firmly by him against all the adversaries of the empire. Therefore in a short time he recovered many things that had been alienated from the empire, and restored peace in the land. He reigned eighteen years and ten months. He had as wife Helizabeth, daughter of Burchard, count of Haerloch, who bore him three sons: Albert, duke of Austria, who afterward was emperor of the Romans, from whom all the dukes of Austria who are today, or have existed in the meantime, were begotten; Hartmann, who was drowned in the Rhine; and Rudolf, duke of Swabia, who took as wife the daughter of Ottocar, king of Bohemia. Of his daughters, the first married Wenceslaus, king of Bohemia; the second, named Machtildis, married Louis, count palatine of the Rhine, as we shall say; the third married Albert, duke of Saxony; the fourth married Charles, prince of Apulia.

In the same year Louis, count of Montfort, the count of Helfenstein, the count of Duckenberg, Count Eberhard of Wurttemberg, with eleven others, conspiring against King Rudolf, sought with all their might either to capture or to kill him. When this became known to him, he brought forth a great army against them, defeated them, and compelled them to obedience to him and to the empire forever. He restored secure peace throughout all the surrounding land, from Italy as far as England.

In the same year Peter, abbot of this monastery, seeing that that division of revenues and proceeds made three years earlier between himself and the convent was the beginning of every future imputation and no small detriment to his authority, grieved vehemently over it. Though all resisted, except Ottuvinus the prior, he publicly revoked it. The monks, angered by this and as if turned to madness, resisted with all their strength; and, after forming a conspiracy among themselves, they resolved rather to kill the abbot than to resign the portion of property once granted to them. When this became known to the abbot, giving place to wrath, he withdrew to Kreuznach and stayed there for more than the space of a year, until at last, when the stronger leader of the faction had died, the matter was composed and settled through the count of Sponheim, the lord.

*1275.*

In the eleventh year of Peter our abbot, Rudolf, king of the Romans, moved an army into Bohemia against King Ottocar, who, after the death of the legitimate heir, had unjustly occupied Austria, Styria, Carinthia, and Moravia against the jurisdiction of the empire. After an engagement was fought, he defeated and subdued him, and imposed upon him agreements of submission. But when Ottocar afterward scorned to keep what he had promised, Rudolf returned again into Bohemia, overcame the perjured king a second time, killed him, and subjected the kingdom of Bohemia to himself. Furthermore he gave the duchy of Austria to his son Albert, count of Habsburg, from whom Maximilian, today king of the Romans, descends in a direct line of generation continued as follows. Rudolf, king of the Romans from being count of Habsburg, begot Albert, duke of Austria. Albert begot Albert the younger by Helizabeth, daughter of the count or duke of Carinthia, Meynhard. Albert, moreover, begot Lupold, duke of Austria, by Johanna, daughter of the count of Pyrreth. Lupold then begot Ernest the duke, and Ernest begot Frederick the third, emperor, by Simburga, daughter of Duke Alexius of Ancazon in Poland. Frederick the emperor begot King Maximilian. Maximilian begot Philip, duke of Austria, Burgundy, and Brabant, and Maria, queen of Spain, concerning whom it will be said in their place.
