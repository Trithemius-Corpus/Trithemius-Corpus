At about these times a new reform of the order began in the monastery of Bursfeld, in the diocese of Mainz in Saxony, which Henry, formerly count of Northeim, had founded around the year of the Lord 1098; from it our observance has been called the Bursfeld Union, and it flourishes to the present day. This reform took its beginning from the monastery of St. Matthias near Trier, over which that distinguished abbot, Master Johannes Rode, then presided. Through him, with Archbishop Otto cooperating, all the monasteries of our order which are around Trier were brought back to a better condition.

This Johannes Rode was a learned, upright man of great devotion. Although he was a canon of the church of Metz, provost of St. Simeon, and official of the Trier court, he despised everything for the love of Christ and became a monk in the Charterhouse near Trier. Since he was a man very learned, mature in life and conduct, and very strict and zealous for the observance of the order, Otto, archbishop of Trier, with the pope's consent, drew him out of the Charterhouse and appointed him abbot of the monastery of St. Matthias. There he soon restored monastic life according to the rule of our holy father Benedict, instituted statutes and ceremonies, and instructed the monks in all religion and holiness by word and example.

After the monastery of St. Matthias had been reformed, at the request of the duke of Brunswick he sent brothers to Bursfeld, with the ordinance, statutes, and customs which he had given. Johannes, abbot of Bursfeld, received them with great joy and devotion, and most zealously conformed himself in all things to their holy seniors. This Johannes had once been a monk in Reynhusen; his abbot had sent him by way of procuration to the Council of Constance, at that same time, namely, when all the abbots of the province of Mainz, as we said above, had been summoned to Constance by the council to hold a Chapter. There, when he had seen the zeal and diligence of the fathers for the reform of the order, and had heard how dangerous it is for a monk to live in deformity, he was moved to compunction [unclear] for the reform of the order; and he diligently urged them to amend their life and to undertake the promised and enjoined reform under the penalties prescribed.

At first they began to mock him, and afterwards even to hate him and afflict him with abuse. Seeing that, though he strove, he made no progress, and wishing to provide for his own salvation, he went to Agnes, wife of Otto of Brunswick, sister of the landgrave of Thuringia, who was then staying at Gamundia, not far from Reynhusen, a woman most devout in Christ. He reported the acts and conclusions of the fathers in the council, and asked for help by which he might satisfy his oath. When the holy woman recognized his holy purpose, she promised that she would provide counsel and assistance.

Meanwhile it happened that the abbot of the monastery of Clus died. When the duchess learned this, she prevailed upon the duke her husband that the aforesaid monk of Reynhusen should succeed the dead man as abbot, and this was done. Once that monk Johannes had been appointed abbot in Clus, he applied every effort to reform, and began to go before his subjects in religion according to the rule. When the monks, accustomed to corrupt ways, saw this, they all fled from him. But he, having gathered a few novices, instructed them in the purity of the rule, and living in great poverty began to serve God in all humility.

At that time the above-mentioned monastery of Bursfeld had fallen into such misery that, for its support, there was no income apart from a single cow.
