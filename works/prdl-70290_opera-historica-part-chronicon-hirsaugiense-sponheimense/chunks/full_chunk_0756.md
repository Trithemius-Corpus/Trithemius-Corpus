*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

I urge you, since repentance is profitable, and consider that your letter of license does not extend to the deformed. I promise that I will take care that you have an honorable place in observance, either with me or elsewhere as you please. Farewell, sound in mind and body, receiving charitably the admonitions of a friend. I shall write to you more fully about these matters at another time. Farewell again, remembering us before God. From Würzburg, on the first day of the month of September, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*LVI.*

*To the Most Reverend Father and Lord in Christ, Matthaeus Lang, Bishop of the Church of Gurk and Provost of Augsburg, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Saint James in the suburb of Würzburg, sends greetings.*

*Joh. Rummel.*

Most reverend prelate, the man who presented these little letters of mine to your most reverend paternity, Johannes called Rummel of Nuremberg, asserting that he had been sent by the royal majesty, came to me some time ago and tried in many ways to persuade me that, in order to approach that same majesty, I should undertake the journey with him toward the city of Augsburg. For he said that he had received thirty-two ducats for travel expenses from the hands of your highness in the king’s name, and that he had been ordered to see that I was presented as soon as possible before his sacred gaze. But because he produced no letters, neither royal ones nor yours, by which he might show that truth supported his assertions, I began to have some doubt, lest perhaps he himself had been the solicitor before the royal majesty concerning me, promising certain great things, useful to his majesty, which I had never professed. I am a man established in the monastic religion under authority, to whom it is not permitted to wander lightly outside the monastery without evident reason. Therefore I have thought it right humbly to ask your most reverend paternity that, if he announced true things to me, and things entrusted by you in the king’s name, you would deign to hold my absence reasonably excused; but if it was otherwise, do not reckon it to me as presumption that I wrote, having been deceived by the man. Indeed, if he had shown me the necessary letters, who am I, who would not most greatly desire to please the imperial majesty, although I know nothing in myself worthy of so great a highness? For our studies promise nothing great: they are humble, such as are accustomed not to delight great princes, but to suit men of middling station. Nevertheless, if there is any strength in me that could delight the royal magnificence, the bearer will open ways by which I may be permitted to speak with the prince in person. Farewell, glory of bishops. Würzburg, the first of September, in the year 1507.

*LVII.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James in the suburb of Würzburg, to Eberhard Senst, dean of the same Church of St. James of Bamberg, royal chaplain, sends greetings.*

That I send my letters to you, though I am unknown to you, most humane sir, the bearer of the same, known to you, has brought about. For he reported to me that you pursue me with great favor, and often mention my name with praise before the Majesty, and that you especially desire both to have acquaintance with me and familiar friendship, and that for this reason you have solicited the imperial majesty to have me stand in his presence, to confer especially about those matters that pertain to the publication of histories. I give you undying thanks for your goodwill toward me, which I shall also, if ever it is permitted, take care to repay according to my strength. But I have deferred coming in person to the imperial majesty on this occasion because I feared being marked down for levity if I had believed myself summoned by the royal majesty at the urging of one unknown man, when there were no testimonies of letters present by which the majesty’s good faith could have been established. I would be judged by all to be frivolous and full of ambition if I were so easily moved by persuasions confirmed by no proof. Therefore, if the royal Majesty wants me to be present before him, let him make me more certain by letters. For I have no intention of wandering anywhere at the urging of an unknown man; to his wishes in this matter I have yielded only so far as to give my letters to you, by which occasion might be offered to you of writing to me and of revealing your mind not by another’s words, but by your own hands. Farewell, remembering us. From Würzburg, on the first day of the month of September, in the year of the Lord one thousand five hundred seven.
