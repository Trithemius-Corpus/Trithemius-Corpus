*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

Around these times Count Eberhard and Countess Ida built in Swabia a monastery of our order, called Schaffhausen, which they endowed with great estates and goods. Their son was Udo, archbishop of Trier after Eberhard, of whom we have spoken above.

In these times Marianus flourished, a Scot by nation, first a monk of St Pantaleon at Cologne, then of Fulda, and finally a recluse at Mainz for the love of Christ, a man devoted to God and studious in the divine Scriptures. He wrote a concise chronicle from the beginning of the world down to the year 1084, neatly divided by notation of the years; its reading could be useful enough and pleasing to students for knowledge of chronology.

*1064.*

In the year of the Lord 1064, which was the 52nd with the monastery vacant, in the twelfth indiction, Saint Cunno, whom the emperor had sent to the church of Trier to receive the insignia of the then-vacant archbishopric, was martyred by Count Theodoric, because he had been appointed by the king without the election of the people; and on the third day, after being hurled down from a mountain, he passed to the Lord. Buried in the monastery of Tholey of our order, he soon began to become renowned through many signs. Afterwards the count, intending to do penance for the crime he had committed, went to Jerusalem and there ended his life. All the others who had consented to the crime were soon consumed and perished by an evil death. Udo, son of Count Eberhard, of whom we have already spoken before, succeeded him. In the same year also Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, Gunther, bishop of Bamberg, Otto, bishop of Regensburg, William, bishop of Utrecht, together with many others, more than seven thousand in number, set out for Jerusalem for the sake of prayer and devotion, and scarcely two thousand returned.

In this year also, when Arnulph, bishop of Worms, a man of episcopal modesty and holiness, died, Adelbero, a monk of St Gall of our order, brother of Duke Rudolf, succeeded him, altogether lame in one foot, a man in every respect worthy of spectacle. For he was of great strength, excessive voracity, and such corpulence that it struck those who saw him with horror rather than admiration.

In these times Williram flourished, from schoolmaster of Bamberg a monk of Fulda [unclear] he flourished in Saxony, a man very learned in both divine and secular writings, practiced in verse and prose, who for many years illuminated the public school of the monks in the same monastery by teaching and writing various things; not least learned also in secular letters, he had taught philosophy gloriously for many years in the Parisian school. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote three little books on the Song of Songs, divided between verse and prose. He also wrote several other things not yet seen by us.

Bernard too, a monk of Corvey of our order, in the same times [unclear] in Saxony, by teaching and writing, illuminated various things. He described in verse, not inelegantly, the life and passion of St Cyriacus the martyr, together with many other things which have not yet reached us.

Theoderic too, abbot of St Trudo of our order, most learned, fully instructed both in secular letters and in the divine Scriptures, wrote in elegant verse the life of Trudo, and also of Saint Bavo, Saint Reinold, and very many others.

In these times Saint Arnulph also flourished, a monk of the monastery of Blessed Medard at Soissons. Saint Arnulph.

Saint Maurilius also, from being a monk of the monastery of Fécamp of our order, archbishop of Rouen after the deposition of Malger, is held to have been renowned in these times, a man adorned with the beauty of many virtues, an outstanding cultivator of charity and abstinence; concerning his holy manner of life exalted proclamations of praise are reported.
