*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

In the same year war was again waged between Rudolf and King Henry, and Rudolf himself fell with many others. He was carried by his men to Merseburg, where he died and was buried. It is said that, when he was at the point of death and looked at his severed right hand, he said with a heavy sigh to the bishops who happened to be present: “Behold, this is the hand with which I confirmed by oath my fidelity to my lord Henry; alas, at your urging I did not keep it, and behold, wretched as I am, I am compelled to forsake life together with the kingdom. See, you who made me ascend the throne against my lord, whether you have led me by the right path as I followed your counsels.” Having said this, he expired. In his place the Saxons and the princes of Alemannia elected a certain Hermann, a very powerful nobleman from Germany; but before long he began to be regarded with contempt as a stranger, and therefore after a few years, laying aside the title of king, he betook himself to Henry’s favor.

*1081.*

In the twelfth year of Abbot William, and in the 1081st year of the Lord’s nativity, King Henry sought Italy with an army; and coming to Rome on the vigil of Pentecost, with Pope Gregory and the Romans resisting him, he set up fortifications before the castle of Saint Peter. There also, though harassed for two years by the frequent attacks of the townsmen, with a small force he performed many deeds bravely. He therefore besieged the city continuously for two years, and at last took it in the year of the Lord 1083, in the sixth indiction, on the fourth day before the Nones of June, which was the Friday of Pentecost week. Pope Gregory, however, fleeing secretly, withdrew to Salerno, and remained there until the day of his death. In his place, at the request of the Romans, King Henry appointed Wighert, bishop of Ravenna, as pope; he was called Clement, the third of this name. *King Henry took Rome by besieging it.* This [installation?] of Wighert the antipope was made while the siege of the city was continuing. In a synod of thirty bishops, as we have said, at Brixen, they falsely accused [unclear] of having invaded the apostolic see by the art of magic, necromancy, and the aid of demons, and with many other falsehoods they deposed him in his absence. When Pope Gregory heard this, after an assembly of bishops and abbots had been gathered, he excommunicated the invader of the apostolic see together with all who had been in the synod of Brixen, and also King Henry with all his supporters, and cursed them with great severity.

*Mainz is consumed by fire together with the church.*

In the same year also of Abbot William, namely the twelfth, the city of Mainz was for the greater part consumed by fire, together with the principal church and three monasteries. Henry, the fifth king of this name, was born. The monastery at Bamberg too was burned on the vigil of holy Easter.

*The marchioness gives expenses to the abbot.*

At that time a certain powerful and wealthy margrave named Hermann, drawn by the divine spirit and despising the world with all its possessions and pomp for the love of God, fled secretly, with no one except his wife knowing, and went unknown to the monastery of Cluny; and, having received from Saint Hugh the abbot the habit of holy conversion, for love of the supreme Shepherd he became shepherd of the livestock of that same monastery.
