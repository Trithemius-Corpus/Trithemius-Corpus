Strabus also, a monk of Fulda, the pupil and scribe of Rabanus, flourished in the same times. He wrote many various works, as we have declared in other published books on writers. He began the Chronicle of the monastery of Fulda, which, continued by others who succeeded him, gave us no small help in the publication of this work. But Angelomus, a monk of the monastery of Luxeuil, appeared in this period most learned in expounding the divine Scriptures. Of him we have made mention elsewhere.

Ansegisus, formerly abbot of the monastery of Bobbio, archbishop of Sens, a man learned in the Scriptures and very highly celebrated in the councils of kings, wrote several works, of which an account is given elsewhere. John, abbot of Vercelli, a Scot by nation, very learned in Greek and Latin, flourished in these times. At the command of Charles, son of the first Emperor Louis, he was the first of all to translate the books of Saint Dionysius the Areopagite into Latin, and adorned them with commentaries not to be despised. He also composed a book of great subtlety On the Division of Nature, together with several other works which we have not seen. Alderic, abbot of the monastery of Ferrieres, of our order, after Jeremiah but immediately before Ansegisus archbishop of Sens, was made in these times. The bodies also of the saints Severus, bishop of Ravenna, Vincentia his wife, and Innocentia his daughter, were translated with the greatest honor to Mainz by Archbishop Otgar.

Heriolus, king of the Danes, with his wife and sons, is baptized at Mainz in the time of this first Abbot Linthert. A great part of the Danes are converted to the faith of Christ through the monks of the monastery of Corvey, who, out of love for heavenly happiness, had entered Dacia to preach the holy Gospel to the nations. Amalarius Fortunatus, a monk of Luxeuil, afterwards archbishop of Trier, and then cardinal of the holy Roman Church, wrote in these days to Emperor Louis books on the divine offices.

In the twenty-third year of Emperor Louis, under Linthert, the first abbot of this monastery, the Northmen first landed on the borders of Holland and Gaul; afterwards, for many years, wandering here and there with a strong army, they perpetrated boundless evils.

In the year of the Lord 838, which was the first year of Abbot Linthert, on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of August, Saint Frederick, the eighth bishop of the church of Utrecht, in the treasury of the same church, after the solemnities of Mass, while he was devoutly intent upon prayer, was cruelly slain by two attendants hired for pay by the empress Judith, and became a martyr of the highest truth. For the bishop had said to the emperor: "It is not lawful for you to have Judith, daughter of the duke of Bavaria, near to you in blood, as your wife: either cast her away, or I shall excommunicate you by the authority of the Almighty." Exasperated by these words, the impious Judith not long afterwards killed the herald of truth. When Gregory IV, the pope, heard of this monstrous crime, he placed Emperor Louis in the monastery of Soissons to perform penance. And Jesse, bishop of Amiens, placed a veil on the empress Judith, a second Herodias, and made her a nun.

By this empress Louis had a son, Charles, surnamed the Bald, who afterwards, in the year of the Lord 876, was made emperor. He appointed him duke of Bavaria, Swabia, and Burgundy, while his other sons by his former wife bore this with considerable displeasure.

Finally, in the year of the Lord 840, when Emperor Louis, after completing his penance, was pursuing his future [unclear] son Lothair, who had rebelled against his father, and came to the monastery of Hersfeld, he fell gravely ill; reduced to a cart and then by boat, he immediately died at the imperial palace of Ingelheim, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of July, in the twenty-sixth year since he had received the empire. His corpse was honorably buried at Metz, in the church of Saint Arnulf, by Drogo his brother, bishop of Metz. This Drogo was a son of the great Emperor Charles, brother of Louis the First, a learned man and a lover of religion; from a monk he was made bishop of the aforesaid church of Metz, for whose adornment he did much.

When the most pious Emperor Louis had died, his son Lothair succeeded him and ruled as emperor for sixteen years. He had three brothers by his father: Pippin, whom he made duke of Aquitaine; Charles the Bald, to whom in the paternal division the western part fell, from the British Ocean as far as the river Meuse; and Louis, who obtained the eastern kingdom, that is, all Germany as far as the streams of the Rhine, together with certain other adjacent cities and districts across the Rhine, perhaps because of the abundance of wine. Lothair himself, the eldest by birth, received in his portion the name of emperor together with the whole kingdom which to the present day we call from his name Lotharingia, although now it is a duchy, the whole province, and also all the kingdoms of Italy together with the city of Rome itself. He had as wife Egilla, daughter of the king of England, who bore him two sons, Lothair and Louis, later emperor.
