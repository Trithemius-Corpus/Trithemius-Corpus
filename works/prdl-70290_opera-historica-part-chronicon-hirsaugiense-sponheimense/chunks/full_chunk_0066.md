*Of the Hirsau Monastery*

*1056.*

In the year of the Lord 1056, in the fifty-fourth year of the monastery's vacancy, Pope Victor II, having departed from Italy, came into Germany, and was honorably received by the emperor at Goslar, around the feast of the Nativity of Blessed Mary, in the presence of many pontiffs, abbots, princes, and nobles.

In the same year Arnold, bishop of Speyer, died; Conrad succeeded him.

In the same year also Emperor Henry III, when he perceived that death was at hand for him, established Henry as king in the presence of the Roman pontiff, by the election of the princes; and, having arranged affairs as best he could, he died on the third day before the Nones of October. He was buried in the church of Speyer, Pope Victor II standing by with many other cardinals, patriarchs, bishops, and princes. He was succeeded by his son Henry, the fourth of this name, a boy of five years, and he reigned for fifty years. His mother, the empress Agnes, prudently governed the empire for some time. At length Saint Anno, archbishop of Cologne, by the counsel of the princes, took the boy king away from his mother, and governed the empire with others appointed for the purpose, until lawful age made the boy fit to rule by himself.

*1058.*

In the fifty-sixth year of the monastery's vacancy, in the second indiction, Pope Victor died. Frederick, abbot of the monastery of Monte Cassino, son of Duke Gothelon and brother of Duke Godfrey, of whom we have already spoken above, succeeded him in the pontificate, being called Stephen IX by name; he presided for ten months. In this same year also the city of Paderborn, together with two monasteries, was miserably burned by fire on the Friday before Palm Sunday.

At that time there was in the monastery of the same city, which today is called Abdinghof, a certain monk of our order named Paternus, a Scot by nation, who for many years [unclear], always devoutly intent upon contemplation. Foreknowing this very fire by the prophetic inspiration of the Holy Spirit, he foretold long before it happened that it would occur, if men neglected to appease God by saving penitence. But when the monastery already stood amid the flames, and the brothers, after breaking the locks, tried to draw him out of his enclosure, he would not consent, saying that he had long since committed himself to the divine will, [unclear] of his enclosure. The approaching flame compelled the brothers, anxious for their own lives, to flee as quickly as possible. But it consumed Paternus together with his cell.

In these times Lambert, monk of the monastery of Hersfeld of our order, flourished, a [religious?] and learned man. Among the other little works of his talent, he composed a chronicle of his monastery, brief indeed, but not useless. He also wrote, concerning the affairs of emperors, pontiffs, and princes carried out in Germany, a fine and not unpleasant work, which, beginning from the times of Charlemagne, who was the first of the Teutons to rule as emperor, he continued down to the year of the Lord 1077, filled with the remembrance of many events. Since he was contemporary with Emperor Henry IV, he described that emperor's deeds very fully in the same volume. From this very volume we have been helped not a little.

In the same times also flourished Anselm, a monk of Reims, a man learned in every branch of letters, who wrote the itinerary of the holy Pope Leo IX, of whom we have spoken before, from Rome into Germany and Gaul. In it he recounts his deeds in synods held in various places: namely, with how much justice and authority he examined ecclesiastical cases, [unclear] monastery.

Peter Damian also, formerly a monk of our order, bishop of Ostia and cardinal of the holy Roman Church, is held to have been famous in these times: certainly a man most learned among the learned men of his time, venerable no less for holiness than for erudition. He produced many distinguished volumes of his talent, whose titles we have fully arranged elsewhere. Out of love for holy religion he left his episcopate and returned to the monastery.
