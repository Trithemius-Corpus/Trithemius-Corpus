*Sponheim.*

In the aforesaid year John, surnamed Mariae, the twenty-ninth prior of this monastery, [died]. After his death the priorship was vacant for almost two years. For the monks were few in number, five in all, namely Peter of Blenich, Andreas of Traiectum, John, Conrad, and Ulrich, afterwards abbot.

*1461.*

In this year, which was the sixteenth of Conrad our abbot, Pope Pius II wrote and committed and commanded the bishop of Eichstatt that, having taken to himself proven, religious, and learned men, and having summoned those who were to be summoned, he should by apostolic authority reduce into one those three observances of our order newly restored in the German nation, Bursfeld, Melk, and Kastl, and any others there might be, compelling opponents to obedience through ecclesiastical censures. On account of this mandate many labors and expenses were meanwhile undertaken at various times, but up to this day no effect has followed, because of the excessive diversity of those three observances in many matters.

*The commission concerning the three observances is entrusted by the pope.*

In this same year Diether, archbishop of Mainz, was deposed by Pope Pius II, and provision was made by the same pope for Adolf of Nassau in the archbishopric. They say that the causes of this deposition were very many. The first was that he had not paid the pope the sum imposed on him for confirmation and for the pallium. The second, that within the time fixed for him he had not had himself ordained priest. The third, that he had treated the legates of the Apostolic See with irreverence and temerity, and had been disobedient to and opposed to their mandates. We do not judge it necessary to explain the other causes of the deposition.

*Diether, archbishop of Mainz, is removed by the pope. Cause of the removal.*

Against this injury and violence done to him, Diether, in no way wishing to be deprived of his archbishopric, invoked the aid of Frederick, the powerful Count Palatine, and deserved to obtain it. Thus with the help of the Palatine, Diether retained nine towns and cities in his obedience, while the rest defected from him. By reason of this assistance the same archbishop Diether gave and assigned to the aforesaid prince Frederick the Palatine the castle of Starkenburg, the towns of Heppenheim, Bensheim, and Morlebach, with all their appurtenances, for one hundred thousand florins, to be held for the use of the Palatinate until he should receive that sum of money from the church of Mainz. Adolf also, calling Louis the Black, duke and count of Veldenz, to his aid, assigned to him for pay the castle and village of Bockelheim, Sobernheim, Montzingen, Nussbaum, and the castle of [Vimam] not far from Mainz, with all their appurtenances, in pledge for a certain sum of money. All these Frederick the Palatine not long afterwards, as we shall say, powerfully tore from him by arms. To the margrave of Baden for assistance he gave Algesheim and Gau-Bickelheim, with all their appurtenances. He hired the count of Wurttemberg to his aid for twenty thousand florins, and likewise, having also purchased Wilhelm of Saxony as a helper, he gave to Henry, count of Schwarzburg, for wages and pay certain towns, castles, and villages in the Eichsfeld with good annual revenues. To the count of Konigstein, who had his sister as wife, he assigned the county of Hohenheim for his pay, lest he serve without reward. From this arose evils, and that once most famous church of Mainz was reduced almost to nothing.

*Frederick the Palatine assists Diether against Adolf.*

With all these the archbishop of Trier, the bishop of Metz, Johannes Nix, bishop of Speyer, Albert, margrave of Brandenburg, and many other counts, barons, and nobles adhered to Adolf against Diether. Therefore, first of all Diether himself with his men, having with him for help [unclear] Count Palatine and Philip, count of Katzenelnbogen, led an army against the Rheingau. At his arrival the men of the Rheingau abandoned their garrisons; but because of the severity of the weather the siege was soon broken off, since the army could not remain longer. After this there came to Diether an almost certain promise that, if he came again, the men of the Rheingau would pass into surrender to him. He therefore came again with power, and found the promise made to him concerning surrender empty and false.

*The Rheingau is besieged by Diether.*

But Duke Frederick, taking up a troop of strong men, moved his army against Ulrich, count of Wurttemberg, laying waste everything with intent of plunder as far as Stuttgart, the chief city of the counts of Wurttemberg.

*Frederick the Palatine lays waste the lands of Wurttemberg.*

Then the great-souled prince Palatine, together with Henry, landgrave of Hesse, moved his army against Louis the Black, duke and count of Veldenz, surrounding the village of Gau-Bickelheim with a siege. He shortly took it, utterly destroyed its walls, towers, and all fortifications, and led away from it as captives sixty horsemen, one hundred foot-soldiers very ready for wars, and all their engines.

*Gau-Bickelheim is taken by Frederick the Palatine.*
