*356. CHRONICLE OF TRITHEMIUS.*

…at the time of the last reformation, which was made in the year of the Lord 1469, [the privilege] was found among the letters of the county of Sponheim in the vault of the castle of Trarbach, and by lord Frederick—the illustrious and Christian prince, count Palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, our first reformer—was restored to us and to the monastery. Reckoning, therefore, the years from then to the reformation introduced, no less than 35 years flowed, in which the privileges of the monastery were lost—although not even today are all restored, as we are taught from certain copies and rescripts, whose originals we do not have. But also certain precious volumes of our library, which those former gluttons had left untouched, he carried off with him, which, dispersed in various places about Trarbach, will never be brought back to us. For (as we said before) rarely was there an abbot in the monastery, but he followed the count's court, and was first among his councilors. Whence meanwhile the state of the monastery, both in spiritual and in temporal [things], greatly declined—so much so that, on account of the excessive debts of the convent, [it] was frequently almost in excommunication; and [the brothers], on the principal feasts (as appears from their hand-registers), that they might be permitted to celebrate the divine [office], redeeming [it] with vexatious money, used to obtain for a time a relaxation of the censures from the judges of the see of Mainz. The term of the indulgence elapsed, they fell again into the censures as before, compelled to intermit the divine offices. And this lamentable calamity lasted for several years, up to the last reformation, [that] of Bursfeld, which was made in the year of the Lord 1465 [as printed]. [Margin: Abbot Gobelin carries off the books of the library. The state of this monastery was bad from [that] time.]

In the aforesaid year, the second of our abbot Gobelin, a most grievous, long-lasting contention arose between him and a certain Craffto, altarist of Blessed Mary ever-Virgin in the church of St Martin at Mainz, over twenty maldra of rye of annual rent, [which had been] promised (as is asserted), by a certain instrument, to the aforesaid altar from our goods in Blenich, by lord Bernhard the former abbot, without the knowledge or will of the convent—by what occasion or reason is not known. When, therefore, lord Gobelin the abbot, successor of Bernhard, by the counsel of the brothers altogether refused to give the aforesaid rye unless he were first taught why, he is first impeded before the secular judgment in Blenich by the same Craffto, claiming [arrears] of 40 years, and then is cited to the council of Basel. And this contention lasted 6 years, not without the greatest [and] most grievous expenses; at length a sentence was pronounced—by John, Dean of St Gangolf of Mainz, commissary of the aforesaid council of Basel—for the plaintiff over the possessory [right], against the abbot, to the grave loss and damage of the aforesaid monastery. For the abbot Gobelin himself, in his own person, was twice, on account of this matter, at the council of Basel; he often sent a monk of the brothers to Basel; he made many other expenses through 8 continuous years in the same cause, which he handled less advisedly. [Margin: Contention between the abbot and the chaplain of St Quentin. The abbot was twice at the council of Basel on account of this matter.]

In the same year, too, marvels were seen in heaven: for there appeared 7 circles in the manner of a rainbow, arch against arch, disposed in a marvelous order; and the winter of that year was hard enough, cold and long, which afflicted the poor exceedingly. [Margin: A marvelous sign appeared in heaven.]

Item, in the same year, the dukes of Austria and of Meissen, advancing with a great army of Christians to war against the Bohemian Hussites, slew more than 12 thousand of them. But in the same year, on the 7th day of the month of October, in the night, a horrendous, most violent wind, coming from the north, from the English sea up the ascent of the Rhine, raging through all the adjacent regions as far as Italy, both in the waters and on the land, inflicted infinite damages on men. For, overturning the strongest houses, towers, churches, and innumerable trees, it crushed many men of either sex and every age. [Margin: Expedition against the Hussites in Bohemia. A horrendous wind.]

In this year, which was the 3rd of abbot Gobelin, the provincial chapter of our order was celebrated the 26th day of the month of June—which was the Sunday next after the feast of the nativity of St John—at Basel, at the time of the general council, in the monastery of the Friars Preachers; in which presided Ortwin of St Peter in Erfurt, Frederick of St Mark in Reichenau, John of St George in Stein, and Henry in Faucibus, abbots of monasteries of our order. To this provincial chapter abbot Gobelin, by the authority of the council, was cited, and by the report of the Visitors accused before the presidents on many articles—chiefly that he had alienated privileges, letters of liberties, and muniments from the monastery, made no accounting, and for the most part resided outside the monastery. Who, answering on all these as he could, obtained from the council and chapter, through the mediation of Louis, count Palatine, that the abbot of St Matthias of Trier be deputed to him as commissary, who [should hear] the cause… [Margin: The provincial chapter is celebrated. Abbot Gobelin is cited to the chapter.]
