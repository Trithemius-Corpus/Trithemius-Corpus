*The Chronicle of Trithemius. Here the Cardinal Panormitanus grants an indulgence.*

In the aforesaid year Frederick, abbot of this monastery, using the counsel of his friends, approached Nicolaus Panormitanus, of our order, formerly an abbot in Sicily, priest-cardinal of the Roman Church, and legate *de latere* through Germany of the Council of Basel, who was then at Frankfurt on his legation; and he set forth the need and [unclear] of the present monastery, asking that through the granting of indulgences he would deign to stir the minds of the faithful to relieve such great poverty. The magnificent man freely consented to his just request and granted great indulgences of five years in the form that follows.

Nicholas, by divine mercy priest-cardinal of the basilica of the Twelve Apostles of the holy Roman Church, commonly called Panormitanus, legate *de latere* through Germany of the most holy general Synod of Basel, legitimately assembled in the Holy Spirit and representing the universal Church, to all and singular the faithful of Christ who will inspect the present letters, eternal salvation in the Lord. Although he, by whose gift it is that he is worthily and laudably served by the faithful, out of the abundance of his mercy, which exceeds the merits of suppliants, repays those who serve him well with things much greater than they can deserve; nevertheless, desiring to render unto the Lord a people acceptable, a [unclear] of good works, we invite the faithful themselves to please him, as it were by certain allurements, namely remissions, so that thereby they may be rendered more acceptable to divine grace.

Since therefore, as we have learned, the monastery of Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, because of pressing wars, other extreme conditions of scarcity, and other things which in times past have prevailed sharply, exists greatly diminished and impoverished in its revenues and buildings, and is in very great need of vestments, books, and other jewels necessary for divine worship, for which the resources of the fabric of the same monastery are by no means sufficient, on account of which the suffrages of Christ’s faithful are known to be very opportune: we, desiring that the said monastery be frequented with due honors and that aid be given it in the said necessities, so that the faithful themselves may flow there more willingly, and also may more promptly extend helping hands for repair and preservation of this sort, the more abundantly they shall perceive themselves refreshed there by the gift of ecclesiastical grace, by the mercy of almighty God and by the authority of his blessed apostles Peter and Paul, [unclear], to all truly penitent and confessed persons who shall come to the said monastery on any days whatsoever for the sake of prayer and devotion, to implore pardon for their sins, or who, for the fabric, lights, vestments, or any other ornaments necessary for that monastery, on the feasts of Saint Martin and his octaves, in whose reverence the church in that monastery was founded, of All Saints and of holy women having [unclear], and of the patrons of all the altars belonging to that church, shall devoutly visit the said church and extend or send their helping hands to that monastery, or shall bequeath, give, or send to that monastery something from their means, we mercifully relax five years and as many quarantines from the penances enjoined upon them, by these presents to remain valid perpetually. We will, moreover, that whatever indulgences have been granted to the said church and monastery in any manner, tenor, for a time or in perpetuity, and still endure and have the force of validity, we confirm them anew by the tenor of the present letters. Given in the town of Frankfurt, in the diocese of Mainz, under the attachment of the seal of our cardinalate, on the fourth day before the Nones of August, in the year from the birth of the Lord 1442.

*1442. The winter was very severe.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Frederick there was a very long and very cold winter, which lasted from the feast of Saint Martin almost until the middle of May; and fodder failed for the draft animals. In many places neither hay nor straw was to be had. Poor peasants were forced, through want, to take down the straw roofs of their houses and to prepare as feed for the beasts straw almost rotted by age. Many also, because of lack of fodder, were compelled to slaughter their livestock; others drove them into fields and woods and voluntarily exposed them to be devoured by wolves.

In the same year also Sigismund, bishop of Würzburg, of whom we spoke above, a foolish man altogether very like one demented, was driven out because of his excessive folly and sent back into Saxony to his brothers. They placed him, as a ruined man, in a certain castle to be guarded perpetually, receiving from the church of Würzburg no small pension for his support, as we said in its place. When he had been deposed, Gottfried of Limpurg succeeded by election of the chapter, and he presided for twelve years.

*1444.*

In the fifth year of Frederick our abbot, on the tenth day of the month of May, which was Cantate Sunday, the twelfth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated at Erfurt in the monastery of Saint Peter. In it the abbots of the monasteries of our order presided, namely of Saint Peter there, of Saint Gall, of Saint Giles of Nuremberg, and of Saint Stephen of Würzburg; in it many things necessary for the reform of our order were enacted.
