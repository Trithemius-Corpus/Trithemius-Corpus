In the same year Philip, abbot of this monastery, had the lease of the goods of our court in Wendelsheim, belonging to the altar of blessed Mary Magdalene, renewed before a notary and witnesses, as is clear in the instrument drawn up concerning it, which is still preserved intact.

In the same year also King Charles entered Italy with a great army of Germans, and, coming to Rome, received the crown of the Empire with great solemnity from the hand of the cardinal of Ostia, whom Pope Innocent VI had sent from Avignon for this purpose. Departing from there, he turned aside to the city of Pisa, where a sedition arising among his [unclear] grew so strong that the emperor himself slipped away in flight and scarcely escaped unharmed with his men. The empress also fled from the city by night with her maidservants; some of them were naked, some in only their shifts, while the rest, half-naked, followed their lady in flight. On the following day the authors of the sedition were beheaded. The like happened to them in the city of Siena.

In this same year, in the month of September, John, king of the French, was captured by the English.

In the same year also, on a certain day around the ninth hour, there was an earthquake at Strasbourg and Basel, so that many buildings and walls collapsed; and a great part of the city of Basel fell in that event and crushed many people. But many very strong castles in the mountains fell as well.

*1356.*

Emperor Charles, having returned from Italy, held a solemn assembly of princes in the city of Metz with the greatest pomp, where nearly all the princes of the whole kingdom came together and received their regalia according to custom. There was present there a certain archbishop, legate of the Apostolic See, with many princes of the French. There William, margrave of Julich, was then created duke by the emperor; from that time down to the present day the dukes of Julich began to exist. Wenceslaus also, the emperor's brother, from being count of Luxembourg, was raised to be duke by the same emperor, with the consent of all the princes then present. At the same time he raised the county of Bar to a margraviate. He also caused the Golden Bull concerning the election of the emperor, along with many other matters pertaining to the advantage of the commonwealth, to be written with the consent of the princes, and confirmed it. When these things had duly been completed, passing from city to city and from monastery to monastery, he collected many relics of the saints here and there, and carried them with him into Bohemia.

*1357.*

In the eighth year of Philip our abbot, Pope Innocent VI demanded the tenth penny from the whole clergy of the entire Empire, but did not obtain it from everyone; for many opposed him, especially those of the dioceses of Trier, Mainz, and Cologne.

In the same year also William, count palatine, lord of Hainaut, Holland, Zeeland, and Frisia, was seized by disease and became insane, wholly delirious. Since he had no children, Albert, duke of Bavaria, his brother, son of the late Emperor Louis, undertook the government of his land, which he held for forty-seven years. He had as wife Margaret, daughter of the duke of Brieg; by her he begot a son, William, and a daughter named Catherine, who married Edward, duke of Guelders, brother of Reynald.

*1358.*

In the eighth year of Philip our abbot, on the seventh day before the Kalends of January, Gerlacus, prior of this monastery, the twenty-first in order, died and was buried in the cloister with the brothers. After him Peter of Schomberg was appointed the twenty-second prior. A professed monk of this monastery, he held the office of prior for fourteen years, a man born more for temporal affairs than for spiritual ones. For in his time the discipline of regular observance had greatly collapsed, and the monks possessed private property; in both spiritual and temporal matters they fell far short.

In the same year Albert, duke of Austria, son of the late Albert, king of the Romans, died; concerning him much has been said above. He left after him Leopold, his son, duke, through whom the genealogy of Maximilian our emperor is continued; Duke Rudolf, who founded the provostship of St. Stephen the Protomartyr in Vienna and subjected the county of Tyrol to the Austrian duchy, whose wife was Catherine, daughter of Emperor Charles IV; Duke Frederick, who died without children; and one daughter named Margaret, marchioness of Brandenburg, and a second named Catherine, who was abbess in the monastery of St. Clare at Vienna, a virgin of most devout conduct.
