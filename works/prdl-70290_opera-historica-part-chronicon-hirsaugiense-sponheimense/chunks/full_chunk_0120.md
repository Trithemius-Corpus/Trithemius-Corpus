*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

In the same year Conrad, bishop of Utrecht, on the eighth day before the Kalends of May, after the celebration of masses, while lying prostrate in prayer in his house, was cruelly killed by a certain Frisian.

The occasion of his killing was this. When the same bishop had decided to build a monastery of canons in a certain marshy part of the city, and because of the muddy site had been unable to lay a foundation, there was present among others a certain Frisian, a very skilled mason, named Ploberus, who promised, under pledge of his head, that by a certain secret art he could build the church in that same place according to the bishop’s will.

But when he demanded an immoderate sum of money for this, the bishop, concealing his intention, won over the son of that Ploberus with generous gifts; and, learning from him the secret of this art, he soon completed the church already begun without any further mastership. For this reason the same Frisian conceived great resentment against the bishop, because he seemed to have been defrauded by him of his profit, and thereafter thought of killing him, which he carried out most cruelly. Therefore, after Bishop Conrad had been killed and buried, Burchard, a pious and devout man, succeeded in the bishopric of Utrecht.

In the same year also Hermann, archbishop of Cologne, died; Frederick succeeded him. This Archbishop Frederick newly built a monastery of nuns on the island of the Rhine which is called Roland, not far from the town of Bonn, and placed in it nuns of our order. He also built at his own expense the neighboring castle Rolandseck, and another, called Wolkenberg, situated very high not far from Bonn, together with the city of Andernach and the provostry of St. Apollinaris of our order beside Remagen, immediately subject to the abbot of Siegburg. Conrad, bishop of Eichstatt, also died in this same year; in his place another Conrad of the same name was ordained.

Pope Urban II also, once a monk of our order, died in this same year. This Pope Urban II, in the seventh year of his pontificate, at the request of the venerable Abbot Gebhard, confirmed and strengthened with fuller grace the privilege of Pope Gregory VII graciously granted to the blessed William, abbot of his monastery of Hirsau. The tenor of this bull follows, and is as follows:

Urban, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to his beloved son [Gebhard?], abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, and to his successors to be regularly substituted forever. Since we stand indebted by the authority and benevolence of the Apostolic See to all households of the Church and to its sons, nevertheless it is fitting that we attend with more ready zeal of charity to those places and persons which adhere more especially and familiarly to the Roman Church, and which stand forth by a fuller grace of religion. Therefore we, following the form of our predecessor of blessed memory Gregory, grant to you and to your legitimate successors the same things which he granted and confirmed to his predecessor William in the same monastery, at the prayers of Count Adalbert.

He, kindled by love of eternal reward, recently restored and beautifully repaired on his estate, which is called Hirsau, a monastery built long ago by his ancestors and lately lost, and enlarged it with many possessions and revenues for the uses of the brothers serving God there. He asked that this institution of his liberty be strengthened by the protection of the minister of the holy Church in a privilege, lest hereafter the audacity of perverse men presume to diminish or violate [unclear]. For this reason, specially embracing the aforesaid monastery under the guardianship and protection of the Apostolic See, by the page of the present privilege we decree that whatever that same monastery today justly possesses, or in the future, by grant of pontiffs, by the liberty of princes, or by offerings of the faithful, it shall be able justly and canonically to acquire, shall remain firm and inviolate for you and your successors.

Among these we have thought that the following should be designated by name: the cells of St. Gregory, which is called Reichenbach; of St. Martin, which is called Vischbachona; and the estate which is called Gilstein, which was given for the cell of St. Peter, which is called Wilhelm. We decree that neither you, dearest son Gebhard, nor any of your [unclear], may rashly or violently presume to destroy the aforesaid cells, or to diminish those things which have already been granted to them or shall be granted.

Furthermore we decree that it be lawful for no man at all [unclear] the same [monastery?]; [the monks?] shall choose according to God and the Rule of blessed Benedict. Consecrations of altars or of basilicas, and also ordinations of clerics, holy oil, and the other things pertaining to the episcopal office, you shall receive from the bishop in whose diocese you are, provided however that he is catholic, [and] communion with the Apostolic [See?] [unclear].

[unclear] they are to be preserved entire and unmixed for the uses of those for whose support and governance they have been granted, and are to be wholly profitable.
