1312.

In this year Peter, archbishop of Mainz, around the feast of All Saints came to the castle of Beckelnheim and stayed there for six days. Willicho, abbot of this monastery, approached him and, among other things, obtained confirmation of the indulgences granted by thirteen bishops at Rome at the time of the first Jubilee, as is clear in the letters, whose transcript we have placed above in its proper place.

In the same year Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, who at Rome [unclear], with his brother Henry VII, emperor of the Romans, fighting and doing battle in his own person, had accomplished many distinguished deeds, for the expenses incurred [unclear]; Boppard and Wesel were then pledged to the church of Trier.

*Copy of the donation.*

In the fourth year of Willicho, abbot of this monastery, the altar of Saints Nicholas and Catherine in our monastery of Sponheim was founded and endowed by Stephan, provost of the canons in Ravengiersburg, as appears in the letters given concerning this matter, reading as follows:

In the name of the Lord. Since every professor of the faith bearing the Christian name ought effectively to attend to those things that concern the praise and honor of God and increase the salvation of souls, we, Stephan, by the grace of God provost of the church of Ravengiersburg of the regular canons, in the diocese of Mainz, establish and ordain in the monastery of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, with the consent and free will of lord Willicho and the whole convent there, one mass to be celebrated every day in perpetuity upon the altar of blessed Nicholas and blessed Catherine by a secular priest, unless he is prevented by lawful causes. In this mass the priest shall hold remembrance and make memorials of our father Emelric, formerly a knight, of our mother Petrissa, and also of our brothers Lambert and Arnold, called Kindelen, formerly knights, and Emelric, formerly an esquire.

To this altar and to the priest serving it we freely give and assign our court at Roxheim, with all the goods and appurtenances of the same, so that the priest may enjoy the court, goods, and all appurtenances quietly and peacefully; we also assign to him in perpetuity one cartload of wine in the village of [Mandel?]. Careful provision has above all been made that through the said priest no prejudice be done to the pleban of the parish church in offerings or in any other dues. The same priest must also be present daily at high mass and vespers. If he transgresses disgracefully, he is to be corrected by the abbot who shall be in office at the time, with [unclear] reserved to the heirs.

We have reserved to ourselves the collation of the altar during our lifetime. After our death, however, Arnold called Kindelin, Emelric his brother, the daughter of our aforesaid brother, Arnold son of Bigela, our kinsman, and their nearer heirs, successively and in alternate turns, shall present. Yet we establish this: that after our death the first collation belongs to the aforesaid brothers Kindelin and Emelric. And so that each and all of the aforesaid things may be preserved with due force, we have had the present letters strengthened by the seal of the lords judges of Mainz, and by the seal of the lord abbot and the aforesaid convent, together with our seal. We also, the judges, at the petition of the aforesaid lord provost, have thought fit to append our seal to the present letters. These things were done in the year of the Lord 1313, on the third day before the Kalends of April.

*The heirs gave the right of patronage to the abbot of this place.*

The heirs of the aforesaid founders, namely Wilhelm of Lebenstein, Simon Booss of Waldeck, and Hartmann of [Halberg?], knights, together with the consent of their wives, transferred in perpetuity to Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery, for themselves and all their heirs, the collation of this altar of Saint Nicholas, in the year of the Lord 1490, as we shall say below in its proper place.

*Death of Emperor Henry in Italy.*

Henry VII, emperor of the Romans, in the aforesaid year still being in Italy, at the city of Pisa, having gathered new forces and made agreements with the Genoese and with Frederick, king of Sicily, strove to go against Robert, king of Apulia. He died by poison, through the deceit and treachery, as they say, of Bernard, his confessor, of the Order of Preachers, who had poisoned him on the day of the Assumption of blessed Mary ever virgin in the Host, while he trusted in good faith.
