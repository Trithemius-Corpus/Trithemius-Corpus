Finally, in the common causes and affairs of the chapter, the order, and the union, by making visitations, by speaking, and by carrying out embassies before princes, bishops, and very many others on many occasions, I have labored not a little, although I have received no reward from men. Yet I hope that the almighty and merciful Lord will bestow eternal good for these things. I have had few faithful helpers for the common good, and, as we plainly see, I have left behind still fewer followers of my works on behalf of our observance. Few there are, I say, not to say very few, who have the necessary zeal for holy religion: all seek what is their own, not what belongs to the commonwealth. Hence visitations are neglected, regular observance perishes, and no one is found who grieves over it. I know certain monasteries of our union and observance, and indeed not few in number, in Swabia and in our eastern Franconia, some of which have not been visited by the fathers in eight years, others in ten, and others in fourteen. Indeed, there are found today in certain monasteries of our union monks and abbots alike who publicly and privately eat meat, in contempt and insult of regular observance, and no one comes forward to correct those who offend so outrageously. The holy endeavors of the former fathers have been brought at once into oblivion and contempt; they, with great labors, vigils, and expenses, splendidly restored in our own times, under the title of the Bursfelde Union, in Saxony, in the regions of the Rhineland, and in the other adjoining parts, the observance of regular discipline, which had long since utterly collapsed. I grieve deeply, and not without cause, that through the negligence of these times the holy labors of the fathers of the past are perishing. It would be worth the trouble to admonish the fathers, when they are at some time assembled in chapter, so that they may forestall the evils that follow and compel those who walk carnally to return to the path of regular observance. Farewell, remembering me before God in your prayers. From the monastery of Saint James in the suburb of the city of Würzburg, on the tenth day of the month of April, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*XXII. To the Most Reverend Father in Christ, Lord Dietrich, Bishop of the Church of Lübeck, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James at Würzburg, prays everlasting felicity.*

Most reverend prelate, most learned father, and ever-honored teacher, you will not wonder that I have changed the title of my abbacy, if you weigh with an even balance the reasons that stirred my mind to yield to my rivals. You are not unaware of the injuries I received without deserving them; lest the abbey suffer more grievous ones, I exchanged it on reasonable grounds. For I am a lover of peace, and therefore I refused to dwell any longer amid disturbances. Why your most reverend paternity wishes to learn from me what I think about the life of Apollonius of Tyana the philosopher, which Philostratus described in eight books from materials gathered by Damis and his other disciples, I cannot sufficiently wonder. For since you yourself are most learned in every variety of both divine and human writings, nothing can be hidden from you that I could either understand or write. Therefore it is rather shamefacedly that I answer your command in this matter, since you surpass me beyond comparison in talent and learning, and whatever I can bring forward is certainly already stored up in many ways in the cabinet of your breast. But to strive against it and to be unwilling to obey your commands would seem to me a manifest sign not only of a rash mind, but also of a mad one, the stain of which I have always been accustomed to keep far from myself. For if I must incur one of the two, I prefer to be accused of foolishness before your paternity rather than of disobedience or rashness. Briefly, therefore, and without the order of disputation, in a confused manner as best I can, I shall set forth in simple narration what I think about Apollonius, without injury to anyone who judges better.

Apollonius lived at the time when the apostles and disciples of Christ, throughout all Greece and Asia, preached the Catholic faith with signs and miracles shining forth, and announced to the Gentiles the life, doctrine, powers, passion, and miracles of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself, converting those who had been predestined to the knowledge of the highest truth, and arousing among the people great admiration and reverence for themselves. Hearing these things, which the holy preachers had spread throughout the whole world concerning the life and miracles of Christ, Damis and the other followers of Apollonius, although they did not believe the deeds that were said of the Savior, nevertheless invented similar things about their own teacher after his death, so that they might persuade foolish men that the one whom they had known as a philosopher was something above man, and for that reason not inferior to Christ.
