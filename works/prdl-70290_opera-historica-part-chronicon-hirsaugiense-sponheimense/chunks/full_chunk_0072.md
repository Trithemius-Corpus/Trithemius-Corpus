The holy archbishop Anno also founded a third monastery of our order on the borders of the Slavs, which is called Salvedia, in which he likewise placed monks of the Rule of our most holy father Benedict with an abbot, and enriched them with many estates and gifts.

In these times a certain monk of our order came from Ireland, who was called John, into the territory of Mecklenburg in Saxony, preaching the word of God to the inhabitants. On account of his zeal for the faith and his tireless constancy in evangelizing, the Roman pontiff set him as bishop over those peoples; and at last, crowned with martyrdom for faith and justice, he passed to the Lord.

Hermann also, from being a monk of the monastery of Saint Felicitas in Schwarzach, not far from Würzburg, of our order, in these days after the death of Gunther, bishop of Bamberg, succeeded in his place. There are those who write that this Hermann had been vicedominus of Mainz, and that by the entreaties and money of friends he bought the bishopric; but afterward, led by repentance, he entered a monastery.

*When and whence the first monks came to Hirsau in this second foundation or restoration. Of Count Adelbert at Constance.*

Meanwhile, while the work begun in the foundation of the new church of Saint Aurelius the Pontiff was being continued by Count Adelbert, every delay seemed too long to the holy woman, since she both knew that her husband’s mind was slack and inconstant, and knew that, not yet sufficiently grounded in the fear of God, he could easily be turned back from his holy purpose. Therefore, by what seemed a shrewd counsel, she decided that the completion of the monastery was not to be awaited, lest perhaps death or some impediments should intervene, if it carried off either her or the count before the work was finished. The devoted wife therefore moved her husband, and at last persuaded him to introduce monks without waiting for the date of completion, which still seemed far distant; with their counsel he would finish what was not yet completed in the construction of the monastic dwellings and cells. The count therefore gave consent to the woman who urged him, and took counsel as to whence monks might be brought who would live blamelessly according to the monastic Rule. At that time there was a monastery of solitaries, which from ancient times, as we said far above, was called Ad eremitas, in the Black Forest, praised above the other monasteries of Swabia for the good reputation and rule of life of the monks dwelling there. Led by the glory of its good fragrance of fame, the count and countess resolved to seek monks from it, if they could obtain them. Therefore, after an embassy had been sent to the abbot of that monastery, Count Adelbert asked that monks outstanding in life and conduct be sent to him, to possess under the Rule of the holy father Benedict the monastery founded by him. Accordingly brothers were sent from the aforesaid monastery with Frederick, a venerable man, whom they appointed abbot over themselves.

*1065.*

And they came to Hirsau in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1065, in the third indiction, on the day before the Nones of December, in the sixty-third year from the desolation of the first monastery, and in the 227th year from its first foundation. When the devoted handmaid of Christ, Countess Wiltrud, saw them, she received them with incredible joy; and after arranging everything that seemed necessary for the sustenance of the servants of God, she, together with the consenting count, handed over the monastery to them in perpetual possession.
