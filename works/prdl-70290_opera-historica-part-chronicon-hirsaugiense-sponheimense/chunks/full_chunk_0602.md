*Familiar Letters*

For it is fitting for a disciple of Christ that, in sufferings and adversities, after the example of his teacher, he should daily, and [unclear], with anxious mind imitate his Lord. *Those who suffer for Christ.* In this life struggles of sufferings are never lacking for a Christian, although not all who are called Christians use them, through patience, unto merit. For not everyone who suffers in this world truly shares in the sufferings of Christ; but only he is made glorious by the testimony of Christ who either suffers for love of Christ and of justice, or, for his own sins, judges himself deserving of every adversity, that he may gain Christ. For the great martyr of Christ, Cyprian, said among holy men: “Punishment does not make the martyr, but the cause.” If, when we suffer, Christ is the cause, let us not be sad, but let us keep our grief within ourselves and bear it bravely, since Christ himself, for whose love we patiently endure, will send us swift help from heaven. For you know what holy Christophilus said: “Tribulation works patience, and patience proof, and proof hope.” And lest we should think hope vain, he added: “But hope does not confound, because the charity of God has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us.” Did not also a certain wise man, considering tribulation necessary for just men, say: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the trial of tribulation tests just men.” Whatever adversities befall us must be borne patiently, since, although the tribulations of the just are many, nevertheless they are delivered by the Lord from all of them after a very brief and momentary hour. It is foolish to fear what you cannot avoid, and on account of bodily adversity to fall into sickness of mind. Let the authority of Menander’s verses put us to shame, if, when struck by adversities, we have been womanish. For he says: [unclear].

Therefore, my brother, since we are both monks and Christians, our spirit must always be strengthened against all adversities that can threaten, and made firm in the love of Christ, so that no circumstance of the present life may ever be able to meet us against whose assault the mind has not already been sufficiently fortified. And this we shall indeed be able to do very easily if, through the fervor of inward love, we continually refer all our thoughts and works, and even the desires of our heart, to the memory of Christ. Great are the things promised us in heaven by our prince; if the constant memory of them faithfully clings to our minds, we shall certainly fear no adversities of this world. O most beloved brother, if with discerning mind you turned over the gifts of future happiness, which are here reserved in heaven for those troubled for Christ, the adversity of the present life would bring you joy, and worldly prosperity immense sadness. The joys of eternal delight are very great and wholly incomprehensible to mortals, because, as divine Scripture promises: “Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, the estimation of the good which almighty God has prepared for those who love him.” Thinking on these things more often, dearest son, you will both bear your illness more lightly and not weep over our misfortune. Although we do not at all doubt that you bear our injury more heavily than every weakness of your body which you suffer. For measuring your spirit by our own, believe us, we have more compassion for you than we ourselves suffer. We have known you to be subdued by many tribulations from tender age, and for that reason we now grieve the more for you, burdened as you are by age and ill health. But suffer patiently whatever adversity comes to you: the time of the evils of our pilgrimage is very brief, but the reward of those who labor in tribulation for Christ is eternal. We hope shortly to temper your sadness by our presence; but if the envy of Satan has shut off bodily presence, then, present here in the spirit of love, in the future life of the heavenly fatherland, by the gift of the Savior of all, we shall rejoice forever in one another’s sight. The day after tomorrow we are to go down to Cologne, called by letters of my most merciful prince Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, whom you know, and whose benefits conferred on us you have seen, one among those princes who have the right to elect the Roman King. Therefore, not knowing the day of our return, we have appointed our prior Nicholas, under a certain limitation, as vicar general, so that meanwhile he may carry on anxious care concerning the state of our affairs and the care of both [unclear], lest anything be done amiss in the business of the monastery while we are absent. For this reason we have thought it right paternally to admonish also your charity, in which we have very great confidence, that you keep sharp eyes upon him and assist him in all matters that come up for handling, as much as your weakness permits, lest the condition of the poor monastery suffer any harm because of his inexperienced levity, by which he trusts that he can do everything.
