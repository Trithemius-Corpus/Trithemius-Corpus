*Sponheimense.*

*Lobenfeld is founded.* In these times the monastery of nuns at Lobenfeld, of our order, in the diocese of Worms, in the district called Kraichgau, about two miles from Heidelberg, was founded under Buggo, the venerable bishop of Worms.

At the same time the count of Leiningen founded the monastery of St Peter of Dumeta, commonly Hugen, in the same diocese of Worms. The aforesaid Bishop Buggo consecrated its church and established there the canon brothers under the rule of blessed Augustine, setting over them Hartung, a very venerable man, as their first father and provost. This consecration was performed in the year of the Lord’s incarnation MCLII, on the feast of St Peter which is called “in Chains.”

In this very year Mainz was burned together with the great monastery; Speyer also and Goslar, on one and the same day, for the greater part perished by fire.

The emperor Lothar also died in Bavaria on the day before the Nones of December. This emperor Lothar founded a monastery of our order in Saxony, which is called Lutera Regalis or Königslutter, where, after the nuns had been transferred to another place, he established an abbot and monks; and when he died he was buried there. Conrad III succeeded him and ruled for fourteen years.

In this year, on the night of Good Friday, the body of St Disibod, under Abbot Cuno, was translated with the greatest devotion from the old monastery, where it had once been placed in the altar by Archbishop Willigis, to the new monastery which Archbishop Ruthard and afterwards Adalbert the Elder had built on the mountain where it now stands; and it was honorably laid behind the high altar in a new marble sarcophagus. Abbot Bernhelm was present at this translation with the whole community of this monastery, and obtained the shoulder blade of the same holy pontiff.

*MCL.*

In the year MCXL Abbot Bernhelm traveled to Rome with Adalbert the Younger, archbishop of Mainz, and Count Gottfried of Sponheim, son of Megenhard, and through the entreaties of the aforesaid archbishop obtained from Pope Innocent many precious relics. Among them was one tooth of the holy apostle Paul; likewise, from the head of St Hippolytus the martyr; also from the hair of the holy apostle Peter; from the wood of the Holy Cross; from the shirt of St Benedict our father and abbot; from the cross of St Andrew the apostle; of Mary Magdalene and Mary of Egypt, together with many others.

In this year Embricho, bishop of Würzburg, founded in the suburb of the same city the monastery of St James the Apostle for the reception of Scots of our order; and he named and appointed as first abbot Macarius, a holy man, a Scot by nation. Among the other miracles of his holiness, as the same bishop declares in the charter of foundation, since out of love for the heavenly kingdom he abstained from wine and had nothing else on the table to drink, he changed the wine set before him into water in the presence of many, and drank it.

In this year St Otto, bishop of Bamberg, formerly a monk of our order, died on the day before the Kalends of October and was buried there in the church of the Mount of Monks, where he had been a monk before his episcopate. He built the monastery of [Aura?], of our order, in the diocese of Würzburg, in which he appointed as first abbot Egard, a religious and most learned man, who composed many little works of fine reading; and he lies buried in the middle of the same church, not without a reputation for holiness. He also founded anew some other monasteries, and magnificently restored several that had collapsed through excessive age, whose names [unclear]. [Unclear] of the martyr was consecrated, and was quite magnificently endowed by him.

*The monastery of Schönau is founded.* In these times also Buggo, bishop of Worms, founded not far from Heidelberg a monastery of the Cistercian order, which is called Schönau, and obtained a community from Clairvaux from St Bernard.

*MCXLI.*

In the year MCXLI [unclear], bishop of Mainz, died on the twelfth day before the Kalends of August; Marolf, provost of Aschaffenburg, succeeded him and presided for one year and two months.

*Marolf, bishop of Mainz.*

*Two bodies of saints are brought here.*

*MCXLIII.*

In the year MCXLIII Abbot Bernhelm, through the intervention of Count Megenhard, obtained from Arnold, archbishop of Cologne, two bodies from the company of the eleven thousand virgins and martyrs for the monastery. Receiving them with great honor, he brought them here to the monastery and placed them in two gilded wooden reliquaries; their heads, however, had been broken, but the individual parts were arranged with their own body.
