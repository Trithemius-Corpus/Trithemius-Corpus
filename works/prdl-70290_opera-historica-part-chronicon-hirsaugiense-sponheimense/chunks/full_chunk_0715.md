*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

... conforming himself in all things to the will, with sagacious mind he ponders the commandments. For thus says the Lord Jesus in the Gospel: If anyone loves me, he will keep my words, and my Father will love him, and we shall come to him, and make our dwelling with him. O holy dwelling, O sweetest visitation of the mind, in which God almighty, the creator of all things, incomprehensible, deigns to hold a joyful banquet, where the creature is joined to its maker through the grace of inward love, and all worldly affections are powerfully extinguished. There are peace, piety, goodness, light, virtue, honor, joys, gladness, sweetness, everlasting life, glory, praise, rest, love, and sweet concord. This, reverend father, is the fruit of our friendship; sighing after its sweetest acquisition in continual meditation, let us walk with gladness in the way of God’s commandments, lest harmful weariness of the journey overtake us; may the consolation of the divine Scriptures always be with us. For as the holy [unclear] writes to the Romans: Whatever things have been written were written for our instruction, so that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope.

Since, therefore, the study of the holy Scriptures prepares for us an open, safe, and most beautiful road to the knowledge of God, and knowledge leads the soul by the most pleasant and sweetest way of the Holy Spirit to love, from these two things, in this mortal life in which we live, the observance of God’s commandments happily occupies the mind; and after the death of the flesh, in that heavenly fatherland, there succeeds the enjoyment of the highest good, destined to endure without end. Those, then, must be judged happy who, despising the vanities of the world and rejecting useless and curious doctrines, have wholly devoted themselves, with pure sobriety, to this most holy philosophy of divine traditions. Among their number, Frederick, most learned preceptor, I have considered that you should be placed among the first; for just as a rose among thorns sweetly attracts the reader by its beauty and fragrant scent, so you, among many nobles by blood of our own age, canons and prelates of the Church of God, shine and gleam with learning in the divine Scriptures and constant study of them, and also with holy morals and innocence of life, and you have so bound me wholly to yourself that in loving and honoring you I shall yield to no one.

Therefore I wish you to be persuaded that I am such a man as has known and wishes to observe most holily, and to preserve with inviolable constancy and unharmed, the rights of the friendship contracted between us. For if rarity increases the price of things, you are to me the more precious a friend to be embraced, inasmuch as among the noble ministers of Christ known and dear to me, applying themselves today to the divine Scriptures, you are held almost uniquely alone. How many today, I say it with shame and reverence, are found among the nobles, canons and prelates not only of cathedral churches but also of other churches, despisers of sacred reading, who therefore think the knowledge of the Scriptures disgraceful to themselves, because, not born of rustic blood, they boast that as nobles they are ignorant. How great a madness turns the breasts of mortals! If contempt and ignorance of the Scriptures made men noble, such men would rightly be called most noble, because they understand less than nothing, since they do not know themselves and are ignorant of God. But it is not our office to censure the haters of saving wisdom; therefore let the blind fare well, groping in darkness, who detest the light of knowledge.

Let us continue on the journey we have begun, the study of the Scriptures, so that by continual meditation on them our understanding may delightfully rise up to the knowledge of the highest truth, and our affection may constantly burn in the love of God. For this is the true friendship of Christians: by common study to believe in God through faith, one in substance, three in persons, according to the tradition of the holy Scriptures, and to love him truly above all things with equal desire, equal virtue, and equal consent. And therefore, Frederick, most loving father, you are to me a more precious and dearer friend, since in loving me you seek nothing earthly, you hunt after nothing perishable, you burn sweetly with zeal for the divine Scriptures, you pant toward God at once with knowledge and with longing for inward love; for that reason you desire to have me as a friend, whom you believe to be inflamed with like fervor. But although I am in no respect to be compared with you, yet I frankly confess myself a lover of the holy Scriptures, and I promise myself as a companion on the journey to the delightful knowledge of the highest good, in love of which may our inviolable friendship remain founded: now committing the seed of good works to the soil of one’s own pure heart, from which we may obtain the fruit of eternal enjoyment among the sons of God’s election.

I have completed, with God’s help, the little book of Saint Joseph, containing the praises of the foster-father of our Lord and Savior and inscribed to your most renowned name. Since I have determined that some things in it must be made clearer, it will necessarily have to be written out again, so that it may come into your hands, worthy of more polished things, as corrected as possible. Meanwhile I ask you to bear this delay patiently until it is brought into the light, because it will admit no other patron than you, reverend father and lord. You urge that I enter your house by right of friendship whenever it happens that I go up to Bamberg; this offering of yours I gladly accept, and I give thanks as much as I can.
