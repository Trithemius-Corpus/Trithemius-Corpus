*Sponheim.*

And he took the rebellious cities of Hagenau, the authors of the rebellion being partly killed and partly proscribed; and all the land was quiet before him in good peace.

In the same year King Rudolf, established at Mainz in the month of December with many princes, made peace between Werner, archbishop of Mainz, with Count Heinrich of Sponheim on the one side, and Johann, count of Sponheim, Heinrich’s brother, with Heinrich his son and those adhering to him on the other side, concerning all wars and battles and damages had by reason of the castle of Böckelheim, as is clear from the letters of settlement that follow:

*The war between the count of Sponheim and the archbishop of Mainz, which they had waged on account of the castle of Böckelheim, is settled.*

*Letters of settlement.*

Rudolf, by the grace of God king of the Romans, ever Augustus, we make known to all and each who shall read these letters or hear them read, that we have wholly, perfectly, and perpetually concluded, reconciled, and pacified all discord, suit, and contention which existed between our beloved princes, Werner, archbishop of Mainz, his church, Heinrich, brother of the count of Sponheim, with all their friends and helpers, parties on the other side, concerning all deeds committed and perpetrated during that whole time in which discord began and endured between them, in this manner.

First, the aforesaid Count Johann, with his wife and his brother Eberhard, for himself and all his heirs, renounced forever before us the castle of Böckelheim, [unclear] with its men and goods, which the aforesaid archbishop had bought, as is contained in the letters of division made between Count Johann and Heinrich his brother; and under my judgment, under which the said goods are known to stand, he must do each and every thing which the order of law requires with warranty. Count Heinrich ought to be content there with his third part, just as he has held it up to now, and the archbishop, together with the church of Mainz, shall remain in quiet possession of the two parts which Heinrich, brother of Count Johann, sold to him, without molestation from any of them. All captives, after making the oath of peace as is customary, have been set free. Johann and Heinrich, counts of Sponheim, swore upon the holy Gospel of God that they would firmly observe all the aforesaid things. But if the aforesaid counts presume to violate this our concord and peaceful settlement to the injury of the archbishop and the church of Mainz, then we, together with the cities of the realm, Frankfurt, Friedberg, Wetzlar, Gelnhausen, Oppenheim, Wesel, and Boppard, and he with us, will give aid and assistance against them to the often-mentioned archbishop and his church. Frederick, count of Leiningen, nor his son, nor Eberhard, count of Katzenelnbogen, nor Emicho, count of Leiningen, nor any of them, must give aid against these things to the aforesaid counts Johann and Heinrich against the archbishop and the church of Mainz. We have made this settlement of concord and peace with the consent and good will of the aforesaid archbishop of Mainz and of the counts Johann and Heinrich of Sponheim, and at their instance we have caused our royal seal, together with their seals and those of Frederick, count of Leiningen, and Eberhard, count of Katzenelnbogen, to be appended to these letters. Done at Mainz in the year of the Lord 1281, on the feast of St. Lucy.

*Captives released.*

*1282.*

In the eighteenth year of our abbot Peter, Johann, count of Sponheim, who in the preceding year, as we have said, had fought against Werner, archbishop of Mainz, at Gensingen, now entered into a perpetual treaty with him and promised assistance against all who should injure him, except Gottfried, count of Sayn, Heinrich, count of Starkenburg, Eberhard, count of Katzenelnbogen, who was the brother of his wife, Emicho and Frederick the younger, counts of Leiningen, as well as the count of Zweibrücken and the landgrave of Hesse, with whom he had previously been allied, as is clear from the letters drawn up concerning this, given at Mainz in the year of the Lord 1281, on the third day before the Nones of May; because they are both too lengthy and contribute nothing to our purpose, we have deliberately passed them over.

*A treaty between Count Johann of Sponheim and the archbishop.*

In the same year Rudolf took and destroyed the robbers’ castles Reichenstein and Schöneck.

*Two castles destroyed.*

In the same year, on the Monday after the feast of holy Easter, the citizens of Mainz killed all the Jews who were in the city of Mainz because of a certain enormous crime, and usurped all their goods for themselves. A similar blow followed against almost all the other Jews throughout all Germany.

*Slaughter of the Jews.*

That year was so temperate that around the feast of the martyrs Marcellinus and Peter, that is, 2 June, the vineyards generally everywhere in these lands had flowering clusters.

*The year temperate.*

*1283.*

In the nineteenth year of our abbot Peter, on the day of St. Potentiana the virgin, the counts of Henneberg led an army against Berthold, bishop of Würzburg, burned the town called Schwarzbach, and plundered the monasteries of our order there outside the walls.

*War at Schwarzbach.*
