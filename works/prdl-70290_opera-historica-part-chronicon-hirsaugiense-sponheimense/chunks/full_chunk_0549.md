In the same year also, on the last day of the month of December, died Melchior, abbot of Schönau of our order, within the borders of the diocese of Trier, a learned and erudite man, the singular ornament of our observance. Among the other monuments of his talent he left a quite beautifully compiled [unclear], that is, one Gospel out of the four Gospels; also certain prayers delivered in chapters, and sermons and other things.

Also in the aforesaid year, on the eighth day before the Ides of November, there fell from the clouds in Sundgau, near the town of Ensisheim, not far from Basel, a thunderbolt or stone of astonishing size, which, as we have learned by truthful testimony, weighed three hundred pounds. Many people wrote many opinions about its meaning; these we pass over in silence. 1493.

In the same chapter, publicly at the table of the prelates and doctors who had come together from the University of Tübingen with others to see the chapter fathers, for three days, morning and evening, there had been read at a suitable time the little Penthic book, that is, the mournful book, concerning the ruin of our order, which our aforesaid abbot Johannes Trithemius had published in the same year at the request of Blasius, abbot of Hirsau, and of the brothers of that monastery. When it had then been read to the end with the greatest attention, all the fathers unanimously decreed, as is contained in the recess of that chapter, that it should for all time be read at the table of the prelates in that chapter whenever it should be celebrated.

In the same year, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of September, Frederick III, emperor of the Romans, died in Austria. He was buried in Vienna, in a tomb previously prepared for him, leaving one son.

In the same [year], with a very large army of his men gathered together, [unclear] burst into Croatia and Carniola and laid everything waste with plunder, sword, and slaughter. When the Christians, gathering themselves together with such strength as they could, advanced against him to battle, because they were unequal in number, they were cut down, overthrown, and led away like cattle. Nine thousand Christians fell in that engagement. On receiving such sad news, King Maximilian interrupted the funeral pomp which he had prepared to celebrate for his father, and moved a strong army of his men into Croatia against the Turks. When he had learned meanwhile that they had fled, after placing the necessary garrisons everywhere, he returned to Austria to perform his father’s funeral rites. These were solemnly completed in Vienna on the Saturday after the feast of St. Nicholas, in the presence of many princes, dukes, counts, nobles, and envoys of the Roman pontiff, of the kings of France, Spain, Hungary, Bohemia, Naples, Sicily, Dacia, and of almost all the princes of the Christian world, pontiffs, abbots, and prelates, with eight hundred [unclear]. Much was diligently discussed between the king himself, the princes, and the legates about establishing peace among Christians and moving war against the Turks; we saw no result from these discussions.

In the same year also certain islands in the Indian sea were discovered by Christopher Columbus, captain of the fleet of Ferdinand, king of the Spains, entirely unknown before then to all inhabitants of Europe and Asia. On some of them he found naked men living like cattle; since a history of them has been written by him, there is no need for us to dwell at length on them. 1494.

In the eleventh year of our abbot Johannes, Maximilian, king of the Romans, took a second wife, Maria Bianca, formerly the daughter of Galeazzo, duke of Milan, the wedding being celebrated with pomp, as is the custom, at Innsbruck.

In the same year the monastery of nuns of our order on Mount St. Rupert near Bingen was reformed by command of Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, the deformed ones partly departing and partly remaining for a short time. The first reforming sisters, six in number, were taken from the monastery of Schönau. Among them Guda, called Specht, of Bubenheim, was appointed mistress by the consent of all.
