*1141.*

In the twentieth year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year 1141 of the Lord's Nativity, King Conrad held his court at Regensburg on the feast of Pentecost, where some princes of Saxony were present. Adalbert, archbishop of Mainz, conspired with the Saxons against the king, but not long afterward, on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August, he died. Marolph succeeded him in the archbishopric of the church of Mainz; he had previously been provost of the church of Aschaffenburg in the same diocese, and he did not complete two years in the pontificate.

In the same year Lady Richiza, empress, wife of Emperor Lothar, died and was buried beside her husband in the monastery of Lutter. Conrad, archbishop of Magdeburg, also died; Frederick, custos of the cathedral church, succeeded him by election of the brethren. Henry, margrave of Brandenburg, likewise died without children and left the march free in the hands of the king and the empire; King Conrad, by counsel of the princes, granted it as an imperial fief to Adelbert, count of Salzwedel. The same king also gave to his brother, Count Henry, as wife the daughter of Emperor Lothar, left as widow of Henry, duke of Bavaria, and created him duke of Austria.

*1142.*

In the [twenty-first?] year of Abbot Volmar, in the year [1142?] of the Lord's Nativity, the king celebrated Pentecost at Frankfurt, where almost all the princes, bishops, and abbots of the whole kingdom assembled; and there the marriage rites were celebrated for fourteen days. The king joined Gertrude, daughter of Emperor Lothar and widow of Henry, duke of Bavaria, in marriage as wife to his brother Henry, the first duke of Austria. There Henry, surnamed the Lion, son of Henry, duke of Bavaria, and of Gertrude, the aforesaid daughter of Emperor Lothar, received the duchy from the hand of King Conrad.

In the same year Marolph, archbishop of Mainz, died. Henry, provost of the cathedral church, succeeded him; in the presence of two cardinals, legates of the Holy Roman Church, at Frankfurt he received investiture of the regalia from King Conrad himself.

In the same year Henry of holy memory, abbot in Breitenau, of whom we spoke above, obtained from Arnold, archbishop of Cologne, four bodies from the company of the eleven thousand virgins; these, on the sixth day before the Nones of July, with the greatest honor, to the monastery [unclear].

*Henry, archbishop of Mainz.*

*The winter was very severe.*

In the following year Adelbero, archbishop of Trier, expelled the monks from the monastery of Saint Maximin at Trier because of their disorderly life, and, restoring others in their place, set over that monastery an abbot named Siger.

In this year the winter was hard and very severe. Gertrude also, daughter of Emperor Lothar, whom we have said was married to Henry, duke of Austria, after the death of her former husband, reached her last day because of the difficulty of childbirth; and, with the inhabitants of Saxony [unclear] mourning, she was buried in the monastery of Lutter with her father and mother and her former husband, Duke Henry. Pope Celestine II died; Lucius II succeeded him.

*1143.*

In the [twenty-third?] year of Abbot Volmar, and in the one thousand one hundred forty-fourth year of the Lord, blessed Felix, pope and martyr, appeared to Saint Henry, most reverend abbot of the monastery of Breitenau, [formerly?] a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, as was said above, revealing to him his head, which had lain hidden for many years in the apse of that church, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of September. The holy man raised the head of the holy martyr from the earth with the greatest reverence, and sent one small part to the brethren of Hirsau as a very great gift of charity.

*1144.*

In the [twenty-fourth?] year of Abbot Volmar, Pope Lucius died; Eugenius, abbot of Saint Anastasius, of the Cistercian order and formerly a monk of Saint Bernard, succeeded him.

*1145.*

In the [twenty-fifth?] year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year 1145 of the Lord's Nativity, a great earthquake occurred fifteen times; a comet appeared, and in the following year a great pestilence followed.
