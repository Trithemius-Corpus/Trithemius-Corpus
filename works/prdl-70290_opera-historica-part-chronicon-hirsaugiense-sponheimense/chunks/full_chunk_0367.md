*Sponheim.*

*1240.*
This year, which was the twenty-seventh of our Abbot Johannes, Johannes X, prior of this monastery, died on the vigil of St Andrew the Apostle, burdened by old age and an abscess. After him Sifridus, a monk born in Trier, was appointed eleventh prior; he presided in his office for four years and was deposed.

*Johannes, tenth prior, dies. Sifridus, eleventh prior.*

*1241.*
In the twenty-eighth year of Abbot Johannes, David, pastor of the church in Roxheim, was most cruelly killed by peasants in Sommerloch near Waldenhausen, because he defended the rights and liberty of his church. Therefore, excommunicated by Sifridus, archbishop of Mainz, and [unclear] cursed, they paid very heavy penalties for their impiety. For all who polluted their consciences with the contagion of this crime, some in pilgrimage, others escaping by flight, perished from this life in beggary and great want.

*The pastor in Roxheim is killed.*

*1242.*
In the twenty-eighth year. In the twenty-ninth year, Johannes, provost of the nuns [unclear], died. In the same year Conrad, bishop of Speyer, together with his chapter, sold to the count of Sayn all the right of ownership and the possessions that he himself and the canons of the church of Speyer had in Kreuznach, for 1,100 marks of pure silver; with this money they bought properties more suitable for themselves.

*Johannes, provost of St Rupert, dies; [Roricus?] succeeds. Worms is burned.*

In the same year Johannes, provost of the convent of St Rupert near Bingen, a monk of this monastery and formerly prior in the order, a good and upright man, died. After him Abbot Johannes appointed as provost of the said monastery the monk [Roticus?]; how long he presided I have not found.

In the same year, on [unclear] April at the hour of vespers, the city of Worms was consumed by fire over more than half its extent, and more than 300 people perished in that fire; this loss was wholly irrecoverable.

*1243.*
Frederick II, emperor, long since excommunicated, was deposed from the empire at the council of Lyon by Pope Innocent IV, but he did not care. The pope commanded the princes to elect another emperor against Frederick. And so at length Henry, landgrave of Hesse, son of St Elizabeth, was elected; near Frankfurt he had a battle with King Conrad, son of Emperor Frederick, and defeated him. Holding the name of king for almost five years, he accomplished nothing worthy of memory, and died not long afterward.

This year Pope Innocent granted to the dean of the greater church the use of the mitre or infula on the chief festivals, as appears in the bull that follows:

Innocent, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to his beloved son, the dean of Mainz, our chaplain, greeting and apostolic blessing. You have deserved our [favor] and that of the Apostolic See [unclear] by the purity of fidelity, [unclear], always watching for this, driving all fear from yourself, that under the experience of grievous labors [you might] enlarge the honor of the same see. Therefore, so that from merits of this kind joy may be produced for your heart and an example of devotion for others, by the authority of these presents we grant to you and your successors that in the church of Mainz, on the chief solemnities, in the celebration of divine services, you may forever use the mitre, which the provost and cantor of the same church are said to use. Let no man, therefore, be permitted to infringe this page of our concession. Given at Lyon, on the fourth day before the Nones of August, in the third year of our pontificate.

And these three, the provost, dean, and cantor of the aforesaid church of Mainz, by force of the granted privilege, use the infula on the highest feasts down to the present.

*1245.*
This year, which was the thirty-second of Abbot Johannes, Sifridus, eleventh prior of this monastery of Sponheim, was deposed. I could not find the cause of his deposition. When he had been deposed, [unclear]. At length, on account of Emperor Frederick and his son Conrad, he was excommunicated by Pope Innocent and began to persecute the clergy. Therefore, when on the vigil of St Andrew he had been joyful with his wife and household, he died suddenly that very night. He left two sons, Ludwig and Henry, who for ten years held their father's principality jointly. But afterward, in the year of the Lord 1256, when they were at odds with one another, they divided the lands and patrimony. Ludwig, who was the elder by birth, received the Palatinate, with certain other things in Upper Bavaria, for his portion; and Henry [unclear].
