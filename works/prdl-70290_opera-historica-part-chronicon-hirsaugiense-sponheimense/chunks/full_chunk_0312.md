Among the rest we thought this also should be added: that for as long as the same Count Megenhard lives, he himself shall hold its advocacy over them; and when he has died, if by chance his inheritance has been divided between two heirs, the one who possesses those estates belonging to the castle, namely Dille, not by usurpation but by hereditary right, being instituted by us or by any of our successors, shall be their advocate without any opposition. Therefore, establishing the tenor of this page, we have marked it with the impression of our seal, commanding and forbidding all Christians under perpetual anathema that anyone presume to come against it, assail it, or destroy it; but let its authority remain firm and unshaken for all, by the authority of almighty God and of blessed Peter, prince of the apostles. These things were done in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1130, in the eighth indiction, while Lord Lothar, glorious king of the Romans, the second of that name, was reigning, in the fifth year of his reign.

At the same time also the provostship of Mount St. John the Baptist in the Rheingau, of the order of our holy father Benedict, which Ruthard, archbishop of Mainz, had built a little earlier and decreed to be subject to the abbot of the monastery of St. Alban the Martyr outside Mainz, was raised to an abbey by his successor Adalbert the elder, archbishop of Mainz, as is clear in the letters drawn up concerning it, as follows:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Adalbert, by the grace of God archbishop of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, wish it to be known to all the faithful of Christ, both future and present, that Lord Ruthard of blessed memory, our predecessor, handed over to the church of St. Alban a certain mountain in the Rheingau, which is called Bischoffsberg, by his own hand, for this reason: that by the aid of the faithful dwelling round about, the discipline of monastic life might be established there. And when part of a monastery had been constructed in that same place, and our aforesaid predecessor had been summoned to consecrate the altar, wishing to share in the labor of those who had put their hand to cultivating that same place, he assigned as a dowry, upon the holy relics, a certain part of the surrounding wood, as far as the hollow called Hunenklingen, and so from there as far as the place called Kalenberg; on the side of the burial-ground, [unclear], and on the other three sides as far as [unclear] places. In addition he added the rent of half a mansus in Winkel, which belonged to episcopal right; this Ruthard, son of Regefo, and a certain Rabodo of Damburg, enclosed there, conferred on the same monastery for the remedy of their souls. It was also established by the aforesaid archbishop that every year, at the nativity of St. John the Baptist, a fair should be held there by merchants both of Mainz and of the province, and he handed over to the brothers of the same place every profit that could come from it to the bishop.

Furthermore, Count Richolf in the Rheingau, his wife Clanckmodis, and their children, Ludovicus, by common consent solemnly gave to St. John, in whose honor the principal altar of the aforesaid monastery was dedicated, without any opposition, the goods described below: namely, the court which lies nearest opposite the mountain, with the small estate belonging to it, that is, vineyards and trees, a mill, a wine-cellar, meadows, and gardens. Likewise one vineyard near Winkel, which is called Exconis; another in the field; a third near the court; a fourth on Culersberg; a fifth on the eastern side of the same mountain; a sixth at the foot of the mountain, beside the watercourse; four portions of arable land. They also offered their son and daughter in the same place to serve God and St. John; these aforesaid goods would have come to them by hereditary right if they had remained in the world.

In our times, therefore, when the resources of the same church were increasing by the working of divine mercy, and it, being sufficient for itself, sought its own pastor, it seemed right to us that we should change its condition for the better and take action to obtain liberty for it. Therefore, having summoned to us Henry, provost of the greater church, and very many other religious and wise persons, we approached the lord abbot of St. Alban, and by the assent and will of all his brothers obtained that the aforementioned church of St. John, free from its former subjection, should henceforth retain free judgment in electing for itself, and should freely present to us or to our successors the man canonically elected, to receive the staff from the hand of the bishop and to be consecrated. Therefore, after this concession had been obtained from the aforesaid Abbot Wernher and his brotherhood, and after we had released the aforementioned church from their hand and power by the counsel and approval of the abbots, archdeacons, and our other persons, we gave certain goods in Lorch for the use of the aforesaid brothers [unclear]; also a part of the tithe in Burnheim, possessed by the same brothers already for many years, we gave absolutely and without opposition to the abbot and brothers of St. Alban as a just and lawful compensation. In addition, for the daughter of Count Richolf, enclosed beside the monastery of St. Alban, we established that each year during her life a cartload of wine and a mark of silver should be given from Culersberg, and after her death the same benefaction should revert to the church of St. John.

We also granted, at the request of those brothers, that whoever of the faithful should wish to be baptized or buried there may find baptism and burial there in peace, all opposition of his own parish priest ceasing.
