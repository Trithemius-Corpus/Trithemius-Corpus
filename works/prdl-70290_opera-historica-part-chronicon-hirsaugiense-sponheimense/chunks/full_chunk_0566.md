*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In this same year Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, sent several messengers and letters here to Abbot Trithemius, whom he wished to have with him.

Reymundus, cardinal of Santa Maria Nuova, legate of the Apostolic See, entered Mainz on the Saturday before Palms. The grace of the Jubilee was proclaimed, although, because of the scarcity of people, the gathering was slow and tardy. Nevertheless afterward devotion increased with time, and a great sum of money was offered in various places to the Jubilee chests; this, not yet converted to the promised use, is kept shut up.

In the same year Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot, reviewed the books of the library. There were found in it 1,646 volumes, all of which, apart from forty in number, he himself had brought together in one place during the time of his rule. Finally, in the Greek language, knowledge of which he had acquired, he purchased many volumes. In Hebrew also he reviewed some volumes brought together in this very year; all of these are kept separately in one register, carefully written out by their names.

In this same year, on the eighth day of the month of April, Gerlach of Breitbach, abbot of Deutz, while on his way to the general chapter at Trier, was captured near the little palace by the officials of Jacob, from the Margraves of Baden, administrator of the church of Trier, and was held in custody for four months, on account of his brother, a canon of the greater church, who, with his adherents, was opposed to the same margrave, as we have said.

In the same year also, on the octave of St. Lawrence the martyr, Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot, had the larger bell, broken ten years before, made anew and larger than it had been before, weighing fifteen hundredweight of the best bronze. Later, on the day of Cosmas and Damian, he solemnly blessed it himself, in the presence of Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate, and Ladislaus of Sontheim, historiographer, envoys of Maximilian, King of the Romans, sent to Trithemius himself on a certain business.

In the year noted above, when the dreadful plague of epidemic disease was raging all around, it also invaded the present place; within the space of almost one month it removed and consumed five brothers from our midst. At last half the people of the little village of Sponheim were carried off by the same disease.

Likewise in the same year Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot, had the summer refectory painted with images and names of the abbots of this monastery of ours, Sponheim, in order from the beginning of the foundation down to this year, his nineteenth, together with their coats of arms. In this same year the annual chapter was celebrated at St. Peter’s in Erfurt, on the Sunday after the feast of St. Bartholomew the Apostle. There our abbot Trithemius, before an assembly of fathers, abbots, doctors, and students, delivered a distinguished oration in praise of the monastic life; received with great favor by all, he returned not without [unclear].

In this year, which was the twentieth of our abbot Johannes Trithemius, the winter was exceedingly harsh and long, and here around us there was great mortality of livestock and human beings. Afterward the greatest drought of summer followed in almost all Germany, so that in very many places it did not rain for four months. Hence the hay and oats dried up completely and were high in price, to such a degree that the poor sold their livestock.

In the same year, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of November, Jacob, from the Margraves of Baden, archbishop of Trier, died. He was buried with honor in the greater church of the people of Trier. His obsequies were performed among the people of Koblenz in Lent after Reminiscere Sunday, with great pomp. Our abbot Johannes Trithemius was present at them, sent and appointed, as is customary, as envoy in the name of the most illustrious prince, lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and prince-elector. Afterward Jacob, from the Margraves of Baden, son of Margrave Christopher, formerly coadjutor of the deceased archbishop, as we said above, was confirmed as archbishop of Trier by Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, and was accepted by the greater part of the chapter, people, and land: an elegant and learned man, a most worthy lover of all learned men, than whom hardly any better man could at this time have been found for the pontificate, about thirty years old.

In the same year also, in the month of June, the aforesaid Christopher, Margrave of Baden, again came here, without burden to us, as he is accustomed, to see and speak with Abbot Trithemius; with him he discussed certain secrets in the hidden mysteries of nature, and shared some matters with him.
