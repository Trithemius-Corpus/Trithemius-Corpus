Therefore in the aforementioned year, the twenty-first of Johannes Trithemius as abbot, in the month of May, at the command of the most sacred Emperor Maximilian, Ulrich, duke of Wurttemberg, Wilhelm, landgrave of Hesse, Alexander, duke of Bavaria and count of Veldenz, who was staying in the little town of Zweibrucken, together with many counts and men of the military order, took up arms in these lower lands against Philip, count palatine of the Rhine, prince-elector. With one mind they invaded the Palatine lands and wrought great destruction.

For Ulrich, the magnanimous duke of Wurttemberg, powerfully took certain Palatine fortifications, towns, and castles alike, and subjected Besigheim, Meckmuhl, Lowenstein, the monastery of Maulbronn, together with certain others, to his dominion. For he was indeed a young prince, but prudent and devoted to God in religion, and he did not exceed the Christian manner of waging war: indeed he neither set Christ's churches on fire nor plundered monasteries, but, vigorous in spirit and virtuous in power, so far as could be done, he spared the innocent and the ministers of the Church. He powerfully besieged Bretten, a town of the Palatine; when he saw that he could not take it, he raised the siege and turned his army elsewhere. He subjected Weinsberg, with all its appurtenances, to imperial authority; and unless at last the king's edict had intervened, wishing to spare the man who had been cast down, he would have inflicted heavier losses on the Palatines.

Meanwhile, while these things were being done in the borders of the Swabians, Wilhelm, landgrave of the Hessians, having led his army through Frankfurt, surrounded Bensheim, a town of the Palatine, with a siege. But when those who held the garrison in the town defended themselves bravely, and the Palatine's army was approaching, he withdrew with the matter unfinished. Nevertheless he plundered the villages round about, and whatever belonged by right to the Palatine, and gave them to the flames. He plundered Lorsch, a monastery of the Premonstratensian order, and assessed it a sum of money so that it should not be set on fire. He set fire to the Palatine's most beautiful new castle, which from its founder was called Friedrichsburg, and utterly destroyed it. He also received into surrender the castle of Stein, not far from Worms, through the betrayal of the castellan. Descending from there to Mainz, with the people of Mainz favoring him, he brought his army across and seized, caused to be burned, and utterly destroyed everything round about that belonged to the Palatine's jurisdiction.

Then, moving his army, he pitched camp near the town called Alzey, and everything round about, both in the fields, because it was harvest time, and in the villages, he seized and granted as plunder to his soldiers. Nothing was safe except what had been fortified by the protection of walls; for he did not have at hand the machines necessary for attacking towns and stronger fortifications, and therefore he never conquered anyone resisting him, but took only the dwellings of fleeing peasants and poor people. All the Palatine hamlets round about that he could reach he burned with fire. Whatever was found anywhere, both in churches and in villages, without delay, without distinction of persons, without reverence for places, fell as common plunder.

He took Gauodorum by the surrender of the inhabitants, and, taking plunder from there, let it go, whether by fire [unclear]. In wine, grain, and other necessary human utensils, he had great plunder from the land of the people of Gaw, which, reserved for his own uses, he sent at that time into Hesse. When these things had been done with excessive cruelty around the town of Alzey and in its Gau, the aforementioned Wilhelm, count of the Hessians, led his army to Kreuznach; and, having pitched camp around Bosenheim in the Nahegau, he laid waste everything round about. There, while he was staying in the tents together with the duke of Brunswick, Duke Alexander came to him from Meisenheim, having long since been allied with him, because both seemed to have not only the common causes of the Empire, but also private ones against the count palatine.

But before these things happened, Alexander's men, on the vigil of Peter and Paul, burned certain little villages of the Palatine around Sobernheim, and in the morning entered the monastery of the Wilhelmites called Marienpforte with carts and [unclear]. Carrying off to Meisenheim whatever they could find, after receiving two hundred gold pieces from the prior for security, they promised to preserve his monastery from fire. It was Friday and, as has been said, the vigil of the Apostles.

When Johannes Trithemius, our abbot, heard this, terrified by fear of the enemies, on the same day he withdrew to the nearest town of the Palatine, which the ancients called Stauronesus and which our people call Kreuznach. Having gradually brought in the books, privileges, and all the utensils of the house, lest they lie open as plunder for the adversaries, he remained there for five months and fourteen days, until the end of the war, and on the vigil of St. Andrew returned to the monastery.

Meanwhile, while the count of Hesse was residing in tents in the province around Kreuznach with a very large army, his soldiers plundered everything round about that belonged to the right of the Palatine, sparing absolutely no person or place. Count Emicho of Leiningen was set over the burning, a savage man, regarding neither God nor men, who burned with fire all the Palatine villages round about, except a few which the payment of money had freed.
