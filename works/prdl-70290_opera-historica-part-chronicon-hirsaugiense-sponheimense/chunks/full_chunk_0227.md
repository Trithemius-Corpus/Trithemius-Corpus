In this same year, on the Nones of August, St. Dominic died, the first founder of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man of incomparable holiness.

*1221.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Everhard, the year of the Lord's Nativity 1221, the city of Damietta, which the Christians had taken and possessed in the preceding year with the greatest labor and much shedding of blood, was restored by the Christians to the Sultan of Babylon for the liberation of the captives in his kingdom and of Coradinus his brother. There is no doubt that this was done because their sins deserved it. For Saladin, a little before, seeking peace with the Christians, had promised that he would restore the kingdom of Jerusalem, rebuild that city itself, and grant Damietta, with the adjacent region for twenty miles, to be possessed in peace, provided they proceeded no farther into his land; and now the Christians seek his favor.

In the same year, on the day of St. Margaret, virgin and martyr, the city of Worms was burned by an accidental fire, and many houses, especially around the market and the surrounding streets, were consumed, so that almost half the city was destroyed. The church of St. Paul likewise was also burned, and endless losses were inflicted on the citizens.

In this same year the Friars Minor, entering Worms for the first time, built there a monastery of their order from the alms of the faithful.

*1222.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Everhard, the year of the Lord's Nativity 1222, Conrad, bishop of Speyer, hearing that the nuns at Hausen of our order near Durkheim had departed too far from the path of regular observance, and were living a wicked and excessively shameful life, united their convent, with the consent of Lord Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, to the monastery of Limburg of the same order, and wholly extinguished the title of a convent of nuns, so that when those who were then living had died, none should thereafter be received there, and the abbot of Limburg, through his monks, should have divine services celebrated there for the time being, according to the capacity and revenues of the place.

In the same year, on the seventh Ides of May, which was the Sunday nearest before Ascension Sunday, Henry, son of Emperor Frederick II, at the instance of his father and with the consent of the princes, while still a boy, was consecrated king of the Romans by Engelbert, archbishop of Cologne.

In the same year also, on the third Ides of January, in the morning at the first hour of the day, there was a great earthquake in Cologne and round about, and it overthrew many buildings. In Cyprus too two cities in the same year collapsed from an earthquake, with many people killed. In Italy also and Lombardy there were very great and terrible earthquakes this year, to such an extent that people abandoned the cities and lived in tents. For on the very day of the Nativity of the Lord, and throughout the two following weeks, almost every day twice in Lombardy there was a dreadful earthquake, which in many places overturned buildings and very strong churches, and crushed people together with priests. It also overthrew almost the whole city of Brescia with its people, tore rocks from the mountains, cast down castles, and wretchedly killed many thousands of people in various places. At Rome also earth mixed with blood was seen to fall from the clouds like rain.

In the same year also, Valdemar, king of Denmark, together with his son, was captured by Count Henry of Schwerin, and for two years was held and kept in custody by him in the very strong castle of Dannenberg.

*1223.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Everhard, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1223, Otto, bishop of Wurzburg, died on the Nones of December. Theoderic succeeded him and presided for one year, two months, and fourteen days.

In the same year Everhard, abbot of this monastery of Hirsau, obtained from Emperor Frederick II confirmation of indulgences and privileges with fuller favor, as is clear in the letters, which were given at Precima in the month of January of the aforesaid year.

In the same year also, after the death of the wife of Emperor Frederick, namely the first, the daughter of the king of Aragon, there was betrothed to him as a second wife the daughter of John, king of Jerusalem, whom not long afterward he took as wife.

In this same year there arose a very great pestilence among cattle, which, lasting almost three years, began from Hungary and infected all Germany and Gaul with its foulness, so that scarcely a green third part of the cattle and draft-animals was spared from so great a pestilence.
