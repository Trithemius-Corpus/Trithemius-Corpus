*Ludovicus, firstborn of the Palatine, is born.*

In the same year, on the second day of the month of July, that is, on the day of the Visitation of blessed Mary ever virgin, at about the third hour after midday, there was born in the castle of Heidelberg the illustrious prince Ludovicus, firstborn of our most distinguished prince Philip, Count Palatine and Duke of Bavaria, who today, being in the twenty-fourth year of his age, does not yet have a wife.

In the same year also, around the feast of Pentecost, Rupert, archbishop of Cologne, according to the report of certain persons, was captured by the soldiers of Henry, provincial count of Hesse, brother of Hermann, governor of Cologne; he was placed in custody in the castle of Blanckenstein, survived there for two years in the greatest distress, and died there.

In this same year Otto, formerly abbot of this monastery, died on Mount St. Rupert near Bingen, where for eight years he had held the office of confessor. He was buried there in the chapel of blessed Mary ever virgin, near the entrance to the cloister.

In the same year also Johannes Schonborn, dean of the church of St. John at Mainz, by commission of Pope Sixtus IV made in the preceding year, incorporated the parish church of this village of Sponheim into this monastery, at the request of Johannes Kolnhausen, abbot.

In the same year also the aforesaid Abbot Johannes closed the principal gate of the monastery, which had been situated on the southern side, where the abbot's residence now is, on account of the excessive movement of those passing through; he made a new one toward the east, where it now is.

*1379.*

In the tenth year of Johannes Kolnhausen, our abbot, Johannes Ruchard of Upper Wesel, doctor of sacred theology, was condemned at Mainz to recant certain articles which he was said to have preached publicly in the church of Worms, compelled by the doctor inquisitor. All his volumes, while he himself looked on, were handed over to the fire and burned; he himself, to carry out penance, was banished to the convent of the Augustinian friars there, and, consumed by grief, died shortly afterward.

The first article which he was said to have preached was: that the prelates of the church did not have authority to establish or add anything to those things which Christ and the apostles had commanded; indeed, that neither the apostles nor the supreme pontiffs received such power from Christ.

The second article: that it is permitted to no man, however holy, learned, or educated, to expound the words of Christ and the Gospel; that sacred Scripture was not interpreted by the holy fathers in the spirit in which it was first handed down and established.

The third article was against the authority of the pope and the keys of holy mother church: for he said that indulgences were nothing other than pious frauds and deceptions of Christians, and that those were therefore foolish and senseless who went to Rome for indulgences, which they could have found at home, provided they were truly contrite for their sins with the intention of amendment.

The fourth article, following from the first: that the commands of the church, of the pope, and of other prelates do not bind under mortal sin, because they do not have authority to establish law.

The fifth article: that there is not, nor ever has been, original sin; nor are little children conceived in original [sin], nor for that reason damned; and that he himself had never been subject to original sin.

The sixth article was: that all priests are in reality bishops; [unclear] priests confer nothing upon men toward salvation, but that men can be saved by faith, concord, and peace without priests.

The seventh article: that fasting, since it was not instituted by Christ, does not bind us to fast; for the church cannot bind one who is unwilling in that which Christ did not command, since it does not have authority to establish canons and laws, as in the first article.

The eighth article: that extreme unction is not a sacrament, because it was instituted not by Christ but by men; but that oil remains oil just as it was before.

The ninth article was: since it is nowhere read in sacred Scripture that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Son, but rather the contrary, it is better to believe, with the wise Greeks, that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone and not from the Son, because the Son said this.

He is also reported to have preached many other erroneous articles, such as that the canonical hours should not be said, that feasts should not be observed, that the continence of clerics should not be observed, that the church also can err and has erred; that blessings of inanimate things in the church, of herbs, lights, water, vessels, vestments, and the like, should not be cared for; and many others. Yet he publicly recanted all of these in the year written above, on the Sunday Esto mihi.
