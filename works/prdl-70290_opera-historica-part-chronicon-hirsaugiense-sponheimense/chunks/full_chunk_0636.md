The one is the pure beginning; the binary is composed by departing from unity, because it is impossible that there be two beginnings. Therefore only the ternary, [unclear], virtuous, and powerful, having overcome the binary, returns into its own beginning, not by nature, but by participation in likeness; in it, without contradiction, the mind understands all the mysteries of the beautifully ordered secret.

This is the most beautiful virtue of all strength, which conquers all worldly things and penetrates every solid body, dyeing each thing with desirable beauty, just as the alchemists indeed promise in composite bodies; but they err and deceive all by whom they have willingly been heard. They wish to imitate nature, and to make by art what belongs to the universal alone, since they do not understand the root of the virtue of nature. Do not assent to the most foolish alchemists, for they are fools and pupils of apes, enemies of nature and despisers of heavenly things, without whose intelligible kinship alchemy is nothing.

But he who does not understand earthly things, as our Lord Jesus Christ had said, how will he find heavenly things? Our philosophy is heavenly, not earthly: that by the gaze of the mind, through faith and knowledge, we may faithfully behold that highest principle which we call God, truly believing, purely knowing, and always adoring with the service and worship of most fervent love the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one principle, one God, and one supreme good, existing eternally in a Trinity of persons, from whom are all things that can exist by any power. Unless the animated mind rises up to him, it will understand none of the things that are beautiful, but will waste away in its own ignorance.

This ascent, O Germanus, is not for common people, nor is it sufficient by imitating those who are borne upward on only one wing, but it is familiar to very few indeed, namely to those who have not rashly reduced themselves into unity. Many indeed try, but the ternary is not in the mind of all. Do we not, when about to look up at the sky, necessarily first lift the head, and lower it afterward when we have looked upward? It is permitted to the eyes alone to behold the sun. Ears do not see. Therefore, for the soul to climb up, let it become not an ear, but an eye and a heart; and from the ternary let it become unity by participation in goodness toward the beginning, because there is one omnipotent good, not two nor more. For unless unity comes to be, there will not be a conjunction of likeness in the mind, nor participation in the good, and without these there is no transcendence; and unless these precede, it will be able to attain neither the understanding of superior things nor the proper operation of inferior things.

Things, however, both universal and singular, necessarily, and the conditions of things, are in some cases manifest, in some most manifest. And some are hidden, others more hidden, others most hidden, both to sense and to reason. The nature of the things themselves produces this diversity. Hence it happens that certain men become wiser than the rest. He is called the wiser, however, who perceives things less perceptible. Number consists in order and measure. Order also cannot exist without number and measure. Measure, moreover, consists in number and order. Here unity and the ternary do not admit the binary, but, stripped of all multitude, they stand in the first principle by the simplest purity innate in them.

This is the way to the higher things, O Germanus, by which the ancient sages [unclear] intelligible [unclear] are now considered wise by our own wise men. Do you wish to hear more fully? Study begets knowledge; knowledge gives birth to love; love to likeness; likeness to communion; communion to virtue; virtue to dignity; dignity to power; and power makes a miracle. This is the one path to the end of magical perfections, both divine and natural, from which everything superstitious, deceptive, and diabolical is kept far away and confounded.

Indeed, by magic we wish nothing else to be understood than wisdom, that is, the understanding of physical and metaphysical things, which consists in the science of divine and natural virtues. You must know that a heavenly harmony, not material, but spiritual consonance, is to be perceived by us, where number, order, and measure come together through the ternary into unity; and to this consonance all our inferior things must be conformed. It is foolish to think that heavenly harmony forms a sound perceptible to the ears, caused by the motion of consonant stars. Heavenly harmony, however, is the inviolable consonance, in number, order, and measure, of the distributions of bodies. But it is necessary to go beyond this, so that through the ternary an ascent may be prepared to that harmony which is supercelestial, where there is nothing material, but all things are spiritual. From there the likeness must be assumed by the mind, whence it came. For stellar harmony neither gave the mind nor flows into it.
