*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

But to the monastery of Lauense, which Father S. built in the province of the Carinthians, as was said above, he appointed as abbot Wetzillo, a vigorous man, revered for religious severity, and sufficiently imbued both with monastic institutions and with knowledge of the Scriptures.

Segewinus, a monk of the monastery of Hirsau, sent into Carinthia with the aforesaid abbot Wetzillo without the permission of Abbot Gebhard and of the brethren of Hirsau, received the abbacy of Rosati near Aquileia. When he learned that this displeased his brethren, he returned, contrite, to the monastery of Hirsau, and in the meantime resigned that abbacy of his. Abbot Gebhard, to test his penitence and contrition, made him sit in the last place of the congregation. When he saw that he bore this humbly, with the counsel of the brethren he restored him to his proper place. But another certain man of ours, named Gaudentius, who had gone with him, having likewise obtained an abbacy without the consent of the brethren of Hirsau, did not return to the monastery for penance, and therefore is not counted among the abbots sent from Hirsau. They also rejected Manegold, a monk of the convent of Hirsau, who without the consent of the abbot and brethren had obtained the abbacy of Campidonum in the diocese of Constance from King Henry V through the intervention of friends, by the unanimous counsel of his brethren and abbots.

To the monastery which is called Beinwil, a certain monk of the monastery of Hirsau was given as abbot, named Esso, a man mature in morals and life and a fervent zealot of regular discipline, under Abbot Gebhard, who succeeded St. William.

But to the monastery in Odenheim of the diocese of Speyer, the abbot who had been sent gave Everard, a very notable monk of the convent of Hirsau; concerning its foundation we shall make mention hereafter in its own time. Gerung also, a monk of Hirsau and prior of St. Aurelius across the bridge, certainly a very religious man, was made abbot of the monastery of Paulinzelle in Saxony, in the diocese of Mainz, under Abbot Bruno; concerning his praiseworthy way of life, when we have come to the year of the Lord 1107 and the times of that Bruno, we shall speak more fully. To the same place, Paulinzelle, after Gerung, Ulrich was taken up as abbot, a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, a man revered in morals and life, and no less worthy and fit for pastoral care by his learning in the Scriptures than by his conduct.

To the monastery also in Godifangia of the diocese of Speyer, the venerable monk of this house named Wolpeto was sent as abbot, a man of great virtues and very well experienced in spiritual as well as temporal matters. To the same monastery also, at another later time, Burchard, a monk of the monastery of Hirsau, was appointed abbot, a distinguished lover of regular discipline.

But to the monastery of Mons Monachorum near Bamberg in East Francia, Wölfran, a monk, was transferred at the insistence of the brethren serving God there, and was solemnly ordained abbot. Evernold, a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, was appointed abbot at the monastery of Briuernugense, a man most holy in knowledge and conduct; he is said to have shone with many miracles in life and after death, and his life and conduct, together with his miracles, written in two little books, are preserved more fully in the aforesaid monastery.

Drutwin, a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, was sent with twelve monks to a newly built monastery of the diocese of Mainz in Hesse, and was ordained abbot in the year of the Lord 1119, under Abbot Bruno. The name of the monastery is called Breitenau; concerning it we shall speak more fully afterward in its own place and time. Egbert, a monk of this monastery, was sent to Passau about the same time and appointed abbot, a man not at all poorly learned, of venerable reputation among us both for morals and for learning in the Scriptures. To the same place also afterward the venerable [unclear] of this [unclear] was appointed abbot, who was a distinguished cultivator of regular discipline. Reginbold, a monk of the monastery, was sent to a certain monastery which is called Navy, and at the insistence of many was ordained abbot. Adelbert also, a monk of this monastery, after the death of that Reginbold, was sent as abbot to the same place, a man worthy of so great an honor, since he was venerable no less in life than in knowledge.
