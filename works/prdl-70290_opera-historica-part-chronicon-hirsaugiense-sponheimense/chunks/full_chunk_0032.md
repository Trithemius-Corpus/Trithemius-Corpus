*On Diethmar, sixth abbot of this monastery, who presided for twenty-seven years, and on the various events of that time in Germany.*

After the death of Abbot Rudolph, great difficulty arose among the brothers in choosing a successor. *Dissension occurs in the election.* For some of the elders, who had the discipline of the regular life at heart, desired to have such a shepherd as would wish and be able, by word and example, to revive that former fervor of monastic religion, now almost extinct in this monastery. On the other hand, the greater, though not healthier, part of the brothers, who by the [bad?] example of Abbot Rudolph [unclear].

At last, after many discussions, with the venerable Hildebert, abbot of the monastery of Fulda, interposing his counsel, they unanimously chose Diethmar, a monk of Saint Gall, already known to them before, since, when Linthelm once held the school of Saint Aurelius, he had lived among them for two years. *By the counsel of Hildebert, abbot of Fulda. Diethmar becomes abbot.*

Choosing him, therefore, as abbot on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of May, they received him, brought with honor from the monastery of Saint Gall, in the same year in which Rudolph had entered the way of all flesh, namely in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 923, in the thirteenth indiction, in the sixth year of Henry, king of the Romans. He presided for twenty-seven years. *Life of Abbot Diethmar.*

He was born in Swabia, begotten of his father Arnold and his mother Helwiug, who, indeed, in respect of worldly rank, were marked by no prerogative of nobility, but who made humble lineage bright by the love of Christ. For they offered this son of theirs, while still a little child, in the aforesaid monastery of Saint Gall, to serve almighty God, trusting that by his merit they too could be reconciled to God.

But the boy, once devoted to God by his parents, like another Samuel began to act more zealously in all things that pertained to conduct, and to preserve most diligently what was said by the elders. He was docile in mind and, while still a little boy, flourished with such liveliness of memory that it was regarded as a marvel [unclear].

At that time there was a monk named Hartmond, who after Notger presided for many years over the school of the monks at Saint Gall; we have already made mention of him earlier. *[Adelhard?] the scholastic. His talent is commended.* Since he had a singular gift for teaching, he took Diethmar, together with others, to be instructed, and in a short time made him more learned than those of his own age.

He was indeed [unclear] in talents; when he had come to [unclear], he became learned in all philosophy, both secular and ecclesiastical, no less distinguished for the integrity of monastic conduct than for learning in letters. In his twenty-eighth year he received the grade of the priesthood. And after seven years, when he was thirty-five years old, [unclear]. *Age of Abbot Diethmar.*

Not many years later, when Hartmond had died, he succeeded him in the direction of the literary [school]. He was sparing in food, still more sparing in drink; and by most prudent governance he recalled regular discipline in this monastery, almost collapsed, to its former state. *The character of his conduct.* In every business to be done he conducted himself prudently; almighty God [bestowed?] on him the grace of his blessing in many ways. He was not only learned, but also in the Roman language as [unclear].

He bore the weaknesses of his brothers so prudently and patiently that no one was ever seen so insolent that, through his most prudent long-suffering, he did not make progress toward better things.
