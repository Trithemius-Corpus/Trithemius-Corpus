In this year St. Hildegard, virgin of Christ, a nun of our order, foundress and mistress of the monastery of St. Rupert near Bingen, died on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of October, in the eighty-second year of her age, in the thirteenth indiction. She was buried with great honor before the high altar of the aforesaid church, with many honorable persons and the whole congregation standing by. This holy virgin was deemed worthy to be visited by divine revelations from her youth; from these, by divine command, she wrote down many things for the use of posterity. But since she was ignorant of the Latin tongue and had learned nothing from any human being except simple psalmody, taught by the inward instruction of the Holy Spirit she perfectly understood every writing, arrangement, or construction of speech. Her revelations, moreover, and heavenly visions she set forth partly in the German tongue; these Gotfridus, monk of St. Disibod, her chaplain and confessor, made into [unclear] Latin, reducing them into that order and form in which they are read today. Among her other volumes, however, there is a great volume in that same place which contains her letters to various persons, homilies, lives of saints, and other things which, taught by God, she published; the nuns of that place falsely assert that she wrote it with her own hand, since she was ignorant of the Latin tongue and, because of frequent illnesses, wholly indisposed in her damp head for writing. For none of those things which are displayed there today should be believed to have been written by her own hand, since indeed she did not know how to write; but the aforesaid monk either wrote everything or caused it to be written.

*MCLXXXI.*

In this year Adelgerus, the third abbot of this monastery, died on the sixth day before the Nones of July, about the twelfth hour in the middle of the night. He was buried in the brothers’ choir before the steps of the presbytery. Under this abbot’s rule regular discipline was quite good in the present place, with the greatest charity among the brothers and religious devotion; and when this reputation was laudably spread round about, it earned great reverence from the faithful peoples. Hence very many, conferring generous alms on this place, commended themselves to the prayers of the brothers; and not only those living nearby strove by their benefactions to relieve the brothers’ poverty, but also men from distant places, stirred by devotion to the brothers, sent their alms here. Among many we shall set down at least one, so that the truth of what has been said may be proved. For thus, among other things, the brothers wrote to a certain man who gave his goods generously to this monastery: Wernerus of blessed memory, pastor of St. Columba in Cologne, gave ten marks for the purchase of a vineyard in Monaecha, so that annually the abbot in Sponheim would pay one cartload of wine to his relative Wilhelm. After the departure of his uncle, however, Wilhelm offered the above-mentioned vineyard of the town, after receiving full fraternity, for the use of the brothers, so that in the granary of his Lord, having full charity in bread, wine, and lean fish, they may rejoice in the Lord, giving thanks to him for this benefaction. And so that this may always be held valid, it was confirmed by the attestation of the present page under the abbot’s anathema: that if anyone should wish to annul this, or should annul it, he be blotted out from the book of the living and not be written with the just, unless through repentance and satisfaction he should regain his senses. The other donations of the faithful made to this monastery under Abbot Adelgerus, since they were numerous, we have not wished to recount in this place, in order to remove tedium.

*Concerning Baldemar, the fourth abbot of this place, who presided for 17 years, 2 months, and 17 days; concerning his deeds with regard to the present monastery.*

After Adelgerus, the third abbot of this monastery, had died and been buried, as we have said, two of the brothers were not present; when their arrival had been awaited, and they had then appeared on the third day, that is, on the third day before the Nones of July, Prior Eberoldus, together with the conventual brothers, began to have discussion concerning the election of a new abbot. After many [unclear], at length they [unclear] gave their consent. He, ordained by Christianus, archbishop of Mainz, presided over this monastery for seventeen years, twelve months, and 17 days, a man sufficiently religious, experienced in both [unclear] of cloistered persons. He was born from the county of Sponheim, in the town of Kreuznach, of parents honored according to the world. Coming to the monastery under Abbot Craffto, he was made a monk when he was eighteen years old, a youth of good character and already, at the beginnings of literary instruction, not moderately learned.
