*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

...to the same monastery, with the consent of the archbishop of Mainz and the abbot of St Alban, she transferred herself, and served almighty God most devoutly in fasts and prayers, day and night, until the end of her life. She was buried in the middle of the choir beside the tomb of her brother, Abbot Bernhelm, not without a reputation for holiness. For the harmony of angels was heard at her passing, and various visions were shown; and St Hildegard, while she was still living in the flesh, bore witness to her holiness.

Around the feast of St Michael King Frederick set out for Italy with a great army. *The expedition of the king into Italy.* Meanwhile Pope Anastasius died. Nicholas the Englishman, first a monk of St Rufus, then abbot, afterward cardinal bishop of Albano of our order, succeeded him, called Adrian IV, and sat for 4 years and 10 months. King Frederick, after his army had crossed into Italy, at the first assault subdued Piacenza, a city of Emilia; he also devastated certain castles by fire because of the insolence of the Milanese and Cremonese. Then he burned Spoleto, the very wealthy city of Umbria, entirely with fire. And so, passing with his army toward the sea [unclear], he came to Rome, where he was honorably received by the Roman pontiff and the whole senate, was consecrated emperor, and, after the solemn ordination, when a sedition had been stirred up by certain common people at noon, he was unexpectedly attacked in battle. But the emperor and his men, suddenly armed, drove the enemy across the Tiber with drawn swords, and raged against them with such slaughter that the very bed of the river was entirely changed by the blood of the slain. Having carried out this bloody victory on 18 June with a small band of his men, he did not delay on the following day but began his return, and led his army back unharmed into his homeland.

In these times the bishopric of Lübeck was transferred from Oldenburg, and Gerold was ordained the first bishop there.

*1155.*

In the 34th year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1155, there was a serious war between Arnold, archbishop of Mainz, and Hermann, count palatine of the Rhine. *War between the archbishop of Mainz and the count palatine.* But Frederick the Emperor, returning from Italy, so subdued the disturbers of the kingdom and of peace that in a short time a very firm peace was restored by the terror of his arrival. For the emperor himself had established Arnold as archbishop, against the wishes of many powerful men round about, since they knew him to be a just and severe man, in no way consenting to their wicked designs. Therefore, while the emperor was in Italy, Hermann, count [unclear], Emicho, count of Leiningen, Godfrey, count of Sponheim, Henry, count of Katzenelnbogen, Conrad, count of Kirchberg, and Hericus, count of Didesheim, with certain others, took up arms against the pontiff. *Counts on the side of Hermann.* The emperor, therefore, returning from Italy, celebrated the Lord’s Nativity at Worms, and, by the counsel of the princes, imposed satisfaction as penance on the aforesaid counts together with the aforesaid Archbishop Arnold, because they had violated the peace that had been commanded. For the aforesaid counts Emicho, Godfrey, Henry, and others, on the side of the aforesaid Count Hermann, carried dogs at the emperor’s command; and so Lord Arnold the archbishop absolved them from excommunication. But on the side of the bishop himself, Count Louis of Lohun, Count William of Glitzberg, and others began to carry dogs, but out of reverence for the archbishop they were ordered by the emperor to desist. *The carrying of dogs as satisfaction.*

In the same year Bernard, bishop of Hildesheim in Saxony, because he had been blind for ten years and was useless for ruling, resigned the bishopric; and in his place Bruno, dean of the same church, was ordained, a holy man and most devoted to blessed Mary ever virgin. He was the first to celebrate the octaves of her Nativity, and solemnly instituted their celebration in his diocese. And the most holy Virgin herself once visibly appeared to him at matins while he was celebrating those same octaves, and, giving thanks to him for so great a service, [unclear] great consolation and hope of future recompense. *The emperor, dismissing his wife, marries [unclear].* [unclear]

In the course of the 35th year of Abbot Volmar, Arnold, archbishop of Mainz, provoked by the injuries of certain men, came into the presence of Pope Adrian; honorably received by him, he obtained from him the apostolic legation, and then, returning to Mainz, was received with honor.

In this same year the emperor Frederick took as wife Agnes, daughter of the prince of Burgundy, having repudiated his former legitimate wife at Constance; from this a very grave [unclear] in the church.

Hermann, count palatine, died. He was succeeded by the emperor’s brother on his father’s side, named Conrad, a man forgetful of God, who by violence inflicted many injuries on churches and monasteries and their ministers, especially on Conrad, bishop of Worms.
