For the aforesaid lord John Kolnhausen, our abbot, at that time had three convents of nuns of our order entrusted to him, namely Bibelis in the diocese of Strasbourg, Nuvvenburg, and Lobenfeld in that of Worms, for which he was compelled to provide. After him there was no prior for two years, but an interprior or president appointed by the said abbot: Henry Molitoris of Gelnhausen, one of the first reformers sent here from St. James, a good and gentle man, but too lax in correcting offenders; for that reason he was later deposed.

*1483.*

In the fourteenth year of John Kolnhausen our abbot, William, provincial count of Hesse, died. He had as wife the daughter of Philip, the last count of Katzenelnbogen; with her, after the death of his father-in-law, he obtained that county with a very great treasure, and joined it to the county of Hesse down to the present day.

*William Ludgerianus of Hesse dies.*

*The county of Katzenelnbogen is transferred to Hesse.*

In the same year, on the seventeenth day of the month of July, after the death of Abbot Reinhard, lord John of Kolnhausen, the twenty-fourth abbot of this monastery, with the consent and will of lord Albert, administrator of the church of Mainz, was transferred to the abbacy of the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter in Seligenstadt, while the convent of this community protested against it and opposed it with all its strength, but accomplished nothing. He afterwards resigned our abbacy into the hands of the convent, on the twenty-seventh day of the same month, which was the Sunday immediately after the feast of St. James. He governed the aforesaid monastery of Seligenstadt for almost nine years; afterwards, weighed down by age and illness, he resigned the abbacy there also. Surviving thirty months, he died: certainly an excellent man, who did many good things for both this monastery and that one.

*John Kolnhausen is transferred to Seligenstadt.*

*Concerning John Trithemius, twenty-fifth abbot of this monastery; concerning certain deeds done by him, and various events of that time. He presided for twenty-three years, two months, and fourteen days, and was transferred to Würzburg.*

After the transfer and resignation of John Kolnhausen, abbot of this monastery, to Seligenstadt, Henry the president and the capitular brothers, setting the twenty-ninth day of the month of July for the election of a new pastor, meanwhile summoned by messengers and letters those absent from the monastery. After a three-day fast had first been observed, with the customary prayers, and after the visitors deputed to them by the order had been summoned for the appointed date, namely Hermann, abbot of St. James near Mainz, and Conrad, abbot of the monastery of St. John in the Rheingau, they appeared together with lord John Kolnhausen, formerly abbot here but then abbot in Seligenstadt, and on that very day of Felix, pope and martyr, which is the fourth day before the Kalends of August and was the Tuesday after the feast of St. James, they began in the name of God to hold the business of the election. Therefore, after the Mass of the Holy Spirit had been solemnly celebrated, the brothers assembled in the chapter place adopted the way of compromise, which seemed safer, to be observed in the election. They committed the matter to two abbots, Hermann of St. James and John of Seligenstadt, and three from the convent: Henry of Gelnhausen, president; John of Donna, custodian; and John of Dreysa, master of the donors, so that, after hearing in secret the votes of each before a notary and witnesses, they should elect as abbot of this monastery, in the name of the whole convent, the one to whom the greater and sounder part had directed its votes. When, therefore, the votes of all had been examined, the greater and sounder part elected John Trithemius as abbot, who had been in the order only eight months and seven days after his profession. He was younger in the order than all the professed, seeing that from the time when he assumed the novices’ habit he had lived in the monastery no more than one year, three months, and seven days up to the day of his election. Therefore, when the election was published, all unanimously consented to him, and each signed the decree of election with his own hand. Thus elected in the year and on the day prescribed, in the same year, on the day of the Assumption of blessed Mary ever virgin, he was confirmed by Albert, administrator of the church of Mainz, in the castle of Steinheim, there being present Hermann of St. James, John in Seligenstadt, Melchior in Schöntal, abbots of our order, and very many others.
