Meanwhile, matters in Italy having been prosperously accomplished, the emperor Lothair, when he had now begun to return home, on the very journey, situated not far from Nuremberg, in a certain castle of Welf, duke of Bavaria, by age as much as by infirmity, closed his last day, the 2nd [day before] the Nones of December—an outstanding man, and worthily to be called father of [his] country; whose body, brought back into Saxony, was honorably laid to rest in his monastery of Königslutter, which he had built, as we said.

In the same year died the bishop of Auxerre, [risen] from [being] abbot of the monastery of St Germain of our order, a man of great sanctity and religion. When he labored with [his] last sickness, and felt the final day to be at hand, around the ninth hour of the day he admonished the brothers standing around with these words: "Dearest brothers, let us pay to the Lord the hours of the day which remain, which we owe; for, the hours finished, I shall sleep and rest in the Lord." They therefore sang praises to the Lord, and, compline said, as he had foretold, the holy man rested in peace, and is buried in the church of St Germain, where he had earlier been abbot.

After Hugh, archbishop of Cologne, whom we said died in Italy, Arnold, provost of St Andrew in Cologne, son of the count of Wied, is raised to the archbishopric—who at length, by Pope Eugenius III in the council of Rheims, was suspended from the pontifical office, as we shall say in [its] place.

The emperor Lothair being dead (as has been said), Adelbero, bishop of Trier, fearing lest Henry, duke of Bavaria surnamed the Proud, son-in-law of the emperor—of whom we made mention above, to whom the regalia had long since been committed by Lothair himself—should prevail in the assembly of the princes (he who already so far reckoned himself secure of attaining [the kingdom] that he did not deign to make words about the kingdom to any of the princes): [Adelbero] acted with great industry, and, together with duke Conrad himself and the bishop of Worms, [arranged] that they should meet in conference at Coblenz in mid-Lent, the see of Mainz being vacant; where Arnold, the aforesaid archbishop of Cologne, met Adelbero, and, treating counsels together among themselves, they swore that they would elect Conrad king.

[1138.] In the year of abbot Volmar 17, which was of the Lord's nativity 1138, Conrad III of that name, duke of Alemannia or Swabia, brother of Frederick duke of Alsace, elected king of the Romans, reigned 14 years. But against this Conrad the princes of Bavaria and Saxony elected Henry the Proud, duke, son-in-law of the emperor Lothair, as king; and evils were multiplied in the land: for Conrad cunningly acquired the regalia which were with him, deposited by Lothair, and wished to deprive him [Henry] of the duchy of Saxony, [it] being given to the same Adelbert, margrave of Soltwedel. Hence slaughters, plunderings, and burnings through all Saxony.

In the same year king Conrad celebrated the Lord's Easter at Cologne. Thence going up, in the third week after Easter, he was received at Mainz with great favor of clergy and people. There Adalbert the younger, nephew through a brother of the deceased elder [Adalbert], by the common consent of all, by command of the king, was designated archbishop. Now the elder Adalbert, Saint Bernard of Clairvaux cooperating, while he yet lived, founded a monastery of the Cistercian order at Eberbach, almost two miles from Mainz, in the Rheingau, in which also he was buried.

In the same year king Conrad held a court of the princes at Bamberg for the feast of Pentecost, where the princes of almost the whole kingdom had assembled, except Henry, duke of Bavaria.
