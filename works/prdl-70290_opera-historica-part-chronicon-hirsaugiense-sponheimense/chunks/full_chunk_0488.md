In this year the Council of Basel, which had lasted for many years, came to an end.

*MCCCCXLIX.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Conrad there was a great pestilence throughout all Germany, which in a short time consumed many thousands of people with a miserable plague.

In the same year Albert, Margrave of Brandenburg, with certain princes allied to his aid, namely the Archbishop of Mainz, the Margrave of Baden, the counts of Wittenberg, the Bishop of Bamberg, and the Bishop of Eichstatt, led an army against the cities and towns of the Empire, and especially against the Nurembergers, to whom he was most hostile, raging against them for a year and six months with slaughter, burning, plunder, devastation of fields, and uprooting of vineyards in an exceedingly inhuman manner. The citizens, however, struck with terror at the first assault, kept themselves within the walls. Afterwards, having assembled an army, they powerfully invaded the lands of the princes and began to devastate everything with fire, sword, and plunder, until at length, the stores of both parties being exhausted, they were compelled to enter into concord and keep peace.

*MCCCCL.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Conrad there was a very great gathering of peoples from the whole Christian world at Rome, for the indulgences and remissions of the Jubilee; and so great was the multitude of people [unclear], in the first year of his age, for whom he appointed his brother Frederick guardian, commending to him that son, his wife, and everything that he had by his own right, namely on this condition: that as soon as the son reached the eighteenth year of his age, he should restore to him without objection the paternal duchy with all its appurtenances. Having arranged these things in this way, he died, and was buried in the church of the Holy Spirit. And so, after his brother’s death, Frederick undertook the government of his whole duchy, and governed, defended, and enlarged it vigorously until his own death, for twenty-six years, down to the twenty-seventh year of Duke Philip; and, waging many wars, driven by necessity, he everywhere with good fortune successfully overcame all his adversaries.

But in the aforesaid year of the Lord’s incarnation MCCCCLVI, on a day of the month of May, after midnight, around the first hour, through the carelessness and negligence of those who had repaired the organ during the day, a great fire was kindled in the greater church of Speyer, which consumed the whole structure of the body of the church, the roof, the organ, the tower, and the bells.

In these times also the art of printing and marking books with characters was newly discovered in the city of Mainz, by a certain citizen who was called Johannes Gutenberg. When, because of the very great difficulty of the new invention, he had spent all his substance in bringing it to completion, he was helped by the counsel and aid of good men, Johann Fust and others, and perfected the unworkable undertaking.

The first spreader of this art after the inventor himself was Peter Schoffer of Gernsheim, who printed many volumes in his time. The aforesaid Johannes Gutenberg, moreover, lived at Mainz in the house called zum Jungen, which house is known down to the present day as marked by the name of that new art.

*MCCCCLI.*

In the seventh year of Conrad our abbot, the forty-fifth provincial chapter of our order was held on the twenty-fourth day of the month of May, which was the fourth Sunday after the feast of Easter, with continuation on the following days, in the monastery of St. Stephen at Wurzburg, Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal of the title of St. Peter in Chains and legate of the apostolic see, being present and presiding over the chapter, together with the abbots of St. Giles at Nuremberg, St. Burkard at Wurzburg, and St. Gotthard in Hildesheim. In this chapter, indeed, many matters for the reform of the order were discussed by the aforesaid cardinal and the fathers.
