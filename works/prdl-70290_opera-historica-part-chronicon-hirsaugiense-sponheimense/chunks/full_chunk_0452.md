At this provincial chapter Bernhard, abbot of this monastery, was present in person. When he had set forth to those presiding the insolence of his monks, who were refusing to undertake reform, it was entrusted to the visitors that they should compel them to reform by ecclesiastical censure. Therefore the visitors came to the monastery, but accomplished little, because they were deceived by the deceitfulness of the monks, who made great promises in their presence but after their departure observed nothing.

In the same year also John, duke of Burgundy, entered France with a strong hand against the king of the French, inflicted very many losses upon him, and took the city of Paris with armed force. There many bishops, priests, doctors, students, and citizens were cruelly slain, and the city was miserably laid waste. But in the following year the aforesaid duke of Burgundy, as we shall say, was beheaded in the presence of the king.

In these times also there were great discords and dissensions between Theoderic, archbishop of Cologne, and the citizens of Cologne. Proceeding as far as open hostilities, they brought many losses on both sides to the parties, until at last they were reconciled.

*1419.*

In this year, which was the twenty-eighth of Bernhard our abbot, on the twenty-third day of September, John, archbishop of Mainz, died. Conrad, son of the Count of the Rhine of Stein, succeeded him in the archbishopric, elected at Ruedesheim on the sixth of the Ides of October, a magnificent and powerful man, who presided well for almost sixteen years.

In the same year John, duke of Burgundy, was beheaded in the presence of the king of France, contrary to sworn faith, since they had sworn upon the Lord's body that peace between them was to be kept most firmly; and as evidence of this, they received from the hand of the priest at the altar the host of the Lord's body, divided into two parts. Philip, his son, avenged this murder of the duke with very severe punishment: for, having taken the king of the English as an ally, he devastated all France with fire and plunder, took Paris, killed many thousands of people, almost utterly destroyed the kingdom itself, and forced the king to flee from France for a time.

*1420.*

In the year of the Lord [1420], the twenty-ninth of Bernhard our abbot, Emericus Ulner of Sponheim died on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of June, a professed monk of this monastery, who for more than thirty years had held the office of cellarer, or provider, quite faithfully and energetically. After his death Bernhard, finding among the monks no one whom he could trust, had the monastery's domestic affairs governed for a time by a secular priest; nevertheless, changing this again not long afterward, he entrusted it to a monk.

In the same year, on the fifth day of the month of May, the third provincial chapter of our order was celebrated in the monastery of Fulda, in the diocese of Wuerzburg. Presiding there were Lambert, abbot of Michelsberg near Bamberg, Frederick of Hirsau, and Henry of St. Godehard's at Hildesheim, abbots of monasteries of our order; many things were decreed there for the reform of the order.

*1421.*

In the year [1421], the thirtieth of Bernhard our abbot, Conrad, prior of this monastery, died, the twenty-fifth man, as we said above, quite good and honorable according to the customs of that time. Since he had been brought up from boyhood among the unreformed, he neither learned nor cared for the reform of monastic life, believing it sufficient for his salvation if he lived according to the customs he had found. After him Gotfridus of Schmidberg, a professed monk of this place, was appointed prior of this monastery. He was the twenty-sixth in the order of priors from the beginning of the foundation of this monastery, and he presided in the office of prior for almost nine years. He did not care for reform, but rather was always opposed in everything. For the monks of this monastery at that time were excessively lax, rebellious religious against both God and their abbot in everything that pertained to religion.
