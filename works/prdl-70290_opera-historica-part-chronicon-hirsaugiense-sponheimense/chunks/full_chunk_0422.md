*Chronicle of Trithemius*

*The Rhine, rising, overthrew many houses.* In the same year also, on the vigil of St. James the Apostle, the Main River, rising contrary to its nature and custom, suddenly discharged into the Rhine so great a flood of waters that no one living at that time remembered having seen the like. It also inflicted many losses on people, overthrew entire villages, and destroyed fields, vineyards, gardens, and many houses adjoining the Rhine.

At this time died Rupert, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, son of Rudolf, who formerly, as we have said, had been driven into England by the emperor Louis; and on the twelfth day of February he was buried in the church of the New Town, which he himself had founded. This same Rupert established the general or universal university at Heidelberg, which, raised up by many benefactions, Pope Benedict XII confirmed. He also built from the foundations, with handsome stone vaulting, the collegiate church in the New Town, as we have said, and endowed it quite magnificently.

1343. In the second year of our abbot William, our estate in Bockenau, which is called the Abbot’s Court, with its fields, vineyards, meadows, gardens, and all its appurtenances, came from his patrimony into the possession of the monastery. For when the mother of that abbot died, his father having died long before, a division of the paternal inheritance was made among the brothers; and the aforesaid estate, with certain other goods, fell to his share. With the consent of his brothers he gave it to this monastery as a perpetual possession, and we possess it peacefully.

But the other estate in Bockenau, which is called the Praesentia Court, came into the possession of the monastery by the gift of Lord Udo in the year of the Lord 1127, as was written above under that same year; and with it came the court of the scabini which we have there, with all the rights and liberties contained in its same description. This estate, however, with everything belonging to it, has from the beginning always been, and still is today, wholly free from every exaction, impost, and service. For greater security, Walram, Count of Sponheim, son of Lord Simon, afterward freed it, together with all the other goods which we have in Bockenau, from every burden, as we shall say below under the year of the Lord 1359.

1344. In the same year died Reynard [unclear], duke of Guelders. His son Reynard, a boy of ten years, succeeded him in the duchy; he afterward took as wife Mary, daughter of John, duke of Brabant, and both died without children. But Edward, brother of the aforesaid Reynard, after some years, led by desire of ruling, seized his brother and held him captive for ten years.

In this year Lord William, abbot of this monastery, bought the house and estate which we have in Bingen, with its appurtenances, from a certain Wentzo called [Kipdeck?]; and [unclear], surrounding [Kipdeck?] and Grenzau together in a siege, captured them and powerfully added them, with certain other things, to the possessions of the church of Trier.

In the same year also, the emperor Louis cast John, son of the king of Bohemia, out of the duchy of Carinthia, which he had previously given him; and his wife, taken away without the judgment of the Church, he gave as wife to his own son, to the grave scandal of the Church. Therefore, both for this and for other crimes, he was again excommunicated by Pope Clement VI and finally deposed from the empire, and Charles, king of Bohemia, was elected against him.

1345. In the fourth year of our abbot William, William, count of Hainaut, Zeeland, and Holland, and lord of Frisia, around the feast of the Nativity of St. Mary the Virgin led a great army against the Frisians, having with him the count of Horn and many noble and very brave men. The Frisians came out against him and, using their customary skill, killed some of those shut in the marshes and drowned others; among them Count William himself was also drowned, leaving no children. After his death his sister, the wife of the emperor Louis, coming down with her son William and a most splendid retinue, as the nearer heir, by decree of the emperor and princes, received into her power all the county of Holland, Hainaut, and Zeeland, and the lordship of Frisia, just as above under the year of the Lord 1323 we have already written
