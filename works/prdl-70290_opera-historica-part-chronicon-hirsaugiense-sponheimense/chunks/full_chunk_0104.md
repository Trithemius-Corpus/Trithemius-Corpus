*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

To the monastery also of Saint Ferrucius in Blidenstad, in the diocese of Mainz, which about three years before this had been transferred by the order, Henry, a monk of this monastery, was sent and was ordained legitimate abbot.

Rudolph also, a monk of Hirsau, having been ordained abbot for the monastery of Gotifau in the diocese of Speyer, by his prudence and by the zealous care of his observance brought back the depraved morals of the monks to the standard of regular observance. Megingus, a monk of this monastery, on account of his diligence and praiseworthy way of life was first appointed prior in Richenbach, and afterwards, ordained abbot of the monastery of Weingarten in the diocese of Constance, presided not idly over the flock entrusted to him.

But to the monastery of Gotifau in the diocese of Speyer, after the death of Abbot Rudolph, at the petitions of the brothers of that place, Conrad, a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, was sent and legitimately ordained abbot.

Another Conrad also, of the same name, a monk of this monastery, was ordained abbot of the monastery of Alpersbach in the diocese of Constance: a man very diligent and experienced in conducting worldly affairs, and not ignorant of divine institutions, who vigorously presided over that monastery and took care to show himself in all things a useful pastor, both by word and by example.

To the monastery in Suerzach beside the Rhine, in the diocese of Strasbourg, Hildebert, a monk of this monastery, was given as abbot; adorned in no low degree with morals and learning, he was held worthy of such an honor.

But Marquard, a monk of this monastery, himself also a man of many virtues and a zealous lover of the divine scriptures, was sent to the monastery in Deckingen in the diocese of Augsburg at the request of the monks staying there, and was duly ordained abbot for it.

To the monastery also in Alpersbach, to me, in place of an abbot of venerable memory, [unclear] of this monastery of Hirsau, named Berthold, who for some time was prior of the cell of Saint Gregory in Richenbach, a man worthy of every honor.

But to the monastery in Otrenburen in the diocese of Augsburg, William was directed as abbot: first a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, and then provost in Roth, of the same diocese of Augsburg, from there called to the abbacy by the bishops and monks of the aforesaid monastery.

To the monastery in Schuttera in the diocese of Strasbourg, of whose foundation we made mention much earlier above, John, surnamed Wedel, a monk of this monastery, was given as abbot. He first existed for some years as claustral prior in this place; then he was made prior in Richenbach; and finally, under Abbot Blasius, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1492, indiction 10, when John Phul, abbot in Schuttera, had died, who first took up our reformation commonly called Bursfeld, he was ordained abbot in his place: a man mild in disposition, yet not lax; sufficiently diligent in handling temporal cares; irreproachable before all who knew him in zeal for regular discipline and in making his life honorable. Since he is still considered to be present in life, we are admonished by religious propriety to refrain from his praises. But now, with the names of those who, from this monastery, from the time of Abbot William down to the present year of the Lord’s nativity 1501, in which we have written these things, Maximilian ruling the Romans, were appointed abbots for various monasteries, having been duly marked in order as far as they have come to our knowledge, although through the negligence of the ancients the names of many have been omitted, we must at last return to the order of the history undertaken.

Therefore in the fifteenth year of Abbot William, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1084, King Henry, the fourth of this name, with Bertha his wife, on the holy day of Easter at Rome received the imperial blessing from the antipope Clement. Afterwards, departing from there and crossing Italy, he besieged and took Augsburg, the city of the Raetians, which had been invaded by the Alemanni.
