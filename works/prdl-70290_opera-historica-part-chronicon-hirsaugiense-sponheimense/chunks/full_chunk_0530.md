*Chronicle of Trithemius*

*The king of France obtains Burgundy.*

*The war of Maximilian, Duke of Austria, against the people of Guelders.*

... of Burgundy, and the County, with many towns and cities of Artois and Picardy, which by right had devolved upon the Duke of Austria together with his wife. In the same year also the aforesaid Maximilian, Duke of Austria, with the help of the Duke of Cleves, entered the Gelders territory with an army, as legitimate heir after the death of Duke Charles his father-in-law. When the inhabitants of the land refused to admit him, hoping that they would receive back their own lord, a captive of the king of France, he besieged Nijmegen; but he made little progress. For, after making a sortie, those who were in the town put his army to flight, carrying off the war engines and no small amount of equipment.

*The twenty-third provincial chapter is celebrated.*

In the year noted above as well, on the second Sunday after the feast of Easter, the twenty-third provincial chapter of our order was held in the monastery of St. Giles at Nuremberg. Presiding there were Martin in Schwartzach, Leonard in Kastl, Bernhard in Hirsau, and Hermann of St. James near Mainz, abbots of our order; many statutes for the reform of the order were made there.

*Louis, Duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Rich, dies.*

*He left a son and a daughter.*

In the same year Louis, Duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Rich, died, a strong and vigorous man, who waged many wars bravely against Albert, Margrave of Brandenburg, indeed, when provoked by Emperor Frederick, against many cities of the Empire, and always came out victorious. He left an only son, Duke George, who had as wife Hedwig, daughter of the king of Poland, and who died in the nineteenth year of John Trithemius as abbot; [and] an only daughter named Margaret, who had married Philip, Count Palatine, a most illustrious prince, of whom we shall speak in her proper place.

*1480.*

*Philip, the second-born Palatine, is born.*

In this year, which was the eleventh of our abbot John Kolnhausen, on the seventh day of the month of May, there was born the second-born son of our most illustrious prince Philip, Count Palatine, Duke of Bavaria and prince elector. He received his father’s name in baptism and was called Philip, and later was made bishop of Freising.

*Rupert, Archbishop of Cologne, died.*

In the same year, on the eighth day before the Kalends of August, Rupert, Archbishop of Cologne, died in his wretched captivity in the castle of Blankenstein, as was said, under the pope’s excommunication. His corpse was carried to Bonn and, after some months, was buried in the church of St. Cassius. O fortune, O envy, O the times and morals: what does ambition not presume?

*Hermann succeeds.*

After his death, Hermann, son of the Landgrave of Hesse, governor of the chapter and church of Cologne, was unanimously elected on the third day before the Ides of August, and now is in the twenty-second year of his pontificate: a good man, excellent, gentle, outstanding in counsel and action, who throughout the whole time of the siege of Neuss fought vigorously for the citizens.

*Sigismund, formerly Bishop of Würzburg, dies.*

In the same year too died Sigismund, Duke of Saxony, formerly Bishop of Würzburg. After his death Bishop Rudolf von Scherenberg was compelled to pay his brothers more than 33,000 florins on account of a delayed payment.

*The mother of Philip the Palatine dies.*

In the year noted above also died Margaret, daughter of the famous Amadeus, Duke of Savoy, mother of our most illustrious prince Philip the Palatine mentioned above. After the death of Duke Louis, her first husband, namely Philip’s father, she had married Ulrich, Count of Württemberg, leaving her little son in the guardianship, as has been said, of Frederick.

*A singular monastery in Kreuznach.*

Around these times the monastery of the Friars Minor of the Observance in Kreuznach was founded by Frederick, Count Palatine, Duke of Bavaria and Count in Sponheim, father of Duke John, who is residing today in Simmern, with the offering of the faithful assisting. For a certain citizen of the aforesaid town of Kreuznach placed a small image of St. Wolfgang, carved from wood, in the chapel or church of that same new foundation. In a short time so great a gathering of peoples began to come to it, and miracles to be proclaimed, that the whole work begun was very quickly completed from the offering of the people. Nor did the repute of miracles cease until the brothers, lovers of peace and poverty, removed the image; then the fame of signs ceased, and the gathering of peoples was gradually reduced.

*1481.*

*The monastery of Limburg is reformed.*

In the twelfth year of our abbot John, the monastery of Limburg of our order, in the diocese of Speyer, was reformed by Hermann, abbot of St. James near Mainz, [and by] our aforesaid abbot Kolnhausen. There Boniface, a monk, was ordained as abbot
