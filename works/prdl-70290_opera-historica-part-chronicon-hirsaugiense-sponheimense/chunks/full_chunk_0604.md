*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

... me, brother, and pray to God that his most holy will may be fulfilled in us. From Neumagen, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of June, in the year of the Christians one thousand five hundred and five.

*XI.*

*To Lady Richmodis de Horst, abbess of the monastery of [Sebacensis?], a virgin dedicated to God, etc.*

It had been our intention, beloved daughter in Christ, together with Jacob, abbot of [Carsae Dei?], now nearer to us, to come to your convent and to fulfill the office of visitation entrusted to us. But while we were considering these things in our mind, behold, there came a messenger of the most excellent prince Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg, who by his letters summoned me to Bonn for the first day of the month of July. Therefore, since the shortness of time does not allow me to turn aside elsewhere, and since to comply with so great a prince is not only honorable but also necessary, we are compelled to change the plan that we had formed for the act of visitation.

Accordingly, since we are to go down by boat on the following day, although we think that it is not fitting to instruct by our letters your charity in the Lord, very venerable to us, how you ought usefully and fruitfully to preside over the virgins entrusted to you, since from childhood at Cologne, at St. Maccabees, in a monastery most excellently reformed to the purity of the rule of our divine father Benedict, you have fully learned both sacred letters and, in holy conduct, have perfectly attained regular observance; nevertheless paternal solicitude prompts us to admonish and exhort your gentleness, so that with good will you may more fully accomplish in deed and speech the right thing that you have learned.

For no one is found among cloistered persons so learned, so holy, so devoted to God, that, by the very fact that he desires to be good, he is not made better whenever another's exhortation of holy charity is applied to his own will. But of your love we cannot doubt that you will both gladly receive the words of our exhortation and keep them at all times to the best of your power. Therefore we exhort your devotion in the Lord Jesus Christ: so live always that it may have no cause to fear death. For perfect love of God alone has no cause for fearing death, inasmuch as it does not doubt that, after the dissolution of the prison of this present life, it will remain forever in him whom, while placed in the flesh, it loves with all its strength.

But the sluggish and lazy servant, knowing the will of his lord and not doing it, fears to come into his sight, whose commandments he recognizes that he has despised. He, however, who has always loved his lord with his whole heart and has been eager to carry out his will in all things, not only does not fear to appear before him, but rather longs to come into his sight, by whom he knows he is to be rewarded. Our Lord Jesus Christ himself set forth clearly for us a likeness of this matter in the holy Gospel, when he commended the faithful usurers for the profit they had brought.

You too, most beloved daughter in Christ, having received the money of our Lord and Savior, have received from him the gift of understanding, by which you ought to bring back a great profit of holy love. We beg you to take the most diligent care not to hide this precious gift of God in the earth of carnal passions, nor to indulge the flesh more than is fitting, nor, having been set over sacred virgins, to grow numb in idleness. Remember how the precious benefits of a very short time have been granted to us, and how many good things it is permitted us, by living well and rightly in the very few days of this life, to send on ahead.

Be an example of purity to the sisters entrusted to you, a standard of a more sacred life, mindful of the saying of our lawgiver Benedict: Whatever the abbess has told her nuns to be contrary, let her show in her own deeds that it must not be done. Then indeed, O daughter, you will rightly carry out the office of Christ in the monastery, when you precede the virgins subject to you in all purity of mind and body, in word and conduct. First of all, therefore, consider yourself diligently, so that, cleansed of all faults and adorned with good and holy virtues, you may please God through the continual fervent longing of love; and then, with whatever earnestness you can, diligently exhort those entrusted to your rule to think and will the same thing in the Lord Jesus.

With all care and solicitude keep watch over the souls entrusted to you, lest any perish through the snares of the malignant tempter, who always prowls about roaring like a lion, and gnashing his teeth from malice, seeking some virgin dedicated to God whom he may devour. Remember, daughter, how you carry the precious treasure of virginity in earthen vessels; and always keep watch lest the malignant enemy be able to penetrate the house of your heart, lest he steal away this treasure of yours, venerable to the holy angels of God.

Close your hearts, virgins dedicated to God, since they are the earthen vessels in which you carry the incomparable treasure; they are the caskets in which you contain the ointment of angelic composition, and you must keep the highest watch lest it flow out.
