In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1077, around the eighteenth day before the Kalends of October, a very great conference was held at Oppenheim, where the princes of almost the whole kingdom were renouncing subjection to the king, giving as their reason that, when summoned to Rome by two apostolic envoys to make satisfaction, he had not come, and for this contempt had, while absent and contumacious, been excommunicated by the pope in a Roman synod. At this council of the malicious, Abbot William was again [unclear] by the pope, and Rudolf, duke of Swabia, by command of the supreme pontiff, was elected king against him by the princes.

Whence, compelled by necessity, he came to Rome and approached the pope barefoot and clothed in wool next to the flesh; and, prostrate on the ground before him, he humbly sought pardon, promised amendment, and obtained absolution. But he did not remain long in his purpose; indeed, he began to rage more cruelly against the Church than he had raged before. At length, when he had been excommunicated, he disdained to go when summoned by this community of Hirsau, since he knew the vicar of Christ to be holy and innocent, and was in excellent agreement with the holy Roman Church.
