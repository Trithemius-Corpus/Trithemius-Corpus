*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

... made profession. When he had learned the monastic discipline according to our most holy father Benedict, he strove to conform his conduct wholly to it, and, living among the brothers without complaint even in the lowest rank, he showed everyone an example of humility. After he had been ordained abbot, he took every care to show himself a shepherd worthy of his sheep, and to teach no less by example than by word.

As for the disposition of his body, he was a robust and strong man, handsomely proportioned in stature, somewhat exceeding middle height, with a sound and not small head, surrounded all about with much hair tending toward black. He had a resonant and trumpet-like voice, by which alone he was accustomed to strike terror into the wicked. In speaking he was sufficiently eloquent, whether the discourse had to be delivered in Latin or in German. He was of a very gentle nature, and was not easily moved to anger; but when he was angry with reason, he did not quickly let the matter pass into oblivion. In all things generous, he was of such benevolence toward his subjects, and of such loyalty, charity, and affection toward his friends, that he had no equal in his time.

Nevertheless he was marked by certain stains of negligence and tepidity in those matters that seemed to pertain to the complete observance of regular discipline, chiefly for this reason: that he had many continual occupations, labors, and cares in household affairs, and that at the beginning of his promotion he necessarily endured dangers, injuries, and innumerable tribulations, both from the advocate of the monastery, Count Adelbert, and from various others whom it was necessary to resist.

For the aforesaid Count Adelbert, advocate of this monastery, forgetful of the duty of his office, undertook, contrary to the liberty and privilege formerly granted by the supreme pontiffs and emperors, to burden, harass, and disturb the household and servants of the monastery. *A certain tyrannical advocate.* For, at the devil’s instigation, he strove so greatly to violate and infringe the rights and liberties of the monastery, which he ought to have defended, that one day he seized the chamberlain of the monastery before the gate, and, with his hands bound behind his back, [unclear] the innocent [unclear], while the abbot protested.
