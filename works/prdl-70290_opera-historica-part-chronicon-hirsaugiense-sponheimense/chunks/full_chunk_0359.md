Whoever presumes to do this, know that, besides our indignation, to which he shall know himself subject, he will pay, in punishment of his rashness, one hundred pounds of pure gold, of which one half shall be paid to our court and the rest to the party that has suffered injury.

Therefore, for the stable memory and firmness of this gift and grant of ours, we have caused the present privilege to be made, impressed below with the golden seal of our majesty [unclear]. The witnesses of this matter are B., patriarch of Aquileia, and others. The sign of the lord Frederick II, by the grace of God most unconquered emperor of the Romans, ever august, king of Jerusalem and Sicily. I, Sifridus, bishop of Regensburg, chancellor of the imperial court, in place of the aforesaid Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, archchancellor in Germany, have reviewed it. These things were enacted in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1232, in the month of April, in the fifth indiction, while our lord Frederick II, by the grace of God most unconquered emperor of the Romans, ever august, most glorious king of Jerusalem and Sicily, was ruling, in the twelfth year of his empire, the seventh of his kingdom of Jerusalem, and the thirty-fourth of his kingdom of Sicily, happily. Amen. Given at Aquileia.

This donation or alienation of the principality from the monastery of Lorsch, then still of our order, made by the emperor Frederick, King Henry his son confirmed thus:

Henry, king of the Romans, by the grace of God ever august, to all the faithful of the empire who shall see this page, his grace and every good. It redounds to royal munificence unto glory if [unclear] with worthy honors, if with increased augmentations of property [unclear] the attendants at its side and those privy to its secrets. For from this devotion springs up in subjects, and the number is increased among the devoted, while munificence multiplies by example whatever it could have [unclear] through grace. Hence it is that we wish it to be known to all our faithful, present as well as future, that, when our beloved Sifridus, archbishop of Mainz, appeared in our presence, he earnestly petitioned our highness that the gift and grant of the principality of the church of Lorsch, with every honor, vassals, ministerials, castles, towns, [unclear], made to him and to the church of Mainz by our lord emperor Frederick, ever august, king of Jerusalem and Sicily, with us present and consenting, we ought to hold and preserve as renewed inviolably. We therefore, considering the gracious services which the said church of Mainz is known to have rendered to us and to our forebears, and also considering that the services owed to the Empire, which through the weakness of the aforesaid monastery have hitherto been withheld from it, can suitably be rendered by the said archbishop and his successors, holding the aforesaid grant and gift ratified as made with our assent, have decreed by the edict of the present royal page that no person, high or low, ecclesiastical or secular, shall presume with rash daring to oppose it. But if anyone attempts this, besides the indignation which he thereby incurs, let him know that he will pay fifty pounds of gold, of which we wish one half to be assigned to our court and the other to the party that has suffered injury. The witnesses of this confirmation of ours are B., patriarch of Aquileia. Given at [Chindacum?], in the year of the Lord 1232, on the fifth day before the Kalends of May, in the fifth indiction.

When this imperial gift, together with the royal confirmation, of the principality of the aforesaid monastery of Lorsch had been received, Sifridus, archbishop of Mainz, in order to establish that same principality for himself and his successors with perpetual firmness, induced the monks of that monastery by crafty persuasion, making great promises, to direct letters to the supreme pontiff Gregory IX and to request that their monastery be united with the church of Mainz. Therefore the monks, having no suspicion of their own expulsion, and firmly hoping that they, together with their monastery, would very soon be restored by the archbishop to their former state, wrote thus:
