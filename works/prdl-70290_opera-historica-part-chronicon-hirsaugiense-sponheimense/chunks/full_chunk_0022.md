*871.*
In the eighteenth year of Abbot Gerung, after the deposition of Archbishop Thietgaud, Bertholf, abbot of [Mediolanum?] of our order, became archbishop of Trier, a man keen in intellect, who did not allow the privileges of his church to be violated even in the least; in his time the people of Trier, from fear of the Northmen, hid all the ornaments, holy things, and relics in underground chambers.

*882.*
In the nineteenth year of Abbot Gerung, Saint Edmund, king of the English, martyred by the Northmen, was renowned for countless miracles.

*883.*
In his twenty-first year, which was the year of the Lord 874, in the Italian region, in the city of Brescia, blood rained from heaven for three days, and the Northmen did many evils to the Christians.

In these times Walo flourished, after Christianus, a monk and himself [from a monk?], bishop of Auxerre; brother of Ansegisus, archbishop of Sens, of whom we spoke at length above, in the first year of Abbot Lintbert. This Walo was a man distinguished in learning and conduct. His greatest zeal was to embrace teachers and professors of letters charitably, to use their counsels, to enjoy their conversations, and rather often to refresh them at his own table. In all his conduct he always wished to have certain men of the clergy with him, lest even a slight occasion be given to laymen for [unclear], so that no trace of habitation remained at all.

About these times flourished Eric, a monk of Auxerre, [chaplain?] of Bishop Walo, [unclear] in prose, concerning which we have made mention in the Catalogue of Writers.

*876.*
In the twenty-third year of Abbot Gerung, in the month of June, a sudden and excessive inundation of waters occurred, so that a certain village in Saxony, far removed from torrents and rivers, was in a moment, with men and beasts, with trees and buildings, with church and altar, wholly swallowed up, so that no trace of habitation remained at all.
