In the same year Mehmed, emperor of the Turks, in the third year of his reign, took that most famous city of the Greeks, Constantinople, on the fourth day before the Kalends of June, assaulting it by land and sea with the greatest force of arms, on the fiftieth day of the siege; and he killed all persons of both sexes who had passed the sixth year of age. He beheaded the emperor Constantine Palaeologus, and cruelly slew the monks after inflicting many tortures on them. There countless thousands of people were killed, so that the whole city overflowed with blood as if with a kind of river. The Turks also killed virgin nuns and beautiful women with pitiable torment, after perpetrating upon them an unheard-of fury of lust. Nevertheless the patriarch of the Greeks, fleeing with many Christians, was saved. At that time that unspeakable emperor of the Turks, having obtained the royal city, profaned all the churches; he made the church of St Sophia, most famous in all the world, a stable for horses, and ordered all the relics of the saints that he found to be thrown into the mud and trampled by the horses of his soldiers. But when the soldiers refused to inflict such an insult on the bones of the dead, he himself first, with his horse, leapt more savagely upon the relics of the saints, and, turning angrily to the soldiers, said: “Whoever has not followed me, by Muhammad, the great messenger of God, shall die this day.” Terrified by this, the soldiers, by the running and trampling of their horses, sank the relics of the saints, scattered over the mud, into the depth of the mud, an impious deed. The most cruel enemy of God also caused the sacred books of the orthodox fathers of the Christian faith, as many as he found, to be placed on perforated ships and sunk into the depth of the sea, so that no occasion for knowledge of the Christian faith might be left there. And after Constantinople was taken, the cruel enemy consequently obtained rule over the whole empire of the Greeks, while our emperor and princes did nothing against it except words.

In these times also the Prussians, having formed a conspiracy against the Master of the Teutonic Knights, stirred up a revolt of fifty-five cities and towns against him; the greater part of these not long afterward defected from the Teutons themselves and subjected themselves to the king of Poland. A very grave war then arose among them; with the forces of the Teutons [defeated?], the town of Danzig also defected to the Poles. And so the defeated Master, who once could be compared with kings, with his Teutons, having almost lost his dominion, was compelled to yield and be subject to the [unclear]. This is said to have happened to him because his men acted too much according to their own pleasure in tyrannizing over the citizens.

*1454.*

In the ninth year of Conrad our abbot, the fifteenth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated in the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter at Seligenstadt, on the twelfth day of the month of May, with continuation on the following days; it was the third Sunday after the feast of the Lord’s Resurrection. Wolfram at Hirsau, Christianus of St Peter’s at Erfurt, Ulrich at Wiblingen, and Berthold of St Stephen’s at Würzburg, abbots of very famous monasteries of the order, presided there; many statutes for the reform of the order were made there.

In these times Dracolus Wayda, a most cruel heathen prince or captain, exercised unheard-of and incredible tyranny in Wallachia; he killed many thousands of innocent people of both sexes and every age, especially Christians, by a new kind of torture never before heard of. At last he was captured by Matthias, king of Hungary, and, converted to the faith and baptized, was restored to his dominion after some years; thereafter he conducted himself well and most modestly, and did many good things. His cruel deeds are circulated in part in a single little book printed in the vernacular tongue.

In the year noted above also Mehmed, emperor of the Turks, obtained Lower Moesia and Wallachia, the princes surrendering because they had no help.

In these times also a monastery was founded in the diocese of Trier, not far from Bernkastel-Cues, of regular canons of the Windesheim reform, which is called Eberhardsklaus, from the offering of the faithful. For a certain poor peasant, born from a village situated on the bank of the Moselle, which is called Piesport, made a small hut there, in which he placed an image of the blessed Mary ever virgin holding her Son in her lap at the hour of vespers. Many miracles were reported concerning it, and from that time down to the present day there has been a concourse of peoples to that very image; from their alms the monastery was built there and sufficiently provided with necessities.
