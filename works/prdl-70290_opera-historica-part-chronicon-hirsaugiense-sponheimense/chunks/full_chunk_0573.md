*Of Sponheim.*

...would be willing to accept what was offered. Philip, Count Palatine and Duke of Bavaria, the illustrious prince and elector of the empire, promised most generously to provide Abbot Trithemius with all necessities, so long as both should live, or either of them.

Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, elector of the empire, of whom we spoke above, embraces Trithemius with such longing down to the present day that he seems to desire nothing more than to have him with him. This is now witnessed by many frequent letters written in his own hand, both to the Bishop of Würzburg, asking for his benevolent consent, and to Trithemius himself, asking him, of his good will, to agree to come. The substance of that prince’s request is this: since Trithemius will not consent to remain with him outside the monastery permanently, he should not refuse to be with him for a short time. These things are true, proved not only by the very frequent letters of the aforementioned man, but often also shown by splendid gifts and presents. For the Margrave himself honored Trithemius with many splendid gifts, and to this very day has not ceased to heap frequent benefactions upon him. For from the time when he departed from us, it is agreed that he has been honored by him to the value of at least five hundred gold florins; nor meanwhile has there been a year, six of which have now passed, in which the often-mentioned prince did not honor Trithemius either with a gold or silver cup, or with some other gift. Moreover, at his own expense he sent him salted fish, pike, and others, with many vessels, to Würzburg.

Finally Hermann, Archbishop of Cologne, promised that his assistance would be available to Trithemius in every need, and several times loaded him with money and silver cups. The Abbot of Fulda also, hearing that Trithemius had departed from us and did not wish to return, had him solicited through Hartmann, Count of Kirchberg, to turn aside to Fulda, promising him a provostship for the support of the rest of his life. With similar generosity many other powerful and wealthy men in the world followed after Trithemius, who promised that they would have him with them and provide for him most abundantly; nor even today are there lacking those who would receive him with honor and provide for him on honorable terms, if he agreed to turn aside to them.

But Johannes Trithemius himself, having from the beginning been laudably received and instructed in the observance of monastic discipline in the present monastery of Sponheim, when he was about twenty-three years old, did not consider it sound or safe for himself to associate in the courts of princes, nor honorable to withdraw from the holy union of observance. Therefore almighty God, having pity on his desolation and unwilling to abandon the afflicted man at the end, looked upon him mercifully and provided, in observance, a place suited to his studies and way of life.

For Conrad, the aforementioned Abbot of St. Stephen at Würzburg, whom we said had been sent from the chapter at Heidelberg to Trithemius with another, after he heard the many reasonable causes by which he was moved and compelled to yield, since he was his friend, had no small compassion for him. Hence he promised that he would give his effort so that Trithemius, elected canonically, might obtain the abbey of St. James at Würzburg with good peace and a pure entrance. At that time the abbot of the said monastery was a certain Cilianus of Ochsenfurt, who had resolved to resign that abbey for certain causes. When he had heard that it was possible for Trithemius to succeed in his place, he rejoiced greatly, saying that he would yield to no one in the world more gladly than to so great a man, and asked that the desired matter take effect as quickly as possible and that no obstacle intervene.

And so Conrad, Abbot of St. Stephen, brought the business to the most reverend Bishop of Würzburg and magnificent Duke of Eastern Franconia, Lorenz, asserting that Trithemius could be induced to give up the abbey of Sponheim and take up that of St. James, if this indeed were pleasing to his paternity. The most reverend prelate, who had previously heard many proclamations of praise concerning Abbot Trithemius, and had seen him before at Heidelberg at the table of the Prince Palatine, had addressed him graciously, and long before would freely have advanced him to the abbey in Schwartach, if, when twice summoned, he had appeared after the death of the abbots Michael and likewise Paul, answered that it not only pleased him but that he especially desired to be able to have Trithemius as abbot in his diocese.

Therefore, once the bishop’s will was heard, a messenger was sent as quickly as possible to Heidelberg, to Trithemius at Würzburg. Trithemius secretly set the matter before the prince, lord Philip, Count Palatine, and with his consent went up to the place to which he was called. And so on the third day of the month of October he came to Würzburg. After the voluntary resignation of Abbot Cilianus, on the twenty-second day of the aforesaid month, which was Monday after the feast of St. Dionysius the martyr, after a commitment made by the brothers into the hands of the lord bishop and two abbots, he was elected and appointed abbot of the often-mentioned monastery; on the fourth day of the same month after the tenth he was confirmed by the same most reverend prelate, and immediately on the next day, which was solemn on the feast of St. Burkhard, he was introduced into real possession of the abbey of St. James.
