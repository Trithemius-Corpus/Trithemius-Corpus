*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In the same year a certain stonemason, who had for some time worked on the construction of the tower of St. Stephen at Mainz, stole the head of most holy Anne, the mother of Mary ever virgin, which was kept in that church enclosed in a little silver vessel, together with the silver itself; and, departing secretly to the town of Düren, in the duchy of Jülich and the diocese of Cologne, he carried off the sacred head, which was received with honor by the citizens. Thus the holy head was held in great honor by the people of Düren; and what at Mainz lay almost wholly unknown is today visited at Düren with the greatest concourse of people from our own and from other more distant provinces, with immense honor, heaped almost beyond measure with prayers and offerings. They say, moreover, that at the invocation of holy mother Anne signs and miracles have occurred there; but since nothing is established for us concerning these, we do not wish to report what is uncertain. The canons of the aforesaid church, making efforts and expenses for the restoration of the most holy head that had been taken away, summoned the townsmen of Düren before the royal hearing, but they accomplished nothing, being deprived of the holy pledge down to the present day. For they cannot by any argument be induced to restore the holy head. What the people of Düren will do hereafter, however, we cannot know at this time.

In the year noted above, Johannes, archbishop of Trier, burdened by old age, took as coadjutor Jacob, son of Christoph, margrave of Baden, his nephew through his brother Karl, a man excellent in talent and character; Pope Alexander VI confirmed him in his assumption, with provision for succession. This act has not pleased four of the canons of the major church, namely the dean and three others, down to the present day. Hence great dissension is feared in the church of Trier after the archbishop’s death, unless God should be willing to forestall it.

In this year the greatest concourse of peoples from all Europe was made to the Roman curia for the indulgences of the jubilee year, and much money was brought to Rome. Men and women, widows and virgins, young and old, monks and nuns, ran together, mingled and confused, and the matter was [unclear]. Many, consumed by hunger and plague, perished on the journey; some also were killed, and some, sold, were brought to the utmost dangers. There is a certain convent of nuns of our order, built in the diocese of Speyer near Limburg, committed by papal authority to Johannes Trithemius, abbot, whose abbess, without permission either of the bishop or of the abbot, set out for Rome for the indulgences of the jubilee and died on the return, and left her place after her [unclear] desolate. To her in the abbacy, by canonical election, succeeded Richmodis de Horst, a noble and devout virgin, sufficiently learned in the Latin language, a nun of the convent of the Maccabees in Cologne, a lover of regular discipline and its most upright guardian, who, among other monuments of her talent, composed an entire history of St. Anne with melody.

Also in the same year, on the [unclear] day of the month of July, Bayezid, emperor of the Turks, in his own person and with an army of thousands of armed men, besieged Modon, a great and most beautiful city of the Venetians; and after many labors and assaults, on the ninth day of the month of August, he violently captured it, cruelly killed many thousands of Christians, and led the rest away into most shameful servitude.

Also in the year noted above, the inhabitants of our village of Auen, when required by Johannes Trithemius our abbot to render him the homage of fidelity, quite insolently refused to obey, alleging prescription, because within memory they had rendered homage to none of the abbots. But when the abbot referred the matter to the most unconquered Prince Palatine, the peasants, fearing dangers and losses, rendered and displayed homage, and not undeservedly. For since they have always recognized the abbot of this monastery, whoever he may have been at the time, as their lord, and no other, they ought deservedly to swear to no one else.
