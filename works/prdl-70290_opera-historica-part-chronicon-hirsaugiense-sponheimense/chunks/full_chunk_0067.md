These things, to be carried out by you as quickly as possible, together with your wife, beloved to us, and to be completed, we earnestly enjoin upon you by the authority of almighty God, for the remission of your sins. But if, which may it not be, you neglect this and are found a despiser of our commands, know that you have incurred the indignation of almighty God and of Saints Peter and Paul the apostles and of all Christ’s elect, and that, bound by the bond of anathema by apostolic authority, you will be subject to the judgment of the divine examination, to be punished with the most grievous torments of Gehenna without end among the impious. Compuncted by this speech of the pontiff, the count fell at his knees, and, publicly confessing with tears that he had sinned gravely, promised that he would do, to the best of his strength, everything that he was ordered. Asking pardon for his offenses and absolution, he heard from the pontiff: “If you carry out those things which, at our command, you promise that you will do, we shall grant you not only pardon for your offenses with absolution, but we also promise you again the manifold rewards of eternal blessedness laid up for you in heaven.”

When these things had duly been done, the most blessed Pope Leo, continuing the journey he had begun to Mainz, at last arrived there with his people, where, in the presence of the emperor, he celebrated a synod with forty-two bishops and many abbots and clerics, and enacted many things for ecclesiastical utility. In this synod Sibicho, bishop of Speyer, cleared himself by holy communion of the crimes of which he was accused. From there the most holy pope, after the synod had been completed, went down to Aachen; there he waited for the emperor, who was conducting an expedition against Baldwin, count of Flanders, until he should return. This most holy Pope Leo IX did many good things in Germany, and, after churches, chapels, and other ecclesiastical matters had been moderately arranged, returned to Rome.
