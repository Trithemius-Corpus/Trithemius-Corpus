*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

*XX.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James at Wurzburg, to George [unclear], prior of the Friars Preachers in Berlin and vicar general throughout the March, his dearest friend, greetings.*

[unclear] [Mindful of your merits?], venerable father, I cannot but favor you as well as I can, and be concerned for your welfare as a supporter. And therefore I wish to know from you what you are doing and how you are faring, although I know very well that you have felt the envy of your brothers, who, as my judgment stands, are in no way worthy of you. But these, if I may so speak, are vices inborn in many cloistered men: to resist holy and excellent prelates, and to expose them to various murmurings and detractions. *Slanderous monks.* When troubled, all come to the prelate for consolation; but if it should happen that the ruler himself is troubled by some occasion, which of the brothers will console him?

Having experienced these disturbances in the abbey of Sponheim for twenty-three years, I long ago wrote a book *On the Misery of Cloistered Prelates*. Although not all suffer these things equally, nevertheless few are found altogether immune from them. For many are the miseries and tribulations of cloistered rulers, which I could enumerate the more certainly, the more numerous and bitter I have always experienced them to be. This one thing alone consoled me: that I knew from the rule of my institution that I had undertaken the care of weak souls. For that reason, for the honor and reverence of the supreme shepherd, I endured all things patiently as far as I could, hoping that I would receive my reward from him who did not refuse to die for our infirmities.

In all these things one thing was still lacking in my tribulations, which, apart from death, was the last of miseries. And when, with the envy of rivals raging against me, I at last began to experience that, and had seen the hardened hearts of the wicked, I resolved to give place to wrath; I refused any longer to serve ungrateful cloistered men. For you know the causes of my disturbances; therefore I shall be briefer. Returning from the March to my homeland, I found certain of my monks not thinking rightly toward me. Provoked by their ingratitude and perfidy, I went to Wurzburg when summoned, and accepted the monastery of St. James in the suburb to govern. But I resigned the abbey of Sponheim because of the wickedness of the monks and the envy of rivals, preferring to live with few monks in peace rather than with many in continual disturbance.

I wished briefly to recount to you the history of my tribulations, so that in your adversities you may know that I am a sharer, sympathizing with you from my heart. I have written a word in your defense to the most serene prince; what sort of word it is, the little sheet enclosed with these letters will report. I should like you to write back to me what is being done with you, and not allow this messenger of mine to depart without your letters. Please greet in my name all our friends, whoever they may be, those who remember me for good. And you, excellent father, take care to be well, praying to God for us. From our monastery, on the tenth day of the month of April, in the Christian year 1507.

*XXI.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St. James in the suburb of Wurzburg, to Johannes de Segen, abbot of the Mount of St. Peter at Erfurt, greetings.*

[unclear] Reverend father and friend, since I was about to send this servant of mine on certain business to the most serene prince Joachim in the March of Brandenburg, I could not, and certainly ought not, let him go without my letter to you, since he must necessarily pass through Erfurt. First of all, I give immortal thanks to your paternity for your kindness toward me, and I shall repay it if ever I can. I have not found such charity in the whole Bursfelde Union, except in Antonius, abbot of St. Matthias of Trier, who seemed to vie with you in benefactions toward me. Hence I would deservedly place you two foremost among my friends, thinking without forgetfulness of your benefits toward me.

I resigned my abbey of Sponheim of my own free will, because of the ingratitude of the brothers and the envy of rivals, at last seeing and openly recognizing the hardened heart of Pharaoh, who gave credence to falsehood rather than to truth. But I came to Wurzburg, called through the abbot of St. Stephen by command of the most reverend prelate, and, canonically installed on the fifteenth day of the month of October, poor myself, I took up the poor abbey of St. James the Apostle, formerly of the Scots; in it I trust that henceforth I shall live with greater peace and tranquility than I have been able to dwell in at Sponheim up to this time.
