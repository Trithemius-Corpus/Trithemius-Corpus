*Chronicle of Jo. Trithemius.*

...observed laudably down to us by their successors without any legitimate reproach from anyone, you dare, by a certain new superstition, to change it, forbidding men the use of those things which none of the saints is ever read to have forbidden. For you ought not to think yourself greater than your holy lawgiver Benedict, who in the Rule which you too have professed, though he forbade monks the eating of meat, did not forbid fish, eggs, dairy foods, or wine. By what rashness, then, do you presume to forbid monks those things which the holy man lawfully permitted?

To this the man of God Rupert replied humbly and fearlessly: "By the authority and leave of almighty God and of the whole heavenly court, I have forbidden gluttony and delicacies, as seemed right to me, to my abbots, who of their own accord follow my purpose for the love of God, knowing that abstinence is pleasing to God and especially adorns the monastic institute. And you, lord bishop, who think that I have presumed this by some new superstition, what great injury you do to me and to our holy fathers of God, who once in the desert and likewise in monasteries, for love of the heavenly kingdom, mortified themselves with greater abstinence than we do, abstaining even from the vegetables which we permit, content with bread and water and a little salt, all who are even moderately wise now understand. If you are a bishop, if a good shepherd of souls, if a lover of God, you ought by all means not to forbid the voluntary frugality of your subjects, undertaken for the love of God, but rather to pursue it with fitting favor, and to encourage the sheep committed to you not toward easier things but rather toward difficult ones. Why do you blame us for that in which you, as shepherd, ought at all times to have gone before us as sheep? Abstinence, voluntarily undertaken by us at divine command for our sins, you [unclear], although it appears in many places in the sacred Scriptures commended as good. But if it is bad, why then is it commended in Daniel with his companions, who, scorning the royal foods, were fed on vegetables out of love for their paternal tradition? If, I say, that manner of abstaining is bad and blameworthy, why does holy mother the Catholic Church also commend it so greatly in the holy fathers Anthony, Paul, Macarius, Arsenius, Moses, Bambo, Onuphrius, and many others? Or do you entirely reject the life of Saint Martin, bishop of Tours, and of the holy monks in the monastery with him, who in their own time observed this manner of life and abstinence, which you reproach in us sinners, much more strictly than we do, or do you wish to forbid it? For if you should persist in wholly forbidding us our way of living, [unclear] to God rather [unclear] through many [unclear] wonderful proclamations of this abstinence were held.

Again the bishop said to the abbot: "We knowingly intend to ordain nothing against the divine will; but we cannot, nor ought we, quickly give credence to your unproved revelations, since Scripture says, 'He who believes quickly is light of heart.' And the holy apostle Paul says, 'Test the spirits, whether they are from God.' What sign, therefore, do you give, so that we ought to believe that these revelations of yours are not feigned, not false, but sent from God?"

When the abbot replied: "I am not a prophet, so that in your sight I might raise the dead or cause fire to come down from heaven. But in this matter I have been sent by God and his saints to announce to you [unclear] of the command of the highest majesty [unclear]. But if you do not believe, the Lord who sent me [unclear]. You have done well [unclear], and you have freed your soul. This will be a sign for you: [unclear] he will judge you; he will cut off your root from the land of the living, and you will live only a short time. Nevertheless I am prepared to undergo whatever judgment you decree, so that you may take proof of the things I have said."
