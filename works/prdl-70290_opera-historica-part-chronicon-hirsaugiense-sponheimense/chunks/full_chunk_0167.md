*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius*

[unclear], the younger, archbishop of Mainz, went to Rome, having been summoned by Pope Innocent, and was honorably received by him.

*Discord between the pope and the king.*

Since in these times King Conrad was attempting to invade the kingdom of Sicily against the will of Pope Innocent, the pontiff, by entreaties and money, dealt with the dukes of Bavaria, Henry and his brother Welf, that they should block his access into Italy. They undertook this the more willingly because they had previously regarded the king as hostile and hateful. And so, when King Conrad wished to prepare an expedition into Italy, while Duke Henry was still living, Duke Welf stoutly resisted.

*War between the king and the dukes of Bavaria.*

Thus a very severe battle took place between that Duke Welf of Bavaria on the side of Pope Innocent and Frederick, duke of the Swabians, on the king’s side, in the diocese of Augsburg, near Neresheim, a monastery of our order; and when many had been slain on both sides, Duke Frederick at last won the victory, and Welf fled with his men into a stronghold. Finally, after the death of Duke Henry, Duke Welf, mindful of the loss he had suffered, gathered a great army and renewed the war against Duke Frederick near the village called Ellenhofen, not far from Weinsberg. In this battle Duke Welf himself fell, and the greater part of his army was laid low.

*Weinsberg is taken.*

Thereupon, in this same year, King Conrad besieged Welf’s town, which is called Weinsberg; and, receiving it in surrender, he granted this liberty to the matrons and women: that whatever they could carry away on their shoulders should be permitted to them by royal munificence. They, neglecting their household goods, took up on their shoulders their husbands and men, as many as they could, and carried them out beyond the gates while all looked on and marveled at their resourcefulness. When the duke advised that such things not be allowed to happen, the king said that it was not fitting for the word of a ruler to be changed.

*The duchy of Bavaria is transferred to the Austrians.*

Moreover, the king conferred the duchy of Bavaria on Leopold, margrave of Austria; yet afterwards Emperor Frederick I restored it to the legitimate heirs.

*John de Temporibus died.*

In this same year died John, called de Temporibus, who had lived three hundred and sixty-one years from the time of Charles the Great, whose shield-bearer he had been; he was a man of a very temperate constitution.

*Hugh of Saint Victor of Paris died.*

In the same year died Hugh, canon at Saint Victor of Paris, a man second to none in his time in religion and in knowledge of letters, and in knowledge of the seven liberal arts. He wrote many excellent little works of his talent for the benefit of readers; of these we have made fuller mention in the book On Ecclesiastical Writers.

*A pseudo-emperor deceived many.*

In these times a certain pseudo-emperor arose in Germany. Living for some years in enclosure at Solothurn and then coming forth from there, he pretended that he was Emperor Henry. And when he was causing scandal to many, [unclear], when he was cited to it, he appealed to the apostolic see; and thus escaping, [unclear], by seducing, he had attracted people to himself to such an extent that serious battles and killings took place on his behalf, some receiving him as the deceased Emperor Henry, others openly crying out that he was a deceiver. Finally, when his falsehood had been made clear, he was shorn as a monk at Cluny.

*Synod against Peter Abelard.*

In the same year of the Lord, [unclear] 19, in the city of Sens in Gaul, a synod of bishops and abbots was held in the presence of Louis, king of the Franks, against Master Peter Abelard, who was scandalizing the Church by a certain profane novelty of words and meanings. *Epitaph of this Peter Abelard.*

Our Aristotle, among logicians, whoever they were,
known to the world of studies as either equal or better;
first in talent, varied, subtle, and keen,
surpassing all by the force of reason and the art of speaking:
Abelard was such. But then he conquered all the more,
when, professing the Cluniac monk and way of life,
he passed over to the true philosophy of Christ.
In this, completing well the final things of a long life,
[unclear] to be numbered among the good philosophers.
