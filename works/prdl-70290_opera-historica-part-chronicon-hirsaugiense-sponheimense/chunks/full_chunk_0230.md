*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

...a man of great virtues and deeds, who was of such steadfastness that, by order of the pope, he publicly declared Emperor Frederick excommunicated and pursued him as such.

In the same year Engelbert, archbishop of Cologne, was cruelly killed by Frederick, count of Isenburg, his brother’s son, with thirty-eight wounds. His body, buried in the greater church, soon began to become famous through countless miracles. But when the killer was, not long afterward, broken on the wheel before the walls, the miracles at once ceased. He was succeeded in the archbishopric by Henry of Molenarck, provost of Bonn, who presided for twelve years and destroyed the castle of Isenburg.

*Engelbert, archbishop of Cologne, is killed.*

In the same year also Henry, king of the Romans, son of Emperor Frederick II, took as wife the daughter of Leopold, duke of Austria; the wedding was celebrated at Nuremberg with great solemnity, where a capital sentence was given against the killer of the archbishop of Cologne.

*Henry the king takes a wife.*

*1226.*

Now in the tenth year of Abbot Eberhard, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1226, Saint Francis, first founder of the Order of Friars Minor, rested in the Lord on the fourth Nones of October, and two years later was enrolled in the catalogue of the saints by Pope Gregory IX.

*St. Francis dies.*

In these times Henry, bishop of Worms, raised from the foundations a convent of nuns of the Cistercian order at Kirsgarten, outside the city walls, installed nuns there, and provided them with necessities. But after 217 years Frederick, bishop of Worms, expelled the nuns because of the enormity of their life, and put in their place regular canons of Saint Augustine of the Windesheim observance; they inhabit that place itself, with profit, up to the present day. Between the aforesaid Bishop Henry and the citizens of Worms a serious dissension arose over ecclesiastical liberty. Since they disdained to obey his command, he placed the city under ecclesiastical interdict, and also ordered all the clergy, except the parish priests, to leave the city, strictly forbidding the parish priests to confer the sacraments on the sick unless they promised the pontiff that they would obey him in all things if they recovered. Nor were the bodies of the dead permitted to be buried in holy places. But when these evils had lasted for the space of a year, some of the citizens, affected by weariness of the troubles, began to incline to the bishop’s side. Seeing this, those who were in charge of the consulship, and fearing to experience worse things, accepted peace.

*Dissension between the bishop and his people of Worms.*

In the same year the Lombards, rebelling against Emperor Frederick at the suggestion of Pope Honorius, formed an association, which for a long time was called that of the Lombards, to no small detriment of the Roman Empire and an impediment to the recovery of the Holy Land.

*The association of the Lombards against the empire.*

And in the same year Pope Honorius III himself died. Gregory IX succeeded him and presided for fourteen years and three months.

*Pope Honorius dies.*

*1227.*

In the eleventh year of Abbot Eberhard, and in the one thousand two hundred twenty-seventh year of the Lord’s nativity, Henry the king, son of the emperor, coming to Aachen around Passion Sunday, held a very celebrated court of the princes and nobles of almost all Germany. Among them the chief were the archbishops of Mainz, Cologne, and Salzburg, with many bishops, the dukes of Bavaria, Carinthia, Brabant, and Lorraine, Henry count palatine of the Rhine, the landgrave of Thuringia, the count of Flanders, who for twelve years had been detained in captivity at Paris by the king of France and had been released the previous year, with many other counts and nobles. In this assembly of princes, the wife of Henry, king of the Romans, daughter of Leopold, duke of Austria, was consecrated as queen by Henry, archbishop of Cologne, and, with great solemnity, placed on the royal seat beside the king.

*The queen is crowned at Aachen.*

In the same year, on the day of St. Mary Magdalene, the king of Denmark, a violator of the faith and oath that he had pledged in the previous year for his absolution, prepared to avenge himself: after gathering an immense multitude of armed men, he challenged Henry, count of Schwerin, to war. By him, with the aid of the archbishop of Bremen and Albert, duke of Saxony, he was defeated and scarcely escaped by flight, four thousand men on his side having been slain, and many captured from the Danes.

*Emperor Frederick is excommunicated by the pope.*

Because of this, he was excommunicated by Pope Gregory IX, and the archbishop of Mainz announced him as such.
