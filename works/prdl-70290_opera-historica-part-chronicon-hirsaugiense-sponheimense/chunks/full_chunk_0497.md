In the same year also Ulrich, abbot of this monastery, besides the many debts that he contracted secretly and openly, sold, disposed of, and alienated many things without the knowledge and will of the convent in Gisenheim, Odernheim, Freymersheim, and various other places, to no small loss for this monastery, spending the money not only uselessly but, what is worse, disgracefully, with frivolous persons and those nourishing the tinder of wickedness.

*1366.*

In the fifth year of Ulrich our abbot, the monastery of St. Godehard in Hildesheim, of our order, newly reformed, was first received and admitted into the chapter and union of the Bursfeld observance, together with Lupold its abbot.

In the same year, on Friday after the feast of the Lord's Resurrection, Johannes de Grumbach, bishop of Wurzburg, died. After him Rudolf von Scherenberg, elected by the chapter on the last day of the month of April, succeeded and presided for twenty-eight years, an energetic and peaceable man, who did much good for his church of Wurzburg, paying off enormous debts and bringing back many things long before alienated from the church by his predecessors.

In the same year also, after Abbot Ulrich had badly governed this monastery for five years and twenty-three days, and had reduced the monastery itself to extreme want and poverty, having no longer anything from which he could live, compelled by necessity, on the third Nones of April he freely resigned the abbacy into the hands of his convent, leaving debts of more than two thousand five hundred florins, besides hidden losses that he had inflicted on the monastery by selling various goods, revenues, and proceeds without the consent of his brothers and utterly alienating them from the monastery.

*Concerning Otto Hawysen, twenty-third abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, who presided for three years, four months, and sixteen days, and concerning various events of that time.*

After the resignation of Abbot Ulrich, alas, far too late, on the same day the brothers of this monastery, gathering in the morning at the chapter place, at about the tenth hour, began to hold among themselves the necessary discussion about electing a new pastor. They were five in number, and these were their names: Ulrich, formerly abbot; Otto, prior; Peter of Blenich, cellarer; Andreas of Utrecht; Johannes of Kirchberg; and Johannes of Bockenvy, who has now been held in chains for thirty years, as we state the reason in its proper place. Therefore at last, after mature deliberation, they unanimously elected as abbot the aforesaid Otto of Cologne, called Hawysen, thirtieth prior of this monastery, who, confirmed by Adolf, archbishop of Mainz, and ordained by his vicar in pontificals in the year noted above, on the day of St. John the Baptist, in the collegiate church of St. Barbara in the town of Bingen, presided over this [unclear]. Made a monk in the monastery of St. Pantaleon [unclear], he remained there until manhood. Afterwards, when in the year of the Lord 1348 the aforesaid monastery of St. Pantaleon had been reformed, as we have said [unclear], by the fathers of the union and observance of Bursfeld, for some time, at the exhortation of his parents, he persevered in it, to test whether he could endure the rigor of the order, and he learned competently the customs and ceremonies of those of the observance; and yet afterward, wearied and departing from there, he came to this monastery under Abbot Conrad and was incorporated into the convent.

He was, however, mild and peaceable in disposition, and more gentle than the customs of the monks of that time required in the present monastery. In his presidency he made great effort according to his strength, and would gladly have reformed the monastery in both states, but he could not. For in temporal matters it was altogether impoverished, and in spiritual matters destitute of every regular observance. Nevertheless, he made more progress in temporal than in spiritual matters. What he desired and attempted in spiritual matters we shall say in part in the following year. When he was ordained abbot, however, no definite prior was appointed after him during the whole time in which he presided in the abbacy, for three years and four months, until the appointment of Abbot Kolenhausen, as we shall say below.

*1367.*
