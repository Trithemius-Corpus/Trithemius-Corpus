Given at Piacenza, by the hand of John, cardinal deacon of the holy Roman Church, on the Ides of March, in the second indiction, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1095, and in the pontificate of lord Pope Urban.

These things were done under the venerable Abbot Gebhard, in the fourth year of his abbacy; he could easily obtain from the supreme pontiff what he wished, since he had previously been known to him and singularly loved by him. Nor indeed could it have escaped the pope how well the brethren of the place thought with the Roman Church, opposing the excommunicated king, and how most steadfastly they had always stood for Roman liberty.

Moreover, when Abbot Gebhard was still claustral prior, having been sent to the pope by Saint Wilhelm, as we said above, he had obtained acquaintance with him through familiarity.

That venerable Pope Urban also gave another privilege to the monastery of Hirsau, of greater exemption, liberty, and quiet; since it is quite notable, we have thought it should be inserted in this work word for word.

Urban, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to all the faithful of the Teutonic region. Since we are about to grant this charter and this privilege in the name of Christ, it is not beside the point if we first make known what is the cause, or what the necessity, of this privilege. Monasteries and dwellings of the servants of Christ, which are built with pious will and excellent zeal, if they were loved no less by posterity than by their founders and ancestors, their condition would not be so weak. Likewise, if all those who are called bishops cared worthily for their office and strove to benefit as much as to preside, this too would contribute not a little to the fitness of monasteries. But now, as we have rather often experienced, what the ancestors build, if their descendants do not destroy it, they omit more from human shame than for God. Yet from the very alms of their parents they exact so much secular service that there is no worthy solicitude for that service which is owed to God.

This too has been seen to happen more than once: that one who seemed to be a prelate of Christ, solely through this occasion, namely that an abbot had to be blessed by him, would [unclear] the congregation of brothers and the election of the abbot [unclear], and would ordain no one other than the one whom he himself chiefly wished, the will of the brothers being wholly set aside. Once he had been ordained, moreover, he thereafter commanded him as if he were his tributary, and as if he had been appointed solely for this, to serve in a secular manner. For that most malignant pest, namely the simoniacal heresy, with its author the devil, goes about the earth and walks through it, so that there is no church which can be secure from its violent invasion. On account of these several things, since the Lord’s dispensation has so ordained concerning our mediocrity that we ought to be servants of the servants of God, it is not fitting for us to omit or neglect whatever aid or support the mercy of Christ may give against hostile incursions, so that we may be able to confer it upon these same our lords.

There is a certain monastery in the district of Speyer and in the forest which is called the Black Forest; the name of that monastery in the Teutonic tongue is Hirsau. When it had come by hereditary right into the power and possession of the most noble Count Adelbert and of his wife Wiltrud, it did not seem sufficiently to avail toward the salvation of their souls if they retained it in their own hand, as it had been left to them by their parents. Therefore the first little fruit of the labor of their good will was that, in place of the canons formerly living there, the ordered life of monks was introduced. This having been done, they also wished that the heir of the same monastery should be more religious than Saint Peter and the Roman Church.
