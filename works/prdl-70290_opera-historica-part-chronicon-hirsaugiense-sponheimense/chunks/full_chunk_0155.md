*1127.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year 1127 from the Nativity of Christ, King Lothar celebrated the Nativity of the Lord at Cologne.

In the same year King Lothar, with the duke of Bohemia, besieged Nuremberg for two months and withdrew without result. William, prince of Burgundy, was killed by his own men. Charles also, count of Flanders, a lover, cultivator, and defender of justice above all the princes of his time, while set in prayer before the altar, was killed by his own men, who hated justice.

*G., archbishop, dies.*
*When Nuremberg was besieged.*

In the same year Gotfrid, archbishop of Trier, weighed down by old age, resigned the episcopate, which he had ruled for two years and ten months; surviving one year, five months, and sixteen days, he rested in the Lord. Meginher succeeded him; going to Rome, he was consecrated by Pope Honorius II and given the pallium. Returning then to Germany, by command of the pope he excommunicated Conrad, duke of Swabia, afterwards emperor, [unclear], who was then troubling Lothar's empire; this did not turn out well for him.

*Conrad is excommunicated.*

For when afterward he too strongly reproved the incontinence of his clerics, and on that account became hated by all wicked men, he resolved to go to Rome so that he might be helped by the pope. But at Parma, betrayed by his own clerics, he was captured by Duke Conrad, whom he had excommunicated, imprisoned, and deprived of the light of his eyes; at last, tortured by excessive misery, [unclear] in Germany. By command of the pope he excommunicated Conrad, duke of Swabia, afterwards emperor, [unclear], and Lothar, who was then troubling his empire; this did not turn out well for him.

In the same year several bishops died: Theodoric, bishop of Münster; Ulrich, bishop of Constance; Eberhard, archbishop of Strasbourg; Rutger, archbishop of Magdeburg; the bishop of Paderborn; and the bishop of Liege. Godebold, bishop of Utrecht, founder of the monastery of Saint Lawrence of our order in Oesbrog, of whom we spoke above, weighed down by old age, resigned his episcopate in this year, entered the aforesaid monastery, took up the habit of holy religion, and as a monk happily rested in the Lord on the day before the Ides of November. After his resignation Andrew succeeded in the pontificate of the church of Utrecht.

*A bishop becomes a monk of our order.*

Norbert, the first founder of the Premonstratensian order, a man of wondrous holiness and an outstanding preacher, was ordained bishop for the church of Magdeburg. For it happened by a remarkable chance that he was made bishop: when the aforesaid archbishop Rutger had died, and Arnold, who had invaded the episcopate, had been killed by the citizens, the clerics sent messengers to the emperor and asked that a bishop be given to them. By chance this Norbert was present, a man most renowned throughout the whole world for knowledge and holiness; with the counsel of the princes, the emperor appointed him archbishop. These things were done in the year of the Lord 1128, in the city of Speyer. Moreover, Alexander was ordained bishop for the church of Liege.

*1128.*

For the church of Paderborn, however, a certain man named Bernard was appointed, in the seventh year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year 1128 of the Lord's Nativity. Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Saint Peter in Erfurt, formerly a monk of Hirsau, died; Werner, also a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, succeeded him in the aforesaid abbacy by the unanimous election of the brothers and with the permission and blessing of Abbot Volmar, a man of much prudence and [unclear]; he introduced [unclear] into that monastery, to the wonder of all.

*Werner becomes abbot in Erfurt.*

In the same year, when King Lothar was celebrating Christmas at Wurzburg, he learned by flying rumor that Conrad, brother of Frederick, duke of Alsace, had usurped the royal name for himself. For this reason the bishop of Mainz, with the others present, excommunicated the same Conrad; afterward Pope Honorius confirmed that excommunication through Meginher, archbishop of Trier, as we have said. King Lothar therefore came with his princes and besieged Speyer, where Conrad himself, after expelling Bishop Sigfrid, had placed his garrison; but, after receiving hostages, he departed.
