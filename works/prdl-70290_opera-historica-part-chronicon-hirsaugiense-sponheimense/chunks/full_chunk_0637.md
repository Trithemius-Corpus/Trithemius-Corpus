The mind was born for the supercelestial realm, in whose likeness it lives. The stars understand nothing, nor indeed do they feel; whence they neither confer wisdom upon our mind nor have any dominion over us, who walk in the spirit, confessing the Lord Jesus Christ, who has all things in his power, to whose likeness we must faithfully conform ourselves as far as we can. For he is the wisdom of God the Father; he is the fountain and origin of knowledge; he is the center of the soul, through whom all things were made. Let rash men go away, vain men and lying astrologers, deceivers of minds and babblers of trifles. For the disposition of the stars contributes nothing to the immortal mind, nothing to natural knowledge, nothing to supercelestial wisdom; but body has dominion only over its own body. The mind is free, and is not subjected to the stars, nor does it receive their influences, nor follow their motion, but communicates only with the supercelestial principle by which it was both made and made fruitful. The rest that had to be answered to your inquiries, O Germanus, we have enclosed in a more sacred chest, to be presented through Narcissus to you alone; and to him we have entrusted also the key to be brought to you, who do not know enough, lest you possess a chest full of secrets without profit. But we have not been able to come to you in person this time, since we have been called elsewhere. Nevertheless you will learn more fully from Narcissus our intention, how and when an opportunity may come to us of coming to you. In other letters we have written everything that seemed to us ought to be written to you. Farewell, mindful of us and not forgetful of yourself. From Neumagen in the city of Speyer, on the 24th day of August, in the year of Christ 1505.

*XXXV. Wolfgang Hopilius to Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim: greeting.*

Recently, my Trithemius, I received from you a most elegant letter, which was pleasant to us and brought admiration to my mind: that you yourself, a most learned man and altogether more excellent than I, did not disdain to write to me, a country ant living a rustic life, and by that token wished to declare the affection of your mind toward our uncultivated talent. But whence this has happened I think I know by conjecture. Our Narcissus, a most diligent investigator of all good sciences, perhaps reported to you about us things like dreams and phantasms, indeed greater than our smallness can furnish; and you give credence to his words, for those who have an honest mind and kindly disposition easily believe whatever good they hear about others, but believe evil slowly. You were moved, as far as I can conjecture, to share with me your most ornate letter. I give immortal thanks, as one who greatly values the letters of so great a man. For whatever in human affairs can be known, I know is not hidden from you, since today you easily surpass and excel all mortals in talent, knowledge, and literary experience, as your writings and the testimonies of the most approved men make clearer than light. Hence you embrace all singularly learned men; indeed, even those whom report has brought to your notice as skilled in anything, and if they are men inferior in praises, by your inborn goodness toward you [unclear] you magnify and esteem very highly. The remaining secrets of our breast you will understand from our Narcissus, our common friend, who has inscribed your immortal name upon marble breasts. Him I commend to you very greatly, although there is no need; and I should like you to persuade him to beware of the dangers of Arisleus and Alehindi, of whom the one ended his life most wretchedly, while the other fell into madness, as you easily know from their little works. Farewell, and love us. From our Parisian workshop, on the 3rd day before the Kalends of August, in the year of the Lord 1505.

*XXXVI. Johannes Trithemius of Spanheim to Wolfgang Hopilius, citizen of Paris: greeting.*

Our Narcissus, coming to me at Neumagen on the 15th day of the month of August, delivered your letter to me [unclear], and explained the rest entrusted to him by you. You wonder that I judged you worthy of my letter, thinking that I had been too credulous toward Narcissus, as though I had not known you before he came to me. For how great you are I had already learned from the report of many. But you do well by imitating the counsel of the wise man who says: The greater you are, humble yourself in all things, and before God you will find grace, since the great power is of God alone, and he is honored by men. Likewise the very power and wisdom of God, the man Christ Jesus, said in the holy Gospel: Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.
