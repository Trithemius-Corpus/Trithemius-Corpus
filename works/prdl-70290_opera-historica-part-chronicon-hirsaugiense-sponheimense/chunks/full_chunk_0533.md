*Sponheimense.*

... judged by most people excessive, for the sake of preserving peace he was deposed and, because of a certain necessity that arose, was appointed cellarer, full of shame.

1487.

In this year, which was the fourth of our abbot Johannes Trithemius, on the fourth day of March died that famous hermit Nicholas in Swida, in the district of the Suitenses or Swiss, not far from the town of Lucerne, in a vast solitude. For many years in that same hermitage, in great poverty, humility, patience, and holiness, he served the Lord strenuously as a solitary; before his death he lived for twenty continuous years without bodily food, as we have learned by the testimony of the bishop of Constance, in whose parish he dwelt, and of several other trustworthy persons. He also had the spirit of prophecy, and foretold to his people certain future things which later came to pass. A great concourse of pilgrims came to him even from distant regions, since his dwelling was not far from the monastery of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of the Hermits. This man of God was a miracle to us in his time: although he was wholly ignorant of all letters, nevertheless he flourished in understanding of the Scriptures with wonderful subtlety, and gave answers to the questions of learned men of every kind, just as that holy hermit Anthony, a most learned philosopher, is said once to have given them without letters. Yet he did not admit everyone indiscriminately to conversation with him, but only those whom he had recognized by the divine spirit to be stirred to speak; finally, he is said to have shone with miracles.

In the same year, since the altar in the castle of Sponheim, long since incorporated into this monastery, had collapsed with its chapel from excessive age and threatened ruin at every hour, so that no one dared to celebrate Mass upon it, Johannes Trithemius, abbot, with the consent of the archbishop prince, transferred it here to the monastery with all its rights and revenues. He, although he was wholly ignorant of all things, nevertheless flourished in understanding of the Scriptures with wonderful subtlety, and answers were given to the questions of learned men of every kind, just as that holy hermit Anthony, a most learned philosopher, is said once to have given them without letters. Yet he did not admit everyone indiscriminately to conversation with him, but only [those?] whom he had recognized by the divine spirit to be stirred to speak; finally, he is said to have shone with miracles.

In the same year, on the third day before the Ides of July, that is, on the day of Saint Margaret, virgin and martyr, our aforesaid abbot Johannes caused the chapel and altar to be consecrated in honor of Saints John, Pancras, Jerome the priest, Bernard the abbot, Catherine virgin and martyr, Catherine of Siena, and the eleven thousand virgins, by Heinrich, bishop of [unclear], vicar in pontifical matters of Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, with the customary indulgences granted. After the meal had been completed, he solemnly blessed here in the monastery two new bells, one of which abbot Trithemius had caused to be cast, and the other he had purchased for a price.

Also in the year noted above, on the third Sunday after the celebration of the feast of Easter, the twenty-sixth provincial chapter of our order was held in the monastery of Saint James near Mainz. Presiding over it were Heinrich of Weissenburg, Conrad at Wiblingen, Bertram of Saint Gotthard in Hildesheim, and Johannes at Seligenstadt, abbots of monasteries of our order.

In the same year, on the day of the Nativity of the Lord, died Conrad of Rodenberg, abbot of our order on the Mount of Saint John in the Rheingau, a very religious, devout, and learned man, a special lover of regular observance. For him our abbot Johannes Trithemius wrote an epitaph, and fixed it to the wall beside his tomb:

Stay your steps a little, and pause a while, traveler,
for here you see the tomb of Conrad Rodenberg.
The holy Mount, which the famous Rhine river beholds,
held him; in character and talent there was no one equal to him.
He had been the most renowned abbot of this monastery,
a cultivator of virtues, a lover of religion.
