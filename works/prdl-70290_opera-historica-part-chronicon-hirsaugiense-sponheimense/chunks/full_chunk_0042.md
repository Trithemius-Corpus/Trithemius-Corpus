*The Number of Those Who Were in This Synod*

Forty-eight in number; all of them, together with the leading Romans and the whole people, with one voice deposing John from the papacy as a monster and slave of the devil, by unanimous vote elected Leo, the eighth of that name, as supreme pontiff, swearing that they would never depart from him, nor thereafter elect a pope without the consent of the emperor and his son.

When these things had been done, the holy emperor, not wishing to burden the Romans with too great an army, and at the same time believing that they would remain steadfast in the promised loyalty, granted many leave to depart. When the deposed John learned this, knowing the instability and greed of the Romans, he secretly sent messengers to them, promising that he would give them all the treasures of the Church if they would butcher the emperor together with the holy pope Leo. The Romans, at once trusting in the small number of the imperial army and encouraged by the promise of money, when the trumpet sounded, hastened and strove against the emperor in order to kill him.

The holy emperor met them on the bridge of the Tiber, which the Romans had blocked with carts. His very brave soldiers, accustomed to war, boldly leapt upon them and, like hawks, terrified the multitude of birds, with no one resisting. Neither hiding-places, nor baskets, nor hollow logs, nor crypts, nor receptacles of filth could protect those who fled. And so the Romans were killed without number, without mercy, without distinction of persons. Not even one of them all would have remained alive, had not the most pious emperor, moved by mercy toward those to whom no mercy was owed, [unclear] his soldiers while they were still killing them. After hostages had been received, the venerable pope Leo, having fallen at the emperor’s feet, begged that the hostages be restored to the Romans and that he be entrusted to their loyalty. Moved by his request, the holy emperor restored the hostages, and, knowing what was to come, entrusted the pope himself to the loyalty of the Romans like a lamb to wolves.
