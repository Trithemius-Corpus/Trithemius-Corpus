*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In the same year [Marcelsus?], archbishop of Mainz, died on the Ides of July; Henry, provost of the greater church, succeeded him and presided for ten years and eight months.

In the same year Adelbero, archbishop of Trier, expelled Gerard, abbot, and all the monks of St Maximin because of their disorderly life, and introduced other monks, who would live according to the Rule of St Benedict. Over them he appointed as abbot Siger, a religious and learned man. There are those who say that he did this not out of love for regular discipline, but because of his own greed: for it is agreed that he violently plundered much from the monastery of St Matthias for his own use, and obtained the abbey of St Maximin itself from the hand of the emperor; he drove out Abbot Gerard, who for a long time remained in exile on Mount St Disibod and here with Lord Bernhelm and our brethren. After he had thus been driven out, the brethren of St Maximin called the count of Namur, now Luxembourg, to their aid against the bishop, after selling jewels and ornaments, with whose price they hired him. But the archbishop besieged his castle of Rudolsberg while he himself was inside; he fled secretly and left the castle to the bishop. After the fortress had been taken, with many captives, the bishop returned; he [unclear] Manderscheid, Colubria, and thirty fortified castles of the counts, and forced the count himself to come into his favor, whereupon the brethren, deprived of all aid, were expelled by him.

In the year 1147, the twenty-fourth of Bernhelm our abbot, the holy virgin Hildegard, mistress of the brides of Christ on Mount St Disibod, having been appointed after the death of blessed Jutta, as we have said, was divinely admonished and passed over to Bingen with eighteen holy virgins. She built a monastery on the mountain across the river Nahe, near the tomb of St Rupert, duke and confessor, and brought that place, with all its goods and possessions, partly by purchase from the hand of the count of Hildenheim, dwelling in the parish of Trier, partly by exchange, and partly by donation of the count of Sponheim and other faithful persons, into the right of ownership; and, with the intervention of an apostolic privilege, she commended it for all time to the protection of the church of Mainz. This most holy virgin was born in our county of Sponheim, in the village called Bickelnheim, begotten of her father Hildebert, a most noble knight of Count Stephen, and of her mother Mechthild, likewise a most noble and honorable woman. When she was about eight years old, she was handed over by her parents to be instructed in the fear of the Lord under the teaching of blessed Jutta on Mount St Disibod. After St Jutta died, Hildegard herself, more perfect than all, was appointed mistress of the sisters by unanimous vote; and then, inspired by the divine Spirit, she was compelled to write certain wondrous things. These were brought for examination to the supreme pontiff Eugenius, then at Trier with eighteen cardinals, by Henry, bishop of Mainz, and St Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux; they were read publicly before all and confirmed by that supreme pontiff himself. But since many nobles round about were entrusting their daughters to her instruction, the place became too narrow for them to dwell in; with the permission both of the supreme pontiff and of her abbot, she passed over to Mount St Rupert, as we have said, with eighteen sacred nuns. Among them was Hiltrud, daughter of Count Megenhard, a virgin of most holy life, very close to blessed Hildegard; on account of regard for her, her father the count bestowed many benefactions on that place, and we shall speak more fully of his place.

In this same year Adelbero, archbishop of Trier, called Pope Eugenius to Trier; and for many months he kept and maintained him, with eighteen cardinals, archbishops, bishops, and [unclear], together with all their household, most abundantly at his own expense.

In this year a great gathering of Christians from all Europe went overseas to recover the Lord’s sepulcher and the Holy Land. Among them were Conrad, king of the Romans, Louis, king of France, with many dukes, princes, bishops, abbots, monks, clerics, nobles, and commoners; yet they made little progress toward their chief purpose.

In the year 1149, the twenty-fifth of Bernhelm our abbot, Craftho, son of Count Megenhard of Sponheim, our founder, inflamed by the divine Spirit, despising for love of Christ all the pomp of the world and the broad possessions of riches, with his father’s consent became a monk under Abbot Bernhelm in this present monastery of ours at Sponheim. This happened as follows.
