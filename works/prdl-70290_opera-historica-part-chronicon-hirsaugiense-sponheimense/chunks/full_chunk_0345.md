For he took up a solitary life in a wood not far from Dalen, where he built a small hut of wood and clay, in which until his death he served the Lord in great abstinence for the seven years by which he survived.

1213.

In this year, on the seventeenth day before the Kalends of September, Rupert, the fifth abbot of the monastery, died; he was buried before the steps of the presbytery in the choir. He was in every respect a magnificent man, who vigorously maintained the observance of regular discipline both in himself and in others, and converted many to righteousness by word and example. For, as we said above, he was most learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, an outstanding preacher of the divine word, and no less suited to the governance of temporal affairs than of spiritual ones, and most prudent in everything that had to be done. His brothers, who were bound to him by the greatest love, did not bury him without many tears. For he did many good things for this monastery: he enlarged its revenues and acquired various possessions, and, what is greater than all, he preserved inviolate to his death the holiness of the spiritual life.

In this same year St. Mary of Oignies, in the parish of Liège, died, a most holy woman in life and conduct, who shone with many miracles in her lifetime. Her deeds were written in a lucid style by James of Vitry, afterwards bishop of Acre, and lastly cardinal-bishop of Tusculum of the holy Roman Church, who had preached the cross against the infidels in the diocese of Liège.

*Concerning Ivan, sixth abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, who presided for forty years and three months, and concerning the various deeds of that time.*

When the venerable abbot Rupert had died, as we have said, and been buried, the brothers of this monastery of Sponheim, coming together in one place in the year of the Lord 1213, in the first indiction, on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of September, unanimously elected as abbot Ivan, the seventh prior of this place, a man sufficiently fit for the governance of souls. He presided in the abbacy for thirty-nine years and three months, and in the same year was ordained by lord Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, cardinal and legate of the holy Roman Church, at Mainz in the monastery of St. James on the feast of St. Michael the Archangel. This Ivan was the son of Gotfrid, knight of Sponheim, who in his youth, under abbot Adelger, had been handed over by his parents in this monastery to serve almighty God, when he was ten or eleven years old, namely in the last year of abbot Adelger. As his years increased, wisdom and knowledge of the scriptures increased in him, in which he made such progress that he far surpassed almost all his contemporaries. At length under abbot Rupert he was made prior, and he served in that office for almost five years. When Rupert died, as has been said, he succeeded him in the abbacy; and he was a man sufficiently skilled in handling both spiritual and temporal matters, whose reputation for wisdom was widely spread here and there in the province. After himself he appointed as eighth prior a certain monk named John, who presided in the priorate for only three years, and was deposed for some reason I do not know, as we shall say in its place.

1214.

In his first year abbot Ivan obtained a privilege from lord Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, that he might bless vestments and ecclesiastical ornaments for the use of the monastery and of the churches subject to it.

In the same year Marquard, brother of John, count of Sponheim, entered the religious life. He brought many gifts to this monastery, and lived peacefully among the brothers, humbly and religiously, without complaint; but he was lame in both feet, so that he could walk only with difficulty, and for that reason he was not of much use to the world, and therefore he became a monk.
