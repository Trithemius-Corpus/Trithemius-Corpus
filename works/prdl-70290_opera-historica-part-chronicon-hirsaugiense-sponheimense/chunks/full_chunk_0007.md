When they came to the place where today the monastery of Saint Aurelius has been built, behold, a certain blind man, coming among the crowd to meet the holy relics, began to cry out in a loud voice, saying, “Give me sight, most holy worshipper of God, Aurelius, by your prayers, just as you promised.” To him the bishop said, “Where did Saint Aurelius promise that he would give you sight?” He said, “Lord, this night, as I was sleeping, in the anguish of my mind, a certain [unclear] in the midst of [unclear] said: ‘[unclear] of Christ, you will be given sight.’ And I said, ‘Lord, who are you?’ ‘I am,’ he said, ‘Aurelius the bishop, a new guest in Germany, whom the son of your lord Count Erlafrid brought here some time ago.’”

When these things had been said, he cried out again: “Saint Aurelius, help me, that I may see you.” And immediately his eyes were opened, and he saw perfectly. All who were present, seeing so unusual a miracle, magnified God, saying, “Praise be to you, Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God.” And each said to the one standing at his side: “Oh, how great is this saint, who can even give sight to the blind.” Bishop Nottingus said to Erlafrid the father: “Father, Saint Aurelius has chosen this place for his dwelling”; and, climbing the mountain at a quickened pace, the pontiff, after offering the sacrifice of our redemption, placed the relics that had been made in the prepared place.

Then, after a few days, Bishop Nottingus explained to the patrician Erlafrid and to his brother Ermesrid the vow which he had long before conceived in his mind, at Saint Aurelius’s urging in dreams, admonishing, asking, and beseeching them that, for love of almighty God, in honor of blessed Peter, prince of the apostles, and of Saint Aurelius the bishop, [they should build?] a monastery [unclear]. The thirtieth year [unclear], the seventh of Louis the first emperor, the two hundred and thirty-first from the passing of our most holy father, which with swift completion he happily finished in seven years. Indeed that land produces an abundant supply of both stone and timber, so that there is no need for these to be brought from elsewhere, as [unclear]. Meanwhile [unclear] provision would be made more freely for God. He also sent gold crosses and silver chalices for the service of divine worship, and books too, and various ornaments for the adornment of the house of the Lord, all of which he knew to be necessary for this.

But Erlafrid kept saying that the undertaking was impossible [unclear]; if he himself, overtaken by death, should depart, the unfinished work would either be completed more slowly or be abandoned altogether. For although Ermesrid, the son, was a collaborator with his father in this holy work, nevertheless, because of his youthful age, the zeal of divine love did not seem to burn in him as much as so great an undertaking required.

Therefore, in the seventh year after the monastery of Hirsau had begun to be built, it was completed; this was from the birth of the Lord 837, in the fifteenth indiction, through the untiring diligence of Count Erlafrid, who spared no expense in order to complete what he had conceived in his mind. The church was quite beautiful, built in the manner of the ancients without the support of columns, spacious, covered above with a wooden ceiling, and adorned within with four altars. Moreover the arrangement of the cloister, in imitation of the ancients, had been spacious indeed, but of a simpler construction, likewise covered above with a wooden ceiling, such as [unclear] not so much for the dwellings arranged around the circuit of the cloister as for the custom of that time.
