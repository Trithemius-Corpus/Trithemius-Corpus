*MCCXXXVII.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Ernest, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1237, Emperor Frederick, being established in Austria, caused his son Conrad, still a boy, born of his overseas wife, to be elected king of the Romans. He was elected by the archbishops of Mainz and Trier, and also by the king of Bohemia and the duke of Bavaria, Otto count palatine of the Rhine, with the consent of the other princes who were present. When this had been done, the emperor himself, leaving a garrison of soldiers there at that time, went up to Regensburg and summoned an assembly of princes at Speyer. When they came there at the appointed time, he again caused his son Conrad, the boy, to be designated king. After this, having sent Count Eberhard of Eberstein into Austria with two hundred soldiers to guard the city of Vienna, he himself set out again for Lombardy with the rest of the army; and around the feast of Saint Andrew the Apostle he had a battle with the Milanese, and, after gaining the victory, captured and killed many thousands of them.

In the same year before autumn, a quarter of wine was being sold for sixteen denarii in Alsace; in the preceding year there had been such abundance of it that a quarter of wine was sold for one denarius, as was said above.

In the same year also Pope Gregory IX excommunicated Emperor Frederick, who was laying waste Lombardy, which was in rebellion against him, and caused him to be denounced everywhere by the Friars Minor; and countless evils followed in every land subject to the Roman Empire and to the Church.

In this same year, in the month of April, Emperor Frederick resigned in perpetuity to Archbishop Siegfried and his successors, with all its appurtenances, the fief of the advocacy of the village of Bischoffsheim on the borders of Franconia, which is [unclear] by the church of Mainz in the name of the duchy of Swabia.

In the same year, at the end of Lent, Henry, archbishop of Cologne, died; Conrad of Hochstaden, a warlike man, succeeded him. At that time many troubles of wars arose in the provinces of Mainz, Trier, Cologne, and especially in the land of Liège; because of these there were many incursions of wars, plunderings, and burnings, followed not without the shedding of blood. At the end of this aforesaid year, on the vigil of the Nativity of Christ, Conrad of Tann, bishop of Speyer, died. He gave the church of Deidesheim, with all its revenues, to the canons of the greater church of Speyer for their common use, as a perpetual possession. At his urging also, Cuno, abbot of Wissembourg, with the consent of his convent, handed over the church in the village of Billigheim, not far from Landau, with all its revenues, to the canonical church of Speyer. And the abbot of the monastery of Klingenmünster, then of our order but now of secular clerics, at the persuasion of the said pontiff, gave the right of patronage of the church in Offenbach to the chapter of Speyer. He [held office] almost eight years.

*MCCXXXVIII.*

In the seventh year of Ernest, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1238, Henry, the last landgrave of Alsace, died without heirs. He had held many fiefs from the Empire, from the church of Strasbourg, and from others; after his death these reverted to their lords. Moreover, the title of the provincial countship has remained with the church of Strasbourg down to the present.

In the same year Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, Conrad, bishop of Speyer, [unclear] Conrad his son into Germany with the aforesaid archbishop of Mainz, [unclear] set out, with whose help he fought many battles there. In the same year Henry, bishop of Basel, died; Lütold of Röttelnheim lawfully succeeded him by election of the canons. In these times the Tartars, an immense multitude also pouring out from their own places, miserably destroyed Hungary, Poland, and Moravia, and all the adjacent lands, devastating them with fire, plunder, and sword.
