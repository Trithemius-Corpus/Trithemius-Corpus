These things were done in the year written above, under Henry, king, but the fourth emperor, while Pope Calixtus II presided, on the seventh ides of June. On the same day Abbot Bernhelm was appointed to this abbacy of mine at Spanheim, etc. Thereafter, on the calends of July, which was the Nativity of Blessed John the Baptist, he received the office of consecration from the same archbishop at Mainz in the monastery of Saint Alban, in the presence of four abbots, namely of Saint Alban, Saint James, Saint Disibod, and Blidenstadt, and of many prelates and nobles.

In the same year Bruno, archbishop of Trier, founder of the monastery of Odenheim, died and was buried in the greater church of Trier. Gotfrid, dean of the same church, succeeded him. Hail of great mass fell in this same year on the [unclear] calends of August. An eclipse of the sun occurred on the third ides of August. There was a great famine and a very harsh winter. In these times also a monastery of our order in Saxony, in the diocese of Halberstadt, named Ballenstetten, was founded, namely after the death of Count Otto of that place; and monks with an abbot were enthroned there, provided with sufficient endowment.

In life, moreover, he was renowned for many miracles, concerning which we have written elsewhere. Rupert also, abbot of a monastery of our order in the diocese of Speyer, of whom we spoke above, a man devoted both to divine scriptures and to secular letters, became sufficiently learned. At length returning to his own homeland, that is, the city of Worms, he exchanged secular philosophy for divine; and, becoming a monk in the aforesaid monastery of Limburg and then abbot, in these times he imposed upon himself and his monks, contrary to the custom hitherto usually observed in that monastery, such distinction, fasting, and abstinence of conversion that some of the monks, though fewer in number, fled from the monastery and, going to Arnold, bishop of Speyer, who had succeeded Bruno, stirred up a grave persecution against him. For as abbot he introduced such a manner of living that they not only abstained altogether from meats, according to the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, but also from fish, eggs, and dairy foods, and generally from all things which had ever lived or could have lived, content with vegetables alone, peas and beans, after the example of the ancient holy fathers, both in the desert and in monasteries, [unclear]. Outside illness they did not drink wine, as we read of Saint Martin and his monks.
