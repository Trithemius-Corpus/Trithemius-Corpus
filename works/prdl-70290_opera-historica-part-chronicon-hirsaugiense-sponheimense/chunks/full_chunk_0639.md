*Johannes Trithemius 475* who died for you on the cross, do not be saddened, but silently, with tranquil mind, follow the counsel of God, since you are called [unclear] of others. Keep your purpose, and care neither for riches nor for the most empty honors of the world. Think of the end toward which we are tending; love the beginning from which we are. I beg you, recognize Trithemius as thrice great in the sharpness of his mind: a Christian by imitating Christ, a monk by despising the world, and a philosopher by not admitting disturbance; when you have become great, in the love of Christ you will at last come forth blessed. Know that Saturn, since he is not moving quite prosperously enough for you, as I had also foretold when I was present with you in your monastery, is to be avoided a little. By Mars indeed and Mercury, in a short time, believe me, you will be relieved with a good though humble condition. For neither God will abandon you, O Trithemius, nor Libanius. Hear the counsel of him whom it is not permitted to despise. Give place to envy; better things are being prepared for you. After fifteen moons, follow when called, and not before. Go now when called by the great [philoctyphus?], with heaven opened, to return; then you will see either me or my messenger at Neometum. But take care lest Saturn behold the books that are witnesses of secrets. Be ready for the journey; forget nothing of those things which are useful for our affairs. Keep the [anacrisis?] in silence, and do not release the dove before the time. Lift up your head and do not turn aside. Leave the lying Phoenicians, so that he may understand too late what is good. Beware, O Trithemius, lest you bring the rays of the sun into caves, since the dragons are destructive. Do not hide the higher air in the earth except once. Do not offer your bread to oxen and goats, nor send out the spirit through the lesser doors. Be one with yourself, and beware lest [you be] alone. Flee every multitude, because one is all things, and without one there is nothing. Do not lead a blind man by the hand, but only with a staff. Do not mix water with wine, nor let there be double bread on your table. Do not feed birds in the sun but in the shade, and do not show glass or a mirror at all to dogs, because it is dangerous. Do not strike the shadow, since it is an injury to the sun. Use ants, if perhaps you take pleasure in catching birds. Divide light in water, lest, when frozen, it creak. Be more even in soul, and do not fail, O Trithemius, because if no one else is willing to have care for you, certainly Libanius will. We have kings and powerful men as friends, who will most gladly favor you even by name alone. The Almighty permits nothing without cause, and one must not despair of the mercy of the highest goodness. I know that you fear God, and, raised up by sharpness of mind, neither care for the prosperous things of the world nor fear adverse ones. Therefore, having found such a disciple in you, I have gladly communicated to you all that is mine. Only remain faithful; in the one thing alone, which you lawfully can, be a Pythagorean. Farewell, more than my other self, in him who alone always is. From Saint-Quentin, the sixth day of the month of June, in the year 1505.

*XXXVIII.*

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to his Libanius, his only teacher, greetings.*

I received, read, and understood your most sweet letter, best of teachers, on the fourteenth day of the month of July at Cologne Agrippina, where an assembly of the princes of Germany was being held, with our king present. I shall follow the counsel that you gave, although there will be a very serious muttering among our men, whose tongue will rage against me. Melantius, with his people, stirred up the whole evil against us; we do not doubt that he will fall into the pit which he prepared. Our Astrophilus advised us the same things in your words, nor did he indeed depart in one point from your opinion. The prince, the Margrave, summoned me to Cologne, and prevailed upon me to go with him, to remain for a time in the March. I shall keep not only your precepts, but also your counsels themselves, to the best of my strength. I have withdrawn hay from the oxen and goats, and I have a great war with the apes, but thus far I have come out the victor. Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, a prince worthy of eternal memory, a young man of twenty-one years, skilled in the Latin language, and a most zealous lover of true wisdom, burns with a great desire for you. O, if it could ever come about that he might deserve to see and address you in person. He is a most humane prince, and one who not only does not blush to learn, but also very greatly desires it. I would like you to write to him as soon as possible, taking the subject from us, and send something from the workshop of Pelagius. Thus I would wish you to act: insofar as whichever of us two departs first, let the one who remains be heir of his other self. By your consolations my mind has been made stronger, and it gladly inclines to you in the return that it owes. Pray with me, I beg, to God, that he may grant us what we wish, and what we ought especially to wish.
