*VII.*

*Joachim, by the grace of God prince-elector of Brandenburg, to Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, his preceptor: greeting.*

Reverend father, most celebrated of preceptors, we are shortly to set out on a journey to the sacred majesty of the King of the Romans, desiring on that same journey both to meet and converse in person with Your Paternity, as you know we have already long wished to do, and to confer with you about many things that are in our mind. Therefore we entreat and ask Your Paternity that, setting aside any other occupations of yours, you come in your own person to the town of [unclear] on the Rhine, on the first day of the coming month of July, where we too shall be present at that same time, with divine mercy favoring us. In this matter Your Paternity will do us a singular pleasure, to be repaid toward the same with favor and every grace. May Your Paternity fare well forever. Given from our Cologne by the Spree, on the eleventh day of the month of June, in the year of the Lord 1505.

*VIII.*

*To the most serene prince Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg, elector of the Roman Empire: the letter of reply of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.*

I shall come to Your Highness, most serene prince, on the day and at the place appointed for me by you, most gladly, although because of various ailments of my body I seem scarcely able to move my foot anywhere. For nothing more pleasing to me, nothing sweeter, could have happened at this time than to approach you, a most wise and most learned prince, to spend time with you, to speak and discuss; not indeed about small, very slight, or common matters, but about difficult and very great ones, worthy of your most fertile intelligence, which do not shrink from our threefold philosophy. For whenever the memory comes to my mind of those discussions of ours which two years ago at Frankfurt we were accustomed to carry on far into the night, so often my mind is much more greatly kindled with love for our heavenly philosophy. Therefore, as soon as, after receiving the letter of your serenity, I understood that you would come to the regions of the Rhine, all bodily distress withdrew from me, and because of the exultation of my mind the feeble limbs of my poor body, which had long lost their strength, recover it, and all my bones hasten as quickly as possible to come to Your Majesty. Meanwhile, most serene prince, continue to attend your Trithemius, as you are accustomed, with kindly favor, since he who of his own free will delivered himself to you as a servant has withheld from your command nothing of those things which he can do, knows, possesses, and understands. Therefore by right you ought henceforth to show yourself a favorable patron to your Trithemius, from whom you have received all that was his own, indeed what he was to himself. I know that you deem me worthy beyond my merit, on account of which, almost forgetful of the excellence of your serenity, I could scarcely restrain my loving frenzy. But spare me, most illustrious prince, since I have been bolder than befitted my littleness. I wish Your Highness good health. From Neumagen, on the twentieth day of the month of June. In the year of the Christians 1505.
