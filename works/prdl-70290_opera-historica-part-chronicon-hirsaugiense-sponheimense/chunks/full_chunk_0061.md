*MXXIII.*

In the year when the abbacy had been vacant for 22 years, in the 6th indiction, Emperor Henry II died on the 13th day before the Kalends of July, and was buried in the greater church of Bamberg, which he himself had founded. When Henry had died, the prince-electors chose Conrad, the second of this name, to the empire: a Frank by birth, a good and vigorous man. He had taken as wife Gisela, widow of Bruno, duke of the Saxons; by her he begot a son Henry, who ruled after him, and a daughter Cunigunde, who lies buried in the monastery of Limburg. This Emperor Conrad II built the aforesaid monastery of Limburg, of our order, in the diocese of Speyer, which he adorned with many estates, ornaments, privileges, and liberties. They write that previously a castle had been built in the same place, and that through him it was changed into a monastery of monks.

*MXXXI.*

In the year of the Lord 1031, which was the 29th year with the monastery vacant, A [Athon?], archbishop of Mainz, died; Saint Bardo, abbot of Hersfeld, of our order, succeeded him and presided over the church of Mainz for 20 years. He was a learned man and of most holy conduct, and after his death was enrolled in the catalogue of the saints. He died in the year of the Lord 1051, in the 4th indiction, on the 2nd day before the Ides of June. Around these times Thietagus, from being a monk of the monastery of Corvey in Saxony, of our order, became archbishop of Prague after Gaudentius. He was a man outstandingly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, and venerable no less for the merit of his life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures.

*MXXXV.*

In the year of the Lord 1035, which was the 33rd year with the monastery of Hirsau vacant, Pilgrim, archbishop of Cologne, died. Hermann succeeded him, son of Ezelin, count palatine of the Rhine, concerning whom we spoke above.

In the same year Rudolph, abbot of Hersfeld, of our order, was made bishop of Paderborn, a learned man and venerable for integrity of character.

But in the 35th year with the monastery vacant, Saint Cunegund, formerly empress, died. After the death of Emperor Saint Henry, for love of almighty God she had despised the world, and in the monastery of Kaufungen, taking up the habit of holy life, she remained until death as a nun with the holy virgins of Christ, and herself a virgin. In the same year Saint Bardo, archbishop of Mainz, dedicated the church of Saint Martin at Mainz, with Emperor Conrad and many bishops and abbots present, on the 4th day before the Nones of November.

*MXXXVII.*

Saint Stephen also, king of Hungary, died in this same year. Henry his son succeeded him, a holy man and beloved of God; he too, together with his father, was enrolled in the catalogue of the saints.

In the same year too Saint Simeon, monk and recluse, died at Trier on the Kalends of June. We have already made mention of him above. He soon shone with such great miracles that, at the request of Archbishop Poppo, he was canonized by Pope Benedict IX immediately after death. His life, as we said before, Eberwin, abbot of [Doley?], described in elegant speech.

*MXXXIX.*

In the year 1039, which was the 37th year with the monastery vacant, in the 7th indiction, on the day before the Nones of June, Emperor Conrad II died and was buried in the church of Speyer, which he had built and endowed with many benefactions. By the election of the princes his son Henry, the third of this name, succeeded him, and ruled as emperor for 17 years after his father. He had as wife Gunhild, daughter of the king of the Danes; when she died without children, he took as his second wife Agnes, daughter of William, count of Poitou. By her he had Henry, the fourth emperor of this name, who reigned after him, and two daughters: Gisela, a nun, and Adelaide, who was abbess of our order in Quedlinburg.

*MXLII.*

In the same year Eberhard, first bishop of Bamberg, died; Suidger succeeded him, who afterward became pope. Reginbold, bishop of Speyer, also died; Sibicho succeeded him.
