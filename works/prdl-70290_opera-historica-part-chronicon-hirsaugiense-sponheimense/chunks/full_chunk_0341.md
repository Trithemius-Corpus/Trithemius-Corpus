*Sponheimense.*

“...with whom you are going. That man, proceeding [unclear], through ignorance turned aside from the right way onto another road, and wandered for several hours in the wood. How gravely I was accused and afflicted because I had not warned him of this, even though he had not asked me at all, I could not express if I had a hundred tongues.

The demons also charged me with another matter, much more serious: once, when in tithing the crops in my field, as is customary, I had not observed the due order, I had kept back for myself one sheaf owed to the church, putting in its place another which seemed to be somewhat more useful to me. I had confessed that guilt of mine to my priest and been absolved by him, and believed that I was now wholly secure. But alas, how wretchedly my confidence deceived me. For because I had not restored the loss inflicted on the church, which was scarcely four denarii, even though absolved, a sentence of eternal damnation was already about to be pronounced against me, had not the most blessed Mother of God and St. Maximinus, together with the holy angels, interceded for the mercy of the Judge.

Nevertheless I was tortured in the unspeakable burning of flames, while demons rushed upon me with what seemed like sheaves of fragments and mocked me with laughter. It seemed to me that I was tortured in that most penal fire for more than four hundred years, and that all the sheaves of the whole world were thrown upon me; although I later learned that I had scarcely been placed in that torture for the space of one hour.

Then, freed by the mercy of God, I was led by an angel to the places of punishments, where I saw torments so horrible, so monstrous and fearful, that no mortal understanding can grasp them and no tongue of anyone in the world can utter them. No comparison can be made between the punishments of this world and those punishments of eternal damnation which I saw in the underworld. So great a multitude of souls also descended into hell during that interval in which I was out of the body, that previously I would not have believed so many people could have died in a hundred years. There were bishops, abbots, priests, monks, nuns, together with innumerable Christians of both sexes, as many as I would have believed were living in the whole world. Moreover the multitude of unbelieving heathens was infinite; like snow from heaven, so they fell into hell.

O how great is the misery of unhappy souls, how pitiable their groaning and outcry, who never cease from wailing, always crying out: ‘Woe, woe is me; why was I born upon the earth, to come into this place of eternal damnation?’ Meanwhile I saw the soul of a certain bishop dragged by demons into hell, with such exultation on their part that it is difficult to say; and she cried out miserably, wailing without end. So great a multitude of demons had poured around that wretched soul that we would never have believed there were so many demons. Fraudulent tithing, the withholding of rents of churches and monasteries, is punished with penalties: they should rather pay a double tithe than subtract from the ministers of the church the value of one denarius.

I saw monks and nuns there enduring horrible punishments, which it is not possible for me to express, because of even slight disobedience, or displeasure or murmuring against their superiors. Of the innumerable multitude of bishops and prelates of the church who were there, some were more tormented because they had no certainty of their liberation. He said that a greater multitude of simple peasants are damned because they commit fraud against one another, especially against the ministers of the church, in tithes, in crops, in pastures, in fields, and in other things; they do not restore what they have inflicted in great measure, and few of them truly and purely confess their sins with true contrition and a firm purpose of amendment. But he asserted that this is a grave sin of subjects: rashly to judge and rebuke the deeds of prelates or ministers of the church, or to speak against those whom God himself will judge.

But it seemed to me, he said, that in the middle of the earth there was a great and very deep well, full of animals foaming with flames and reaching upward as far as the clouds; led to it by the angel, I was ordered to look inside. O good God, how great a horror seized me; if I live a thousand years, I shall never be able to forget it. When I had looked inside, if I had as many tongues as there are drops of water in the great sea, I could not describe the least punishment of those who were being tormented there. For I saw infinite souls of men being horribly tortured there, and innumerable demons mixed among them, tearing them most cruelly. Nothing was heard there but lamentation and ‘Woe’ without interruption, with such blasphemy against God and against every creature that I cannot remember it without horror. So great was the tumult there, so great the crash, so great the tempest, that if a thousand worlds were struck together at once, they could by no means make so horrible a din.
