In the same year, the fifteenth of Abbot Haderad, in the month of July, King Louis held a general assembly of the princes at Tribur, a royal estate not far from Mainz and situated above the Rhine, which, now deserted, keeps no trace of its former honor except the ruined walls of a castle. It is a place between Oppenheim and Mainz, on the right hand for those descending the Rhine, where once this imperial estate had been situated with a castle and a most beautiful monastery; now it has only a few small houses of peasants. Then Louis, moving his army through Franconia, surrounded with a siege and took a certain castle of Count Adelbert, called Tharissa, which is now a monastery of our order in the diocese of Würzburg. Count Adelbert, however, was not in it; but while he was in the castle of Bamberg with his very strong army, [unclear], by the treachery and deception of Hatto, archbishop of Mainz, [unclear], he was beheaded on the fifth day before the Ides of December. *905.*

In the same year, the fifteenth of Abbot Haderad, which was the year of the Lord 905, after the death of Pope Benedict IV, Leo, a monk of our order from Brandalo, elected supreme pontiff, the fifth of that name, sat forty days, [unclear] a certain priest named Christopher, a deceitful man, who, led by lust for rule, cast the pope into chains, where not long afterwards he died of grief of mind. And, invading the pontificate, which as he had acquired it wickedly, so after seven months he shamefully lost, and thrust by Sergius into a monastery, unwillingly he was made a monk.

*Leo the monk becomes pope.*

*Sigbertus.*

In the nineteenth year of Abbot Haderad, Wymo, a monk of Corvey in Saxony, of our order, a learned and holy man, was elected archbishop of Bremen and presided for eighteen years. Afterwards, for the love of God, he entered Gothia, preached the word of God to those peoples, and after some years died and was buried there, shining with many miracles.

In these same times there flourished in the same monastery of Corvey a very learned man, Sigbertus, a monk, disciple of the aforesaid Wymo, most holy bishop, and companion of his pilgrimage. After Wymo had died among the Goths, Sigbertus returned to the monastery and described his life in a twofold style, that is, in verse and in prose. He also wrote certain other things, of which we have already made mention in the second book of the illustrious men of our order.

*910.*

In the twentieth year of Abbot Haderad, which was the year 910 of the Lord’s nativity, the East Franks and Saxons waged many wars with the Hungarians, but made little progress.

*Adelbero.*

In the same year Adelbero, a monk of this monastery of Saint Aurelius of Hirsau, a disciple of Luithelm, was made scholasticus in the monastery of Saint Alban near Mainz, where at that time a great multitude of monks flourished. Because he instructed them so gloriously in every knowledge of the scriptures, both secular and divine, he acquired among all the people round about a great fame for his learning. After a few years Archbishop Hertiger made him abbot of Saint Ferrucius. For he was a man worthy of honor, not only because of the teaching of incomparable knowledge, in which he was distinguished before many others, but also because of his prudence and the complete holiness of his life.

*912.*

In the twentieth year of Abbot Haderad, Emperor Louis died without children. He had indeed had as wife Lutgard, daughter of Ludolf, duke of the Saxons, sister of Duke Otto, but he received no children from her. In his place Conrad I was elected by the princes, son of Conrad, duke of Lotharingia, and brother of the aforesaid Emperor Louis, whom Count Adelbert of Bamberg, as was said above, killed in battle. This Conrad was the last emperor from the stock of Charlemagne, and he ruled for seven years.
