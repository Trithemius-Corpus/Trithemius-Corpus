*1474.*

*King Frederick of the Romans came to Trier.*

In the fifth year of John Colnhausen, our abbot, Emperor Frederick III came to Trier with many princes and remained there in the Archbishop's Palace for almost two months. Among the other princes at that time at Trier was Charles, the last duke of Burgundy, who had his lodging in the monastery of St. Maximin outside the walls of Trier, and there, before the emperor, the princes, and all the people, displayed the magnificence of his power and riches. There are some who say that he coveted the glory of a kingdom, and for that reason had always invited the emperor to Trier, so that, raised by him to be king, he might carry out the rest with still more conspicuous pomp. As evidence is the fact that a diadem, sceptre, globe, and the other things pertaining to the coronation of a king had been prepared with great splendor. But afterward, when the emperor departed secretly, he was disappointed in his hope.

*The magnificence of Charles of Burgundy.*

Rupert, archbishop of Cologne, took the aforesaid Charles, duke of Burgundy, as his guardian and defender against Governor Hermann and the chapter of Cologne; and he at once, in the same year, on the fourth day before the Kalends of August, besieged the notable town of Neuss, in the place and name of that archbishop, with a very great multitude of armed men. The aforesaid Governor Hermann was in the town, and throughout the whole time of the siege, for ten months and twenty-seven days, persevered with the townsmen. Charles had many thousands of men in his army, and with engines and bombards often made very great assaults, but nevertheless achieved nothing.

*Neuss is besieged by the duke of Burgundy.*

*The twenty-first provincial chapter is held.*

Also in the same year, 1474, Philip, the illustrious count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, on Quinquagesima Sunday, took as wife at Amberg Margaret, daughter of Duke Louis of Bavaria, surnamed the Rich, sister of Duke George. By her he begot Duke Louis, Philip bishop of Freising, Duke Rupert, Duke Frederick, and other sons as well as daughters. Moreover, this wife died in the eighteenth year of John Trithemius, abbot of this place, as we shall say in its proper place.

*1475.*

In the sixteenth year of John Kolnhausen, our abbot, Adolph of Nassau, archbishop of Mainz, died on the eighth day [unclear] before the Ides of September in his castle of Eltville. After his death the canons of the greater church, using wise counsel, in order that they might bring the revenues of the church back into one, elected again as archbishop Diether of Isenburg, who had once been deposed; he presided a second time for six years and as many months. This Diether built anew a fortress at the end of the city of Mainz, which he wished to be called the castle of St. Martin. This archbishop Diether, as prelate for the second time, first established the university of the studium generale in the city of Mainz; after it had been endowed to some degree, he obtained its confirmation by the supreme pontiff and its enrichment with the customary privileges and graces.

*Diether is made archbishop of Mainz a second time.*

*Foundation of the University of Mainz.*

In the year noted above, on the vigil of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, Charles, duke of Burgundy, raised the siege of the town of Neuss and withdrew, by the persuasion and counsel of the emperor, who was then present to aid the people of Neuss with a very great multitude of armed men from all the princes and cities of the empire. After his departure, Hermann, governor of the church of Cologne, gradually obtained the castles, towns, fortifications, and villages of the greater part of the bishopric, so that Rupert's obedience was greatly diminished in a short time, which was pitiable to see. But Duke Charles moved his army against the duke of Lorraine around the feast of St. Martin, and, after the duke had been put to flight, received in surrender all his land and the whole duchy of Lorraine, and compelled all the inhabitants to render him homage.

*The siege of the city of Neuss is raised.*

*The duke of Burgundy obtains the duchy of Lorraine.*

In the same year, on the Ides of December, John, archbishop of Magdeburg, primate of Germany, son of Stephen duke of Bavaria and count of Sponheim, and brother of Duke Frederick of Simmern, our reformer, died, a devout and holy man.

*John, bishop of Magdeburg, dies.*

*1476.*

In the seventeenth year of John Kolnhausen, our abbot, the twenty-second provincial chapter of our order was held on the Monks' Mountain near Bamberg, on the third Sunday after the feast of Easter; at it presided Martin in Schwarzach, Hermann of St. James near Mainz, Henry in Blaubeuren, and John of St. Giles in Nuremberg, abbots of monasteries of our order.

*The twenty-second provincial chapter is held.*
