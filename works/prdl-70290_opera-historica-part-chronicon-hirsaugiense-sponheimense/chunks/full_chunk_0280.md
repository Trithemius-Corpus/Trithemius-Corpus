The duke said to him, “Who, then, are you?” To him the spirit replied, “Do not ask who I am, because [unclear] has nothing to do with the matter; but mount this horse, which I offer you, and I will bring you, safe and unharmed, freed from here, back to Austria.” When he heard this, a certain horror seized the duke, otherwise a most daring man; and presently, when he fortified himself with the sign of the holy cross, the spirit disappeared together with the black horse which he had offered. He returned empty-handed to the one who had sent him, and, when reproached by him because he had not brought the captive, he narrated the course of what had happened. At length Duke Frederick, released from custody, confessed that this had happened to him in captivity on the recorded day. After this, Emperor Louis, having received an oath and the necessary guarantees from Duke Frederick that he would no longer involve himself in the kingdom, let him go free. He ruled alone for nearly thirty-three years, a strong man, exceedingly bold.

In the same year also Pope John XXII enrolled Thomas Aquinas, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a most acute theologian, in the catalogue of the saints, forty-eight years after his passing.

*1324.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Sigismund, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1324, Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, John, king of Bohemia, his nephew through his brother, who was then staying at Luxembourg, and also the duke of Lorraine and the count of Bar, together with certain other counts and nobles, entered into alliance with one another and with a great army besieged the city of Metz. Since they could not take it by storm, they laid waste everything round about by devastation.

In these times Emperor Louis, desiring to enlarge his dominion, violently took away the wife of a certain powerful man, and joined her in marriage, with pomp and solemnity, to his son Otto, margrave of Brandenburg; while her husband, with whom she had lived matrimonially for eleven years, protested and invoked the aid of the supreme pontiff, though in vain.
