We are treating a rustic Camena, and we sing, as far as cast-off scraps permit, a Muse suited to our purpose. We have completed a moderate-sized book on Saint Joseph the Christ-bearer. We have also brought the [Nepiachum?] of our Annals from the hour of the nativity down to this day. At the prayers of friends we have begun a great work of twelve books on demons, in which we have resolved to destroy all the profane arts of magicians, both in general and in particular, and to censure by name and properly their causes, inventors, books, methods, and deceptions. Amid these things we are also applying ourselves to the newly invented Steganography, which we do not yet promise to publish, being deterred by the malice of scoundrels. Someone used to say: “He who does what no one does, all admire.” But now one must say: “He who does something new, all disparage.”

From Würzburg, on the sixteenth day of the month of July, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*XXXIII.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St. James at Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, to Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate, brother and dearest friend, greetings.*

*Augsburg.* I had hoped, brother, that you would come to us from Augsburg, so that I might have explained to you the whole history of my affairs, lest you either accept false things as true about me, or appear ignorant of a friend’s fortunes. But since you have turned your journey back toward Austria, I have been compelled to do by letter what I had prepared to do in speech. Therefore this nephew of yours by your sister, Nicolaus Wilandt, has met me quite opportunely; through him you may obtain knowledge of the things that have happened around me. In the year of the Christians 1504, our most serene king of the Romans, Maximilian, with the help of many princes, declared a most savage war against the Palatine forces; and, after crossing the Rhine at Mainz and entering the Nagau, the Hessians with the [Wedreffs?] carried off everything found around Alzey, a town of the Gau, and Kreuznach, under the rule of the Palatine and of the duke of the [Cynonoti?], while churches, castles, and little villages, unless they were ransomed with much gold, were burned and destroyed. There was one frenzy and incredible [unclear] among all against the most merciful Palatine prince; they thought they would cover his name and memory with the shadows of hell. Terrified by fear of the Hessians and by dread and threats of the neighboring [Melantagori?], I was compelled to leave my monastery, which is subject by command of that Palatine to the equal authority of the duke of the [Cynonoti?], because Alexander, son of [Melantagus?], who has now long since performed the duties of life, had already then given his right hand to the Hessians and was bringing them aid, devastating all the Palatine countryside and sparing neither age nor religion; and against me in particular, through hatred of the most illustrious prince, the count of [Budorianus?], he had prepared [unclear]. I therefore betook myself to Kreuznach, where I remained for twenty-two weeks, for so long did the fury and madness of the [Spatic?] Hessians last.

Meanwhile, while these things were being done, two of my monks, [Melantius?] Remigius the prior, and Johannes [Pingionita?], together with some [petronibus?] who had been given, long accustomed to favor the party of the [Cynonoti?], began to assail me first with hidden hatred and then with open hatred. Above all they made hostile to me the duke of Transylvania, who rules the country people of the [Cynonoti?], inventing many things, the principal charge of which in sum was that they always grieved that I had very strongly favored the Palatine interest. All the rest, however, was contrived and falsely invented. Then in the following year, which was the year of the Christians 1505, on the first day of the month of April, summoned to [Budoricm?] by command of the prince, I betook myself there on account of the monastery of Limburg, which had been utterly burned by the wicked enemies of the Palatine. While I was necessarily staying there for some time, the [Cynonoti?], by order of the duke, invaded the monastery, and, seizing certain members of the household in hatred of me, violated ecclesiastical liberty with reckless daring. When these things had been reported to me at [Budorim?], they indeed displeased me greatly; and, after taking counsel with friends, I resolved to defer my return to my monastery until a fitting expiation should follow so great a wrongdoing. I therefore remained at [Budoris?] and [Neometis?] for three months, until the assembly of princes with the royal majesty at Cologne, whither I went down at the beginning of July, summoned by Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg and prince elector, and I stayed there also for five weeks. From there, at the insistence of that most illustrious prince himself, I entered the March after him.
