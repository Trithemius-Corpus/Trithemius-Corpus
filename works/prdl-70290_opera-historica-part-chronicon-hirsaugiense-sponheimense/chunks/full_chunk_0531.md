*Sponheim.*

*1484.*

In the first year of Johannes Trithemius, our abbot, Albert, administrator of Mainz, a young man of good promise, died in May and was buried in the church of Mainz itself. After his death the canons of the greater church, on the twentieth day of the month of May, unanimously elected as archbishop Berthold of Henneberg, dean of the aforesaid church, a prudent man, outstanding in counsel and deed, who today governs his church vigorously and is spending the eighteenth year of his pontificate in peace and tranquility.

*Berthold becomes archbishop of Mainz. Johannes becomes prior of this place, the 34th.*

In the same year Abbot Trithemius appointed as the thirty-fifth prior of this monastery Johannes of Tonna, a Thuringian by birth, first a monk of the monastery of Gerode and later of this place: indeed a good and disciplined man, but without vigor, and for the most part more clouded in brain than was proper. Therefore he could not persevere long in the priorate, because he did not know the measure that should have been distributed to each man according to place and time.

In the year noted above also, on Monday after the octave of Easter, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of this monastery, had the altar in the sacristy consecrated in honor of St. Anne, mother of the most blessed Mother of God, of St. Jerome the priest, of St. Benedict the abbot, of St. Scrutatius, bishop of the Tungri, and of St. Barbara, virgin and martyr, by Berthold, bishop of [Pariadensis?], vicar in pontificals of Albert of Mainz, then administrator and still living; in that altar many relics of the saints were, according to custom, deposited.

In the same year the Friars Minor of the Observance began to inhabit the place and monastery for them in Kreuznach, built by Duke Frederick, count in Sponheim, formerly residing in [Siernem?], as we said above, in the ninth year of Abbot Johannes Kolnhausen.

In this same year there grew a very great abundance of wine throughout the whole land, such as, or as great as, had not existed in human memory. This abundance was first observed at the very time of the vintage, and because of it a great shortage of vessels arose. For one vessel, bought for three florins, which they call a [plausitale?], contained only one florin’s worth of wine, and a wagonload of the best wine was sold for one florin of common money, although the vessel had been bought for three gold coins. For wine had become so cheap in this year that some of the peasants, disregarding water, mixed the mortar of walls with wine; others, so that they might draw new wine, poured the old out upon the ground; and some did not even take care to gather it all, because of the excessive abundance. And for us, as is known, there grew in this year one hundred wagonloads of wine, something which, in human memory, happened neither before nor after; the shortage of the following year offset this abundance. For the ingratitude of the people deserved the vengeance of God, as the matter itself showed.

*1485.*

In the second year of Johannes our abbot, in mid-Lent, on Laetare Sunday, Berthold, elected and confirmed as archbishop of Mainz, was consecrated by Johannes Camerarius Dalburg, bishop of Worms, at Mainz in the principal church, with Ludovicus of Speyer and Berthold of [Panadensis?], bishops, standing by, and with many abbots also present: Johannes Trithemius of this monastery, of St. James, in Blidenstadt, in Seligenstadt, in Amorbach, in Limburg, in Otterberg, on Mount St. Disibod, and many others, with great pomp and solemnity. When the office had been completed, they were all called to the table [unclear].

In the same year, on the third Sunday after the feast of Easter, the twenty-fifth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated in the monastery of St. Ulrich at Augsburg. Presiding there were Johannes of St. Egidien in Nuremberg, Johannes Trithemius of this our monastery, Heinrich in Weissenburg, and Paul in Elchingen, abbots of monasteries of our order; and in that same chapter many things were established for the preservation of the order.

At about this time the monastery in Salza, in the diocese of Strasbourg, at the urging of apostate monks who sought this by their cooperation, was transferred from the order into a secular college, and from bad monks they were made worse canons. Violating without cause the vow made to God, they will find the wages of their crime.

In this same year God punished the ingratitude shown to him by the peasants in the preceding year with a harsh enough chastisement, since as great as the abundance of wine had been in that year, so great a shortage followed in the next. For very little wine grew that year. And, to measure the others by ourselves: we, who in the preceding year had had one hundred wagonloads, scarcely received one in this year from all the growths, tithes, and revenues together.
