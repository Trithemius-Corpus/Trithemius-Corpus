After him came his son Duke Liudolf, who completed the work begun by his father. The first abbess of the same monastery was Hadmodis, the elder daughter of that same Duke Liudolf; she presided over the virgins of Christ for twenty-two years, in great holiness of life and conduct. When she died, her sister, named Gerburga, succeeded in the abbacy for 14 years, herself also a most holy virgin, who shone forth in life and after death with many bright virtues. When she too was at last taken from their midst, a third daughter of Duke Liudolf was elected abbess, named Christina; by living religiously in the holy resolve of virginity she did not depart from the holiness of the sisters who had preceded her. The same Duke Liudolf also built another monastery of virgins in Saxony, called Brunshausen, and obtained confirmation of both from Pope Nicholas, together with many relics of saints. In these times there flourished from the monastic order many most excellent bishops, distinguished for holiness and knowledge of letters, and also abbots and monks very celebrated both for life and for learning in the Scriptures, who published many and varied volumes in literary study for the edification of posterity.

Abbo, from being abbot of the monastery of Saint Germanus, became bishop of Auxerre; his brother Helibald had been bishop there before him. Both were energetic and most holy men, and nobly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular knowledge. Christianus, also called Druthmar, a monk of Corbie and a venerable priest, composed in these times many volumes in exposition of the divine Scriptures. Of him we have made fuller mention in other books on ecclesiastical writers. Egil, abbot of the monastery of Prum in the parish of Trier, was appointed in the first year of Gerung: a noble and very learned man, a friend of kings and princes, who acquired a very great name of praise in regular observance.

*855.*

In the second year of Abbot Gerung, which was the year of the Lord 855, the Emperor Lothar, worn out with age, summoned the leading men of the realm and divided the empire among his sons: to Louis he entrusted Italy, and appointed him emperor; to Lothar he granted the kingdom which, from his name, we have said was called Lotharingia; and to Charles, who was the younger by birth, he gave the kingdom of Provence. When these matters had been duly arranged, bidding farewell to his people he left the world and entered the monastery of Prum under the aforesaid Abbot Egil, and was made a monk. Having lived laudably in holy religion according to the Rule of our most holy father Benedict until the end, he at last died most happily on 29 September, and was buried there. Therefore, after Lothar became a monk, his son Louis reigned as emperor for twenty-one years.

*856.*

In the third year of Abbot Gerung, which was the year of the Lord 856, King Lothar, brother of the Emperor Louis, took as wife Theutberga, daughter of his uncle Charles the Bald, later emperor. But not long afterward he repudiated her and brought in Waldrada, his former concubine, sister of Gunther, archbishop of Cologne, from which union [unclear]. In the same year Rabanus, archbishop of Mainz, died, and in the monastery of Saint [unclear]; for in a time of famine, which in the thirteenth year of Abbot Lintbert had oppressed all Germany, he relieved the need of the maimed. For at that time he was staying in a certain estate in his parish, where, receiving those coming from various places, he daily sustained with food more than three hundred, from among those who were constantly fed in his presence. One day, among the other beggars, there came a certain woman, almost consumed by starvation, with a very small boy; before [unclear] she breathed her last. The child, drawing out the breast of his dead mother as she lay there, as though she still lived, and trying to suck, compelled all who looked on to groan and weep. There followed him [unclear].

*The invasion of the Northmen. The districts are destroyed by the Northmen.*

*Note his mercy in misfortunes.*

Around these times the Northmen, together with the Danes, landing with fleets in Holland in a great multitude, utterly destroyed the very famous village called Dorestad, in which there are remembered to have been fifty-five parish churches, after all its inhabitants had been killed. Then, taking the city of Utrecht, they destroyed it, cruelly killing utterly all the people together with the whole clergy. Hunger, bishop of Utrecht at that time, escaping in flight with a few canons, came to Lothar, formerly emperor, in the monastery of Prum.
