*Dionysius, bishop of Milan, is sent into exile into Armenia.*

... he pricked the idle [unclear], and recalled the proud and restless to penitence. Meanwhile, while he was intent upon these holy works, it happened that blessed Dionysius, archbishop of the church of Milan, because he refused to subscribe to the blasphemies of the Arians, was relegated by command of the emperors to exile in Armenia, namely to that city of Rediciana, over which Saint Aurelius was set as bishop. This relegation of the most holy man Dionysius was made by the impious bishops of the Arian faction under Constantine and Constans, emperors, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 361, Liberius presiding as pope over the Roman church; and he himself also was sent into exile in the same year.

But Saint Aurelius received blessed Dionysius, when he came into his city Rediciana, as a true disciple of Christ, with great joy, and after [unclear]. *The greatest charity between them.* When he heard [unclear], “Brother, restrain your tears; the earth is the Lord’s and the fullness thereof, and the whole world is a homeland for a Christian.” Bishop Dionysius was received by blessed Aurelius with the greatest reverence, and provided for by him with no less kindness of hospitality and generosity; and in that same city he remained, relegated in exile, until his death. Therefore, when the habit of mutual association had been established, a great friendship and familiarity arose between them, which then began in time and now remains and is perfected in eternity.

So great, indeed, was the holiness of them both that their fame ran through all Armenia and drew all people into admiration. The sick flowed together to them and were cured of long-standing illnesses; those also possessed by demons were freed by the prayers of these men. For almighty God deigned to work such miracles through these servants of his that, as a merciful father, he converted all Armenia to his worship by their virtue and teaching. Finally Saint Aurelius, by his teaching and preaching, most diligently warned each of the faithful to flee the pestilence of the Arian heresy and to remain with the church of the saints in the faith of the Holy Trinity.

Although blessed Saint Dionysius was held under guard, as exiles were customarily kept at that time, nevertheless access to him was open to Saint Aurelius whenever he wished; and they were joined to one another by such great affection that they agreed both to live together while alive and to be buried together when dead. But after some years, when blessed Dionysius saw that death was near him, he addressed blessed Aurelius in these words, saying: “I know, dearest brother, that the end of my living in this world is at hand, and the time of my dissolution is now near. I have finished the course; I have kept the faith. In the present age there remains one thing that I ask of you: when I have died, as soon as you can, carry my body back to my Milanese church, so that the church which could not have me alive may at least deserve to have me dead. Let not the inconvenience of a long journey frighten you; the Lord will be with you, and your reward will be very great.” Saint Aurelius, bound to him by great affection and love, consented and promised that he would do what was asked.

And so the holy confessor of Christ, indeed also martyr, Dionysius, having completed the order of his life, died on the eighth day before the Kalends of June, in the year of the Lord 379. There are those who write that he was relegated to Cappadocia, died there, and that his relics were sent back to Milan to Saint Ambrose through Saint Basil the bishop.
