Understanding, therefore, that it would not be sufficiently safe for me henceforth to associate with rivals and dog-eared scoundrels, I decided to move myself to other, safer places. Accordingly, poor as I was, on the fifteenth day of the last month of October, I accepted this poor abbey of St James at Wuerzburg, offered to me by lord Lawrence, the most reverend prelate, and afterwards of my own free will resigned Sponheim with its most ungrateful monks. Let the men of darkness and the rivals now fare as they may, those to whom our holy studies were not pleasing; and we, with the Psalmist, shall sing gladly: The snare of useless occupations has been broken, and we have been freed. For I do not regret what has been done, since, having obtained a quiet place, I now seem to myself for the first time to have found a beginning for philosophizing. I left my whole library at Sponheim, the loss of which I do not greatly esteem, since by now I have learned in large part to philosophize without it. The most illustrious Prince Palatine had been ready to provide me with the necessities of life as long as I should live, and no less the aforesaid Margrave of Brandenburg, if I had wished it; but it did not please me to live among courtly flatterers. I therefore chose, poor, to dwell in a monastery rather than, rich, to live in the world contrary to the monastic rule. You now have, most loving brother, the history of my calamity written in summary form; from reading it you may recognize that men are not to be trusted rashly. These things were done to me by those who seemed friends; these things sons perpetrated against a father. But I fear that the evil which they prepared for me may meet them in their own draughts, and that they may fall into the pit which they made. My abbey of Sponheim Prior Melantius has obtained; in it, without doubt, he will receive a share of the injuries done to me, which he has prepared by his own desert. Our Vigilius, Maecenas of philosophers, has obtained a canonry at Worms, but since the clergy has not yet returned to the city, he is staying at [unclear]. Farewell, and write to me more often. Again farewell. From Wuerzburg, on the first day of the month of July, in the year of the Christians 1507.

But enough about these matters. For what swine will teach Minerva? Let us therefore come to the matter. Celtis, and especially Peutinger, have asked me to write to you and ascertain whether you were minded, once a stipend had been obtained from our most serene King, to betake yourself to Augsburg; for they would exert themselves so that an honorable condition might be offered to you. They offer both a house and very many other things, by the possession of which you may be able to philosophize at leisure, far from all noise. It will therefore be for you to consider what you must do, and then to inform me, so that I may be able to write to our friends about this matter. Be assured that I shall strive earnestly to attend to everything that is to your advantage. Farewell, and love me as one most devoted to you. From Nuremberg, on the Kalends of July, in the year of the Lord 1507.
