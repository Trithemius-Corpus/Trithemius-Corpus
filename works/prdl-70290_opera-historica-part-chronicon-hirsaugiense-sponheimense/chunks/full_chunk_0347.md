*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In this same year Brother Conrad of Marburg, of the Order of Preachers, began to preach in Germany by apostolic authority; he continued for nineteen years, and caused many, indeed almost countless, persons to be burned as heretics, and he himself was at last killed.

1215. In this year, which was the second year of lord Iuanus the abbot, many princes, meeting at Aachen, anointed Frederick, king of Sicily, grandson of the emperor Frederick I, as king of the Romans, through Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, and placed him on the royal seat, Emperor Otto having been abandoned and left almost by everyone. When the consecration had been completed, John Scholasticus of Xanten at once preached the cross there for the relief of the Holy Land. Hence there were soon signed with the cross Frederick the king himself, Sifrid archbishop of Mainz and legate, also the bishops of Liege, Bamberg, Poitiers, and Strasbourg, likewise the duke of Moravia, the duke of Brabant, Henry duke of Limburg, the duke of [Ancei?], the count palatine of Tubingen, the margrave of Baden, the count of Los, the count of Julich, the count of Sayn, the count of [Monte?], also John count of Sponheim, and many other nobles, clerics and laymen; and a great army of horsemen and foot-soldiers was signed. In the same year, on the fifth day before the Kalends of September, around the first hour of the day, there was an earthquake.

1217. In this year Iuanus the abbot, with lord John count of Sponheim and many other pilgrims signed with the cross, crossed the sea and went to the Holy Land. At the same time William count of Holland and George count of Wied also went with them, with a great crowd of those signed with the cross. Andrew king of Hungary also, and Leopold duke of Austria, with bishops and counts and a numerous multitude of those signed with the cross, crossed the sea at the same time and set out for the aid of the Holy Land. Moreover Iuanus committed the governance of the monastery to Riginfrid the prior, whom in the preceding year he had substituted after John had been deposed. He was a prudent and noble man, and administered the office of the priorship vigorously for twenty-two years, and maintained good discipline.

1218. In this year Iuanus the abbot and John count of Sponheim returned from the regions overseas and entered the monastery on the eighth day before the Kalends of April. Lord Iuanus the abbot brought back with him many small portions of relics of the saints, and especially from those places which the Lord Jesus sanctified by his bodily presence, stones and pieces of rock cut out; all these holy things he enclosed in a fabricated [unclear].

In these times the monastery of nuns of the Cistercian order in the valley of Saint Catherine, virgin and martyr, near Mandal, was built and endowed through the munificence of Udo, pastor in Mandal and archpriest, and of his associates; he, warned by the vision of the aforesaid deceased [unclear], gave to that place everything he was able to have. A copy of this foundation follows, and is as follows.

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. Amen. Sifrid, by the grace of God archbishop of the see of Mainz: by the tenor of the present document let it be known to all the faithful of Christ that the beloved sons Gosfrid, provost of Creutzenacht, Udo archpriest of Mannendal, Frederick dean of Hilbersheim, and their fellow brethren, having in their hearts zeal for God, brought their properties together into one, and from them founded the church of Saint Catherine of the Cistercian order. Therefore, arranging that these goods be stripped of every burden of administrators and of service by any [unclear] whatsoever, the advocates, if they held any right in them, renounced their right. Since, however, we are bound to strengthen this donation of property by our authority, so that it may be lawful for those dwelling there justly and lawfully to possess both this property and the things belonging to it, we confirm it by the authority with which we are invested, and we decree that the order of monastic religion, according to the rule of the blessed father Benedict and the institution of the Cistercian brethren, shall always be observed inviolably by those dwelling there. We also firmly decree and establish this for them: that what is known to have been granted by lord Pope Alexander to all of their religious profession, and by several pontiffs of the Roman see, namely that from newly broken lands, enclosed places brought under cultivation by their own labor and at their own expense, and also from gardens and from the nourishment of their animals, they shall pay no tithes to anyone; but they shall enjoy this indulgence granted to them. We have also ordained that the aforesaid overseers of the monastery, in selling or buying the goods of the church, or in admitting sisters or lay converts, shall do nothing without the consent of the abbess and convent; and that whatever is brought or sent to the aforesaid church from remote or neighboring parts shall be kept in a place appointed for this, under the custody of the abbess, the provost, and the servants. The household utensils with their appurtenances, namely wool and dairy produce, shall be kept under the custody of the ladies. The abbess and convent shall arrange nothing in the aforesaid matters without the counsel of the provost; the provost, however, shall dispose of nothing without them.
