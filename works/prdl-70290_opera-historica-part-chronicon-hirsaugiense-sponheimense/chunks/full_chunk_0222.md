*1217. Lupoldus.*

In the first year of Abbot Eberhard, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1217, Lupoldus, bishop of Worms, died. Caesarius, monk of Heisterbach, mentions him in the second distinction of his Dialogues, chapter nine, bearing witness to his wicked manner of life. After his death Henry, provost of Neuhausen, son of the count of Saarbrücken, succeeded; he presided for nearly seventeen years.

In the same year Andrew, king of Hungary, Lupoldus, duke of Austria, with many bishops, counts, nobles, and a numerous multitude of those signed with the cross, crossed the sea for love of Christ in aid of the Holy Land. Likewise William, most powerful count of Holland, George, count of Wied, John, count of Spanheim, with Juvanus, sixth abbot of my monastery, and many other men of Germany signed with the cross, set out to recover the Holy Land. Among them were also the bishop of Bamberg, his brother the duke of Merania, Poppo also, count of Henneberg, Ludovicus, count of Oettingen, and one cardinal, legate of the apostolic see.

*1218.*

In the second year of Abbot Eberhard, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity [1218], on Palm [Sunday], the duke of Austria, Lupoldus, with the pope’s legate, the Templars, the Hospitallers, and a numerous army, approaching the borders of Egypt, besieged for some time the very wealthy city of Damietta. By storming it on the vigil of Saint Bartholomew they took the very strongly fortified tower beside the shore of the Nile, which is called the Key of the Land; afterwards they also obtained the city, which however they could not hold for long, as is read in their acts.

In the same year Berthold, duke of Zähringen, died, a strong and warlike man, who in his life waged many wars with adversaries.

Also in the same year the sea, passing beyond its bounds in Phrygia and going out far, occupied the land in length round about, destroyed entire villages, and overthrew churches, houses, castles, and innumerable towns; it consumed more than one hundred thousand people. There are those who write that the cause of this flood was a certain irreverence shown to the venerable sacrament of the Lord’s body by a drunken man, who had struck the hosts from the pyx in the priest’s hand to the ground with his fist; hence divine vengeance raged.

*1219.*

In the third year of Abbot Eberhard, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1219, Jerusalem, queen of cities, which seemed impregnable, was destroyed by Coradinus, son of Saphadinus. Its walls, inside and outside, together with its towers, were reduced to heaps of stones, except for the temple of the Lord, the church of the Holy Sepulchre, and the citadel which they call the Tower of David.

In the same year King Frederick set out for Rome with the greatest display for the imperial blessing; around the feast of Blessed Cecilia he was anointed and crowned emperor by Pope Honorius. Proceeding further into Apulia, with a great army of his men he waged many wars there and subjected all the land, together with Sicily, to his empire. Immediately dissensions followed between him and Pope Honorius, which lasted for many years to the loss of both parties.

Also in the same year the duke of Lorraine, with the consent or connivance of the bishop of Strasbourg, entered Alsace with a strong army, intending to take the vengeance he had planned upon certain men who had injured him.

*1220.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Eberhard, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1220, Emperor Frederick, being established in Apulia, waged many wars with the neighboring Saracens; after many had been cut down and killed, he compelled the rest to be subject to his lordship. Henry, his son, a boy of about ten years, meanwhile was said to govern the kingdom of Germany in his father’s absence through Otto, bishop of Würzburg, to whose guardianship he had been entrusted, with the cooperation of Engelbert, archbishop of Cologne.
