*The Chronicle of Trithemius, 1498*

In the fifteenth year of Johannes Trithemius, our abbot, a great war arose between the king of the Romans and the whole Roman Empire, and the confederates who are called the Suitenses or Swiss; many thousands of men fell there on both sides. At length, after many losses had been suffered on both sides, concord was made between the king and the Swiss. This pleased not many people, especially the Swabian nobles of the Swabian League, which the emperor had established ten years before.

In the same year the tomb of Saint Hildegard, virgin, on Mount Saint Rupert was opened with the greatest reverence, in the presence of the whole convent there and of others appointed for this purpose, one of whom was Johannes Trithemius, our abbot. By his prayers he obtained the arm of the same holy virgin from the mistress and convent, for whom he had done many good services in the time of their tribulations. He had these sacred relics, together with others, enclosed in a certain panel.

At nearly the same time the abbot and monks of our order in Blidenstatt, in the diocese of Mainz, by a dispensation of the supreme pontiff obtained through false accounts, apostatized from the order, having no other cause than perverse will and hatred of regular discipline; from dissolute monks they became still more shameful clerics. An evil matter, without doubt contrary to God. Therefore let both those who consent to, and those who assume, such an unjust transfer consider what they will one day answer to God.

In the year noted above a great assembly of princes with Maximilian, king of the Romans, was held in the town of Freiburg for many days. There many matters were discussed for the peace and advantage of the commonwealth, but no result, or only a small one, followed. Many counsels were held about moving war against the Turks, about continuing the judgment of the imperial Chamber, instituted at Worms three years earlier, and about correcting rebels; but because, the sinews of money being lacking, the body of the Empire was not sufficient for especially difficult wars. Many agreed in words to contribute, but in deeds many did not fear to do the contrary.

In the same year also Wilhelm, provincial count of Hesse, took as wife the daughter of the most illustrious prince Philip, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria. The wedding was celebrated in Frankfurt with a very great gathering of princes and nobles.

In this same year there was a great tumult of wars in the duchy of Guelders. The king of the Romans in his own person, with other princes, received many fortifications in surrender; but when the war of the Swiss unexpectedly supervened, as we shall say in the following year, he was compelled to leave the people of Guelders and move his army against the Helvetians. Then the people of Guelders again defected from the king to their duke.

*1499*

In the sixteenth year of Johannes Trithemius, our abbot, the thirtieth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Stephen at Wurzburg, on the fourth Sunday after the feast of Easter. Presiding over it were the same Johannes Trithemius, who delivered an extemporaneous oration and was leader of the word; Andreas of Monchsberg, Johannes of Saint Michael in Hildesheim, and Thomas in Seligenstadt, abbots of monasteries of our order of the observance.

In the same year the theological faculty of the nourishing University of Cologne established and decreed, by a most firm constitution, that it was to be held, thought, believed, preached, and most constantly defended by all its subjects: that the blessed Mother of God, the Virgin Mary, by the singular prerogative of divine power, has always been preserved, exempted, and privileged from every stain of original sin. Whoever should do the contrary was to be cut off from the University itself and entirely deprived for all time of the honors and emoluments of the theological faculty; and no one was to be promoted in that faculty unless he swore that he would always observe this statute.

In the year noted above, on the Monday after Palm Sunday, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of this monastery, having been asked by letter by Arnold Bostius, a Carmelite staying at Ghent, a most learned and most religious man, what he was writing, and intending to reply to him as to a true friend, wrote back that he had in hand a great work of Steganography, at the request of a certain prince, which had not previously been known to any men, as the work itself proves. But before the letter came to Ghent, Bostius himself, to whom it had been written, on the day before the Nones of April, that is, on the fifth day after the feast of Easter, Trithemius not knowing it, migrated to the Lord. Therefore the prior of the convent at Ghent opened the letter sent to Bostius, read it, and everywhere proclaimed it as a miracle; within a short space of time it spread through all Gaul and Germany [unclear]
