*Sponheim.*

*1168.*

In the seventeenth year of Abbot Craffto, Arnold, archbishop of Mainz, was cruelly killed by his own citizens in the monastery of St James near Mainz, on the day of St John the Baptist, namely [the eighth day before the Kalends?] of July. The leaders of this conspiracy from the clergy were Gotfridus, abbot of St James and provost of St Peter, with many others. When the slaughter had been carried out, Abbot Gotfridus, with the rest of the accomplices of his impiety, immediately withdrew into the city. They compelled the clergy and elected Rodolph, son of Conrad, duke of Zähringen, as archbishop. When he wished to appease the emperor with gifts, he stripped the church of all its adornment; but the emperor despised both him and his gifts.

But Conrad, count palatine of the Rhine, the emperor’s brother by one parent, drove out Rodolph and raised a certain Christian to the archbishopric; he nevertheless did not on that occasion attain the bishopric. For the emperor, rejecting both of them, raised Conrad, brother of Otto, count palatine of Wittelsbach, to the archbishopric. After two years he also expelled him, as will be said there, and caused Christian to be ordained in his place.

Moreover, after Arnold had been killed, as has been said, in the monastery of St James, that place itself was destroyed, all the dwellings of the monks were overturned, the monks were put to flight, and the abbot was banished from the country. And so for twelve years no divine offices were celebrated there. The monks who had been put to flight betook themselves to various monasteries; of these, four were sustained here for a long time out of fraternal charity, six in the monastery of St Disibod, and others were received in other places. The wall of the city of Mainz also, by command of the emperor, was destroyed all around, so that Mainz stood without a wall for thirty-six years. But all the citizens who had consented to the death of the archbishop, except the poor and beggars, fled before the emperor’s return from Italy.

*1170.*

In the nineteenth year of Abbot Craffto, Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, bound himself by oath to Pope Alexander. Wherefore the emperor, angered, expelled him from the archbishopric and from the country, and compelled him to flee to the pope; the pope gave him the bishopric of Sabina. Hence, for as long as he afterwards lived, he styled himself archbishop of Mainz and bishop of Sabina. After he had been expelled, the emperor raised Christian, who had been elected before him, as we have said, to the archbishopric. He presided for thirteen years. When he died, Conrad returned to the church of Mainz, as we shall say below in its place.

*1183.*

In his twenty-second year, Lord Abbot Craffto covered the head of St Irmina the virgin, which at the beginning of his promotion he had obtained from Hillin, archbishop of Trier, with a beautiful silver reliquary, and adorned it all around with precious stones, for the adornment of the church. But after many years, when the condition of temporal affairs had collapsed greatly through the bad governance of the abbots and the wicked life of the monks, under Craffto the second of this name the silver head itself was sold, and a copper one, which is seen today, was substituted.

*1175.*

Craffto, the second abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, son of Count Megenhard and our founder, died this year on the fifth day before the Kalends of June, and was buried in the same tomb with Bernhelm, his predecessor, in the middle of the choir: a man in every respect religious and most holy, who did many good things for this monastery and acquired many possessions for the support of the brothers. On his tomb verses are seen carved, which, with the names of both men set before them, sound thus in prose:

Illustrious abbots, to whom it has fallen to be entombed here,
Both dear to God, I am now in equal peace.

Anselm the prior is believed to have made these little verses, because he was a studious and learned man. Abbot Craffto died, released by the disease of dysentery, in the fifty-eighth year of his age, and in the twenty-fourth year and second month of his abbacy.
