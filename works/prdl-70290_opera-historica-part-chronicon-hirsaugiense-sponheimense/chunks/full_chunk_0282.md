But when Peter was created, not as pontiff but as antipope, he created as cardinals and bishops men very like himself, and in all things conducted himself as a true pope. But after the emperor’s return into Germany, the antipope, left at Pisa, was captured by Boniface, count of Pisa, and brought to Avignon to Pope John, where, cast into prison, he exchanged life for death; although he acknowledged his error, nevertheless he could [not] be freed.

In the same year Gotfrid, bishop of Würzburg, died. He was succeeded by Wolfram of Grumbach, forty-sixth in the order of bishops, and he presided for six years, a man very well suited to rule, who compelled his citizens to every kind of subjection.

In the same year also Matthias, archbishop of Mainz, celebrated a provincial synod of his suffragans, abbots, and prelates, in which he issued many constitutions for the benefit of the Church.

*1328.*

In the second year of Abbot Sigismund, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1328, in the second indiction, on the Ides of September, Matthias, archbishop of Mainz, of the Order of Saint Benedict, born of Buseck, died, a man upright in [unclear] conduct and worthy of every honor and perpetual memory; and he was buried in the greater church of Mainz. After his death, the clergy again postulated Baldwin, archbishop of Trier; but since he was unwilling to abandon his own church of Trier, which he had already made wealthy, Pope John XXII gave the church of Mainz to Henry of Virneburg without the consent of the canons, and, having ordained him archbishop, sent him to Mainz with apostolic letters and mandates. But the canons, alleging many crimes against him, appealed to the Apostolic See. Therefore, while they litigated for three years before the pope in Avignon, lest the church of Mainz, again deprived of a pastor, incur grave losses, the pope, at their request, entrusted that church of Mainz to the aforesaid Baldwin, archbishop, in the name of administrator until the outcome of the suit.

After he had been appointed administrator, he took into his power the towns, castles, and fortifications of the whole diocese, and he began to build everything anew from the foundations, strongly and with great diligence, against the incursions of the wicked; and he surrounded the village itself on every side with a wall. Meanwhile the people of Mainz, fearing, as they said, a siege by Baldwin, but in fact [unclear] because of ecclesiastical liberty, with the former defending their rights and liberties, while the latter, on the other side, labored to infringe and violate everything.

*Baldwin becomes administrator of the church of Mainz.*

The castle called Eltville, situated two miles from Mainz down the river Rhine, he defended with wisdom. In these days there was great dissension between the clergy and the citizens of Mainz because of ecclesiastical liberty; and they destroyed in great part the notable buildings and structures of Saint Alban the Martyr, of Saint James the Apostle on the Fair Mountain, and of Saint Victor, belonging to secular canons, together with the castle in Weisenau, monasteries of our order distinguished by buildings and structures, as the traces especially in the monastery of Saint Alban show down to the present day.

*Henry, archbishop of Mainz.*

The monastery of Saint James, however, because it was very near the city, they utterly overthrew, together with its distinguished church. For this rashness they were afterward summoned to the emperor’s chamber, and by judicial sentence condemned to pay a very great sum of money to those monasteries. The monasteries were rebuilt in the course of time, but they did not attain their former splendor. From that time, however, until the capture of the city, which took place in the third year of Wignand, abbot of this monastery, with the power avenging the wrong done to the saints, fortune was always adverse to the people of Mainz. With God avenging the wrong done to the saints, fortune was always [unclear] to the people of Mainz, until at last, having lost their liberty, they were reduced to perpetual servitude.
