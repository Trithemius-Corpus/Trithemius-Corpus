*Sponheimense.*

No abbess or provost shall be appointed except the woman or man whom the convent by common consent, or the sounder part of the council, according to the fear of God and the Rule of blessed Benedict, shall have elected. We also decree that an abbot shall be chosen by the same convent only from their order, for preserving the rigor of the order, or for deciding questions for which the convent shall seem to him insufficient; and he, according to the mandate of our institution, ought not to oppose the election made of him. The lay brothers shall promise obedience to the provost, and the sisters to the abbess, and shall render it according to the Rule of blessed Benedict without murmuring. We likewise command that lay brothers departing from the aforesaid place without due permission shall not be received in any places.

Whoever therefore, with any rash boldness, which God forbid, shall presume to act against this page of our constitution, and shall endeavor to break or disturb the ordinance noted above, secured and confirmed by the impression of our seal, let them recognize that they will incur, unless they make answer, the indignation of almighty God and of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, condemned by him with anathema, by the authority of lord Pope Honorius and our own. These things were done in the year of the Lord’s Incarnation 1219, in the eighth indiction, when lord Honorius III presided over the apostolic see, and lord Frederick II, most glorious king of the Romans, ever Augustus, and king of Sicily, was reigning; under these witnesses: Henry, major provost; Poppo, dean; Gotfrid, custodian; Christian, cantor; Henry, provost of Burg; Albert of Cogelenberg; Frederick of Firnenberg, canons of the major church of Mainz; Luano of Sponheim; Hartvernus of Mount St. Disibod, abbots; Henry, provost of Struvabenheim; Philip of Oterburg, abbots; Mechthild, son of the count of Sponheim, with his brothers; Godebold of Wierbach and Micho his brother; Wulfram of Stein; Marquard and Barthold of Strumberg, knights; and very many others, both clerics and laymen, worthy of trust; also Diepold of Eberbach, Eastachius of Hennenrode, Philip of Oterburg, abbots; Mechthild, abbess of the same place, in the 121st year from the beginning of the Cistercian order, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of June, in the twentieth year from the beginning of the house in the valley, was absolved from excommunication and interdict.

In the same year also King Frederick, having held an assembly at Frankfurt, set out for Rome, and was invested as emperor by Pope Honorius III.

*1231.*

In this year Count Silvester Conrad, commonly Wiligraf of Dhaun, was excommunicated by Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, and his land was placed under interdict, on account of the advocacy in Heimbach, which he had violently invaded against the will of Henry, major provost, and the canons of the church of Mainz. At last, agreement having been made between them, and one hundred marks having been received from the canon, the count resigned the advocacy of the church, and was absolved from the sentence of excommunication and interdict.

In the same year Worms was burned on the day of St. Margaret the Virgin, and many houses by a certain knight of Beckelnheim, who was called Henry Spon, which Crafftobas III afterward alienated from the monastery, redeemable by the procurator for Rhenish florins, as will be said in its place.

In the same year Abbot John, in the eighth year of his rule, bought a manor for twenty-five marks of silver from [unclear]; especially around the market and in the adjoining streets they were burned. In those days Bishop Henry, son of the count of Saarbruecken, was there. In the same year also the Friars Minor entered Worms and built a monastery.

*1232.*

In this year, which was the ninth of Abbot John, Conrad, bishop of Speyer, hearing that the nuns of our order in the monastery called Haulen near the monastery of Limburg in his diocese had slipped too far from the path of regular discipline, and had failed no less in temporal and necessary goods than in spiritual conduct, to such an extent that they were compelled to relieve their poverty by shameful traffic, decreed with the consent of Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, that henceforth no one should be admitted to the order there; and he incorporated that monastery, with all its revenues, into the monastery of Limburg, so that, when all the nuns of that place had died, the abbot of Limburg should have divine services celebrated there by his monks.

*1234.*

In the eleventh year of John there were still only four nuns, sisters, in our enclosure. Our nuns [unclear] passed over to the monastery of St. Rupert. All the others having died in the course of time, and no one being admitted any longer, according to what Abbot Rupert had decreed almost eighteen years before, as was said above. Hence, with the license and authority of John the abbot, the four who remained were transferred to the monastery of St. Rupert near Bingen, with all their possessions, and there were received into the convent. Their names are these: Agatha, Radegundis, Helwigis, and Margaret. And thus the place of our nuns—
