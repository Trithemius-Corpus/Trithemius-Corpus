*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In the same year also Johannes Thoringus, the thirty-fourth prior of this monastery, because of his importunity, since he was unable to be at rest in the office of the priorate, was deposed at his own excessive insistence; and Johannes de Dreysa, a Westphalian, formerly and afterward again a monk in Marienmünster, was appointed thirty-fifth prior of this place by Abbot Trithemius. He himself, unable suitably to moderate his behavior, although he was an old man, gray-haired, before he had completed two years in the priorate, was deposed for the sake of peace.

In the same year also Wilhelm of Arburg, who three years before had killed Johann of Bourbon, bishop of Liège, was captured by the brother of Johann of Horn, the succeeding bishop, taken to Maastricht by the Meuse, and beheaded, after peace had first been made between them, since nothing of the sort was suspected. Many evils followed in the whole land of Liège and the county of Horn, and many thousands of men were killed.

In this same year grain was being bought at a low price: for three malters of the best rye [unclear] after the necessary written records had been received. But the [inhabitants?] of the town of Bingen, a few days after the promise, forgetful of it, rashly violated the peace and concord, and again raised themselves up in arrogance against their lords, the canons of the greater church. When this had become known to the aforesaid archbishop, taking up nearly four hundred armed men, on the fifth day before the Kalends of December in the year noted above he came to Bingen; he was secretly admitted by a postern into the castle that lies next to the town, without the citizens knowing. And so, after those to be summoned had been called to him, and counsel had been held, he banished from the town certain of the citizens who had been leaders of the sedition, and forced the rest to obedience to their lords. Then, on the first day of the month of December, deposing the council, the scabini, and the judges, who had done many things wrongly, he appointed new ones. Lest frauds and deceptions continue in judicial business, he gave the city a common seal. For previously each judge or scabin had sealed with his own seal the case handled before him and reduced to writing, and many deceptions of the poor occurred.

In the same year Johannes Trithemius, abbot of this monastery, brought back into the possession of the monastery, as to one half, the estate in Nussbach, which Crafft the second, a knight, about one hundred years before had pledged for a fixed sum of money to his same kinsman, after it had been redeemed with much labor and expense; he could not obtain the other half, because the sale had been beyond the memory of men. That this estate of the monastery had been alienated under a certain title of repurchase, and not the other, could not be shown clearly enough.

In the same year also Johannes Trithemius, abbot, newly built the greater Stube, a guest-house, within the enclosure, and likewise altered the chambers. He also began to have made a new bell, which is rung at the elevation and at the brothers’ collation.

In this year wine, growing in small quantity, rose very greatly in price: for a cartload, which in the first year of Abbot Trithemius was sold for one florin, in this year could scarcely be bought for twenty gold Rhenish florins.

In the same year also, on the sixteenth day of the month of February, in the first week of Lent, Maximilian, duke of Austria, son of Emperor Frederick III, at the very great insistence of his father, was unanimously elected king of the Romans at Frankfurt, being in the eighteenth year of his age. The electors were Berthold, archbishop of Mainz; Johann, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria; Ernst of Saxony; and Albert, margrave of Brandenburg. After the feast of Easter the aforesaid princes, with many others, accompanying their chosen man with the greatest honor, led him to Aachen, and had him anointed and crowned according to custom by the hands of the aforesaid archbishop of Cologne, with the greatest pomp and solemnity, on the fifth day of April.

In the year noted above also Johannes Westphalus, the thirty-fifth prior of this monastery, was deposed because of the disorderliness of his conduct.
