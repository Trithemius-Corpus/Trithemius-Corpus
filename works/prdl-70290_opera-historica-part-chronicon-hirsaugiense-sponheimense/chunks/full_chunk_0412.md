*1331.*

In the twenty-second year of Willicho, our abbot, the noble John, count of Sponheim, at the request [unclear], freed the possessions belonging to the monastery in perpetuity from every service, contribution, exaction, and any burden, for himself and all his heirs, with the consent of Lord Simon his brother and of Walram his son, counts of Sponheim. For this liberty the same abbot Willicho and the convent of this monastery handed over to that count and his heirs, in ownership and perpetual possession, the mill in Rüdesheim, formerly in Gensingen, with all their revenues, as appears in the letters drawn up concerning this by the same count, strengthened with his own seal and with those of his brother and of his brother’s son aforesaid, and delivered to the monastery. Their date is the year of the Lord 1331, in the middle of Lent, on Laetare Sunday.

In the same year Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, bought from the abbot and convent of the monastery of St. Matthias the chapel of St. Alban with the adjoining garden outside the walls of Trier, and began to build there a monastery of the order, which he brought to its appointed proper completion within ten years. In the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1400 he consecrated the church of the same monastery.

The same archbishop also, around this time, built anew at great expense a stone bridge over the Moselle at Koblenz, decreeing that all people of the diocese of Trier, of whatever status or condition they might be, could pass freely over it. But of the rest, who, being from outside the diocese, wished to cross the bridge, each one, clerics excepted, was to pay one obol for its maintenance, which they call Hallenses from Halls.

*1332.*

In this year, on the seventh day before the Kalends of January, being the twenty-third year of Willicho, abbot of this monastery, Lord John, count of Sponheim, aforesaid, with the consent of his brother Count Simon and of Walram his son, leased to the abbot and convent of this monastery the whole jurisdiction and district, which he calls Bann, of the grist-mill in the villages of Sponheim and Bockenau, at a fixed tenure, that is, in perpetuity, according to the tenor of the letters which the same count had made concerning this and delivered to the abbot. Their date is the year of the Lord [1423?], on holy Easter day.

In the same year, on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of January, Gerlach Schenk, bishop of Worms, died. Salmann, a native of Mainz, succeeded him and presided for almost twenty-three years. *Salmann becomes bishop of Worms.*

In the same year also, on the seventh day before the Kalends of January, Henry, the eighteenth prior of this monastery of Sponheim, died, in the twenty-ninth year of his office and before the sixteenth [unclear] of his age had been completed: a good and devout man, a very notable lover of regular observance. Under his hand and direction a sufficiently fitting integrity of monastic life flourished in this monastery, although it had not been restored quite to the first fervor of its original institution. After him Lord Willicho the abbot appointed Wernher, a monk of this monastery, as the nineteenth prior; he presided in the office of prior for six years. Concerning his life, zeal, and manner of life I find nothing in particular except this alone: that after the death of Prior Henry, when Abbot Willicho had already grown old, the observance of regular discipline began little by little to fail, until at last it lay wholly collapsed for almost 137 years.

In the same year also the consulship of the city, together with the government, was changed in Strasbourg.
