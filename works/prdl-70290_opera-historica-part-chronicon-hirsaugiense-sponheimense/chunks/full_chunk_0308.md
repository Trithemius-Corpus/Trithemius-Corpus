The aforesaid village of Dalen, moreover, was situated not far from the monastery, between east and north, on a plain at the foot of the mountain which in the vernacular is called [Gauwefberg?]. At that time the parish church was very ancient, and existed long before Sponheim and Bockenau were established. In it there was at that time a parish church, which had collapsed from excessive age; therefore Count Megenhard restored it anew, and had it consecrated by the aforesaid Bishop Bruno, who had then been driven from his church by the emperor’s violence. He wished it to be a chapel, and transferred the parish to that place where it now is, around the monastery, with the consent of the often-mentioned Archbishop Adelbert. Furthermore, he gave the chapel in Dalen, together with the village, plots, vineyards, meadows, fields, and other possessions, to Abbot Bernhelm and his monastery in perpetuity; he assigned the collation of the parish church to the abbot.

In the same year, also, on the same day, Bishop Bruno consecrated the altar before the choir of this monastery church, in honor of the holy and only cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, at the request of Abbot Bernhelm.

In this same year also Abbot Bernhelm transferred his sister Mechtild, enclosed for the love of God at Saint Alban’s, with the consent of Archbishop Adelbert and of Volbert, abbot of the same place, from there and placed her in the new cell which he had founded for her around the monastery, on the slope of the mountain toward the west, a place suitable for enclosure, which place down to the present is called Clusa. She was a most devout virgin nun, who by her example stirred many to fear and love Christ; concerning her more is to be said in its proper place, [unclear].

She also brought with her another woman, named Sophia, a virgin of holy conduct, who herself remained enclosed here with her for many years, serving the Lord, and inhabited the place even after her death.

In these times also the count of Lurenburg founded a monastery of our order within the bounds of the diocese of Trier, which is called Schönau. Over it he first set Hildelin, an abbot of most religious conduct; afterward there succeeded him Egebert, most learned in both divine and secular writings, brother of Saint Elizabeth, nun and mistress of the monastery of Schönau, which the aforesaid Abbot Hildelin built for Christ’s virgins near his aforesaid monastery in the fields toward the south. Adelbert, archbishop of Mainz, confirmed the foundation of this monastery when it was offered to him, as is clear from his letters, which follow and are as follows:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Adelbert, archbishop of the see of Mainz, legate of the apostolic see, make known to all the faithful in Christ, both future and present, how our kinsman of Lurenburg delivered over to blessed Martin the monastery of Schönau, founded on his own estate and assigned to monastic life under the appointed abbot Hildelein, for the remedy of his soul and of the souls of his parents, with everything belonging to it: households, churches, fields, vineyards, cultivated and uncultivated lands, woods, meadows, pastures, mills, fisheries, waters and watercourses, roads, approaches and exits of the church. He assigned it to the church of Mainz as a perpetual possession, and took care that it should pass to the knowledge of later generations also by this present privilege of ours. He obtained that this abbey should remain free from every service and released from it. When the abbot of the place has died, let the brothers of the same [canon?] freely elect the one whom they find suitable among themselves. But if, which God forbid, no suitable person is found in that college, let them have the power to pass over into another monastery which is known to agree with their religious discipline. The one elected shall be invested by us or by our successors and consecrated by his own archbishop. Each year on the feast of blessed Martin he shall present a clean corporal upon that altar, as a memorial and proof that his [unclear] is from the patrimony of blessed Martin. It also pleased the abbot and brothers that they should celebrate the day of our ordination while we lived, and after our death should perform the anniversary, both ours and that of our successor archbishops, with solemn obsequies. The count himself received the advocacy from our hand on this condition: that whoever, as his successor, shall hold the estate of Mullen with its ministerials and household, and shall be lord in the castle of Lurenburg, shall be the hereditary lawful advocate of the same monastery, yet so that he himself shall not have the power of appointing a second or third advocate after him. And so that this whole act of conveyance may remain firm and unshaken for all, we have written out the page of this present chirograph concerning it, and have secured it with the impression of our seal, by the authority of almighty God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and of blessed Peter, prince of the apostles, and by our own authority forbidding, under perpetual anathema, that anyone should presume to come against it, seek to undermine it, destroy it, or impair it in any way. But if anyone does this, let him know that he is reserved for the dread judgment of the Holy Spirit, and that at the last examination he will have his portion with the Devil, author of all iniquity.
