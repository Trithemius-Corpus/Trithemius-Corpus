*Familiar Letters*

As for the things that are narrated of him, I am persuaded by certain reasoning that he could by no means have been able [to do them]. Nor is it to be believed that an idolater had anything in common with the holy spirits of God. It remains, therefore, to be held as an undoubted conclusion that, if he performed those miracles which his followers wrote about him, they were accomplished by no other cooperation and power than that of demons; for it is not difficult for them, by false miracles, to deceive even in greater matters the senses of men devoted to them, since by nature and by skill, through long practice, they are more subtle than we are in producing illusions.

But his followers do not allow Apollonius to be accused of magic. I therefore adhere to the former opinion which I stated: that the miracles ascribed to Apollonius were devised, fabricated, and falsely invented either by him or by his disciples. Nor do I think I can easily be led to think otherwise, unless perhaps they concede that they are to be attributed to the deeds of demons. What wonder is it if men devoted to worldly wisdom and philosophy, and wholly ignorant also of divine knowledge, most eager for praise and esteem among men, fabricated about their teacher greater false miracles than the apostles preached throughout the whole world about our Savior and truly performed in his name? Therefore, to sum up all the miracles of the Tyanean: if he did the things which his followers wrote in his praise as wonders, he was certainly a magician and an enchanter of demons and infernal specters. If he did not do them, as is my opinion, no one can doubt that all were devised, fabricated, and falsely invented by Damis and his other disciples, so that they might adorn their master with false praises and thereby themselves obtain eternal glory among mortals.

If you marvel at the rashness of the deed, consider what the audacity of Greek writers has not presumed from the beginning. It is the opinion of many that the histories of the Greeks teem with fabricated tales; that great Trojan destruction is a fiction of the poets. We readily believe that demons devoted their efforts to the fictitious Apollonian tales, so that through these imaginings of Damis and his companions the Christian religion might become cheap among the gentiles, since the Tyanean seemed to have done among the Greeks no less than they had heard Christ had worked among the Jews.

Perhaps they also thought that men would easily be persuaded that Christ was not God on account of the display of miracles, since Apollonius himself, a worshiper of idols, who for that reason wished no divinity to be proclaimed about himself, had been no less distinguished in performing them. How desirous this Tyanean was of the glory of human esteem is testified in many places by the things written by Damis, especially where Apollonius himself recounts to his disciples that gods, the souls of the dead, appeared to him in the temples of idols and spoke great things. Whoever, therefore, believes that the Tyanean performed the miracles written about him either by his own natural power, or by philosophical wisdom, by purity of sanctity, or by natural science, which they call physical magic, should beware lest he do injury to Christ, the Savior of all; for if he did them, he was an enchanter of demons rather than a philosopher.

This, most candid prelate, is my opinion concerning the miracles of Apollonius, which you wished to know. If anyone wishes to oppose it, he seems to me not sufficiently to understand how far the powers of human nature extend. Farewell, most illustrious pontiff; keep your Trithemius commended.

From Würzburg, on the 16th day of the month of April, in the year 1507.
