*To the Reader.*

Thus far, not only the earlier Basel edition, produced under the care of Guilhelmus Radens, but also the autograph itself, compared by us with that edition, which is preserved in the most splendid Palatine Library, copied with astonishing elegance by the careful hand of Trithemius. It is uncertain, however, whether the author carried the work further, and perhaps down to his own time, as he did the Spanheim chronicle. This raises a doubt, because in the preface to the Spanheim chronicle, written in the year 1506 in that monastery, he says as follows: “The chronicle of the monastery of Hirsau, begun here indeed, but at length completed at Würzburg, the city of the Franks: a great work, and divided into two volumes.” But in the letter to Rogerius Sicambrus, which is found among his familiar letters printed at Hagenau, p. 320, dated at Würzburg in the year 1507, he writes thus: “At the request of Blasius, abbot of Hirsau, I undertook to write the chronicle of that same monastery and completed the greater part; but after his death I have meanwhile halted its publication, uncertain with what reward his successor will recompense the labor.” Again, in the *Breviarium of the Annals of the Franks*, published at Paris, fol. III, he says: “We long ago wrote, at the urging of the abbots Blasius and Johannes, two volumes, of no small size, of the chronicles or annals of the distinguished monastery of Hirsau of our order, which is situated in the diocese of Speyer on the border of the Nemetes; in the first of which,” etc. It therefore appears either that the writing was suddenly broken off at this point by him in indignation, after the greater part, as he said, had been completed; or it must be regretted that a further volume of these annals, of equal size besides, is wanting. Yet this loss, if such it is, the following complete Spanheim chronicle, brought down to the author’s own time and now first published by our efforts, will be able in large part to repair.
