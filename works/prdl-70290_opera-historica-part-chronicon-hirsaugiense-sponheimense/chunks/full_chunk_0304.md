*Chronicle of Trithemius*

*Here happily begins the letter of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, to the chronicle of that same monastery of his.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, to dear posterity: to fear God, and never to depart from the path of the regular life.

*Letter to posterity.*

I had often resolved with myself to write the origin, foundation, and progress of this our monastery of Sponheim, together with the successions of its abbots; but, always hindered by various occupations of the world, I have not until now been able to satisfy my desire. For it seemed to me that it would be worthwhile if I brought to the knowledge of posterity those things which, over a long time, with great labor, I was able to gather from various monuments of the ancients, lest through oblivion they should wholly perish from the memory of mortals. Many abbots and monks in various monasteries did this before us: for the common advantage of those then present and afterward of those to come, they wrote in order of time the histories of their monasteries and the names and successions of their abbots. Provoked by their example, I have taken care, with studious reflection, to arrange into one orderly book, according to the sequence of the years of the Lord, whatever I could find anywhere concerning this present monastery of Sponheim. Indeed, neither diligence was lacking in my labors, nor did continual solicitude ever abandon our inquiry. For almost fifteen years, at certain intervals, I labored much before I was able to bring the series of the abbots’ successions into one order. For the negligence of those who preceded us in time in this present monastery of Sponheim doubled my labor, since they committed nothing, or little, to writing that would serve my purpose; even that very material which they had received from others as committed to writing seemed almost wholly consumed by decay and age. Yet I did what I could; I have brought together a history, though not altogether such as I wished. For beginning from the first foundation of the present monastery, I arranged the narrative of the histories that occurred according to the years of the Lord’s nativity, and in agreement with these I placed in order the names and successions of the abbots from the first down to me, who am the twenty-fifth. I have [unclear] integrity. Whoever reads these things after my death, say, I pray, with faithful mind: God have mercy on the soul of Trithemius, who compiled these things.

And at the same time I ask the reader not to condemn anything of what we have written before he has diligently examined it, or has found a manifest error. For it is a detestable race of men that gnaws with impious tooth at the slips of men, which they can neither imitate nor improve, and tears apart with presumptuous rashness what they are unable rightly to discern. Rather, if anywhere you find a manifest error, be present as a benevolent corrector, because I gladly admit those who correct with sincere charity. But enough now about these matters. Moreover, there is a reason why I am moved to set down in order all my lucubrations which I have published during the twenty-three years that I have been abbot of this present monastery of Sponheim: that posterity may know that I always wished well, although, hindered by the insolence of Nayaths, I could not at all accomplish the good that I wished.

*Reason for undertaking the work.*

First I published a large work, On Vices and Virtues, in 2 books. Likewise, On the Temptations of Monks, 1 book. On the Rule of St. Benedict, 1 book. Exhortations to Monks, 2 books. On the Brevity and Misery of the Present Life, 1 book. On the Visitation of Cloistered Persons, 1 book. On the Manner of Celebrating a Provincial Chapter, 1 book. Also the Statutes of the Provincial Chapter, 1 book. Also the Statutes of the Annual Chapter, 1 book. The Chronicle of the Monastery of Hirsau I indeed began here, but at last completed at Würzburg, a city of the Franks: a large work, divided into two volumes. Likewise, the Chronicle of the Dukes of Bavaria, to Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, 1 book. Also the Chronicle of this monastery of Sponheim, to which I have prefixed this Letter, 1 book. On the Ruin of Our Order [unclear], 1 book. On the Praise of Manual Scribes, 1 book. Praises of the Order of Carmelites, 2 books. Praises of St. Anne, 1 book. On the Institution of the Priestly Life, 1 book. On Ecclesiastical Writers, 1 book. On the Illustrious Writers of Germany, 1 book.
