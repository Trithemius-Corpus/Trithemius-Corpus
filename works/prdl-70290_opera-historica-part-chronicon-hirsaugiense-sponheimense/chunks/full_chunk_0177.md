When Lothar the emperor had received the royal insignia at Aachen on the holy day of Easter, this man publicly excommunicated his brother Simon and, under the Gospel, with such [unclear], in the presence of the emperor himself, compelled him to leave the church. But from the monastery of St. Matthias and the other monasteries of monks he violently seized many things and converted them to his own uses. Hillinus, dean of the greater church, succeeded him in the archbishopric and presided for eighteen years. Arnold, archbishop of Cologne, having set out for Rome, was ordained by Pope Eugenius; by him he was sent back to his see with the pallium and honor.

*1153.*

In the thirty-second year of Abbot Volmar, which was the year 1153 of the Lord’s nativity, Henry, archbishop of Mainz, by order of Pope Eugenius, on the holy day of Pentecost, was deposed by two cardinals, Gregory and Bernard; and he died in the same year, on the Kalends of September. St. Hildegard foretold to him this shortness of life two years before; in a certain letter to him she says thus: “Hear, you who neglect me amid many inquiries. Heaven is opened for the vengeance of the Lord, and now the boundaries have been released to enemies. But you, rise, because your days are short; and remember that Nebuchadnezzar fell, and that his crown perished. And many others fell who rashly exalted themselves into heaven. Ah, you ashes, why are you not ashamed to scatter yourself on high, when you ought to be in corruption?”

Therefore, when Henry had been deposed from the archbishopric, Arnold, the king’s chancellor and provost of Aachen, succeeded; he was afterward slain in the monastery of St. James outside the walls of Mainz, as we shall say in its place. To him St. Hildegard writes thus, among other things: “O father, living in human fashion, these words he gave me for you. Why are you not strong in my fear? And why do you have zeal, displaying as it were [unclear], so that by separating you cast down what is contrary to you? But this I do not will. Wipe away, from the eye of your heart, an unquiet mind; and cut off injustice from your people, because the time of wars now stands in the morals of men, so that they are neither in discipline nor in the severity of the fear of the Lord. You shall endure; do not be afraid, because the Son of God suffered the same things. Therefore rise to God, because the time is at hand [unclear] will come.”

In the same year also, when Henry, bishop of Minden, could not clear himself concerning a certain cleric who had been [unclear] in the city by his men, with his own consent, as was said, being wearied by many adversities, he renounced the bishopric before the aforesaid cardinals. Werner, provost of the same church, succeeded him.

Pope Eugenius III also died. Conrad, abbot of the monastery of St. Rufus in the diocese of Velletri, of our order and a cardinal, succeeded him and was named Anastasius IV; he sat one year, four months, and twenty-four days. This Pope Anastasius sent, in the first year of his pontificate, letters to St. Hildegard the virgin, full of paternal affection and showing devotion, in which he wonderfully extols her writings and humbly commends himself to the prayers of her and of the whole congregation, beseeching from her with great prayers a divine response. The holy virgin, taught divinely through revelation, wrote back to him all the things that were necessary for him for the amendment of life, even the most hidden things.

In the same year at Cologne, a certain man born of a Jewish father, but a convert, after he had received by mouth the sacrament of the body of Christ at the solemnity of Easter, and had gone out of the church and thought of carrying it home, intending I know not what to do with it, was struck with divine terror and shuddered; and, not knowing where to turn, he made a pit in the cemetery and buried in it the most holy body of the Lord. But a priest came upon him, and, learning the matter, opened the pit; finding the form of a little child, and reverently lifting him in his hands, he wished to carry him to the church. But, wondrous to say, when light was sent from heaven, the little boy slipped from the hands of the one holding him and, ascending in that light, penetrated the heavens.
