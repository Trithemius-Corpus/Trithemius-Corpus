And because the archbishop is especially likely to send to the Apostolic See for the pallium, if he asks the abbot to be his legate for such a matter, and gives him everything necessary for the journey, we approve that he should not refuse to go. For it serves the matter if by this office he should be bound more familiarly to his friendship; which, without injustice from us, a son and member of the household may obtain more than one who has not been so privileged.

Likewise, whenever the bishop of Speyer is to be ordained, it is expedient that, if he has not neglected to be present there also, before he is ordained he should remind the archbishop what he should do, and seek assent for this decree of ours. Or if he wishes anything else, let him see what the archbishops do for those commended to them; and if need be, let the matter come even to our notice or that of our successor. But if God has given to that bishop this spirit, that he should not unwillingly favor the humble servants of Christ, then, lest we seem to have passed him over here without honor, we command that in your convent of brothers, on any private day, Psalm ninety-nine be said for him throughout his life. After his life, however, for him and for his predecessors from this day henceforth, all those who have not opposed the present precept, let a private Mass of the dead be held every Monday. He can know that this is more expedient for the profit of his soul than if he were able to rule in the monastery by his own vow.

As advocate, however, we appoint the Roman emperor, who is also our advocate. Yet because of his many cares and the arrangements of the realm, the abbot and the brothers may choose from the family of Calw, if a suitable man is found, who is willing to defend the often-mentioned place not for the sake of gain or because of his own necessity, but for the remedy of his soul and of his parents. But if not, being free from every side, they may choose another, who, when summoned into the presence of the abbot and the brothers, shall also swear with his household under oath that he will not act against the privileges of the Church. Thus brought into the presence of the lord emperor, he shall confirm it in like manner. Not only over the greater cell may the abbot appoint subordinate advocateship, but also throughout each of his estates and cells, where no right is withdrawn from him by those priors established there in providing benefices or even in investing churches. But if the advocate so appointed should have acted contrary to the privileges, let the abbot have full power to reject him as excommunicated by our ban and to provide for that same monastery another useful to himself, from wherever he pleases, even with the help of the emperor if there is need.

[Note: This privilege the venerable abbot Gebhard, induced by the counsel of the brothers, favorably obtained from the supreme pontiff Urban II himself, going to him in his own person; and with Ruthard, archbishop of Mainz, consenting in his own person as far as pertained to him, he brought it into use and practice.]

Furthermore, this statute, of which mention is made in the bull, namely that no one should be received as a member of the monastery before completing his twentieth year, the holy father William had begun with the counsel of the brothers, knowing indeed that the observance of regular discipline is tolerable not for boys, but for strong men. For just as today we see in all places where boys are admitted to religion, little or no observance of monastic integrity flourishes. Therefore the most excellent men, brothers of this monastery, fearing that in the course of time this holy institution might perish by some occasion, with prudent counsel offered it to be confirmed by the approval of the Apostolic See.

[Note: For in those times, in this monastery under Gebhard, the venerable abbot, there were men great both in number and in wisdom, who illuminated our order like the brightest stars of heaven; many of them, before entering religion, had been of great name and honor in the world. At that time the greatest charity flourished among them, and such observance of regular discipline [unclear] prelates of churches of great name, who nevertheless, after they had been converted to the order, cast themselves down with such humility among the poor for the love of Christ, as if during all the days of their life they had been ignorant peasants, servants, or wretched beggars in the world.]

Many among them were most learned in all knowledge of the Scriptures; many also [unclear]. Several adopted the solitary life.
