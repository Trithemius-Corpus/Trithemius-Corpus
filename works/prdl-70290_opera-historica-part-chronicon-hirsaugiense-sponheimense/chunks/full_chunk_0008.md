We shall give the reason for this. The monks of old once had cells, churches, and other dwellings that were humble and dark, but their hearts were very bright in the love of almighty God and in the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Our men, however, now build churches, cells, houses, and all dwellings exceedingly bright, but their hearts are dark, full of vices and idleness, since they neither love God as they ought nor apply their minds to readings of the Scriptures. Thus too, when the chalices once were of copper, the priests were of gold; today, however, the chalices are of gold, and the priests of copper. This monastery, built by Erlafrid, had been in the place where, as we have said, the blind man was enlightened, and where he himself, because of his hunts, had a house in the forest. Yet after almost two hundred thirty-four years, as we shall say below in its proper place, it was demolished by Count Adelbert and reformed into the shape in which it is seen today. For today no trace at all of the earlier monastery remains, although the site of both is one and the same.

*When and whence the first monks were brought to Hirsau, and who was appointed their first abbot.*

After the most noble Count Erlafrid, having completed so notable a work, had brought his purpose to effect, not without very great expenses, he was impatient of delay, since he said that he desired nothing more than to live to see that day on which he might behold choirs of monks in his little estate praising God together. He began anxiously to consider whence he might obtain for the new monastery gloriously founded by him monks of the old way of life, religiously instructed according to the rule of the most holy father Benedict, who would both themselves be most holy in their conduct and fit for that same purpose, [unclear].

At that time, it was said that the religion of monks flourished very greatly at Fulda above many others, and that they were learned. Hence many of them were raised in various churches to the summit of the pontificate. Therefore Erlafrid sent messengers to Rabanus, abbot of Fulda, [unclear]. The praise and memory of the monks of Fulda was in the mouths of all, and was held in great esteem among emperors, kings, and princes, not only because of the holiness of their life, but also because of their incomparable knowledge of the Scriptures. [He asked] Rabanus [Maurus], abbot of Fulda, for the monks and an abbot to be brought into his monastery which he had built; and he promised that he would do everything that, in their judgment, should seem fitting for the usefulness of monastic institution.

In the year 838, in the first indiction, Abbot Lindbert came to Hirsau with fifteen monks, sent by Rabanus, abbot of Fulda.
