*The Monastery of Hirsau*

Here rest the limbs of Theodore the monk, who is buried here: vigorous in piety, renowned for simplicity, a priest by office, fulfilling it in due order; [unclear] in the praises of the highest Lord; prudent and learned, second to none in piety. All that he learned, he soon handed on to others, so that the just and pious Lord might increase a hundredfold the talent entrusted to him, by well praising it. He passed away on the Nones of December, with a happy end. You who read this inscription, pray to God for him.

1001.

In the twelfth year of Conrad, Emperor Otto, setting in motion his third expedition into Italy with a vast army, laid waste to the Romans, defeated them, and overcame them. Moreover he caused Crescentius, who had seized the principate of Rome and had committed many evils against the Church of God and the vicars of Christ, to be beheaded. And when, while staying at Rome for some time, he began to deal rather leniently with the Romans, and they began to hold him in contempt, they formed a conspiracy, killed several of his soldiers, and besieged him stoutly in the palace. By the efforts of Henry duke of Bavaria and Hugh the margrave, after a feigned agreement, he was scarcely extricated, and departed from Rome, accompanied by the pope. And because the wife of Crescentius, a most beautiful woman, who had long consented to the emperor in acts of Venus, saw, and grieved, that the emperor had gone away leaving her behind in hope of reigning, she arranged for a poisoned drink for him; after drinking it, he died in Italy on his way back.

The pontiffs, however, and the princes who were with him, skillfully arranged the corpse, clothed in purple, upon a horse as if he were alive, lest perhaps a tumult should arise in Italy if the emperor’s death had become known; and, removing the entrails, they left them at Augsburg, but buried the body in the basilica at Aachen.

Henry, sprung from the dukes of Bavaria, but in office count of Bamberg, succeeded him by election of the princes, and at Mainz received the diadem of the kingdom from Archbishop Willigis. This is Henry, the second emperor of this name, who deserved to be written in the catalogue of the saints; of him we shall speak later in his own place, whom we have noted here in anticipation.

1002.

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 1002, in the fifteenth indiction, Conrad, the tenth abbot of this monastery, died on the Ides of February; when he had been buried with the lamentations of the brothers in the church of Saint Aurelius, the abbacy of Hirsau was vacant for sixty-three years, as we shall say in order in what follows.

*Concerning the abbacy of this monastery being vacant for sixty-three years, and certain deeds in the meantime of the monastery, the order, and Germany*

When Conrad, the venerable, not to say pitiable, tenth abbot of this monastery, had entered the way of all flesh, a few monks in number remained surviving in this place; and, since even now they had not entirely returned to the way of truth, they brought upon the rest a tribulation greater than they had experienced up to that point. For when they were consulting among themselves about the election of a new pastor, the count violently and with armed hand thrust himself into their affairs, promised that he would give them an abbot, and utterly forbade them to proceed to an election.

In order that, under the appearance of piety, he might gradually make the monastery empty, and might hand over to his own greed all that remained, he set over them as steward one of his own household. He knew [unclear] would come, and would govern everything according to the Rule; then he promised, in humble words and deceitfully, that he would never [unclear] the gifts which he had taken away. In truth, however, he was seeking this, which happened not long afterward: that, when the monks were either dead or expelled, he himself might be called to his own right over all.

For after almost three years, when a certain dissension arose between the brothers and the steward, the monks, except for two who had long adhered to Eberhard and had always defended the count’s party, were expelled from the monastery.
