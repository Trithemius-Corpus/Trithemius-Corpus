*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

*Page: Charters of Liberty*

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. Henry, by the favor of divine clemency king: let the diligence of all the faithful of the holy Church of God, present namely and future, know that in our kingdom there is situated a certain regular monastery, in the province which is called Teutonic Francia, in the bishopric of Speyer, in the district called Wernigau, in the county of Ingersheim, in the forest which is called the Black Forest beside the river which is called the Nagold, which is named Hirsau or the cell of St. Aurelius, where also that same saint rests in body. In the time of King Louis the Pious it was honorably built in honor of St. Peter and of Aurelius the bishop, and dedicated to God by Erlafrid, a certain noble and religious senator, and by Notting, his son, the most reverend bishop of Vercelli, and by other kinsmen of Count Adalbert of the castle of Calw; but [unclear] by their descendants it was dispersed.

Now however that same Count Adalbert, touched by the prompting of God, and also repeatedly urged by his wife Wiltrud, has now restored that monastery, and has restored the monastic life which for some time had failed there; and the estates which in ancient times had been handed over thither by his kinsmen, but had been partly seized by their descendants and partly possessed even by himself as if by hereditary right, he has restored. Moreover, from his own property he has handed over to the aforesaid place so many and so great expenses, that the service of God, now carried out there by fifteen brothers under the Rule of St. Benedict, may by the grace of God be maintained and established there; his sons, the aforesaid Bruno, Adalbert, and Gosfrid, and his daughters Vita and Irmingard, agreeing with him in this and in all things consonant with this ordinance.

And first, namely, the place itself, Hirsau, with all the lawful endowments and appurtenances now at present conferred there, and whatever things in their entirety, he has restored, delegated, and handed over upon the altar of St. Aurelius to the Lord God, to Saint Mary, to Saint Peter the Apostle, to St. Aurelius the bishop, and to St. Benedict, into their power and ownership. And all things of the aforesaid monastery to the abbot named William, and to his successors, for free disposition [unclear], so that, as by his kinsmen, the service of God may thereafter be able to be [unclear] there, prudently to profit and [unclear] the same cell with all its appurtenances now conferred and duly to be conferred, from this [unclear] domination, ordering, and power.

Thus they [unclear] it with the right and privilege of complete liberty, and for the inheritance of the heavenly kingdom in Christ, in every way for hope of sins [unclear], with his wife, sons, and daughters aforesaid, he faithfully renounced [unclear]. But this whole thing the truly happy merchant prudently effected: first for the pledge and reward of eternal life, for the remission of sins, for the health of bodies, and the everlasting [unclear] of souls, and also of all kinsmen, descendants, blood-relations, friends, and all who belong to the care of holiness. Also for the memory of kings, bishops, and lords, for the diligence of the aforesaid monastery and of those still to be gathered in Christ, so that they may be able more safely and freely to serve the Lord God in the service of holy profession, he endowed them with this kind of gift of liberty.

And whenever they shall be bereft of their spiritual father, they shall have free power among themselves, according to the Rule of St. Benedict, or from anywhere where there is need, not only of choosing an abbot for themselves but also of constituting him. Whomever the brothers shall have regularly chosen, thereafter, as is customary, the prior of that place shall take the staff of government from the altar of St. Aurelius, in order to set him in the choir as monk, and without any contradiction whatsoever shall hand it into the hand of him whom the whole body of brothers [unclear], private greed having been removed, [unclear]. He, then, having been canonically ordained abbot, without domination or impediment by any person, shall fulfill the ministry he has received [unclear] among his own, and shall serve God alone freely according to his order, if he shall be bound in all things.
