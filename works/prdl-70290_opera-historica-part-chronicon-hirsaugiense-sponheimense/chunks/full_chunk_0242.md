*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*1239.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Ernest, which was the year 1239 of the Lord’s Nativity, the greater church of Mainz, newly repaired by Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, was consecrated with great solemnity.

*The church of Mainz [unclear].*

In the same year there occurred a failure of the sun, which the Greeks call an eclipse, and a great pestilence followed it in very many regions. In these times there was much persecution of the ministers of God’s Church, especially in the kingdom of Germany, on account of the excommunication of Emperor Frederick II, who did not cease persecuting all who agreed with the pope, despoiling churches of their ornaments and revenues, and afflicting prelates, monks, and priests with many injuries.

*1240.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Ernest, and in the year 1240 of the Lord’s Nativity, Pope Gregory IX died; Celestine IV was elected pope.

Celestine IV, succeeding him, presided for only seventeen days. After his death, because the cardinals did not agree, the Apostolic See was vacant for one year and nine months, not without danger to many souls.

*1241.*

In the following year, which was the tenth of Abbot Ernest, Pope Innocent IV, elected after Celestine, presided for eleven years and three months.

In the same year, a mountain in Burgundy, splitting away from the mountains and constricting the valleys, wretchedly crushed many thousands of farmers.

*1242.*

In the eleventh year of Abbot Ernest, and in the year 1242 of the Lord’s Nativity, Theoderic, archbishop of Trier, died in the thirtieth year of his pontificate, in the fifteenth indiction, on the first day before the Kalends of April; he was buried in the greater church of Trier before the altar of St Martin. Arnold succeeded him, provost of the greater church, son of the count of Isenberg, nephew by his sister of the aforesaid Archbishop Theoderic, and he presided for almost sixteen years. Against this Arnold, elected by the greater and sounder part of the chapter, Rudolph, provost of St Paulinus, born of Ponte, elected by a few, set himself, trusting in the aid of the duke of Lorraine and the counts of Luxembourg and of Sayn, and resisted as much as he could the election that had been made of him. At length agreement was made between them. Pope Innocent confirmed Arnold as archbishop and granted to him, for his table, that he might lawfully retain all the ecclesiastical benefices that he had held before.

*Worms perished by fire.*

In the same year, on the Ides of April at the hour of Vespers, the city of Worms, set on fire by a chance blaze, was half consumed by that fire, and more than three hundred people perished in the same fire.

*1243.*

In the twelfth year of Abbot Ernest, which was the 1243rd year of the Lord’s Nativity, [unclear] archbishop [unclear], monks of the Premonstratensian order, began to inhabit that famous monastery of Lorsch of our order, as we said above by anticipation.

*1244.*

In the following year, which was the thirteenth of Abbot Ernest and the 1244th of the Lord’s Nativity, Emperor Frederick II, cited by Pope Innocent to that famous council at Lyons and not appearing, was [unclear] from the imperial majesty. Henry [unclear], provincial count of Hesse, having been elected king against Frederick, defended his party as much as they could. Henry, once elected, had a war with Conrad, the emperor’s son, in the fields near Frankfurt, and, emerging victorious, overcame Conrad.

Therefore, bearing the royal name for five years, he accomplished nothing worthy of memory, but died without ever obtaining the crown of the empire. For as long as Emperor Frederick lived, for almost six years none of the princes could prevail against him; but, not heeding the pope’s sentence, which he called unjust and frivolous, he conducted himself as emperor, and had a great adherence of princes, nobles, and cities until his death.
