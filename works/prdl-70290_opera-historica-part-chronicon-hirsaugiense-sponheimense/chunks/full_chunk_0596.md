*Johannes Trithemius.*

...we shall pursue the sacred delights of books. For us, placed in this mortality, to live is nothing other than to toil in the pursuit of true wisdom. Therefore, if one of the two must be done, we shall bid farewell rather to the abbacy than to the study of letters. Meanwhile, dearest brothers, lest the state of our community be more gravely disturbed on account of our absence, let each one of you diligently attend to his own order and the office entrusted to him, until we deliberate better what it is useful for us to do in this disturbance. To the abbot of Speciosus Mons, who does not follow Saturn’s motion very gracefully enough, we give thanks; we do not have them. For he is the chief cause of all evils against us, inciting ministers of crime against our people, by whom he was never harmed, nor indeed could have been harmed. What have our innocent familiars to do with the threats he feared from outsiders? As if we lacked the aid of princes, whom the wanton tongue and intemperate insolence of the old man offends no less. We are staying at the town of Büdor belonging to the prince palatine, and we are indeed awaiting the most serene prince Philip, now absent by necessity; to his judgment we shall refer everything that occurs to trouble us. For we shall do nothing in this disturbance of ours without the counsel of the most mild prince, whose favor and clemency [unclear] has provoked hatred against us. Farewell from Büdor, a town of the prince palatine, on the tenth day of the month of April, in the year of the Christians 1505.

*III.*

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Jacob Trithemius, Doctor of Arts and Philosophy, his most sweet brother, greeting.*

What the rashness of our rivals has presumed against us, we have learned from the report of Brother Theoderic. See to it and take care that you guard our books and the things entrusted to you, especially [unclear], [unclear]. For we do not intend to return to Sponheim unless we have brought to the knowledge of very many people this insult inflicted undeservedly upon us and ours. The keys of our possessions, especially [unclear], which on our departure, suspecting nothing adverse, we entrusted to your good faith, you are to hand over to no one without our commission. For we are held by a vehement suspicion of a conspiracy of certain brothers against us, especially of the prior of the other, to whom he was always hostile. But you, sweetest brother, are not to be moved by these evils, if indeed those things should be called evils through which a road to God is being prepared for us. By experience they will learn that they have begun no childish game, when at last they themselves have fallen into the snare which they prepared for us. Brother Theoderic, who carries our letter to you, has received certain instructions from us; give him the trust of belief, and render him aid. For among our people we have found him, a foreigner, faithful to us, one who grieves at our lot and gladly carries out whatever we have ordered. Farewell. From Buri, on the tenth day of the month of April, in the year of the Christians 1505.

*IV.*

*To his prior in Sponheim, Brother Nicolaus Remitius.*

The old proverb has been fulfilled in me, by the permission of divine providence: no [unclear] without leaves. Power has been given to the adversary to rage against us, and he has not spared us, first stripping us, by the example of the innocent man, of our goods, friends, and sons whom we brought forth in the Lord, and then now tearing the body with various wounds; the mind alone, with God’s compassion assisting us, he will by no means penetrate. We lie here grievously afflicted by fevers, full of swollen ulcers, deserted by our own people, and, what is more serious, exposed without cause as a reproach and a tale to the adversaries of letters. No one from all our dear ones consoles us; no one of our old friends, being far away, visits us; no one appears beside us in our grief. But you, Lord God, compassionate and merciful, raise me up again; I shall repay them, not evil for evil, for this has been forbidden to us through your word, but by reviving in good I shall overcome evil, so that I may do what your word most rightly commanded to be done by his disciples toward enemies who persecute them. For, with you taking pity on me, this casting-up of temporal inconveniences will be for my salvation, if, while the little body outwardly suffers insults and weaknesses, the mind perseveres immovable amid the internal [unclear]. I know that I was born for labors, and that through many tribulations I am to return to him from whom I withdrew through disobedience. I, perhaps, before God, suffer what I deserved; but let those who persecute me though I am innocent see by what zeal they are moved.
