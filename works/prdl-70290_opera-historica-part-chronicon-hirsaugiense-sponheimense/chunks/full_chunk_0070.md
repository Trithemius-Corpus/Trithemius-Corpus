*Of the Monastery of Hirsau: concerning the second restoration of the monastery of St Aurelius, made by Adelbert, count of Calw, begun in the year of the Lord 1059.*

In the one thousand and fifty-ninth year of the Lord’s nativity, which was the fifty-seventh year of the monastery’s vacancy, and the two hundred and nineteenth year from the first foundation of this place, which had been made by Count Erlafrid and his son Notting, bishop of Vercelli, Count Adelbert, being a strong young man, rich and wholly immersed in worldly cares, seemed gradually to forget the words and threats of his most holy uncle, Pope Leo IX, and his own promise and oath, and he kept putting off from day to day the restoration of the monastery of St Aurelius of Hirsau, which he had sworn, by promise, to hasten under obligation of anathema. Wilterud, his devout wife, a handmaid of Christ, fearing and at the same time dreading that too long a delay might utterly void her husband’s holy [vow?], or that, if death should befall him, thereafter none of the things that the holy pontiff had commanded would be brought to the desired effect, began to press her husband urgently with frequent admonitions, praying and beseeching him that, mindful of the words and threats of the most holy pontiff and uncle Leo, and of how harsh a sentence of excommunication and eternal damnation he had thundered against him if he neglected it, [unclear]; that he should finally spare the whole offspring of his lineage, which in future time would succeed its parent, if he had resolved to be wholly more cruel to himself; and that he should take care to commit to execution as quickly as possible the things that the most holy man had commanded. Otherwise, she said, she greatly feared that the justice of almighty God, once provoked, would at last punish with a most grievous and eternal penalty the disobedience presumed against the commands of so great a pontiff.

When his wife was frequently impressing these and like matters upon the count her husband, and daily recalling to mind the terror-filled words of the supreme pontiff, and when she saw that, though he said he would shortly do everything according to the command of the holy lord and would restore the monastery of St Aurelius, [unclear] was being put off from day to day, and that always graver obstacles were being added to grave ones, she resolved to overcome by importunity him whom she considered she could not overcome by gentleness and mildness. Therefore from then on the devout woman ceased neither by day nor by night to admonish, beg, and with a certain seasonable force drive her husband to begin as quickly as possible the work promised, lest he provoke against himself the wrath of almighty God. Accordingly, overcome by her excessive importunity, he resolved to redeem his fault. Craftsmen were sent for the work; seeing that the church of blessed Aurelius, built with wooden boarding according to the custom of the ancients, and half-ruined through the negligence of the clerics and the evil of the times, could not stand together with the other dwellings of the cloister, they gave the advice that, after the old structure, too simple as it was, had been taken down, he should begin a new monastery of stone, whose firmness would both remind posterity of the deed and, being most stable, would not allow the expenses to go to waste. For, as we said much earlier in the first foundation, the church first founded by Count Erlafrid was indeed sufficiently spacious, but, in the manner of the ancients, too simple and less firm, and it seemed unable to stand long without danger. Therefore Count Adelbert, having received the advice both of architects and other craftsmen and of prudent men experienced in the dimensions of monasteries, had the first [site?] for the new church cleared, to be restored according to the form handed over to him by experts. Meanwhile he had squared stones from a nearby mountain, timber, and the other things necessary for so great a work brought in at great expense, and whatever was required for the new building very abundantly prepared.
