*Sponheim.*

He built from the foundations a conventual heated room for the brothers’ winter refectory, adorning it inside all around with fine wooden paneling, out of love for the brothers, because it was very necessary for them, since from the time of the reform they had had no conventual heated room, but both in winter and in summer had been content in the summer refectory with its underground fireplace. He also rebuilt anew from the foundations, with stone walls, the mill that was lower down, which in the vernacular is called ter Leymule, since it had completely collapsed from excessive age. Through neglect of the cattle, too, in this same year one house utterly collapsed; because of necessity he immediately built it anew from the foundations with a wooden wall and four chambers.

In the same year, just as the plague had taken hold in the preceding year, indeed in the preceding three years, fevers, heats, and pains of the head, together with dysentery, attacked many thousands of people throughout this whole region; consumed by these, many died everywhere. For this illness was so great and widespread in Kreuznach, Bingen, Mainz, Frankfurt, Worms, Speyer, and the neighboring villages, and here in Sponheim, that there was scarcely a house in which someone was not lying sick with fevers or dysentery.

In the same year also the lord Johannes, abbot, obtained for himself and his successors a privilege concerning the use of a portable altar from the lord Raymond, cardinal, legate de latere of the Apostolic See to all Germany, whose tenor follows and is as follows.

Raymond, by divine mercy priest cardinal of the holy Roman Church of the title of Santa Maria Nuova [unclear].
