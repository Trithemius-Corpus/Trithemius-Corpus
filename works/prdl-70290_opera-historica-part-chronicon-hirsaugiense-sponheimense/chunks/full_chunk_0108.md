*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

[Cluniacensis?], having received the letters of his very dear Wilhelm and learned of his desire, sent with letters, by the hands of that same prior Gebhard, most precious and true relics from the hair of St. Peter the Apostle. But before Gebhard returned to Hirsau, the servant of Christ Wilhelm passed to the Lord, as we shall say in its proper place. For after the dedication of the new monastery he survived only nine weeks; and therefore, because of the shortness of the time, the congregation did not leave the monastery of St. Aurelius while the holy abbot was alive.

Therefore, as the feast of the most blessed apostles of Christ Peter and Paul was approaching, an illness seized the most reverend abbot Wilhelm. Although he was weak in body, he nevertheless attended the night watches and the divine offices with most devout presence. On the feast day of those Apostles he celebrated Mass privately at the principal altar of the new monastery, and this was his last; he was supported by the hands of the brothers on both sides, since he was wholly unable to stand by himself. Thus with the greatest devotion of mind toward God, as if about to depart to him whom he had faithfully served in this life and about to receive the reward of his work, he began the Mass with as loud a voice as he could, saying in the words of the most blessed Apostle Paul: “I know whom I have believed, and I am certain that he is able to keep my deposit until that day, the just judge.” On the fourth day before his death he had himself carried into the chapter, and, all having been summoned, with Mass [unclear] before him, he received the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ from the hand of the priest with great devotion. [Unclear] they might make progress, first admonishing them to maintain continual love of God, zeal for monastic religion, the observance of mutual charity, and hospitality to be carefully kept. And, like another Moses in Deuteronomy, he repeated everything whatsoever he had taught them from the beginning. Then, on the third day, he again had himself carried into the oratory of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin, which he himself had built on the southern side, and, Mass having been celebrated before him, he received the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ from the hand of the priest with great devotion. Then, joined with the sacred oil, he was carried back to his cell, where, saying farewell to the brothers, on the following day he happily rested in the Lord, on the fourth day before the Nones of July, in the year of the Lord’s nativity [unclear], in the twenty-second year of his rule, and in the sixty-fifth year of his age.

Two bishops, five abbots, and many clerics with the people came to his funeral rites; for five continuous days they celebrated his obsequies. When these had been completed, they buried his holy body with honor and reverence in the middle of the new church which he had built, and they adorned his tomb with suitable construction.

After the holy abbot Wilhelm had died, and while prior Gebhard was absent, great sorrow overtook the brothers, because to remain long without a shepherd seemed full of danger to them; and they did not dare to establish [unclear]. But while they were awaiting the arrival of their prior, behold, after a few days the man they desired arrived, bringing with him the precious relics of the prince of the Apostles, namely true hairs from his head, which St. Hugh, abbot of Cluny, sent as a great gift to the most reverend father Wilhelm as a sign of mutual love. But Gebhard, using prudent counsel, did not immediately bring the relics of the blessed Apostle into the monastery, but entrusted them to a certain faithful priest living nearby, until at an opportune time, with fitting honor, [they might be brought] to the monastery.
