*The Chronicle of Trithemius*

... those marked [with the cross], who for their transgressions and for withholding tithes had been excommunicated for many years, had shown themselves contemptuous of the ecclesiastical keys; and, often fighting with bishops, counts, and other nobles, they were frequently victors and rarely defeated. But when an army of crusaders came against them, they were defeated, and more than two thousand were killed; the rest fled to the neighboring Frisians.

*The Count of Holland is killed. Worms is burned. Henry, bishop of Worms, is dead; Landolf succeeded him. Nonnenmünster is reformed. King Henry opposes his father Frederick and comes to a bad end.*

In the same year the Count of Holland was killed in a certain tournament at Corbie in the diocese of Amiens. Also in the same year, on the night of 10 Kalends of July, the city of Worms was set on fire, and a great part of it was burned, not without very great loss to many. In the same year Henry of Saarbrücken, bishop [unclear], died. He expelled the nuns of our order from the monastery in the suburb of the city of Worms, which in the common tongue is called Nonnenmünster, because of their disorderly life, and introduced nuns of the Cistercian order there, who inhabit that place down to the present. Also in the same year King Henry held an assembly of certain princes at Boppard, where he received counsel from certain wicked men that he should oppose his father Frederick, and this he did. For from that time he began to solicit everyone he could, by threats, entreaty, and payment, to stand with him against his father; and he found not a few.

MCCXXXV. In the twenty-second year of Abbot John, Emperor Frederick, returning from Italy into Germany with many treasures, held a council at Worms on 6 Nones of July, where he received his son Henry back into favor. But when Henry did not keep his promises and occupied the castle of Trifels against his will, he was seized by order of his father and first [kept] in a certain very strong house [unclear] because of their disorderly life expelled, and they introduced nuns of the Cistercian order there, which he did. Then at last, after being sent to Alzey and finally to Sicily, he was held prisoner until death, and in the fifth year of his exile and captivity he died there in custody.

In this year, which was the twenty-third of Abbot John, in the village of Gebrad a male child was born with two heads, very monstrous; he lived six months and nine days. His two heads were joined opposite each other at the back, but there was one body, two confused bellies, and lower down they were wonderfully reduced into one; two feet.

In the same year Saint Elizabeth of Marburg was canonized by Pope Gregory. Emperor Frederick was present at Marburg with an innumerable multitude of people when her most holy body was raised from the tomb; he himself was the first to lift the stone from her sarcophagus, and he gave a golden crown to her holy head. Present there were the apostolic commissioners: Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz; Theodoric, archbishop of Trier; and the bishop of Hildesheim, with many other bishops and princes. There are those who write that more than twelve hundred thousand people assembled there at that time. These things were done in the ninth year from the death of Saint Elizabeth, on the day of Philip and James, where among other signs oil flowed from her sacred body.

MCCXXXVII. In the twenty-fourth year of Abbot John, the grange of our monastery situated on the mountain near Dalen, which was called Gauwershausen, with all the surrounding buildings, [unclear] or six, was burned, and afterward was not restored. For on that very mountain, which is called Mount Gauia, commonly Gauwersberg, there was once a small village, which gradually declined and in this year was entirely consumed and so desolated that today no trace of it appears in the place.

In the same year Conrad of Than, bishop of Speyer, died; Conrad of Eberstein succeeded him on the feast of Saint Agnes the Virgin, and he presided for eight years.

MCCXXXIX. In this year, which was the twenty-fifth of Abbot John, Reginfrid, the ninth prior of this monastery, died on the day of Saint Cecilia, virgin and martyr, a good and upright man; after him no one like him in zeal and fervor for regular observance and the keeping of monastic discipline arose in this monastery for many years. When he had died, Abbot John, with the counsel of his brethren, appointed as the tenth prior of this place John, a venerable old man, who held his office for only one year and six days, and died. In the same year the greater church of Mainz was consecrated by Siegfried the archbishop. MCCXL.
