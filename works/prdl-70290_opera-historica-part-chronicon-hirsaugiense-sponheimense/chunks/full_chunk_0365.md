[He] took Premonstratensians from the monastery of All Saints, of the diocese of Strasbourg, and established a convent in the aforesaid monastery, setting over them not an abbot but a provost, and after him he appointed a prior, according to the custom of that order. He confirmed by his letters this establishment of the Premonstratensian order in the often-mentioned monastery of Lorsch; their date is the year of the Lord 1248, the sixth ides of January, in the eighteenth year of his pontificate. Since these are too lengthy and do not serve our purpose, we have reasonably omitted a copy of [unclear]. But the Premonstratensians introduced into Lorsch, at the archbishop’s instance, renounced every right of lordship over the said monastery, as is clear in these letters:

“To all the faithful of Christ, present as well as future: the provost and convent of the monastery of Lorsch, of the Premonstratensian order, in the diocese of Mainz, offer devout prayers in Christ. Since the venerable father lord Siegfried, archbishop of the see of Mainz, by the counsel and consent of the chapter of Mainz, has established us by apostolic authority in the monastery of Lorsch, revenues suitable and honorable having been assigned to us, with which we are also content, namely all the revenues of the former convent and of its offices; by reason of this establishment, and also because our place lies in the diocese of Mainz, we subject ourselves and our place, and acknowledge them subject, to the church of Mainz, to the said lord archbishop and to all his successors, according as is contained in the privilege of our establishment.

“Moreover, since imperial and likewise royal authority, by the counsel and assent of the princes, has seen fit, for certain and legitimate causes, [unclear] the lordship of the church of Lorsch, with every honor, vassals, ministerials, castles, towns, revenues, rights, and its appurtenances, to the church of Mainz, as we have perceived to be contained in the imperial and royal privileges: we acknowledge that the church of Mainz, lord Siegfried the archbishop, and his successors possess a firm and stable right in that lordship itself and in its civil appurtenances; and concerning these matters we shall never raise, nor shall any of our successors raise, any question whatsoever. But if by our establishment, by reason of the monastery of Lorsch, any right had been acquired for us or our successors in that lordship itself, or in the aforesaid goods and rights pertaining to the abbey, we have renounced it, and we give and grant that same thing to the church of Mainz and to the archbishops who shall be in office from time to time, to be possessed by them; since we are wholly unable to restore and maintain the same lordship in its rights and honor, and the statutes of our order forbid us to possess such things. In memory of this act and for its perpetual validity, we have caused the present letters to be written and to be strengthened by the protection of the seals of the churches of St. Peter and St. Stephen of Mainz, and of our own church of All Saints in the diocese of Strasbourg. Done at Mainz in the year of the Lord 1247.”

And so that noble abbey, once famous and most powerful, of the monastery of Lorsch, was taken away from our sacred order, given into the hands of others, and its lordship was, less justly, as it seemed to many, made subject to the church of Mainz; although prudent and religious men of our order, noble and powerful, could certainly have been found who would have reformed that monastery in both estates. Duke Tassilo of Bavaria built this monastery in the time of Emperor Charlemagne, about the year of the Lord [unclear], and endowed it magnificently; in it he later became a monk, and with Theodo his son ended his life in great religion.

*1233.*

In the aforesaid year, which was the twentieth of John our abbot, a concord was made at Frankfurt between the bishop of Worms and the citizens on the third kalends of March, through Siegfried archbishop of Mainz, Conrad bishop of Speyer, and the margrave of Baden. In the same year many fires occurred in various places.

In this same year brother Conrad of Marburg, as we have already said above under the preceding year, was slain by the knights of those of Dornbach near Marburg.

*1324.*

In this year, which was the twenty-first of John the abbot, our village in Dalen, on the fifteenth kalends of March, was burned by an accidental fire at night, and remained utterly desolate down to the present day. For the demesne lands were transferred here to the village next to the monastery, so that today no trace of the former village in Dalen appears. The church also, long ago built in honor of St. Gregory, perished in the same fire, and lay desolate for almost sixty-two years, so that trees grew in it, until the time of Abbot John, when it was restored anew by Hadwig the widow, as we shall say below in its place. And thus the village around the monastery grew and was enlarged by a multitude of people and houses.
