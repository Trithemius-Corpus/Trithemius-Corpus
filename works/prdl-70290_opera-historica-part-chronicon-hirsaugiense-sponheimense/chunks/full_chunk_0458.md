*Chronicle of Trithemius, 1412.*

*1412.*

In the twenty-first year of our abbot Bernhard, John, bishop of Würzburg, died, killed, as was commonly reported, by [poison?] at [Forchheim?] on the eleventh day of January. Another John, of Brunn, succeeded him and presided for twenty-eight years: a man excessively lavish, who on that account pledged to creditors almost everything belonging to the episcopal table.

*1413.*

In the twenty-second year of our abbot Bernhard, Stephen, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, son of Rupert, formerly king of the Romans, and brother of Ludwig the Palatine with the Beard, took as wife the only daughter of the last count of Veldenz. She bore him [unclear]: Frederick of Simmern, father of Duke John, now dwelling in Simmern, who obtained the county of Sponheim; Ludwig, surnamed the Black, who was father of Duke Alexander and obtained the counties of Zweibruecken and Veldenz; Rupert, bishop of Strasbourg; Stephen, canon and dean of the church of Cologne; and John, bishop of Muenster, and afterwards archbishop of the churches of Magdeburg and primate of Germany.

*1414.*

In this year, which was the twenty-third of Abbot Bernhard, the very famous general council was begun in the city of Constance, by the aid and effort of Sigismund, king of the Romans. It lasted for three continuous years. At it John, Pope XXIII, first appeared; afterwards, in the same place, he was judicially deposed by the council, together with the two others contending for the papacy. He entered Constance in the aforesaid year on the fifth day of November, with great solemnity, received honorably as pope, and he began the council and held a session on Friday, which was the sixteenth day of the same month. In this council, over three years, forty-four public and solemn synodal sessions were held, and many things were laudably established for the peace and unity of the Church. A very great multitude of princes, pontiffs, abbots, prelates, nobles, clerics, and laymen came together there from the whole Christian world, all of whom were unanimously obedient to the decrees of the council.

*1415.*

In the twenty-fourth year of our abbot Bernhard, on the vigil of St. Matthew, Frederick, duke of Austria, was born, afterwards emperor, the fourth of this name. In the same year, while the sacred council of Constance was still continuing, Saint Bridget, formerly queen of Sweden and Norway, was canonized on the vigil of the Purification of St. Mary. In the same year also Sigismund, king of the Romans, with the counsel and assent of the princes, was constituted in the general council at Constance on the twenty-fourth day before the Kalends of May; and from that time the dukes of Cleves began to exist, and they exist down to the present day. In the same year also, when Frederick, archbishop of Cologne, had died, Theoderic, son of the count of Moers, succeeded him and presided for almost forty-eight years, quite energetically.

*1416.*

In the twenty-fifth year of our abbot Bernhard, the men of Berg burned the village of Deutz, situated opposite the city of Cologne across the Rhine, so that Archbishop Theoderic would not station his garrisons there. For there had been hostilities and dissensions between the aforesaid archbishop of Cologne and the duke of the land of Berg. In the same year a certain cloud, burst open at night, fell upon the town of Muenstereifel; and while all were sleeping it overthrew the walls, gates, and the greater part of it, with many men and livestock unexpectedly submerged. Their corpses were found outside the town at a distance of one mile, and much damage was inflicted on the inhabitants of the place, which down to this day has not been repaired.

*1417.*

In this year, which was the twenty-sixth of our abbot Bernhard, all the abbots and prelates of our order throughout the whole province were summoned and cited to the Council of Constance, for instituting the celebration of a provincial chapter and beginning the reform of our order in all the monasteries. Therefore the abbots of our order came together at Constance; among them was Bernhard, abbot of this monastery, summoned and cited with the others. By command of the council itself, in the monastery called Petershausen, of our order, in the city of Constance, they first celebrated a provincial chapter. And because there was no one among all the abbots of the province of Mainz who knew how to celebrate a chapter.
