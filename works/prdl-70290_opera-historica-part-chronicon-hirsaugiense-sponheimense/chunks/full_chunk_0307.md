*Sponheim.*

*On the solemn donation by which Count Megenhard handed over the church and monastery of the order, in the presence of Adelbert, archbishop of Mainz.*

After these things the illustrious Count Megenhard, so that what he had done for the place might gain firmness by authority, went to Adelbert, archbishop of Mainz, together with his brother Count Rudolph; and before him, in the church of Mainz, with many persons present, he resigned this church, before the altar of St Martin, with every right and jurisdiction which he had had over it, into the hands of that archbishop, under this condition, namely, that monks should serve almighty God in it for all the days of the world, under an abbot and under the Rule of the holy father Benedict, as is clear in the letters of confirmation of the same archbishop, which he issued concerning this matter, and which read as follows.

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Adelbert, by the mercy of God archbishop of this holy church of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, wish it to be known to all the faithful, both of future and of the present age, how Lord Megenhard of Sponheim and his wife Machthild, Count Rudolph and his wife [Regnat?], moved by the impulse of divine inspiration, came of their own accord to the altar of St Martin, and, for their own safety and that of their people, and in hope of eternal recompense, conferred forever upon the church of Mainz, with pious devotion, the church in Sponheim with all the property and jurisdiction which they had possessed over it by hereditary right up to that day: under this condition, namely, that monks should be established there according to the Rule of St Benedict, by whom continual praises to God and supplications for them should be frequently made in the same church. These, moreover, are the benefices and estates belonging to the aforesaid church: the tithe of the same village, with the household and demesne land, in fields, vineyards, gardens, meadows, pastures, and woods. In Petersheim, two hides; in Rudersheim, three hides; in Auen, four and a fourth part. In [Montzechaarea], with buildings, and twelve iugera of vineyards. In Wergesbach, two hides. In Eselbrunnen, seven malters of wheat and sixteen of rye from the hide which is called Eberhardt. These, moreover, are the things which the aforesaid Megenhard handed over for himself and for the same church as a benefice: nine hides in Hussen and in Stirscheit, with the demesne land and wood which he had possessed there. In Dalen, eleven hides, in fields, plots, vineyards, meadows, and woods. The aforesaid Lady Riggart also added to the aforesaid goods a meadow and a garden suited for planting vegetables, with two fields adjoining the same meadow. And when these things had been done by the above-named men without any contradiction, we confirmed it by the obligation of our ban. Moreover, we endowed the aforesaid church with the same liberty, honor, and [unclear] which the other abbeys and monasteries in our episcopate enjoy: namely, that it should look only to the bishop; and that, when the brothers serving God there are bereft of their spiritual father, they should choose another from among themselves, if only they find one suitable. But if they do not have among themselves a person suitable for that office, they shall have the liberty of choosing another of their own life and institution, from whatever congregation it may happen, yet within our diocese. Furthermore, at the request of the lords already named above, we have forever remitted the payment of the same church which pertained to us; and we have decreed that the parish priest should look to the abbot, in this manner, namely, that he should be invested by him with the benefices of the church, but with the gift of the altar by the lesser provost.
