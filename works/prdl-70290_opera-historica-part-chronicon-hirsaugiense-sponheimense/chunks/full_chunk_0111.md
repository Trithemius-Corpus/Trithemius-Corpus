He was elected, as is reported, on the first day of the month of August, in the year of the Lord 1091, indiction 14, under Emperor Henry IV and Pope Urban II, and presided over the monastery for 14 years and 3 months. When these had elapsed, he was elected bishop of the church of Speyer, as we shall say in its proper place.

He was, as we have said, of Swabian origin, born of noble stock, in bodily stature neither too short nor ungainly through excessive height, but proportioned with due moderation; adorned with black hair around his head, stout in body, and disposed with handsome balance. When he was still a boy, he was handed over by his parents to teachers to learn letters; in these, over time, he made such progress that he scarcely had an equal in all literature, both secular and divine. For he was a man most learned in every respect and endowed with singular eloquence, both in the Roman and in the Teutonic language.

At length, when the years of boyhood had been passed in the study of letters, he was made a canon in the church of Strasbourg. There, when he daily increased in wealth and honors, he thought of nothing less than conversion. For contrition is rarely accustomed to be present among the rich, especially clerics, because the more they devote themselves to worldly cares, the less they are solicitous for their own salvation. So also this Gebhard the canon, of whom we are speaking, when he was vainly raised up above himself by nobility of birth and abundance of riches, not only showed no good to the poor of Christ, and especially to monks, but even, despising and contemning them, was accustomed to inflict many wrongs upon them.

For the wine which, from the donation of the faithful, accrued in Alsace to the brethren of the monastery of Hirsau, he seized by violence and, against the will of all, turned to his own uses. He was able to do this the more easily because he knew that the holy man William, who agreed with the Roman Church, could hope for no aid of defense from King Henry, to whom he was subject. Yet, admonished by good men concerning this rash deed, he came to Hirsau to reach an agreement with Abbot William, swollen, proud, and exalted, showing in dress and gestures nothing less than love of religion. But almighty God, in whose power are the hearts of all men, who made Saul, the persecutor of the Christian faith, into Paul, its defender, suddenly changed the mind of this man also to another purpose.

For with keen consideration he began to observe the manners and way of life of the brethren, and he heard the holy father discoursing on contempt of the world. Captivated by his speech, with the Holy Spirit acting within, he suddenly changed his purpose; and, bidding farewell to the pomps and cares of the world, he became in this monastery, under the monastic habit, a servant of God.

After he had for some time lived laudably among the brethren, sound and unharmed, in order that his patience might be proved before all, [unclear]. For apart from his tongue, up to that time he could move none of his members. But one day the man of the Lord William entered the cell of the sick man and exhorted Gebhard, as he lay there, to seek from the Lord with devout prayers at least the use of his right hand. He refused, [saying that] with [unclear] purged away he could reach God. But the kindly father, moved by mercy, took his right hand, blessed it, prayed, and kissed him; and suddenly, with all looking on, restored him to his former health.

After a few days, however, the wife of this Gebhard’s brother, a noble lady, came with a large household for the grace of visitation. He was carried to her in a carrying-chair; and when, in conversation, he brought forward the miraculous healing of his arm, she took that healed right hand and, in womanly fashion, kissed it as though it were something sanctified. Then, with all looking on, what had happened suddenly occurred: soon at the touch of the woman his arm was miraculously returned to its former rigidity. Then the holy abbot William said: “This is the punishment for your rashness, brother, because you offered to be kissed by a woman, out of the touch of empty glory, the hand consecrated to God and healed by his own compassion.” Admonished by what had happened, although the devoted [woman] [unclear], he concealed what had occurred, greeted his brother’s wife, and had himself carried back to his cell.
