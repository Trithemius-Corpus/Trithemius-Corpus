*Sponheim.*

Having been ordained vicar on the day of St. Lawrence the martyr, he presided here in the monastery for seven years, one month, and fourteen days, and endured many tribulations and adversities, especially after the death of that count. He was by origin from the county of Sponheim, from the town of Kreuznach, born of humble but honorable parentage; his father, Andreas, and his mother, Barbara, citizens there, sustained a modest life by the labor of their hands. While he was still a little boy he was handed over by his parents to teachers to learn letters; after he had devoted several years’ effort to these, he was at last made a monk in the monastery of St. Disibod, of the Cistercian order. There, since he was subtle in intellect, he advanced in many things beyond those of his own age, among other things also laboring over curious arts, and [more frequently devoted?] study to alchemy and medicine. When he had made some progress in these things, he came into the acquaintance and intimacy of the aforesaid last count of Sponheim; by him he was drawn out of the monastery and made first his chaplain, then his secretary, and finally, as we have said, abbot of this monastery. Yet he was rarely here in the monastery, but was accustomed to stay more often at [Trarbach?] with the count. He had his own house there, in which, with the necessary household, receiving support partly from the count’s table and court, partly from the monastery, he lived apart. Mention of his deeds will be made more fully in order.

*1433.*

In the first year of Gobelin, Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, and Ludwig, landgrave of Hesse, had serious dissensions and enmities between them. After threats, raids, plunderings, and burnings, they at last met for battle in the field; there, after a long and uncertain contest, with not a few killed on both sides, Archbishop Conrad was defeated by Ludwig the landgrave. More than six hundred of his men were captured by that count of the province; these afterward ransomed themselves with money, not without loss to the archbishop.
