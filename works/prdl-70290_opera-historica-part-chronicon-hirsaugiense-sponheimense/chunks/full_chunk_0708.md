*XXIII.*

*Joachim, by the grace of God Margrave of Brandenburg, Prince Elector, to Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St James at Würzburg, greetings.*

[unclear]

Reverend father in Christ, most distinguished man: let your Paternity know that by divine mercy I am sound and unharmed, together with my wife and son. This same thing I wish for your Paternity above all; I pray and beseech, because it greatly delights me to hear often of that same happy condition.

My lord brother, the Archbishop of Cologne, Prince Elector, sent letters to me, and with them he sent your Paternity’s letters, which you gave to him in the month of March, containing an excuse and impediments why you would not come down to him in person after returning from me to your homeland, and why you cannot send without a sure messenger the things entrusted to you by me; all these things pleased me. His Paternity, moreover, gives infinite thanks for the books and other medicines which I entrusted to you to carry to him, if you could, in person together with my letters.

But since at this time you do not intend to go down to him, and since he could ill do without the gifts sent by me for longer, I have sent this messenger of his to you, by whom I again and again ask that you send to him the things that are to be sent. But the other, more secret matters, which I entrusted to be carried by word of mouth, should remain with you until you are able to approach him in person. Farewell, sound and happy, praying to God for us.

From my Cologne on the Spree, on the 28th day of the month of May, in the year of Christ 1507.
