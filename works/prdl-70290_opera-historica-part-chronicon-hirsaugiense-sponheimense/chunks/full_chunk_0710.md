*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

...is held. To him I am accustomed often to say: “Look, the blood of my Trithemius is being sought; and as you have done to him, though he did not deserve it, so it will be done to you according to your deserts.” Duke Johannes himself would not have thought, nor, as I have heard, would he have believed, that you ought to have reckoned that deed, in the capture of your servants, as an injury to yourself, or taken it so much to heart that on that account you would leave your abbey. For if he had known this, he would never have allowed it to be done. But I, knowing what you have written to me, am accustomed to answer those who say such things with that Virgilian line:

“The herdsmen came late.”

The snare of the impious has been broken, and innocent Trithemius, my own, has been delivered, God preserving him. These things, most reverend teacher, I wished to write back to you from the sincerity of my heart, and I ask you again and again graciously to understand them in that spirit. For I am yours, whatever I am; therefore I write confidently whatever comes into my mouth. Master Conradus Antonianus of Cologne read your letter not without tears; as you know, he loves you greatly, honors you in every way, and greets you. Farewell, excellent teacher, and remember to write to me more often, because your letters are more precious and dear to me than gold. Farewell again. From my house in the village of Mandal, on the fourth day of May, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1507.

*XVIII.*

*To the Most Serene Prince, Lord Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, Duke of Stettin and of the Pomeranians, Prince of Rügen and Cassubia, etc., Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St. James at Würzburg, wishes eternal happiness.*

What shall I repay to your majesty, most wise Prince, or in what way shall I ever be able to make return for your benefits? Behold, with how many gifts you have already honored me, a humble and lowly man. First, at Frankfurt, with a golden cup, sufficiently precious and very beautiful. Secondly, when I was staying with you in your March of Brandenburg, you adorned me with very many gifts in gold, silver, and precious stones, whose price was of quite great value. But now, wishing still more abundantly to provide for my poverty, you have sent me three vessels filled with salted fish, the use of which during this Lent has been no little useful and timely for me and my brothers. For what shall I repay to your most sacred majesty for all these things, O most noble Prince, except that I most gladly devote all that I am, can, and am worth to your service? For I am in no way able to return thanks to your Highness; I have and offer as many immortal thanks as I can, offering myself at all times for the services of your Serenity, according to my strength, from the heart of the fathers. Let your Serenity know, excellent Prince, that our Libanius, my dearest teacher, is still living, and healthy and safe; and he promises that he will come to me in the present year, if this can be done in any way. He writes, however, that he was ill almost to death for nearly two months.

Concerning my return to you in the March, most gracious Prince, I presented your letter to my lord, the most reverend bishop of Würzburg; when he had read it, he also handed it to me to read. He would indeed gladly grant assent to your desires, unless he feared that my absence for so long a time would be harmful to me and to my monastery, especially because I am still new in the abbacy, and the rashness of the Scots threatens us with snares. But in order that your Serenity may know his friendly disposition toward you, at length he concluded, as you will clearly understand from the letter of his most reverend Paternity which I am sending, that I should complete for your Highness whatever things have to be written; and when these have finally been finished, I can betake myself to your Serenity, to remain in your March for one or two months. I desire above all to obey your commands in everything, so far as I can with God, and to serve your majesty before all princes. But because I am a man placed under authority, it is necessary for me to submit to the command of a superior. This, however, I ask and advise: that your Serenity deign to have patience until the next coming year; meanwhile I will write here what you have ordered, and when I come I will bring it with me completed. For I trust without any hesitation that, at the appointed time, at the feast of Easter of the following year, my aforesaid most reverend lord bishop, in accordance with his promise, will not deny me leave to come to your Magnificence. I have been able to send nothing remarkable this time, since I have not yet sufficiently corrected the things I have written; nevertheless I shall take care to bring all these with me, and to the best of my ability I shall strive so that I may not seem to have been honored by your Highness in vain, nor your confidence in me be deprived of the fruit hoped for.
