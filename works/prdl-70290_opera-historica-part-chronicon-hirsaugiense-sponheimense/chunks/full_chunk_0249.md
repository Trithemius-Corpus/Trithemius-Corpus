*The Monastery of Hirsau*

*1262.*

In the eighteenth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1262, Richard the hermit died, formerly a priest and monk of our order in Alsace, in his hermitage near Paffenborn, on the Kalends of November, that is, on the feast of All Saints; he had for many years led a hard life in the strictest penance.

In the same year Werner, archbishop of Mainz, excommunicated Philip of Hoenfels, whom King Richard, after returning to England, had appointed captain in the regions of the Rhine, placing his land under ecclesiastical interdict, because he was oppressing too heavily, with new and unwonted exactions and demands, the courts and goods of the clergy of Mainz situated in his lordship, and, though often admonished, had refused to desist. But afterwards, changing his purpose, he remitted the unjust exactions he had presumed to impose on the clergy; and, agreement having been made between them, he was absolved.

*1263.*

In the nineteenth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1263, the citizens of Cologne, after long disputes, wars, and losses, suddenly shut up in a certain house Engelbert their archbishop, who was in the city and suspected no such thing, together with Theoderic of Valkenburg his brother, and compelled him to conditions of peace, although thereafter no such concord was maintained between the parties.

In the same year there was great discord between the aforesaid archbishop and the count of Juelich, which grew even to arms. And so, when the forces of the parties advanced to battle, the archbishop, relying on the aid of Henry, bishop of Utrecht, and of the Hollanders, defeated the men of Juelich, with many slain.

*1264.*

In the following year, which was the twentieth of Abbot Volpold and the year of the Lord's Nativity 1264, the hermit brothers of the rule of Saint Augustine, whom the common people call Augustinians, entered Worms and there built a convent and monastery of their order, with Bishop Everhard consenting and the citizens providing aid and support.

In the same year, on the second day before the Kalends of May, John, the seventh abbot of my monastery of Sponheim, died. He was succeeded by Peter, the eighth abbot, who presided for twenty-five years and four months; in the first year of his rule he gave the church in Genzingen, with the right of patronage, tithes, and all its revenues, to the canons of the greater church of Mainz, for perpetual possession for the augmentation of their prebends.

In the same year also Pope Urban IV died, and the Apostolic See was vacant for five months, until the following year, because of the discord of the brethren.

*1265.*

In the following year, which was the twenty-first, begun, of Abbot Volpold, and the year of the Lord's Nativity 1265, after the Apostolic See had been vacant for five months, Clement IV was elected supreme pontiff, by nation a Narbonese, from the town of Saint-Gilles. He presided for three years, nine months, and twenty-one days, a man of holy conduct and illuminated, as was said, by a prophetic spirit. He enrolled with great honor in the catalogue of the saints Hedwig, formerly duchess of Poland, a widow of wondrous holiness, who had been canonized.

In the same year Abbot Volpold, touched by grave illness, ended his last day on the fifth day before the Kalends of March, having been healthfully fortified by good confession and the ecclesiastical sacraments, after completing the twentieth year of his rule with the same number of days; he was a man of modest conduct and very solicitous about the condition of household affairs, although the monastery in both its conditions declined not a little in his time, and also under certain other abbots, as we shall say in what follows.

*Concerning John, the Twenty-Sixth Abbot of This Monastery, who presided for 10 years, 3 months, and 4 days, and concerning the various deeds of that time.*

After Abbot Volpold had died and been buried in the year and on the day written above, according to custom [unclear] to lament the misfortunes, they appointed the day for the election of a new shepherd, the penultimate day of the month of February; on that day they assembled, and, after the customary prayers had first been offered and the other things that are accustomed to be done in the act of election, after various discussions had been held among them, at last the greater and sounder part elected John, prior of this monastery, as abbot, the rest being divided among various others. Yet these, yielding to the election of the greater number, all unanimously consented to him.
