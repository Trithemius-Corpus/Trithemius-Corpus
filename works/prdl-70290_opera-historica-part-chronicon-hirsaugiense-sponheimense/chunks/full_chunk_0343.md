*Chronicle of Trithemius*

That place, as I had learned from the Angel, was a place of hell: whoever once descends into it will by no means come out for ever.

After this the Angel again led me around through all the places of punishments, in which souls were being tormented, as he said, for their purification. Their multitude was countless, and they were being punished in different places by various kinds of torments, according to the nature of their offenses for which they had not made satisfaction in life. O what misery, what calamity of unhappy souls, whom I saw there suffering the bitterest punishments even for faults which in this life they had neglected to confess and wash away with tears as very slight.

Afterward I came to a certain very deep valley, as it seemed to me, in which there was a certain great and very foul-smelling river. There was a slender bridge, slippery and exceedingly narrow, over the river, stretched from one very high mountain to another at such a height that four towers of the castle in Kreuznach could not, if set beneath it, have touched that bridge. It was so narrow that it could scarcely take two feet, and from either side toward the middle it rose gradually, so that one could climb it only with the greatest difficulty. I saw many souls wishing to cross over this bridge; some of them fell into that dreadful river at the very beginning, others about the middle, and others about the end. It was full of dragons and very great serpents, which stretched their heads upward as if to devour those who fell; and it was a pitiable sight.

Those who fell into the river were submerged: some up to the neck, others up to the knees, and others otherwise, either wholly or in part, according to their deserts. By swimming and repeatedly sinking, they labored with the greatest urgency to reach the bank of the river; some reached it sooner, others later. But as soon as they had reached the bank, far more beautiful than they had been before, they were received by the holy Angels with the greatest rejoicing and were led into the palace of the heavenly kingdom. I saw certain people there, laden with gold or silver, or burdened with any other loads of cares for temporal things, wishing to cross; they fell at the very beginning, and after many labors could scarcely at last reach the bank. For the more often anyone fell from the bridge, the longer he remained in the river. There was no possibility of going back, but only of going forward; all were being driven to plunge into the depth. Some, after being tossed about in that river for many years, labored almost to the failure of their strength.

Meanwhile I saw one naked but very beautiful soul come to the bridge and cross with such speed and assurance that it did not tremble even slightly at anything. I asked the Angel what that soul was, and he said to me: “This is the soul of Theodebert the monk, who, because he possessed nothing besides God, therefore walks securely. For when he was a canon of the church of Mainz, wealthy, young, and powerful, he despised everything for the love of God and became a monk in the monastery of St. Ferrutius, which is called Blidenstadt, where until death he served the Lord in all purity and simplicity of mind and tranquillity of heart. And because for God’s sake he despised everything, God granted him this privilege, that in securely crossing the bridge he should not fall. With the same assurance all those crossed who serve God in a monastery in the true observance of the rule, without occupation in worldly affairs.”

Many other things also I saw and heard there, which I cannot tell on this occasion. Then the Angel took my hand and led me to the places of the blessed spirits, who were exulting in such happiness and joy that no mind can grasp these joys and no human tongue express them. What I saw and heard there I cannot tell; nor, if I went on telling it, would you grasp it. There I saw several whom I had formerly known in the flesh. Among them was my lord Hildebert, formerly pastor in Sponheim and afterward a monk there, who had upon his breast a golden star shining like the sun, because of the light of knowledge and doctrine by which he had recalled many in this life, by teaching and admonishing, to the practice of justice.

What very sweet songs I heard there, what beautiful and delightful things I saw, which have been prepared for those who truly love God, which no one understands unless he receives them. Through all the choirs of Angels I was led in some manner I do not know, and I saw secrets which I neither ought nor can tell. And when I had come to those first, most splendid blessed spirits, they invited me to remain with them. The Angel of the Lord answered them: “He must return to the body. If he has washed away past evils through penitence, if he has guarded himself from sinning thereafter, if he has devoted his zeal to works of justice, he will come to us happier.” [unclear] “preach to those who fear God; and what he has forbidden, you shall cover for ever with silent [unclear].”

When these things had been said, I was immediately led back to my body and ordered to enter. O good God! How [unclear] I dare say: all things that are done or suffered there are spiritual, and greater and more sublime than all mortal things by as much as the spirit is more subtle than the body. But to me, as I was taught in writing by the Angel, they were shown in the likeness of sensible and material things known to me, because I was to return to the body.
