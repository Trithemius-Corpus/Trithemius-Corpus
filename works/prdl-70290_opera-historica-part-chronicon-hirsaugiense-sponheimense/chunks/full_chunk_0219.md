For Lupold, protected by the power of King Philip, had occupied the see of Mainz for a full two years after Siphrid had been expelled, despising the pope’s excommunication, because he was a wicked man, as we have said.

At the same time Bruno, archbishop of Cologne, sent by the pope together with the aforesaid Siphrid, archbishop of Mainz, returned to Cologne; and on the feast of the holy martyrs Protus and Hyacinth he was received with the greatest exultation and joy by all the people and clergy of Cologne. At his arrival peace was restored to the lands, the province, purged of robbers, was quiet, and the counts and nobles of the land subjected themselves to him by oath in obedience. Adolf also, formerly archbishop, came to him and promised him obedience according to the apostolic mandate. But this fair weather in the province did not last long. For after everything had gone according to his wish, Archbishop Bruno was seized by illness, and in the same year, on the day after the feast of All Saints, he departed from this life and was buried in the greater church, not without the tears of many, all of whom hoped that, while he lived, they would have peace in the province. After his death Theoderic, provost of the Apostles, with the counsel and aid of King Otto, was unanimously elected archbishop and presided for twenty-six years.

*1209.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Lutfrid, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1209, Pope Innocent, hearing that the princes of the whole empire had elected Otto as king, and that by their counsel he had taken in marriage the daughter of King Philip, although she was joined to him by blood relationship, for the good of peace and concord, praised the deed, and again sent the cardinals Hugh and Leo into Germany, so that [unclear] was received at [unclear]. For after everything had gone according to his wish, Archbishop Bruno [unclear] of many departed from this life and was buried in the greater church, not without the tears of many, all of whom hoped that, while he lived, they would have peace in the province. After his death Theoderic, provost, was unanimously elected and presided for twenty-six years.

In the same year King Otto entered Italy with great splendor, where the princes of all Lombardy came to meet him, received him with glory, subjected cities and castles to his lordship, and handed over into his hands an infinite amount of money from taxes and tributes reserved from the time of Emperor Henry. After this, proceeding to Rome, with the pope and the cardinals coming to meet him, he was received with honor by the senate, and on the following Sunday after the feast of Saint Michael, that is, on the fourth day before the Nones of October, in the greater church of blessed Peter the Apostle, he was crowned emperor by Pope Innocent himself.

Around the same times Saint Francis began the order of the Friars Minor in Italy with much religion and holiness; in it, in the course of time, many holy and most learned men shone forth, who by word and example magnificently illumined the church.

This year the summer was very violent with thunders, lightning-bolts, and rains, and the winter exceedingly hard and most harsh, and it lasted a long time.

*1210.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Lutfrid, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1210, very grave dissensions arose between the pope and Otto IV, emperor, concerning the March of Apulia, which the emperor asserted belonged to the empire, but the pope to the Roman church, by the testamentary donation of Lady Matilda, daughter of the famous Prince Roger of Sicily and wife of King Conrad, son of Henry IV, emperor. The pope said that this donation, made by a woman without the consent of the princes, was valid for the church; the emperor said it was invalid. At last, when the emperor would not concede that march to the pope, he was excommunicated by him and finally deposed from the empire.

*1211.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Lutfrid, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1211, Emperor Otto, enjoying prosperous successes, subjected all Apulia and Calabria to himself, received the cities in surrender, entrusted castles to his soldiers to be guarded, attempted to exterminate utterly King Frederick, son of Emperor Henry VI, and scorned to obey the apostolic commands. Wherefore the pope, moved by this, ordered Siphrid, archbishop of Mainz, to announce throughout all Germany that the emperor was excommunicated and deposed from the empire; this he did. When this had been done, Henry, count palatine of the Rhine, the duke of Brabant, and many other princes and nobles favoring Otto began, around the feast of Saint Michael, to devastate the whole bishopric of Mainz with fire and plunder, leaving nothing untouched except fortified cities and the strongest castles, but despoiling villages, churches, and monasteries by laying them waste, the whole land almost in [unclear].
