*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

When, therefore, the most reverend abbot Wilhelm had obtained a charter of this tenor, concerning the restoration, donation, liberty, and immunity of his monastery of Hirsau, fortified by the hand and seal of the royal majesty, he gave thanks to God, who does not forsake those who hope in his mercy; and, so that the donation, strengthened by the king, might also be confirmed by the supreme pontiff and secured with greater stability, on the advice of his friends he resolved to go personally to Rome, to obtain from that vicar of Christ also, according to the canonical sanctions, a perpetual privilege of liberty.

*Abbot Wilhelm resolves to go to Rome.*

*Count Adelbert [unclear] provides for the holy abbot.*

*Pope Gregory VII.*

This purpose of the holy man Count Adelbert, now wholly turned to God, advanced not only by counsel but also by help, providing everything that seemed necessary for the accomplishment of so great a matter and for the expenses of the roads. After some days, therefore, the holy father undertook the proposed journey, and, with the Lord protecting him, arrived safe and unharmed at the apostolic see.

At that time the most reverend pope Gregory VII, formerly called Hildebrand, presided over the holy Roman universal Church. He had been taken up as pope from being abbot of the monastery of St Paul outside the city walls, of our order: the most vigorous cultivator and defender of justice and ecclesiastical liberty. When Abbot Wilhelm came to him, he was received by him honorably and lovingly; and, when the causes of his coming had been learned, he most willingly granted all that he had requested for the liberty of the monastery.

*Pope Gregory VII.*

First, he received the place itself, Hirsau, with all things acquired and lawfully to be acquired in the future, persons, bondsmen, present and future possessions, under the protection of the holy Roman Church. Then he confirmed by apostolic authority the royal privilege, a copy of which we have set forth above. Third, he gave such a privilege concerning all these things.

*Privilege of Pope Gregory VII*

Gregory, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to his beloved son Wilhelm, abbot of the monastery of Saint Aurelius, which is called Hirsau, in the bishopric of Speyer, and to his successors forever. Since the holy apostolic faith ought, from the general providence of pastoral rule, to provide and take counsel for the welfare and security of all the sons of the Church, then indeed it is necessary more eagerly and more earnestly to receive the vows of pious intention and religious devotion and to bind them to salutary affection, so that it may gladly meet the petitions of good endeavors, and may establish, to be held firmly by the solidity of its authority, those things that have been instituted for the praise and glory of the divine name. Therefore we too, rejoicing together at the praiseworthy desire of our beloved son Adelbert of Calw, have decreed to assent to his prayers humbly directed to the holy apostolic see, and have effectively granted what was religiously requested. For he, kindled by love of eternal recompense, has restored and finely repaired on his estate called Hirsau a monastery built of old by his forefathers, recently lost, and has more amply enlarged the [unclear] of the brothers serving God there by adding and handing over many possessions and revenues. And, lest in the future the audacity of perverse men should presume to diminish or violate this establishment of his liberality, they request that it be fortified by the privilege of apostolic authority and strengthened by the guardianship of the holy Roman Church, with an annual pension of a gold bezant being given, considering this indeed: that while honor, fear, and obedience are shown by the faithful of holy mother Church to the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, what has been made solid and strengthened by their decree and in their name may not be shaken by the unlawful presumption of any [unclear].

Wherefore we generally embrace under the guardianship and protection of the holy apostolic see that same aforesaid monastery, with all things justly belonging to it at this time and legally conferred, and thereafter to be conferred by authentic conveyance of whatever persons; and, under the [unclear] of the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, we confirm by the testimony of the present writing that they are to be possessed freely and absolutely, and ordered regularly and usefully, by you, son Wilhelm, and your successors, with the disquiet of all those causing trouble removed and forbidden.

We also establish that the forms of constitution, immunity, and liberty which the aforesaid illustrious count Adelbert inserted in his written deed of conveyance and caused to be impressed with the royal seal, are to be diligently observed for the caution of posterity and for [unclear] the attacks of those causing trouble: only those, that is, which do not obstruct the canonical sanctions, so that no one may be neglectful in permitted matters, nor may anything be inflicted concerning forbidden ones. Therefore we decree that the things contained in this page of our [unclear] command and decree are to be observed forever both by you and by all who shall succeed in the rank and place in which you are. But if anyone of the order of priests, clerics, judges, and secular persons, recognizing this page of our constitution, shall attempt to come against it, let him lack the dignity of his power and honor, and let him know that he is guilty before the divine judgment for the iniquity committed. And unless he restores those things that have been wrongly taken away by him, or with fitting penance laments the things unlawfully done, let him be subject to the vengeance of the most sacred [unclear].
