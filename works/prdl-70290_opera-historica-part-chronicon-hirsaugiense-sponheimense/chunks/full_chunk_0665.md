For although, by the favor and piety of princes, sufficient provision could have been made for me, yet because I am a cloistered man and devoted to the study of the Scriptures, I prefer to lie hidden in a poor monastery rather than be held most illustrious among courtiers. I shall imitate that man of innocence who said: I have chosen to be lowly in the house of the Lord, rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners. I shall come, therefore, around the end of September, meanwhile dispatching certain affairs of mine here and at Neometum; when these have been completed, I shall take care to be with you without delay. And I beg that it not be irksome to make this intention of mine known to the most reverend bishop of Würzburg. But if some circumstance intervenes by which I shall be unable to be present at the end of September, do not think at once that my coming is to be despaired of, because I shall certainly come, if God prolongs my life with health, and I shall not disappoint you in your expectation. Meanwhile farewell happily, mindful of me in your prayers to God. Again farewell. From Budori, on the 17th day of September, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*LXXIII. Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, to Roger Sycambro, greetings.*

What I had promised you, most beloved Roger, in my last letter to you, I now do, though in this very brief one, with great willingness. I received the letter of Conrad, abbot of St. Stephen, on the 16th day of this month at Budora, in which he indicated that the most reverend prelate of Würzburg, Laurence, was well disposed toward my affairs; and also that he was offering me the abbey of St. James, formerly of the Scots, not only willingly but indeed most cheerfully, and had commanded me to hasten as quickly as possible to accept it. I shall therefore remain here at Neometum for only a few days, for the dispatch of certain affairs of mine; when these have been arranged, we shall direct our journey toward Würzburg. But before we depart from these regions, we wish to see and speak with you. Yet since, as you know, we are quite unable to come to you because of the savagery of the incarnate demon, who is to be called a devil rather than a man, not by nature but rather by imitation, we urge, ask, and beseech you again and again to come to us. For we are turning over in our mind many things to discuss with you which we cannot commit to letters. We have also written to your prior, asking that by his authority he grant you permission to come. Bring with you your little book on the varied use of letters; for there are certain things written in it, if I remember rightly, for which a more delicate file will be necessary, especially in those matters that concern the Greek language, in the understanding of which you have not yet made sufficient progress. I shall assist as much as I can, lest this little work of yours go out from your workshop into public uncorrected. Break off delays, since we shall not remain here beyond four days. Farewell. From the city of Neometum, on the 20th day of September, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*LXXV. Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, to Richmodis von Horst, abbess in Sebach, greetings.*

Today, dearest daughter in Christ, the eighteenth month is passing since we have been living away from our monastery, moved by the rashness of rivals. Whence, after many counsels and mature deliberation, having resolved that we must by no means return to Spanheim any more, it has at last been worthwhile to make provision where we should stay. A certain abbey is offered to us, situated in the suburb of the city of Würzburg, which we know well: poor and modest indeed, but very quiet and tranquil, and, as we hope, sufficiently suitable for our purpose; persuaded by the counsel of friends, we have judged that it should not be spurned. Your charity knows very well with how many labors we have been afflicted for twenty-three continuous years now in Spanheim, not only in the government of our own monastery, but much more for the common utility of the whole order. But now, after we have plainly experienced the brothers' ingratitude toward us, we do not believe it safe to remain with them any longer, since neither will they ever hereafter be faithful to us, nor shall we truly be able any longer to trust those by whom we have been so enormously deceived.
