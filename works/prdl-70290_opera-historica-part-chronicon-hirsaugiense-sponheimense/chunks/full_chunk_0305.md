*The Chronicle of This Monastery of Sponheim, from the Beginning of Its Foundation, Briefly Comprising the Successions of All the Abbots, Begins Happily.*

[unclear]

In the year from the incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ 1043, the illustrious Count Eberhard founded the church in Sponheim. It was called in ancient times “of the field”; he founded it in the fifth year of Emperor Henry, the third of this name, in the time of Pope Benedict IX, when the holy church of Mainz was governed by Bardo, the venerable bishop, a man in all respects most holy, who also consecrated that same church in honor of the most blessed Mary, Mother of God and Virgin, on the eighth day before the Kalends of July, in the year of the Lord 1056.

Count Eberhard, for the remedy of his soul, conferred on that same church no few goods, from the proceeds of which those who served the altar in that church might live. First, the tithe of the village of Sponheim, with the household and demesne land in fields, vineyards, gardens, meadows, pastures, woods, cultivated land, uncultivated land, and land to be cultivated, roads and trackless ways. Also in Pedersheim, two manses. In Rudesheim near Kreuznach, three manses of demesne land. In the same place, four and a quarter manses, which tenants possessed. In Hagenheim, two manses and two iugera of vineyards. In Basenheim, a manse and six iugera. In Dromersheim, half a manse and four vineyards. In Mannendal, a manse. In Bockenau, two manses. In Auen, four and a quarter manses. In Moncecha, a plot with buildings and twelve iugera of vineyards. In Wernigsbach, two manses. In Escelbrun, eight maldra of wheat and sixteen of rye, from the manse called Eberhard’s.

These and certain other things the aforesaid count conferred on the church founded by him, and he installed in it certain clerics who were to read masses. But he reserved to himself and his heirs the right of instituting clerics to benefices in that church. Saint Bardo, the aforesaid archbishop, also confirmed this solemnly made donation. And so for almost seventy years the church of Sponheim stood before it was raised into a monastery. There were four altars in it, which, according to custom, were conferred on successive priests by the elder count of Sponheim.

*How and When the Church of Sponheim Was Raised into a Monastery by Stephen, Count of Sponheim, and His Son Megenhard.*

In the year of the Lord 1101, in the ninth indiction, when Emperor Henry IV held the Roman Empire and Ruthard, the venerable archbishop, governed the church of Mainz, Stephen, the illustrious count of Sponheim, forewarned by divine inspiration, took care to raise into a monastery his church on the Montis Campi, founded long before by his ancestors, as we have said. He was a good man and feared God; for God’s honor he bestowed many benefactions on the ministers of the church and on the poor. In the year noted above he began to build places and dwellings for monks, suitable according to the rule of Saint Benedict. But, almost hindered by various occupations intervening, he did not continue the work begun. Therefore, when at last he saw that he was already on the point of dying, he called to him his son Megenhard, heir of his paternal estate, and entrusted to him the task of completing the building begun for the monastery; and so he rested in the Lord on the fifth day before the Kalends of March, in the year of the Lord 1118.
