*1228.*

In the twelfth year of Abbot Eberhard, and in the year of the Lord's Nativity 1228, the Emperor Frederick, having already for many years been signed with the cross, at last undertook the journey overseas with many other princes; nevertheless Pope Gregory meanwhile caused him, as excommunicate, to be proclaimed throughout all Germany, especially by the Friars Preachers.

In the same year Odakar and Wenceslaus, kings of Bohemia, gave such a privilege to the church of Mainz that the consecration of the kings of Bohemia should belong to the archbishop of Mainz for all future times forever. This was observed for many years, until at last by a certain archbishop of Mainz this right was sold to the church of Prague.

Also in the year noted above a grave dissension arose between Berthold, bishop of Strasbourg, and his kinsmen, the counts of Pfirt; and as it grew stronger day by day, almost the whole province for nearly three years was laid waste and lay open to the fire and plundering of robbers. Therefore, after many injuries had been suffered, the bishop, having gathered an army of his men, together with Albert, count of Habsburg, who himself also had sustained many losses, prepared himself to resist. The counts of Pfirt, then, having as aid Egon, count of Freiburg, and the imperial cities between Blodelsheim and Hirnfeld, met the bishop with a very great army; and when battle was joined, they were defeated by him, many of them being killed, others wounded, others led away into captivity.

*1229.*

In the thirteenth year of Abbot Eberhard, and in the year of the Lord's Nativity 1229, the holy city of Jerusalem, with certain castles, was recovered by the Emperor Frederick II, under certain conditions, from the sultan of the Egyptians without any engagement or shedding of blood, and was handed over again into the hands of the Christians. The Emperor Frederick himself, moreover, on Laetare Sunday entered Jerusalem crowned; and after making truces with the Saracens for ten years, on the next day he set out for the ships and returned to his own land. But when he entered Sicily, he found that many towns, castles, and fortifications had gone over to the pope's side. Recovering all these by the strong hand of the Germans, he utterly destroyed some of them; and from that time grave enmities arose between him and the pope.

*1230.*

Now in the fourteenth year of Abbot Eberhard, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1230, concord was made between King Henry, the counts of Pfirt, and Berthold, bishop of Strasbourg, and the land fell silent from the tumult of wars. But it could not be cleansed of thieves and robbers, who by night, breaking the doors of monasteries and churches, since they had been accustomed to live from plunder, secretly carried off whatever they could find.

In the same year in Spain the king of Leon and his son the king of Castile, taking up arms against the Saracens, occupied a great part of the land, powerfully taking the city of Merida with many castles from their hands. The king of Aragon also, in this same year, took by armed force the island of Majorca, which was once a kingdom and had for many years been possessed by the Saracens; he led away its king captive with the whole royal household, entrusted the island to his Christian knights to be guarded, and carried off much spoil.

In the same year also a dissension arose between the archbishop of Cologne and the duke of Limburg over the advocacy of the monastery of Siegburg of our order, which each contended belonged to his own right. From this fires and plundering followed around the territories of both, until at last truces were interposed by King Henry.

In this same year the Emperor Frederick was absolved by Pope Gregory at the entreaty of Leopold, duke of Austria, and restored to the communion of the Church.
