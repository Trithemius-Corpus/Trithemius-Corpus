These also, Roger, are the works which, being established in this present year in this abbey of Saint James, I composed by lamplight, the sequence of whose titles you have so often desired to know. Some things are still in hand unfinished; I shall let you know their prefatory titles when they have been brought to completion.

I have resolved, however, hereafter, if the Most High wills it, who alone can do all things, to compose a work, indeed the greatest of all my works, which is to be divided into twelve books. Its subject will be: concerning demons and the profane arts and malefices of the same, by which they wretchedly deceive the human race. And it is my intention, to the extent of my powers, to drive out and refute all the little superstitions of demons’ arts among men, and to demonstrate in many ways from the very books of the fools who handed them down how utterly vain, pernicious, and detestable they are. For the work seems to me necessary, especially in these times, in which men are excessively curious, some learned, and likewise some unlearned, seeking out things which they would better not know.

Nevertheless, being not a little deterred by the magnitude of the arduous labor, I have thought it useful to put off this intention of mine for a time, so that meanwhile the material necessary for so great a work may gradually be gathered into one. These are the things which, in my judgment, had to be written back to you in reply to your letters. But if hereafter I add anything to the light, you shall not be among the last to know. Farewell, mindful of us before God. From Würzburg, on the last day of August, in the year of Christians 1507.

*LII.*

*To the Reverend Father in Christ, Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James in the suburb of Würzburg, his distinguished and most learned preceptor, Bartholomew Lescanius, unworthy priest, chaplain of Margrave Albert, wishes eternal salvation.*

Although I spent only a short time with you, reverend father, in Ursulo, yet I gained no small benefit from that very association, since by your medicine, through the mercy of the Lord God, I was cured and fully recovered from the very grave pain in my head, which for eighteen years continuously and without any interruption I endured and from which I could not be relieved by any of the physicians. For this I give your paternity infinite thanks, and I offer, give, and devote myself wholly as your possession. May almighty God deign to preserve your reverence safe and sound forever in body and soul, just as your kindness mercifully looked upon me, a wretched man deserving pity, and cured me of so grave and long-lasting a pain of the head, concerning which all our physicians, as many as I consulted, always despaired. O my only hope after God, excellent promoter of health, I cannot set down with my slender pen how greatly I am bound to you in love and inclined toward you, so much so that, if it pleased your paternity, I would wish and desire to bind myself as your servant for all the days of my life, together with those things which I possess. For the remembrance of your gracious paternity is always with me, nor can it ever be erased from the memory of my heart for a single moment, since there is no hour in which I do not think of your presence. And I am very sad; many of the citizens of Berlin grieve deeply with me over the continual absence of your paternity, all of us desiring nothing more than, if it were possible, that you should sometime return to us. For many of us know that our most illustrious prince greatly desires your return; he is said daily to await your arrival. O, if your paternity should quickly return to us, with how great a joy you will be received by us! Let your paternity know that, through the promotion of Lord Georg, prior and your very dear friend, I have been made chaplain of the illustrious and magnificent prince Albert, Margrave, with whom I seem to myself to be in a place of paradise, because, although he is young, yet he lives very religiously and conducts himself most honorably in all things. Praise to almighty God, who does not abandon those who hope in him; may his kindness deign to preserve your paternity, deservedly to be honored by me, happy forever. Amen. From Berlin, on the thirteenth day of the month of May, in the year of the Lord 1507.
