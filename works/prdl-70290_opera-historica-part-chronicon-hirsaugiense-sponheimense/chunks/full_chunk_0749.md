*Familiar Letters*

First of all, when I was twenty-three years old, I compiled from the writings of learned men a little summary of the virtues in two books, which I have not yet been able to bring to light. This work begins thus: “Great is the virtue of the soul: to love virtue.”

At the urging also of Johannes Curtenefius, now provost of the nuns at Neuburg near [Budorim], then a monk and my chaplain, I wrote two books on the temptations of monks, which have not yet been published. This work begins thus: “The human race.”

Finally, at the urging of Nicolaus Stauronefius, then my claustral prior at Sponheim, I wrote two books on the Rule of our holy father Benedict, which begins thus: “Come, children, listen to me; I shall teach you the fear of the Lord.”

At the request also of the aforesaid Johannes Damius, I wrote two exhortatory books for monks, which lie hidden with him. This work begins thus: “It befits Christian warfare.”

At the urging of a certain friend, I wrote one book on the misery of the present life, in which I tried to lead the reader’s mind to contempt of the world. This work was printed some time ago; it begins: “Since life is nothing,” etc.

Against the vice of private ownership among monks and cloistered persons, I wrote one book, at the urging of a certain monk in Blidenstadt, which was printed at Mainz many years ago. This work begins thus: “All and life,” etc. [unclear]

I also composed, with great labor, the chronicle of my then monastery of Sponheim, from the time of its foundation down to the year of the Christians 1502; in it I arranged most diligently, as far as I could discover them, the successions, times, and deeds of all its abbots from the first down to myself, making a large volume of histories, in which under individual years I inserted, by way of digression, many matters worthy of remembrance in Germany. This work begins thus: “In the year of the Lord.”

On the manner and form of visiting cloistered persons I wrote a book, which some time ago was printed at Nuremberg by command of the fathers. It begins thus: “You will visit your brothers, if rightly.”

On the manner and form also of celebrating a provincial synod I wrote one book by command and order of the fathers, which was printed together with the preceding one. It begins: “Since in celebrating.”

The statutes also of the provincial chapters, from the time of the Council of Constance down to the chapter celebrated at Hirsau, I compiled by command of the fathers, abbreviating them into one book; this was printed at Nuremberg together with the two preceding works. It begins thus: “Since for one wishing each [unclear] of the order statutes.”

The statutes also of the annual chapter of the fathers of the Observance I reduced, at their order, into one book by abbreviating them in a more polished style; it is kept at Cologne in the monastery of Martin, and it is kept at Sponheim. It begins thus: “To the reverend in Christ.”

At the urging of Johannes, abbot of Bursfelde, I described the manner and form of celebrating the annual chapter. Before I completed this work he died, and the little book remained at Sponheim with the others. It begins thus: “To the reverend father lord Johannes.”

At the prayers of Blasius, abbot of Hirsau, I began to write the chronicle of that same monastery and completed the greater part; but when he died I discontinued the edition, uncertain with what reward his successor would be going to compensate me.

At the prayers of the same abbot Blasius I wrote a book on the ruin of our order, which I entitled “Mournful.” This work was printed at Mainz; by command of the fathers it has been customary for it always to be read at table in the provincial chapter. It begins: “Since the most ancient beauty of our order.”

At the urging of Gerlach von Breitbach, abbot of Deutz near Cologne, I wrote one book On the Praise of Scribes, which was printed at Mainz. It begins: “To the venerable father lord,” etc.

At the prayers of certain fathers of the Carmelite order, I wrote two books: in the first of these I set forth the beginning, growth, and praises of that same order; in the second I recorded the illustrious men who shone in it. This work was printed at Mainz. It begins: “By the prayers and urgency of the venerable,” etc.

On the praises also of Saint Anne, mother of the untouched and most chaste Virgin Mary, the Mother of God, I wrote one book at the prayers of Rumold, prior of Frankfurt, of the aforesaid order. It has been printed and begins: “The necessity of a vow compels.”
