*982.*

In the following year, which was the second of Abbot Lupold, Otto died at Rome on the sixth day before the Ides of December, and was honorably buried in the church of St. Peter, prince of the apostles. His son, Otto of the same name, succeeded him and reigned for almost eighteen years. He had as wife Maria, daughter of the king of Aragon, who was barren, whence he received no offspring.

*985.*

In the third year of Abbot Lupold, Warinus, archbishop of Cologne, died; Everger succeeded him. In these times Lubentius, archbishop of Bremen in Saxony, formerly a monk of Hersfeld, flourished, a man zealous in the holy Scriptures, who vigorously governed the people committed to him for twenty-five years.

Osdacus also, formerly a monk of the monastery of Reichenau, afterward made bishop of Hildesheim after Othwin, is held to have been distinguished about these times, and he vigorously ruled the church divinely committed to him by word and example for twenty-nine years.

In these times also there flourished in England Osbern, a monk of the monastery of [unclear] of our order, a man very renowned both in divine Scriptures and in secular letters; indeed there exists a notable work in which, beginning from the origin of that city, he describes the deeds of the bishops of [unclear], inserting the life or passions of several saints.

He also composed, on music, various chants with sweet melody, and also another monk of our order, of the monastery of [unclear].

In these same times also at Mainz, in the monastery of St. Alban the Martyr of our order, Theoderic the monk flourished, very learned in divine Scriptures and in secular letters. He wrote the successions and deeds of the bishops of Mainz. He also commented on the Song of Songs; likewise he composed very fine commentaries on the Gospel of John. But he left to posterity many other splendid monuments of his talent, the titles of which do not now occur to memory.

In the same year also Willigis, archbishop of Mainz, anointed [unclear] the third as king, and after three years he was consecrated emperor at Rome by Pope Gregory V. Overcoming Lothar, king of the Franks, who was attacking Lotharingia, near Verdun, he put him to flight, and compelled him to restore all the abbeys. About these times there also flourished Bruno, a monk of the monastery of St. Vitus of Gladbach, of our order, in the diocese of Cologne, a very studious man and excellently learned both in divine Scriptures and in secular letters. He is said indeed to have written many works, but few of them have come to our knowledge; of these we have made mention elsewhere.

In these times also there flourished in this monastery of Hirsau a noble man, studious and excellently learned in every variety of writings, a priest and monk named Adelbert. On account of the merit and eminence of his life, his learning, and the philosophy of his reputation, he was taken up as abbot of the monastery of Klingenmünster. Would that he had been placed over this monastery; perhaps the condition of the monastery of Hirsau would never have fallen into such desolation and ruin, of whose misery we shall speak after a little. He governed the aforesaid monastery [unclear], taking care to show [unclear] in all things. In all Germany his name was famous, and princes and bishops of churches depended much upon his counsel.

*Concerning Hartfrid, the ninth abbot of this monastery, who presided for only two years, and concerning certain deeds of that time.*

In the year of the Lord 986, in the fourteenth indiction, Lupold the abbot, after completing the fourth year and three months of the rule he had undertaken, died on the eighth day before the Kalends of December, and was buried in the church of the monastery of St. Aurelius. He was, as regards his own person, an excellent man, to whom nothing was sweeter than monastic quiet, silence, and contemplation. Yet he was somewhat more indulgent concerning the sins of others and readier for mercy.
