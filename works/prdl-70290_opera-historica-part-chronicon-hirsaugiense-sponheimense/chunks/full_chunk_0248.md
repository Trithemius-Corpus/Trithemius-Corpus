*Chronicle of Jo. Trithemius.*

*1259.*

In the fifteenth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1259, the monastery of Saint Disibod, of our order, in the county of Sponheim, in the diocese of Mainz, near the town of Sobernheim, was transferred from our order by Gerard, archbishop, and the canons of the church of Mainz, and given to fathers of the Cistercian order. Their first abbot, named Johannes, as though in thanksgiving for the benefit received, with the consent of his brethren, promised back to the canons of the aforesaid church, from the revenues of the same monastery, an annual rent of one hundred malters of wheat, to be rendered every year at Mainz for all time, as appears from the letters drawn up concerning this matter, whose date is the day before the Nones of March, in the aforesaid year, at Mainz, done in the presence of many witnesses.

In the same year Albert, surnamed the Great, of the Order of Preachers, of the convent of Cologne, a Swabian by nation, a man most learned in every respect, by the provision of Alexander IV was made bishop of Regensburg in Bavaria, when he was sixty-six years of age; but not long afterward he resigned.

Also in the same year Arnold of Isenburg, archbishop of Trier, in the town called Montabaur, in the seventeenth year of his pontificate, closed his last day; he lived magnificently in every respect and did many things vigorously in peace and war. He took the very strong castle of Thuron after it had been besieged for two years. He most beautifully fortified anew a great part of the city of Trier with a wall, and likewise enclosed the monasteries of Meinfeld and Koblenz with a wall. When he had died and had been buried with honor in the greater church of Trier before the altar of the Holy Trinity, two men were elected by the chapter in disagreement, namely Arnold of Sleyda and Henry of Bolanden. When they were contending over their elections before him at Rome in consistory, Pope Alexander IV, by the counsel of the brethren, annulled them, and [unclear], provost of the church, and he presided for twenty-four years and six months, a man excellent in ability and prudence, who ordained the bishop; and when he came to Trier with the pallium and apostolic letters, he was received by all the clergy and people of the city as archbishop, and he presided for twenty-seven years, a steadfast and [unclear] man.

Also in the aforesaid year, on the last day of the month of April, the city of Worms, again set alight by accidental fire, burned through the whole night around the hour of Compline, and with the loss of many people was consumed almost by half.

*1260.*

In the sixteenth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1260, in the third indiction, Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, died. Werner of Falkenstein, provost of the greater church, succeeded him and presided for four years, a man excellent in ability and prudence, who governed his church very well and greatly increased its revenues.

In the same year two knights, Jacob of Lapide and Simon of Guntheim, [unclear] enmities [unclear]. For in those times, since there was no king in the lands, [unclear], movements of robbers [unclear], things were done rather at whim.

Also in the aforesaid year, on the last day of the month of April, the city of Worms, again set alight by accidental fire, burned through the whole night around the hour of Compline, and with the loss of many people was consumed almost by half.

*1261.*

In the seventeenth year of Abbot Volpold, and the year of the Lord's Nativity 1261, in the fourth indiction, Conrad of Hochstaden, archbishop of Cologne, died; he had many disputes with his citizens during his pontificate. He was buried in the greater church of Cologne. Engelbert II of Valkenburg, provost of Saint Gereon, succeeding him by election of the chapter, presided for almost fourteen years; he too had many and continual dissensions with the people of Cologne.

In the same year Pope Alexander IV died. Urban IV, a Frenchman by nation, succeeded him and presided for three years, one month, and four days. He instituted the feast of the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ, to be celebrated every year on the Thursday after the octave of Pentecost throughout the whole Christian world, and granted indulgences in its favor. The kingdom of Sicily also, which Manfred, duke of Swabia, son of the late Emperor Frederick II, had occupied after the death of [Conrad, his brother?], as we have said, Urban conferred on Charles, count of Provence, brother of the king of the Franks, and summoned him from Gaul into Italy through legates; and he occupied Sicily after Manfred had been put to flight, not without the bloodshed of many.
