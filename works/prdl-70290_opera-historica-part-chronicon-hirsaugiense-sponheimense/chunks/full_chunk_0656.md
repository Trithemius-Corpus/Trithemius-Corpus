*Familiar Letters*

Benno does not yet seem among us to have been enrolled in the catalogue of the saints, though we do not doubt that he reigns with the saints in heaven; [lest] we be compelled to desist from venerating him, and it not be permitted us to invoke the intercessions of so great a man. But since his most holy life, full of virtues, is clearly described by older men and by his own contemporaries, and since many miracles are done daily in the church of Meissen at his tomb, the aforementioned George, most upright and most Christian prince of the Saxons and duke of Meissen, lover, cultivator, and defender of Christianity, moved by pious devotion, judged that efforts should be made for his canonization, and obtained from your Holiness's predecessor, Pope Alexander VI, commissioners to examine each point. But Alexander, carried off by death, could not carry through the matter of the holy Benno, and perhaps ought not to have done so, because divine providence assigned the honor of so great a man to your times, and reserved for you the prelate most dear to itself for the catalogue of the saints. Therefore, most blessed father, we beg your Holiness with what humility we can: grant that Benno, an apostolic man, be venerated by us, and enroll his name to be celebrated with the holy confessors of God, and do not allow what has been reserved for your gift to pass into the glory of another. Believe me, greatest of pontiffs: by conferring Benno upon the holy fathers you will have enlarged the worship of God in no small measure; you will also obtain everlasting remembrance on earth for your most celebrated name, and, with the holy Benno interceding, for the enlarged divine worship you will receive a good reward in heaven. This we all, as many as have experienced the merits of the holy Benno either in ourselves or in others, unanimously ask of your most holy majesty. May we be heard by you, most merciful father; again and again we beg it, and you will do a thing pleasing to God, useful and honorable to the Christian people, and to be remembered by us, your suppliants, with perpetual gratitude. May almighty God, whose vicar you are on earth, long preserve your Beatitude unharmed for our salvation.

From Büdingen, 21 June, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*LXV.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, to his beloved [sons].*

To his most beloved sons in Christ, sincere charity in the Lord.

How quickly the minds of men change we experience daily enough, not only among strangers, but also, what we greatly wonder at, among you, if indeed the things reported to us in your name by your prior are true. You beg that we return to the monastery; yet the obstacles which not undeservedly offend our mind, about which we have often given orders, you have not yet removed. Consider, brothers, whom this negligence of yours can rather harm. Do not think that I am panting after Sponheim, for elsewhere, with God's help, I can easily provide better for myself. I only have pity on you; unwillingly do I abandon you, whom, gathering together in Christ, I nourished. But if through you occasion should be given me whereby I may justly abandon you, know for certain that I shall never for all eternity agree to return to you, but shall follow the fortune offered me. You are not ignorant what rash injury our rivals have done us, although they themselves now say that they planned nothing out of hatred of us, but rather took up the injury against those who brought threats against the abbot of Mainz. But why you advise letters of request to the duke is a cause forbidding us, especially since it is manifest that we have never sinned in anything against him, whereas he is [unclear] in mind against us, and for what reason is not hidden from us. But you, as seems good to you, deal with him; we indeed shall not write to him on our behalf. It has pleased us, not without reason, to defer the whole matter until the annual chapter, which will shortly be held at Mainz, so that we may both ascertain the opinion of the fathers and be shown not to have passed them by in so arduous a matter. Meanwhile bear with it a little, since, when the chapter has been held, we shall make our mind manifest to you in deed. Farewell, and keep the memory of your pastor before God.

From Büdingen, the 6th day of July, in the year of the Christians 1506.
