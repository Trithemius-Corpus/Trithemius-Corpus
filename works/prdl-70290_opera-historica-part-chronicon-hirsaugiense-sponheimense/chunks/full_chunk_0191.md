But when Abbot Manegold saw that he could by no means bring the brothers back to concord, being a lover of his own salvation he wished to resign the abbacy. But lest he should rashly offend the eyes of the divine majesty, he sought counsel on this matter through a trusted messenger and by letters from the blessed virgin Hildegard, to whom he sent this letter:

To the most holy lady and his venerable mother Hildegard, Manegold, unworthy abbot of Hirsau, [sends] prayer with service. I have indeed heard that among the Cyrenians there is a certain [unclear] vein of waters, and [unclear]; I know you, and have found by experience among the Alemanni, good mother, to be the vein [unclear] of those entering and going out, and the bosom of kinds in things, like the pupil of the eye [unclear]. For you draw, O my lady, and pour forth; you give form and distinguish into species; and in practical matters [unclear] into contemplation [unclear]. Therefore it has been my impulse, and long has been, to love you, to honor you, to admire you, to serve you and yours, and in all things, by word and deed, to be yours and of your women, in prayers insofar as is right for me, and in services. I ask you, my mother and lady, remember me in your most holy prayers; love one who humbly loves you; acknowledge one who reveres you in Christ; and write back to me by letter concerning the questions asked, having been petitioned in God.
