*LV.*

*To the Reverend Father in Christ, Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, Brother Jacobus Trithemius Sends Greeting.*

[unclear]

The letters of your paternity brought me great joy, by which I learned that you are safe and well. Moreover, you wish to know my condition. Know briefly: it is middling. By God’s goodness I am healthy, and rich without money. After your departure from Neometum I came to Wachenheim, and after staying there a few days, I soon perceived that men, forgetful both of my services and of your benefactions, were casting grim little glances at me, and, envying my studies, were recalling me, unwilling, to stones and mud. Therefore I did not wish to remain longer with ungrateful and cold-hearted men, who neither remember benefits nor fulfill promises. As you know, I served the abbot for many days at Cologne as a scribe, and on account of him I offended those whom I could have had as friends; I spent time with him without profit, wore out my clothes, and obtained absolutely nothing from him as wages. The church which he wished to assign to me is very poor, in the valley of the willows; the priestly dwelling-house, together with the village, has been consumed by fire. Let him have it for himself and be well, because I have no mind to remain with him. Would that I had yielded to you rather than to his requests, and had gone on with you into the March; it would be better for me than to have served him for nothing. Therefore, despising that most empty hope, I went down to Trier and stayed for some time with my parents, until I advanced through the several grades to the priestly order. Now hereafter I shall do what you urge, and I shall remain with our Damius until you come. Farewell, in health. From Speyer, on the second day of the month of November, in the year 1505.

*LVI.*

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, to Jacobus Trithemius, His Most Loving Brother, Greeting.*

[unclear]

By God’s grant we are still well; if you too are well, it is good. It was not unknown to us whom you served, whose ungrateful [unclear] divinity we knew very well, having long since experienced it. But since it pleased you so, if you have lost your labor, you will know that it must be imputed to yourself. As soon as you can, write to our parents that we are safe and sound, and that they should have care for their old age, not being anxious about us. Almighty God, by his mercy, will, as we trust, provide what is necessary for us without the anxiety of our parents, even if we never return to Spanheim. The prior Remittit wrote humble and very sweet letters, desiring that we return to the monastery as soon as possible; answering him, we gave words in return for words, until we may examine the minds of the men more fully. For henceforth we shall not easily believe, having once been so enormously deceived. Therefore, when we have returned to Neometum or Budoris, we shall act so that the dispositions of the brothers do not escape us, and then at last we shall do what seems to us more useful. But if it should happen that we remain outside, it is nothing to us, since we choose rather to lack an ungrateful monastery than [unclear] wicked men. Farewell, from Berlin, on the eighth day of the month of January, in the year of the Christians 1506.
