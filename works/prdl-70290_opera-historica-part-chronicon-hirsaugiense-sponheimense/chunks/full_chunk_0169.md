…was buried. He presided over the church of Worms strenuously for 36 years; to whom in the episcopate succeeded Conrad, the first of this name, who sustained many persecutions from many. For in his time Conrad, count Palatine of the Rhine, brother of the first emperor Frederick, violently plundered the possessions of the churches; whom, since no one could resist (because the days were evil), the ministers of the Lord were compelled either to redeem the things taken away with money, or patiently to tolerate the injuries with loss. There is extant a letter of this bishop Conrad to the holy virgin of Christ Hildegard, abbess on the Mount of St Rupert near Bingen, in which he mentions the calamities which he suffers, and humbly asks her counsel and the support of [her] prayers. To whom the holy virgin writes back words of holy admonition, divinely inspired, and makes [him], as he had asked, a participant in her prayers.

[1147.] In the year of abbot Volmar 26, which was of the Lord's nativity 1147, an eclipse of the sun occurred, the 7th [day before] the Kalends of November, around almost the fourth hour of the day. In that year a famine followed. After the famine, too, pestilence and a great mortality followed, incredible to all posterity.

In the same year too Embricho, bishop of Würzburg (of whom we spoke above), returning from Greece, dies at Aquileia, and is buried there; for whom Siegfried is constituted bishop. Eggebert, bishop of Bamberg, also dies, in whose place Everhard is ordained. Conrad the king entered Hungary with a strong hand.

In the same year the greatest army of the Christians—from all Germany, Gaul, Italy, Spain, England, Hungary, indeed from all Europe—after the paschal feast, was moved to recover the holy land, the cross being preached by Bernard, by command of Pope Eugenius, against the infidels; whose journeyings and deeds, since they have been copiously described by many, we have resolved to pass over in silence.

In the same year St Malachy, archbishop [of Armagh], coming from Ireland to Clairvaux to visit holy Bernard, migrated to the Lord, the 3rd [day before] the Nones of November, a man of apostolic life, the very form of all perfection and the example of virtue; who, among the other marks of his virtues, raised a dead man, reformed the faith in Ireland, [and] wonderfully spread holy religion. So holy a man, Bernard the abbot, described [his life]. About these same times the same St Bernard the abbot, to preach the cross of the Lord against the infidels, by command of Pope Eugenius, was traveling through Germany, and shone forth everywhere with many signs and miracles. For in the territory of Constance, in one day he enlightened eleven blind men, healed ten maimed, and to eighteen lame, the powers of walking restored, fully reformed [them].
