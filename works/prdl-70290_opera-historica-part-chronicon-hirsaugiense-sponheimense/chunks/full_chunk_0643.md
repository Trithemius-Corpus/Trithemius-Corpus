*Johannes Trithemius*

... if lying men have proceeded against us, instruct them; but if not, behold, I again declare to you that I shall not have him as lord who does not have me as prelate, nor shall I submit myself to the command of one who, armed with injustice and tyranny, does not wish to be subject to the justice of God. I have favored Palatine affairs. For this reason they have persecuted me without cause. What will my rivals do if I say, indeed, that I shall favor them as long as the breath of life remains in me? The lord defender of my monastery, as you know always a most gracious prince to us, Philip the Palatine, in those things that are not against the Lord of all, I shall never be able to disown. Permit to each man his right inviolate. Farewell, brothers; pray for me, placed in a foreign land. From Berlin, on the twentieth day of the month of October, in the year 1505.

*XLII*

*To the most serene and unconquered prince, Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, prince elector. Johannes Trithemius sends greeting.*

Most serene prince, I am compelled to remain in the March longer than I had intended, detained by the command of the most excellent prince Joachim, Margrave, whose most holy will I ought not to oppose. For he pursues my smallness with much kindness; he has often pressed me with a great request, desiring that I not depart from here before the feast of Easter. At last, with this condition interposed, I consented to remain until Easter: that, when a messenger with letters has been sent to your Serenity, permission to remain may also be obtained from you by your excellence, as he requests. I thought your clemency should most humbly be asked that meanwhile you deign to have regard, in your piety, for the brothers and my monastery, lest the rashness of my rivals also burst forth against the sheep, which did not spare the shepherd. They have indeed persecuted me, innocent and without cause; they have hated me without reason. Therefore, with the consent of your serenity, I willingly withdrew for a time, so that a more useful plan may be prepared for me with delay. But, most serene prince, the rashness of my rivals is not hidden from you; you consider exactly whence they are moved against an innocent man. Nor is there need to describe to you the characters of men very well known to you, since they sufficiently showed their minds hostile to your affairs at that time when the enemies raged against your people; nor now, as they persecute me secretly under the appearance of good because of your blameless favor toward me, do they conceal them. But I shall overcome their insolence by patience; they themselves will at last waste away in their own envy. Farewell, magnificent prince. From Berlin, on the twentieth day of October, in the year 1505.

*XLIII*

*Johannes Trithemius to Jakob Trithemius, his sweetest brother, sends greeting.*

If you are well, it is indeed well. For we, by the mercy of God, are well. Intending to enter Neometum, on the twenty-seventh day of the month of August we departed from here with our household by carriage, while you were still staying in Cologne; on the eleventh day of the month of September we entered the town of the Margrave’s residence called Berlin. But the prince was then occupied in hunting, according to his old custom. On the next day a messenger was sent to him, to report that we had come. When he learned that I was present, he rejoiced, and asked the messenger why I had not brought you with me. We are now living in a foreign land indeed, but an excellent one, abundantly supplied with all things necessary for the body. The prince is munificent and most gracious to us; by his providence we have been most richly provided for in all things. We had resolved to return to the regions of the Rhine around the feast of Saint Martin, but the prince’s insistence changed our plan and brought us to the point that we consented to remain until Easter. Therefore we exhort you to betake yourself to the New Castle, to stay there with our faithful Johann Damius until we come. We do not wish you to wash a brick, to serve those lacking a brain, but rather we desire you to be occupied with the study of letters, especially divine letters, through the knowledge of which the mind rises up to the knowledge and love of the highest truth. Do not be ashamed to flee curiosity, and detest those [unclear] which are opposed to divine love. For there is a great difference between a curious man and a studious man. The curious man is eager for knowledge of those things which contribute nothing of honorable usefulness. But the studious man is eager for knowledge of those things whose cognition pertains, by honorable ordering, to nourishing and adorning the soul in a liberal way. We desire you to be studious, not curious, since, as Saint Bernard says, curiosity is the first step of pride, contrary to the grace of the Holy Spirit, whose teaching does not sharpen curiosity but kindles charity. Therefore Scripture says: Do not seek things higher than yourself; do not search into things stronger than yourself. Ecclesiasticus 2.
