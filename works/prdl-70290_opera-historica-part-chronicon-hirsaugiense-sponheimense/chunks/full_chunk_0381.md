*Sponheim.*

... was made with Count Simon, after the death of Siger the monk, who was the chief and head of the whole disturbance; namely, that the division of revenues once made between abbot and convent, as we have said before, should henceforth remain, and that the monks should be bound each year to render an account to the abbot of receipts and expenses. The abbot himself also, lest he be able to alienate anything without consulting his convent, is in every way similarly bound to render an account to the convent each year; and neither the abbot without the consent of the convent, nor the convent without the consent of the abbot, shall presume to sell or alter anything.

In the same year noted above, Ortwin, the fourteenth prior of this monastery, died. *Prior Ortwin dies.* This division of revenues between abbot and convent was greatly displeasing to him, and with all his strength he labored to prevent it from being made, or to revoke it once made; but he was able to accomplish little, although he brought upon himself the hatred of all the brothers. After him Megenhard, a monk, son of John, knight of Weinsheim, was appointed fifteenth prior; he presided in that office for eighteen years. *Megenhard, prior [unclear].*

*1277.*

In the thirteenth year of our abbot Peter, Count Simon of Sponheim, our advocate, died. *Count Simon of Sponheim dies.* He left two sons, John and Henry, about whom we shall speak more fully in the following year.

In the same year Hermann, son of King Rudolf, was drowned in the Rhine and buried at Basel, not without many tears from his mother.

*1278.*

In the fourteenth year of our abbot Peter, John and Henry, counts of Sponheim, brothers, sons of Count Simon, of whom we spoke above, had a serious quarrel with each other. *Discord among the brothers, counts of Sponheim.* John was the elder by birth, to whom his father had left the county of Sponheim to govern, a spirited and steadfast man; since he yielded to his brother Henry in nothing, he made him his enemy and adversary. And so Count Henry, brother of John, betook himself to Werner, archbishop of Mainz, and entrusted himself to his protection; he received a fief of twenty marks of Aachen denarii for annual service, but the castle called Böckelheim, with the adjacent castle-villages there, with revenues of eighty marks, to the grave loss of the county, he sold to the aforesaid archbishop for 1,300 marks of Aachen denarii, as is clear from the letters of sale which he delivered to the archbishop, whose tenor follows.

I, Henry, a noble man, son of the late Count Simon of Sponheim, by the tenor of the present document publicly acknowledge and wish it to come to the knowledge of all, that I have sold my castle of Böckelheim, together with the castle-villages now held there in fief, with the castle-fief there, and revenues of eighty marks, with the consent of Cunegund, my wife, to whom the same castle had been assigned as a marriage-gift, by joint hand, to my lord Werner, archbishop of the church of Mainz, to be possessed in perpetuity as property, for [unclear] marks of Aachen denarii, to be paid at fixed terms, with sureties appointed for me for the same, as is more fully contained in the letters of the same lord archbishop drawn up concerning this matter; promising [unclear] the archbishop of the aforesaid church concerning the castle and its goods for the space of one year and one day, as shall be just and customary, [unclear]. And because the aforesaid lord archbishop has acquired for me, for 400 marks of the aforesaid money, in [unclear] of his castle-fief of the church of Mainz, at Olm or Bingen, the choice having been given to me of selecting a residence in one of these places, I declare and protest that I am bound, after those two hundred marks have been paid to me, to convert them into goods situated at Olm or Bingen, from which they will be able to have revenues of twenty marks annually, or to show to the same lord archbishop, from my goods which I possess by right of ownership, revenues of twenty marks, receiving them from him in the name of the church of Mainz as a castle-fief, to be held. Furthermore, if it happens that the aforesaid lord archbishop has war on my account with Count John of Sponheim, my brother, and [unclear] has stood by him in pursuit of my right, I will accept absolutely no settlement from that count without the knowledge and consent of the same lord archbishop. And he likewise promised me that he would do this. In testimony and firmer assurance of this, I have caused the present letters to be written and fortified by the protection of my seal. I also, Cunegund, wife of the aforesaid nobleman, lacking a seal of my own, in testimony and evidence of my consent, have asked that these letters be sealed with the seal of Lucardis my mother. Given and enacted at Mainz in the year of the Lord 1278, on the eighth day before the Kalends of August.

Count John, gravely offended, conceived a hostile mind against his brother and the archbishop; and since he could not obtain from the prelate Werner, either by prayers or threats, the aforesaid castle of Böckelheim, a portion of his paternal inheritance, he resolved to attack the matter by arms. Thus, having become the public foe and enemy of the archbishop, he inflicted much damage on the church of Mainz, by himself and through his men.
