Dietgerus also became a monk of our monastery under the holy father Wilhelm, a man distinguished in character and learning; on account of the merit of his life and his learned knowledge, he was ordained bishop in the church of Metz. Many monks from our monastery, both under the holy father Wilhelm and under his various successors, were at different times given as abbots to other monasteries. Of these, some in the course of time became bishops in various places. We have the names of all of them, although we do not know the dates of some; therefore, lest any be overlooked in the reckoning of the years, we have resolved briefly to arrange in this place a universal catalogue of all who have been reported to us.

Sigefrid, a monk of our house under the holy father Wilhelm, was ordained by the same Wilhelm as abbot in the monastery of Schaffhausen, of our order, in the diocese of Constance. That monastery, almost collapsed through age, the holy father restored with fine panel-work and gave to the aforesaid abbot and monks. Heinrich too, a monk, an energetic man and a most upright zealot for regular discipline, was appointed by the holy father Wilhelm as first abbot of St. George the Martyr in the Black Forest, which he himself had built from the foundations, as we said far above. To the monastery of Petershausen, in the same diocese of Constance, he gave as abbot Dietheric, a venerable man, distinguished in character and knowledge, a monk of this house, who was a fervent cultivator and restorer of regular discipline and led many by word and example to contempt of the world.

To the monastery in Hasungen, in the diocese of Mainz, situated in the regions of Thuringia, about which we spoke above, he sent as first abbot one of his monks, with a whole community, named Giselbert. After he was later expelled by King Heinrich IV because of the excommunication of the supreme pontiff, and returned to Hirsau with seventy monks, he appointed him abbot of the monastery in Reinhardsbrunn, on the borders of Thuringia, in the same diocese of Mainz; he also undertook the governance of the monastery of Erfurt.

To the monastery in Zwiefalten, in the diocese of Constance, he also gave as abbot Notger, a monk, a man very well imbued with monastic disciplines, suitable in every respect, who could instruct the monks by words and examples according to the norm of regular life.

To the monastery in Camburg, in the diocese of Würzburg, which has now been transferred from the order, he gave as abbot Gunther, a monk, a man venerable for prudence and religion, who before his conversion had gained a great reputation for experience in courtly affairs.

To the cell of St. Peter, which had been transferred from Weilheim above, he appointed as abbot Adelbero, a man not moderately distinguished in monastic disciplines and experience; taught under his mastership the whole foundation of the institution of regular life, he converted many to piety by his example.

To the monastery in Altdorf, in the diocese of Strasbourg, he gave as abbot, by name Welchio, a venerable monk, very well instructed in monastic life. Trained by the best teacher in all disciplined conduct, he strove to show himself a not useless shepherd to the sheep of Christ.

To the monastery called Buren, in the diocese of Augsburg, he sent as abbot Azelmus, a venerable monk, who, born of illustrious family, [unclear]. Otto, a monk of our house, was appointed abbot after Azelmus. Whether this was done under Wilhelm or under his successor Gebhard, we do not know sufficiently. The same Otto afterwards also undertook the governance of the monastery of Rheinau, in the diocese of Constance.

To the monastery of St. John of Magdeburg in Saxony, whose foundation we described far above, he sent as abbot the monk Hildebold, a very venerable man, about whom many proclamations of praise are reported. To the same house later Brother Hugo, a monk of our Hirsau community, was sent and ordained abbot, a man of great prudence, honesty, and religion.

To the monastery situated in the diocese of Constance, which is called Isny, the holy father ordained as abbot a monk of his community, of much religion and devotion, named Erckenbald, very well instructed in monastic institutions. To the same place Bruno, a monk of the Hirsau house, was afterwards ordained abbot, a man venerable and very distinguished in piety and religion, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures.
