In these times the pernicious heresy of the Poor of Lyons, which by another name is called Waldensian, began through a certain wealthy citizen of Lyons, who, after leaving everything behind, professed evangelical poverty; and, after certain books of Sacred Scripture had been translated from Latin into the French tongue, he took upon himself the office of the apostles, and both by himself and through his accomplices in error preached everywhere the Scriptures badly understood. They despised the rulers and prelates of the churches, together with their constitutions, and made nothing of established orders. When they were admonished to come to their senses, they were accustomed to reply that God must be obeyed more than men. At last, stubborn and obstinate in wicked errors, they were driven from the Church, excommunicated, and declared heretics.

About these times, when Saint Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux, passed from this life, which happened in the year of the Lord 1153, in the first indiction, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of September, in the sixty-fourth year of his age, a dreadful vision was shown to the bishop of Langres. For there appeared to him a certain dead hermit, whom he had once known while living; moved by fear of God, some years earlier, when he was called a wealthy dean, he had despised everything and entered a hermitage for the love of Christ. But among the other places which he sanctified by his presence, he also came to Hirsau, and Abbot Manegold and the community of brothers received [him] with inestimable joy, as an angel of the Lord coming from heaven. But she, after the customary prayer, sitting in the midst of the brothers, opened her mouth in the name of Christ; the word which the Holy Spirit had entrusted to her, so fervently [unclear] others were sent to purgatory; all the rest, by the sentence of the just judge, were condemned and went down to hell, to be tormented forever.

In the third year of Abbot Manegold, which was, as we have said, the year 1160 from the Lord’s nativity, Saint Hildegard, virgin of Christ, abbess of the monastery of the holy confessor Rupert near Bingen, compelled by the Holy Spirit, personally announced the word of God to people in many places. Among the other places which she sanctified by her presence, she also came to Hirsau, and Abbot Manegold and the community of brothers received her with inestimable joy, as an angel of the Lord coming from heaven. But after holy prayer, sitting in the midst of the brothers, she opened her mouth in the name of Christ and delivered to her hearers the word which the Holy Spirit had entrusted to her, so fervently, soberly, and prudently that all were turned to amazement. A wondrous thing happened. While she spoke the word outwardly, the Holy Spirit inwardly worked its effect in the hearts of the hearers; and he poured over all so great a grace of compunction that they were compelled, from deep feeling and because of the sweetness of mind, to shed tears. Among the other discourses of her sweetest sermon, she foretold to the brothers that, unless they cautiously guarded against the snares of the ancient enemy, they would within a very short space of time experience some tribulation. This happened in that same year in the pernicious dissension which, at the devil’s instigation, arose between Abbot Manegold and the community; of this we shall speak in its proper place. All together, in those matters which concerned the community, instructed by the Holy Spirit, she reproved hidden and manifest faults, and showed with most prudent reasoning what things in their assembly or conduct displeased the divine majesty. Proceeding from there, she went to the monastery of Zwiefalten of our order, and there likewise announced both to the nuns and to the monks what the Holy Spirit had commanded her. In many monasteries and places of Swabia, too, she opened the word of the Lord to the people with the attestation of miracles, and by her admonitions turned many away from iniquity.

In the fourth year of Abbot Manegold, which was the year 1161 from the Lord’s nativity, a grave discord arose, according to the prophecy of blessed Hildegard, through the devil’s instigation, between the abbot of Hirsau himself and his brothers; this spread great scandal and evil report among the people in almost every region of the Swabians. For it seemed to Prior Diemo and to the brothers that the abbot, by indulging his familiars too much, yielded to whatever they wished, and that by dissembling he tolerated many dissolutions and insolences of theirs against the peace of the community and monastic propriety. Hence, when he had several times been admonished about this by the seniors, and, using free power too much, seemed to amend none of the things being put forward, the brothers, thinking themselves held in contempt by him, conceived the gravest displeasure against him. This grew so much that it came to the knowledge of all dwelling in that region, not without disgrace, confusion, and detestation [unclear]. And since the dissension among the brothers thus produced many ordinances against the abbot, insults, and murmurings against him, the abbot, provoked against them, not with paternal mildness, as it seemed to them, but with severity, punished harshly whomever he could catch; the less affection of paternal gentleness he showed, the greater [unclear].
