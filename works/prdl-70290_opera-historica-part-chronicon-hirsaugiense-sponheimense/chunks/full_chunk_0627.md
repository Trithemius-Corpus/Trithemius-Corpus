“He says, ‘It is better to bury a woman than to take her as a wife.’ Since, then, it is very difficult to find a good wife, we greatly rejoice with you that you have found such a one, concerning whom you may persuade yourself of all the best things. It remains that, as Saint Christophilus says, those who have wives should be as though they had none, because the fashion of this world passes away, and nothing among mortals is lasting.

The evil things that happened around us in the commonwealth in the preceding year, no doubt as our sins required, we have thought it worthwhile to report to you briefly, as far as we can, insofar as they concern those known to you or your friends. In the year of the Christians just past, namely 1504, in the month of May, many princes of Germany, at the command of Maximilian our king, conspired against Philip, count palatine and duke of Bavaria, and elector of the Roman Empire, and waged very grievous wars against his commonwealth, both here in our region and in Bavaria. Between Worms and Kreuznach, and likewise on the other side of the Rhine, whatever was under the dominion of the Palatine outside cities and castles fell as plunder to the Hessians; respecting no one, they consumed with fire almost all the villages round about, together with their churches, many and very beautiful. The better courts or granges of our monastery also, and almost all of them, from which we had been accustomed to have all our substance, either perished or were utterly laid waste by the incursion of the Hessians. Because of this war we have suffered the greatest hardships, by which we have been driven almost to the final point of poverty.

That distinguished monastery of Limburg, in which you once fulfilled the office of teaching, was burned and utterly laid waste on the thirtieth day of August of the aforesaid year, by the setting of fire by the attendants of the count of Duroburg. For the fire lasted for twelve continuous days, the servants of Duroburg going out daily and always setting a new fire, so that nothing might remain. The church, with all its altars, bells, panels, and paintings, was completely burned. And, to embrace everything briefly, all the buildings, with all their structures, were reduced to ashes in that fire, and nothing at all remained except the stones. The abbot is here in Cologne; he is imploring the king’s aid against the count, but as yet he has obtained nothing. His monks, because of want, have been scattered from here and there to various monasteries.

Our Rogerius is still alive and well, and philosophizes in his own manner; his monastery remains intact, although it too has undergone many hardships. Many of the brothers of my monastery also, from the time when you were with us, have departed this life; some, too, driven by the want which the affliction mentioned above brought upon us, we have sent to other monasteries, so that few now remain in the place. Nor has the envy of impious men spared me; they persecute me for this reason, that I have necessarily adhered to the side of the Palatine prince and my defender. But the Lord Jesus, who knows all things before they come to pass, will himself be my helper, and I will not fear what man may do to me. Farewell, in good health. From Cologne, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of July, in the year 1505.

*XXVIII.*

*To the reverend father in Christ, her lord and superior, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, Richmodis de Hoerst, humble handmaid of Christ, unworthy minister of the poor sisters serving God in Sebach, with continual prayers, prays eternal salvation.*

[unclear] I hear that you, father always reverend to me in Christ, have the favor of many princes, and especially of the lord archbishop of Cologne, a thing I also knew long before; therefore, compelled by necessity, humbled [unclear] may you deign [to aid] your poor daughters, whom you were accustomed to feed with the milk of heavenly doctrine. For we are oppressed by the hard and unbearable yoke of the count, and by continual servitude, against justice, we are being reduced to the utmost poverty. Until now, twelve times a year only, and only at vintage time, we had been accustomed to send his father a cart with three horses for service; but now, at his command, he demands from us a cart with four horses for service twice or three times in almost any week, and this at our expense; indeed he violently compels our household, threatening prison and death. He does not distinguish day from day, nor does he observe reverence for any feast, but in the building of his castle of Duroburg near us, on feast days as well as on other days without distinction, both men and beasts of burden are worn out. For on the feast of the Lord’s Ascension, and also of Pentecost, of Trinity, of Corpus Christi, on Sundays, and on all other days whatsoever, he always has workers on the walls.
