When this donation had been solemnly made, the aforesaid Archbishop Otgar, in the place of the Apostolic Lord Pope Gregory IV and of the most pious Emperor Louis I, fortified it with the ban of perpetual stability, and delivered the monastery itself, with all donations already made and those duly to be made in future, into the hands of the aforesaid abbot Lintbert and of his successors, to be canonically appointed according to the rule of our most holy father Benedict, to be possessed forever. This was on the condition that the monastic life, according to the standard of the aforesaid father, should remain vigorous in it all its days; and that, whenever the monks should happen to be bereft of a spiritual pastor, they should have free power, without contradiction from anyone, to choose another suitable man, either from among themselves or from another monastery of the same order, in place of the deceased father.

The aforesaid archbishop appointed Count Erlafrid himself advocate of the monastery of Hirsau, under this condition: that when the father died, the son should not succeed in the advocacy unless he had been taken up into it by the abbot and the monks who should be there at the time, by their free and spontaneous will. For the most devout count feared lest the monastery, after his death, should suffer some injury or violence through his son or grandsons, as we see happening today among many, if the advocacy were to pass to descendants by hereditary right. Therefore he decreed that it should depend on the abbot's will whom he wished to have as a suitable advocate for his monastery. And this liberty, confirmed in the course of time by many privileges, both apostolic and imperial, has remained inviolate in the monastery of Hirsau down to the present day.

*Life of Saint Aurelius, bishop.*

Therefore blessed Aurelius, born in Armenia of Christian and most noble parents, as soon as his age permitted, was given over to letters. Instructed by divine prudence, and in a short time making no small progress in every learning of the Scriptures, he turned everything that he could either learn or know to the exercise of divine love. Hence, through all the grades of the ecclesiastical order, he deserved to ascend, though reluctant, to the summit of the episcopate in the city of Rediciana, with the greatest applause of the people no less than of the clergy; and, established in this honor, he shone more brightly with greater virtues than before.

He presented himself as irreproachable [unclear], and stirred the people to the observance of God's commandments no less by example than by speech. He was a father of the poor, furthermore a consoler of widows; whatever he could have, he ministered it all to the uses of the needy.

*Aurelius was a true bishop in deed and speech.*

He was of so holy and irreproachable a way of life that there was no one at that time in Armenia who did not fear Aurelius the bishop, servant of God, with proclamation of praise. He was accustomed to visit all the villages of the diocese of Rediciana, admonishing and preaching in his own most holy person, ministering the word of salvation to all. He instructed the ignorant, strengthened the faint-hearted, raised up the downcast, and exhorted the good and humble, in order that they might advance to better things.
