we receive under perpetual [protection], strictly forbidding, both for those present and those to come, under penalty of the crime of lese-majesty against the emperor, that anyone inflict any molestation, injury, or damage upon the said abbey of Sponheim, which we wish to enjoy every freedom that the other abbeys in our realm are known to enjoy, in goods, persons, household, property, donations already made, or those duly to be made in the future. Moreover, the advocate who shall hold office at the time, if he rashly transgresses the law established for him by the often-named count Megenhard, after being admonished up to a third time by the archbishop of Mainz, when the abbot has made complaint against him, if he does not amend, shall be deprived of such honor; and another, who may be useful to the church, shall be substituted in his place by the counsel and assent of the brethren of the abbey. And so that this charter may remain forever unshaken, and no one’s rashness may presume to weaken it, we have strengthened it by imperial authority with our ban, and, marked by our own hand, have secured it with the impression of our seal. Given on the sixth day before the Kalends of March, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1125, in the third indiction, in the twenty-sixth year of the ordination of lord Henry V, king of the Romans, the thirtieth of his reign, and the fifteenth of his empire. Done at Mainz. In the name of the Lord. Amen.

Abbot Bernhelm also, in the third year of his institution, obtained from the apostolic see confirmation of all the foregoing, together with certain other privileges. Since the sequence of the order requires it, we shall insert here a copy of their rescript.

*Copy of the confirmation of Honorius II, pope.*

Honorius, bishop, servant of God, to his beloved son Bernhelm, abbot of the monastery of St. Martin in Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, and to his successors to be regularly instituted forever. It is fitting to grant ready assent to religious desires. And therefore, beloved son, assenting to the just petitions of you and your brethren, we have taken under the guardianship and protection of the apostolic see the aforesaid monastery of Sponheim, over which, by God’s authority, you preside, and have fortified it by the page of the present writing, decreeing and ordaining that the regular life now newly instituted in that place shall be inviolably preserved in future times according to the rule of Saint Benedict. Nor let anyone have the faculty, after making profession among you, to pass over to another place, under pretext of a greater or lesser religious observance, without the abbot’s permission. But if anyone at any future time departs contrary to this our prohibition, no abbot or anyone else shall dare to retain him. Whenever, with the abbot absent, there must be discussion among the brethren of that place concerning a successor, no one shall be intruded there by any cunning of surreption, or by the violence of any power, whether ecclesiastical or temporal, except the one whom the brethren by common consent, or the part of sounder counsel, shall judge should be elected, according to the fear of God and the rule of blessed Benedict, from the same community or from another, if, which God forbid, no suitable person is found among them. Moreover, the consecration of altars, chrism, holy oil, the blessing of abbots, and the ordination of monks are to be received from the diocesan archbishop of that same parish, if indeed he has the grace and communion of the apostolic see, and if he is willing to provide them graciously and without any depravity. Otherwise it shall be lawful for you and your successors to approach whatever catholic bishop you wish, and from him to receive consecrations and sacraments.

*The pope took the monastery under the protection of the apostolic see.*

*That the monks should not depart.*

*That no one should intrude an abbot not canonically elected.*

We judge also that burial in this place shall be entirely free, so that no one may oppose the devotion and will of those who have desired to be buried there, unless perhaps they have been excommunicated. We also confirm by apostolic authority all the possessions that you hold under the title below, and that you shall acquire in the future by offering or devotion, decreeing that it shall be lawful for no human being at all rashly to disturb the aforesaid monastery; and if he does not quickly make suitable satisfaction, let him lack the dignity of his power and honor, and let him know that he stands guilty in the divine judgment for the iniquity he has perpetrated; let him be estranged from the most sacred body and blood of our Lord God and Redeemer Jesus Christ, and at the last examination be subject to strict vengeance. But to all who preserve what is just for the same place, let there be the peace of our Lord Jesus Christ, so that here they may receive the fruit of good action, and before the strict Judge find the rewards of eternal peace. Amen. Given by the hand of the deacon of the holy apostolic see, on the tenth day before the Kalends of April, in the fifth indiction, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1127, in the fourth year of our pontificate.

When the foundation and institution of the present monastery had been sufficiently confirmed both by the apostolic see and by imperial authority, the monks who had come from Mainz began to inhabit the place and to arrange dwellings necessary for themselves according to the rule. Mechthild, the wife of the founder Megenhard, a woman most devoted to Christ, furnished the monks with everything necessary for life and showed herself a most faithful co-worker in completing the buildings of the monastery. But now, as we are about to proceed to what follows, let us take our beginning from the first abbot.
