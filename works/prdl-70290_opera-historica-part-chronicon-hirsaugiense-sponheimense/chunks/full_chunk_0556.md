*Sponheim.*
*1497.*

In the fourteenth year of Johannes Trithemius, our abbot, the monastery called Sunsheim, not far from Heidelberg, in the diocese of Speyer, following the example of other apostatizing monks, was transferred with its abbot and monks from our order into a secular church, by the permission and dispensation of Alexander VI, Roman Pontiff; and from perverse monks they were made criminal secular canons.

In the same year, on the tenth day before the Kalends of July, Johann, archbishop of Trier, led out a great army against his disobedient citizens of the town of Boppard. When they refused to consent to his will, he surrounded them with a very strong siege, having with him certain very powerful princes of the empire hired for pay, with many fighters and engines of war. But when the citizens perceived that they could not resist so great a multitude, on the twelfth day of the siege they submitted themselves to the command of the bishop.

In the aforementioned year also, Laurentius, bishop of Würzburg, after the death of the abbot of the Scots in the suburb of the same city across the bridge, expelled the Scottish monks, of whom there were scarcely two, because of their reprobate life, and introduced observant brothers of our order.

In the aforementioned year also, Eberhard, duke of Württemberg, who had succeeded the first duke created at Worms, is said to have committed many things wrongly and shamefully against faith and good morals by his own men; when he proposed to designate for death several of the more important of his councillors, they, having been warned, [unclear] to seize him and [unclear] perpetually from him. When this became known to him, on the fourth day before the Kalends of April he fled secretly with his treasures, first to the king of the Romans, who was then in Ulm, a town of Swabia of no small rank. The king made an agreement in this way between that duke and the regents of the land of Württemberg: with Eberhard renouncing the duchy and yielding to Ulrich, the little son of his brother, the Württembergers were to pay him six thousand florins yearly for life. But the duke, not accepting the agreement, departed, wandering with a few men through the land, first among the people of Strasbourg, afterward in Baden, and then in various other places, always everywhere leaving traces of his instability. Finally he fled to the most unjust prince, lord Philipp, count Palatine; for a time he was in Kreuznach, afterward in Heidelberg; now in the castle of Lindenfels, at the expense of the same prince, he is staying in every abundance and liberty, yet in such a way that he may not flee from there.

In the same year there was a certain doctor at Paris, of the order of the Friars Preachers, named Johannes Veri, a rash and presumptuous man, who on the day of the conception of the most pure ever-virgin Mother of God, as is the custom of those men, publicly preached from the pulpit in a certain town, Dieppe, in the diocese of Rouen, that the most blessed Virgin Mary was cleansed from original stain, not preserved from it. From this preaching a great scandal arose among the people, because almost everyone today, except a few Preachers, believes, holds, and preaches that the Mother of God, most worthy of all praise, was never subject to original sin.

But on the following day, that is, the seventeenth of the month of September, there assembled at Saint-Mathurin in the city of Paris eighty-two professors of sacred theology, very learned men, all promoted with rigor in the same University, in the presence of the whole university, and with one voice and one judgment they enacted and determined that henceforth no one should be enrolled in the aforesaid University unless he confirms by oath that he will be, to the best of his powers, an assertor and vigorous defender of this determination: namely, that the most blessed Mother of God and ever-virgin Mary was immune from and preserved from original stain. They themselves also had all sworn beforehand, as is contained in the statute, that at all times they would adhere firmly to this truth of Mary’s most pure conception.
