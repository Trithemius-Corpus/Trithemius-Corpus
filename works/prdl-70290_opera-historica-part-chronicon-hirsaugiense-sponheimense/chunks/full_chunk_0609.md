So that it may be directed not to empty glory and display, but to one’s own salvation and that of others. But just as there is not one single intention among all who hasten toward knowledge, so also there is not one single end. For some desire to know many things only for this end, that they may know, and they receive the vain fruit of their curiosity, consisting in nothing. Others wish to know for temporal advantage, namely so that they may obtain riches, honors, and dignities; what else are they doing but practicing a base trade, and they must be judged wholly unworthy of the sacred name of knowledge itself? And how many today will be found to apply themselves to knowledge for this end. And there are some who wish to know so that they themselves may be known by men; the fruit of their knowledge is vanity. The intentions of all the aforesaid in seeking knowledge are blameworthy, and their end is subject to the reckoning of guilt and judgment.

But there are others who desire to know so that they may instruct and more ardently build up their neighbors in the love of Christ; their end is charity, and the fruit of [unclear] doctrine, eternal happiness. Others, again, wish to know so that they themselves may be built up in the law of the Lord; their end is prudence, and their reward is the due recompense of the life of good everlasting blessedness. You see now, friend, what the end of our knowledge ought to be, and in what manner we ought to know. Since, therefore, the end of speculative knowledge is truth, and of practical knowledge is work: we apprehend only so much from the knowledge of almighty God, who alone is truth, and we know only so much as we love. For true and saving knowledge begets knowledge of God; love begets frequency, frequency familiarity, familiarity trust, and trust the easy obtaining from the Lord Jesus of all things that you shall have requested.

Indeed, knowledge precedes the cultivation of virtue, because no one can faithfully seek what he does not know. Knowledge of the true and love of the right prepare a most certain entrance to happiness. But [unclear] truly precedes what it inwardly does not know. Therefore the Lord Jesus Christ said to the Father in the Gospel: This is eternal life, that they may know you, the only God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. What else is that enjoyment of the supercelestial delight of the blessed spirits, if not knowledge and love of the divine majesty? For the knowledge of saving learning has love joined to itself; nor can the intellectual mind have fellowship in everlasting enjoyment if either knowledge is without love, or love without knowledge. Evil demons do know, but because they do not have love, they by no means attain the enjoyment which is born from both and not from one alone.

Certain gentile philosophers, and perhaps today many placed outside Christianity, are held to have love of the one highest truth; but because they do not know the Father of the universe, the only true God, and him whom he sent, our Lord Jesus Christ, they have become vain in their thoughts, and by no means arrive at the enjoyment of the highest goodness. And our savior himself, the Lord Jesus Christ, says in the holy Gospel about those who do not know by faith that he came into the world: He who does not believe has already been judged. And Saint [Christophilus?] says: Without faith it is impossible to please God. For true knowledge is from faith, and love from knowledge. Therefore the man who does not have faith does not have knowledge; he does not have love. And he who does not have love cannot have enjoyment. For this is what the Lord Jesus himself, when about to ascend into [heaven?], revealed to his disciples, saying: Go into the whole world and preach the Gospel to every creature. He who has believed and has been baptized will be saved; but he who has not believed will be condemned.

Thus the first road to God is knowledge or cognition through faith, without which no one will be saved. Let all other knowledge and your studies be referred to this true knowledge, O mathematician; but if you do not do this, you will be consumed in empty and foolish labor. This is true knowledge, which moves the one who knows to compunction and love, not exalting him; nor does it make those whom it has filled proud, but groaning, according to that saying of the wise man: He who adds knowledge also adds sorrow, because in much knowledge there is much indignation. Now therefore, friend, let us provide for ourselves at the opportune time, so that we may make our studies good while we have time, as the holy Apostle says: [unclear].
