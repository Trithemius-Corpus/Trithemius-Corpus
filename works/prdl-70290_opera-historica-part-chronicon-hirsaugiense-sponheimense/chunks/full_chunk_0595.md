*The Familiar Letters of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim. Book One of the Familiar Letters, to Jacob Trithemius, his most beloved brother.*

*I.*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James the Apostle at Würzburg, sends greetings to Jacob Trithemius, his most beloved brother.*

You have reminded me quite opportunely, dearest brother, that we should gather into one volume the letters, witnesses of our calamities, which it is known that we sent familiarly from the day of our departure from Spanheim down to that day on which we undertook the governance of this abbey of St. James, and that we should send them to you, so that you may always have at hand a remembrance of us. You have reasonably added what moved you to ask these things of us: that no one will say it impossible that a man growing old hastens toward his setting, and that he who entered the world earlier will depart it earlier. Since, therefore, you are joined to us by the bond of brotherhood, we can deny you nothing, especially when you ask for that which in no way lacks the rationale of honor. Receive, then, now, as a witness of our love toward you, this last Spanheim book of letters, so that you may have with you a reason for remembering us diligently, and, if our writings have any power, for your consolation. We rejoice that you survive us and are the heir of our studies, so that, although it is necessary for us to be dissolved in body, it may at least befall us to live with you after death in memory. Yet to us it is a thing of very little estimation to commend one’s memory to posterity, since no account either of praise or of blame can reach the dead. Having entered upon this one fate, we ask this of you: that as often as you serve God at the altar, you humbly interpose remembrance of us. It will aid the soul, enlarged after the completion of its purgation, we do not doubt, if you, purified, offer for it the saving host. Farewell, remembering us before God. From our monastery of St. James the Greater in the suburb of Würzburg, on the fourth day of the month of November, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*II.*

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, to his dearest sons in Christ, the Prior and Convent in Spanheim: greetings and sincere charity in the Lord.*

On the ninth day of the month of April, Brother Theoderic, a monk, came to us at Budorim [Heidelberg?], bearing news not very pleasant; by this occurrence we were disturbed in no small measure, especially because none of you set himself against the rashness of those malignant attendants, none contradicted it. Who will be angry with me, my brothers, if I suspect some of you of being both aware of and consenting to so great a rashness perpetrated against us? An argument conspires toward suspicion: that not only did none of you intervene on our behalf, but not even did any of you think of reporting to us a deed too rash indeed. We know also that some of you were present at the prelude, when the agents of rashness first broke down the doors and rushed upon the servants, and laughed at the insult to our men when they were ordered to leave. O always pernicious sloth of the unskilled: what great calamity and desolation you are accustomed, through neglect, to bring upon the commonwealth from a slight beginning. And indeed, brothers, gravely harmed in this matter, we very much fear that this presumption will soon do harm no less to you and to the poor monastery than to us. For I certainly am not ignorant of the hidden causes of the Cynonoti’s hatred toward me; nor shall I be able to love the rule of him who, without cause and contrary to justice, opposes me. Yet it cannot yet be sufficiently clear to me by whose urgent command such things were attempted against our men. For I cannot easily be brought to believe that the Prince [unclear] thundered such decrees against us. Even if perhaps he has given credence to the unjust denunciations of the Battologi against us, shall we therefore abandon our holy purpose of philosophizing?
