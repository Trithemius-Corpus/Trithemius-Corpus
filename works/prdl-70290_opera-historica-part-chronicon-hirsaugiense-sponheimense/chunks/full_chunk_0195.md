*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

Against heretics he wrote one book. This book of his against the Cathars is extant, printed at Cologne in the year [1530?]. Also, On the Beginning of the Gospel of John. Also, on the Gospel, 'The angel was sent': a fine treatise, not to be despised. He also composed several letters to various persons, and many and varied sermons. He also wrote the life, conduct, revelations, and death of his blessed sister Elisabeth, of whom we shall speak later. He also published several poems of different kinds, and prayers suited to devotion, concerning the Lord Jesus, and concerning his most holy mother, the Virgin Mary, and many other diverse saints.

*He was abbot of Schonau.* At length, when Hildelin, first abbot of the aforesaid monastery of Schonau, had died, on the Nones of December in the year of the Lord 1167, by the unanimous consent of the brothers he was appointed abbot in the same monastery. He presided for eighteen years, and on the fifth day before the Kalends of April he passed from this life, not without a reputation for sanctity.

He was of such great learning that, in a public assembly of learned men at Koblenz, in the first disputation he convicted a certain heretic, [of Gardena?] by origin, who held wrong opinions and taught most wickedly concerning the body and blood of the Lord, and who, because of the subtlety of his mind and the fluency of his tongue, was formidable even to all the most learned; and he called him back from his error to the profession of the truth.

*1163.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Manegold, and in the year 1164 of the Lord's nativity, the archbishop of Salzburg was cruelly killed by the ministerials of St. Rupert. The Emperor returned again into Italy.

*The archbishop of Salzburg is slain. The sea oversteps its bounds.* The ocean in the lower regions, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of March, went beyond its boundary for twelve miles and submerged many thousands of people of both sexes and of different ages, especially around the river Weser.

In the same year, on Good Friday, namely the fourth day before the Ides of April, while Pope Alexander, who was also Roland, was celebrating the divine rites at Sens, horrible darkness came on; and then such a dreadful lightning bolt fell, and after it such a great crash thundered, that the pope, leaving the altar, and the deacon, leaving the reading, and all who were present judged that they must look to their safety by flight.

*Dreadful tempest of the air.* In that same year Pope Victor died at Lucca and was buried there. Wido, bishop of Crema, was ordained by Henry, bishop of Liege, on the tenth day before the Kalends of May, and was called Paschal III.

*Pope Victor died; Paschal III succeeds. Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, is expelled; Christian is elected.* In the same year Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, of whom we spoke above, bound himself by faith and oath to Pope Alexander and against the will of the Emperor, and withdrew from the Emperor and his antipope. Therefore the Emperor, indignant, expelled him from the archbishopric of Mainz and compelled him to flee to that father Alexander himself. When he had been expelled, Christian was appointed in his place; as we said above, the men of Mainz had elected him before Conrad. There is extant a letter of this Archbishop Christian to Saint Hildegard, in which he asks and obtains. At length, however, after Christian, Conrad again [unclear] to the archbishopric.

On the appointed day for battle, on the second feria of Rogation week in the field of Andernach, after the men of Cologne had been reconnoitered by scouts, Conrad the Palatine did not dare to come, although he had been awaited by them for twelve days.

In the same year, on the Kalends of October, the Emperor returned from Italy and held a famous court at Bamberg in the octave of St. Martin, where he restored peace between Conrad, count Palatine, his brother, and Reinold the elect and the men of Cologne.

In the eighth year of Abbot Manegold, which was the year 1165 of the Lord's nativity, Saint Thomas, archbishop of Canterbury in England, while he was stoutly defending the freedom and rights of his church against the tyranny of the king of the English, was driven by him from his country and at the same time from his archbishopric. Coming into the kingdom of the Franks, he first stayed in the monastery of [Pontigny?]. Afterwards he was maintained at royal expense at Sens, in the monastery of St. Columba.

*Saint Thomas, archbishop of Canterbury, is exiled.* In the same year a son named Henry was born to the Emperor at Nijmegen.
