*Monastery of Hirsau*

Alfrid flourished in these times, formerly a monk of the monastery of Corvey, the fourth bishop of Hildesheim, a man truly beloved of God and most holy in his whole life. He governed the Church committed to him vigorously for 28 years, and built the greater church of the aforesaid city, in which he was also buried. He was elected bishop in the ninth year of Abbot Lintbert, which was the year of the Lord’s incarnation 847, in the tenth indiction.

*848.*

Rabanus, archbishop of Mainz, at the command of Emperor Lothar, convened a synod of bishops and abbots at Mainz in the year of the Lord 848, in the eleventh indiction. In it he condemned a certain heretic, a priest named Gottschalk, and established many things for the adornment and utility of the Church. Among others there were present the most celebrated men, the bishops: Hetti, archbishop of Trier; Hildebold, archbishop of Cologne; Alfrid, bishop of Hildesheim; Haymo, bishop of Halberstadt; Luderic, bishop of Bremen; Lintbert, this first abbot of Saint Aurelius of Hirsau; Waldo, abbot of Fulda; Einhard, abbot of Seligenstadt; Brunwart, abbot of Hersfeld; Bertolph, abbot of Mettlach; Adventitius, abbot of Metz, afterward bishop there; Radulf, abbot of Saint-Médard of Soissons, afterward archbishop of Bourges; and many other bishops, abbots, and monks, among whom several were most renowned for life and learning.

In these days a certain woman, pretending that an angel of God had appeared to her and had announced that the last day of judgment was soon at hand, deceived many, both of the clergy and of the people, throughout the diocese of Mainz. At length, when she had been seized by Archbishop Rabanus, she confessed that she had been led into this, for the sake of gain, by the counsel of a certain priest [unclear].

*849.*

In the eleventh year of Abbot Lintbert, the body of the holy empress Helena, mother of Constantine the Great, was transferred from Rome to Gaul by the monk Theogisus, and was placed in the territory of Reims; her head is shown today in the church of Trier.

In these days of the most excellent Abbot Lintbert, his mercy toward the poor greatly increased both his own name and the profit of the monastery of Hirsau. For the more he distributed to the use of the poor, the greater things he received. Rabanus the archbishop also displayed great generosity to the poor while [unclear] endured.

Around these times Adolphus, the most Christian king of England, going to Rome under Pope Leo IV, out of very great devotion to the Roman Church made his province tributary, instituting that from each house each year a silver coin should be paid to Saint Peter.

*851.*

In the thirteenth year of Abbot Lintbert, Lupus Servatus the priest, a most learned man, to whom Rabanus, while still abbot, once wrote eight books on the Epistle of Paul to the Romans, departed to the Lord. Two years earlier, being present at the synod of Mainz, he had plainly overcome and confuted before all who were there, in a very powerful disputation, that priest Gottschalk, who held erroneous opinions on free will, predestination, and the blood of Christ, and was troubling many. He also, for a clearer declaration of the truth, among other things that he wrote, composed a very fine treatise on these matters as well.

*853.*

In the fifteenth year of Abbot Lintbert, which is the year of the Lord 853, Saint Haymo died, the third bishop of Halberstadt, formerly a monk of Hersfeld, a man beloved of God in every respect, who by word, writing, and example recalled many to the paths of righteousness; he was buried in the greater church of Halberstadt. Saint Hildegrin succeeded him in the episcopate for almost 34 years.
