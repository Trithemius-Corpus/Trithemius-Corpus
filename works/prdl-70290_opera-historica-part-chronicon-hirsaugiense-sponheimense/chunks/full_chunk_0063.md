*1048.*

In the year of the Lord 1048, in the forty-sixth year of the vacancy of the monastery of Hirsau, Bruno, bishop of Toul, by the appointment of the emperor and the election of the cardinals, became pope of the city of Rome, called Leo IX, and he sat for almost six years. He was the son of the count of Egisheim and a monk of our order, a learned and most holy man. When he was going to Rome to take up the papacy, he heard voices of angels singing in the air: “The Lord says, I think thoughts of peace and not of affliction.” He placed Christ, in the form of a leper, upon his bed. He also wrote against the heresies of the Greeks to Constantine, emperor of Constantinople, and chants concerning various saints. At the urging of this pope Leo, the monastery of Hirsau began to be restored, as we shall say later.

In these days there also flourished Humbert, from a monk of our order, cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, a most learned man, whom Pope Leo had brought with him to Rome, and whose counsel he used in the conduct of all affairs, especially those of the order. We have treated more fully of this man’s little works in the second book of the illustrious men of our order.

Lambert, a monk of our order from the monastery of Saint Lawrence at Liege, once a pupil of Adelman the grammarian, bishop of Brescia, a man certainly splendidly learned in every science, also at this time wonderfully adorned our order by his learning. He wrote the life of Saint Heribert, bishop of Cologne, and many other works, of which we have spoken elsewhere.

*1050.*

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 1050, in the third indiction, the holy Pope Leo IX, having left Rome with many cardinals in order to settle affairs and the state of the churches and to restore peace to the Gauls, came into Germany. At that time Count Adelbert of Calw was advocate of the monastery of Hirsau, but a patron of its desolation.
