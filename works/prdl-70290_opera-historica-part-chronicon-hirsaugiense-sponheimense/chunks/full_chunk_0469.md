*Sponheim.*

In the same year Abbot Bernhard removed Friedrich and Wilhelm, monks and procurators of this monastery, from the office of their provision, which they had held for almost eight years, and he raised thirty-eight articles against the account which they had made. He therefore again appointed a secular provisioner, whom his successor Gobelinus later removed.

*Change of the provisioner of this monastery.*

In the same year also, in the month of December, a general council was begun in the city of Basel, and it lasted more than thirteen years; in it forty-four public sessions were held. It had a fair and glorious beginning, but a weak and confused end, because Pope Eugenius IV, not agreeing with the council itself, stirred up many contrivances against it. After he was deprived by it of papal honor and dignity, he did not trouble to obey the council, but recalled it, and procured that the Dauphin, first-born son of the king of France, be sent against the people of Basel for its dispersal, as we shall say in its place. From this followed many evils and scandals in the Church, not yet wholly quieted, some preaching that the pope is above the council, others saying the opposite.

*The Council of Basel is begun.*

In the aforesaid year also a great expedition of bishops, princes, nobles, and citizens was made against the Hussites in Bohemia; but no fruit came of it, while very great loss befell the Catholics. For, because of the ambition and greed of the princes, the heretics again prevailed against our men; many were slain, and the rest fled with loss and disgrace, all their horses, wagons, valuables, money, and the whole equipment being lost.

*Expedition against the Hussites in Bohemia.*

1432.

In the forty-first year of our abbot Bernhard there was a very harsh winter; and at last, when the ice and snows had melted, so great a flooding of waters followed that many villages, houses, and churches along the Rhine were overthrown in various places by the excessive force of the flood, and great losses to sown fields and meadows followed.

*A very harsh winter.*

In the same year, on the holy day of Parasceve, during the office, two Jews came to a little village not far from the monastery of St. Catherine, which is called Wiler. With smooth speeches and promises they took a certain little boy, seven years of age, and secretly led him outside the village into the neighboring wood, which is called Dumus lupina, in the vernacular Wolfisheck, and is a possession of this monastery. Cruelly martyring him, they killed him; and, having collected his blood, they went away, leaving the innocent boy’s little body unburied among the bushes themselves. Afterward, when the innocent corpse was found, it was carried with honor to Kreuznach, and for some days it stood unburied in the parish church, many hoping that his martyrdom would be made known by signs and miracles through the mercy of God. But when no miracle appeared, they buried it in the cemetery behind the choir, toward the east, where that stone cross is seen erected today. The Jews too, having been apprehended in Kreuznach, were burned with fire outside the town.

In the same year also, on the eleventh day of the month of May, which was Jubilate Sunday, the eighth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated at St. Ulrich in Augsburg. Presiding over it were Ortwin of St. Peter in Erfurt, of the diocese of Mainz; Johannes in Stein, of Constance; and Heinrich in Amorbach, likewise of the diocese of Mainz, abbots of monasteries of our order; and many things were decreed in it.

In this same year Sigismund, aided by the power of Filippo, duke of Milan, was conveyed to Rome and crowned with the imperial diadem by Pope Eugenius.

In the same year also Bernhard, eighteenth abbot of this monastery, weighed down by old age and the disease of the stone, died on the fifth calends of June, in the seventy-seventh year of his age, and with the forty-first year, together with three months and nine days, of the rule he had undertaken completed. When he perceived that death was near him, having first made confession with tears and received the ecclesiastical sacraments with great devotion, he had the whole community called to him. As they stood by, he said: “Brothers, always indeed beloved to me in Christ, you now see that the end of the present life is upon me, and that I can no longer remain with you. I therefore ask: call to mind the injuries which you have so often inflicted both on me and on God, since, when I wished to bring your conduct back to the integrity of monastic life, you always resisted me with too much contempt. Behold, now I die, and if I have done well, my reward is with me; but you, I greatly fear, after my death will have [unclear], so that, as you were unwilling to hear me as father, you may hear another [not as father?]. Consider therefore, I pray, the brevity of the present life, and amend your ways according to the monastic rule and the decrees of the holy Fathers, lest in the article of death, in which you see me placed, you repent of rashness. Moreover I charge you that, after my death, you seek back as far as [unclear] those farms in Winzheim from unjust possessors, of which I bought one for a price, and acquired the other from my brothers by [unclear] edict for my son.
