Oh, how beautiful it would have been in that time to be present at the life of so many servants of God, who, of one mind in spirit and concordant in will, walked in the house of God with agreement; although their bodily dwelling was in the present age, yet their mind and desire were occupied in heaven.

For the fuller instruction of morals and minds this holy father issued decrees conformable to the holy Rule. After brothers had been sent to Cluny to learn and bring back their customs, he compiled these in one volume with varied and beautiful distinction. The book of these customs is found today in many monasteries, a witness of the ancient religion and sanctity of the monks of Hirsau, although through the publication of the new Bursfeld constructions it seems now almost abolished from use.

It is pleasing to touch briefly on the institution of the lay brothers, wisely made by Saint William the abbot, so that posterity may know with what solicitude, diligence, and zeal the brothers of this monastery sought their salvation under a most pious shepherd. All were to come to the church for the night vigils with the greatest haste and reverence, to say their prayers most devoutly, and all things with [unclear]. And lest they be burdened beyond their strength by the excessive length of the vigils, matins were sung briefly for them; when these were finished, those who wished were permitted to return to bed, or, if they preferred, to remain in the church until the night offices were completed by the monks in choir.

In the morning every day all heard the high Mass. When it was finished they assembled at the chapter of faults, where, accusing themselves for the negligences they had committed, they received fitting penance from the master. Then, if anyone wished, he confessed his faults to the priest, so that he might complete the day with more fervent cheerfulness in the love of God. After this each went to the work assigned to him, and all labored with incredible fervor of charity, just as if each man were bound by his own labors to provide what was necessary for all.

For each had duties assigned to him within or outside the monastery, which they administered so faithfully, diligently, and honorably as if they had been divinely commanded. Among them there was the greatest devotion of mind toward God, and they were joined together among themselves in such charity and brotherly love in unity of spirit that no disturbance and no displeasure ever weakened their peace and tranquillity. They all lived in common, guarding the renunciation of all property so strictly and inviolably that they seemed to wish or feel nothing at all of the world.

They were most prompt, cheerful, and always willing in obedience to their seniors, ready for whatever those men wished to command, not only in light matters [unclear], but also in those which seemed impossible. Among them there were not only no quarrels or disputes, but not even an idle word or one provoking laughter could be heard from any of them. Every day all the lay brothers came to the church together with all the monks, and each, communicating, received the body of our Lord Jesus Christ with the greatest reverence and devotion [unclear].
