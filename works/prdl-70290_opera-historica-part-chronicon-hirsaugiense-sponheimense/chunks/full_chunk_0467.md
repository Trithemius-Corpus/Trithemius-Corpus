Johannes Rode, the abbot, consenting to his pious desire, assigned certain men of proven conduct from his monks, and sent him back with joy to Bursfeld. From that time the holy reform of the monasteries of our order, originally transmitted through the Trier men into Bursfeld, began to revive, and within a few years was enlarged. For when the princes and bishops of the churches saw the devoted and holy way of life of the monks of Bursfeld, they desired that the individual monasteries subject to them be reformed by them. Therefore, little by little, certain monasteries in Saxony and beyond began to be reformed from the monastery of Bursfeld, and from those others also; and their abbots, making a certain union and confederation among themselves, met once every year, celebrating a synod, which we call, by customary though barbarous and improper name, a Chapter, in which there would be discussion of those matters that pertain to the usefulness of the reform of the order.
