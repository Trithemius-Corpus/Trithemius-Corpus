*152 The Monastery of Hirsau* ... might have understanding. For the Spirit who spoke in her formed the words as he wished. Her whole life was, as it were, a certain long martyrdom, since, placed under regular discipline, she bore the Lord’s yoke for a long time under the manifold scourge of sufferings. For the hand of the Lord was always stretched out over her, and the scourge of her pious Creator was never lacking to her, weighing down her soul and crushing her weak body with continual illness. Although she was vexed by continual infirmities, nevertheless, always patient, always cheerful, mortifying herself by [unclear] chastisement, she offered an acceptable sacrifice to the Lord.

Instructed by divine revelations, moreover, she wrote several works for the edification of the faithful, among which we have read the following: *The Ways of God*, a beautiful and necessary work, in which several sermons are set down concerning the way of contemplatives, of active persons, of martyrs, of married persons, and of the continent; it begins thus: “It happened in the fifth year of my visitation,” etc. Likewise, instructed by angelic revelation, she issued a little work on the history, origin, discovery, and names of the eleven thousand, which begins thus: “To you who bear pious affections toward those things which are holy, I, Elisabeth, little woman of the handmaids of the Lord who are in Schönau, reveal the things which have been revealed to me through the grace of God.” Likewise she composed three books of various revelations, on various matters, causes, and propositions, containing one hundred forty-four chapters, in which she described many beautiful and useful things. The first of these begins thus: “You ask of me, brother; you have come for this, that I may recount to you the mercies of the Lord.” The second thus: “Benign is the mercy of our God.” The third thus: “The angel of the Lord took me up in the spirit to a place of great height.” Likewise, to the question [unclear] Saint Hildegard, mistress of the sisters at Bingen, which, after careful consideration, it has pleased us to insert word for word into this work of ours, as follows:

“To Lady Hildegard, venerable mistress of the brides of Christ who are at Bingen, Elisabeth, humble nun, mistress of the sisters who are at Schönau, offers devout prayers with all affection. May the grace and consolation of the Most High fill you with joy, because you have kindly had compassion for my disturbance, as I understood from the words of my consoler, whom you diligently [unclear] concerning my consolation. For, as you said that it had been revealed to you concerning me, I confess truly that recently I conceived in my soul a certain cloud of disturbance, because of the false words of people saying many things about me which are not true. But I would easily endure the words of the common people, if those who walk in the habit of religion did not grieve my spirit with bitter words. For these men, driven by I know not what goads, mock the grace of God in me; concerning things of which they are ignorant they do not fear to judge rashly. I hear that certain letters written from their own spirit are being circulated under my name, defaming me as having prophesied concerning the day of judgment. This, certainly, I presumed to do, since its coming escapes the knowledge of all mortals. But I shall disclose to you the occasion of this report, so that you may judge [unclear].

“On a certain Sunday, when I was in an ecstasy of mind, an angel stood by me, saying: ‘He has also frequently shown me through his angel what things would come upon his people in these days, unless they did penance for [unclear]; gold in the mud, that is, the word of God which has been sent through your mouth onto the earth because of the misfortunes of prophecy. Why do you hide it? Not that it may be hidden, but that it may be made manifest to the praise and glory of our Lord and the salvation of peoples.’ And when this had been said, he lifted over me a scourge, which with very great anger he inflicted on me five times most bitterly, so that for three days I was weak in my whole body from that blow. After this he placed his finger on my mouth, saying: ‘You will be silent until the ninth hour, when you will reveal the things which the Lord has worked with you.’ I therefore remained mute until the ninth hour. Then I signified to the mistress that she should bring me a certain little book, which I had hidden by my instruction, containing in part those things which the Lord had done with me. When I offered it into the hands of Abbot Hildelin, who had come to visit me, my tongue was loosed in these words: ‘Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory.’ After this, when I had revealed certain other things to him, which I had not wished to commit to writing, namely concerning the great vengeance of the Lord which I had learned from the angel would in a short time come upon the whole world, I asked him very diligently to keep that word hidden with himself. But the lord abbot himself commanded me to devote myself to prayer, and to ask the Lord to grant me understanding whether he wished the things which I had said to be governed by silence or not. And when for some time I had applied myself to prayer over this matter, the angel of the Lord stood by me, saying: ‘Cry out strongly, say “alas” to all nations, because the whole world has been turned into darkness.
