*1109.*

In the third year of Abbot Bruno, which was the year 1109 from the Lord’s nativity, in the second indiction, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of May, Saint Anselm, archbishop of Canterbury, died, the greatest supporter and lover of the brethren of Hirsau; of him we made mention much earlier in connection with Blessed William.

*Saint Anselm died.*

In the same year Saint Hugh, abbot of Cluny, also died, on the third day before the Kalends of May; he too was a father and a most wholehearted lover of the men of Hirsau, whose life was distinguished by many virtues and signs.

*Saint Hugh died.*

*Adalbert becomes bishop of Mainz.*

In the same year also Ruthard, archbishop of Mainz, died; Adalbert, the king’s chancellor, succeeded him in order.

*1111.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Bruno, a great synod of bishops and abbots was held at Rome, with the lord pope presiding, where many useful measures were enacted. King Henry, at Liège, married Matilda, daughter of Henry king of the English, who had been betrothed to him; by her he had no children.

*The king came to Rome.*

In the following year, coming to Rome, he was indeed received honorably by the Apostolic lord; but when they had sat down and discussion was held concerning ecclesiastical and royal affairs, suddenly a tumult was stirred up by the faction of the Romans. The king was so hard pressed by their wickedness that he was now almost taking to flight, had not Frederick, archbishop of Cologne, come to his aid with a strong force and compelled the Romans, overcome with great slaughter, to flee. Having therefore gained the victory, he led the pope away. Yet afterward, having returned to concord, they again returned to Rome; and, after peace had been made with the Romans, the pope consecrated the king as emperor. During communion the king [unclear].

*The emperor is blessed.*

In the following year [Pope?] Dominic called a great synod, and, in a synod of one hundred twenty-five bishops held at Rome, excommunicated all who sided with the emperor against him; and evils in the Church were renewed. When Emperor Henry entered Italy, he had in his army thirty thousand armed men, besides servants and many others following the army without order.

*Conspiracy of the Saxons against the emperor.*

In this year a comet appeared. In the same conspiracy with the Saxons against the emperor were these princes: Lothar, duke of Saxony; Rudolf, margrave; Wibert, duke; the aforesaid Siegfried, count palatine, whom we have mentioned; and Adalbert, archbishop of Mainz, who for this reason was captured by the emperor and in this same year was consigned to custody for nearly three years.
