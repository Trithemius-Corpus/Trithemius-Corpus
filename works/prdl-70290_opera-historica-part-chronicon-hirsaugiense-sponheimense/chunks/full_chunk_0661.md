Above all, it would have been grievous for me to leave my monastery with my friends forever; to have built the very beautiful structures which I built for another, perhaps an enemy and an ungrateful man; and, what surpasses everything, to leave behind me the library of two thousand very precious and very rare volumes, which, as is manifest, I alone brought together, without which I seemed to myself unable to live. This indeed held me back for several years by too great a love of it, so that I would not consent to richer abbacies, which were offered to me, as you know; it always drew me back too much. But now, after the persecution of rivals began to rage against me, I have bravely broken the bonds of love; for the love of Christ I have resolved to leave not only the library most dear to me up to this point, but also the monastery with everything belonging to it, my country, and my friends, lest perhaps, placed in the midst of rivals, the memory of injuries corrupt my mind. Nor, daughter, should you therefore think yourself deserted, since, God calling us, we depart to the Franks. For the Lord Jesus is able to give you a better provider, one who can look after the affairs of your monastery, both spiritual and temporal, much more usefully than I. Your bishop of Metz, in whose diocese your monastery is known to be situated, will not abandon you, but without doubt will provide you with a better abbot. Farewell, with all the sisters committed to you, praying to God for us. From Metz, on the twentieth day of the month of September, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*LXXVI.*

*To the reverend father in Christ, Dom Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, Richmodis, abbess, Anna von Helmstatt, prioress, humble daughters of the whole convent of the monastery of St. Lawrence in Seebach, with prayers, would that they were devout, desire eternal happiness.*

We wish your fatherhood to know that we have been saddened almost to death after reading the letters you sent us, in which you signify that you will leave your abbacy of Sponheim and proceed so far away from us. O why do you leave us, most reverend father, or to whom do you entrust us, most poor little sheep, whom you have been accustomed to feed so sweetly with the milk of heavenly doctrine? Who will there now be, most [unclear] father, who, in your manner, will distribute to us the sweetest sermons on the passion of Christ, on divine love, and on the delightfulness of the heavenly kingdom? Or who hereafter will console us in tribulations? To whose counsel shall we flee, or whom shall we call upon, bereft of your presence? Would that you could see the tears of your daughters; would that, having pity on the sheep committed to you, you would return to your monastery, since there is no one, as we have truly been informed, who does not most greatly desire your return to Sponheim. For the prior of Sponheim was here with us recently, and he said that that Duke Johann, together with all the rest, greatly desires that you return to Sponheim, and that he wishes always to be kind and gracious to you, and not to permit you to suffer any trouble. This same thing your brothers all desire, pray for, and beg; likewise the abbots of the order, who are said to have been at Mainz in chapter, all desire the same thing. Woe to us poor little women, captives enclosed, who are bereft of every human consolation, placed in the midst of enemies: the one refuge which we had, a father and faithful and most loving shepherd, we are compelled to lose while he is still living; we shall never be able to find one like him, who may have such power in learning, [unclear]. If it were lawful for us poor little women to leave the place in which we have been enclosed together, we would follow you wherever you would move, if only we could obtain the permission of the pontiff. But since it is necessary for us to remain fixed in the place, we humbly ask, petition, and beseech your fatherhood by this one thing always to be observed by us, for the shedding of the most precious blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, whom you have always been accustomed to honor in your preaching, that if you have altogether resolved to leave the abbacy of Sponheim, you should by no means on that account renounce the charge of this our monastery, which the lord bishop once gave to you, but should remain our visitor hereafter, just as you have already been until now for many years. For although Würzburg is farther from Seebach than Sponheim, your fatherhood will nevertheless be well able to visit us in its own time, if not every year.
