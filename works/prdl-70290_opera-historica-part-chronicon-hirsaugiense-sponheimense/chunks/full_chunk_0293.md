But the pontiffs of the churches, considering that this sect was not sincere and would bring no small harm upon the faithful peoples, hastened, by the authority of Pope Clement VI, to remove it from their midst as quickly as possible, leading back the correctable, through public penance and the renunciation of error, to the way of truth; but the obstinate, handed over to secular judgment, they burned with fire. And so at length the new and foolish presumption of the Flagellants ceased.

Also in the aforesaid year there was a division of the duchy of Bavaria among the six sons of Louis, formerly emperor. Louis, who was also [unclear], the first-born, margrave of Brandenburg, Louis the Roman, duke, and the younger Otto, duke, received by lot Upper Bavaria, with all things belonging to it. But Stephen, William, and Albert received into possession Lower Bavaria, Holland, and the lordship of Frisia, although afterward another division was made.

In the same year there was a great earthquake in Germany; it overthrew the city of Villach, and many buildings, both in Italy and in Germany, fell from the violence of this commotion.

*1350.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Wighard, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1350, Pope Clement VI, by a solemn constitution previously issued on the matter, recalled the twentieth year of indulgences, which they called the Jubilee in imitation of the Hebrews, from the hundredth year, as Boniface VIII had instituted it, to the fiftieth, moved by the pious consideration that the very brief span of human life grants to very few the ability to live to the hundredth year. Accordingly the second Jubilee is celebrated this year in the city of Rome, by command of Pope Clement, with many flocking there from every side.

In the same year William, fourteenth in order, abbot of my monastery of Sponheim, freely and voluntarily resigned the abbacy into the hands of his community around the feast of Saint Lawrence the martyr; weighed down by old age and infirmity, he died in the same year, on the fifth day before the Kalends of October, and was buried in the chapel of the blessed Mary ever Virgin, which he himself had founded from the foundations in the cloister. After his resignation Philip Meyvvin, the fifteenth abbot, succeeding by election of the brothers, presided for twenty-four years, one month, and twelve days, confirmed by Gerlach, archbishop of Mainz, because Henry, as we have said, had been deposed and excommunicated by the pope and was not regarded as bishop by good and religious men.

*1351.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Wighard, and in the year of the Lord 1351, Emperor Charles IV, in order to please his princes, by whose help and favor he had come to the empire, established many new tolls around the Rhine and granted them to them, to the very great harm of the commonwealth. But when the merchants, too heavily burdened by this, avoided the traffic of the Rhine and brought their complaint to the cities, all with one accord opposed themselves to the emperor. They could in no way go either up or down from [unclear]. But when the princes saw that through this they were being deprived of the revenues of both the old and the new toll, they entered into concord and peace with the citizens and, content with the old and ancient tolls, renounced the new ones.

In the same year the empress Margaret, formerly wife of Emperor Louis IV, having assembled a great army of Bavarians and others, moved an expedition against her son William, count of Holland, who was oppressing his poor men and subjects very cruelly, after being invoked by the inhabitants as the sole daughter of that land. But when a naval battle was joined on the Meuse, on the fourth day of the month of July, that is, on the feast of the Translation of Saint Martin, the mother was defeated by the son; fleeing with a few, she went down to England.

Also in the same year, in the month of December, a comet appeared beneath the very north; when it had disappeared, violent storms of winds followed, and a heavenly beam, burning at its front part, was seen to glide through the sky.

*1352.*

In the tenth year of Abbot Wighard, which was the year of the Lord 1352, Reinhard, son of [unclear] of Sponheim, and Ernest of Molenack, canons of the greater church of Cologne, on the Friday before Palm Sunday, in the court of the official of Cologne, were killed very cruelly for a trivial cause, from rashness, by the leading men of the city.
