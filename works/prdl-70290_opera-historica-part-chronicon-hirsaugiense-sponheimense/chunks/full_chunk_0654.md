We all die, and, as it were, we are poured out like water upon the earth, as that man said: We are owed to death, and we and our mortal deeds will perish, and death is the final boundary of things.

Sweat over useful studies, by which you may provide not only what is necessary for the body, but also that the mind may be kept immune from dreadful death. When, about to return to our own affairs, we shall shortly be with you in your Albiburg, we shall disclose in conversation what moved us to write these things rather boldly. But you, knowing our love toward you, will not curse the exhortation which we have made. Farewell, mindful of yours. From Berlin, on the 20th day of the month of April, in the year of the Christians 1506.

*LXII.*

*To the Most Serene Prince Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, Duke of Stettin and Pomerania, Count of Ruegen and Cassubia, Prince Elector, etc., Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, wishes happiness.*

On the fourteenth day of the month of May I left Berlin, the town of residence of your Serenity, as you know, most illustrious prince, at about the eleventh hour. According to the predictions of our Astrophilus many adversities befell us, but by God's mercy we overcame them all. For, suffering for six days from the disease of stone in the bladder and gravely afflicted, I was compelled to remain in Leipzig and to entrust myself to the hands of physicians, something I had never done before. And when we had come to the Rhine, through the retainers of a certain most wicked count, the incendiary of the monastery of Limburg, I, together with the messenger of your Serenity and my servants and one monk of the aforesaid burned monastery, were captured on the feast of the Most Holy Trinity at about the eleventh hour, and led to a nearby castle. Yet that same evening, recognizing that I was kept under royal protection, they released us all, giving no other reason why they had seized us except that they thought I favored the affairs of the burned monastery of Limburg. By this insult it happened, as you will understand from your messenger, that I could not carry your commissions either to the Archbishop of Cologne, or to the Archbishop of Trier, nor even to the Archbishop of Mainz. Yet I shall act as soon as possible so that they may have your letters and understand your will.

Concerning my own affairs, however, I can write nothing certain until I have spoken with my most excellent prince, who is now absent, and until I have understood more clearly the state of my brothers and likewise the minds of my rivals. For I shall in no way be seen at Sponheim unless all obstacles have first been removed. But if the rashness of my rivals has not been turned aside, I shall do one of two things: either I shall return to you, as you requested, or I shall choose for myself a place in the order suited to my studies, where I may be able to make your Serenity a participant in my studies. You have often heard me saying what I think: that following courts is [unclear], and neither suits my purpose nor plainly agrees with my institution. Therefore I shall seek a place suitable for me in the breadth of my order, if it can be done; if I cannot find one, I shall necessarily do what I can. To your Serenity, however, I give immortal thanks for your very great benefactions toward me, not only those already shown, but also the greater ones promised, if there should be need. I desire that your Excellence, with all his people, may remain unharmed for Nestor's years. From Heidelberg, on the 18th day of the month of June, in the year of the Lord 1506.

*LXIII.*

*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to the brothers beloved in Christ, his sons, the prior and convent in Sponheim: sincere charity in the Lord.*

Having at last returned safely from the March of Brandenburg to our homeland, we sent ahead to you from Frankfurt our chaplain Theodoric, so that, with him reporting the things which we entrusted to him, you might understand our state and mind. Returning to us at Budoris, he reported to us your desire, by which you pray that we return to you as quickly as possible, and that there is and will be no one who can or wishes to trouble us. We indeed believe that what you say is true in your hearts; nevertheless we have not thought that trust should be placed in rivals, whose presumption against us is judged by no one. Therefore we shall do nothing without counsel, especially since certain men among you, full of ambition and faithlessness, are manifestly proved to consent to Cynonotus rather than to us, their pastor.
