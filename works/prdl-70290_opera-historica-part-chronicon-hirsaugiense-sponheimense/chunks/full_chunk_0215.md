Therefore Bodo, Henry, his vassal, knights, with Herold and Conrad, his servants, came into our presence, confessing that they had killed the aforesaid bishop. Since they tearfully repented of their excesses, they humbly asked that penance be enjoined upon them by us for the enormity of so great a crime. Because the door of indulgence must not be closed to the penitent, by the counsel of our brothers we committed them to our beloved son Hugh, cardinal priest of the title of St. Martin, to be heard. After hearing their confession, he made them stand for several days before us, naked in their breeches, with ropes around their necks, in the presence of crowds of people; and by our command he enjoined upon them this penance: that henceforth they should never use arms, except against the Saracens or in defense of [unclear]. They should not wear vair, gray fur, ermine, or colored cloths. They should not go to public spectacles; they should not contract marriages after the death of their wives. As soon as they are able, they should go into the province of Jerusalem, to serve the Lord there for four years against the Saracens. Also in the year noted above, the Tartars, having come out from the mountains of India with their wives and children, plundered the neighboring provinces, to which they had always been accustomed. Then they sought the Parthians; with equal terror they overran Media, Assyria, Persia, Armenia, and the Sarmatians in no long time; at last they fixed their settlements around the Maeotic marshes and did many evils to the neighboring Christians.

*M.CC.III.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Marquard, and in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1203, King Philip, having gathered a strong army, entered Thuringia in order to humble Hermann the Landgrave, because he had abandoned him, adhered to Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, against his will, and supported him, and because he had converted to the uses of his enemy, that is, King Otto, the revenues and proceeds of the church of Mainz falling due in Thuringia. The Landgrave, perceiving openly that the duke of Bohemia was surrounding the city with a siege, and that Philip would capture him, consumed everything by fire, and thus betook himself with his men into Erfurt.
