*Familiar Letters*

Happy are those mortals in whom holy devotion ever burns in the love of God, who, recognizing themselves as pilgrims of this world, sigh in spirit for the heavenly homeland, and, loving nothing of corruptible things, long with constant desire to be brought into the eternal mansions. Death will be pleasant to them, not dreadful; through it they go with joyful prayers from prison, receiving the end of all evils and the beginning, without end, of good things. Death is terrible to those who live in such a manner that after death there is no hope of any good for them, who die in mind and body together, receiving eternal oblivion as the reward of their despair. Farewell from Würzburg, on the fourteenth day of the month of June, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*XXVI.*

*Hermann, by the grace of God Archbishop of Cologne, Prince Elector, etc., to the venerable, sincerely beloved by us in Christ, Johann Trithemius, Abbot of St. James at Würzburg: greeting.*

The matters which you sent to us, entrusted to you on behalf of and by commission of the most excellent prince lord Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, arch-chamberlain of the Holy Roman Empire and Prince Elector, our kinsman and brother, we have received with a grateful mind. And since we do not have, according to our will and judgment, the necessary manner of using those same matters, we desire of you with special longing, wishing that by our present courier you send us in writing full information about each of them; or certainly, if it will be possible for you in any way, that within the space of fifteen days you come down to us in person, to some place of our principality near the Rhine, since we do not intend to remain in these regions beyond fourteen days. For we desire to obtain from you complete knowledge of those matters. Show yourself diligent and willing toward us in these things, as our confidence in you holds. For in these matters you will do us a special kindness and a pleasing service, to be acknowledged with favor and grace, without forgetfulness. Given at Aldenahr, on the Saturday after the feast of Barnabas the Apostle, in the year 1507.

*XXVII.*

*To the most reverend father and most serene prince, Lord Hermann, Archbishop of Cologne, etc., Johann Trithemius, abbot of St. James at Würzburg, wishes happiness.*

Most reverend prince, there would indeed be in me a very ready will to come to you in person, were I not this time prevented by two impediments. The first is that the excellence of your most reverend paternity set for me a period of fourteen days for coming; interpreting these from the sending of your letters, I found them exceedingly short. Hence, fearing that perhaps, when I came down, your serenity would either, as usual, be in Westphalia, or elsewhere far away from the Rhine, and that I would have labored without fruit, I did not dare to undertake so long a journey unless I was certain. The second, which also drew my mind not a little back from the plan, was the absence of my most reverend prelate, stationed outside the homeland; without his will and consent it did not seem to me sufficiently fitting to enter upon so great a length of journey, especially because, by his munificence and kindness, having been placed in charge of this poor monastery, I have placed my whole hope after God in his humanity. I add a third impediment to a voluntary journey, since hitherto, being ignorant of your highness’s counsel in the matters proposed, I have not prepared those things which seemed to me necessary for their completion. But to please, according to my strength, your most reverend paternity I am wholly ready, willing, and prompt. Lest I delay longer, I have decided, around the feast of St. Michael, when your most gracious highness is accustomed to return from Westphalia to the Rhine, God having mercy and my most reverend bishop consenting, to come down to Bonn to your sacred presence, bringing with me those things which in so short a time I have been able, which seemed to me to have to be sent, because the most serene prince of Brandenburg handed over to me nothing except the bare forms themselves of the matters, which I sent some time ago; the rest he sent to be carried by word of mouth. Now, however, I send to your most reverend paternity, by the messenger sent to me, those things which he commanded, namely the manner of using the matters sent long ago, together with the rest tied up in a little bundle; with these would that I may have presented to your most serene highness a pleasing gift of service.
