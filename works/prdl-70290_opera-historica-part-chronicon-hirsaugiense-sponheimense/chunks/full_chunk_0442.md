*Chronicle of Trithemius*

Cuno of Falkenstein, mentioned above, and lord of Münzenberg, born of a mother who was the daughter of the count of Saarwerden, who while he was still living had been ordained archbishop, vigorously ruled the church of Trier for [unclear] years, and did many good things for the church. For the services rendered in many ways to Emperor Charles IV, he obtained Boppard and Wesel, together with half of Sternenberg, which Baldwin, once archbishop, had received in pledge for expenses from Emperor Henry VII, his brother, as a firmer pledge to the church of Trier for a great sum of money, so that this later pledging was greater in amount than the earlier had been.

In the same year there were serious quarrels between Ulrich, count of Württemberg, and the counts of Eberstein. Therefore, when the Württemberger was one day in a natural bath, which we call a woodland bath, the aforesaid counts of Eberstein came secretly with an army and surrounded the place with a siege, hoping that they would certainly capture the count shut up inside. But vain confidence deceived them; for, led at night through the woods by a certain peasant, he escaped from their hands, and a great discord followed in the province of the Swabians. For all the nobles of that land made a conspiracy together against the counts of Württemberg. But the citizens of Esslingen, adhering to the Württembergers, encircled Heisheim with a siege and compelled the nobles to obedience to the aforesaid counts.
