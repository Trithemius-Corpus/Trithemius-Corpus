*Guimundus.*

In these times Guimundus, archbishop of Aversa, formerly a monk of our order, flourished: a man most learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, who left many distinguished monuments of his lively talent. Among these, that famous work is commonly known which he published, divided into three books in dialogue form with Roger the monk, on the body and blood of the Lord, against the new errors of Berengar, deacon of the church of Angers. The same Roger, monk of the monastery of the Cross of Saint Leufridus of our order, a studious and learned man, afterward a monk of Saint Pantaleon in Cologne, wrote, among other things, the deeds of Bruno, archbishop of Cologne and founder of the same monastery, and certain other little works not contemptible for reading. In the same times there also flourished Theodoric, monk of Hirsfeld, who wrote to Richard, abbot of Fulda, on the life and praises of our most holy father Benedict, certain little works, and certain others, whose titles we have arranged in the second book of Illustrious Men.

*1043.*

In the following year, which was that of the Lord’s Nativity 1043, in the second indiction, Amichadus, a monk of Fulda and a Scot by nation, died. For many years he had been enclosed in the same monastery for the love of Christ, a man of most holy life; over his tomb lights were seen at night.

There also flourished about these times Clarus, monk of the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter in Seligenstadt, in the diocese of Mainz, of our order, a learned man of most religious conduct. In that same place, enclosed for the love of God, devoting himself day and night to prayer and contemplation, he is said to have attained the spirit of prophecy. His feast day is kept on the Kalends of January.

In the year of the Lord 1044, which was the forty-second while the monastery of Hirsau was vacant, in the twelfth indiction, Anshelmus, abbot of the monastery of Lorsch, of our order, founded a provostry in honor of Saint Bartholomew the Apostle near Heidelberg, in the diocese of Worms. In it he placed a provost and monks; afterward, in the year of the Lord 1195, Conrad, count palatine of the Rhine, with the consent of Sigehard, abbot of Lorsch, changed it into a monastery of nuns of the same order of ours, and appointed Cunegund as its first abbess.

*1046.*

In the year of the Lord 1046, which was the forty-fourth while the monastery was vacant, Saint Guido, abbot of Bobbio in Italy, of our order, born in the suburb of Ravenna in the villa of Casa Marensis, a man of most holy life, who raised a certain monk named Martin from the dead and performed many other miracles, died on the day before the Kalends of April, in the forty-eighth year of his rule. His feast is celebrated on the Kalends of May, for on that day his holy body was translated from Italy by Henry IV to the city of Speyer and honorably deposited in the church consecrated in his name.

In these times there also flourished Bruno, formerly a monk of the monastery of Monichenneburg of our order in Saxony, bishop of Verden, brother of Count Frederick, a wise and learned man, who energetically governed the church entrusted to him by word and example.

In the forty-fourth year while the monastery was vacant, Emperor Henry entered Italy and deposed the three men contending for the papacy, namely Benedict IX, Sylvester III, and Gregory VI; appointing Suitger, bishop of Bamberg, as the true vicar of Christ, he called him Clement II. From him he received the crown of the Empire on the day of the Lord’s Nativity. But Pope Clement died in the ninth month of his pontificate and was buried in the choir of the greater church of Bamberg.

In these times Heribert, bishop of Auxerre, flourished, a learned and very famous man. After some years, during which he energetically presided over the church of Auxerre, he abandoned the world and became a monk of our order in the monastery of Saint Saviour in the district of Sens.
