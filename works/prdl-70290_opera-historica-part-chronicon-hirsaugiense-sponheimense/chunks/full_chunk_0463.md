*1424.*

In this year, which was the thirty-third of Abbot Bernard, on Jubilate Sunday, which was the fourteenth day of the month of May, the fifth provincial chapter of our order was celebrated, continuing through the following days, in the monastery of St. Stephen the Martyr in the city of Würzburg. At it presided Baldwin of Lüneburg, Theoderic of Northeim, Appello in Sinsheim, and Helfrid of St. John in [Rinckau?], abbots of monasteries of our order; and many things were ordained and decreed there for the reform of our order.

In the same year died Ernest, Duke of Austria, father of Emperor Frederick III.

In this same year Adolf, Duke of Mont, again entered the land of Bar with a strong army and obtained the greater part of it. While he was spending the nights in a certain monastery, as was his custom, in shameless pleasures with women consecrated to God there, he was betrayed and captured one night and presented into the hands of the Duke of Lorraine. After being held in custody for a whole year, therefore, he could not be freed until he himself, together with his son Rupert, renouncing every right that belonged to him in the aforesaid land, granted it into the hand and power of the Duke of Lorraine and his heirs forever.

*1425.*

In the thirty-fourth year of our Abbot Bernard died John, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and Count of Holland, Zeeland, and Hainaut, and Lord of Frisia, son of Duke Albert and grandson of Emperor Louis IV. He had once been elected bishop of Liège; by dispensation of the Council of Constance he had given up the bishopric together with the subdiaconate, had taken a wife, and, subjecting Holland to himself together with the other counties, had deprived Jacoba, daughter of his brother William, of her paternal inheritance, leaving no children. When he died, the aforesaid daughter of his brother sought to reclaim the aforesaid counties of her father William as the nearer heir; but the Duke of Burgundy, son of Duke Albert’s sister, resisted and brought all the land of Holland, with the other appurtenances, under his rule. And so the counties of Holland, Zeeland, and Hainaut, with the lordship of Frisia, which the line of the Palatines and Dukes of Bavaria had possessed for eighty years, came into the surrender of the Dukes of Burgundy down to the present day, not without grave injury to the aforesaid Jacoba, daughter of Duke William, and serious loss to the house of Bavaria.

Also in the year noted above Otto, Archbishop of Trier, kindled by the fire of pious devotion, crossed the sea with much patience and humility and visited the holy sepulchre of the Lord at Jerusalem, carrying with him precious ornaments marked with his arms. These, for the honor of divine worship, he bestowed with eager devotion on the Friars Minor living there, as a perpetual memorial of his pilgrimage, and with humble insistence commended himself to their prayers. The sole cause of this pilgrimage was devotion.

*1426.*

In this year, which was the thirty-fifth of our Abbot Bernard, Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, prince-elector, son of Rupert, once King of the Romans, a pious and renowned man, set out beyond the sea to the Holy Land for love of our Lord Jesus Christ, and visited the sepulchre of our Savior in Jerusalem with great devotion. Returning from the lands beyond the sea, he no longer allowed the beard which he had grown on the holy pilgrimage to be shaved; but as long as he lived he preserved it inviolate in memory of his pilgrimage. And from that time onward he was called by the people the Duke with the Beard.

John also, Count of Sponheim, the last, set out on the same pilgrimage with Duke Louis to the Holy Land, having in his retinue Gobelin, a monk of St. Disibod, his chaplain and secretary, of the Cistercian order. After the death of Bernard, abbot of this monastery, John intruded him, against right, privilege, and the will of the convent, and made him abbot, as we shall say below in its proper place. This Count John of Sponheim, once driven out by his brother Simon, fled to the Margrave of Baden; treated honorably and kindly by him, he promised that in return for so great a benefit he would give to that same Margrave a considerable part of the County of Sponheim. And indeed, after Count Simon died, having returned to his paternal inheritance with the help of the aforesaid Margrave, he gave it as he had promised; and by such means and donation the Margraves of Baden came to the County of Sponheim down to the present day.
