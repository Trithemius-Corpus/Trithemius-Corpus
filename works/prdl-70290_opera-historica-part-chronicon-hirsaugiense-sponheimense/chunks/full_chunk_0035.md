*Concerning the wife and sons of Emperor Otto the First*

In this same year died Edith, daughter of the king of England, the first wife of Emperor Otto, a holy woman and very devoted to Christ, buried in the aforesaid monastery of Magdeburg. She bore Otto two sons, Liudolf, duke of Saxony, and William, who after Frederick was archbishop of Mainz. After this he took as wife Adelaide, daughter of the duke of Burgundy and a widow, by whom he likewise had two sons, Otto [unclear] Quedlinburg.

In the same year also died Matilda, mother of Otto the First and empress, and she was buried in the aforesaid convent of nuns beside her husband Henry.

In these days Wenceslaus, prince of Bohemia, a holy and most Christian man, was martyred by his brother, while ambition for rule was raging. In vengeance for him Emperor Otto declared war on Boleslaus the fratricide, and there was a long contest between them for fourteen years.

Around these times Arigandus, abbot of the monastery of St Peter in Francia, was held famous for religion and sanctity in the city of Sens; he took care to reform the monastic life in that place splendidly, no less by example than by word. In these days also, after William, Germandus, a monk of St Germanus of Auxerre of our order, became archbishop of Sens, a learned and religious man; after he had governed the church of Sens energetically for some years, he passed to the Lord and was buried in his own monastery of St Germanus, from which he had been taken. After him Hildemannus, a monk of St Denis of Paris of our order, a very learned man, was made archbishop of Sens. In the twenty-first year of Abbot Diethmar, a great famine prevailed in Gaul. The Hungarians fought with the Bavarians and the emperor, and were defeated. In the twenty-third year Emperor Otto conquered the Danes and [unclear] their king Gotfrid. Moreover he established the archbishopric of Magdeburg, and built that city.

In the twenty-sixth year of Abbot Diethmar, which was the year 951 of the Lord’s Nativity, Emperor Otto celebrated his wedding with Matilda, his second wife, in the city of Pavia, with the greatest pomp and solemnity; in the previous year he had freed her from the chains of Berengar, after defeating him.

In the following year, which was the last of Abbot Diethmar, the aforesaid tyrant Berengar entered, conquered, and overcame the same man, and drove him from Italy. Finding nowhere that the sole of his foot might rest, he at last at Augsburg submitted himself with his wife in surrender to the emperor; and, sent to the castle of Bamberg and committed to custody, not long afterwards he [unclear]. In this year great earthquakes occurred in various places in Germany and Gaul.

In the same year of the Lord’s Nativity 952, in the tenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Nones of May, in the twenty-seventh year of his rule and the seventy-first of his age, [he died and was] buried in the church of St Aurelius: a man in every respect worthy of God, who splendidly reformed, by word and example, the regular observance in this monastery when it had almost collapsed. Nor did he show himself zealous only in the observances of regular discipline, but he also exercised great diligence in preserving and enlarging the possessions of the monastery. For he greatly ennobled this place with books and relics of the saints, and adorned it with many ornaments. A great multitude of monks flourished in this monastery under his rule; some of them were very distinguished for holiness of life, while more were held in very celebrated repute because of their knowledge and learning in the Scriptures.
