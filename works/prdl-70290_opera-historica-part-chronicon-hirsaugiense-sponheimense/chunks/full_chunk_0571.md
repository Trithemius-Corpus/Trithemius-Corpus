In the same year also, after Trithemius, our abbot, had returned from Cologne to Speyer, there came to him a certain doctor of medicine named Narcissus, from the kingdom of the French, with letters from a certain bishop of [Cahors?], who summoned Trithemius to him in France, proposing great gifts and having much and sufficient money ready at hand for travel expenses. For the aforesaid prelate, moved by Trithemius’s reputation, desired to have him with him in France, either permanently or for a time, and for that reason promised many things. But Abbot Trithemius, who had promised the Margrave Joachim that he would follow his steps to Ursulum, and had already received from him fifty florins for travel expenses, desiring above all to keep the faith he had promised, could by no means enter France with the doctor who had been sent. Therefore, answering the prelate by letters concerning certain questions, he sent the doctor back to the French, declaring to him that on that occasion he could not come at all.

In the year noted above also, on the seventh day of September, Reymundus, cardinal priest of Santa Maria Nuova, bishop of Gurk, died and was buried at Viterbo. For three continuous years he had been legate a latere of the Apostolic See throughout the whole German nation: a man most holy in morals and life, conspicuous for all integrity, a great friend and singular patron of our Abbot Trithemius, a great cultivator of justice, and at the same time the most constant despiser of all the honors and riches of the world. Our age had no one like him.

*1506.*

In the twenty-second year of Trithemius our abbot, the winter was quite harsh and very prolonged.

In the same year the aforesaid prince Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, established a new universal school in his town of Frankfurt an der Oder. On the twenty-sixth day of April, which was the second Sunday after the feast of the Lord’s Resurrection, he inaugurated it with great solemnity, Abbot John being present. Its first rector of the university was appointed from the faculty of theology: a lecturer from Buchen, an East Franconian, a Leipzig doctor called [Wimpina?].

At these times, while John Trithemius our abbot was staying in the March, our monastery, both because of his long absence and because of the recent losses which it had lately suffered in the war, was sufficiently afflicted in both conditions; there was no one to furnish help. But certain of the brothers, by their incautious speeches and foolish complaints, all of which were reported to Trithemius himself by a messenger going between, wounded his mind very deeply and finally alienated him completely from us.

In the year noted above, on the fourteenth day of May, John Trithemius our abbot, honored with gifts and presents by the most wise Margrave Joachim, and having obtained permission to depart, left Ursulum with an honorable company, set out on the journey toward the regions of the Rhine, and on the second day of June reached the city of Speyer. From Frankfurt he had sent ahead Brother Theoderic, his chaplain, to Sponheim, so that he might diligently inquire into the condition of the monastery, investigate whether there was any disturbance by rivals, and summon his prior to Heidelberg; for the abbot was unwilling to return to the monastery unless he had first understood the brothers’ feelings toward him. He therefore remained with the abbot of Limburg until the end of September at [Budoras?], silently awaiting the outcome of the matter. But after he understood that the feelings of certain brothers toward him were less sincere in the ardor of charity, and that the mind of Duke John of [Simmern?], a prince otherwise very pious, had been somewhat moved, as he was told, by the careless denunciation of certain rivals, he judged it altogether safer to leave this abbacy of his own will than to place himself in the midst of disturbance.
