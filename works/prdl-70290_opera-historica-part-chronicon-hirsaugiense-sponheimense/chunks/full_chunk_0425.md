*Chronicle of Trithemius*

For after Wenceslaus, king of Bohemia, had died, the aforesaid Count John married Elizabeth his daughter, and with her obtained the kingdom for thirty-six years. When he too died, the aforementioned Charles succeeded, and after him his son Wenceslaus.

1347.

In this year, which was the sixth of our abbot Wilhelm, on the day of Saint Gereon the martyr, Emperor Louis died, and he is buried in the church of blessed Mary ever virgin in the city of Munich. Report says that he perished by the poison of a certain duchess of Austria, whom all were accustomed to call Maultasch because of the foul and hideous shape of her face. This, indeed, we hold as certain: that after returning home from hunting, and after taking food with her at the same table, he at once began to feel ill; and, hoping that by movement of the body he would be better, he mounted his horse and went off again to the hunt. But as his body grew hot, not far from Furstenfeld, he fell from his horse and expired in the hands of his attendants. He was a man for a long time good and peaceable, who always bore injuries with an even mind, and conquered more by piety than by severity.

After Emperor Louis had died, Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine, who was also called Adolphus, son of Adolphus and grandson of Rudolf, brother of the deceased Emperor Louis; Louis, duke of Bavaria, son of the deceased emperor; Otto, margrave of Brandenburg; and Henry, archbishop of Mainz, who, although he had been deposed by the pope, nevertheless conducted himself as archbishop, came together as one. By letters and solemn envoys they invited Edward, king of England, who in that year had most gloriously triumphed against the French, to assume the empire. Giving thanks to them, he sent back word that he was so occupied in governing the kingdoms of England and France that he could by no means attend to the empire of the Romans. When the aforesaid princes had received this from the king of England, they sent letters and envoys to the margrave of Meissen, son-in-law of the deceased Emperor Louis, and elected him king of the Romans against Charles, who, as we have said, had been elected by the other princes while Louis was still alive. He at once consented to the election made of him. Meanwhile the year passed while these things were being done.

*Our estate in Blanich is bought.*

In the year noted above, Wilhelm, abbot of this monastery, bought the estate that we have in Blenich, with all its appurtenances, fields, vineyards, meadows, gardens, pastures, and houses, for a certain sum of money from Clementia, widow left by Tilmann the knight, with the consent of George of Savvelnheim, her son-in-law, for herself and all her heirs, as is clear in the letters of his consent drawn up concerning this, whose date is in the year of the Lord 1348, on the days of the martyrs Felix and Adauctus.

In the same year Otto, bishop of Wurzburg, died. Albert of Hohenlohe succeeded him and presided for forty-four years. In the sixth year of his pontificate he tightly besieged Wurzburg for three weeks, and powerfully forced the citizens to his obedience, which they were refusing.
