*Chronicle of Trithemius* 428

Now the aforementioned monastery of St. James the Greater, of our order, was once built and endowed in the suburb of the city of Würzburg by the most holy prelate Embricho, around the year of the Lord’s nativity [MCXXX?], as was said far above, in the sixteenth year of Bernhem, our first abbot. In it monks of the Scottish nation were accustomed to dwell from the beginning, who lived commendably for many years according to the Rule of our most holy father Benedict; but little by little, with time, at last falling away from the first fervor of their institution, they finally brought the monastery itself down into utter poverty, and they themselves failed entirely. For in the year of the Lord 1397 Philip, the last abbot from the Scots, died, and left no monk in the community.

After his death the most reverend lord prelate of Würzburg, Laurentius, by apostolic commission and at the same time by ordinary authority, commendably undertook to reform the vacant place in both estates. For the sake of this good reform, [unclear] he introduced from the monastery of St. Stephen in the same place; he supplied them with the necessities of life, and paid in full almost all the debts contracted by the Scots. He made the church, darkened by age, as it were new and very beautiful; he gave ornaments, chalices, and several books for the library; and, restoring the community magnificently almost more than the first founder had done, he generously gave vineyards, annual rents, ready money in no small amount, and many other things.

Finally, even down to the present day, not ceasing from the good work which he began, he has bestowed more upon Trithemius himself and upon the monastery, and promises without doubt that he will do greater things, conferring notable annual rents and money in no small quantity and number for buying new [unclear].

Therefore, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1506, in the ninth indiction of the Romans, on the fifteenth day of the month of October, Johannes Trithemius, elected and confirmed abbot of St. James at Würzburg, accepted real possession in the presence of Conrad the abbot, Master Peter the seal-bearer, Caspar the fiscal officer of lord Laurentius the bishop, and also the notary Master Martin Mosius, and very many witnesses and other notable men. These things were done when he had not yet completed the forty-fifth year of his age, but when twenty-two years, two months, and eighteen days had been completed and passed in many tribulations and labors from his assumption of the abbacy of this monastery in Sponheim.

While these things were being done concerning Abbot Johannes Trithemius at Würzburg, we were here in the monastery daily expecting that he would return to us, having hope and consolation that the abbots sent to him from the annual chapter at Mainz would by their exhortations induce him to return. But our confidence deceived us, because, without our knowing it, he assumed the aforesaid abbacy of St. James, and afterwards resigned this one.

Accordingly, in the aforesaid year, on the fourteenth day of the month of November, letters sent by that lord Abbot Johannes Trithemius came to us at Sponheim; by them he informed us that he had accepted the often-mentioned abbacy at Würzburg and had resigned ours, advising and exhorting us that in his place we should elect as abbot whomsoever we wished, according to the Rule. Therefore our abbacy of Sponheim was vacant after his resignation for almost two months, both because knowledge of Trithemius’s resignation came to us late, and because, being less experienced in the installation of a new abbot and necessarily seeking the counsels of many, we also awaited for a rather long time the arrival of our visitors, who had to be present.

But after the transfer of Abbot Trithemius to Würzburg had been reported to us by his brothers, Master Jacobus Trithemius and Johannes Damius, a monk of our monastery, now confessor and provost in the nuns’ community of Neuburg near Heidelberg, who had been summoned and had gone up with him, we all marveled beyond measure, since we had not thought that the matter would have such an outcome, and that he would abandon us so suddenly, who for so long a time had been our prelate and abbot. Many would have believed that, at least for love of the library, which he had assembled precious and rich in every variety of writings, he ought to have remained with us, held back no less by the beauty and convenience of the new abbatial house, which he had built at great expense, than by regard for his friends and the fame of his great name.

But all these things, being both vain and nonetheless someday to be left behind through death, he abandoned of his own free will, and humbly committed himself to the mercy of almighty God, knowing that divine Scripture has promised: Cast your thought upon the Lord, and he himself will nourish you, who is Jesus Christ, God blessed forever. Amen. Here is the end of the abbacy of Johannes Trithemius, abbot in Sponheim, whom Prior Nicolaus of the land of Luxembourg succeeded, DE
