*The Chronicle of Trithemius*

*1378.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Crafto, Henry, count of Sponheim, son of Count Walram, set out for the Holy Land for the remission of his sins; Abbot Crafto attached to him as chaplain Wernher of Dille, a monk of this community. But the abbot’s brother, called Eytelwolf of Sponheim, who set out with the count to Jerusalem, received there the dignity of knighthood.

In the same year the tower of St. Martin in Cologne, of our order, was burned up by fire, and a great part of the adjoining [buildings?] perished in the same fire; and the tower itself remained without a roof for nearly 116 years, until at last a certain merchant, Ewald of Bacharach, a citizen of Cologne, when dying bequeathed so much money for its restoration that it was rebuilt in the form in which it is now seen. For his soul a solemn mass in honor of the blessed Mary ever virgin is sung every day in the same church.

Also in the same year, on the vigil of St. Andrew the apostle, Emperor Charles IV died. After him came Wenceslaus, his son, with whom he had reigned for eight years, having been emperor for twenty-two years without the crown of the empire. While still alive, his father had caused him, as we have said, to be elected king of the Romans, namely as a boy of fifteen years, promising each prince-elector one hundred thousand florins. Since he could not pay these, he was forced to assign to the princes, as pledges until the money should be paid, the best cities of the empire. Thus Rupert the Palatine [received] Oppenheim, Odernheim, Ingelheim, and Kaiserslautern, with all their revenues, which his heirs still possess today and will possess until the aforesaid money is paid.

*1379.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Crafto, Louis, king of Hungary, besieged Venice and inflicted much damage on the Venetians, compelling them to change their seal so that henceforth they would not write themselves “lords of the sea,” a title they had presumptuously usurped for themselves.

In the same year Wenceslaus, king of the Romans, came to Cologne and was received with great honor, having in his retinue a cardinal, legate of the apostolic see.

Also in the same year Otto, margrave of Brandenburg, son of the late Emperor Louis IV, died and was buried in Salzwedel. This Otto, like two of his brothers, William, who held Holland, of whom we have spoken, and Louis called “the Roman,” who was born at Rome, departed without children, leaving to his brothers everything that he possessed.

*1380.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Crafto, almost all the cities and towns of the empire around the Rhine and in Swabia, together with Pilgrim, archbishop of Salzburg, and the city of Regensburg, having made a league among themselves, attacked with plundering and fire, with an army of nearly eight thousand, the dukes of Bavaria and certain others in their vicinity who were troublesome to them. But after five years, when they had begun to act more insolently, they stirred up against themselves the minds of all the princes around the Rhine and in Swabia, Bavaria, and Franconia. These, after uniting a very great army of their men, advanced against them, inflicted very great losses, and destroyed that league or confederation. Pilgrim, archbishop of Salzburg, was at that time captured in [unclear]; the castle Hoensolms was captured by the citizens of the aforesaid confederation and, fire having been set to it, [unclear].

*1381.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Crafto, Lampert, from being an abbot of our order, bishop of Speyer, was transferred by apostolic authority to the church of Bamberg. Adolph of Nassau succeeded him in the bishopric of Speyer and presided for fifteen years, a man acute in affairs and of penetrating intelligence, who had certain disputes with his citizens.

In the same year Wenceslaus, king of the Romans, because of the plague, left Bohemia; between [unclear] the count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria serious disagreements arose, which went so far that, becoming public enemies against each other, they inflicted much damage on one another. At length, however, through the mediation of friendly princes, they were brought back to concord.
