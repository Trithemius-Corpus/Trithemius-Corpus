*1150.*

In these days, when St. Hildegard, the most devout handmaid of Christ, was dwelling on Mount St. Rupert near Bingen with her holy virgins, the necessary dwellings and convent having been built, as we have said, it happened that St. Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux, while travelling down the river Rhine from Frankfurt to Cologne, visited in person her whom he knew by reputation and writings. When he had come to her, after the customary prayers and the courtesies of greeting, he himself ordered the volumes that she had written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit to be brought forth. After examining them carefully, and admiring them beyond what can be said, he is reported to have said to those standing around: “These writings were not devised by human means, but divinely inspired through the Holy Spirit; nor can any [mortal?] man understand them unless he has been inwardly reformed in his soul to the likeness of God.”

*St. Bernard visits St. Hildegard.*

*St. Bernard commends the writings of Hildegard.*

To this the provost of the virgins, a monk sufficiently learned and of most devout way of life, replied: “Reverend father, what you have said is indeed true; but many people, not only secular laymen, but also religious and learned men, torment the soul of the handmaid of Christ with daily disparagements, asserting that these things are not divinely inspired, but are either women’s dreams and phantasms of a disordered brain, or deceptions sent in by demons.” To him the man of God said: “We do not wonder, dearest brother, if those sleeping in sins think divine revelations to be dreams, knowing that what the Apostle said is true: ‘The animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God; for it is foolishness to him, and he cannot understand, because it is examined spiritually.’ For to all who lie as if asleep in sins of pride, greed, and lust, divine admonitions are accustomed to seem dreams, because if they were awake in the fear of the Lord, they would without doubt recognize the signs of divine revelations as true. But those who say that these things are sent in by demons do not know the hidden and mystical signs of divine inspirations. They are to be judged like those who, puffed up, said that our Lord and Savior Christ cast out demons by the power of Beelzebub.” He also said to St. Hildegard: “And you, dearest daughter, do not fear the reproaches of ignorant people, since you have God as protector; for they will perish like stubble, but the truth of the Lord remains forever. We also, for the honor of almighty God, will take care that these writings of yours be read by our lord the pope, just as also those that you sent to Trier to be approved.” Holy Hildegard then gave the man of God, at his request, a small portion of the relics of St. Rupert, duke of the people of Bingen and confessor. Then, bidding one another farewell, St. Bernard continued the journey he had begun, shining everywhere with miracles.

In this same year Abbot Bernhelm, through the intercession of Count Megenhard the founder, obtained from Henry, archbishop of Mainz, and St. Hildegard the right leg of blessed Rupert, the aforesaid confessor and duke, complete from the knee down to the foot or ankle, with skin, flesh, and bones; and he brought it with great reverence and honor to this monastery of his at Sponheim.
