*Johannes Trithemius.*

Today you see among strangers him who displeased his own, whom henceforth you will by no means be able to recall to yourselves. Your wickedness could never endure the best men, because they would by no means consent to your frivolities. O Sponheim, Sponheim, always abhorring good and useful men from the beginning of your reform, with how many efforts I wished to reveal your miseries, and you refused to agree. Behold, then at last, full of weariness, I am compelled to follow better brothers whom you repelled by injury. Did not the envy of petty men compel Henry of Northeim, an excellent man and a most zealous guardian of regular discipline, to return to the Saxons? After him, by the equal madness of rivals, John Thoringus, now prior in Pegau, was driven out from you; likewise John of Dreifa, now prior in the monastery of Mary; Nicholas of Stauro[polis?], cellarer of Limburg; John Damius, provost of the nuns in Newcastle near Budorim; and John Venatoris, instructor of the novices in the aforesaid monastery of Pegau. Each of these, succeeding one another in turn, performed the office of prior in you; and because they had zeal for monastic discipline, you refused to bear them. I pass over the rest, not priors, monks as well as novices, to whom the envy of your men granted a place of better fortune. This, Sponheim, was always your weakness and nausea: to vomit up what was sound and whole, but to retain in the stomach of the belly what was sick and useless, not without danger. Good, mature, and useful men, in whom I might have rested secure, you cast out through envy, so that, deprived of their aid, you might at last more easily rage against the shepherd. When the harmless members of the household have been expelled, what remains except that the father of the household be expelled? Fabricated words do not bring a lawful excuse, since the one who gives occasion for damage is considered to have caused the damage. For besides those things wrongly committed by you against us, which we have listed above, your lying detraction stirred us no less to indignation, since, God being witness, we have committed none of the things which you fabricated against us with an impious mind. We blush to speak of the crimes of certain of you, which are manifest to you all; but since these things which we write this time will remain secret among us, and since integrity compels us, let us bring back to your memory things of which you were never [unclear]. Good brothers, we exhort you by the strict judgment of God, in which the secrets of hearts will be manifest: cease to persecute an innocent father, concerning whom you know that whatever crime has been spoken was fabricated and lied. Rather examine yourselves and do penance, since it is small consolation if your crimes lie hidden from men, when they cannot by any means lie hidden from God. Read the schedule enclosed with these writings, and you will understand how much penance you need. We cannot sufficiently marvel at your rashness, by which you seek to involve innocents in your dangers, not fearing your own, to such a degree that you have not even known how to spare us. If shame had not prevented us, we would have caused your reprobate way of life, by which in our absence you always advanced toward worse things, to be brought to the knowledge of the fathers, so that all might recognize how great an injury we have suffered from you. But we trust in the Lord Jesus, who alone knows our innocence, that all the things which you have committed against us will come to light even though we reveal nothing, and that by the just judgment of God you will pay deserved penalties in this time, lest you perish in the future examination. You indeed gave us occasion to believe this by your wickedness, committing against us such things as good cloistered men never committed against their shepherd.

Therefore the abbacy of the monastery of the divine bishop Martin at Sponheim, which, having been canonically elected and confirmed long ago, we undertook, and for twenty-three years, two months, and twenty days, with God’s aid and according to our strength, took care to rule, maintain, and govern according to the purity of the rule of our most holy father Benedict, today by the handwriting of our own hand, on account of your incorrigible rashness and the manifold injuries received both from you and from others, before the crucified Jesus Christ, into the decision of the convent, not induced nor persuaded or compelled by anyone, but of our own free will, in the name of God, we resign, content with those things which on the day before the feast of Saint James the Greater, Apostle, in the suburb of the city of Würzburg, we received to govern, offered by the most reverend bishop Laurentius. Moreover we confess and publicly protest in these writings that we believe, hold, and pronounce John Nutius the elder, John Pingionita the elder, and Nicholas, son of Scultetus, innocent, guiltless, and free from all injuries and insults inflicted upon us by you. For their innocence and integrity are sufficiently clear to us: they always obeyed our commands with pure devotion, and everywhere withdrew themselves from the pursuits of your depravity. Therefore we declare them exempt and guiltless of all your injuries, insults, and detractions, by which you brought it about that we departed from you. Likewise, among those dead in life, except for Haymo, we believe and pronounce all innocent of your conspiracies. May almighty God forgive you the things that you have committed against us, and not impute them to you as a sin unto death, but remit in this life both the fault and the penalty due for it.
