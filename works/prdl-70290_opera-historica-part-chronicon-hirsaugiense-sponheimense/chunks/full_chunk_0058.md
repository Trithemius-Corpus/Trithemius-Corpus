*Of the Monastery of Hirsau* ...to have first built that monastery by the counsel and aid of a certain Saint Pirminius, one of the disciples of the blessed abbot Maurus, at that time a bishop; to have introduced monks of the rule of Saint Benedict; and also to have provided the necessary revenues.

The Hungarians are converted to the faith through Gisela [unclear] of the Hungarians, with their king Stephen. For that holy woman herself, by her insistence, brought the king to desire that he and his whole people be baptized; in baptism he was called Stephen, and thereafter he adorned his life with such great holiness that after death he was enrolled among the number of the saints.

*M X.*

In the sixth year while the abbacy was vacant, Bruno, from a monk of our order made archbishop [unclear], after being afflicted with many tortures and having his hands and feet cut off, at last completed his martyrdom by beheading.

About these times flourished Burchard, formerly a monk of the monastery of Gembloux, or, as others prefer, of Bobbio, bishop of Worms, a man most zealous in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, once a hearer and disciple of Albert, monk of Bobbio and afterwards abbot of Gembloux, as we have said above. Among other little works of his talent, he laboriously and quite usefully brought together from various sources into one that great volume of Decrees, divided into twenty books, which the ancients, from the author’s name, called the Burchard.

There also flourished in these times Meginfrid, monk of Fulda, a chronographer, who, like a rose among thorns, shone out clear as a studious and learned man among the idle, carnal, and ignorant monks of his monastery. He, among other little works of his talent, [unclear] of the holy abbot of Gembloux, a certain hearer and disciple, as we have said above. [Unclear] inserted those succeeding one another in time, and briefly noted the deeds of each; he also made mention, in like manner, of those monasteries to which he knew either monks or abbots had been sent from Fulda, and by interweaving their successions of abbots and more memorable deeds, described them concisely. From the labors of this man, I confess that I have necessarily inserted much in this work of ours concerning the history of Hirsau.

About these times there occurred a wondrous deed of the emperor Henry. A certain young man, by the maleficium of two old women, was changed into the form of an ass, so that to all who saw him he appeared an ass. This seems impossible to many, who do not understand that by divine permission the evil spirit can deceive the eyes of men with astonishing illusions. In these times also Balderic, bishop of Liege, the immediate successor of Notker, founded the monastery of Saint James on the island of Liege, in which he placed monks of our order.

*M XI.*

In the ninth year while the abbacy was vacant, the greater church of Bamberg was consecrated, with the emperor and forty-six bishops standing by. And in the same year Willigis, archbishop of Mainz, died.

About these times, when Ezzelinus, count palatine of the Rhine, brother of Saint Cunigund the empress, opposed the king, and for ten continuous years, [unclear] and, with his magnanimity confirmed to his opinion, by oaths and public pledge from all the Lotharingians, all royal honor [unclear]: the king, judging it more prudent to placate an excellent man with benefactions than to trouble him with any annoyances, sought his friendship. Having obtained it, he granted to him, to be possessed by his children as a perpetual inheritance, the island which is in the Rhine of Saint Suitbert, with all its appurtenances, Duisburg also and Saalfeld, no small supports of the kingdom.

This count had taken a wife named Mechtild, daughter of the empress Theophanu and sister of Otto III, from whom there were born to him three sons and seven daughters: Hermann, afterwards archbishop of Cologne before Pilgrim, surnamed Noble; Everard, after Poppo archbishop of Trier, namely from being provost of Worms; and Ludolph, successor of his father’s duchy. Mieszko, duke of Poland, took Richiza, the first daughter, as wife. Adelheid in Nivelles, Theophanu in Essen, Helwig in Neuss, Mathildis in Dietkirchen, and Willichalda in the monastery of Saint Mary at Cologne on the Capitol, and Sophia likewise in the monastery of Saint Mary at Mainz and Gandersheim, were all at that time placed in positions of rule as abbesses of nuns of our order.

This count Ezzelinus, a rich and vigorous man, built a monastery of our order not far from Cologne in honor of Saint Nicholas, formerly archbishop of Myra, which is called Brauweiler; he adorned it with many estates and ornaments, and in it he was buried. In these also times...
