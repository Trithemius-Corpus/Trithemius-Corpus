*1284.*

In the twentieth year of Peter our abbot, on the second day of the month of April, Wernher, archbishop of Mainz, died, and was buried in the church of Mainz. The see of Mainz was vacant for three years, two men having been elected in dissension, namely Peter, canon of Basel, provost of the greater church, who had once been physician to Rudolf, king of the Romans, and Gerard of Eppstein, archdeacon of Trier. After they had contended for the bishopric at the apostolic see for three years, at last Pope Honorius IV, setting both aside, raised Henry of the Order of Minors, bishop of Basel, to be archbishop of Mainz. But the aforesaid Peter the physician he appointed in place of the said Henry as bishop of the church of Basel.

In the same year Berthold, bishop of Wurzburg, died. Mangold of Neuenburg succeeded him and presided for twelve years; he was the forty-third in the order of the bishops of Wurzburg.

*1285.*

In the twenty-first year of Peter our abbot, the church of the Blessed Virgin Mary at the Steps in Mainz was burned. In the same year the canons of the church of Wurzburg, together with all the clergy, were violently expelled by the citizens on the sixth ides of January, and were stripped of all their goods.

In these times there came a certain unknown man, very like Emperor Frederick, who said that he was Emperor Frederick; and first indeed in Meissen, then in Wetzlar and in other [unclear], at last, after Pope Honorius IV had set both aside, Henry of the Order of Minors, bishop of Basel, was raised to be archbishop of Mainz. But the aforesaid Peter the physician he appointed in place of the said Henry as bishop of the church of Basel.

In the same year Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, died, and was buried in the monastery of Furstenfeld, which he had founded [unclear]; he left two sons, Rudolf the count palatine, and Louis, afterwards emperor of the Romans. Rudolf, the firstborn, begot Rudolf, Rupert, and Adolf, of whom mention will be made in their place.

*1286.*

In the twenty-second year of Peter our abbot, Pope Honorius IV, as we said above, after Peter and Gerard had been contending at the apostolic see over the archbishopric of Mainz, set them aside, and ordained Henry, bishop of Basel, of the Order of Minors, as archbishop of Mainz. He presided for one year, eleven months, and five days. But Peter the provost was made bishop of Basel, and that long-lasting and destructive dissension for the church came to an end.

*1287.*

In the twenty-third year of Peter our abbot, the Jews secretly seized a certain boy named Wernher, born in a little village called Wamraidt, without his parents’ knowledge, and took him with them to Wesel. On the thirteenth calends of May, piercing him with needles in order to draw blood, they cruelly killed him after many torments. His body, placed in a certain chapel situated on the mountain in the town of Bacharach, is shown to the present day incorrupt and whole in memory of his martyrdom; it is said that immediately at the beginning of his burial it shone with many miracles.

In the same year Frederick, bishop of Worms, died in the sixth year of his pontificate. Simon of Schoneck succeeded him in the bishopric and presided for four years. This Bishop Simon, in the first year of his episcopate, consecrated the church and monastery of nuns of the Order of Preachers near Worms, called Hochheim, then newly built by Rudolf the knight.

*1288.*

In the twenty-fourth year of Peter our abbot, William the monk died; having been elected in dissension against Peter, and because of the many evils he had done, he had been captured by Wernher, bishop of Mainz, and imprisoned for nearly twenty years, as has been said.

In the same year, on the fifteenth calends of April, brother Henry, archbishop of Mainz, died and was buried in the greater church at Mainz. After his death two men were again elected in dissension, namely Gerard of Eppstein, canon of Mainz and archdeacon of Trier, who had been elected before with Peter, as we said, after the death of Wernher; and Emmerich of Schoneck, scholasticus of Mainz.
