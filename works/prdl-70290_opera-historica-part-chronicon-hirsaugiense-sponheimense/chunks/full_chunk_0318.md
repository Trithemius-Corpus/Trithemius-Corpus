Henry of Ulmen, a knight born in the diocese of Trier, likewise, when Constantinople had been captured at the same time, carried away, among other relics of the saints, a large piece of the Holy Cross. This he divided into three parts: one he gave to the monastery of Saint Matthias near Trier; another to a certain convent of nuns of the order of Saint Augustine, which is called Stuba, in the same diocese of Trier; and the third to the monastery of Saint Pantaleon in Cologne, together with the head of the same holy martyr. The remaining relics he distributed to Laach and to other churches.

*1151.*

Udo, a nobleman of Sponheim, who twenty-four years earlier had made to this monastery that solemn donation concerning the estates of Bugenavy and Gentzigen, in the presence of Adelbert the elder, archbishop of Mainz, and many others, after the death of his wife Judith came to conversion in his old age; and in this year, under lord Bernhelm, he was made a monk in this our monastery. After living for three years in great devotion, he at last, under Craffto the First, on the sixth day before the Ides of June, rested in the Lord, a gentle and most devout man.

In this year Abbot Bernhelm, weighed down by old age and labor, began to be more infirm than usual. When he perceived that the day of death was drawing near him, having received the ecclesiastical sacraments with the greatest devotion, and having most prudently ordered the affairs of the monastery, not without the sadness of the brethren, whose number had now risen beyond twenty, he passed to the Lord on the sixth day before the Kalends of April in the aforesaid year, in the twenty-seventh year of his pastoral office, not yet completed, and in the seventy-third year of his age. He was buried with great honor and with many tears of the brethren in the middle of the choir, a man worthy of eternal memory, beloved by God and men; he instructed the flock committed to him most excellently in all religion and holiness by word and example, and converted many from iniquity to the path of justice and uprightness of life.

*Concerning Craffto the First, the second abbot of this monastery, who presided happily for 24 years, two months, and five days.*

In the year of the Lord noted above, 1151, in the fourteenth indiction, after lord Bernhelm, the first abbot of this monastery, had died, as has been said, and had been buried, on the same day, namely the sixth day before the Kalends of April, the brethren, assembling together, unanimously, with absolutely no one dissenting, elected as abbot Lord Craffto, a monk of this monastery, of whom we spoke above, son of Megenhard, count of Spanheim. He was still new in the order, but mature, religious, and prudent; he did many good things for this monastery, and was venerable no less for humility than for nobility of blood. Though elected, he consented unwillingly, preferring for the love of God to dwell humbly among the monks rather than to be imperiled by the honors of this world. Yet, overcome by the prayers and tears of the brethren, he could not resist longer, but, weeping and declaring himself unworthy, gave his consent. Afterward, on the Nones of June, which was the day of Saint Boniface the martyr, he was consecrated by Henry, archbishop of Mainz, with great solemnity and honor in the greater church of Mainz, with abbots, clergy, and many of the number of the nobles standing by. He was indeed a man of great prudence, competently learned, who understood very well those things that pertained to his station. He was fully grounded in the fear of the Lord; in his conduct with the brethren he was mature, not turbulent, and easily drew to himself the love of all good men. For he was gentle, mild, and humble; he knew how to offend or harm no one, but to benefit all. Having been ordained abbot, he took care with all zeal to show himself a worthy and useful shepherd to the brethren committed to him, and he always went before them in all religion and humility by word and example. He presided over this monastery usefully and energetically for 23 years, two months, and five days, as we shall say in order.

*1152.*

In this year Adelbero, archbishop of Trier, died; Hillin, dean of the greater church, succeeded him for eighteen years. From this archbishop Hillin, Craffto, the second abbot of this monastery, of whom we have spoken, obtained by many prayers the head of Saint Irmina, virgin, daughter formerly of Dagobert, king of the Franks. She was the first abbess of the monastery in the Horrea at Trier, which she herself, with Saint Willibrord the bishop assisting, had founded anew from the foundations; her feast is observed on the vigil of the Lord’s Nativity.
