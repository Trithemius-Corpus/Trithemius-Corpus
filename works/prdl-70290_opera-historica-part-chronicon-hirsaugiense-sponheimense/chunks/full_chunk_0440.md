Therefore Boemundus, rejoicing, obtained from Pope Innocent VI permission to resign the archbishopric of Trier to Cuno, which the pope freely granted. *Cuno becomes archbishop of Trier.* Boemundus, therefore, after resigning the pontificate to Cuno, received for his support the fortress and castle of Saarburg, with its appurtenances and revenues, for as long as he should live, and, suffering from gout for almost five years, survived there.

In the same year also died Wilhelm, archbishop of Cologne, leaving many possessions in gold, silver, and revenues; the archbishopric was vacant ten months. Meanwhile Johannes of Virenburg, dean of the greater church, was elected by the chapter and went to Rome for his confirmation. Pope Urban V not only refused to confirm his election, but even annulled it entirely, and, after consulting the chapter, raised Adolf, brother of the count of Mark, bishop of Munster, to be archbishop of Cologne; he was young in age and by no means suited to such a dignity. He governed the church of Cologne badly for ten months and eighteen days, and afterward, resigning, took a wife, as we shall say in its proper place.

*1363.*

In the thirteenth year of our abbot Philip, on the day of the Holy Innocents, Gerlaeus of Ernberg, bishop of Speyer, died and was buried in the greater church. Lampert of Burnen, abbot of the monastery in Gengenbach of our order, in the diocese of Strasbourg, succeeded him and presided for eighteen years; afterward he was transferred by apostolic authority, through Pope Urban V, to the bishopric of Bamberg. To this bishop of Speyer Emperor Charles IV renewed all the privileges of the kings granted to that church, and solemnly confirmed them under the Golden Bull before the princes. In the same year died Theoderic of Boppard, bishop of Worms, whom Johannes Schatlandt of the Order of Preachers succeeded: doctor of sacred letters, apostolic nuncio, inquisitor of heretical depravity, sometime administrator of the churches of Hildesheim in Saxony, Augsburg in Raetia, Kulm in Prussia, and Constance in Swabia. He presided for almost fourteen years, and had many disputes with the people of Worms.

*1364.*

In the fourteenth year of our abbot Philip, Adolf, archbishop of Cologne, accused before the apostolic see of being a squanderer of ecclesiastical goods, resigned the archbishopric into the hands of the pope, not by force or fear but voluntarily, out of love, as was reported, for a certain girl of the count of Mont, with whom he hoped that he would receive the county of Cleves; and this happened. For after resigning the pontificate, since he did not have holy orders, although he had held the bishopric of Munster for [unclear] years and Cologne for almost one year, he took the aforesaid girl as his wife, and not long afterward obtained the lordship of the land of Cleves, which belonged to her. After Adolf’s resignation Engelbert, count of Mark, bishop of Liege, paternal uncle of the aforesaid Adolf, was postulated as archbishop of Cologne; confirmed by Pope Urban V, he presided for four years. He accomplished little, because he found the church in very great debts and joined new debts to the old.

*1366.*

In the sixteenth year of our abbot Philip, there was a great sedition and discord between the bishop and the citizens of Worms over the institution of the consulate of the Schultheiss, and certain other matters pertaining to jurisdiction. *Sedition of the citizens.* At length, after much contention, it was amicably settled by Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine, who was called Adolf, [unclear] the consulate of Mainz and Speyer, on the day of the Conversion of St. Paul in the aforesaid year. In the same year the king of England brought the king of France, with his son, and likewise the king of the Scots, captured, into England. In these times also a great confederation was made among the cities of the Empire.
