*Johannes Trithemius.*

To Johann Camerarius Dalberg, the most learned bishop of the Vangiones, I wrote a book entitled *On Ecclesiastical Writers*, which was printed at Basel; after the letter it begins thus: “Having often been asked at the urging of many.”

At the request of Jakob Wimpfeling of Schlettstadt, a most distinguished poet, orator, and theologian, I wrote a little work on the illustrious writers of Germany, which I entitled *Catalogue of the Lights of Germany*. This work, printed at Mainz, begins thus: “Since there are some, Jacob.”

I also wrote four books *On the Illustrious Men of Our Order*, which have not yet come to light. The first speaks of the beginning and growth of that order itself in general. The second is a catalogue of the saints canonized from that same order. The third contains the Roman pontiffs chosen from it. And the fourth describes the distinguished men of the order, who magnificently adorned the Catholic Church no less than the order itself by their writings and their own lucubrations. This work begins thus: “As I was thinking, and more often [unclear].”

To brother Albert Latro of the Order of Minors I wrote one book on ecclesiastical computation, which has not yet been brought before the public. It begins: “Johannes Trithemius, abbot,” etc.

At the command of the fathers of my order, concerning our observance, which is called Bursfeld from the place of its origin, I wrote an arduous work *On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Men*, taking the occasion from certain [unclear] in the prologue; the first of these describes the region of cloistered beginners, the second that of those making progress, the third that of the perfect. This work was printed at Mainz; after my letter to the presidents it begins thus: “Since the state and condition of the monastic life.”

At the request also of a certain friend, after the end of the aforesaid work on the three regions of cloistered men, I appended a single epitome on spiritual exercise, which I extracted at the petition of the same friend; it was printed together with the preceding works, and begins thus: “Even if the form of spiritual [unclear].”

After this I undertook to write the great and exceedingly arduous work of the Steganography of wondrous [unclear], to be divided into eight books. I brought the first book and indeed the second through to the end, but left the third unfinished and all the rest abandoned down to this day, buried in spirit, persuaded by many considerations, since the labor would be great and perhaps the reward small. It therefore remains unfinished, imperfect as it lies, until usefulness persuades otherwise. This work begins thus: “The most ancient sages, whom the Greeks call philosophers.”

At the request of Ulrich of Esslingen, theologian and canon of the greater church of Cologne, I wrote a little book, according to the Greek text, on certain doubtful questions in the Gospel and John, which has not been printed. It begins: “By the frequent [unclear] of friends.”

To the same man I wrote another little book on certain doubtful passages in the Psalter according to the copies of the Greeks, of which I made a brief resolution. It begins: “When recently at Cologne.”

Concerning the most holy mother Anne I also arranged a rosary divided into fifty articles, out of my devotion toward her, together with the hourly prayers which we are accustomed to call the course, the office of the mass with the sequence “Let them rejoice on this day,” and another which begins: “Let us praise the holy [unclear] of the king of the heavens.”

At the request of Macharius, abbot of Limburg, I wrote two books on the misery of cloistered prelates; they have not yet been brought to light, but remained at Sponheim. This work begins thus: “As to the misery of cloistered prelates,” etc.

I wrote many orations delivered in chapters of the order outside, more than twenty in number. Some of these were printed at Mainz, while others were dispersed among friends.

I also wrote many rosaries concerning various saints, Peter, Paul, Mary Magdalene, and others; collected proses or sequences; and very many supplicatory prayers, without a fixed number, at the urging of devout men. I did not retain copies of all of these. I also wrote several small works besides, whose titles I cannot now all recall to memory. Some of these, brought into the light, have already appeared long since; others, not yet sufficiently worked out, I left at Sponheim, perhaps to be completed in the future.

On the origin and progress of the dukes of Bavaria I also wrote a history, at the command of the most serene prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine; the opening of this work has slipped from my memory.
