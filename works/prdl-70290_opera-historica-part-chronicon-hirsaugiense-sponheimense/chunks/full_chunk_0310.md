*1128.*

In the year 1128, the fifth year of Abbot Bernhelm's prelacy, after he had most prudently ordered everything that seemed needful for the peace of the brothers, he devoted himself wholly to inward things, and more frequently gave his attention to quiet and to the reading of the Scriptures. Knowing, however, that the requests of many could not easily be disregarded, after taking counsel with his brothers he obtained confraternity with many monasteries round about. And first, indeed, he entered into spiritual association with the brothers of the monastery of St. Disibod, who were nearer neighbors. Its tenor follows, and is as follows. *Bernhelm with many monasteries [unclear].* *Fraternity with the brothers of St. Disibod.*

This is the fraternity of association and fellowship between the brothers of St. Disibod and of Sponheim, a definite declaration and firm confirmation: that, when the brief announcing some deceased brother has arrived on either side, thirty vigils with the same number of Masses are to be performed in the convent; the third, seventh, and [thirtieth?] day is to be solemnly celebrated, namely with three lights kindled before the altar of the Mother, while all the bells ring together. At Masses for the dead, within thirty days, the tract De profundis is to be sung. After Matins the psalm Verba mea is to be recited through the same thirty days, with the other customary things. The commendation after chapter is to be regularly observed for the same time. [Each?] of the priests is to sing ten Masses; the literate are to recite three psalters; those who do not know this are to repeat 450 Our Fathers. The names of deceased brothers are to be written here and there in the Rule; anniversary days with vigil and Mass [unclear]. Brothers arriving on either side go to chapter, and do not leave it except with permission. *[Unclear] suffrages [unclear].*

This grace of fraternal association is granted in all respects to the brothers of St. James and of St. Alban at Mainz, because these two cloisters are known to have sprung from the root of their religious life. *These were the suffrages which were performed here for [them].* The custom shown to the Bearded Brothers is excepted, whose life among them is not [unclear].

[At St. Maximin?] there also; at Seligenstadt; on Mount St. John in the Rheingau; at Hasungen; at Gerode; of the nuns on Mount St. Rupert; of the nuns of St. Peter near Kreuznach; and of many others. As soon as the death of anyone here or in other places in the fraternity had been announced to the confederates, immediately seven offices of the dead were performed for him, with vigils and Masses; besides this, each priest did not delay to say one Mass, those below priestly rank fifty psalms, and the illiterate the Lord's Prayer as many times. There was very great charity among them; this, shame to say, afterwards miserably grew cold and never sought to rise again. *Copy of the letter of foundation.*

*1130.*

In the year 1130, which was the sixth of Abbot Bernhelm, Count Megenhard of Sponheim, our founder, offered in perpetuity to the Church of Mainz the canonical monastery founded in Schabenheim, into the hands of Adalbert the elder, archbishop of Mainz. The same pontiff confirmed this donation, as follows. *Foundation of the monastery of the Regulars in Schabenheim.* *The Regulars in Schabenheim.* *Privilege of the parish church.*

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Adalbert, by the mercy of God archbishop of Mainz and legate of the apostolic see, make known to all the faithful of Christ, future as well as present, how Count Megenhard of Sponheim, for the remedy of his own soul [and] of his wife Mechthild, [unclear] that which by hereditary right had come to the same Mechthild, his wife, from her ancestors, with the churches in the village called Schabenheim, with all tithes, the ninth part of the demesnes, dependents, villages, fields, vineyards, woods, meadows, cultivated and uncultivated lands, also coloni with certain manses, with every right, [unclear] to the brothers, on this condition: that there, from all the aforesaid goods, we should establish brothers living according to the rule of St. Augustine, lest, cooling from the rigor of the rule, they should ever transgress their institution. If a father of the monastery is to be appointed for the brothers, let him be only the one whom, by common counsel and assent, at the dictation of the Spirit, [unclear]; it shall be permitted to them, in the name of the Lord, to assume a person of the same profession and purpose, whomever they find more suitable. The parish priest also is appointed by the brothers with their father from their college, so that, every disturbance being removed, it may persevere in secure liberty in the praises of God.
