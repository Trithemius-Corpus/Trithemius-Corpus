*280 Chronicle of Trithemius*

*[1253?].*

John the abbot, in the first year of his rule, continued for some time the work begun on the church of St. George the martyr in Dalen, which nevertheless he afterwards left unfinished.

In the same year there was a great and powerful wind in these regions, which overthrew many trees and buildings in various places.

*1254.*

Conrad the king, son of Emperor Frederick II, died. After his death his son Conrad, whom the Lombards called Conradin, wishing to succeed to the kingdom of Apulia as the legitimate heir, was repelled by the contrivance of the pope, and Charles, count of Anjou, was introduced by deceit. Hence Conradin, having set out for Apulia with a very great army, in the first encounter Charles, with papal aid, was defeated. After the victory, while our men were intent on plunder and spoils, he, having recovered his strength, rushed upon them, conquered the conquerors, killed many, captured others, and put others to flight. There Conradin was captured with many nobles and was beheaded; in him the line of Frederick II and of the duchy of the Swabians failed. Conradin was a youth most beautiful of all, who, by the counsel of the pope, through envy of the German name and especially of his grandfather Frederick, our emperor, was killed with many counts and nobles.

In the same year Godescalcus, abbot of St. James at Mainz, with the consent of his convent and of Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, conferred upon the chapter and canons of the greater church of Mainz, for the augmentation of their prebends, the parish church in Schornsheim with its tithes, revenues, and all its appurtenances, as is clear in these letters:

To the honorable men and beloved in Christ, the provost, dean, and whole chapter of Mainz, Godescalcus, by the grace of God abbot, and the whole convent of the monastery of St. James at Mainz, offer the gift of prayers and a will ready for services. When we bring to mind with due meditation how liberal, how magnificent, in the time of her abundance, the church of Mainz has been toward us and other churches, which, brought forth from her inward parts, through her have been planted and founded in the great abundance of other goods, we can scarcely think of anything by which we may be able to render to her the return that is owed. Yet so that, for the relief of the poverty of our mother, in which the general disturbance of the land has involved us by occasion of the business of the church, our scant means may offer at least some small thing, and so that the filial fervor of devotion and the effect of due compassion in us may shine more clearly: behold, the parish church in Schornsheim of your diocese, whose collation pertains to us, with the right of patronage and all revenues, we give to you and your successors, by concordant will and liberal spirit, to be possessed forever, for the augmentation of your prebends. Thus, as soon as it shall happen to fall vacant, you may apply it to your common uses. In memory of this deed and for everlasting and perpetual firmness, we give you the present letters, strengthened by the protection of our seals. Done in the year of the Lord 1254, on the day of blessed Barnabas the apostle. Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, confirmed this donation by letters in the same year, on the vigil of St. James the Apostle.

In like manner also, in the same year, Otto, abbot, and the convent of St. Disibod, of the order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, conferred the church in Osternach upon the canons and chapter of the greater church of Mainz; and he gave letters concerning this matter of the following kind:

To the reverend and beloved in Christ, the provost, dean, and whole chapter of Mainz, Otto, abbot, and the convent of St. Disibod, of the order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, with the affection of filial devotion, offer a will ready for services. Since the distinguished beauty of the honor of your church, our mother, shines forth famously, and the wondrous splendor of the work of excellent dignity [unclear], and we, considering in some measure the poverty of the ministers, as we are bound by the compassion of filial devotion, have resolved to redeem the deformity of this sort of defect, and at least to redeem the ministry of one minister set before your choir: behold, the parish church in Osternach, of your diocese, whose collation pertains to us, with the right of patronage and all its appurtenances which the pastor has hitherto been accustomed to receive, saving the other things which our church has and has had in that same village, with unanimous consent we freely so give and [unclear], so that when it shall first happen to fall vacant, all the revenues of it which, beyond the suitable prebend of the perpetual vicar of that church to be instituted henceforth by you, shall be able to remain, may be wholly converted to the prebend of a perpetual vicar in your church, who is to serve in the name of our church. Thus I, Otto, and my successors, the abbots, may always freely confer that vicarage upon an honorable person and a priest useful to the choir, who, like the other vicars of your church, shall be bound to due service in the choir and obedience to the dean. In memory of this deed and for perpetual firmness, we have caused the present letters to be strengthened by our seals. Done in the year of the Lord 1254. Gerard, archbishop of Mainz, confirmed this donation in the same year, on the Kalends of January, by his letters, in the third year of his pontificate, which he delivered to the chapter of Mainz.
