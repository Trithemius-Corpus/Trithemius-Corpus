*SPONHEIM. 297.*

…[before] the aids of [his] friends had come, too inconsiderately and precipitately, unequal in strength, [Adolf] advanced to battle against Albert, between the village which is called Göllheim and the nuns' monastery named Rosenthal; there it was once sharply fought, and many fell on either side. King Adolf fought most bravely with his own hand, and, when victory now seemed to be approaching him, his [men] began to flee, and he himself was killed in the same place, and his son captured. There fell also in the same battle lord Otto of Oxenstein, the first captain in duke Albert's army; likewise the count of Eisenberg, supreme standard-bearer in king Adolf's army, and a most brave soldier, was killed. Counts, soldiers, and nobles also, more than sixty notable [men], fell in the same place. But the body of the slain king Adolf was buried in the aforesaid monastery of Rosenthal, until the times of Henry, king of Luxembourg, who ordered his bones to be entombed in the tombs of the kings in the church of Speyer. There are [those] who write that all those who conspired against king Adolf perished by an evil death. For they report that Albert himself, the usurper of the kingdom, was slain by his brother's son; the count of Holloch killed; Otto of Oxenstein (as we said) fell in war; the archbishop of Mainz, sitting on his chair, died unexpectedly; the count of Zweibrücken drowned in the water or bank called Blies; Conrad, bishop of Strasbourg, before Freiburg, slain by a sword by a certain peasant; the count of Leiningen made insane and frenzied; and almost all, by a similar kind of death, emigrated from this world. Albert, therefore, escaping victor, with his princes sets out for the city of Aachen, and there is consecrated king of the Romans by Wichbold, archbishop of Cologne. He reigned 10 years. He had as wife Elizabeth, daughter of Meinhard, count of Carinthia, who bore him 5 daughters and 6 sons; of whom these are the names. The first-born was Rudolf, king of Bohemia, who died in the year of the Lord [MCCCCLXX, as printed—recte c. 1307], buried at Prague. The second was Frederick, duke of Austria, who afterward was elected against Louis the Bavarian, the emperor; of whom it will be said in [its] place. The third was Leopold, duke of Austria, Styria, and Carinthia, who died in the year of the Lord [MCCXXXVI, as printed—recte 1326], buried in the monastery of Königsfeld. The fourth was Henry the duke, who died in the year of the Lord [MCCCCLIIII, as printed], leaving [his] wife Elizabeth, daughter of the count of Virneburg. The fifth was Albert the duke, who founded a monastery at Gaming, from whom the line of Maximilian, king of the Romans, descends. The sixth was Otto, duke of Austria, who had as wife a daughter of the duke of Bavaria, by name Elizabeth, and died in the year of the Lord 1344. But of the daughters, the first, by name Agnes, married Andrew, king of Hungary; he being dead without children, she again married the duke of Breslau. The second was Elizabeth, who married Frederick, duke of Lorraine, and bore him Rudolf, who [succeeded his] father. The third was Anna, who married Hermann of Brandenburg. The fourth was Catherine, betrothed to Henry of Luxembourg, the seventh king of the Romans; he being dead before the marriage, she was given to Rupert, king of Apulia, duke of Calabria, and died in the year of the Lord 1434 [as printed]. The fifth daughter, by name Jutta, married Udalric, count of Oettingen, and died in the year of the Lord 1424 [as printed].

In these times the citizens of Cologne, on account of suits and contentions which they had had with Sifrid, archbishop of Cologne, were under excommunication for 7 years, as many months, and nine days, and interdicted, so that through all that time the celebration of the divine [office] among them was suspended. Sifrid the archbishop being dead, Wichbold did not restrain the presumed license of the monks departing far from the integrity of regular observance, but permitted the division of revenues with pernicious private property, as he found [it].
