In this same year, the seventh of Bruno, Burchard, abbot of St. James at Mainz, died, in the sixth year after he had undertaken the governance of the abbey of Mount St. Disibod the Confessor by command of Ruthard, archbishop of Mainz, on the third day before the Nones of March. Adelhunus succeeded him in the monastery of St. Disibod, a man vigorous in every work and distinguished for uprightness of conduct; he presided for fifteen years.

In this same year St. Bernard, about twenty-two years old, entered Citeaux with more than thirty companions under Abbot Stephen, who had succeeded Alberic.

The whole Church of the faithful solemnly celebrates the praises of this most holy man Bernard. His life, miracles, and most celebrated deeds are contained in five little books, written in sufficiently lucid style by William, abbot of St. Thierry. Later, being ordained the first abbot of Clairvaux, he illumined the whole world by sanctity and learning. Besides the 140 monasteries of his order built by him, indeed at others’ expense but for the greater part through his own labor, direction, and care, he also by his preaching converted innumerable souls to Christ. Moreover he wrote many outstanding little works of his most excellent talent, the titles of which we have for the most part noted in the book On Ecclesiastical Writers.

*1114.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Bruno, which was the year 1114 from the Lord’s nativity, Emperor Henry moved a great army against the people of Cologne. Around these times the monastery of Laach of our order, in the territory of the diocese of Trier, was founded in honor of Blessed Mary ever Virgin and Mother of God, and of St. Nicholas the bishop, not far from Andernach. For Henry, count palatine of the Rhine and lord of Laach, with the consent of his wife Adelheid, began the foundation of that monastery in the aforesaid place of his paternal inheritance in the year of the Lord 1093, in the first indiction. Prevented by death, he entrusted its completion to his stepson Siphid, heir of the same palatine county and of all his goods. When he had neglected this for some time, as being a young man, at last, led by repentance, he completed it, namely in the year of the Lord 1112, in the fifth indiction, and magnificently endowed it with estates and revenues for the support of the abbot and monks.

*1115.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Bruno, the emperor, to the injury of Lothar, duke of the Saxons, occupied Brunswick and laid waste Halberstadt. Against him the same duke, with the Saxons, joined to himself Frederick the archbishop and the people of Cologne, also Frederick, count of Westphalia, with his brother Henry, and Hermann of Calvelage, and Henry, duke of Limburg, and with Reinhard, bishop of Halberstadt, hastened to battle in the place called Wulpheholtz, on the day after the virgin Scholastica. On that day battle was joined twice; the emperor with his men turned his back, and the Saxons triumphed gloriously. In that battle, on the emperor’s side, the count of Mansfeld fell with a very great multitude of men, and the emperor himself barely escaped. Departing from there to Mainz, he celebrated Easter there, and, compelled by the princes, restored to the citizens Adelbert, archbishop of that place, who had already been held captive for three years.

*1116.*

In the tenth year of Abbot Bruno, the one thousand one hundred sixteenth from the Lord’s nativity, on the Nativity of the Lord, many bishops, abbots, and princes assembled at Cologne, by the counsel and authority of Theodoric, cardinal and legate of the apostolic see, whose reputation was now celebrated throughout the kingdom, to deliberate on the affairs of the Church. But the same cardinal died on the journey, and his body was brought to Cologne to be buried.

In this year there was great irregularity of the air.

*1117.*

In the eleventh year of Abbot Bruno, which was the one thousand one hundred seventeenth from the Lord’s nativity, the monastery in Breitenau of our order, in the diocese of Mainz, in Hesse, began to be founded by Count Werner, a most excellent man. After he completed it, he magnificently endowed it in the third year thereafter; and he obtained an abbot and brethren, as we shall say, from this monastery of Hirsau.
