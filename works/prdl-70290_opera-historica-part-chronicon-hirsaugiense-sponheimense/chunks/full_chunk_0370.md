Yet there is to be left from the revenues of the same church a definite and suitable portion, from which the person who is to serve God perpetually in that church of Gensingen, and who is to be appointed by you, may be able to be honorably supported. In easy remembrance and perpetual confirmation of this, we give you the present letters, strengthened by the protection of our seal and that of our convent. Done in the year of the Lord 1265, on the seventh ides of July.

Confirming this donation, Archbishop Werner delivered the following letters.

Werner, by the grace of God archbishop of the holy see of Mainz, archchancellor through Germany of the Holy Roman Empire, to his beloved in Christ, the provost, dean, and the whole chapter of Mainz, greeting in the Lord. It is fitting that we meet the just desires of petitioners with gracious consent, diligently pursue the same, and, so far as ability suffices, commit them to effect. Therefore, inclined by your prayers, we, holding as very welcome and valid the donation of the church in Gensingen which, for the increase of your prebends, the beloved in Christ, the abbot and convent of the monastery in Sponheim of our diocese, freely and kindly made to you, as is contained in their writing drawn up concerning it, confirm it by the testimony of the present writing. Let no man, therefore, be permitted to infringe this page of our confirmation, or with rash daring to oppose it. But if anyone shall presume to attempt this, let him know that he has incurred the indignation of almighty God and of blessed Martin, and the sentence of our excommunication. Given at Mainz in the year of the Lord 1265, on the tenth kalends of October.

In recompense for the aforesaid donation, the aforementioned Archbishop Werner, with the consent of his chapter, incorporated the parish church in Sponheim, with all its revenues, into the monastery, as is clear from the letters that follow.

Werner, by the grace of God archbishop of the holy see of Mainz, archchancellor through Germany of the Holy Roman Empire, to his beloved in Christ, Peter, elected abbot of the whole monastery in Sponheim of our diocese, greeting in the Lord. We, considering with fatherly affection that your church, on account of the manifold burdens of exactions and through various unfortunate events, has been so diminished in many things that you labor under no small poverty, in order that, so far as we can, we may somewhat relieve your need for our salvation, grant to you the parish church in Sponheim with its attached chapels, to be held by you in full right forever, [unclear] when it shall happen to become vacant, freely and perpetually applying its revenues to your common uses, provided that you present to the archdeacon of the place a faithful and perpetual vicar for that church, to whom you shall assign from the revenues of that church a definite and suitable portion from which he may be able to be honorably supported; and we also freely impart our consent, and that of our archdeacon, to you concerning a donation of this kind. Let no man at all, therefore, be permitted to infringe this page of our donation, or with rash daring to oppose it. But if anyone shall presume to attempt this, let him know that he has incurred the indignation of almighty God and of Saint Martin.

For the greater confirmation and evidence of this, we have caused this present writing of ours to be strengthened for you by the protection of our own seal, that of the provost, dean, and chapter, and also of you, the elected abbot. Done in the year of the Lord 1265, on the seventh ides of July.

Thus, in order that the church of the village of Sponheim might be incorporated into the monastery, Abbot Peter gave the church in Gensingen with all its appurtenances to the church of Mainz.

In the same year the citizens of Würzburg, because of the many and various injuries which they had inflicted upon Iring, their bishop, and upon the clergy, were condemned to pay him two thousand two hundred marks, and, after the silver had been paid, were compelled to return to his obedience.

*1266.*

In this year the provost, dean, and chapter of the major [church] bought from Philip the elder of Falkenstein and from his two sons, Philip and Werner, [unclear] which they had held by feudal right from the church of Mainz, with Archbishop Werner consenting, as is clear from the letters of purchase drawn up concerning it, whose date is in the year of the Lord 1261, on the third ides of February.

In the same year Iring, bishop of Würzburg, died; Berthold of Sternberg succeeded him and presided for eighteen years.
