*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius*

After the act had been done for Adalbert and confirmed by the necessary fortification, the holy father, together with his brothers, whose number was then still small, began earnestly to take action with the greatest care, diligence, and zeal, by admonishing, promoting, and cooperating, so that the work begun might be completed as quickly as possible. And when they applied themselves most diligently to the work begun, Count Adalbert and his most devout wife providing the expenses, and no less also the faithful round about, noble and low-born, contributing by the largess of their alms, the whole building was completed in a short time.

*1071*

For in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1071, in the ninth indiction, the structure of the church, begun almost ten years before, was completed with God’s help, together with the remaining dwellings of the monks begun at the same time. Therefore in that same year, which was the second year of Abbot William, on the day before the Nones of September, the same church was dedicated by Henry, bishop of Speyer, the ordinary in whose parish the monastery was built, at the request of Count Adalbert and his most devout wife Wiltrudis, and of Abbot William.

When these things had been done, since William, a lover of truth, believed that the monastery had been handed over to complete freedom according to the count’s promise, and suspected no fraud, he rejoiced in the Lord and carried out his purpose according to the rule of our most holy father Benedict. But after a few years had passed, the most devout handmaid of Christ, Countess Wiltrudis, hearing from her husband how deceitfully and fraudulently he had circumvented the holy man in the imperial letters granted for the freedom of the monastery, took care to reveal this very secretly to Abbot William, to see whether he could in some way restrain the deceiver’s frauds, admonishing him to obtain new letters concerning the freedom of the monastery after the earlier ones had been cancelled, and saying that she would diligently see to it that, with her husband’s consent, none of the sons should set himself against it.
