*1280.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Volandus, in 1280 of the Lord’s Nativity, Albertus Magnus of the Order of Preachers died, formerly bishop of [unclear] in Bavaria, in the eighty-seventh year of his age. He was buried at Cologne in the middle of the choir of his convent, not without a reputation for sanctity.

In the same year Pope Nicholas III, in the third year of his pontificate, confirmed all the privileges, liberties, and goods of this monastery of Hirsau, at the request of the abbot and convent, giving them a bull under lead at Avignon, as is customary, on the eighth day before the Kalends of December. He also gave the same persons another bull confirming their privileges at Rome at St. Peter’s, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of January, in the first year of his pontificate.

*1281.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Volandus, in 1281 of the Lord’s Nativity, Rudolph, king of the Romans, after matters in Austria and Bohemia had been carried out prosperously, and after he had given his daughter in marriage to Wenceslaus, son of Otakar, king of Bohemia, whom, as we said above, he deprived of life and kingdom, surrounded [unclear] with a siege and took it. He also defeated the count himself, who came against him for battle with a very great army, and compelled him to surrender. Returning from Burgundy, he captured by assault Colmar, Zurich, [Bern?], and Hagenau, cities joined in rebellion; the leaders of the rebels were partly slain and partly outlawed, and the land was silent before him in peace.

In the same year Pope Nicholas III died at Rome, at St. Peter’s, and was buried with honor; and the Apostolic See was vacant for five months because of the discord of the cardinals. When these months had passed, at last they elected Simon, cardinal priest of St. Cecilia, a Gaul by nation, born in the city of Tours, who was crowned on the tenth day before the Kalends of April and called Martin IV. He presided for four years and one month, a man of most holy conduct, who is said to have been renowned for miracles.

*1282.*

In the following year, which was 1282 of the Lord’s Nativity, since Abbot Volandus, by his bad rule, had inflicted very grave losses on this distinguished monastery, both in spiritual and temporal matters, the brothers, no longer able to bear his insolence and excessive prodigality, using the counsels of friends, finally induced him to resign the abbacy, which he had not been able usefully to govern and rule. Therefore he resigned the abbacy into the hands of the brothers on the twelfth day before the Kalends of February, in the year of the Lord 1282, in the tenth indiction, after he had ruled it badly for six years, eight months, and fourteen days, not without damage to the monastery.

*Concerning Lord Crafto, the twenty-eighth abbot of this monastery, who presided for eleven years, and concerning various deeds of that time in Germany.*

[unclear]

Crafto became abbot [29?]. He presided for thirty-one years.

In the aforesaid year, King Rudolph violently captured Richenstein and Schonek, very strongly fortified castles of robbers, and utterly destroyed them.

Also in the same year, on the second weekday after the feast of the Lord’s Resurrection, the citizens of Mainz, after making an uprising against the Jews, killed many and drove the rest out of the city, stripped of all their goods; and soon afterward a general persecution of the Jews followed throughout all Germany.
