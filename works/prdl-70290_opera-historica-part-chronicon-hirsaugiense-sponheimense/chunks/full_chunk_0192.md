*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

... who has sinned, if he does not have the one who anoints him, straightway flees into despair and withers away, because no medicine is administered to him according as he himself can bear it. Now, dear sons, hear the voice of the living light: Take hold of mercy, which has arisen not from us, but which comes from God. And therefore do not withdraw it from those to whom it must be shown. Therefore draw them back to the health of their souls.

After the brothers of Hirsau had received these letters from Saint Hildegard, handmaid of Christ, they rejoiced in a wonderful manner; and, changing their plan, they began to treat among themselves concerning peace and the tranquillity of concord. But Abbot Manegold too, according to the counsel of the lady virgin Hildegard, forestalling them in a spirit of meekness and gentleness, showed himself in all things, in word and deed, a most loving father. The brothers likewise, through the sweetness of his fatherly promise, promised that they would show themselves humble and obedient sons in all things, and would never thereafter provide an occasion for discord. Thus, therefore, by the merits, prayers, and counsels of the blessed Hildegard, virgin of Christ, concord between the abbot and the convent was restored with the Lord's help; and thereafter, while Abbot Manegold himself lived, both sides firmly preserved it inviolable among themselves.

*1162.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Manegold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1162, the emperor Frederick, while he was in Lombardy together with the leading men of the church of Mainz, passed over Christian the prior, or rather despised him, and set up as archbishop of the aforesaid church Conrad, brother of Otto, Count Palatine of Wittelsbach; and he presided for two years.

In the same year also, when the Milanese, forgetful of their pledged faith and oath, refused obedience to the emperor, the city of Milan itself was again besieged by Caesar and was shortly captured. The people were expelled, the wall all around was stripped bare, and all the buildings, except the churches of the saints, were destroyed down to the ground, so that, at the emperor's command, a plow was drawn through the middle of the city in the form of a cross. Reinhold, archbishop of Cologne, taking away from Milan, with the emperor's consent, the bodies of the holy Magi who had adored Christ in the cradle, transferred them to Cologne and placed them in the greater church of Saint Peter, where [unclear] they promised to do [unclear]. On Tuesday the men of Brescia swore similar things.

At the same time Louis, king of France, sent envoys to the emperor for arranging peace and unity of the holy Church, and he received them graciously. Therefore, when a general synod had been proclaimed in the town called Leona, on the river Saone, in the bishopric of Besancon, the emperor was present with Pope Victor and many archbishops and abbots on the Beheading of Saint John the Baptist. The king of France, however, had arrived the day before on the other side of the river, but by the counsel of Pope Alexander and those obedient to him, he remained in a nearby castle and did not come to the emperor. Waldemar, king of the Danes, was present at this synod, and there he received the crown of the kingdom of Denmark from the hands of the lord emperor. Therefore the emperor and the aforesaid king of the Danes, with that assembly of bishops, abbots, and princes, just as in the first and second synod, named Victor universal pope, but designated Alexander with his adherents as schismatics.

*1163.*

In the sixth year of Abbot Manegold, which was the year of the Lord's Nativity 1163, the emperor celebrated Easter at Worms, and in the octave held a solemn court at Mainz. But almost all the citizens, except for a few of the lower sort, fled from the city so that they would not be found by the emperor, on account of the murder of Archbishop Arnold, committed in the monastery of Saint James, concerning which I have spoken above. [Unclear], having been brought to court, received a capital sentence. Godefrid, abbot of Saint James, was presented to the emperor so that he might clear himself of the charges brought against him, and received a delay. But when he could not clear himself of the killing of that archbishop, by decree of the emperor and the princes he was expelled from the abbacy and was compelled also to abjure his homeland and the province of the church of Mainz. But the monks of the same monastery, all shut up in one house by order of the emperor, who had previously been obedientiaries, seeing that they had been placed in an evil situation, sought flight, some through the windows, others by whatever device they could. The rest, the older men, the weak, and the sick, who were unable to flee, were permitted to depart.
