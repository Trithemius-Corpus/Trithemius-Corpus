In the same year also, since the agreement made between the margrave of Brandenburg and Louis, duke of Bavaria, as we said above, did not please Frederick, count palatine, it was broken off again, and war was renewed between them. For that count palatine himself and Louis, duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Rich, stood stoutly the whole time against thirteen princes, both spiritual and secular. Among them were especially Diether, archbishop of Mainz; Louis also, the Black Duke, count of Veldenz; Ulrich, count of Wurttemberg; Charles, margrave of Baden; Albert, margrave of Brandenburg; William, duke of Saxony; Emich, count of Leiningen; with many other princes their confederates; Louis, duke of Bavaria aforesaid, against the margrave of Brandenburg, the duke of Saxony, and the cities of the Empire.

This victory of the Palatine took place in the year noted above, on the day of St. Ulric the Confessor, which is the fourth day before the Nones of July; in that year it was Friday.

In the year noted above also, but before Frederick the Palatine led out his army to Pedersheim, Diether, archbishop of Mainz, Louis the Black Duke, and Emich, count of Leiningen, with their adherents, having gathered a great army, entered Ingelheim and burned it. But when the peasants fled into the imperial hall and stoutly defended the walls, the adversaries accomplished nothing and withdrew. Meanwhile the Palatine prince was announced as about to come; when they heard this, they abandoned the siege and withdrew. But when the Palatine came with his army, he destroyed near Mainz certain houses of priests at St. Victor, St. Alban, and Holy Cross in the fields; he extorted four thousand florins from those same priests, in order to make them secure from burning, which in the vernacular is called Brandschatz.

But once an agreement had been made with Diether, archbishop of Mainz, as we have set forth, Frederick, the Palatine prince, led out his army against Louis, surnamed the Black, and by besieging him very strongly in Meisenheim, compelled him to petition for peace. And so an agreement was made a second time between them with this settlement: Duke Louis would remit to the Palatine in perpetuity one half of the five hundred florins accruing to him annually in the toll of Bacharach; the remaining part he would receive from him and his successors as a fief, which can never be renounced; he would also resign the castle of Scharpffenburg. But from this agreement the counts of Leiningen and the other adversaries of the Palatine were excluded. After this the magnanimous prince Frederick led an army against the counts of Leiningen. First he encircled with a siege the very strong castle of Hasloch; when it was taken, he burned it, destroyed it, and razed it to the ground. There he freed many of his own men who were captives, and, finding abundant booty of various furnishings, carried it away. Then, moving the army, he besieged another little castle of the counts of Leiningen, Minfelt, stormed it, destroyed it utterly, and carried off the booty.
