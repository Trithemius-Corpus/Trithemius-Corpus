The holy pope, having summoned workmen, began to dig the earth in various parts of the church and to search diligently for the hidden treasure; but after the craftsmen had labored long with fruitless effort and had found absolutely nothing, all who were present began to despair of finding the saint’s body.

Then, while the holy pontiff was prostrate in prayer, and while he himself urged the craftsmen not to despair, one of them, more skilled than the others in his craft, who had recently come with his sons from the dominion of the Venetians, and who bestowed much good upon this place by his labors, searched each place industriously. At length, from the reverberation of the hammers, he perceived that the earth beneath was hollow. On his advice the craftsmen began to dig in that place, and at last, not without great difficulty, when the soil had been removed, they found a subterranean chamber, formed quite beautifully, in which a sarcophagus appeared, very ornately arranged on the outside.

When the most holy pope saw it, filled with great joy, he ordered that all [unclear]. When the prayer had been completed, Saint Leo came nearer and ordered the tomb to be opened. When it had been opened, the most sacred bones of the most sweet confessor of Christ, wrapped in silken cloths and beautifully arranged in due order in the form of a body, [were found], and at his head a lead tablet containing these words: “The body of Saint Aurelius, bishop, deposited in the time of King Arnulf, under Abbot Herderad, on the eighth day before the Kalends of September.” When the holy pope had read this inscription before all, he groaned and said: “Woe to those who have despoiled this place, sanctified by the presence and merits of so great a prelate.” The sarcophagus was closed, lest the relics of the saint be taken away in any manner, and he returned with his people to the count’s castle.

On the next day, when the holy Pope Leo saw that the time was suitable for fulfilling his intention, he summoned the often-mentioned count Adalbert and Wiltrud, his wife, a most devout woman, in the presence of the holy cardinals, all the rest being excluded, and said to his nephew: “Yesterday, dear nephew, we saw the sacred relics of this confessor of Christ despoiled of due honor, and that place itself, for love of the heavenly fatherland, duly dedicated of old by your ancestors to almighty God and to sacred monastic religion, and for many years inhabited by the servants of Saint Peter, which your forefathers and grandfather, led by the worst and cruel avarice, caused to be despoiled of the abbot’s possessions and goods. This is more wicked than every crime: after the monks had been expelled, the workshop of holy virtues was turned into a den of all vices by most dissolute secular clerics.

“You also, adhering to the depraved footsteps of your ancestors, as we have learned by proved testimony, not only possess without fear of God those things which they, polluted by sacrilege, most unjustly took from the monastery of the servants of God, but you also, utterly forgetful of salvation and penitence, have seized everything which they, by sparing it, had left untouched. By provoking God and Saint Aurelius, indeed all the citizens of the heavenly fatherland, you have made them your adversaries. Nor should it be regarded as a small sin when it is committed against the saints of God, since even among men themselves the unjust usurpation of property belonging to another’s right is judged a very grave crime. Behold, you, together with your ancestors, have violated the covenant and corrupted [unclear], with [unclear] added, the holy testament: the things which they had offered to almighty God with simple and holy devotion for the remedy of souls.

“You, inflamed by impious cruelty and cruel avarice, did not fear to despoil God and his saints. Having been made an enemy and adversary not only of God and his saints, but of yourself and your own race, [unclear] the cruel bishop, declining as an enemy, will destroy your very offspring from your seed, the generation failing, so long as it is polluted by a sacrilegious inheritance, even unknowingly, or, failing within a few generations, [unclear]. But what hope of obtaining eternal happiness will there be for you, O my son, when through such a manifest crime of sacrilege you have made almighty God, with all his saints, hostile to you by your injuries, and your enemy and adversary? It is dangerous for you, and utterly damnable, believe us, to have God opposed to you; for although he may seem to be patient and to endure injuries done to him in this present time, yet, since he is an avenger and a just judge, he will without doubt avenge this patience after death with eternal punishments.

“But we, to whom the care of your salvation and that of your people belongs, not only by reason of the height of pastoral office, whereby, though unworthy, we are bound by office to the salvation of all, but also by kinship of blood, by the authority of almighty God and of his blessed apostles Peter and Paul, whose place we hold on earth, warn and require you; and under threat of eternal damnation, and by the strict judgment of God, we adjure you; and, under anathema, strictly commanding, we order you: that, the clerics being expelled from the aforesaid place, from which by apostolic authority [unclear] every benefice of residence in this same [place], you establish there, according to the rule of the divine father Benedict, monks of this our order, as they are remembered to have been there from the beginning; and that you restore to them, without delay, each and all the things taken away by your ancestors, by you, or by any others whatsoever.
