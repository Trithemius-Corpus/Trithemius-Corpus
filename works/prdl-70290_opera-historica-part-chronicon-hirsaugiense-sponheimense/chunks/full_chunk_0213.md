At the same time the Lotharingians, whom Otto had summoned to his aid near Andernach, being unable to withstand King Philip and fearing for their own skins, secretly favored his side, perpetrating many unheard-of crimes in that region. It will be enough to have set down one of these, from which the other unspeakable things they committed may be weighed. They stripped a certain nun of all her clothing, smeared her with honey, rolled her in bed-feathers, and, thus made monstrously shaggy, put her on a horse, with her face turned toward the horse’s tail. And when they had led this ridiculous, indeed pitiable, spectacle around for several days, the matter became known to King Philip, who, inflamed with zeal for God, ordered all those guilty of this crime to be handed over to boiling water. The town of Andernach also was burned by the army of the same King Philip. Afterwards he returned to his own territories.

Meanwhile King Otto entered Saxony with his army and began to attack Goslar, together with the other neighboring towns. In the year of the Nativity 1200, Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, returning from Hungary, whither in the preceding year he had gone to restore peace between two brothers contending over the kingdom, after concord had been made in the parts of Austria, in the territory of Passau, released from the flesh, passed over to the Lord for his merits. Wolfher, bishop of Passau, with solemn obsequies brought his body back to Mainz, where it was buried with great honor.
