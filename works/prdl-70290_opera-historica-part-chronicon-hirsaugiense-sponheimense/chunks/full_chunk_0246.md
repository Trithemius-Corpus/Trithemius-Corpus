*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

[unclear], the sixth abbot, whom Johannes de Schonberg, cantor, succeeded; he presided for 12 years, 4 months, and 21 days.

In the ninth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1253, there was a great and dreadful wind, which in various places overturned many buildings, tore up several very strong trees from the earth by the roots, and with infinite losses crushed many people.

In the same year Manfred, son of the late Emperor Frederick by a concubine, after Conrad had, as they say, been killed by poison in the preceding year, occupied the kingdom of Sicily, and held it for a few years while he lived.

*MCCLIII.*

In the following year, which was the tenth of Abbot Volpold, and of the Lord’s Nativity 1254, in the twelfth indiction, Pope Innocent IV died, and the Apostolic See was vacant for two years because of discord among the brethren.

In the same year, in the month of February, the citizens of Worms and Mainz, who for a long time had stood in mutual dissension, established perpetual peace, concord, and confederation among themselves. In these times also Count Emicho of Leiningen, brother of the bishop of Speyer, bearing a hostile spirit against Richard, bishop of Worms, strove with every effort to capture him; but Richard, defended by the aid of his own citizens and admitted into Worms, although before this he had been in discord with the citizens, escaped his threats. In those times there was great tumult in the kingdom, and various dissensions, because not all [unclear] of King William of the Romans.

In the year noted above, when the cities of these parts that sided with King William, such as Mainz, Worms, Frankfurt, Boppard, Wesel, Bingen, Oppenheim, Gelnhausen, Friedberg, Fritzlar, and certain others, while he was in the lower regions, had no defenders against their adversaries, after counsel was held at Mainz on the day of St Margaret, virgin and martyr, they joined themselves together in a very firm mutual confederation, to defend everything against all persons whatsoever, laying aside the tolls which they had for the common peace.

*MCCLV.*

In the eleventh year of Abbot Volpold, and of the Lord’s Nativity 1255, King William held an assembly of certain princes at Worms, where envoys and legates sent from many cities were present. But on a certain day, when the queen his wife was proceeding from Worms to the castle of Trifels, which now belongs to Duke Alexander, not far from Landau, with an honorable company, she was captured by Hermann, knight of the castle of Rieberg, together with the count of Waldeck and certain others, and was brought to the aforesaid castle of Rieberg. He kept her captive for some time; afterwards, stripped of all her jewels and possessions, he allowed her to go, to no small disgrace of the king and of the whole country.

Afterwards in the same year, since the Apostolic See had been vacant for almost two years, not without danger to many souls, Alexander IV was at last elected pope by the cardinals, a Campanian by nation; he sat for six years and as many months, and canonized St Clare of the Order of Minors.

*MCCLVI.*

In the twelfth year of Abbot Volpold, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1256, King William resolved to set out for Rome for the imperial crown, when suddenly the most restless people of the Frisians, stirred up by a tumult, began to rebel, and, having entered the borders of Holland, to devastate everything with plundering and fire. But the king, seeing that their ferocity had to be subdued by the sword and no otherwise, gathered a huge army of his men and advanced against them at the end of this year, that is, on the sixth day before the Kalends of January; and, showing them many kinds of hardship, he overcame them with a strong hand, building the castle of Corenburg against their assault. After this, intending to advance farther, when the harshness of winter had drawn ice over the waters and marshes, he went ahead of the army on horseback, alone, to investigate all the hidden places of the enemy, as he thought. But behold, when the ice broke, his horse sank and stuck fast in a marshy ford, unable to go forward anywhere. Seeing this, the Frisians, who were hiding in the reeds, ran together and killed him, not knowing that he was the king, in the seventh year of his reign, the twenty-first of his countship of Holland, and the twenty-seventh of his age, and buried him in a certain house. But afterwards his body was transferred to Middelburg and honorably entombed in a certain monastery. After his death his son Florentius, a boy of six months, received the countship of Holland, and [unclear] it for almost forty years.
