*928.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Diethmar, Emperor Henry, fighting against the Hungarians, Danes, and Bohemians, came away victorious, killing almost twenty thousand of them and leading away nine hundred.

In these days Diethard, abbot of the monastery of Hersfeld, was made bishop of Hildesheim in Saxony, ninth in order; he presided over the aforesaid church for twenty-two years, and in his place Burchard was made abbot.

Around these times Ratherius flourished, from a monk of the monastery of Bobbio bishop of Verona: a man of great simplicity, yet renowned for learning both in secular literature and in ecclesiastical literature. Driven from the bishopric of Pavia into exile by Hugh, king of Italy, which [unclear] he composed in a lowly style. He also wrote many other things useful to readers, of which we have made mention elsewhere. He labored much by writing and preaching against the heresy of the Anthropomorphites, which in his time had begun to sprout again in Italy, saying: that God consists of a body and human limbs.

At the same time Hildewin flourished, from abbot of Bobbio bishop of Verona immediately before Ratherius, and afterward appointed archbishop of Milan by Hugh, king of Italy.

*929.*

In the fifth year of Abbot Diethmar, Emperor Henry, by entreaties, threats, and immense gifts, extorted from him the lance of wondrous workmanship, sanctified by the nails of Jesus Christ crucified, which is said to have belonged to the first and great emperor Constantine and which Count Samson had given to Rudolph, king of Burgundy. He left it to posterity as an emblem and protection of the empire. In our own times the Nurembergers profess that they possess this, together with the other insignia of Charlemagne, and we have seen it.

In these times also Emperor Henry built that famous convent of nuns under the rule of Saint Benedict in Saxony, which is called Quedlinburg, in honor of blessed Servatius, once bishop of Tongeren, and appointed as first abbess his daughter Mechthild, a virgin renowned for many virtues, namely the sister of Otto the first emperor and of Bruno, archbishop of Cologne.

*933.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Diethmar, in the city of Genoa a spring of blood flowed forth very abundantly, perhaps portending the imminent ruin of that city, which followed in the same year. For that city, when African unbelievers sailed to it with a large fleet, was captured in the same year, and all the citizens were either slaughtered or led away captive.

In the same year Burchard, abbot of Hersfeld, was made bishop after Diedo in the church of Wurzburg; in the abbacy Megingus succeeded him, a vigorous man and distinguished for honorable morals.

*875.*

In the tenth year of Abbot Diethmar, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 935, a great synod of bishops and abbots was celebrated at the city of Erfurt in Thuringia.

Many marvels preceded the death of Emperor Henry in this year. The sun, in clear weather, belched forth flames in many places, [unclear] where Henry was afterwards buried. The left hand of a certain man, cut off with iron, was restored whole to him after a year while he slept; and, as a sign of the miracle, there remained at the place of joining a fine line of blood-red color.

In the following year King Henry, after subduing the nations all around, when he had determined to set out for Rome, was seized by illness and abandoned the journey. When he saw that he was about to die, he summoned the princes and all the people, designated his eldest son Otto as emperor, distributed estates with treasures to his other sons, and died on the fourth day before the Nones of July, at about the sixtieth year of his age and the eighteenth of his reign.
