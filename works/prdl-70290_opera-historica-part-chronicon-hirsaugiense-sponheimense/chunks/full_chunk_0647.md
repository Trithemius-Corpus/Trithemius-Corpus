*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

*XLIX.*

*To the most reverend father in Christ, Theodoric, bishop of Lubeck, Johannes Trithemius sends greeting.*

Iamblichus, priest of the Academics, brought into my house by the hand of our most serene prince, greeted me in your name. Gladly I admitted the philosopher, who at times intrudes himself upon our studies; when I read him together with Proclus, his fellow philosopher, and look more deeply into him, I often find that I am reading not them but Dionysius the Areopagite. And this practice, after the manner of the ancients, pleases me, since for the present I am content to drink from the little stream until I may be allowed to reach the spring of the purest fountain. I thank your kindness, most learned prelate, for honoring me, though I did not dare ask, with such guests. I shall make use of reading them for a short time, and shall soon arrange that they return to your bookcases. I recently brought with me from Cologne a Life of Apollonius of Tyana printed in a certain handbook; the occupations of my prince do not permit me, on this occasion, to enter into judgment on it. For I fear to give a hasty opinion, especially because Saint Jerome, in the preface to the Sacred Library, seems to have introduced Apollonius not without praise. Each point must first be examined by me as soon as leisure permits; afterward I shall state my own view without prejudice to anyone.
