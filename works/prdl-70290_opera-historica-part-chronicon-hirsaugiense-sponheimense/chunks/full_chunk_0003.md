Most devoted to Your Most Reverend Highness,

Marquard Freher.

*To the Most Reverend and Most Illustrious Prince Johann Adam, of the Most Noble Family of the Bickens, Archbishop-Designate of Mainz, Elector of the Empire.*

Up to this point, having duly been called to great affairs, endowed with the keen valor of Pallas, offspring of the Bicken stock; and, the difficulty and mass of business overcome, raised to an illustrious summit, you touch the stars with your lofty head, O long-lived Adam, appointed to the office of supreme prelate after the faithful [unclear] of the lords of Dalberg fell through great age. All congratulate you on this honor, at the same time on this burden, on this tiara.

For how great is the care of the weary empire, and of the realm already threatening collapse and drawing with it whatever props are applied to walls that are to be well sustained, you know most clearly; and you know the manner in which the commonwealth ought justly to be ruled and administered. With this better Minerva as pilot, when the ship is shaken by the motions of the wave-tossed sea and its oarsman is weary, you as helmsman will steer it and will set the swimming keel in a fixed station amid the slippery whirlwind.

You have Philip Rosebach, a relief to your weary right hand, ready to bring you aid, a pillar of equity, a safeguard of Justice. But you yourself, divine prince, with how many and how great arts are you more elegantly cultivated, and with gifts of talent! Abundant plenty [unclear], and much knowledge of languages adorns the man; elegant historians, critics, and philosophers delight you as you read. Indeed you also examine poetry inwardly, acting, as we know, as a competent censor. Therefore a marvelous love burns freely in you toward the learned company: among whom Themis loves to set, under the auspices of sacred law, the distinguished Freher, now bringing forth many serene monuments of historians into the breezes, some shaken free from ancient decay, some lying almost buried, and some by private labor published for public reading.

You, highest prelate, patron of men of Mercury, will rightly think him both to be cherished and to be embraced before the rest with a singular affection of mind and with triple Grace. His candor deserves this, and his learning, virtue, keenness, and the watchful lamps of his works: through them monarchs, and the famous deeds of Caesars and kings, and the lineages of princes, gain an immortal name, never to perish in any other age.

Paulus Melissus, a Franconian, Count Palatine and golden knight, citizen of Rome.
