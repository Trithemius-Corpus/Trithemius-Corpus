*1153.*

*An Almeinda is established for the poor.*

Abbot Craffto, for the use of all poor persons arriving from anywhere, especially of those who serve the count and the monastery with their conveyances, obtained from his father Count Megenhard the Almeinda which is next to the vegetable garden, which Countess Richart had once bestowed on the monastery at its first foundation, toward the east and on the right side of the road to Mannede, with every right of ownership. He wished it to be confirmed forever with this limitation: namely, that every right and ownership of it should belong forever to the monastery, but that the usufruct should be common both to the monastery and to the household of the villages of Sponheim in Dalen, and to all poor persons arriving with beasts of burden and wishing to remain there as shepherds, unless the count with the abbot should wish to revoke it.

In this year Henry, archbishop of Mainz, by command of Pope Eugenius, was deposed by two cardinals, Gregory and Bernard. Wasted away by disease, he died in the same year on the kalends of September. Arnold, provost of Aachen, chancellor of Emperor Frederick, promoted and assisted by his aid, succeeded him.

*1154.*

In the third year of Craffto, Machthildis, a consecrated virgin, died; she had once been the sister of Abbot Bernhelm and was enclosed here beside our monastery at the beginning of its foundation, as was said above in its place. She died in her enclosure on the fourth day before the kalends of March. At her passing, angelic harmonies of praise were heard, and certain visions were said to have appeared. She was buried in the choir of the brothers before the steps of the presbytery, not without a reputation for sanctity. She left after her in the same cell five sisters, virgins consecrated and devoted to Christ, whose names are these: Sophia, who had come with her from Saint Alban, and who died in the same year on the day before the kalends of May; Gerlindis, Demodis, Luigardis, and Gertrudis, who served the Lord in the holy purpose of virginity until the end of life. In the course of time some others were joined to them to serve the Lord, but the names of few have remained among us. For after about seventy years the sisters failed in the same place; the enclosure was left desolate in the course of time, the buildings were ruined and utterly destroyed. At length, after many years, Abbot Dieterus had the stones carried away, the place cleared, and a vineyard planted there, which had continued there down to the present.

*1155.*

In the fourth year of Abbot Craffto, his father Megenhard, count of Spanheim, founder of this monastery, died on the day before the kalends of March: a man magnificent in every respect, father of the poor. For his soul Craffto caused many good works to be done. Finally, with the whole congregation of this monastery consenting and approving, he decreed that his anniversary day should be observed every year with vigils and masses, on which every priest should read one mass; non-priests should read fifty psalms, and those who did not know them, as many Our Fathers and Hail Marys. On that day twelve poor persons were to be fed in memory of the twelve apostles of Christ. Count Gotfridus, son of Megenhard, having obtained the county of Sponheim after his father’s death, governed it quite vigorously. At the urging of Lord Abbot Craffto, his brother, he [unclear] the castle called Coppenstein, with the forest and adjacent hamlets, to this monastery forever as the portion of a lawful inheritance [unclear]. About 170 years later, under Willicho the first abbot, a certain exchange was made by Count Johann [unclear], his brother Simon of Birgberg, Count Heinrich of Katzenelnbogen, Count Conrad of Sponheim, and others, for the little castle of Coppenstein with its appurtenances. This Count Hermann Palatine gave the church in Lainstein, with the third part of the tithes, of no small usefulness, to the archiepiscopal mensa of Trier, and bestowed many benefactions on the same church. For he was a man magnificent and glorious in every respect.
