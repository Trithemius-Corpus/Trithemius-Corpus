*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

In the same year Albert was born, later surnamed the Great, in a village of Swabia whose name is Laugingen. There was also an eclipse of the sun.

*1193.*

In the following year, which was the sixth of Abbot Henry, Bruno, archbishop of Cologne, resigned his episcopal office into the hands of Emperor Henry VI and became a monk of the Cistercian order in the monastery of Altenberg. His brother, named Adolf of Altena, a canon of the cathedral church, succeeded him in the archbishopric and presided for twelve years.

*1195.*

In the seventh year of Abbot Henry, the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1195, Henry VI, emperor of the Romans, took Sicily with a mighty hand, and Tancred, the usurper of the kingdom, having been captured and his eyes gouged out,

In the same year Conrad, Count Palatine of the Rhine, who was dwelling at Heidelberg, changed the provostship of the monastery of our order near the town of Heidelberg itself, which is called Neuburg, in the diocese of Worms, into a monastery of nuns of the same order of ours, with the consent of Sighard, abbot of Lorsch, to whom that provostship belonged by full right and without intermediary. There Cunegundis, a very devout and religious virgin, was appointed by him as the first abbess.

*1196.*

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1196, when Henry, abbot of this monastery of Hirsau, called to mind the negligences of his past life and the wrongs he had committed, and fearfully pondered the vengeance of divine retribution, inwardly touched with sorrow of heart, he began to discuss with certain friends the resignation of the abbacy. But although all who knew themselves to be his intimates and friends opposed his will with one steadfast resolve, nevertheless they could not turn him away from his decision so as to prevent him from carrying out in deed the thought he had conceived in his mind. For he was moved to resign the abbacy chiefly by two causes: [first, that] he knew he had committed [unclear] in temporal matters; for these, unless the occasion of affairs were cut away, he could by no means [make satisfaction?] as he desired. The other was that, [unclear] with peace and tranquillity, much exhausted in body, he had greatly declined, and could no longer usefully attend to the affairs of the monastery as he ought.

And so, pierced in heart, grieving and repentant for his past negligences, in order to save his soul, in the eighth year of his rule, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1196, indiction 14, in the month of [June?], he freely and voluntarily resigned his abbacy into the hands of his community before them all. After the resignation had been made, he received from the brothers the provostship in Rothe for the support of his life, where, surviving for almost [eleven?] months in sorrow of spirit and continual tears, at last in the following year, on the fourth day before the Nones of June, he rested in the Lord. His body was buried with reverence beside the body of Rupert, formerly abbot, here in the same monastery, at the very entrance of the church.

*[H.] after [a year?] died in Rothe.*

*On Marquard, twenty-first abbot of this monastery, who governed for 8 years, 6 months, and 21 days, and on the events of his time.*

After the resignation, therefore, of Dom Henry, the twentieth abbot of this monastery of Hirsau, which took place in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1196, indiction 14, on the last day of the month of June, that is, on the feast of the Commemoration of Saint Paul the Apostle, on the same day the brothers, invoking the grace of the Holy Spirit and proceeding to the election of a new shepherd, after much deliberation at last unanimously elected as the twenty-first abbot of this monastery Marquard, a conventual monk, a venerable and most prudent man. Confirmed and blessed according to custom, he governed this monastery vigorously for 8 years, 6 months, and 21 days, a man of great deeds and many virtues, who prudently and bravely defended the rights of the monastery against the attacks of tyrants, resisting without fear.

*Under Abbot Marquard [unclear].*
