*Chronicle of Trithemius*

*Copy of the donation.*

In the name of the Lord, Amen. Ulrich the prior, with the whole convent of Limburg, sends greeting in the Savior of all to all who shall see this writing. Lest those things which are done in time should slip away and perish with time, salutary provision has been made for human frailty that all acts be strengthened by the testimony of writing. Therefore we wish it to be made known to all the faithful of the present age and the future that, after mature deliberation had first been held, by unanimous common consent, freely and absolutely, we have handed over to the greater church of Mainz, to be possessed perpetually, the church in Heldenberg, the right of patronage of which is known to belong to us; namely on this condition, that when the pastor who now is there shall resign or die, whomsoever the chapter of Mainz shall choose for the governance of the said church, he shall be presented, with letters of that chapter, to the abbot, or to the prior if there is no abbot; and he, as a sign of recognition of the benefice received from the church of Limburg, shall request from him a [unclear], and shall obtain it without any difficulty; namely so that the same cleric shall be presented with letters of the abbot or prior to the archdeacon, that he may receive from him the care of souls. The same shall be observed in the case of others substituted thereafter. But if either the abbot or the prior, which however be far from the case, should wish on some pretext to refuse, no prejudice shall thereby be generated either to the person substituted or to the chapter of Mainz itself. And so, after a suitable and sufficient stipend has been assigned to that priest, from which he may now be able to answer to the diocesan as well as to the archdeacon of the place concerning their rights, and nevertheless the parish priest himself be suitably maintained, whatever remains from the revenues which the pastors hitherto have been accustomed only to receive, shall go to the improvement of the prebends of the chapter of Mainz, saving in all things the right of the church of Limburg. Furthermore, so that provision might be salutarily made by a firmer bond of charity for the entry of the church of Mainz into the church of Limburg, it has been decreed that, whenever one of our brothers, having entered the way of all flesh, is announced to the church of Mainz, the memory of the deceased shall be performed in vigils and obsequies as for one of themselves. And we likewise, in the customary manner, shall offer prayers and hosts for their deceased brothers. But this must not be passed over: that in all affairs of our church the church of Mainz shall faithfully, as in its own affairs, bring aid and counsel, as often as it can and ought, amicably and kindly. These things were enacted in the year of grace 1231, in the month of October, in the fourth indiction.

Conrad, bishop of the church of Speyer, also together with his chapter gave consent to the aforesaid donation of the abbot of Limburg, at the instance of the provost, the dean, and the whole chapter of Mainz, declaring that it had been made not to the loss of the monastery of Limburg, but rather to its advantage and honor, as appears in the letters made thereupon by him, which we have judged it superfluous to insert into this history of ours. Lord Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, also confirmed by letters that same donation made to his chapter.

*1232.*

Abbot John, in the nineteenth year of his rule, had a grave dissension and dispute with the villagers of the village of Sponheim, on account of the woods, pastures, and common land beside the vegetable garden of Lady Richardis, countess of Sponheim, which she had donated to this monastery at its first foundation. Hence, after many contentions, an agreement was at last made between them by Lord John, count of Sponheim, in this manner: first, the woods of the forest were divided and distinguished by signs set between them, so that the abbot separately and also the villagers separately should henceforth possess their woods in peace, and neither party should henceforth presume in any way to inflict damage on the other in the woods or elsewhere, or to hinder the other. The pastures, however, throughout the whole march must be common both to the abbot and to the villagers, except for certain districts of the abbot in which the villagers are to have no power of pasturing. The common land must be common, and remain common in every form for the monastery, the villagers, those of Dal, and the other poor of the [unclear], just as nearly eighty years before it is known to have been established by Count Megenhard and by his son Craftho, the second abbot of this monastery; nor shall anyone be allowed to sell anything from it, neither from its land, since it belongs to the monastery, nor from the usufruct, which the count, the abbot, and the county granted to the poor villagers.

In this year Conrad, landgrave of the Thuringians, waging unjust war on account of a certain [unclear] against Lord Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, caused him much damage. For he took the town of Fritzlar and almost destroyed it utterly, along with certain others. At last agreement was made between them in this manner, as follows:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. Concerning the destruction of Fritzlar, Witzenhausen, the towns, and the other damages which Lord Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, and Lord Conrad the Younger, landgrave, felt on both sides when they had war between them, such a settlement intervened: the old settlement, as it was ordained by Lord Siegfried, formerly archbishop, and Lord Ludwig, of pious memory, formerly landgrave, shall be kept unshaken in every way forever. Likewise the archbishop granted the town in Wolfheim as a fief to the aforementioned Conrad the landgrave, and grants it to his legitimate heirs, even to daughters, if there are no sons to succeed after his death by hereditary right. And if the same Conrad should ask in his lifetime that he grant the same town to his brother or to his brother’s son, this shall in no way be denied him.
