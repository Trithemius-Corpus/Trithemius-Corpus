In these same times the monastery of Corvey in Saxony, under the rule of our holy father Benedict, took the beginning of its new foundation; it began to be founded in the year of the Lord 822, in the fifteenth indiction, and, having been completed almost at the same time as this monastery of the holy bishop Aurelius, began to be inhabited. At almost the same time the monastery of the martyrs Saints Marcellinus and Peter, in the place called Seligenstadt in the diocese of Mainz, under the rule of our holy father Benedict, was first founded by Emperor Louis, with the cooperation of the venerable man Einhard, formerly chancellor, but then a monk and the first abbot of that same monastery; [unclear], in which the bodies of the aforesaid two martyrs Marcellinus and Peter, brought from Rome in the month of November in the year of the Lord 838 through the emperor’s agency, were honorably laid to rest.

*840. Distinguished men of our order. Foundation of the monastery of Seligenstadt.*

In the times of Lintbert, first abbot of Saint Aurelius of Hirsau, around the year of the Lord 840, under the most pious Emperor Louis and his sons, many very celebrated men flourished in our Benedictine order, distinguished for holiness and learning in the Scriptures; like stars of heaven, they wonderfully illuminated the whole Church by their morals and doctrine. Rabanus Maurus, abbot of Fulda, of whom we spoke above, was held in esteem by everyone in these times because of the integrity of his life and the inestimable discipline of his knowledge. What and how much he wrote, both in our book *On Ecclesiastical Writers* and in the second *On Illustrious Men* [unclear].

*842.*

Finally, in the year of the Lord 842, which was the fourth year of Abbot Lintbert, stirred up by the deceits of certain men, he left the monastery of Fulda and, fleeing to Louis, son of the elder Emperor Louis, remained almost as an exile until the death of Otgar, archbishop of Mainz, who had expelled him for what cause I do not know. But after five years, when [Otgar?] had died, he was appointed in his place in the year of the Lord 847, in the tenth indiction, by the vote of all, and sat for nine years.

Haymo [unclear], third bishop of Halberstadt, who in the year of the Lord 840, in the third indiction, was ordained bishop and presided for thirteen years, was outstanding in all holiness and doctrine. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which we have made mention in our aforesaid books. Einhard also, from imperial chancellor a monk, and from monk the first abbot in Seligenstadt, a learned and most religious man, among other monuments of his learning wrote the deeds of Charles the Great, emperor, in one volume; also a history of his own time, and certain other things, by which he showed himself skilled not only in the Latin language but also in Greek. Frechulf, a monk of Fulda, who wrote a very notable chronicle from the origin of the world down to the incarnation of the Lord, which he entitled *On the Seven Ages of the World*, was bishop of Lisieux in these times.

There also flourished around these times Hetti, from abbot of [Mediolanum?] archbishop of Trier, who founded, at the admonition of Saint Maternus in dreams, a monastery of regular canons at Koblenz, where the Moselle flows into the Rhine; into it he transferred and placed the body of Saint Castor from Karden. But [unclear] of Saint Castor [unclear] the eighth, in which Hirsau was founded, he died, and he was buried at Saint Matthias outside Trier.

Hilduin also, abbot of Saint-Denis of Paris, a man most learned in every respect, wrote the life and passion of Saint Dionysius for Emperor Louis the First. In the year of the Lord 827 he transferred the bones of the holy martyr Sebastian from Rome into Gaul; after many years, his head was placed by its founder in a monastery of our order in the diocese of Trier, which is called Viridisaugia.

At the same time Theodulf, from abbot of the monastery of Fleury bishop of Orléans, was in great esteem among the Gauls, venerable to all the people no less for the holiness of his life than for his learning in the Scriptures. Placed in custody by Emperor Louis, I know not on account of what suspicion of conspiracy with the sons who were then very much at variance with their father, he composed the hymn which we are accustomed to sing on Palm Sunday, at the station.
