*The Monastery of Hirsau*

*Volmar the abbot presided for 35 years and 10 months. The Life of Volmar.*

By providence and ordinance he vigorously presided over this monastery for thirty-five years and ten months. He was born in Swabia of rather free/noble stock; from his earliest age he devoted himself to the study of letters, and became sufficiently learned both in secular letters and in divine Scripture. For, having obtained by nature a good and teachable intelligence, he seemed fit for learning anything at all. Then, having become a monk, he walked blamelessly in the sight of his brothers, a devoted lover and cultivator of the rule of our most holy father Benedict and of the traditions of the fathers.

He was of tall stature; and, so far as concerns the proportion and outward disposition of the body, the outward bearing of his body too seemed to display a certain majesty of soul lying hidden within. Being mature in all his words and deeds, he bore before him a certain severity of spirit, so that he appeared worthy of reverence and fear not only to religious men, but also to secular persons of every sort. He detested all frivolity and looseness, of mind as well as of body; he was always firm, stable, and consistent in his manner of life, and at no time was he ever seen to change toward laxity. For in his morals he was always accustomed to be so composed, so mature, so religious, that in his presence even dissolute and otherwise most frivolous men, as though violently restrained within by some divine power, displayed nothing but propriety and maturity. At times he was cheerful, but never unrestrained; and he was accustomed to moderate even his cheerfulness with his usual gravity, so that, when he laughed with holy Job, no one would believe it laughter.

He was also endowed with great wisdom, and not only in those matters which pertained to the order of the monastic state, but also in all worldly business he was so circumspect and intelligent, and most skilled in both Latin and the native tongue, and so distinguished by such eloquence in each, that in his time he had no equal among the Swabians. Whatever he wished to say, he said so aptly, so ornately, and so elegantly that, persuading men to everything, he seemed to have the minds of his hearers in his power. *Endowed with wisdom.*

Now as soon as he was ordained abbot, he took care with all zeal to show himself a useful shepherd to the brothers; and not only by words in exhorting, which he could do excellently, but also by going before them in morals, which few do, he diligently led the sheep committed to him to the pastures of life. He was a most bitter enemy of vices, and immediately corrected offending subjects, without respect of persons, according to the tenor of the holy rule. Hence at all times he was solicitous with the utmost zeal for monastic discipline, both with respect to himself and to his subjects; he continually went before them by good example, and with pastoral authority he ceaselessly urged them to follow.

But the brothers, seeing in Abbot Volmar such love of regular discipline, zeal, diligence, and irreproachable conduct, cherished him with great love, showing themselves his helpers in all things with the greatest cheerfulness. Therefore, when by the grace of God he was notably increased, almighty God blessed them, and through the offerings of the faithful their temporal substance was increased in many ways. For many princes, nobles, and rich men in the time of this Abbot Volmar bestowed many gifts on this monastery on account of the holy manner of life of the brothers.

Of these, to recount a few: [Udafo-Hildebrandus?], count palatine, bestowed on Saint Peter and Paul in the monastery of Hirsau estates of no small utility; yet Godfrey himself violently drew these to himself and received their produce as long as he lived. When he was now about to die, in the presence of many he resigned them into the hands of Wolfram of Weinsberg, to be restored to the monastery of Hirsau. Then, after a short time had passed, Welf, son-in-law of that Count Godfrey, not only restored what had been taken away, but also added much greater things from his own possessions; all these are contained by name and expressly in the book of donations.
