*Chronicle of Trithemius*

In the aforesaid year, on the Ides of October, the high-hearted prince Frederick, Count Palatine of the Rhine, guardian of Philip, with the aid of Theoderic, Archbishop of Mainz, surrounded with a siege Montfort, a castle that was a common haunt of robbers, which is not far from here; and, after vigorously assaulting it for [unclear] days, he took it on the nineteenth day of the month of October and destroyed it utterly. Thus it remains destroyed down to the present day, nor can it be rebuilt without the consent of the Palatine who shall be in office at the time and of the Archbishop of Mainz. For the church of Mainz suffered many losses from this robber-castle through those who were able to have their dwelling there.

Also in the aforesaid year Mahomet, emperor of the Turks, having dispatched a great army of his men, encircled with a very strong siege Nicopolis, a notable city of the Christians, which they call Schiltaw, while the Christians who were within stoutly defended the walls, towers, and fortifications. But when the Turks boldly pressed on toward the walls with engines, our men, animated to fight for the Christian faith by the exhortation of John of Capistrano, of the Observant order of Minors, a learned and most religious man, burst out through the gates of the city, with John, Cardinal of St. Angelo and Apostolic Legate, carrying before them the banner of the holy cross; and they attacked the Turks so vigorously and with such great boldness, cut them down, struck them, killed them, and at once turned them all to flight, that in their excessive haste many of them were drowned in the river, and many too were slain by our men. On account of this victory of the Christians, Pope Callixtus III instituted the feast of the Lord’s Transfiguration, on the eighth day before the Ides of August, to be celebrated every year.

At the same time Mahomet stormed Corinth; many thousands of Christians were killed, and he imposed tribute on the Christians throughout the whole Peloponnese.

In these times also a great gathering of boys from all Germany arose toward St. Michael on the borders of Spain; leaving their parents without counsel, with incredible fervor, led along together in order, they went on, no one being able to prevent them, begging, singing about St. Michael, and proceeding in order under a banner on which the same angel was depicted. Nor could it be written what that spirit was which so strongly urged their youthful age to so hard a journey, so that boys of ten or twelve years would cross so many miles on foot without provisions. Afterwards not only boys, but also men, women, male servants, maidservants, and virgins, suddenly, inspired by I know not what spirit, left everything and girded themselves for the same journey.

*1457.*

In the thirteenth year of Conrad our abbot, in the month of November, Ladislaus, king of Bohemia and Hungary, a youth of eighteen years, died, killed by poison by the Hussites in the city of Prague; he was buried there in the church of St. Wenceslaus.

In the same year, on the day of St. Matthew the Apostle and Evangelist, Conrad, abbot of this monastery, appointed as provider the cellarer John Mariae, of whom we spoke above; he had for some time been prior-cellarer. Frederick of Nackheim, however, formerly abbot, was living at this time in Kreuznach, living on his pension.

In this very year Abbot Conrad made a reckoning for his convent, in which it was found that they had paid off the greater part of the debts; nevertheless, at the creditors’ request, the brothers were often compelled to undergo ecclesiastical censures and to suspend the celebration of divine services. For, as is gathered from their manuals, four times in this year they purchased relaxation of the censures for a price.

*1458.*

In this year, which was the twelfth year of Conrad our abbot, on the Friday after the Sunday *Invocavit*, there was a very great flooding of the waters of the Rhine, the Moselle, the Nahe, and all the rivers in the surrounding region, which inflicted many losses on people. For, when the snows had melted, the flooding of the waters increased so much that they even filled the whole parish church on the island of Kreuznach with water, overturned certain altars, shook the walls, dislodged stones from the tombs of the dead, and, after causing many losses, almost destroyed the church.

In the same year Pope Pius II, in the first year of his pontificate, confirmed the reform and union of Bursfelde and the annual chapter; and, along with the many privileges that he granted, he also extended to our Bursfelde union the privileges of the congregation of St. Justina of Padua, which are many and great.

In the same year also the church of St. James outside the walls of Mainz, of our order, which is now seen, began to be built, and it was completed in the following year. For from the time when the monastery was last laid waste, after the death of Archbishop Matthias, there had been no church on the aforesaid mountain except the chapel of the blessed Mary ever virgin.
