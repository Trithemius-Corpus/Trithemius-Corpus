It is to be believed that he had no other craftsmen for this building than his own monks, since they were nearly two hundred in number; among them were stonecutters, carpenters, blacksmiths, and architects undoubtedly most experienced in every art and science of architecture. Thus they completed the whole work without the help of secular craftsmen, with excellent timberwork, as the church is seen today. For the other buildings of the monastery, apart from the church, were almost all changed in the course of time by various abbots.

*1083.*

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1083, Abbot Wilhelm founded the cell of Saint Gregory in Reichenbach. We have thought it better to insert the sequence of this foundation in his own words rather than in those of another.

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Wilhelm, by no merits of my own but by the mercy of God alone abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, desire it to be known to all, both future and present, that a certain free elder named Bern gave his little estate, situated in the Black Forest, in the place which is called Reichenbach from the stream that there flows into the Murg, to Saint Aurelius of Hirsau, in the presence of suitable witnesses, as an eternal inheritance, requesting this in return: that I should arrange for a monastery to be built there. Therefore, having received the common and concordant counsel of our elders, [unclear] we sent three of our brother monks and five lay brothers to that little estate, bristling with very dense woodland, so that, the forest having been cleared and the place made ready, they might build a monastery for blessed Gregory.

And so, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1082, indiction 5, around the Ides of May, when those brothers came to the aforesaid place, for not a few days they made use of bare fir-wood as a hut. One of them was called Ernest, an upright and courtly man, who had already some time before given himself and all his possessions to our obedience; him, together with all his goods, we assigned to the same place as a helper. This Ernest therefore became provider for the place and the brothers, and as it were a second father: for the clearing of the forest, the preparation of the site, the building of the monastery, and the construction of workshops, he himself especially gave the expenses and suitably procured resources from every side. I therefore judged it fitting that his memory and that of his parents, as also mine and my people’s, should be celebrated there, and I gladly confirmed it. Indeed, the foundation of the church was laid in the fourteenth year of the aforesaid Abbot Wilhelm, when [unclear] was completed.

In the aforesaid monastery of Reichenbach there is a priory of our order, which from the time of its foundation down to the present day has been immediately subject to the abbot of this monastery in full right and lordship. To him belongs the right of instituting and removing the prior of that place as often as there is need, and of consecrating monks there, and of doing the other things which are recognized today as held by custom and privilege. He also has similar jurisdiction over the priory in Schönrain, situated in the borders of Franconia, which is simply and immediately subject to the abbot of Hirsau in all things; he has the right of instituting and removing the prior and of arranging each matter that is known to pertain to the authority of priories of this kind. The provostry also in Rode, in the diocese of Augsburg, belongs immediately to the right of the monastery of Hirsau; its provost is subject in full right to the abbot of Hirsau. But the holy Abbot Wilhelm at that time still possessed under his jurisdiction several more priories of our order, which in the course of time, having been changed into abbeys, were alienated from the jurisdiction of the monastery of Hirsau. At this time the monastic order flourished in this monastery, highly distinguished not only by the number of monks but also by the rank of persons and the quality of their merits.

For, as we said in the preceding sections, when the fame of the most holy man Abbot Wilhelm had been spread far and wide through the world, many men began to flock to him, noble and wealthy in the world, who, despising all the things and pomps of the world, undertook to bear the sweet yoke of the Lord under his obedience. And although it is not within our power to preserve the memory of all of them, nevertheless, lest we seem to have recited a tale to those ignorant of the glory of the old monks of Hirsau, let us set forth at least the names of a few out of many, joining to them the dignities over which they presided.

Gebhard, son of the duke of Alemania and brother of Duke Berthold, a man distinguished not only by nobility of blood but also by wisdom, eloquence, and learning in the Scriptures, despised the world and, for love of God, became a monk in this our monastery under the holy father Wilhelm. He was afterward ordained bishop of Constance, and together with his brother Duke Berthold did many good things for this monastery of Hirsau. Another Gebhard of the same name, a noble and learned man, became a monk in this our monastery under the same father Wilhelm; he succeeded Wilhelm himself in the abbacy, and at length was appointed bishop of the church of Speyer...
