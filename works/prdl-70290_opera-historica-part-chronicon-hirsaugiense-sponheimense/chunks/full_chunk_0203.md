*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*1174.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Rupert, Craffto, the second abbot of my aforesaid monastery, died on the fifth day before the Kalends of June; he was the son of Count Meginhard of Spanheim, aged fifty-eight, and was buried in the middle of the choir, in one sarcophagus with his predecessor Bernhelm. Adelger, a monk and custodian of my monastery, succeeded him and presided for six years, [unclear] month, and only three days. Before entering religious life he had for a long time been in charge of the public school at Mainz, and left behind after him many distinguished pupils.

*Adelger becomes abbot of Spanheim.*

In the same year Herold, bishop of Würzburg, died; Reinhard succeeded him and presided for four years, ten months, and fifteen days.

*1175.*

In the tenth year of Abbot Rupert, which was the year 1175 of the Lord’s Nativity, there was great disturbance throughout the whole Roman Empire because of the disobedience and rebellion of Frederick, the first emperor, against the apostolic see. Rupert himself, abbot, and the brothers of this monastery, openly taking the side of the holy Roman Church from the first, suffered many troubles and injuries in goods and persons from the emperor’s supporters, until at last they were freed by the counsel of friends.

*Great disturbance of the Roman Empire.*

Therefore, since throughout all Germany there was an enormous disturbance of the people and of the clergy, because the excommunicated emperor was persecuting the pope with words, writings, and arms, and held in excessive hatred all who agreed with him, and both by himself and through his men inflicted many losses and inconveniences on them and permitted many injuries to be done, good and God-fearing men, seeing that they were placed in an evil situation, desired to save themselves either by flight or by silence.

*The emperor persecutes the pope.*

But since Rupert, the distinguished abbot of this monastery, was among those prelates who had agreed with the supreme pontiff, and was not the least of them, and daily endured many injuries from his rivals, fearing a general loss to the monastery because of the emperor’s personal hatred, and wishing to give place to anger, he resolved to resign the abbacy for the peace of the brothers and the benefit of the monastery.

*Abbot Rupert agrees with the pope.*

*1176.*

Therefore, in the eleventh year of his promotion, which was the year 1176 of the Lord’s Nativity, already greatly weighed down by age and cares, he freely resigned the abbacy into the hands of his brothers, and for the support of his life received from the brothers the provostship at Roth. There he lived for some time amid many labors, and at last rested in the Lord, being buried there, according to his prior arrangement, on the threshold of the church, so that he might be trodden by the feet of the brothers; this was a great sign of his humility. For he was, as we said above, a good and [unclear] man, who was accustomed to live among the brothers in all peace and tranquility.

*Rupert resigned the abbacy.*

For this reason he had formerly left the abbacy at Götzau, in which he could not live peacefully, and returned to his monastery of Hirsau. The monastery of Götzau is of our order, in the diocese of Speyer, in the county of Vorchheim, in eastern Francia, in the district called Albegau, beside the river called Alba; it was built by Count Berthold of Hennenberg in the year 1010 of the Lord’s Nativity, which was the eighth year during which this abbacy of Hirsau was vacant after the death of Abbot Conrad. In it Rupert presided for almost ten years. When he compelled the monks, who had strayed too far from the right path of monastic life, to observe regular discipline, he provoked the hatred of many against himself. Therefore he gave up both the place and the dignity, preferring to live humbly among his brothers of Hirsau rather than, among wicked and rebellious monks, to suffer harm to his soul under the burden of a pastor.

*The monastery of Götzau is founded.*

In the same year in which Rupert resigned the abbacy of this monastery of Hirsau, Clementia, a nun of our order in the monastery of Horrea at Trier, daughter of the late count of Homburg, died on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April. She was of most holy conduct, and by day and night intent upon [unclear] and contemplation, desired nothing more than to please God in all things.

*Saint Clementia the virgin dies.*

This most holy virgin had formerly been betrothed by her father, Count Adolf, to Lord Craffto, son of Count Meginhard of Spanheim; but since she had a holy purpose of virginity, with the consent of her betrothed she became a nun at Trier in the aforesaid monastery. Her betrothed, however, became a monk in my monastery of Spanheim under Bernhelm, the first abbot, succeeded him in the abbacy, and rested in holy conduct.

*Craffto, abbot of Spanheim.*
