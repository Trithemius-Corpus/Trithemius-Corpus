*1361.*

In the eleventh year of our abbot Philip, there arose between Edward and Raynald, brothers and dukes of Guelders, a very grave dispute and struggle over that duchy. After they had contended with one another for some time, Edward at last emerged victorious, captured his brother, and ruled the duchy of Guelders alone for ten years. In the same year, in the month of February, the sky on a certain night seemed to be burning. On that same night died William I, duke of Jülich, whom the emperor, at the assembly of Metz, about five years earlier, had made duke, as we said above.

In this same year Gerard, count of Berg, and Arnold, count of Blankenheim, who had long had hostile minds toward one another, attacking each other with sharp lances in a certain joust at Cologne, both died, each cruelly pierced through by the other’s lance.

*1362.*

In the twelfth year of our abbot Philip, certain petty robbers coming out of England were ranging through France and Spain, devastating everything with sword, fire, and plunder, and everywhere inflicting much damage. When they had increased in number, they set over themselves a captain, whom they called the Archpriest. The very multitude of robbers was called either the Great Company or the English Company, having more than forty thousand fighters. Therefore, after ranging through France and entering Spain and the neighboring regions, they plundered everything. Then they came in a very great multitude to the borders of the Trier lands. When Boemund, archbishop of Trier, heard of their arrival, he sent against them Cuno of Falkenstein, his coadjutor, Wenceslaus, duke of Luxembourg, Walram, count of Sponheim, the count of Veldenz, the count of Starkenburg, with many others both of the counts and of the nobles, with a large army. As soon as they learned that so many princes had come together against them to fight, they fled from the borders of Trier and entered Alsace. They inflicted much damage on the peasants around Strasbourg, carrying off everything they found in the villages.

Coming therefore by night to the city of Strasbourg, they stationed themselves outside the walls, burning Königshofen and certain other villages round about. But before they came, the peasants in the villages everywhere, terrified by the report of them, had brought whatever they could carry to the neighboring cities and safer places to be kept. Then, making quarters in the villages around Strasbourg, they seized whatever remained. The gates of Strasbourg, however, were closed for fear of them, and no one was permitted to enter. Whatever peasants they found in the fields they subjected to unheard-of torments, unless they redeemed themselves with money. Women and virgins, both secular and consecrated to God, whomever they found, they violated with an incredible kind of lust, sparing neither rank nor age. They encircled several towns with siege, but because they did not have the necessary engines, they could not take any at all. It was lawful for no one to pass safely without their safe-conduct; and when they had given it to anyone, they observed it with inviolable firmness. Captives who could not redeem themselves with money they killed without mercy, although many, fleeing under cover of night, escaped safely from their hands. Young males they kept as captives for themselves in servitude.

At last, after they had remained many days in Alsace and had done much damage in the fields and villages by various devastation, they were put to flight by the army of the emperor, the princes, the Strasbourgers, and other cities gathered for this purpose. When our men wished to pursue them, they fled with such speed that the journey which they completed in one day, ours could scarcely have covered in four days. And so this disturbance ceased in Alsace; although the emperor with his men, in departing, inflicted greater damage on the peoples of Alsace than they seemed to have suffered from the English, because the harvest was near, and the grain in the fields was trampled down by the army. From this a great famine followed, which lasted for six years.
