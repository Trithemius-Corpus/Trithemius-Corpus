*496. FAMILIAR LETTERS.*

…I remained outside [the monastery] up to the present day. My rivals, seeing that I was unwilling to return to the monastery, began to rage the more; whose threats I cared for little. I remained at Heidelberg and Speyer for two months, sick and infirm with fevers and the indignity of the deed, without the aid or consolation of my [friends], until at length, called by the most serene prince Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg, I went down to Cologne; and thence, my own Palatine prince consenting, I entered the March of Brandenburg itself with the margrave. Thence, after nine months, returning to [my] homeland, when I believed the tumults of [my] rivals settled, I found [them] stirred up by the ambition of certain agitators, as is said. Having considered each [thing], therefore, with myself, I resolved by no means henceforth to serve the ungrateful, nor to be under the dominion of one with whom lies prevail more than truth, and with whom the integrity of anyone's life took no confidence, [but] only the flattery of the impious took firmness. For I am of a will to leave the monastery, with the abbey of Sponheim, unless you reasonably decree otherwise—whom I desire to obey to the [best of my] powers. What [have] I [to do] with the Cynonoti [men of the Hunsrück]? Or what rest of mind can there be for me, set in the midst of the perverse? I trust in the LORD JESUS CHRIST, faithful Savior of souls, that he will not despise me, a poor servant, in the end, even if I be desolated by you all. For twenty years now and more (as you yourselves know) I have continuously labored for the community of the order and the chapter—by writing, speaking, [and] visiting, [and] by expediting innumerable causes and commissions of ours before bishops and princes and various others; nor does anyone live today in the order who has tolerated such great labors for the common good as you know me to have borne. Through Swabia, all Franconia, and Alsace, [and] through the dioceses of Trier, Cologne, Mainz, Speyer, Bamberg, Würzburg, and Strasbourg, not once or twice, but many times, at your command I visited; and for a long time, absent from my monastery at your command, I neglected my own [affairs], caring for others'. What reward have I obtained for all these? Unless the omnipotent God, in that future retribution of the just, confer on me, through his mercy, the reward of [my] labors, I should say all [is] lost before men. See what I have done, and you will understand how unjustly I suffer. For many years these evils have sprouted upon me, especially on this occasion: that, I being long occupied in visitations, my brothers did not live religiously enough, and secretly provoked the Cynonoti—of whom I now have experience—to hatred of me. I do not write from opinion: for manifest are the [things] which I say. But the faults of my brothers I neither will nor ought to bring into the light; rather, embracing patience, after the example of the holy fathers, I shall dismiss the incorrigible sheep. The rest I have committed to the aforesaid bearer, to be set forth to your paternities. I wish you well in Christ, mindful of me. From Boppard, the 20th day of the month of August, in the year of Christ [1506; the source misprints 1566], indiction 9.

JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, to the abbot of the monastery of Saint Stephen in the city of Würzburg, greeting and charity. Reverend father, friend, and dearest brother: since, according to the common proverb, in necessity a true friend is proved [to be] whether he is true or false, I ask nothing of you in this my desolation more than that (if in any way it can be done) you come to me at Boppard, the chapter being celebrated. For there are many [things] conceived in my heart to be told you, which I can by no means commit to dead letters. Nor do I think it hidden from your charity by what storms of persecutions of certain Cynonoti I am driven—who think [it] a service to God if they have persecuted me gratis, laboring no less from envy than from hatred of me. But I, who know full well that through many tribulations the entrance to the kingdom of heaven lies open, have armed myself, as far as I could hitherto, with patience—the LORD JESUS bestowing [it]—not willing to render evil for evil, but rather to give place to wrath; and, absent from my monastery, I have remained to this day for the seventeenth month. The cause, indeed—not one only, but several—persuaded me never to return to the monastery. The duke of the Cynonoti, too credulous to my rivals, favors [their] evil parties, although he seems to wish that we return. But I will not follow the command of him who is reported to have said: "Let your abbot return to the monastery, fearing nothing; for I will do him neither evil nor good." You have sufficiently declared your mind toward us, O prince, nor is any interpreter needed, for we understand how well you favor our affairs—you who manifestly promise us nothing good to be done, just as you have not before done anything. Therefore, farewell; since henceforth I will not consent to be subjected to your dominion in eternity…
