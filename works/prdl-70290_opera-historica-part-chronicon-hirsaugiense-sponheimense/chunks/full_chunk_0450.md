*Sponheim.*

*1389.*

In the fifteenth year of Craffto, our abbot, on 11 March, Margaret died, widow of Philipp Viner, knight of Sponheim, mother of Emeric, monk and cellarer of this monastery. She bequeathed thirty florins for making the panel on the high altar, which her aforesaid son Emeric, the cellarer and steward, had made, adding from his own patrimony the remainder of the cost; and he had himself painted below, beside the image of the Holy Trinity, at the foot of that panel, with his arms.

In the same year, on 6 December, Peter of Sponheim, monk of this place, died.

In the same year also Wilhelm, count of Berg, was created duke by Wenceslaus, king of the Romans, with great pomp and solemnity, as is customary.

*1390.*

In the sixteenth and last year of our abbot Craffto, Diethardus, count of Katzenelnbogen, became a public enemy and foe of the people of Cologne, and inflicted much damage on them, Frederick the archbishop dissembling and furthering the affair.

In the same year also, on the day of St Egidius the Confessor, lord Craffto died, the sixteenth abbot of this monastery, in the fifty-ninth year of his age. He was buried in the choir of the monastery, at the head of Bernhelm, the first abbot, beneath a stone: a laborious man, but not very fortunate, who governed with insufficient foresight in both states, leaving the monastery in many debts and its observance utterly dissipated.

In the same year John, duke of Bavaria, son of Albert, count of Holland, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, was postulated as bishop of Liege; although he had not yet completed the nineteenth year of his age, he was received by the people of Liege with great honor.

*Concerning Philipp the Second, seventeenth abbot of this monastery, who presided for six months and eighteen days, and concerning his deeds in that time.*

After lord abbot Craffto had died and been buried in the aforesaid year and on the aforesaid day, the brethren assembled at the chapter place on the following day, that is, 2 September, to hold the election of a new abbot. After mature deliberation among themselves they elected [unclear], who soon in the same month was confirmed by Conrad, archbishop of Mainz, and ordained according to custom by his vicar in pontificals. He presided for only six months and eighteen days: an excessively pompous and vainglorious man, who immediately at the beginning of his rule, wishing to appear splendid, hired a large and useless household; he acquired superfluous horses, contrary to the custom of all his predecessors, who used to be content with three horses and a household chaplain. With four or five he began to ride outside the monastery, making great expenses, the magnitude of which the poverty of the monastery could not sustain without utter desolation. The precious golden cross [unclear], which abbot Craffto, the first of that name, had had made 255 years before for the adornment of the house of the Lord, he sold, in order to satisfy the pomp of his rank, and utterly alienated it from the monastery. Furthermore he sold certain possessions which the monastery, from the beginning of its foundation down to these times, had possessed in all peace and usefulness.

Philipp was by origin from Bechtolsheim, son of Heinrich the knight. When he was twelve years old he became a monk under abbot Philipp Meysvvin, and was instructed in the manner of the rest, more toward the luxury and vanity of the world than toward the love of God. When, however, he was in the thirty-first year of his age, after the death of abbot Craffto, he was elected abbot, as we have said. He did evil at the beginning of his rule, and if he had lived longer he would have entirely dissipated the whole substance of the monastery.

In the same year in which abbot Philipp was instituted, on 11 October, Frederick [unclear] regular discipline and observance. For all the monks were proprietors and had separate houses of their own; neither the refectory, according to the Rule, nor the dormitory was common to them. After Frederick had died, the abbot instituted as twenty-fourth prior Rudolf, a professed monk of this monastery, who presided in the office of prior for thirteen years. He seemed little concerned for the discipline of regular life, after the manner of his teacher.

*1391.*

In this year, on 18 March, Philipp the Second, seventeenth abbot of this monastery, died. He is buried in the choir before the steps of the presbytery; his death saddened no one more than [unclear].

*Abbot Philipp dies.*
