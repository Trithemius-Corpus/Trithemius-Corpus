After he was made abbot, overcome by the prayers and love of his sister, he enlarged the enclosure, which was too cramped, and gathered in it sisters serving Christ, up to the number of twelve; all of them lived in common from the portion of their paternal inheritances and from the labor of their hands, without burden to this monastery.

*1183.*

Christian, archbishop of Mainz, died; Conrad was restored, whom the emperor Frederick had expelled because of his adherence to the Apostolic See, and he presided for 17 years. He wrote the title thus: “Conrad, by the grace of God bishop of Sabina, archbishop of the holy see of Mainz: After divine clemency restored us, after our long exile, to the governance of the Church of Mainz, by the office enjoined on us by God, by which we were and are bound universally to provide for our [unclear] church, graciously to reform all things which pertain to the state of rectitude and the vigor of justice ...” He retained this title together with the bishopric of Sabina, which Pope Alexander had conferred on him when he had fled, as was said above, as long as he lived.

In this year, on 20 July, a great fire broke out in the town of Kreuznach, by which many houses were burned and great damage was inflicted.

*1184.*

In this year Abbot Baldemar built the great house on the eastern side, which is now the lower refectory, with the brothers’ dormitory above, for the reception of guests, away from the abbatial house, in which he constructed various chambers in the manner of the ancients; all of these were afterward changed into another form.

In this same year Lugardis, a virgin nun enclosed here, very devout, daughter of the late Eberhard, knight of Sobernheim, died on 25 October and was buried in the cloister of this monastery. It was said of her that she had often had visions and angelic revelations.

*1187.*

In this year Eberold, the third prior of this monastery, was deposed by Abbot Baldemar, because he seemed too lax in the observance of regular discipline. Rupert succeeded him in the priorship as the fourth, a very venerable monk, a lover, cultivator, and strenuous preserver of regular observance, who after Baldemar’s death was later elected abbot in his place. He was by nation a Thuringian, sprung from honorable parents, very learned in both divine and human writings; of him we shall speak in his own place. He greatly enlarged the library according to his abilities, and wrote and caused to be written many volumes.

*1188.*

Abbot Baldemar built and completed the wall around this monastery, which Abbot Craffto had begun long before but, being overtaken by death, had not completed, because not everything necessary could be accomplished at the beginning.

In this same year the virgin Hildegund died on 20 April in the monastery of Schönau, of the Cistercian order, in the diocese [unclear]; she had been received there among the novices, concealing her name and calling herself Joseph. Caesarius, monk of Heisterbach, wrote her history in the first distinction of his Dialogues.

*1189.*

In this year Ida, countess of Sponheim, became a nun in the monastery of St. Rupert at Bingen, brought to this by a wondrous vision given to her from heaven; there, living in holy conduct and very severe penitence, she was an example to all of chastity and holiness. Her sister was that most holy virgin Margaret of Hohenfels, prioress for many years in the same monastery, who is said to have shone with miracles in her lifetime; the feast of her commemoration is celebrated on 29 October.

*1190.*

In this year, on the vigil of St. John the Baptist, there was a great general eclipse.

*1192.*

Abbot Baldemar, in the twelfth year of his rule, had a certain dispute with the villagers of Sponheim over pastures and woods, which nevertheless was settled by Count Gerlach and other good friends with the consent of the parties.
