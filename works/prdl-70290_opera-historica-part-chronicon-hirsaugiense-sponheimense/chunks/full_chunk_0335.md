1195.

In this year Abbot Baldemar purchased the village which is called Argenschwang, with all its jurisdiction, justice, and property, for twenty marks of silver from the count of Sponheim, whose advocacy he gave in fief to Erinfrid of Sponheim, knight.

Also in this same year Conrad, count palatine of the Rhine, duke in Heidelberg, [unclear] of the diocese of Worms, changed [unclear] of the monks of our order in Nuwenburg, appointing as the first abbess Cunegund, a very religious virgin, with Sigenhard, abbot of the monastery of Lorsch, agreeing to everything; to him that provostry of Nuwenburg belonged by full right, without any intermediary. 1197.

In this year Udegeba, sister of Abbot Baldemar, a nun enclosed here among us, died on the fourth Ides of July, a virgin of holy conduct, who for many years served almighty God in great devotion. Five other sisters died successively not long after her; these are their names: Sister Walpurgis, on the eighth Kalends of August; Gertrude, on the third Ides of August; Adelheid, on the thirteenth Kalends of September; Lucardis, on the seventh Kalends of September; Otilia, on the sixteenth Kalends of December, at night around the eleventh hour, who was the daughter of Henry, knight of Kreuznach, a most beautiful virgin, renowned no less for her conduct than for her blood, who shone with many virtues. There remained, however, six or seven in number, who also died successively within a short time; nor were as many gathered again in their place as before, but far fewer.

1198.

In this year the little village called Bockenau was set on fire on the fifteenth Kalends of January by a certain fugitive peasant, and was entirely consumed by fire. He himself, however, was afterwards apprehended and was judicially burned for so heinous a deed.

In the same year Drutwin, Schultheiss in the village of Sponheim, was captured by Count Gerlach and beheaded on the day before the Kalends of June for the crime of homicide.

1199.

Baldemar, the fourth abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, died in this year on the tenth Kalends of July, and was buried in the cloister before the door of the church, in the fifty-fourth year of his age, not yet completed. He was a good and religious man, who profitably governed this monastery both in spiritual and in temporal matters, and did many good things. Rupert, prior of this place, succeeded him in the abbacy.

*Concerning Rupert, the fifth abbot of this monastery, who presided for fourteen years and two months; concerning deeds relating to the present monastery.*

In the year of the Lord's incarnation 1199, in the second indiction, two days after the death of Lord Abbot Baldemar, that is, on the eighth Kalends of July, the brothers came together to hold the election of a new pastor. Having invoked, according to custom, the grace of the Holy Spirit, they unanimously set over themselves as the fifth abbot of the place Lord Rupert, the fourth prior of this place, a learned man, very religious, no less prudent in counsel than mature in action. After his long and humble excuse of himself, he presided vigorously for thirteen years and three months. He was ordained abbot in the following year by Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, in the church of Saint Alban, because Archbishop Conrad, returning from Jerusalem, had remained for some time in Italy because of the dissension between Philip and Otto, who were contending over the empire. Afterwards, having returned to Mainz, he died in the following year, as we shall say. This Abbot Rupert was by origin from the province of the Thuringians, of honorable parents of moderate fortune; having been given over to letters from infancy, so to speak, he advanced with age in every branch of knowledge, both secular and divine, and became most learned. And first indeed in his own country he received the rudiments of youthful studies; then he went to Gaul, and returning from there after some years, at the admonition of a fellow-countryman of his, he was detained here and became a monk under the venerable Abbot Adelger, when he was about twenty-four years old. But after he assumed the habit of holy conduct, he was so devoutly, so religiously and blamelessly planted in the midst of the brothers' life that he seemed to be the master of all in regular observance.
