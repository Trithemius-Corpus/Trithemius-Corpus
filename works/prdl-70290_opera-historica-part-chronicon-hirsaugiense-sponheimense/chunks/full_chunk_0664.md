*Familiar Letters. LXXI.*

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, sends greetings to his excellent brother Roger Sycambro.

There came to me at Budori, on the fourth day of this month, John in Schorteren, Conrad of St. Stephen’s at Würzburg, and George in Schwarzach, abbots of monasteries of our order, sent by the annual chapter lately held at Mainz. In brief, they had these instructions: that by their words they should persuade me to return to the monastery. They said also that two other fathers had been appointed by the chapter, who were to approach the Duke in the name of the whole Bursfeld union and make him reconciled to me.

When I heard this, I answered that there was no need for men appointed on my account to approach the Duke, since I neither seek nor desire his reconciliation or favor. Far be it from me hereafter to be subject to the lordship of a man with whom falsehood has very great power and truth can do nothing. Having suffered an exceedingly rash injury, both from the Duke and from three men of my community, I shall not return to them any more, but shall resign the abbacy as soon as I have made some provision for my affairs.

After receiving this answer, when they saw that my opinion was fixed, the envoys troubled me no further, but wrote back to the abbots appointed by the chapter that there was no need of any effort with the Duke, because I had altogether resolved not to return any more to Spanheim. God will provide for me where I may remain, with greater peace than I have ever lived in at Spanheim. But if you wish to know whither I intend to betake myself, I do not know what I should answer. Three great princes await me, but it does not please me to follow the courts of princes. Three abbots also, more powerful men of the order which I have professed, ask me to come to them.

But lest you think it a fable, I shall give the names of each. Maximilian, most serene King of the Romans, has several times, as our men know, caused me to be solicited to enter his service. There is no need for me to bring the Margrave of Brandenburg forward, since you know how highly he esteems me. Philip also, Duke of Bavaria, Count Palatine of the Rhine, a most gracious prince, has promised to provide for me abundantly as long as I live, either with the Abbot of Limburg, or in his own principality, wherever I may wish. Lastly, the above-mentioned Abbot of St. Stephen’s has now told me that the abbey of St. James the Greater, apostle, in the suburb of Würzburg, will shortly become vacant through the resignation of the present abbot; if I am willing to take it up, he said that he would make effort that the matter should take effect as soon as possible.

I have agreed to the plan, preferring to live in poverty under monastic discipline than to live richly in the courts of princes. These matters being thus concluded, the fathers sent to me by the chapter departed on the eighth day of September, to return to their own places. But I await the answer of the often-mentioned abbot, namely whether the matter so pleases the most reverend father, the Bishop of Würzburg; when that has at last been received, I shall make you more certain about everything.

Farewell, from Budori, on the twelfth day of September, in the year of the Lord 1506.

*LXXII.*

Conrad, Abbot of St. Stephen’s at Würzburg, sends greetings to Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim.

Reverend father, in accordance with the departure from our mutual conclusion most recently made in the monastery of the nuns of Neuburg near Heidelberg, as soon as I arrived home I approached the lord most reverend Bishop of Würzburg. Among other conversations concerning the monastery of St. James, I made mention of your paternity, asserting that it would be possible, if it pleased his most reverend paternity, for your paternity to be placed over the same monastery. Hearing this, he said that it was greatly pleasing to him, and he promised that he wished graciously to favor your paternity and faithfully to lend helping hands; he also enjoined me to signify these things to your paternity as soon as possible.

Therefore let your paternity now make no delay, which our most reverend lord himself is accustomed to take very ill in all matters. The rest I reserve for mutual conversation, which may the Most High deign to make prosperous and pleasant. Given at Würzburg on the day of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, in the year of Christians 1506.
