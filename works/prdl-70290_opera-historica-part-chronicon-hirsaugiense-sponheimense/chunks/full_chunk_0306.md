To this holy work Mechthildis, his wife, a most devout woman, contributed many benefactions with great eagerness; and she did not cease to press the count himself, her husband, with continual prayers, admonishing and beseeching him that he would deign to bring the work begun to completion as quickly as possible. For that most religious woman feared that delay, which is customarily harmful and dangerous to all good works, might by some occasion change her husband’s holy purpose. Count Rodolphus also, Megenhard’s brother, together with his wife Richardis, expended no small costs for the completion of the work.

*Concerning the consecration of the church and of this monastery of Sponheim, by the venerable prelate of Worms, Boggo, vice-archbishop for Adelbert.*

*Count Rudolphus had begun at the consecration.*

When, therefore, the building that had been begun was at last completed, that is, the church formed in the shape of a cross, with the cloister and the necessary houses, the refectory and dormitory adjoining the cloister on the western side, the magnificent Count Megenhard, before he handed over the monastery for monks to inhabit, wished the church, which he had reformed in many respects, to be consecrated anew, as is customary. Therefore, after the clerics had been removed and provided for elsewhere, he approached Adelbert, archbishop of Mainz, concerning the consecration of the church, and also revealed to him the desire of his heart concerning the introduction of monks to Sponheim. That most reverend prelate greatly commended the count’s good purpose; and because he could not come in person to the consecration, as was requested, he designated in his place the venerable prelate of the church of Worms, Boggo, who at that time had for many years been expelled from his see, and whom he kept with himself honorably.

Therefore in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1114, on the Sunday Quasimodogeniti, Buggo, bishop of Worms, consecrated it.

*They consecrate this place of Worms.*

Eugenius XII. Therefore in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1178, in the second indiction, under Emperor Henry V, Count Megenhard, the renowned founder of this place, handed over this monastery itself, with the church and all their appurtenances, to the order of our most holy father Benedict for perpetual possession, and gave it to monks to inhabit. The first monks, moreover, who from this point first began to inhabit this place, twelve in number, came from the monasteries of Saint Alban and Saint James near Mainz, by order of Archbishop Adelbert, at the urging of Count Megenhard, in the year noted above, on the Wednesday after Passion Sunday. Of these, eight were priests and four were conversi, distinguished and notable men, some of whom, before entering religion, had formerly served as soldiers in the world. These are their names: Bertholdus, priest; Diemus, priest; Adelbero, priest; Anshelmus, priest; Ergenbaldus, priest; Herbordus, priest; Sifridus also, priest; Benno, conversus; Lupoldus, conversus; Hartungus, conversus; and Emicho, conversus. These twelve brothers were taken partly from the monastery of Saint Alban, properly at Mainz, as we have said, and partly from the monastery of Saint James in the same place. When they came to the present place, they were honorably received by Count Megenhard and his wife, and they began to inhabit the monastery solemnly handed over to them. Therefore, after possession had been obtained in the name of God, the aforesaid monks unanimously elected for themselves as abbot and pastor, according to the monastic rule, Bernhelmus, a monk of the aforesaid monastery of Saint Alban, of whom we shall speak later. Bertholdus, a venerable old man, was appointed claustral prior, and for thirteen years he vigorously presided over the office of prior in this place. Furthermore, Adelbero was made cellarer of the monastery and presided for many years; since he was a good and prudent man, he did many good things for this monastery.
