These [things] said by the countess, archbishop Baldwin was moved with mercy, and, peace given to the counts, raised the siege. There was made, therefore, between the said archbishop of Trier and the aforesaid counts, peace; and the Sponheim [counts], reconciled to one another, departed in concord. But count Sylvester held himself in a certain fort called Felsberg; which, after archbishop Baldwin perceived [it], encircling [it] with a siege, he took and utterly destroyed, and forced the count himself to surrender. These [things] were done in the year of the Lord 1320, in the 3rd indiction.

After this, in the year of the Lord [MCCXXXVIII, as printed—recte 1338], between the aforesaid bishop Baldwin and the countess of Starkenburg arose a certain contention over certain goods in Birkenfeld, whence the archbishop decreed to gather an army against the countess herself; but by the industry of Henry, count of Sponheim, provost of Aachen, and Emerich von Stein, soldier, laboring for the countess, the archbishop's intention was impeded. [Margin: The archbishop of Trier is captured by the countess of Starkenburg.] It happened meanwhile that archbishop Baldwin, hidden in a small boat, wished to go down the channel of the Moselle to Coblenz; and he, betrayed to the countess, was captured by her, and for some time detained in the aforesaid castle of Starkenburg. At length he redeemed himself from that captivity for thirty thousand gold florins, and renounced all the goods in Birkenfeld over which the contention had arisen. With this money the same countess built a castle called Frauenburg, which is now placed in the dominion of John, duke of Simmern.

[1335.] In this year, which was the 26th of our abbot Willicho, John, count of Sponheim, brother of count Simon, handed over to abbot Willicho and his successor abbots of this monastery, in perpetuity, the part of his castle of Sponheim concerning him, with all its appurtenances—namely so that the abbot, who should be for the succeeding time, should hand it over in fief to the elder of the counts who should be lord in Kreuznach, according to the tenor of the letters which the same count, drawn up thereupon and fortified with his seal, handed to the abbot; of which the date is the year from Christ's nativity [MCCCCXXV, as printed—recte MCCCXXXV], the sixth feria after the day of the beheading of St John the Baptist. And thus the whole castle of Sponheim thenceforth, with all pertaining to it, depends on the fief of the abbot of this monastery. For what John performed of his part in this year, the same Simon his brother, who had received the other part in the division, did six years before (as we said there).

In the same year, on the day of Simon [and] Jude the apostles, the greatest wind was in all these parts about the Rhine, which overturned many buildings [and] trees.

[1336.] In the year 28 of abbot Willicho, on the day of St Augustine the bishop, died Walram, bishop of Speyer, buried at Speyer in the church of the Friars Preachers before the high altar, whom Gerard of Ehrenberg succeeded, elected on the day of St Catherine virgin [and] martyr: he presided 27 years, 1 month, 6 days.

In the same year the city of Mechelen, kindled by celestial fire, was miserably burned up on the day of Corpus Christi.

[1337.] In this year the 11th abbot of this monastery, lord Willicho (who was also called Wilhelm, as appears in the superscription of his seal), the 16th [day before] the Kalends of April, rested in the Lord, buried in the choir before the steps of the presbytery; who did many good [things] for this monastery. First of all, in the 8th year of his rule, he obtained from Pope John [XXII] a confirmation of all the goods of this monastery, as appears in the bull itself, whose tenor is as follows: "John, bishop, servant of the servants of God, to [his] beloved sons the abbot and convent of the monastery of St Martin in Sponheim, of the order of St Benedict, of the diocese of Mainz, apostolic greeting and benediction. Since [something] which is just and honest is asked of us, both the vigor of equity and the order of reason demand that, through the solicitude of our office, it be brought to its due effect. Therefore, beloved sons in the Lord, agreeing with grateful assent to your just petitions, [we take] your persons and the place in which…"
