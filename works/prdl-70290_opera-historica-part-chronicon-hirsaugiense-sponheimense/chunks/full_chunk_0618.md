... there is need, if we desire to obtain the fruit of true wisdom with the most blessed apostles of Christ in eternal blessedness. For Saint Christophilus says again in a certain letter: “Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no one will see God.” In Leviticus too the Lord said to Moses: “Speak to the whole assembly of the children of Israel, and say to them: Be holy, because I, the Lord your God, am holy.”

But what holiness is to be thought to be, Dionysius the Areopagite did not pass over in silence in chapter twelve of *On the Divine Names*, saying: “Holiness, to speak according to our manner, is purity free from every crime, perfect, and utterly unstained.” Our God is holy; we, however, are sinners and unclean. Since, therefore, there is no union between unlike things, how will there be concord between God and us? Let us hear the divine teacher, Saint Augustine: “No holy and just man is without sin; yet on that account he does not cease to be just and holy, so long as he holds holiness in affection.” For Scripture says: “Seven times in a day the just man falls, and will rise again; but the wicked will fall into evil.”

“If he falls,” says Jerome, “how is he just? If he is just, how does he fall? But he does not lose the name of just who always rises again through repentance.” And not only to one sinning seven times, but even seventy times, if he is turned to repentance, sins are forgiven. And although no one becomes holy by his own powers, since it is not of him who wills nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy, nevertheless our good will is necessarily required for our sanctification, so that the grace of God may work holiness in us. For indeed, just as neither a man without a woman, nor a woman without a man, begets a human being from himself or herself, so neither does the good will of a human being without the grace of God sanctify anyone, nor does divine mercy sanctify anyone unto good without that person’s own will.

God gave free choice to man, whereby he might have his will in his own power: not so that by himself he could do the good which he wills, but so that he might will what God himself has granted him to be able to do. Hence also the holy Christophilus said: “To will is present with me; but to accomplish it I do not find.” Why is this, O holy observer of divine things, when nothing is so sufficient for a good will as it is for itself?
