*1332.*

In the fifteenth year of Abbot Sigismund, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1332, Henry the Elder, duke of Bavaria, again began to contend for the duchy with his brother Otto and Henry the Younger. When Emperor Louis ordered peace for him, and he did not desist, he gathered an army and in his own person surrounded the town of Straubing with a siege and attacked it vigorously for eight weeks, until at last the aforesaid John, king of Bohemia, arrived and restored peace between them.

*Straubing is surrounded with a siege.*

In the same year, on the fifteenth day before the kalends of January, Gerlach Schenk, bishop of Worms, died. He was succeeded by Salamannus, born at Mainz, who presided for twenty-three years, confirmed by Pope John XXII but ordained by his successor Benedict XII. When the people of Worms refused to admit him, they were marked by the pope with an ecclesiastical interdict; and many of the canons, especially those of Saint Cyriacus, disobedient to the apostolic commands and rebellious against Salamannus, were excommunicated. At length, after satisfaction had first been made, he reconciled them by apostolic authority.

*1334.*

In the sixteenth year of Abbot Sigismund, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 1333, Wolfram, bishop of Würzburg, died on the day before the kalends of July. Otto Wolfskeel succeeded him and presided for fifteen years, having been promoted and ordained at Liège on the twenty-first day of July by Pope John XXII against Hermann of Lichtenberg, Emperor Louis’s chancellor, out of hatred for the emperor, who wished him to be ordained bishop. For, as we said above in its place, after Emperor Henry VII had died in Italy, Louis, count palatine and duke of Bavaria, brother of Rudolf, count palatine of the Rhine, by the election of the princes and after Frederick, duke of Austria, had been defeated, was raised to be king of the Romans in the year of the Lord 1314. He soon fell into great discord with Pope John XXII on such an occasion, as was reported.

*Thus Otto becomes bishop of Würzburg.*

*Cause of the dissension between pope and emperor.*

In the same sixteenth year a certain chancellor, Ulrich by name, a native of Augsburg of the Rhaetians, a rash and proud man, hostile to the clergy and to religion, and a very great hater and adversary of ecclesiastical dignity and liberty, in letters to the pope in the emperor’s name put certain unworthy things in that beginning, and among other things called the pope himself the beast rising from the sea, and wrote many insults against him. Provoked by these, not undeservedly, to indignation, the pontiff excommunicated King Louis. This chancellor, already near death, publicly confessed this wickedness of his before many people. Hence Louis, having set out for Rome in the year of the Lord 1327, wishing to be reconciled to the pope, while he was staying at Avignon, as we said above, in the ninth year of Abbot Sigismund received the office of blessing from a certain simple bishop, and remained in the dispensation of Pope John, appointing another as antipope. To this excommunicated Emperor Louis there came into Bavaria William of Ockham, of the Order of Minors, English by nation, a man indeed learned in philosophy and sacred letters, but exceedingly rash, excommunicated by Pope John XXII, against whom he wrote many things in blasphemy, as we have said more fully in the book On Ecclesiastical Writers.

In the same year Henry the Younger, duke of Bavaria, son of Otto, king of Hungary, died.

In this year a very great abundance of wine grew, so that casks were lacking.

*1333.*

In the seventeenth year of Abbot Sigismund, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1354, Henry, archbishop of Cologne, died. He was succeeded in the archbishopric by Walram, son of Gerard, count of Jülich, provost of Liège and treasurer of Cologne, who presided for sixteen years, six months, and nineteen days, a man very learned in both laws, energetic and peaceable; he governed the church committed to him quite energetically, peacefully, and profitably in its condition. This Archbishop Walram, among the many buildings which he erected in various places during the time of his pontificate, also built anew the monastery of the Carthusians in Cologne, which he honorably and abundantly endowed for the necessary support of the brothers.

*Walram becomes archbishop of Cologne.*

*The Charterhouse at Cologne is founded.*

In the same year, on the day before the nones of December, Pope John XXII died at Avignon in the ninetieth year of his age, leaving an inestimable quantity of gold in his treasuries. He was succeeded in the pontificate by Benedict XII, of Toulouse, of the Cistercian Order, doctor of sacred theology, previously called Jacques Fournier, and he presided for seven years, three months, and eighteen days.
