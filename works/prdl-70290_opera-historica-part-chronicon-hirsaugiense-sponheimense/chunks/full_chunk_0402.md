*1314.*

In the fifth year of Willicho our abbot, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of November, the princes of the Empire assembled at Frankfurt to elect a new emperor; and, when the votes were divided in different directions, Peter, bishop of Mainz, Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, John, king of Bohemia, and [Volmarus?], margrave of Brandenburg, elected Louis, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, as king of the Romans. Against him Rudolf, count palatine of the Rhine, brother of that same Louis, Henry, archbishop of Cologne, and the duke of Saxony elected Frederick, duke of Austria, son of Albert, formerly king of the Romans, as king. But Louis obtained the kingdom and the Empire, and, after defeating Frederick in battle, took him captive. And he compelled his brother Rudolf, count palatine, who sided with Frederick, despoiled of his duchy and every possession, to flee to England, where he remained until death. Yet he restored the principality to his sons.

In the same year, on the fourth day before the Ides of February, Emerich, bishop of Worms, died; Henry, son of count Silvester of Dune, provost of the greater church of Worms, succeeded him, and presided vigorously for almost five years.

Also in the same year, on the day before the Ides of January, Sibodo of Lichtenberg, bishop of Speyer, died; Emich of Leiningen succeeded him, and presided for fourteen years.

*1315.*

In the sixth year of Willicho our abbot, there was a great famine throughout all Germany and Italy, in which many thousands of people perished from want; and it lasted almost two years, and a great plague followed.

In the same year a battle took place between Louis, king of the Romans, and Frederick, duke of Austria, his adversary, near Speyer; in it Leopold, Frederick’s brother, consumed many villages round about by fire. There was, as we have said, a great famine, and Baldwin, bishop of Trier, a wealthy and most provident man, supplied abundantly to the king and his army whatever had been necessary. Victory fell to Louis, who compelled Frederick with all his men to flee; nevertheless he did not afterward acquiesce.

*1316.*

In his seventh year, Willicho, abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, around the feast of Pentecost, was appointed by Pope John XXII, together with the other prelates of the church of Mainz, as commissioner concerning the inquiry into the life of Hildegard of holy memory, formerly mistress of the virgins of Christ on the mount of Saint Rupert of blessed memory at Bingen. A commission for this matter had also previously been made by Pope Clement V, who gave his consent to her canonization. But when the inquiry had been made and communicated by letters to the aforesaid supreme pontiff John XXII, she had been canonized; and I do not know by what intervening occasion it was neglected.

In the same year King Louis, Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, and certain others summoned to his aid, besieged the fortifications of Wiesbaden and Scharfenstein against Frederick. When they saw that they could accomplish little, the matter unfinished and the siege lifted, each returned to his own affairs.

Also in the same year King Louis confirmed peace with all the princes and cities of the Empire; Henry, archbishop of Cologne, and the other men who were on the side of Frederick, duke of Austria, paid no heed to it.
