This agreement contains many other articles also concerning the law of the parties alleged, which there is no need to insert now. At this agreement, made between the aforesaid lords Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, and Conrad the Landgrave, there was present Master Conrad of Marburg, of the Order of Preachers, a man of great learning and authority in the Sacred Scriptures, who had been appointed general inquisitor of heretical depravity by the holy Apostolic See. In this same year, on the third day before the Nones of May, he caused four heretics in the city of Erfurt, condemned to the fire, to be burned. Many, almost innumerable, throughout nearly all Germany, who had long lain hidden as heretics, after being brought to light and examined, he condemned, and handed over to the secular court to be burned.

There are those who say that he was too ready and prompt in condemning simple people, to such an extent that he caused many innocent persons to be killed; for as soon as anyone had been denounced before him, without the cause of the denunciation or the merits of the accuser having been examined, he would pronounce sentence, crying: “Take away, take away the impious heretic, to be burned with fire.” Hence in the following year, which was that of the Lord’s incarnation 1233, it happened that the Count of Sayn was falsely accused before him of heresy. For his excuse or purgation Henry, son of the emperor Frederick II, held an assembly of many princes, bishops, and abbots at Mainz; all of them, with one voice, bore united testimony to the count’s Catholic faith, since, a few years before, not without great loss of his men and goods, he had crossed the sea in aid of the Lord’s sepulchre. He had always honored the Church of God and its ministers. Finally the count himself, standing before all, with tears publicly proclaimed his innocence, gravely injured in so unjust a manner.

For this reason brother Conrad became hateful to all, noble and ignoble alike, since on account of true and fabricated heresies he had caused many nobles and commoners, clerics, monks, nuns, enclosed women, townsmen, and peasants, in various provinces of Germany, to be condemned by an excessively hasty sentence, if it is right to say so, to the punishment of fire, and cruelly killed. For on the same day on which anyone was accused, whether justly or unjustly, with no refuge afforded him by any appeal, any defense or excuse, or even, what was impious, any recantation, he was condemned and thrown into cruel flames, so much so that he seemed to rejoice more in the multitude of the condemned than of the innocent. Therefore, seeing that he could not prevail against the Count of Sayn, confounded and exposed to the hatred of many, he resolved to go from Mainz toward Marburg, and [unclear] safe-conduct both from the king and from the archbishop [unclear].

In the same year the Sultan of Babylon sent to the emperor a tent, constructed with wondrous skill, in which images of the Sun and Moon, moved artificially, [showed] their course with certain proper and due [unclear]. He was killed by certain nobles; and when he had sought pardon and mercy with tears and sighs, he was not heard. He was buried beside St. Elizabeth.

In the same year also the emperor Frederick II took away from the same monastery the principality of a monastery of our order not far from Heidelberg, which is called Lorsch, with all its honor, vassals, ministeriales, [unclear], towns, revenues, rights, and appurtenances, which the abbot of that same monastery had always possessed up to this time, and gave it to Sifrid, archbishop of Mainz, and to his church, as is clear from the letters that follow:

“In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity: Frederick II, by divine favoring clemency Emperor of the Romans, ever Augustus, king of Jerusalem and Sicily. The dignity of the Roman prince is exalted, and the narrow bounds of the empire are adorned, when he restores and preserves by suitable provision the principalities in which the empire, established in its dignity, flourishes, and chooses and appoints over them for their restoration and preservation those persons who, both by the prerogative of the souls committed to them and by the merit of their own dignities, are judged sufficient and acceptable for this. We wish it to be known to all the faithful of the empire, both present and future, that we, considering the weak condition of the church of Lorsch, falling in the course of time from the honor and name of a principality, so that it seems to fail no less the empire than itself; considering moreover that through our beloved prince Sifrid, venerable archbishop of Mainz, whose favor and services daily rendered to the empire present themselves to our sight with much gratitude, not through the same see of Mainz, always effective in obedience to the empire and to our honors, the service owed to the empire by that principality, which because of the weakness of that same church has hitherto been withdrawn from us, can be rendered in full: with our beloved son Henry, illustrious king of the Romans, ever Augustus, standing near us and wishing to provide for these matters, together with the deliberation and counsel of the princes, from our inborn prudence, by the grace of majesty, [we grant] that same principality of the church of Lorsch, with all honor, vassals, ministeriales,”
