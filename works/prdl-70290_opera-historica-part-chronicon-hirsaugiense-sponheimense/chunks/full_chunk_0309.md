*Chronicle of Trithemius*

... consented, demanding nothing at all and imposing a burden in no respect, and wished the place and its inhabitants to enjoy perpetual peace and freedom, burdened by no service to anyone. And lest perhaps in future any one of his heirs, who bore the name of advocate, should bring any trouble upon the monastery's possessions or persons, he took care to make provision by letters, which, at the request of Bernhelmus, the first abbot, he issued in the following form of words:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Megenhardus, by the grace of God count of Sponheim, wish it to be known to all the faithful of Christ, both present and future, that when our father, Count Stephanus of Sponheim, had determined to raise his church, set on Monte Campi beside the village of Sponheim, into a monastery, and, overtaken by death with the work unfinished, had entrusted it to me to complete, I, with the consent to this of Mechildis my wife and of my brother Count Rodolphus, for the remedy of my soul completed the work begun by my father, placed there monks according to the rule of St. Benedict, provided for their sustenance from my patrimony, and freely handed over that place with all its goods forever into the hands of my lord Adelbertus, archbishop of Mainz, legate of the apostolic see, at the altar of St. Martin.

As advocate of the same monastery, however, as long as I live, I have wished no one other than myself to be such. But after my death, the eldest of my sons who shall be lord in Creutzenacht shall hold the advocacy of the aforesaid monastery. If he, forgetting the name of his office, should wish to attack, trouble, or disperse the goods of the monastery or the persons for whom he ought to have been guardian and defender, the abbot with the brothers shall bring complaint against him to the examination of the archbishop of Mainz, who has authority to correct and amend him. But if, after a third warning by the archbishop, he has not amended, making satisfaction for the damage and injuries done to the monastery, the same archbishop shall have power, with the consent and will of the abbot and brothers, to remove him and give them another advocate, whom they themselves shall have chosen. And he shall do this as often as it has seemed necessary to the abbot and brothers, always appointing such a man as is more suitable in power, kindness, and fitness for protecting them.

Let the advocate beware that he ever burden the church in its goods or household; nor shall he demand any ferms, exactions, or precariae, but shall preserve them wholly free. He shall not sit at a public plea within the boundaries of the monastery unless he has been invited by the abbot. But when he comes invited, let him be served in such a way as is fitting for his honor, [unclear], lest by the size of any retinue or retinue of gold and sum the brothers themselves who serve God be saddened. Therefore let him act in all things so piously, kindly, and moderately, showing himself in all things toward the household and the goods of the monastery such that he may be worthy of his name, and, for the office faithfully fulfilled on account of God, may receive from him the reward of eternal life.

That all these things may remain ratified and unshaken for all time, I have caused this charter to be written concerning them, and have strengthened it by the impression of my seal. Witnesses of this my will were present: lord Adelbertus of Mainz, Bruno bishop of Strasbourg, Bernhelmus abbot of the aforesaid monastery, the whole congregation; Adelinus abbot of Disibodenberg, Hermannus provost of Schwabenheim, Goswinus of Lurburck, Count Dudo, Ernestus vice-lord, Adelbertus Warmundus the schultheiss; Henricus, Vdalricus of Stein, peers, and others.

These things were enacted in the year of the Lord's incarnation 1105, in the third indiction, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of September.

Finally, for the greater security of the goods of this place, the aforesaid man approached Henry V, king of the Romans, and obtained confirmation of the aforesaid matters as follows:

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. Henry, by favor of divine clemency emperor augustus of the Romans: we wish it to be known to all the faithful of Christ who are ours, both future and present, in what manner Megenhardus, count of Sponheim, with the assent and cooperation of Mechildis his wife, moved by heavenly inspiration, for the salvation and welfare of himself and his own, founded a monastery in his county in honor of Saint Mary ever virgin and of Saint Martin the bishop, which he lawfully endowed from his patrimony, establishing in it religious men who, under the rule of St. Benedict and an abbot, would praise almighty God continually in times present and future and pour out devout prayers for him and for his forebears.

He also caused to be written down and strengthened with his seal the right of advocacy, by which he wished them after his death to be free from every service, exaction, precaria, and any other burdens and grievances, and handed it over to the abbot and brothers of the same monastery, which is also called Sponheim. All these things Adelbertus, archbishop of Mainz, in whose parish that monastery was built, confirmed by his ban. Thereupon the aforesaid Count Megenhardus, for an increase of greater stability, requested from us that so holy and pious a work be confirmed by our imperial authority, lest any malice of later men [unclear] by right of imperial [unclear] might be able to alter them, once confirmed by imperial majesty. And because divine compassion has advanced us to the throne of the kingdom so that by royal provision we may care for the churches and monasteries established in our kingdom, and protect their rights and possessions from every injury, and [unclear] the same church or monastery in Sponheim, with all the goods, possessions there, and properties, under the guardianship and protection of the royal defense of our Roman empire forever...
