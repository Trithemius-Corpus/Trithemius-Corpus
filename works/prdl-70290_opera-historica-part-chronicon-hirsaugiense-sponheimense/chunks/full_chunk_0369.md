*Sponheim.*

1251.

In the thirty-eighth year of his rule, Iuanus, abbot of this monastery, began to rebuild the church of St. Gregory the martyr; and, sending his prior with certain brothers to Hugh, Cardinal Priest of St. Sabina, legate of the Apostolic See, who was then staying at Bingen, he obtained from him indulgences of this kind.

“To all Christ’s faithful established throughout Germany, to whom these letters shall come, brother Hugh, by divine mercy Cardinal Priest of the title of St. Sabina, legate of the Apostolic See, greeting in the Lord. Since, as the Apostle says, we shall all stand before the judgment-seat of Christ, to receive according as we have done in the body, whether good or evil, it is fitting for us [unclear] eternal life. Therefore, since [unclear] and with a view to eternal things to sow on earth what, when the Lord gives it back, with multiplied fruit we ought to gather in heaven, holding firm hope and confidence, because he who sows sparingly shall also reap sparingly, and he who sows in blessings shall also from blessings reap eternal life. Therefore, since, as the beloved sons, the prior and convent of the monastery of St. Martin in Sponheim, of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, have taken care to inform us, the same chaplains have begun to rebuild their chapel of St. George in Dalheim, consumed by excessive age, at great expense; and since their own resources are not sufficient for this, we admonish and exhort all you Christ’s faithful in the Lord, and enjoin upon you for the remission of your sins, that from the goods conferred upon you by God you bestow upon them pious alms and kindly aids of charity, so that through your assistance the said work may be brought to completion, and you, through this and through other good deeds which, by the Lord’s inspiration, you shall have done, may be able to come to eternal joys. We, trusting in the mercy of almighty God and in the authority of his blessed apostles Peter and Paul, mercifully remit forty days of the penance enjoined upon all truly penitent and confessed persons who shall have stretched out to them a helping hand for this purpose; which [unclear] through questors we more strictly forbid, decreeing that, if otherwise done, they shall lack force. Given at Bingen, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of August, in the ninth year of the pontificate of the lord pope Innocent IV.”

And so the church of St. George, the rebuilding of which was begun in this year, was not completed. For only the choir, where the altar is, was roofed; the remaining part of the church remained unfinished until the time of Abbot Johannes, as was said above under the twenty-first year of Iuanus.

In the same year also Christian, archbishop of Mainz, died; Gerard succeeded him [unclear] years [unclear]. In the same year Abbot Iuanus began to be sick unto death.

1252.

In this year, which was the thirty-ninth and last of Abbot Iuanus, there died the most devout virgin of Christ, Jutta, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of April; she had formerly been the maidservant of blessed Elizabeth, landgravine of the Thuringians, of whom mention was made above. This holy virgin was renowned for many signs and miracles both in life and after death, and while her lady was still living she was always the most faithful witness and helper of all her virtues; and she strove in all things to imitate her most holy manner of life, in which she prevailed so greatly, and was a singular advocate of the poor.

In the same year Iuanus, the sixth abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, weighed down by old age and long illness, closed his last day, and died on the fourth day before the Kalends of December. He was buried in the cloister of the monastery with his brothers. Under his rule, in the last years, the observance of regular discipline began to fail.

*On Johannes, the seventh abbot of this monastery, who presided for 12 years, 4 months, and 21 days, and on the deeds of that time around this place.*

When Iuanus, the fifth abbot of this monastery, had died and been buried, after mature deliberation among themselves the brothers came together as one in spirit and intention to set a new pastor over themselves. Having invoked the aid of the Holy Spirit, in the aforesaid year of the Lord’s incarnation 1253, in the tenth indiction, on the vigil of St. Andrew the Apostle, that is, on the third day before the Kalends of December, they unanimously elected as abbot Johannes, a monk and cantor of this monastery, who presided for 12 years, 4 months, and 21 days. He was a good and simple man, son of the same nobleman of Schönberg, who in his boyhood, under Abbot [Opre Cantoso?], came to religious life through the urging and offering of his parents, and for a long time held the office of cantor. When he was ordained abbot, he found that the observance of regular discipline was gradually failing, but he could not bring it back to its former fervor because of the malice of the time and the conduct of wicked men. And so, in succession from his time down to the most recent reform, which is called Bursfeld from the place where it began, the observance of monastic purity in this monastery, growing lukewarm and failing, was less cared for through [unclear] years, although in the meantime reform was attempted several times, but it did not attain a stable effect. For the monks began to have private property, and little by little always to fall into worse condition, until they wholly failed.
