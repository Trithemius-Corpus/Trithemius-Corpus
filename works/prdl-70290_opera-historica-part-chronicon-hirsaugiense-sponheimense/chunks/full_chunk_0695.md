*Of Johannes Trithemius.*

*VII.*

*Joachim, by the grace of God Margrave of Brandenburg, prince elector, to the reverend father Johann Trithemius, abbot of Saint [unclear].*

Our great love and goodwill toward you, most learned father, together with the recent memory of those evidences of pious learning which you lately presented to us, strongly impel, urge, and move us to write to you often, making known first of all by this present writing of ours the happy and unharmed condition of ourselves, and also of our wife, son, brother, and sister. We greatly grieve with your paternity on account of the envy of rivals who are stirring up adversities against you, as we understood from your last letter and received from the report of our messenger. Yet we trust that by the counsel of your wisdom, and likewise by divine aid, you will easily overcome all the contrivances of human depravity.

We have learned by an exceedingly bitter report that your once most learned teacher Libanius has laid down the burden of the flesh and rendered his spirit to the Most High who created him; concerning his death, if indeed he is dead, for we still hope that he is alive, [unclear] than if any one of our dearest friends had departed. We always loved and admired in him his distinguished learning, his inestimable knowledge of almost all writings, and the singular subtlety of his intellect. We ask your paternity, therefore, that if you find him dead, you apply every diligence regarding the books left by him, so that they not come into the hands of strangers, especially those which you understand to be useful to us. For we shall gladly pay whatever you decide must be paid.

Moreover, most loving teacher, we beseech you again and again, most urgently, that you not be reluctant to return to us around the feast of Easter or Pentecost. For, as you know, your service is necessary to us in many matters; without it we cannot long be lacking without detriment to our affairs. Write back to us your counsel on these matters through this messenger of ours, whom for that reason we have sent to you. But if you consent to our requests, as we have full confidence in you, indicate the time; we shall send the same courier back to you on the appointed day with the expenses necessary for the journey, nor will you depart from us unrewarded.

Nor should it escape you, uniquely beloved one, that the new teacher whom, on your persuasion, I admitted is doing well in all things, and I have some slight hope of greater progress from him in time. We have also marked on the schedule enclosed with this letter certain volumes of which we do not have a copy, asking that, as soon as you can, you refresh us with the reading of them. We also wish you to know that our sister will consummate the marriage contracted with the Duke of Mecklenburg here in Werder, with solemn celebration of the wedding, in this Shrovetide. Again we ask you that in all the foregoing matters you show yourself toward us such as the confidence we have in you expects; you will always have us grateful to you in all things. Farewell, loving and mindful of us; may the friendship long since contracted between us remain inviolate. Again farewell, and ever fare well. From our town of Tangermünde on the Elbe, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1506, on the day of Saint Burchard. And by our own hand.

*VIII.*

*To the most illustrious prince and lord, Lord Joachim, Margrave of the Brandenburgers, etc., Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James in the suburb of the city of Würzburg, but formerly of Sponheim, wishes happiness.*

I rightly rejoice greatly, most serene and most pious prince, in the well-being of you and yours; I have rejoiced at the prosperous successes of your affairs more than I could either say in person or write while absent. For the compassion of your excellence over my troubles I give and hold immortal thanks, and I shall repay them if ever I can by some opportunity.

I hope, moreover, that by prudent counsel I have at last escaped the envy of my rivals, with God’s help. And so we make known to your serenity, unconquered prince, that when I departed from your highness I did not return to Sponheim, because, although [unclear] seemed desired for my monastery, nevertheless certain contrivances on the part of my unfaithful monks intervened, by which I was provoked to such great weariness that, by a firm conclusion in my own mind, I resolved that henceforth I must by no means return to Sponheim. Therefore I remained outside, not without counsel, with my most serene prince Philip, Count Palatine, Duke of Bavaria, and prince elector, at [unclear] for four months, until at last the most reverend prelate of Würzburg had me called, elected, and confirmed to the abbey of Saint James, formerly of the Scots, in the suburb.
