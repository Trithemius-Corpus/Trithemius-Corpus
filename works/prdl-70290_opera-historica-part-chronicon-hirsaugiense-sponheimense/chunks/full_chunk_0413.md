In the same year the Count of Eisenburg, at the fair of St. Remigius in the city of Cologne, killed Lord Stephan, knight of Oirsbeck, publicly in a certain social joust, against his will. With his neck broken he suddenly breathed his last, and even when dead he did not fall, but remained upright, sitting in the saddle upon the horse, until he was taken down by the hands of his household; this was a sign of great strength.

In this same year, on the eighth day before the Ides of August, died Wilicho the Second, the thirteenth abbot of this monastery, a man sufficiently provident and experienced in the care of temporal affairs. If he had been able to live longer, he would without doubt have enlarged the revenues and possessions of the monastery. Nevertheless he did not take away private property from the monks, nor did he restore the division of revenues to common use, as he ought to have done.

*Concerning Wilhelm, the fourteenth abbot of this monastery of Sponheim, who presided for nine years, and concerning his deeds and the various events of that time.*

When, therefore, Lord Wilicho the Second, the twelfth abbot of this monastery, was dead, and his body had been committed to burial according to ecclesiastical custom, the brethren assembled in the chapter place to appoint a new shepherd over themselves. Having invoked the grace of the Holy Spirit, in the aforesaid year of the Lord’s nativity 1341, in the ninth indiction, on the day of St. Cyriacus and his companions, martyrs, which is the sixth day before the Ides of August, they unanimously elected Wilhelm of Beckelnheim, a professed monk of this community, a prudent man and, according to the custom of that time, sufficiently religious. Confirmed by Heinrich, archbishop of Mainz, and blessed by his vicar in pontificals, Rudolf, bishop of Salona, and adorned with the mitre according to custom, he presided for quite useful years and enlarged the revenues of the monastery according to his powers.

He came from the village of Beckelnheim, of honorable parents, and was born of a knightly stock, namely from that lineage under which the holy virgin Hildegard, foundress and abbess of the monastery of St. Rupert near Bingen, had once been born. In boyhood he was entrusted by his parents to letters; applying himself diligently to them, he became sufficiently learned according to his measure. Then, when he was twenty-three years old, under the rule of Johann and Simon, counts of Sponheim, he began to serve as a soldier, and for some years continued in that office of the military order. In the same year Count Sylvester of Daun, who had already for many years, not without very great loss to himself, carried on enmities against Balduin, archbishop of Trier, at last, wearied and utterly exhausted in strength, came of his own accord and not under compulsion to the palace of that same archbishop of Trier, sought and obtained mercy, and thereafter remained at peace. He publicly acknowledged that the castle of Droneck, with the village of Dalvang and the other revenues there, was held in fief from the church of Trier and the aforesaid archbishop, and as a sign of fidelity he accepted this same fief from his hand.
