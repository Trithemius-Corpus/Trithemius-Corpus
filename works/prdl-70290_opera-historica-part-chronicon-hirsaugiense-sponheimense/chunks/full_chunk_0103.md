*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

Religious and prudent, he enlarged the monastery entrusted to him, adorning it in no small measure in both estates.

Adelhelmus also, a monk of this Hirsau monastery, a learned and religious man, of whom someone sang thus:

This man by his conduct drew the loves of all,
And did not change his pious ways because of honors.

He was first appointed abbot of the monastery called Ammineberg; and then, after some years, having been transferred to the monastery of Mediolac in the diocese of Trier, he undertook, by public consent and at the request of all the monks dwelling there, the abbacy of that place to be governed, which he administered energetically and laudably all the way to the end of his life.

To the monastery of St. Peter in Erfurt, in the diocese of Mainz, after the death of Abbot Rupert, there was sent from this Hirsau monastery, in the year of the Lord 1128, under Abbot Volmar, a venerable monk named Vernerus, who, having been appointed abbot there, acquired many goods for that monastery by his own industry. With marvelous ingenuity and no small expense he began an aqueduct into the monastery and brought it to a sufficiently useful completion.

After the death also of Drutnuinus, first abbot in Breitenau, of whom we spoke above, Henricus, a monk of this Hirsau monastery, was appointed abbot of the place in the year of the Lord 1132, in the tenth indiction, under Abbot Volmar; of him more broadly must be said at the proper time.

Diethericus, however, a monk of this Hirsau monastery, under the same Abbot Volmar, was sent to the monastery of St. Felicitas near the city of Würzburg, which is called Schwarzach, at the insistence of its monks and the prayers of the bishop of Würzburg, and was ordained abbot.

Conradus, a monk of Hirsau, a very venerable man, was first made prior in the monastery of Schōrein, and then, ordained abbot of the monastery of Amorbach in the diocese of Würzburg, he notably enlarged it with buildings and revenues, and in his time magnificently renewed there the fervor of regular discipline, already almost collapsed.

Another Conradus of the same name, also a monk of this Hirsau monastery, was given as abbot to the monastery of Schwarzach beside the Rhine in the diocese of Strasbourg, at the petitions and insistence of the brothers and of the bishop of that place. He was a very religious man, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures.

Burchardus also, a monk noble by birth, but nobler in virtue of conduct and in learning of the Scriptures, was given as abbot far away to the monastery called Vinea, commonly Weingarten, in the diocese of Constance, in the time of Abbot Manegold.

Dithmarus, a man of reverend memory, after the death of Burchardus was sent to the same place from this Hirsau monastery and ordained abbot; by life and conduct he took care to show himself, in word and example, a worthy shepherd for the sheep of Christ.

Rupertus also, a venerable monk of this Hirsau monastery, was given as abbot to the monastery of Gottesaue in the diocese of Speyer under Abbot Manegold; because of the wickedness of his subjects, he at last resigned the abbacy and returned to Hirsau, and after the death of Manegold was elected abbot of this place in the year of the Lord 1165, in the tenth indiction. Of him we shall make suitably fuller mention at the proper time.

To the monastery of Tharis, which is situated in the parish of Würzburg, about five miles distant from Bamberg downstream along the Main, Rudigerus, a monk distinguished in conduct and learning, was given as abbot from this Hirsau monastery under the venerable Abbot Manegold.

To the monastery called Besselbornen, in the diocese of Augsburg, Lupoldus, a monk from this Hirsau monastery, was sent as abbot: a man not ignoble according to fleshly kinship, but by virtue of mind and knowledge of the Scriptures far nobler than many of the most noble.

Diepoldus, a monk of this monastery, a very studious man and an exceptional lover of books, was sent to the monastery in Schaffhausen, in the diocese of Constance, and solemnly ordained abbot.

To the monastery of Zwiefalten in the diocese of Constance, another Godfridus, also a monk of this Hirsau monastery, a man worthy of honor, was ordained abbot; he always most diligently strove to feed the flock entrusted to him by word and example.

Furthermore, to the monastery of Lorsch in the diocese [unclear], which the Premonstratensians inhabit today, Sigehardus, a monk from this Hirsau monastery, outstanding in life and conduct, was given as abbot; he magnificently restored that place in both estates.

*Sigehardus, abbot in Lorsch.*
