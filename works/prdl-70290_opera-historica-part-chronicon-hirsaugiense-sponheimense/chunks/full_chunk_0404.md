1322.

In the thirteenth year of Wilicho our abbot, a certain brother named Walter was burned at Cologne, the father and prince of heretics; he had long lain hidden in secret and infected many simple people with his errors. When he was put to torture, he could not be induced to reveal the sharers in his error.

In the same year Isabella, daughter of Philip IV, king of the French, wife of Edward, king of the English, seized that king, her husband, because of the devastation which he had wrought in the kingdom with his servants, and committed him to custody, in which he remained until death; nor by any threats or prayers could he get out.

1323.

In this year, which was the fourteenth of Wilicho our abbot, Frederick, duke of Austria, having gathered a great army of more than six thousand armed men from Hungary and Turkey, came against Louis the Bavarian, king of the Romans. Louis met him with a great military force, and, when battle had been joined between Mühldorf and Oettingen in Bavaria, Louis proved victor. Duke Frederick, captured by John, king of Bohemia, was presented to Louis on condition that he not be killed; Louis committed him to custody in a certain castle not far from Nuremberg. After some time, while Frederick remained thus in custody, a certain magician came to his brother, Duke Leopold, who was staying in Austria, promising that by his art he would snatch Duke Frederick the captive from custody and bring him back safely to Austria in one hour. The duke believed the master of the art, and promised that he would give whatever he asked if he matched his words with deeds. So both enter a circle, on a night appointed in advance. The master summons a spirit accustomed to come at his conjurations. It came in the form of a pilgrim man and received the command to take the duke out of custody and bring him back without delay to Austria. The spirit answered: “Your commands, good master, I will gladly obey, and I will free that captive duke and bring him back to you safe and unharmed, provided only that he does not refuse to come with me.” Immediately the spirit flew away and, in the same form, came by night to the captive duke, who was eating, saying: “Leopold your brother has sent me here to lead you out of the prison-house. Rise, then, quickly; mount this horse. I will lead you safe and unharmed to your brother.” The duke said to him, “Who are you?” The spirit answered: “Do not concern yourself who I am, but mount the horse, if you desire to be freed from this custody.” At once horror seized the duke and all who were with him; and when the sign of the cross had been made, the spirit vanished and returned empty-handed to the master. After this Duke Leopold harassed King Louis with iron and fire for so long that at last, when princes intervened and he was moved to mercy, he released the captive, after receiving from him a pledge that he would no further involve himself with the royal title. Thus Louis reigned, from the beginning of his election until his death, for almost thirty-three years; Frederick thereafter did not involve himself with the title of the kingdom of the Romans. This Louis was the fourth of that name. He had as wife Margaret, daughter of William, count of Hainaut, by whom he had six sons: namely Louis, margrave of Brandenburg; Stephen, duke of Bavaria in Landshut; Albert, duke of Bavaria in Straubing; William, duke of Holland, Zeeland, and Frisia in Breda; Louis, who was called the Roman because he had been born at Rome; and Otto of Brandenburg. And so through this empress Margaret the counties of Holland, Zeeland, Frisia, and Hainaut came to the dukes of Bavaria and counts palatine. For in the year 1345, as we shall say below, after her brother William had been killed by the Frisians, the empress, by decree of the princes, went down to Holland with a most splendid retinue, received homage from the vassals of the land and an oath of fidelity from the inhabitants, enlarged liberties and privileges for all, and made truces with the people of Utrecht. When these things had been done, she handed the county over to her son and returned to Bavaria. William, the father of this empress Margaret, the aforesaid count of Holland, died in the year of the Lord 1436; William his son, brother of the empress, succeeding him, was later killed by the Frisians, as we have said.

1324.

In the fifteenth year of Wilicho our abbot, and the two-hundredth from the first entry of the brothers into this monastery of Sponheim, Baldwin, archbishop of Trier, John, king of Bohemia, who at that time was staying at Luxembourg, and also the duke of Lorraine and the count of Bar, making alliance with one another, besieged the city of Metz with a great army, laying waste and devastating everything around.
