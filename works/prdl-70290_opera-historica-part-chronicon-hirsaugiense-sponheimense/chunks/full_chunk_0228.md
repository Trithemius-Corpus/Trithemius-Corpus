*Of the Monastery of Hirsau*

In the same year Henry, bishop of Strasbourg, also died; Berthold, a prudent man, a vigorous defender and supporter of the Church, succeeded him. In that same year also King Henry, son of Emperor Frederick, held a court of princes at [Nordhausen?], where Engelbert, archbishop of Cologne, labored greatly for the liberation of the king of Denmark.

In the same year, as Philip, king of France, was dying, he bequeathed one hundred and fifty thousand marks for the aid of the Holy Land. In this same year also Pope Honorius, sending messengers everywhere, ordered the cross to be preached to the faithful, announcing to all that after two years, on the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist, they should be ready with Emperor Frederick to cross the sea and again liberate the Holy Land.

In the same year also Pope Honorius III, with the consent of the cardinals, confirmed the Order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis on the third day before the Kalends of December, two years before the death of that same holy confessor of Christ.

*1223.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Eberhard, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1224, Conrad, bishop of Speyer, died. Berengar of Entringen succeeded him, the forty-fourth bishop of the church of Speyer, namely on the sixth day before the Kalends of April; he presided over it for almost nine years.

In the same year Theoderic, bishop of Würzburg, died. Hermann of Ladenburg succeeded him and presided for almost twenty-six years; he endured many pressures and persecutions from his citizens on account of the excommunicated Emperor Frederick, but always obtained victory.

In the same year also, around the middle of the month of May, King Henry held a court of princes at Frankfurt, where messengers of the emperor were present with his letters, announcing that he would come from Sicily to Germany as quickly as possible, and would hold counsel with the princes concerning the recovery of the Holy Land and the maintaining of possession by force.

In the same year Pope Honorius sent as legate into Germany Conrad, bishop of Porto and cardinal of Santa Rufina. As he was about to go down to Cologne, he made his journey through Swabia, and came with his men to Hirsau, where he was received with honor by Abbot Eberhard and the brothers.

At the same time Henry, with the aforesaid legate of the Apostolic See, Engelbert archbishop of Cologne, and certain princes, set out for Saxony for the liberation of the king of Denmark and the restoration of the land of the empire unjustly possessed by him. There that same king, with his son, was being held captive by Count Henry of Schwerin in the very strong castle of Dannenberg. When the aforesaid counts came to the river Elbe, they sent ahead Hermann, master of the Hospital of the Teutons, who had been sent by the emperor into Germany for the sake of reconciliation and of making an agreement between both parties, namely the king and the count. Through his mediation, the same king was induced to restore all the land taken from the empire, to receive the crown of the kingdom of Denmark from the hand of the emperor, and furthermore to promise to give one hundred thousand marks for his liberation. When the princes who were present with the king accepted this, Albert, count of Orlamünde, [unclear] son of the king, and the barons of Denmark rejected this agreement, annulled what had been promised, and, boarding ships, departed in indignation, taking with them the immense sum of money that they had brought to redeem the king. And the aforesaid princes, the affair unfinished, returned in confusion to their own lands.

*1225.*

In the ninth year of Abbot Eberhard, the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1225, King Henry, son of Emperor Frederick II, held a court of princes at Frankfurt, in which he dealt with many matters.

In the same year, after the Nativity of the Lord, Count Albert of Schwerin resolved to attack; Henry met him, and when battle had been joined with great fierceness and much bloodshed, they fought from the first hour of the day until evening, with many falling there. There also Count Albert himself, with many nobles of Denmark, was captured by Count Henry and committed to custody in the castle of Dannenberg.

In this same year the winter was very long and very harsh. There was also a great and unheard-of famine, lasting for two years, in every province.

In the same year also, on the Ides of September, Siegfried, archbishop of Mainz, the second of that name, died; Siegfried III succeeded him and presided for twenty-four years, a man [unclear].
