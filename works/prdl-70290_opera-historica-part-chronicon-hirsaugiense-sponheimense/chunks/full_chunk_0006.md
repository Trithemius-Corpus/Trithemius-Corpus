*The Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*The foundation of the monastery of Hirsau, made by Count Erlafrid and Notting, his son, bishop of Vercelli, in the year of the Lord's incarnation 830.*

[unclear]

With God's help intending to write the history of the distinguished monastery of Hirsau, I have thought it worthwhile to begin from its first foundation, and to show what cause moved the founders themselves to undertake this matter. At the time when Louis the First governed the empire of the Romans, Erlafrid, count of Calw, was a rich and powerful man with the emperor. He had a son named Notting, a man learned in every doctrine of the Scriptures, who, because of his knowledge of letters and the honorableness of his life, had obtained the bishopric of the church of Vercelli under the Emperor Charlemagne, father of Louis. After he had ruled his church well for some time according to the canonical ordinances, there came upon him a sweet desire to see his homeland; and, lest he should seem to have returned to it empty-handed, he began to consider what special gift he might bring with him by which he could make it more illustrious.

Indeed, not long before, at very great cost he had obtained the relics of the most holy bishop of the [Rediciani?], Aurelius Armenius, more precious than jasper and gold; and, carrying them to his own church of Vercelli, he placed them in a very beautiful mausoleum which he had prepared. And so, when he had resolved, on going to see his homeland and his dear parents, to bring this treasure to the Germans, wavering in mind between hope and fear lest this removal should perhaps displease Saint Aurelius, or, if it were learned by the people of the church of Vercelli, he should be forced to draw back from his purpose, he committed the whole matter to be settled by divine counsel. Therefore, turning his whole mind to God, and lying sleepless prostrate on the ground beside the tomb of Saint [unclear] the most gentle Aurelius, he addressed Aurelius himself, the divine friend of Christ, with burning desire and tears, saying: Holy father Aurelius, my glory, and after God the protection of my soul, to you I lay open the desire of your servant, which, being joined to God, you undoubtedly cannot fail to know. I beg you, by the divine love which has made you venerable to me, celebrated in the Church, and one with God, holy and immortal, do not permit me to waver longer in this uncertainty, but hear me, your servant prostrate before you, and teach me, that I may know what I ought to do according to God's will and yours.

When he had persevered for a long time in prayer, in tears and lying on the ground, wearied by prayer and the prolonging of his vigils, he fell asleep for a little while. To him as he slept, divine Aurelius, clothed in pontifical vestments and more splendid than the sun, soon stood by; and, addressing Notting by name, said: Peace be with you, brother Notting. Rise; do not weep. I have come for this purpose, that I may go with you into Germany, to bring more peoples there to Christ when dead than if, still living among the nations and preaching, I were shining with miracles. When you come there, you shall establish a monastery of servants of God in the place where you shall suddenly behold a blind man meeting us restored to sight. Having said these things, he vanished.

But the venerable bishop Notting, rising from sleep, joyfully opened the sarcophagus in secret at once in that very night, and secretly carried the relics themselves of the most sacred bishop Aurelius home. The mausoleum, most carefully closed again, deceived the people of Vercelli for a long time, since they thought they had a guest whom they did not have. But Bishop Notting, when all things had been prepared which seemed to pertain to the plan of the journey, took as companions of so great a pilgrimage certain of the more faithful among his household; and, placing on a pack-animal the little bundle containing the sacred relics of Aurelius, accompanied by the best guide for the journey, he entered upon the road he had set before him.

And when, having happily entered Germany, he came after some days into his own homeland, he was received with great joy by his people; and what a treasure he was bringing into the region he showed to his father and to all the other inhabitants of the land, while absent by letters, and while present by words and by the display of the deed. But since there was not at hand a place consecrated by the gift of blessing and worthy of so great a guest, and since they were unwilling to keep such venerable relics beyond three days [unclear], rising early in the morning Bishop Notting with his household, and Erlafrid his father with his other son, Ermefrid, the pontiff's brother, together with very many of the more noble [unclear].
