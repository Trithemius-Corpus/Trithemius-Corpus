*1382.*

In the eighth year of Abbot Craffto, the king of France and the count of Flanders besieged Ghent; and although they had twice emerged victorious, nevertheless the men of Ghent advanced against them to battle and forced them to flee to the city of Bruges. Then, surrounding the city of Ghent with a siege, they took it at the sixth hour after midday, on the Sabbath day, when the blood of the Lord’s circumcision is carried in procession. There more than seventy-seven thousand men were said to have fallen. The count of Flanders, having slipped away in flight, scarcely [escaped?]; the king of France, suddenly gone mad, raged.

In the same year Lupold, duke of Austria, went out to war against the Swiss; defeated by them, he was killed together with many other nobles and commoners, and many were captured.

*1383.*

In the ninth year of Craffto our abbot, Johannes of Sponheim, brother of Count Heinrich, died without children.

In the same year, on the seventh day before the Kalends of August, Wernher of Dille died, a monk of this monastery, who three years earlier, with Count Heinrich and with the permission of his abbot, as we have said, had set out overseas to the sepulchre of the Lord.

Also in the same year, on the eighth day before the Ides of December, Wenceslaus, duke of Brabant and Luxembourg, brother of Emperor Heinrich IV, died. He had governed Brabant for twenty-seven years, and was buried with great pomp in the monastery of the Blessed Virgin Mary near Luxembourg, which is an abbey of our order. After his death his wife, who was the daughter of Johann III, duke of Brabant, ruled those lands for almost twenty-two years with the aid of Arnold of Horne, bishop of Liège, whom she had lifted from the sacred font of baptism.

*1384.*

In this year, which was the [unclear] of Abbot Craffto, Johannes Frie died, a monk of this monastery, who held the office of cellarer for many years.

In the same year, on the seventh day before the Ides of August, Hermann Stumpf died, a monk and custodian here. After the death of Johann, the elder cellarer mentioned above, Abbot Craffto appointed as cellarer Emerich, a monk of this community, son of Philipp Viner, knight of Beckelnheim, who previously had been rent-collector of this monastery for seven years.

*1385.*

In this year Abbot Craffto sold this court [unclear] for florins to Heinrich Wolff of Sponheim, knight; thus alienated, it remained apart from the monastery until the time of Abbot Johannes Trithemius, for one hundred years. He redeemed half the court, but was compelled to give up the other half, as we shall say in its place.

In the same year, on the eighth day before the Ides of September, Philipp Viner, knight of Beckelnheim, died, father of Emerich the monk and cellarer, a singular benefactor of this monastery.

Also in the same year Wilhelm, count of Holland, son of Albert, duke of Bavaria, and grandson of Emperor Ludwig IV, took as wife the daughter of Philipp, duke of Burgundy, and celebrated the wedding with great pomp and notable solemnity.

*1386.*

In the twelfth year of Craffto our abbot, a very great abundance of wine grew, so that at vintage-time, because of the plentifulness, vessels were lacking; a cartload was sold for one florin, and a vessel holding a cartload of wine for three florins.

In the same year, on the first day of the month of March, which was the fifth feria of Sexagesima, [unclear] leaving Worms [unclear] of our abbot, whose wife Catharina delivered to us certain goods from his legacy. He was buried in our church before the altar of St. Margaret, virgin and martyr.

In the same year, on the ninth day before the Kalends of March, Johannes, called Reyle, a professed monk of this monastery, died, and is buried in the cloister of the church.
