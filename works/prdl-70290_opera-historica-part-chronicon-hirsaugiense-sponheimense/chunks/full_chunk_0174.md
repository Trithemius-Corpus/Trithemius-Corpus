But also through priests, philosophers, and masters, both from remote and from nearer places, not a few letters were sent, to which responsive letters of hers, by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit and full of mysteries and secrets, are joined.

For she was accustomed to aid all who sought from her the consolation of the Holy Spirit, or asked for a word of holy admonition, by writings and prayers; and she never despised anyone, however weak, lowly, or poor. She was renowned for many miracles both in life and after death, and by her prayers in the name of Christ she cured the sick, burdened in whatever way, who flocked to her. In this same year there was a severe famine, and a great mortality of men followed. Arnold, archbishop of Cologne, died; Arnold the second, provost of the major church and chancellor of the king, succeeded him in the pontificate. In this same year there also died Conrad, landgrave of Thuringia, and Frederick, archbishop of Magdeburg; Wichmann, son of the count of Tegenburg in Bavaria, succeeded him, governed for forty-two years quite usefully, and was buried in the monastery of Husburg of our order.

*1152.*

In the thirty-first year of Abbot Volmar, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1152, Conrad the third, king of the Romans, lying ill in the city of Bamberg and sensing that the day of his death was near, handed the royal insignia to Duke Frederick, his brother’s son, commended to him his own son Frederick, still a little child, and asked him to speak to the princes about acquiring the kingdom for him. Having arranged his affairs, he died on the second day before the Kalends of March, in the fourteenth year of his reign; he was buried in the same city and did not receive the imperial benediction from the pope. After his death, a celebrated assembly of princes and bishops was held at Frankfurt. There, with the highest favor of all, the aforesaid Frederick, duke of Swabia, was elected king of the Romans, a vigorous man and one of many works, as we shall partly say in what follows. He reigned as emperor for thirty-seven years.
