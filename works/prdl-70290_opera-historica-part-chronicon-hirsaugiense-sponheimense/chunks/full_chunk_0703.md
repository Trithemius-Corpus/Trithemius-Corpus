*Familiar Letters*

You know, and therefore there is no need for us to write much about him. For this reason they did not notify us in time of the day of the election, so that we too might have been present for guidance and counsel. If any man can know the characters of men, none of the monks of Spanheim was unknown to us. But Prior Melantius, knowing how great the evils were that he had committed against us, dreaded our presence, fearing that he could not obtain the abbacy if the election had been held with our counsel. Therefore, if any evil has followed upon this election, let it be assigned to the visitors, who neglected to summon us when they ought to have done so. You write that, after the election had already been completed, they departed from the monastery in indignation, because, forestalled by the brothers’ conspiracy, they were unable to appoint an abbot suitable for the place. We do not wonder that they were forestalled by the brothers’ fraud and scheming, since both of them are altogether inexperienced in the actions they had presumed to undertake. For the one, the abbot of Seligenstadt, accustomed to stay at home, has never been engaged in public affairs of the order. The other, however, the abbot of Bischofsberg, what is he but a skin without understanding, puffed up with banquets and cups? By foolish and ignorant prattling he is accustomed to throw into confusion and overturn whatever he undertakes. But since we cannot take back what has been done, let us pass over it in silence. For the Lord God is able, through mercy, to raise up the fallen, so that the elected man himself, with whom now no one seems to be content, as divine grace advances in him, may become much better than anyone could have judged. Nor will our exhortation be lacking; but as soon as we have the opportunity of a reliable messenger, we shall take care, for the love of Christ, to urge him by our letters toward better things. Farewell. From Würzburg, on the fourteenth day of the month of January, in the year of the Christians 1507.

*XIIII.*

*Joachim, by the grace of God Margrave of Brandenburg, prince elector, to John Trithemius, abbot of St. James at Würzburg, greeting.*

[unclear]

Reverend father and most beloved teacher, with gracious affection we congratulate you on the new abbey which you have lately received, in which, as we hope, you will have greater peace than it is agreed you had in Spanheim. We devoutly and humbly beseech almighty God that, according to his most holy will, he may long preserve your paternity in it healthy, fortunate, and unharmed, just as from our heart we favor you. We very much desire to know what has happened concerning your teacher Libanius, whether he is still alive or has departed from human affairs. But if he is dead, we earnestly ask your paternity not to sleep over the acquisition of his books. For you know how useful and necessary they are to us. In your last letter to us you promised that, as quickly as you could, you would complete those things which we requested to be published by you. We ask that, in your accustomed manner, you apply diligence, and that as soon as you have completed the things agreed upon, you not be slow to send them as quickly as possible, after hiring a faithful messenger at our expense. We send you, O our glory, one cask of salted pike and two of herrings; we ask that you receive them with a grateful mind, not as a gift but as a remembrance of us. This time, however, we were unable to have a supply of sturgeon and [unclear]; otherwise we would have sent those to you too very gladly. For whatever good we shall be able to bestow upon your paternity, we are prepared to do with singular favor; your fidelity, diligence, and munificence, abundantly shown to us, deserve that we do this. For we would rightly be charged with the fault of ingratitude if we seemed to forget your kindnesses toward us. With the most fervent desire, moreover, we long for your return to us around the feast of Pentecost, or sooner if it could be done; and we shall take diligent care that this happen without inconvenience to you. For we have many things to discuss with your paternity which we cannot entrust to letters. But so that, for this very matter, you may have suitable access to your lord the bishop, and our friend, since you cannot come without his consent, we send you our letters to him, in which we earnestly ask that he consent to us concerning your return to us, not at all doubting that your paternity will most diligently cooperate with us before this same friend of ours for the obtaining of this matter. Whatever you finally obtain, please write back to us through a messenger to be hired at our expense, and you will have us ready to gratify you in all things according to your wish. We desire that your paternity fare most happily. From our Ursulum, on the day of Saint Anthony the abbot, with our own hand, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation one thousand five hundred seven.
