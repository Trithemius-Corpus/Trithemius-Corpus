*The Hirsau Monastery*

*Diemar the knight becomes a monk and gives all his possessions.*

... of Helfenstein, of Count Hugo of Tubingen and Ulrich of Lustenau, Reginbodo of Tallacker, Mascelinus of Rupburg, and very many others under the same venerable abbot Bruno. Diemar also, a knight, noble, rich, powerful, and very famous among the princes of Eastern Francia, having his more usual residence in the diocese of Wurzburg, in the county of Mergentheim, at Rutingen beside the river Tauber, drawn by divine inspiration, gave by solemn donation to this our monastery of Hirsau all that belonged to him by right, both in that same place Rutingen and in other places, for the remedy of his own soul and of the souls of his parents. And at last, offering himself to God and to his holy apostles Peter and Paul as a perpetual servant, under Abbot Gebhard he received the monastic habit in the present monastery, and persevered in holy conduct until death. This solemn donation of Diemar the knight was made in the time of Abbot Gebhard, in the year of the Lord 1103, in the second indiction, under Emperor Henry IV, on the day of St. Prisca, virgin and martyr, in the presence of Count Ebo of Mergentheim and many other witnesses.

Another knight also, of no obscure family, from the village of Merlenheim, named Conrad, was made [unclear] in this monastery of Hirsau under the venerable Abbot Bruno; before he had [unclear] the holy monastery, he handed over to this monastery, by solemn donation made upon the altar of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, all the goods that he possessed by hereditary right, in the presence of Abbot Bruno himself and the whole congregation, with many others. The confirmation of this donation was made in the presence of Bruno, bishop of Speyer, Egbert, count of Speyer, Stephen, brother of this Conrad, and many others.

In the year of the Lord 1109, while Emperor Henry V was reigning, Hermann, count of Leiningen, and Diemar, count of Brachfeld, for themselves and for all their kin, handed over to God and blessed Peter, as the perpetual right of the monastery of Hirsau, one hundred hides in Roth and in the little surrounding villages in the bishopric of Augsburg, on this condition, namely, that by the provision of the abbot of the same monastery the service of God according to the Rule of St. Benedict should be established there. And this was done. For in the same place a monastery was built by the work and expenses of the abbot of Hirsau, with the help of the faithful round about, in which brothers sent from this monastery established the regular life, having over them a provost with [unclear], going out and coming in, [unclear] to the abbots.

In the time of the same Abbot Bruno there was a certain citizen of Speyer named Bebo, a very rich man and a very great lover of the brothers of Hirsau, who for the remedy of his own soul and of the souls of his parents did many good things for this monastery. For at various times, either for relieving the needs of the brothers or for acquiring estates, both in his lifetime and at his final departure, he bestowed on this monastery more, in the value of pure silver, than 450 marks, as can be found more clearly in the book of donations. William also of Hissigheim [unclear] bestowed on the monastery, in the presence of Bruno and the whole congregation. This donation he afterwards confirmed anew for perpetual stability at Ingersheim, in a public court of laymen, in the presence of the same Abbot Bruno, Count Adelbert, and many others. Count Frederick also, brother of St. Otto, bishop of Bamberg, bestowed certain estates on this monastery of Hirsau in perpetuity by solemn donation in the time of Abbot Bruno. Abbot Bruno himself also, in the time of his rule, acquired many goods, estates, rents, and revenues for the advantage of this monastery through the hands of his brothers, to whom after him the chief management of affairs had been entrusted. Many counts also, nobles, citizens, and villagers, in his time bestowed many goods on this monastery; their names are written in the book of donations of the monastery [unclear], handing themselves over to the Hirsau monastery for the sake of the kingdom of heaven, and taking up the habit of holy conversion, and becoming monks, they remained until death in regular observance.

In the thirteenth year of Abbot Bruno, which was the year 1119 from the Lord’s nativity, in the twelfth indiction, Drutwin, a monk of this monastery of Hirsau, was ordained the first abbot at Breitenau, in the diocese of Mainz in Hesse, concerning whose foundation it was said above. Sent by Abbot Bruno at the request of Count Werner the founder, he came with twelve monks to the aforesaid monastery of Breitenau on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of December, and presided for fourteen years: a man venerable in character and learning, and an outstanding founder, propagator, and preserver of regular discipline.
