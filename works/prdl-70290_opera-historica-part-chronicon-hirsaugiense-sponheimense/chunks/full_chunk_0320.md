After all these things had been observed in due order, we solemnly consecrated and appointed Lord Eigelward as abbot over the same place, granting and confirming that whenever a father of the monastery is to be ordained for the same brothers, only the man whom they shall have unanimously chosen by common counsel and consent, at the prompting of the Holy Spirit, shall be invested by the archbishop. And so that the sequence of this matter might remain stable and unshaken, we have signed this page with the impression of our image. But if anyone shall attempt to break what has been so lawfully done, we strike him with the sword of anathema until he comes to his senses. The witnesses are Wernbold, abbot of St. James, Henry of the greater [church?], Cuno the custodian and dean, and very many others. These things were done in this year from the Incarnation of the Lord 1130, in the eighth indiction, while Lothar, most glorious king of the Romans, was reigning, in the fourth year of his reign.

On the western side of the aforesaid church of St. John a reclusory was built, in which virgins wishing to serve Christ, almighty God, in purity of mind and body were received under obedience to the abbot and under the rule of our holy father Benedict. Day and night they devoted themselves to prayer and contemplation, and the abbot of the place provided them with the necessities of food and clothing. There also the daughter of the aforesaid Richolf, count of the Rheingau, as is noted in the archbishop’s privilege, was enclosed with the other virgins. But after many years, the holy nuns and virgins succeeding them in order were transferred from the mountain into the valley, for the quiet both of themselves and of the brothers; a monastery with the necessary dwellings had first been built for them, which is called Clusa down to the present day, because virgins were enclosed there.

Around these times also the monastery of our order called Groinavy, within the borders of the diocese of Trier, not far from Schonaw, about which we have already spoken above, was founded by the count of Lurenburg. In it the head of St. Sebastian the martyr is shown, which is said to have been brought there by the count.

*1133.*

In the year 1132, the ninth of Bernhelm, the monastery of the Cistercian order in the Rheingau, called Eberbach, almost two miles distant from Mainz, was built by Adelbert the elder, archbishop of Mainz.

In these same times Abbot Bernhelm, led by singular foresight, acquired a house and courtyard in Mainz for the use of this monastery, so that, if necessity should perhaps arise, we might use it for the preservation of the goods.

In the year 1135 Abbot Bernhelm obtained by his prayers from Count Megenhard the stream flowing around the castle of Sponheim, and the whole right of fishing, shared with him and his heirs, from that castle downward as far as the middle of the road toward Wimesheim, so that none of the ministerials living around it or of any others whatever, except only the domestic ministerials of the count and of the abbot, ought to fish in it. From the castle upward the fishing right is the count’s, and then to the beginning of the wood, where the boundary of those belonging to the castle extends. In these times the monastery of regular canons at Frankenthal [unclear] as far as Buggenau further on, and thence subsequently as far as the new church, today Naunkirch. The fishing belongs to the abbot by the gift of the aforesaid Udo. *The monastery of Frankenthal is founded.*

He appointed a mistress, and both were renowned for miracles. In the year 1136 Lutta, a most chaste virgin, daughter of Stephen, count of Sponheim, and sister of Count Megenhard, our founder, rested in the Lord on the second day before the Kalends of January. She was buried in the monastery of St. Disibod, beside the great altar toward the south, where for love of the heavenly kingdom she had been enclosed with certain other virgins for twenty-four years in a strict, although [unclear], service to the Lord.
