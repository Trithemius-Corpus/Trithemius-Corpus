1526.

*On Johannes of Siemern, twenty-seventh abbot of this monastery of Sponheim.*

Nicholas, abbot of Sponheim, died [on the Kalends of July?] in the present age, a pious and merciful man, devoted to all religion and honor. He never saddened anyone by his own presumption, nor did he burden the monastery with the expenses of his person; rather, he maintained regular discipline excellently, both in himself and in his subjects, until the end of his life. He was buried in the middle of the choir, amid the outpouring of many tears by his brothers.

After the death of the reverend lord Nicholas, abbot, the abbey of Sponheim was without a shepherd for nine days. When these had been completed, two abbots arrived: Johannes, abbot of St. James at Mainz, and Frederick, abbot of St. John in the Rheingau, called by us for this purpose according to the ordinances of our chapter.

Therefore, in the aforesaid year 1526, on 4 July, around the eighth hour, the prior with the brothers of our convent betook himself to the chapter place, with the aforesaid prelates presiding and witnesses and notaries also present. After the invocation of the gift of the Holy Spirit had first been offered, and after a canonical summons had first been held, as is customary, he began the election by way of scrutiny in the name of God.

Thus, when the votes of the individual brothers had been heard, the greater part of the brothers directed their votes, recorded by the notary, to brother Johannes of Siemern, at that time prior. For when the election was proclaimed, all consented to him, and they subscribed with their own hand to the decree of election. The man elected was, in the same year, on the [unclear] day next after the feast of St. James the Apostle, confirmed by Albert, cardinal of the Apostolic See and archbishop of Mainz; and on the same day he received the office of blessing from Bishop Johannes, vicar in pontificals of the aforesaid lord Albert, in the church of the Mount of St. James, with the aforesaid abbots assisting in pontificals.

*The family and origin of Johannes, our abbot.*

This Johannes Siemern was, by native country, of the [Cynonoti?],* born of honorable parents in a certain town, [Argenthal?], not far distant from the city of Siemern. His father, named Peter, was from the said town; his mother, named [Helena?], came from the village of Mirsbach. They sought their livelihood by rustic labor. Johannes himself, in his boyhood years, was entrusted by his parents to scholastic letters, to which he devoted himself until his twenty-third year. In that year he entered the monastery, with no mortal person urging him on. He lived under regular discipline for nineteen years, and, promoted to the priestly [office], as a preacher of the divine word for sixteen years [unclear] with vigilant zeal. And now, made abbot, he presides for as long as He shall will who alone governs all things; to whom be praise and glory forever and ever.

* Hunsrucker.
