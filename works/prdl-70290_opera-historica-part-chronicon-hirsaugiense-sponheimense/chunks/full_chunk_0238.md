*1235.*

In the year [unclear], with 4 subtracted, which was the Sunday of the Nativity, 1235, Henry the king, son of the emperor, hearing that his father would shortly come into Germany, and fearing him, began to seek the friendships of princes and cities for himself; and beginning from Basel, he received hostages from all the cities with which he was confederated in Alsace, as a precaution, lest they should fall away from him when the emperor came, but should remain firm with him against his father in the conspiracy already begun until the end.

But the emperor, returning from Apulia around the Nativity of John the Baptist, came through Austria into Germany. Soon, after sending his messengers throughout the whole kingdom, he summoned the princes to an assembly to be held at Mainz on the feast of the Assumption of the blessed Mary ever virgin. Meanwhile Henry the king, who because of fear of his father was staying in the very strongly fortified castle of Driuels, at the persuasion of Hermann, master of the hospital of the Teutonic Knights, came to his father at Worms on the Nones of July, where he was with certain princes. He immediately placed him in custody, first at Worms, in a very strong house near the church of Saint Andrew the Apostle; then in Heidelberg castle, under the custody of Otto, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria. Afterwards he was led from Heidelberg to Alzey, and finally, sent to Sicily, he was held in close custody until death for five years: God rendering to him the way according to his merits, since he had made the noble kingdom of Germany common to robbers, had violently oppressed religious and ecclesiastical men with unjust services and exactions, and had exposed the province to such plundering by the impious that there was now no security of peace for travellers.

When the feast of the Assumption of the blessed Mary ever virgin arrived, a very great multitude of princes, bishops, abbots, counts, and nobles from all parts of Germany assembled at Mainz according to the emperor’s edict. There the emperor, crowned on the octave of the feast of the Assumption, proceeded and ordained many things for the peace and advantage of the empire. In this assembly there are those who testified that more than twelve thousand noblemen and soldiers had gathered. In the same assembly of princes the discord which had lasted for almost eight years between the emperor Frederick and Otto, duke of Saxony, was settled by the counsel of friends. For when Henry, count palatine, who was also lord of Brunswick and Lueneburg, had died eight years before and left two daughters, Otto, duke of Bavaria, taking the first daughter, Gertrude, as wife, as was said above, obtained with her the county palatine of the Rhine; but the margrave of Baden took the second as wife, with whom he received the lordship of Brunswick as dowry, which he sold to the aforesaid emperor Frederick for a certain sum of money. And when the emperor had sent his men to Saxony to take possession of the lordship newly purchased, Otto, duke of Saxony and Lueneburg, taking it very ill that the inheritance of his forefathers, which they had possessed by right for a long time, had come into the emperor’s hands, constantly opposed himself. By violent force he compelled the soldiers sent by him to leave Brunswick.

*The duke comes into the emperor’s favor.*

*Albert of Anhalt becomes duke of Saxony.*

But Duke Otto, persuaded by the counsel of friends, came into the emperor’s favor in the aforesaid assembly of princes at Mainz, where concord between them was confirmed in this manner, with the consent of the princes: that the same Duke Otto, for himself and his heirs, should hold in perpetuity the duchy of Brunswick and Lueneburg. Further, the emperor transferred the title of the duchy of Saxony to Albert of Anhalt, who was in the county of Lauenburg, creating him duke of Saxony and prince elector.
