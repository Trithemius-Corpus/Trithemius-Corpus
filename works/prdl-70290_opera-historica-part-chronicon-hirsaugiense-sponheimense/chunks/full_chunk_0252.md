*Chronicle of Johannes Trithemius.*

*Rudolf is elected king of the Romans.*

Therefore the princes, assembling in the aforesaid year at Frankfurt, after many and various negotiations, at last unanimously elected as king of the Romans Rudolf, count of Habsburg, who was then absent and besieging Basel with a great army. As soon as he heard that he had been elected king of the Romans, he raised the siege and came with great display to Frankfurt; going down with the princes to Aachen, he received the rite of consecration in the customary manner. There the princes, after swearing peace and obedience into the hands of the new king, received their fiefs from him, promising him firm and faithful assistance, to the best of their powers, against all men.

*Rudolf reigned nineteen years.*

Thus Rudolf, having received the kingdom, reigned vigorously for eighteen years and ten months. He restored peace in the land, checked robbers, and destroyed many of their strongholds and fortifications; he repaired, as far as he could, the fallen empire, and in a short time recovered by his labor and industry many things that had been alienated from the kingdom. He had as wife Elisabeth, daughter of Burchard, count of Haigerloch, by whom he begot three sons and four daughters.

*The sons of King Rudolf.*

The first son was Albert, whom he afterwards made duke of Austria; he succeeded Adolf in the kingdom of the Romans, as we shall say in its place. From him our illustrious king Maximilian is descended, together with all the dukes of Austria who have lived in the meantime. The second son, Hartmann, was drowned in the Rhine not far from Basel. The third son was Rudolf, count of Habsburg and duke of Swabia, who married the daughter of Ottokar, king of Bohemia.

The first of the daughters married Wenceslaus, brother of the king of Bohemia. The second, named Mechthild, married Louis, count palatine of the Rhine. The third married Albert, duke of Saxony. The fourth married Charles, king of Apulia. Thus Rudolf, the renowned king, advancing with his army from city [to city?], was everywhere received as the true lord.

*Count Albert, father of the king.*

The father of King Rudolf was Albert, count of Habsburg, who had once been landgrave of Alsace and captain of the city of Strasbourg, a warlike man, of whom many wars are reported to have been bravely fought. After his death Rudolf, rising in his place, like a lion in the midst of shepherds, so overcame all his adversaries without fear. While still a count, by military force he claimed the county of Kyburg, Baden, and Mersburg from the hand of the count of Savoy, and he obtained Molenberg, Grötzingen, Durlach, and a great part of Swabia.

*1274.*

In the ninth year of Abbot John, which was the year of the Lord's birth 1274, Eberhard, count of Württemberg, the count of Montfort, the count of Helfenstein, and the count of Dockenberg, with eleven other counts and certain nobles, having formed a conspiracy, strove with all their might either to capture or to kill King Rudolf. Against them he led a great army; he defeated them all and bound them forever to obedience to himself and to the empire, and [unclear] peace in [unclear]. [Asked when he would set out for Rome] with an army for the crown of the Roman Empire, he would answer: “Italy has consumed many kings of the Germans. I shall not go to Rome. I am king, and I trust that I shall do no less for the benefit of the commonwealth than if I had been crowned at Rome.” Reigning, therefore, for almost nineteen years, as we have said, he did not receive the crown of the empire from the supreme pontiff, for the reason he stated.

*The king's expedition into Bohemia.*

In the tenth year of Abbot John, and in the year of the Lord's birth [unclear], around the feast of the Assumption of the Blessed Mary ever virgin, King Rudolf set out with a great army into Bohemia against King Ottokar, who was occupying lands of the empire. For when the duke of Austria had died in the preceding year [without?] children, and that duchy itself, together with the counties of Tyrol and Carinthia, was vacant to the Roman Empire, the aforesaid king of the Bohemians invaded and occupied those lands, alleging a certain hereditary right, without the authority of the empire. But Rudolf, raised to be king of the Romans, admonished him by letter to restore to the empire what had been unjustly taken away, or else to expect danger. Scorning his commands, he said to those standing by: “Behold a new thing: the count of Habsburg, who was the captain of our army and the servant of our majesty, has sent commands to us as if he were lord. By our crown, we shall not be his obedient subjects.”

*The duchy of Austria is vacant to the empire. Rudolf defeated the king of Bohemia.*

Moved by this, King Rudolf led an army into Bohemia, defeated him in war, and compelled him to terms of subjection. Later, as we shall say, when he violated his promises, Rudolf defeated him again in battle in the fourth year of his reign, killed him, and gave the duchy of Austria to his son.

*Pope Gregory died. Innocent V sits for six months.*

In the same year Pope Gregory X died and was buried at Arezzo; Innocent V succeeded him in the pontificate and presided for only six months, an excellent man, from whom all good things were hoped. He had previously been called Peter of Tarentaise, of the Order of Friars Preachers of St Dominic, a most learned theologian, who wrote most profoundly on the four books of the Sentences and certain other works; and when he became pope he strove with all zeal to restore peace in Italy.
