*Chronicle of Trithemius*

... was promoted to the dignity, and finally under Abbot Henry, who succeeded Wilicho (as we said above), he was appointed the twentieth prior of this monastery. When he had completed twelve years in that office, after Wilhelm, as it is, he was ordained abbot. For he was a prudent man of many occupations, who would gladly have reformed the monastery in both estates, if he had been able. Once ordained abbot, he appointed after himself as prior, the twenty-first in the order of succession, Gerlacus, a professed monk of this monastery, a man sufficiently mature in life and morals for the quality of that time, elderly in age, weak and infirm. He presided in the office of the priorship for almost eight years; because of his old age he accomplished little.

*1351.*

In the first year of our Abbot Philip, Emperor Charles IV, to gratify the princes on this side of the Rhine, established many new tolls in various places on the Rhine, by whose help he had been raised to the empire. But when the merchants, being exceedingly burdened by this, avoided the traffic of the Rhine, the people of Strasbourg so closed the Rhine around Strasbourg with chains and timbers that for two years ships could neither go up nor down. Seeing that they were being deprived of the revenues of both the new and the old toll, the princes entered into an agreement with the people of Strasbourg; content with the old tolls, they renounced the new ones.

In the same year Wilhelm, Duke of Bavaria, Count of Hainaut, Zealand, and Holland, lord of Frisia, son of the late Emperor Ludwig, who was the first from Bavaria to rule in Holland, oppressed the people with new and unusual exactions, nor any with the poor [unclear], who was the only daughter of that land (as we said above), they implored; when she admonished him in writing to desist from his presumptuous tyranny, the son despised the command of his mother. Therefore the mother, seeing herself despised by her son, gathered a great army and advanced against her son in a naval battle on the Meuse on the day of the translation of Saint Martin. Defeated by him, she fled with a few to England; the son exercised tyranny against the poor.

*1352.*

In this year Philip, abbot of this monastery, had renewed before the Schultheiss and the scabini there the complete liberty of the goods of our court in Blenich, exemption from every burden of exaction, precaria, and any servitude, in the presence of Walram, Count of Sponheim, and very many others, as is clear in the instrument drawn up concerning this matter.

In the same year Reynaldus of Sponheim and Ernestus of Molenarck, canons of the greater church of Cologne, were cruelly and less than justly killed for a trifling cause by the leading men of the city of Cologne in the house of the official.

*1353.*

In the third year of our Abbot Philip, on the eve of Saint Thomas the Apostle, died Heinrich of Virneburg, formerly archbishop of Mainz, long before deposed and excommunicated by Pope Clement VI (as was said above). Gerlach of Nassau remained in peaceful possession.

In the same year, on the day of Saint Oswald, the second division of the duchy of Bavaria among Stephan, Albrecht, and Wilhelm, sons of Emperor Ludwig, was made at Regensburg by Ludwig, margrave of Brandenburg, Adolf, count palatine of the Rhine, and Johann, burgrave of Nuremberg, as is clear in the letters.

*1354.*

In this year, which was the fourth of our Abbot Philip, the emperor held a solemn court or assembly of princes at Mainz in the month of January, and dealt with many matters for the peace and usefulness of the commonwealth.

In the same year, on the Kalends of February, died Balduin, archbishop of Trier, a man [unclear], and he is honorably buried on the western side of the church. After him Boemund of Edendorff, raised to be archbishop, presided for twelve years and one [unclear]. In the same year died Salamannus, bishop of Worms; Theoderic the Bavarian of Boppard succeeded him and presided for nine years.
