*Polygraphiae libri sex* — "Six Books of Polygraphy" — is Trithemius's systematic cryptographic manual and the first printed book on the subject in the West. He completed it late in life — the dedication to Emperor Maximilian I is dated 1508 — and it appeared in print only after his death, in 1518. Where the companion *Steganographia* hides its cipher beneath angelic conjurations, the *Polygraphiae* is openly didactic: it teaches the craft in plain language, walking the reader from simple substitution alphabets through transposition grids and the famous "Ave Maria" cipher, in which any plaintext is recast as a grammatical Latin prayer. Its sixth book culminates in the polyalphabetic *tabula recta* — the rotating-alphabet table that anticipates the Vigenère cipher by the better part of a century.
