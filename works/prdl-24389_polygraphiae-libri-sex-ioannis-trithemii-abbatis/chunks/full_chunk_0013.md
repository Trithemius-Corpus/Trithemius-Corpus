*A Little Index, or Table, of the things contained in the sixth book*

In the sixth book, which is the last book of our *Polygraphia*, once mental narration has been introduced, we have set forth in order the minute details of almost all the preceding signs and degrees, and with arithmetical distributions we have brought the functions of the letters into the service of cabalistic operation. One thing lies hidden here in the bark, and another is found in the common operation of the signs. For just as the spirit in the blood gives life to the human body, so the vigor hidden in the art commends the operation. Therefore whoever needs knowledge of this invention should open the eyes of his understanding, so that he may comprehend what is the property of each operation. For we desire all things to be open to good men in such a way that, if it were possible, they should nevertheless never become manifest to the reprobate. A reason compelled us to fit the letters of the Latins to numbers, lest, if we were too obscure, the intention of our mind should be clear to no one. Yet this novelty, so that it should not become public without reasonable cause, had to be hidden mystically under [unclear] signs and marks of numbering. For nocturnal winds are always stronger than diurnal ones, and just as night overtakes day in its order, so light shudders at darkness. What nature denies, no one happily dares. We ask the reader not to despise this laborious work of ours before he understands it, but, so that he may be a judge without rashness, let him prudently remove the bark, in order to reach the hidden kernel. Good and natural is a work that can be turned to good use or bad, according as the desire of each person’s will is moved. A sword is a good instrument, and is turned also to good use whenever it is carried for defense without injury. But if it is directed unjustly to the wounding of anyone, through abuse a good thing is loosened into evil. Therefore, so that the good may have something by which they may prudently provide for their affairs, the *Polygraphia* is written by us; and, lest an occasion for wrongdoing be furnished to the depraved, the kernel is hidden in the bark under various enigmas. He who is good does not abuse a good thing for evil; rather, if he can, he turns evil into good.

*What are the causes of this work of ours, the Polygraphia, together with its advantages*

We have undertaken the labor of the present work, both rare and arduous, for no other reason than that we might provide for the usefulness of those in need. I say those in need, not of food, not of drink, but of assistance in preserving secrets without danger. Therefore we judge it worthwhile if we briefly note the uses and advantages that can come to those who use these inventions of ours. Indeed, since in our own domestic affairs we have no business such that we need these teachings for concealing secrets, we have certainly labored for those whose command had to be obeyed. The first advantage is that, when understanding of this work is complete, it gives an easy method by which you will make the secret of your mind known safely, secretly, and without any suspicion on the part of any observers, to one who knows the art, as widely as need shall be, across any distance of places, by regulated speech.

What lies hidden in these things the work of steganography draws out, which requires neither letters nor a messenger who knows the secrets he is to carry. It would be otherwise in matters that admit the light, according to the common custom of all our people. Do not take the matter otherwise than it is. The second advantage is that, through the service of this work, in a short space of time you will teach a man ignorant of the Latin tongue, provided only that he knows how to read and write letters under his mother tongue, to write under the Latin language as you wish, ornately or neatly, and to conceal all the secret mysteries of his will, and to announce, safely, secretly, and without suspicion, the art to one who knows it, as widely as he pleases. Again I warn you beforehand not to understand us as having promised otherwise than we promise.
