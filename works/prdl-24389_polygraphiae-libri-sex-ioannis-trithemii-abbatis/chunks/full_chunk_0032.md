Such is Catoptrics, which teaches how to present visible signs of things to the sight in a moment, by means of certain images projected far from us out of mirrors into the air, and devised to signify some discourse; these are easily understood by one who has knowledge of the signs. But in what way it may be possible for all things to be transferred to the use of signs is not part of the present undertaking, but belongs to steganography; the subjects of that work the most learned man Johannes Trithemius lists in a certain letter to Bostius in these words, etc.

I already have in hand a great work which, if it is ever published, which God forbid, the whole world will marvel at; the title of its first book is *Steganography*. There will be four books, and each will be divided into at least one hundred chapters. I began this work at the insistence of a certain great prince, whom there is no need to name. You ask what this work teaches? It teaches very great things, astonishing and incredible to all who do not know them, things that have never been heard of since the beginning of the world. For the first book contains more than one hundred methods of writing secretly whatever you wish, without any suspicion, without transposition of letters, without any fear, so that there is no man in the world who by natural ingenuity could know, or even suspect, what is contained in my letters, except the man who knows the art from me, or from him whom I shall have taught. The words are plain and familiar, devoid of all suspicion, but no one without the art will ever be able to perceive my intention, however learned he may be; and the thing is truly astonishing. The second book will contain much more wonderful things, by which, namely, [unclear] in this art I can make known the conception of my mind [unclear].
