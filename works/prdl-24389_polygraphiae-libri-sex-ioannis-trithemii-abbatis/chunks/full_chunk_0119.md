*How we may safely write any secrets whatsoever also by numbers and by many other various methods.*

These things are childish, I admit, since anyone, according to his own capacity and judgment, can devise many more elegant alphabets than we have set down in the volume. Therefore we knowingly omit many things which, by a similar reckoning, we judge to be childish, commonplace, and of little use. We fear that one day we shall be ashamed to have applied our mind to these trifles, more useful matters having been set aside. But I pray that my readers will pardon me, since my own will indeed drew me back from these childish studies, not to call them at least in part vain; but the urging of certain friends compelled me to write these things, friends to whom I could not refuse this without injury to the bond of friendship contracted. Nevertheless, moved by their entreaty, I have also connected the following chapter with the preceding writings, and have put numbers in place of letters. For the ancients set down various and manifold modes of writing by means of numerical figures. Therefore, persuaded by the prayers of friends, at the end of this sixth book we have placed a few methods out of many, by which the way to knowledge of others lies open even to those who do not know them. For if anyone takes pleasure in knowing these things, he will easily be able, once these are known, to ascend to higher matters, and to vary the methods discovered in manifold alternation according to his own judgment. As a beginning for this matter we have placed a table under the orchema, which, when the reading is varied in the form of a cross, will contain within itself ninety-six alphabets.

*Orchema*

*Numerical orchema in place of letters.*

[table of letters and numbers]

Whoever rightly understands the table of the orchema will compose for himself from it as many alphabets as he wishes. For example, we have placed the letters of the Latins transversely, facing one another at the four corners, so that we might provide the leap of transposition without limit. Writers will need diligence, lest the multitude of numbers confuse memory or understanding.
