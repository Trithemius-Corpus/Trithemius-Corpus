Marcus T. Cicero, an eloquent orator, using both natural talent and art, changed the small elements of phrasing into signs, and placed the careful mark, or character, of his intention in place of customary phrasing. Indeed he made use of such abundance in this very novelty that his invention could suffice for almost all things in the world. Moreover, to these heralds of mysteries the institution of the art gave marvelous agility, so that, beyond the nature of all things, solid and whole things might serve the good will of the master. A king can always make use of their service most safely, though not without great labor and manifest suspicion of the [unclear]. But Cyprian, most holy prelate and martyr of the church of Carthage, wishing to provide the very teachings of Ciceronian instruction for the use of Christians, enlarged them by no small degree, and added what had been lacking for our customs.

Pharamund also, the forty-third king of the Franks in Germany after Marcomer, most powerful, when in the year of the Lord’s nativity 424 he powerfully entered the borders of the Gauls, in order that everything might be done safely, bound foreign small elements to his secrets.

Clodius also, called Crinitus, the elder son, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, after he had violently subjected all Gaul to his rule, ordered all the Gauls, as conquered men and slaves, to be shorn and their beards shaved; but he commanded the Franks, conquerors and lords of the Gauls, to let their hair and beard grow. From this he was called Crinitus. In imitation of his father, he wrapped the mysteries of counsel in his own and foreign signs.

Charles, surnamed the Great, the sixty-third king of the Franks after Marcomer, who first of the Franks ruled over the Romans, with Nanno the grammarian as his teacher, wishing to adorn the German language, which was his native tongue, with grammarians after the model of Latin, began an arduous work, which nevertheless, entangled in many matters and overtaken by death, he did not complete. He bound owls with varied and many small elements; Brabant, or Namur in Germany, had no greater native than him. To the several prefects of the kingdom he gave, by art, individual owls. The Northmen, having come out from the borders of Gothia, when once they were cruelly devastating Gaul, summoned owls from Greece and prepared a safe hiding place for their secrets, which today is known to few.

Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the deed of the Northmen, described many inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made a path, bright with mysteries, dark. He was the first to commend artrology to the memory of posterity, and invented dactilogy. There were those who distinguished small elements by visible accidents; by their prudence night was turned into day, and without the service of owls light shone in darkness. Concerning these matters, the illustrious count palatine, prince elector, once an investigator of our Steganography, received from us a manifest experiment of the teaching.

But others, multiplying small elements by regulated division, after the manner of barbarians overturned the figures of nature, hiding in darkness the dances of the shadows of things coming into being; and where the secrecy of counsel required it, they supplied [perdiapyrana ta cabalam] to those absent.

Great labor is involved in these things, and a long exercise of the mind over many years, in which no one suddenly becomes skilled in practice. Necessity bears all things. There were also those whom heavenly harmony taught to compose foreign voices, of which there was a twofold use for those who understood, while one suspended the sense, the other [unclear] the mind. God can do all things. Many tried to seek light in shadow without light, yet they could not at all, because the fire lies buried in ashes. Some practiced painters of masks divided one wind into two to a point; one of them, a hater of light, always hides in darkness, while the other, a lover of light, a philosopher, hates darkness, loves light, and guards the secret with an owl. Again there were certain constant men of Bede, whose intention, advancing to broader things, bound an invisible vein to visible things before the eyes, and compelled many plants, and no less stones and fruits, which lack voices, to speak with a human tongue.
