For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books, beyond all whom I know today living in the flesh. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot in Sponheim, has alienated many volumes; whether this is true, I certainly do not at all know. In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had arisen between the kingdom and Philip, count palatine, Trithemius, from fear of the enemy, withdrew with his library and the possessions of the monastery to Kreuznach, a nearby town, and I likewise returned to my own affairs. But in the year 1506, in the month of October, Trithemius himself, spurred on by the envy of certain men, accepted the abbacy of Saint James at Würzburg, and resigned that of Sponheim. There, in quiet, he writes various works, whose titles and number he sent to me through these persons when asked.

To Emperor Maximilian he published a wonderful work, which he entitled *Polygraphia*, six books. To the same man, a key to the same *Polygraphia*, one book. Responses to eight questions of the same emperor, one book. To the same man, on the seven intelligences that move the spheres, one book. To Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg and prince elector, two books of various questions, orations, and propositions. To the same man, two books on foreknowledges lawful and not lawful for a Christian. To the same man he wrote that wonderful and laborious work of *Steganographia*, eight books, which that rash Bouillus, not understanding it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely. To the same prince, one book of letters. To the same man, against sorcerers and all arts and superstitions prohibited by the Church, five books. To the same man, on the causes and cure of epilepsy and of sorcerers, three books. To Jacob Trithemius his brother, an itinerary of his life, two books. To Burkhard von Horneck, physician, on the private property of nuns, one book. He wrote a history or annals on the origin, kings, and deeds of the people of the Franks and Germans, three large volumes, beginning the narrative from King Marcomer, who in the year 433 before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany to the mouths of the Rhine, down to the twenty-eighth and ninth year of Emperor Maximilian.

To Lorenz, bishop of Würzburg and duke of East Francia, he wrote an epitome or breviary of the same three volumes of histories, three books. To the same man, on the dukes and bishops of the Franks of Würzburg, one book. On the miracles shown at the memorial of Our Lady Saint Mary at Dettelbach, two books. On the miracles of the same woman shown in the nettle-patch at Heilbronn, six books. An apologetic work against Charles Bouillus, who calumniated things he did not understand, one book. On the origin and kings of the Franks, one book. A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, Saint James at Würzburg, one book. To Albert, of the margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, the life of the divine Rabanus, archprelate, three books. On his translation from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To the same man, the life of Saint Maximus, bishop, one book. To Johann, count palatine, duke of Bavaria and count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, on the origin, kings, and dukes of the Bavarians, together with the counts palatine and those of Sponheim, four books. He has in hand a laborious work on demons and profane, magical, and superstitious arts, which will have twelve books, in which he demolishes, confounds, and overturns all the deceits of the diabolical arts. Of the Würzburg letters, which he wrote in that abbacy, four books.

He also wrote several other smaller works, suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as: courses, a rosary, a missal office, and various prayers, together with prose sequences on Saint Anne, the grandmother of our Savior. He also wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene. A rosary on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord. A rosary on Saint Peter the apostle. A rosary on Saint John the evangelist. At the instance of Johann, archbishop of Trier, a sequence on Anne, which begins: *Jesu, king of the heavens*, to the melody *Ave praeclara*. Another to be sung on her feast, which begins: *Let them rejoice on this day*. A third to the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, and it begins: *Let us jubilate in honor*.
