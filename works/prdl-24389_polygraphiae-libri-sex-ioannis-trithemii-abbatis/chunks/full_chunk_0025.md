Let us also set down an example of this alphabet, to prove that a secret can be hidden in it. Gquog, o[unclear]uqi, yar, din, u, c, qacquxua, riqdi u[unclear]i, aqqurixr 12, siti, idta, yocsaqui g, uc, coddotacuo[unclear], osoqui, iug, g, cj, hidiog. This writing contains in itself the following meaning: Know, friend, that the king in council has decreed that you are to be killed. Therefore flee as quickly as possible into Narragonia from his presence, lest you perish. This manner of writing too is tortuous, and is subject to no regular norm, since it can be varied and changed by arbitrary transposition at the writer's pleasure as much as anyone wishes. Next, it should be noted that we have handed down numerals, which people commonly call ciphers, under whose covering you will safely write to an absent friend whatever you wish, and no one unskilled in the art will be able either to discover or suspect that a secret lies hidden, especially if the rules that we have given concerning these things are observed with careful diligence. This is that the paper should be formed in the manner of an astronomical table, as though it contained something pertaining to the reckoning of the stars, namely so that the characters of the signs or planets are interspersed, in order that someone into whose hands it perhaps happens to fall, led by its tabular appearance, may think that no secret lies hidden in it, but may suppose that everything pertains to some astronomical calculation. We have arranged twelve alphabets according to the number of the twelve signs of the Zodiac, assigning to each a number of twenty-four in place of the letters; the first of these, Aries, begins from one and ends at twenty-four. Yet in great and difficult matters we advise beginning from one, because that manner of writing can easily be suspect for that very reason. But whoever wishes prudently to use natural industry will find subtler things from ours, and will easily make better the things that we have handed down not sufficiently polished. In the numerical orchema great secrets lie hidden; if anyone understands them properly, he will penetrate many mysteries of numbers. For we had neither the time nor the reason on this occasion to pour everything out into public view; but in our judgment what we have done will suffice for the instruction of those to whom they will someday be pleasing, so that they may have several methods not only of writing secretly whatever they wish to absent sharers in the undertaking, but also a way of bringing forth one method into several better and subtler ones according to the capacity of each person's understanding. Likewise, since the numerical orchemata are sufficiently open and manifest in their own places, and their tables likewise set out, it was not necessary here to repeat what has once been said, lest excessive length produce weariness for the reader. Nor did I think it necessary to set down an example of more secret writing by means of the figures of numbers, since there is no difficulty in knowing the method for all who are strong in even a moderate intelligence. With similar ease the very useful signs of numbering that we have brought back to light can be understood and comprehended, by which knowledge of numbers is extended to infinity. For whenever a small stroke is placed above a lesser number, a thousand is signified. Thus xͣ signifies ten thousand, C one hundred thousand, M a thousand thousand, X ten times a thousand thousand; .M. signifies a thousand times a thousand thousand, and so in succession, as far as you wish to proceed in numbering, without limit.
