Before all invention and use of letters, in the beginning of the world, when human beings were still crude in understanding and uncultivated in manners, under the [unclear] governance of alternation, as some suppose, of the intelligences, succeeding one another in order for one thousand sixty-three years, they were, in the sphere of the planets, by their influence exercising power in their own fashion over bodies on earth. The ancient speculators on this matter say that Saturn, from the beginning of the world, presided by his influence over things germinating and springing up on the earth, principally through his governing intelligence, which they called Orisiel, that is, “the vivifier of God,” for three hundred fifty-four lunar years, likewise four months, and the same number of days and hours. We recount these things as poetic fictions; we do not affirm them. After Saturn, Venus; after Venus, Jupiter is said to have assumed dominion. But after Jupiter, Mercury, fourth ruler in order, adorned the talents of human beings with various arts. Under the three intelligences just mentioned, namely while Saturn, Venus, and Jupiter were ruling, they report that human beings did not have the use or knowledge of letters. Hence whenever one person wished to announce some secret matter to another, he did this not by writings, since the service of letters was lacking, but symbolically, by certain likenesses of animals, trees and plants, or of some other perceptible things, according as each had agreed among themselves. The time of Saturn, they say, gave to human beings manners properly corresponding to his influence. For it made them farmers, hunters, founders of cities, crude, woodland-dwelling, gloomy, simple, uncultivated in life and manners, and living for a long time in the flesh. After Orisiel of Saturn, as those who hold such opinions say, the intelligence Anael, mover of Venus, receiving the governance of the world by influence, continued it for three hundred fifty-four years, months, days, and four hours. Under him human beings began to be more cultivated, to construct houses more frequently and cities, and also to invent various manual arts, namely the work of weaving, wool-working, and many other things similar to these. They also began to flow more fully into the pleasures of the flesh, and to take beautiful wives; and with many children begotten from them, they notably fell away from natural simplicity. They invented games and songs, began to play the cithara, and whatever they affirm to pertain to the influence of Venus they devised more zealously, following the impulse of the flesh too much. They named the third intelligence, that of Jupiter, Zachariel; and when it took up dominion of the world after Anael, it made human beings restless with desire to rule, to go hunting, to make tents, and to adorn themselves with various garments. While he ruled, in accordance with the quality of Jupiter, who deprived his father of the kingdom, there arose a great division between the good and the bad, the former calling upon God, the latter devoted to the flesh and the world. Under that governance Adam, the first man, died, leaving to all posterity the testament of dying once. But the fourth mover or angel of Mercury, Raphael, influencing worldly things after Zachariel, began in the one thousand fifty-fifth year, at the end of the month of February, and lasted until the one thousand four hundred tenth year of the world. Under him the use of letters is first said to have been invented. The letters then were, as they report, very different from those which the world has today, devised according to the form and likeness of trees, plants, and animals; yet these afterwards, over time, were altered in many ways, and assumed an easier manner of being formed and a more cultivated ornament. For the fictions of the poets said that Mercury was the presiding power over all literature, promising that great hidden secrets of things lay concealed in fables. After this, when the human race had multiplied and been spread everywhere over the whole earth, the unity of letters did not long remain; but the separation of bodies and minds far from one another discovered diverse and varied modes of writing, about which a long narration would be required from us.
