What we promised at the beginning of the third book of our work, the Polygraphia, for attaining the shadow and likeness of a new gloss before the inexperienced, we have sufficiently supplied by way of example, with a saving of labor. We have entrusted night, safely serving secrets, to be guided by the shadows of circumlocutions, and we have inserted numbers in the order of four lines [unclear], adapted for the use of those who wish it. The places are degrees of minutes having fixed points, three thousand one hundred and fifty, although, if you wish, there remain infinite moments of hours for keeping secret, in every nation and language of the world, whatever secret matter may arise. The circle, arising linearly from a point, whenever night exceeds the image of light, you will be able to bring back, as much as you wish and as there is need, to whatever path you have striven after. If much of night remains, bring the dragon’s tail back to the original tail-place in the head. But the binary is made wholly certified by the marks and little signs of unity. He who hungers much, let him labor much, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is there need of great labor to lead that wherever you wish, since it proceeds of its own accord. Here, therefore, we set the end to the third book of our Polygraphia, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell. From Peapoli, on the seventh day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ the Savior one thousand five hundred eight, in the eleventh indiction.

*End of the Third Book of the Polygraphia.*

*The fourth book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Saint James the Greater, Apostle, at Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict, begins.*

Order of reasoning required that the fourth book of our Polygraphia be made unlike the preceding ones in quality, invention, and ease, so that those who wish to share in the secrets of our matters may attain this not by one method but by various and different ones. We have extended the magpie, comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that night, always joined through a minute to the monad in any order, may be recognized in the light of day. Here no disagreement is found between unity and the binary, nor will the person wishing to work the art need to have the instrument at hand, since what succeeds the first immediately, while hidden, shows the mystery. Hence the prudent man, understanding and experienced in the science, will satisfy the operation at every time and place. The binary receives from unity, whence it produces the ternary. The knowledge of this cryptology is easy at first sight, after the moon, or a single point, has been illuminated by the sun. And yet it may come to pass that the knowing man becomes skillful without the guidance of a master. I think, however, that this is to be understood only in this book, in none of the preceding ones. As for the following books, judgment will be given in its own place. Among all the cabalistic modes of secrets that we have given up to the present, the progression of this fourth book can more easily be extended to infinity. Therefore it is enough that I have handed down to those desiring fuller things both the form and the reason of the example.

The light of understanding easily penetrates all these things. I wrote at Peapoli, on the ninth day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
