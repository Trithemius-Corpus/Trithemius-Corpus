In both, according to the capacity of human talent, multiple progressions can be made, since the words are varied by metathesis. The [feronomata] too are changed at will, whenever minutes are converted into whole degrees. When this has been done, a work altogether wonderful in the art will arise, and the world, brought forth by an image, will stand in the light, and there will be no fear at all of the Bacuceans in the one who is operating. I could have given these matters a more beautiful face, if I had been induced to write them by some hope of profit or by the use of my own advantage. But since none of these things induced me to write, it seemed enough to have given those who desired it an occasion and material to add more polished things to our inventions, and to adorn art by art.

*An indication of the things that are contained in the third book of the Polygraphia.*

*In the third book of the Polygraphia*, having by a reasoned plan overstepped the bounds of our prescribed nation, we are led to foreign things, and, being essentially set in the midst of the Franks, we wander farther abroad through images and likenesses of barbarians. For with nearly four thousand mythological phrasmata arranged in order by art, we show a very easy way to artificial unity, through which it will at all times safely communicate symbolic notions to its binary. Here the binary is harder than unity, and unless our philologist is sharp-sighted with prudence, he will make the binary contrary to the monad. For only the ternary, which proceeds from unity and the binary, as a palindrome joins both together in itself. But if the usefulness of necessary reason is extended beyond the limits of the proposed matter, let the paraphrasmata be counted each time by minutes from the beginning of the day, until the minutes establish the degree in which night is equal to day. The face of this image knows perpetual silence and does not fear the deceits of the Bacuceans. It is extended in as great an abundance as you wish, and is varied without number.

*Brief index or table of the things that are treated in the fourth book.*

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia we place much confidence in the binary, which, with all obstacles removed, without the labor of difficulty, at first sight we have reduced to unity. Hence here we use a circle in great things, a line or point in the smallest things. Nor does it matter if night exceeds day, or if light does not agree with darkness, because the circle does not receive an end, and fullness does not admit a void. For they are contraries, and whenever they succeed one another, one is corrupted by the other. A singular mystery assisting the binary is handed down in this fourth book of the Polygraphia, since the airy wind itself, the composer of the base, is never designated in anything other than the binary. But in that part the lightness of the owls is always suspect to us in difficult matters. The construction of this thing makes paraphrasmata with infinite atoms.

*Index or table of the things that are treated in the fifth book.*

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia, we have inscribed in order on two tables the letters of the Latins, which we Germans also use; one of these is called the straight table, while the other is named the reverse. Beneath these we have placed windy dances, so that what we seek with labor among foreign things we may see among our own without difficulty. For besides those things that have been set down by us openly, many other secret things still lie hidden within, through the understanding of which a path is opened for the operator toward greater things, though these are by no means proposed to all. We have also set letters beneath numbers, by which hidden things may be made manifest at our will. Hence through the skilled operation of this book, all messages are safely handed on to the cabalists, although the mysteries are not free from suspicion of the Bacuceans. For every form of metathesis of signs produces suspicion in the observer, although it brings no harm to the owls. But you, who have attained the understanding of this work, will easily penetrate every metathesis of letters among our countrymen and the Latins.
