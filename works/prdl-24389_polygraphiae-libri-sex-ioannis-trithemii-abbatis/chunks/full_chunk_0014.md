We are permitted not only to write and to read, but also to understand, if you have prudently applied the duty of the office, which we have thought should be disclosed not without reasonable cause. For the pupil of the new art will write a language which he did not know, and will understand and interpret Latin discourse composed under regulated instruction, if you yourself command him word by word. Finally, this instruction can be extended to all the things and affairs of the world. If anyone wishes to learn from me how these things can be done, let him remember first of all to show himself such that he deserves to receive it. For not all things are owed to all people, but one person is to be preferred to another.

The fourth usefulness is that, through the enlargement of the likeness of the thing received, all these things can be extended into any language whatever, with the instituting binary reduced to unity. Yet not just anyone among men can do this everywhere, but only he whom, with God going before him by his special grace, art and nature have made worthy.

The fifth usefulness is that, by imitation of likeness, we can communicate the art of our invention to one who knows it, and as much as we wish of our mind, abundantly, in many ways, and very widely, by various methods, which cannot be known or observed by anyone else. Here is given a manifold speech without voice, by which, safely, securely, very widely, with all suspicion removed, we shall be able to indicate fully to those who know the art all the conceptions of our mind, however difficult. And this wonderful invention has innumerable progressions, capable of all mysteries.

The sixth usefulness is that, through understanding this work, you will be able to discover any regulated metathesis or transposition of letters, and nevertheless to form them in such a way that they serve only those who know the art, and absolutely no one else in the world. And great is the power of metathesis in its form and also in its operation, which both has many modes and is varied in many ways.

The seventh usefulness is that, through understanding this volume, a very great faculty is given to those who desire to enlarge the memory without labor, whenever the seeds of minutiae are set in their proper degrees. All things, for the sake of goodness, lie hidden, wrapped in the secrets of words and figures; if anyone wishes to understand them more fully, let him recall to mind that unremitting labor conquers all things. For it will have been enough for me to have discovered the principles of the art and to have handed down a rough method of writing, and one intentionally unformed. For there was neither time nor reason for our discoveries to be shaped in a more polished form, lest honey produce vomiting in the nauseated, or truth produce hatred in the indignant. Every age is to be valued according to its own merits, and swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor loaded with precious jewels. All things are untimely which do not agree with the habits and times of the mortals upon whom they are thrust. There have been many very noble talents among men in unsuitable times, and more new ones are found almost daily in times not their own. What advantage did the wonderful invention of nautical art bring to the excellent [versifier?] Bartholus under Pope Innocent VIII, which in a certain little book he offered for sale for one hundred thousand ducats, when neither the supreme pontiff himself, nor the Venetians, nor the Genoese, to whom he proposed that his invention be bought, deigned even to meet with him for a small amount? For he promised that by his art he could keep a ship unharmed in the middle of the sea for thirty months, repair one that was broken, and bring back without danger whatever had been cast into the deep. But no one cared. Nor has it escaped me that many, with Naso, have perished by their own genius. It is therefore better to set a limit to knowledge than to expose oneself fruitlessly to the hatred and detraction of many, for whom it is more familiar to despise any knowledge whatever than to have learned it.

*End of the Preface*
