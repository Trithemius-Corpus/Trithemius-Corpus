From their number, first, for the sake of an example of the proposed matter, we introduced Caesar Augustus, whose secret method of writing is thus made plain. He changed the breathings of conceived speech from clear letters into obscure ones; and their barbarism, resisting his wishes, by a certain fine invention, by the movement of metathesis, he made obedient to his command. That is: the great Caesar Augustus, in order to write secretly and safely what he wished, changed the letters of the alphabet by various transposition, so that he put B for A, C for B, and so consequently for each one. And so that the writing might remain more twisted and more secret, at his discretion he transposed the letters in breadth and length to any distance of order, number, and place.

*Mystery.* And this receptive order, as though perpetual, subject to the will and to the reckoning of time, although it keeps messengers safe on the road from the attack of [unclear], nevertheless, since it has no regulated limits, makes all who encounter it anxious with suspicion of a hidden matter.

*Explanation.* Although no one can easily comprehend with the understanding the secret mystery hidden beneath the transposition of letters, nevertheless this writing makes those into whose hands it comes suspicious about the hiding-place of the matter enclosed within.

*Mystery.* That Archimedes of Syracuse, easily the foremost of mathematicians, [unclear] his white bird, suitable to his institutions, bare through head and feet, and the rest.

*Explanation.* Archimedes had two little rods made, of one length and thickness, rounded or squared, of which he kept one with himself and delivered the other to his absent friend. And whenever he wished to write something more secretly to the other, he wrapped the bird, that is, a sheet of papyrus of small width but of the length of the doubled rod, around the wood, and bound it below and above with wax. Afterwards he wrote on the sheet, continuously from the sides, artificially joined together into a mass, whatever he wished; then he released the sheet from the rod, and at once there appeared such a distance between the letters that not even one word cohered with another.
