*TABLE, OR INDEX OF THE THINGS THAT ARE TREATED IN THE FIRST BOOK.*

In the first book of this *Polygraphia* of ours we arrange and distribute the words of the Latin language, as many as are visible, placing them according to the order of our ternary; by the use and service of these, all princes throughout the world will find, in any secrets whatever, a secure assistance, which, without danger to the mystery, without [unclear], and without suspicion of [unclear], will remain forever hidden. With these owls the journey is secure, and never to be violated by any investigation of anyone. Knowledge of the hidden matter is granted to two only, if the binary is skillfully brought back to the monad. Ignorance is the mother of error, and he who scorns to learn the good which he does not know loves blindness. These periphrases themselves are extended in the manner either of one advising or of one entreating, and they can be varied for every visible subject of this world. For they are suited to individual mysteries, and are set before the secrets of the Greeks no less than before our own; when you have understood the things you see, do not rashly despise the hidden things which you do not see. You perceive the accidents; the substance remains invisible. Hence the greater things lie hidden, while the lesser are open to those who look upon them. Since this has been done with reason, let no good man take it for evil, since from these every suitable person could advance to higher things, provided he wished to do so. But to me, at this turn, it has not seemed good to pour forth the higher things into the light, since an unfavorable time seems to stand in the way. I have set the letters of the Latin alphabet in order before the individual words with this consideration: that the operator may have an easy method in what he has set before him, and that the reciprocal correspondent may be relieved from excessive labor in the relation.
