He also invented various modes of writing, and, with Nanno the grammarian lending his assistance, devised different alphabets, by which he communicated secrets in a special way to the governors of the kingdom. *Mystery.* The Northmen, having once gone forth from the borders of Gothia, when they were laying waste Gaul with a cruel incursion, called in owls from Greece and prepared a safe hiding-place for their secrets, and the rest. *Explanation.* The Northmen, roaming through Gaul, as Bede the monk in England bears witness, in order to write their secrets safely among themselves, substituted Greek numerals for Latin letters, from A, the first, up to the twenty-fourth point, such as W, or gu, according to the custom of the Gauls. This mode of writing is altogether very beautiful: in it all the letters are Greek, and according to the Greek order of numbering they designate our Latin letters, as A, 1; B, 2; C, 3; D, 4; E, 5; F, 6; G, 7; and so consequently up to W, which is 24. Neither a Greek nor a Latin will understand this writing, unless he has first learned the method of the device and the art. *Mystery.* Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the deed of the Northmen, described many inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made what was clear dark in their mysteries. He [unclear], and the rest. *Explanation.* This Bede, the monk, in his little book on the inventions of the ancients, reviewed many and various modes of writing and noting secretly, by which they safely reported their secrets to friends and spoke without voice to those present. He was the first of all to commit artrology and dactylogy to letters; by the use of these one person most safely discloses to another whatever secret he wishes, which no one else sitting by will notice, much less understand.

He taught that this very thing could be done in many other ways too; since we have written nothing about these in the Polygraphia, we rightly decline to say more about them here. We long ago assigned a proper place for these very difficult matters in the Steganographia, which we do not yet intend to publish publicly. And who could set forth all the modes of secret writing among the ancients? For some had as familiars in the service of the proposed art owls called Philophotos, others Mysophotos, others called both into practice from the art. Again, some transposed the known letters of the common alphabet; others devised their own characters for themselves under the form of those of barbarians. Again also, some used the service of visible letters, writing with minium or ordinary encaustic; others, however, formed invisible characters at their own will on command. The modes of visible characters for secret writing are very many and utterly innumerable; to mention all of them belongs neither to our will nor to our power. Of invisible characters, however, there are three kinds more commonly used among mortals today. The first is called dermatic, by which a certain artificial writing is made on the backs of messengers, visible only through the washing of a certain fluid [unclear]. The [second?] is written in cloth with a certain penetrating [unclear], which, when dried, can by no means be seen until, when moistened in cold water, it is perceived. The third kind is named alleotic, which is manifold and various, both in form and in material, and has almost innumerable modes of writing secretly. Its writing is done on paper or without paper, with [nemilua] and [cala], with [raphocam], [lapolce], and other diverse things. Letters are likewise written in various ways and hidden in places, as under [race tabulata], under [rosi rilacubior], in [leopi], in [necostas], in [ceocali], [locubat], in [ratera], in [lispilia], and in other similar things almost infinite in number. But because none of these things on this occasion serves our purpose, therefore it is fitting that we hasten to those matters which the plan demands. There is also another kind, dactiletron, not of writing, however, but of signs, by which one person can make known the conception of his mind to another in very subtle ways.
