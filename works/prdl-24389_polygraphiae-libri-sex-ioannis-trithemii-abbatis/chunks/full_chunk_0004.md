Since you, as a friend, ask me for their titles, I gladly assent to your wishes, so that from the consideration of such great labors of his you may have a more exact memory of our common friend and teacher. And first I shall record the titles of those lucubrations which he laboriously composed while he was abbot at Sponheim. First he wrote the constitutions of his order, which were made in the provincial chapters by command of the presidents, one book. On the visitation of monks, one book. On the manner of celebrating a provincial chapter, one book. On the Rule of Saint Benedict, two books. Homilies and exhortatory sermons to his monks, two books, which he dedicated to his predecessor. On the temptations of cloistered persons and their remedies, two books. To Nicholas, priest of Mernic, on the priestly life, one book. Against the private property of monks, one book. A penthic work, that is, a mournful one, on the ruin of his order, one book. On the illustrious men of his order, four books. Praises of the order of the Carmelite brothers, two books. To Gerlach, abbot of Deutz, praises of manual writers, one book. To Johann Dalberg, bishop of Worms, on ecclesiastical writers, one book. On the praises of Saint Anne, grandmother of the Lord, one book. Chronicle or annals of the monastery of Hirsau, two large volumes. Chronicle of the succession of the dukes of Bavaria and the counts palatine, to Philip, count palatine and elector, one book. Life of Saint Irmina, nun and first abbess at [Horrio?] in Trier, one book. On the misery of cloistered prelates, two books. Praises of Saint Andrew the Apostle, one book. Praises of the monastic life, one book. Praises of true penitence, one book. Monocollogion of a man devoted to God, addressed to himself, one book. On the miracles of the holy mother Anne, one book. To Ulrich, canon of Cologne, on questions concerning the Gospel of John, one book. To the same, on questions concerning the Psalms, one book. To the presidents of the annual chapter concerning the Bursfelde observance, he abridged the statutes of the same union, one book. An epitome or breviary of the same, one book. On ecclesiastical computus, one book. The spiritual exercise of monks, one book. To Jacob Wimpfeling of [unclear], one book. On the illustrious writers of Germany, to Jacob [unclear]. Letters from Sponheim to various persons, four books. Orations delivered in chapters and elsewhere are twenty-four in number, which make one book. He wrote forty very fine sermons to his brothers of the monastery of Sponheim, one book. Sermons on feast days to the people, one book. Several supplicatory prayers to God and the saints, one book. From Greek speech he translated into Latin Maximus the abbot on the incarnation of the Lord. Also the anathema of Saint Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria, against the heretics, promulgated in the third synod of Ephesus; and certain letters of the same synod. He also wrote for the edification of monks a notable work which he entitled On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons, three books. He also wrote certain other smaller works, whose titles he did not record for me. These, most loving brother Nicholas, are the Trithemian works which, during the twenty-three years in which he was abbot at Sponheim, he wrote by lamplight for the honor of God, amid cares and continual occupation with household affairs, reading or writing day and night whenever he was able to steal leisure for himself. During the years in which he presided he brought into the library of Sponheim a very great treasure of books in every discipline, not only by the art of printing but also by the hand of scribes. For when I was with him for the space of one year, to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, I counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eight hundred had been properly written by pen on parchment and paper.
