From Latin letters, too, manifold alphabets are made—whether reduced to number according to the exemplar which we handed down in the preceding fifth book, or otherwise, in imitation of the Normans, with Greek letters also they afford our [men] a most safe means of announcing whatever we wish. Let us accordingly set down, according to the number and order of our usual alphabet, the letters themselves changed in an unusual way, and we shall have a secret and mystic mode of writing—fair and graceful enough, confirmed by usage. But this mode of numbering differs from that which we set down in the fifth book, and is more suited to our purpose.
