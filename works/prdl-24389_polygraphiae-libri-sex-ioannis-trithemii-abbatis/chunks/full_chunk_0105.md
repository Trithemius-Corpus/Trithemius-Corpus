Now that the demonstration of the possibility of our new invention has been shown briefly, and no less sufficiently, I do not think that I myself should be wearied any longer on this same occasion, since the route and method extends to infinity, if anyone should need an example for further matters. Nor do I think it at all necessary to recall to memory the things that have been recited in the preceding sections, since the keen reader, if he is studious, will also be able, with the Lord’s help, to discover the greater things that lie hidden in these matters. We have handed over to the wise and studious man both the material and the cause; if he wishes to use them prudently, he will surpass us by his own talent. But whoever desires this path to be more beautiful in the shade, let him join hand and mind to talent, and let him make for himself a route more beautifully adorned by paraphrase. If these things delight anyone, let him keep it fixed in memory that he must always remain within the prescribed circle; if he passes beyond it in any way, the whole operation vanishes uselessly. There are many names of men; indeed there are almost infinite words for things, villages, cities, provinces, trees, stones, and words, all of which serve our purposes. From these it is clear that a book and a method can be made, not impossibly, if talent is present together with diligence, which may surpass our inventions infinitely. But he who is of such dullness that he cannot understand these childish things of ours, when will he penetrate the subtler things that remain? We set an end to the fourth book of the *Polygraphy*, to which we join the fifth, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

*End of the Fourth Book of the Polygraphy.*
