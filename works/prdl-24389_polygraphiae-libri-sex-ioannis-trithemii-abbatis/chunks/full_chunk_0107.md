*The Straight Table of Transposition.*

[A transposition grid of the 24-letter alphabet (a b d e f g h i k l m n o p q r s t u x y z w), each successive row shifted by one — rendered as printed.]

In this canonical or straight table of letters you have, from our one and usual [alphabet] of Latin letters themselves, by their permutation or transposition, as many alphabets as there are monograms throughout it—namely four-and-twenty times twenty-four, which make in number 576; and multiplied by as many again, they yield a little less than fourteen thousand.

*The Reverse Table of Transposition.*

[A reverse transposition grid, as printed.]

In this reverse table you have, by the transposition of letters, as many alphabets as we likewise marked out in the preceding straight [table]. For, as you see, under the metathesis there are 24 to the right; to the left likewise as many; downward 24. Which, multiplied 24 times by 98, reduced in the form of a cross to the four sides, make alphabets not fewer than 112.
