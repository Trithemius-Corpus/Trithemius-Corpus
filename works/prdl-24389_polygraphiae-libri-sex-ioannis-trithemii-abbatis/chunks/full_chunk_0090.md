Minds change, and garments too are varied; the simplicity of languages is altered, the ancient character of idioms is varied, and nothing stable is found among human things. For there is a change of all things, and variety succeeds to the simplicity of minds. Who will be able to reduce this perpetual flowing-away, so to speak, to the concord of unity? For times pass away, as Scripture says, and knowledge will be manifold. But the fact that we add a third book to the two preceding books of the Polygraphy has not lacked a special reason. For, since human wills are various, multiple methods are rightly given for honorable operations. In those, day illumines night; in these, however, day lacks light. There light leads shadow about in light; here, however, the blind illumines the blind, and, being and remaining sight without eyes, supplies to darkness what it does not have. Here night darkens day with perpetual gloom, and, walking in darkness without light, gives guidance to the blind. These paraphrases serve all people throughout the world, and in every language safely, securely, and without danger transmit any secret whatever to the cabalistic binary. Latin moves barbarians to laughter, and barbarism itself produces suspicion among Latins. Yet it matters nothing, or little, since suspicion, however great it may have been, will nevertheless contain every most secret mystery. Let the diligent operator recall to mind the rules and precepts that we have handed down, lest he labor uselessly and in vain for himself and for others. For understanding is needed, without which the usefulness of no art will stand. In a few words we have shown the path to the greatest things, and with finite things we have given a method for infinite things. From things given and received, greater things are easily prepared by friends. Reader, remember that the retrograde mark can be fixed in every place, until you apprehend the mark of rest.

Farewell. From [Peapoli?], on the first day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
