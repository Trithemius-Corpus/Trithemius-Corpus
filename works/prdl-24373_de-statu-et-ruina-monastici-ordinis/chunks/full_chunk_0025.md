For fools are not corrected by exhortations, but become proud. Yet in these matters measure must be observed, and necessary discretion, without which no rebellion is ever mitigated. For it is better to have weak sheep than none. But who is so fit that he may preserve discretion everywhere? It is difficult to apply measure to all deeds. But he who wishes to cure infirm souls must be both severe and pious, and, as it were, tempered from a certain mean between both, so that severity may not be excessive, nor piety slack. For the one breaks, the other dissolves. But the fairest manner of restraining rebels and the contumacious is that which the long experience of elders commends.

*On Celebrating the Provincial Chapter. Chapter VIII.*

When we have come together into one place, most excellent fathers, let us diligently investigate what the celebration of the provincial chapter requires. For the greatest force of our stability is placed in it, if it is celebrated rightly. For it is the sole stronghold of our order; without it our whole religion would be thrown into confusion, as is apparent in those who for many years have celebrated no chapters. For the chapter is the preservation of the order, the increase of regular discipline, the uniting of unity. For in it integrity is given to morals, purity is increased in minds, the quality of regular life [unclear]. But what does it require, that it may be celebrated rightly?

Hear what its sincere celebration demands. It requires zeal; it requires knowledge; it requires piety. Without these three the celebration of the chapter is worth nothing. For if zeal is lacking, who will rebuke the delinquent? If knowledge is lacking, what fruit will excessive [unclear] bear? Again, if piety is lacking, zeal does not sharpen, knowledge directs; piety produces fruit. Without zeal knowledge remains cold; without piety, fruitless. Likewise zeal without knowledge comes forth excessive and indiscreet, without piety hateful. But piety is of value for all things, although without zeal and knowledge it does not suffice for the governance of souls. For it pleases all; it benefits [unclear], apart from the humble. Therefore the whole matter of celebrating the chapter consists in the disposition of those presiding, which requires men who are zealous, learned, and gentle. For if they are remiss, if unskilled, if cruel and impious, how then will they reform others to piety? If he who sits to punish crimes admits them, whom, like himself, will he correct? Therefore this fault must be referred to you, fathers, if you have chosen deformed and vicious abbots for the presidency. Therefore take care with the greatest solicitude that you appoint such men to preside over the chapter as an irreproachable life commends, the zeal of holy religion sharpens, and instruction in saving knowledge tempers. For one who presides over others ought to be a man sound in morals, mature in conduct, instructed in knowledge of the Scriptures, adorned with gentleness. Such a man, O fathers, is deservedly to be preferred by us, whose life may provide an example, whose doctrine an increase of virtue. If the presidents of the chapter are such, those subject to it will easily be brought back to obedience. For those will be fit to preside who by word can instruct the teachable, by example the rude, and by authority the contumacious. In these ways indeed the chapter will be celebrated rightly, and the souls of subjects will also be edified. The other things that render presidents fit I am not permitted to describe on this occasion, since I believe none of them is ignorant what sort they ought to be. Therefore, O fathers, I turn to you again, you who have come together in this place for celebrating the chapter. I address you, I exhort you, that you direct your intention to the restoration of the order, and carefully consider why the chapter was instituted. Do you wish to know why chapters were instituted? Seek out the decrees of the supreme pontiffs, and the cause of their institution will be known. But lest either sloth or occupation impede the understanding of this matter, I shall briefly explain the occasion for the erection of the chapter instituted.
