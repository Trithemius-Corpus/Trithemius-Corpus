O betrayers of your order and transgressors of your vow, do you believe that lying words can remain hidden? Your deceits are not concealed from us, nor are we ignorant of the riches which you deny. If you were poor, as you pretend, you could not expend for your transfer some two thousand, others four thousand florins, and some even more. Why then do you allege poverty, you who are not poor? Why do you say that you have nothing from which you can live as monks, you who can expend such great sums of money so as to become canons? Finally, if the poverty of the monasteries oppresses you, why do you not undertake reform? Why do you not bring private possessions, contrary to the rule, back to common use? For while each man loves his own purse, the state of the community falls. If, according to the rule which you have professed, you lived without property of your own, by the Lord’s providence you would abound in all riches. But you, neglecting spiritual things, devote your labor to the pleasure of the flesh; for this reason you have come down to lack of temporal goods. I would have said these things against those who, because of contempt for spiritual goods, also suffer want of temporal goods. But those who transferred themselves from the order, if they had been poor, would have persevered in it. For there is need of many expenses, costs, and provisions, which the poor could never have laid out. Therefore those who take poverty as the cause of their transfer are lying. As for the fact that they allege wars, battles, and the devastations of enemies as a defense of their dismemberment, there is no prudent person who does not know that this is frivolous, vain, and insufficient. For all the things they recount can be hostile no less to canons than to monks. But when they call the rule of the abbot among the causes, it is not undeservedly ascribed to the fault of those whose election sets a bad ruler over the monastery. And not undeservedly. As the monks are, so is the abbot of the monks. For bad monks are judged unworthy of a good abbot. Finally, if the abbot is bad, how does a good provost arise? You ought to have sought other remedies from experienced men, by which you might make progress in the order without complaint. But he seeks an occasion who wishes to withdraw from a friend; he invents countless causes who desires to dismember himself from the order. Now he has exhausted the debts of the monastery, now he falsely asserts the hardship of hostility, now he invents a harsh lordship, now he blames the sloth of prelates. He seeks everything, he takes up everything, so that he may either disparage the whole order or show the place unfit for regular observance. “If,” he says, “most blessed father, such a monastery is transferred into a secular church, especially the divine worship is thereby increased; it will make princes beholden to the Roman Church; it will prepare peace round about for the peoples, and the greatest honor for the Christian name.” O deceitful tongue, O lying lip, O contriver of deceit, O inventor of fraud, O minister of iniquity, who recount false things for true, evil things for good, sacrilegious things for holy. Kings and princes have been accustomed to build monasteries, not also to destroy them; you ought not mendaciously to have alleged them as patrons of your treachery. For if they seek the praise of God, they will never consent that monasteries be transferred from the holy order. But if they wish to consider their own advantages, they are accustomed to receive more profit from monasteries than from collegiate churches. Whence, then, comes such unheard-of presumption to you, that before the vicar of Christ you should falsely claim as pleasing to princes a matter which is known to be contrary to them? Thus you act, thus you deceive the vicar of Christ, master of evil, deviser of deceit, inventor of lying, author of crime, craftsman of iniquity, you who live by fraud, who fortify yourself with impiety, who attack religion, who gape after gains, who are fed by pleasure, to whom everything consists in crime and nothing in the love of God. Far be it from faithful princes that they should take away from Saint Benedict the monasteries which their once devout forebears built for him. Woe to you, perverse monk, who presume to inflict such great insult on the order, that you destroy monasteries which neither you nor your parents built. Woe also to you who give patronage to evil matters against God and his saints, who entangle yourself in the sins of others, who sell your tongue, who for a paltry price incur the indignation of God and of all the saints. Woe to you, I say, advocate of reprobate monks, who do injury to God, who by wicked work mock his saints, who deny the right of equity, who by words and deeds exhort monks to return to the world.
