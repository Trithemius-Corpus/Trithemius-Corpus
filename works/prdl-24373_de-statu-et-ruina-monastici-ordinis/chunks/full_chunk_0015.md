When will those be able to be absolved from guilt who turn the dwellings of the servants of God to alien uses, and monasteries, once taken away, will never be brought back to the order? Woe to those who did not fear to draw up for themselves this testament, which they will be able to revoke by no satisfaction whatever. O monk, O abbot, you who revoke your vow solemnly made to God, who cast off the habit of holy religion, who transfer monasteries consecrated to Saint Benedict into the hands of clerics: what will you answer when accused before almighty God? Perhaps you will say: We have changed the vow for the better, since indeed they are not ignorant of the difference of a vow. To live as a canon is good and holy, if a man has been called by God to that state. But if he has been called to the rank of monastic perfection, it will never be lawful for him to descend to the canonicate. For no one doubts that canons are inferior to monks.

Tell me, whoever you are, who are a cooperator in the depravity of monks, who transfer them from the order, who defend unjust causes against God and Saint Benedict, who are held to be the favorer and patron of their apostasy. Tell me, then, what seems to you? Is it lawful for monks professed in a holy order to take off the habit and become secular canons? If it is lawful, why do you obtain, with such expense, labors, wearisome efforts, and importunate princes, the dispensation of the Roman pontiff? For lawful things do not need dispensation. But if it is unlawful, who will make it lawful? A dispensation? I very much doubt it. Whether the pope can dispense in this matter or cannot is not mine to discuss, since it is agreed that he has the fullness of power. Far be it from me, therefore, to derogate even in the least from the authority of the supreme pontiff. For he is the vicar of Christ, and it is not lawful for a man to judge anything concerning him. Therefore are you secure if he does what he seems able to do? Believe me, you are not secure, because you have told a false cause for your transfer; you have fraudulently deceived the vicar of Christ, because both the narrative was false and the supplication, not to say extortion, was importunate.

Finally, the bull of your transfer, written carefully and cautiously, does not absolve you from the order, nor remove the vow, nor take away religion, nor consent to laying aside the monastic habit. Do you think that the supreme pontiff wishes to entangle his conscience in your sins? Read the bull attentively; consider the words; note the intention of the one dispensing; and you will find that even after the dispensation you are a monk before God and the Church. For I have both seen and read the bull of this transfer, in which the pope consents that monks may have prebends and benefices, and nevertheless commands that they render to Saint Benedict what they have vowed. He grants them the habit of the canonicate, but by no means takes away the monastic garb. See how secure you are, who transgress the limits of the indult, who fashion false things as true, who lie to the vicar of Christ, who extort bulls no less than you obtain them.

In those matters that are of positive law, a dispensation makes lawful what otherwise was not lawful, if a reasonable cause requires it. But you are commanded by divine law to render the vow made to God, from which the pope never intends to absolve you without necessity. But tell me, I ask you: what is the cause of your transfer? You will perhaps answer what all those so transferred are accustomed to answer: There are many causes, which necessarily drove us to this matter. What, then, are they? The poverty of the monastery, the incursions of enemies, the exaction of princes, the bad rule of abbots. These are the causes of our transfer. But a web of spiders, fashioned with blood, is easily broken, if anything of weight is placed upon it. You apostates, however, narrate false things as true, and under the fiction of poverty deceive the vicar of Christ, indeed your very selves. Would that your poverty were what you allege; would that you had less earthly substance, as you falsely pretend. Why do you allege poverty, you who possess the greatest riches? Who does not know that the monastery of Saint Alban, before it could be transferred in temporal matters, always abounded in many riches? What shall I say of Ellwangen, once a distinguished monastery, whose riches could have sufficed for a prince? Surely the monastery of Saint Burkhard ought not to have been transferred on account of poverty, since to this very day it exceeds all the monasteries of that province in riches. What shall I say of Seltz, what of Klingenmuenster, monasteries most abundant as far as moderate measure is concerned? Surely in their transfer poverty could not truthfully have been alleged?
