For the holy monks, the more they loved God, the more they were also loved by men. And the less they sought earthly things, the more, by God’s providence, they abounded in what was necessary for the body. For to those who love God, the whole world is riches.

O how comely is the face of your youth, holy religion of Benedict; how fair your aspect, how adorned your majesty with the splendor of all virtues. Who could suffice to weigh you worthily? You offer the way of three days to the people of God, freed in the desert from the servitude of Pharaoh, when you persuade men, taken out of the darkness of the world, to come by the three substantial vows of religion to the desert of cenobitic life for the love of God. Blessed are the professors who, withdrawing from the Egypt of this world, pant with all their desires along this way of three days toward the land of the eternal promise.

This is the threefold cord of holy religion, which is not easily broken, which is adorned with the brightness of obedience, chastity, and poverty; which calls back its lovers to the heavenly homeland; which presents its cultivators joyful to the supreme emperor, Christ Jesus. For although many things are necessary for regular observance, yet I would dare to say that whoever has kept these three inviolate stands secure in the order, like a threefold cord. For all the statutes of the order were made for the preservation of these substantial things.

The holy monks of old, attentively considering this, observed this way of the three substantial things with the greatest zeal: poor in spirit through contempt of all worldly things; continent through chastity of mind and body; obedient through humble subjection; and rich in every virtue. They shone forth most renowned in learning and holiness, and by word as well as by example converted many from injustice. Hence, as their merits advanced, many of them were promoted to the governments of churches; and the higher they had been placed, the more renowned they became in virtues.
