Finally, if you should meet this man as he proceeds through the city, and see him surrounded by so great a multitude of servants, you would think that not an abbot but an archbishop was passing by. If they did not feign themselves poor, but were poor, they would by no means run to waste in such great solemnity of vanity. For I am certain that this crowd follows plunder, not the man. Who would expect to find plunder among the poor?

O abbot, runaway shepherd, ungrateful and unfaithful steward of the goods of your lord, who so uselessly expend the substance of the monastery; who live voluptuously and shamefully: who will help you at the final examination? Whom will you appoint as manager for the account that must be rendered? Behold, you consume the goods of Saint Benedict in pleasure; from them you assist your relatives; you lavish gifts on shameful men; you distribute presents to your flatterers; day and night you serve gluttony and drunkenness. You eat the richest things of the earth and drink the best wines; poor monks live in want; humble brothers eat the bread of sorrow; the sheep committed to you are fed on worthless things. O vain shepherd, who neglects the Lord's flock, who scorns the necessities of your brothers, remember to whom you are to render account; recall how dreadful it is to fall into the hands of the living God, who does not regard the person of man, who repays each one his reward according to his works.

But hear my counsel and do penance, and redeem your sins with alms; rather, absolve them by repayment of what is owed, so that before the inward judge you may deserve to find mercy. Lay aside haughtiness; cast off [unclear]; leave vanity behind. Be abbot of your brothers according to the Rule; be a shepherd in solicitude, a father in charity. Feed them with word; feed them with example; feed them with sustenance. If you omit one of these, you will be a useless shepherd; for without spiritual things temporal things avail nothing for eternal salvation; without the help of temporal things, spiritual things do not long subsist in this life. Therefore, if you wish to be a good shepherd, you must not deny that the care of both kinds of feeding, namely spiritual and bodily, has been laid upon you.

I had said that your sins must be loosed by repayment of a debt. Of what debt? That, namely, by which you are held bound to Saint Benedict. What is that? All that you have. For from him you have that you are a monk; from him that you are an abbot; from him that you are wealthy. Now, therefore, he from whom you have all these things knocks at your door; because of the necessity of his order he asks for help; he desires to receive the least portion from the greatest things that he has entrusted to you. Why do you deny him his own goods? Why, when they are demanded, do you not immediately restore what has been entrusted to you?

Divine Scripture commands and says: If you see the naked man, cover him, and do not despise your own flesh. Behold, your order is naked. Behold, your torn [origin?] cries out; it asks alms; it seeks back its own; it seeks help. If there are any bowels of compassion within you, if any inner sweetness, if any love of your order, if any love of God, do not despise your own flesh; do not scorn so great and public a necessity. Honor him who first honored you. Give alms to him who raised you up. Bestow a little on him who made you rich in many things. However much you give him, nothing at all is diminished for you, if only you bring a good will. You will give a little and receive much; you will bestow earthly things generously and hope for heavenly things.

Nor should it escape you that, as is commonly said, whatever is withdrawn from honor is reserved for disgrace. For the jurist says in his maxim: The treasury takes what Christ does not receive. He is not free from the guilt of crime who sets avarice before the piety owed to the order. If they despise the words of God, what wonder if they scorn ours? Although I may say many things, speak many things, urge excellent things, I shall never bend rebels by words to contribute to the order. Indeed, unless your authority, O presiding lords, intervenes, the speech of one who admonishes labors in vain. Therefore it belongs to your office to bend the rebellious; to you it is committed to humble the disobedient; ours is humbly to obey your sanctions. Therefore, if you desire to defend our order from the attacks of the wicked, take care first of all to recall the abbots to obedience, because unless unanimity is restored to the order, the wickedness of adversaries will easily prevail. For the Lord says in the Gospel: Every kingdom divided against itself will be desolated, and house will fall upon house. Division alone confounds the order. The treasury takes what Christ does not receive.
