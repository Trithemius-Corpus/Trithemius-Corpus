Unless deeds are added to words, exhortations accomplish nothing among malicious minds. Still the stiff-necked people murmurs in the desert; still the impious nation blasphemes its lawgiver; still the wicked nation resolves to return into Egypt. They fear lest they die in the wilderness, when they ought to have feared this alone: lest they die outside the wilderness.

O hard mind of men, O ruinous freedom of monks, how long does your cruelty rage, which does not know how to spare even those whom it has as authors and friends? He who is worthless to himself, to whom will he be good? O unheard-of zeal of monks, O monstrous crime: to mock God, to deny him what was promised, to break faith, to withdraw the service owed. What is more cruel than this impiety, what more wicked than this sacrilege? O perverse monk, O unjust apostate, O most wicked perjurer, how long do you set yourself against the kindness of God, how long do you mock the mercy of the Almighty? You are a monk; as a monk too you will be judged, even if you have put off the skin together with the habit. Why do you depart from the holy order? Why do you lie to God? Believe me: the Almighty will not give his honor to another; he will not forgive you forever what he once solemnly accepted as an offering. Before you made your promise to God you were free and of your own choice; but after you promised, you began to belong to God. Now it is not lawful for you to abandon what belongs by right to God. Your voice has bound you to God: either you will be saved as a monk, or otherwise you will not be saved.

Behold, before your eyes is the way of life and the way of death; choose whichever you wish. If you keep faith with God, if you fulfill your promise, you will have reward; if you deny it, you will receive punishment for contempt. Hence Paul, admonishing you, says: Brothers, do not err; God is not mocked. For whatever a man has sown, that also will he reap. What do you presume you will receive, you who sow in the flesh? For he says: Those who sow in the flesh, from the flesh also will reap. But the fruit of the flesh has no share in the inheritance of the saints. Why then do you deceive yourself? Why do you trust vainly in vanity? Why do you transgress the ancient bounds which the holy fathers set? The vow which you solemnly made to God you deny, and yet you expect a portion with the elect of God as though righteous.

There is no peace for the impious with the sons of God. Behold, you squander the inheritance of the Lord; you enrich strangers from the goods of the holy father Benedict; you deny your promise; and, so that you may sin freely, you heap up false things as true. If you are a good monk, do not desert your purpose; do not abandon the seclusion of the monastery. But if you are a bad monk, you will never be a good canon. For he who is not good is bad; and he who is bad is not as he ought to be, and therefore is unjust. But the unjust have no share in the kingdom of God. And it matters little where you are, so long as, being bad everywhere and wretched everywhere, you are tormented. Unless perhaps you wish to be liable to a double damnation, so that, while you are a bad monk and an unjust canon, guilty and false in both places, you demand the wages of both.

Nor should you object to me the dispensation of the Roman pontiff, which, unless God approves it, will by no means excuse you. Not all things please God which are done on earth by the supreme pontiff. Now if you cry out and are angry with me, as though I were judging the acts of the vicar of Christ, I answer: I embrace, honor, and venerate the supreme pontiff as the true vicar of Christ; I by no means reprehend his deeds, but I know that he is a man, and that as a man he can both be deceived and deceive. No wonder. For in so great a care of all the churches, in so great an occupation with affairs, when false things are reported as true, a mind distracted in many matters is easily deceived.

But you, apostate from a holy order, who think yourself secure because of a bull of dispensation, see to it that after death, as the common saying goes, you do not meet an illiterate demon, one who does not know letters, who does not defer to an appeal, who despises the bindings of your immunity. Believe me: there is no craft that can deceive God. O wretchedly unhappy man, O senseless apostate, what will you answer to Saint Benedict concerning the profession which you spurn? What will you answer to the almighty ones concerning the vow which you contemn? Whom will you be able to appoint as advocate of your case in that dreadful judgment, where nothing can lie hidden? There all things will be naked, all things open; there no place of saving repentance is now left; there the space for making excuse is denied to all. What will that dispensation rashly presumed here profit you there, when it will be permitted neither to prove it by testimony nor to defend it by law? Then you will see, O perverse monk; then you will understand what strength it contained in itself
