If one who violates a man’s testament is guilty of a crime, what punishment does he deserve who unjustly diverts to alien uses the foundations of monasteries made by holy bishops and princes? For kings, princes, and prelates, and the rest of the faithful who founded them to the honor of God and of the monastery, are proven to have wished this above all: that the foundations of this institution should obtain an inviolable effect. If they had wished to have canons in the monasteries, they would not have placed monks there. But the blind ambition of men has grown so strong that it thinks of nothing less than future things, and loves nothing more than perishable things. O every late condition of human frailty, O ever damnable forgetfulness of the commandments of God. Rights consented to sins, and what is public began to be more lawful. Offenses blaze everywhere, and here and there, through wicked minds, by manifold kinds of sin, [unclear] works harmful deceits and frauds. This man confounds the order; that man utterly destroys it. This man withdraws the will of the saints; that man writes false allegations of dismemberment. Here monks are shut out from monasteries; there the goods of the order are given to others. The enemy assails those who stand firm; the slanderer attacks; the false witness defames. Already, among the guilty, to be innocent is a crime. What love of honor could there be there, what integrity could exist in minds, where the profession of the order is deserted, where the vow made to God is revoked? What sense of shame for things remains there, what virtue could subsist, where divine right is violated, where there is no fear of the laws, no dread of the judge? What can be said more inhuman, what more cruel, than to wish to usurp divine right for oneself, to confound right and wrong, to break a vow, to withhold the worship due to God?

O that day, dreadful and horrifying to all mortals, on which, after long tolerating these evils of the monks, the wrath of God will be vindicated, on which apostates will feel the punishment owed them, because the adversaries of the holy order will pay the penalty for their rashness. What good will it then do you to have trafficked in the vow made to God, to have extinguished the sacred order, to have had a brief, shameful pleasure for a time in the flesh? When the Lord begins to count his worshippers, to give true monks the promised rewards, to separate the good from the evil, to divide the truthful from the false, what place will you receive, what success will you have, you who deny the holy profession of religion, who break vows, who despise the order, who violate faith?

Would that you considered the brevity of this life; would that you feared the examination of future recompense; would that, pierced by salutary teachings, you attended with repentance to the voice of the Lord, saying: I do not desire the death of the one who dies, but that he return and live. And through the prophet: Return to the Lord your God, because he is merciful and kind, patient and of great compassion, who may revoke the sentence, if you are willing to wash away your fault with tears. But you do not fear the judgment of God; you despise his clemency; you contemn his patience and long-suffering. Therefore the Apostle says to each of you: Do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But you, by your hardness and impenitent heart, store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works. He said that the judgment of God is just because it is late, because it is long and greatly deferred, so that God’s long patience may provide for you unto life. But how will you do penance, you who do not acknowledge your fault? You will grieve, but too late, when there will now be no place for repentance, but the repayment of vengeance. Then, therefore, punishment will repay the impious man and the sinner, when repentance for sin can no longer profit.

O what guilty men they will then remain, what wretches, what unhappy men, who, for the sake of this briefest time, did the will of the devil, so that they must be tormented with him in inextinguishable fire. Would that these things clung to the hearts of those men; would that they thought on these things, who desert their order, who break their vow, who so boldly lie both to God and to his saints. But we strive in vain; our words do not receive them, who contemn the words of God and of the Church and of Saint Benedict. Therefore this cause of the order must be carried on not with words, but with deeds. Let us therefore be armed, fathers, with all our strength, and let us prepare ourselves for the contest with incorrupt mind, devoted virtue, firm constancy, against those who desire to destroy our order, who strip off the monk, who take away monasteries, who desert the rule, who are rebellious against their visitors.
