Reform, according to your powers, the order that has been deformed; raise up what has been cast down; restore what has collapsed. For unless you quickly apply a remedy, you will soon suffer grave harm.

For dissolute monks, enemies of regular discipline, always aspire to worse things, and, lest they be saved in the order, they strive to abandon it. For beginning reform you have the aids of law; you have the Rule; you have the statutes. This alone is required of you: that you put them into practice. Therefore, if you love your order, indeed because you love it, do not neglect it; do not delay, because the greatest necessity presses, since the impiety of perverse monks presumes everywhere. Behold, the rich of the world violently seize the goods of monasteries; behold, devotees of avarice invade the possessions of the order; behold, secular clerics receive the abbeys of Saint Benedict in commendam. None of these things would happen if abbots and monks had persevered in regular observance. Let us therefore impute to ourselves what we suffer, since we did not resist the rashness of wicked men from the beginning. To us, I say, this blame is justly ascribed, since, intent on our own advantages, we do not care for the losses of the order, as though they were another’s.

Rise up, I pray, fathers; rise up, even if late at last, after so many losses, after the perdition of so many souls, and let us do what lies within us, henceforth imploring the mercy of God, that he may supply what is lacking in us, direct our zeal, and bring holy endeavors to completion. And if we cannot bring the whole order back to unity of good conduct, let us strive at least to reform, [as far as possible?], those monks who are in our province. But if complete conformity cannot be had in all things, let them at least be reformed in three essentials, without which the life of monks is to be judged nothing other than the shameful brothel of an unfaithful soul.

Reform, moreover, must begin with the abbots, and thereafter be continued among the monks. For unless the shepherd has been brought back to sincerity, the health of the sheep will not endure long.
