Now they despise [it]. From the lowly they have been made great, but they impiously despise the order that honors them. Saint Benedict raised you up, O ungrateful abbot; he made you rich. He received you as a monk. He appointed you shepherd of his brothers. Why then do you not acknowledge the munificence of your lawgiver? Why do you not repay his benefits in return? What do you have that you did not receive from his rule? Who endowed the monastery committed to you? Was it not the devotion of princes and the alms of faithful peoples? Why then are you ungrateful for such great benefits? Behold, you think of nothing less than regular observance. You reject discipline. You spurn God; you despise his precepts, and you presume upon security. Behold, contrary to the rule, you play with the birds of the air; behold, contrary to the law of clerics, you hunt with unclean dogs; behold, contrary to honesty, you take delight in the pleasures of the flesh; behold what is most shameful: despising the oath of chastity, you consume the substance of Saint Benedict with prostitutes and pimps. You do not fear to spend thirty or forty gold coins in the bath, and to offer Saint Benedict even one [unclear]. I lie if I have not known abbots who consumed more than fifty florins at the baths, of whom some are the first to resist the subsidies of the chapter. Others too, from among those who wish to be called observant, impatient of seclusion and quiet, unable to remain long in the monastery, consider every occasion suitable by which they can move from place to place at great expense. What shall I say of elaborate and superfluous building works, which are liable to no less censure than expense? The walls are adorned with paintings; the pinnacles gleam with gold; the windows are marked with memorials. The poor cry out at the door; the sacred order, hard pressed, asks for help, and no one deigns to give assistance. O abbot, servant of self-will, cultivator of vanity, enemy of piety, you who shamefully pour out the substance of the monastery, who pour wine in gold, who deny support to your order: how will the account of your stewardship be rendered to Saint Benedict? If the rich glutton was buried in hell because he did not, for God's sake, give help to Lazarus from his own goods, where will the abbot be buried who denied to his own lord what belonged to another? If he will burn forever who did not give alms from his own goods, what does the abbot deserve who has committed robbery? What do you have, O abbot, that you did not receive? For everything that you possess belongs to the right of Saint Benedict. Moreover, it is a moral rule for all that he who lives by the support of justice should dare to deny no one what is just. The definition of justice is to render to each person what is owed to him by right. But we who are placed in the monastery have nothing of our own; rather, in good faith we possess what belongs to another. What belongs to another is judged liable to restitution. For the gifts that princes and faithful peoples conferred upon monasteries for the support of monks are remembered as having been handed over to Saint Benedict. If, therefore, you have squandered his substance, if you have despised rendering to him what is his, you will be subject to a twofold damnation, since from unfaithfulness you will be judged sacrilegious, and from trust denied you will be judged criminal. Render, therefore, to each what is his, and do not usurp for yourself what is not yours. Give to your lawgiver his share, who bestowed the whole upon you. Why do you deny a little to him from whom you received much? Surely you will not be able to allege the poverty of your monastery, you who so shamefully consume so many things. I am ashamed to say what many abbots are not ashamed to do: they, not so much submerged in the pleasures of the flesh as maddened by them, preserve nothing of monastic honesty and omit nothing of worldly shamefulness. They think that all things are permitted to them, who [do not ask what is lawful, but] seek what pleases them. Behold, that one, abhorring monastic quiet, seeks solace in the world by wandering excursions. This one, impatient of regular seclusion, proceeds pompously in public. Another, disparaging the discipline of honesty, sits all day drunk as a companion with frivolous persons. This one, despising the meekness of the saints, involves himself in the cases of litigants. Another sits at the gaming table; another, [unclear], dances to the drum. What do ambitious abbots not presume? What pleasure do they pass by untried? As he dines, the knees of servants are bent. As he passes through the church, a whole [cloth?] is spread. As he goes forth in public, a great abundance of servants follows.
