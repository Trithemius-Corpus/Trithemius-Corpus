Or has your soul come to abhor your sons? Help us. Come to our aid. Bring assistance, for never has your religious order been surrounded by such grave evils. Behold, it is destroyed from without by lovers of the world; behold, it is torn apart within by perverse monks, so that no one can easily discern which is the greater crime: for monasteries to be transferred into the hands of secular clerics, or to remain longer in the hands of such wicked monks.

Alas, holy father, what shall I say? You, when once instituting abbots, commanded them to be solicitous toward weak monks; now pastors have no care for the sheep, no diligence for the salvation of souls. All seek the things that are their own, and not those of Jesus Christ. Behold, father, your sons, having zeal, desire to come to the rescue, but they accomplish nothing, hindered by the rashness of the wicked.

Who, amid these things, will truly come to the aid? The bishop? But he cares for his own affairs; he seeks his own interests; he despises ours. The prince? But he sells favor, rejects discipline, seeks temporal things. There is no one who grieves over the ruin of your order, no one who comes to its aid, no one who renders assistance. All who can restore it neglect it; others who seem to have zeal for discipline try to help, but cannot.

O abbots, abbots, you who are the occasion of all evils in your monasteries, who, given over to vanities, do not at once correct delinquent monks: how harsh a sentence of damnation you will receive. To you, believe me, to you will be imputed the damnation of your monks, you who were set as their rulers and neglected to form their morals. Woe to you. It would be better if you had never been abbots. You, given over to avarice and lust, neglect the work of the Lord; you despise the care of souls; you think nothing of the path of observance.

You who ought to correct the vices of monks commit vices yourselves, and, lest your own be reproved, you pretend not to see those of others. You see, most honored fathers, what the state of your order is: what abbots, what monks; how those who might correct the wicked are lacking, and only those who quietly [unclear] among them come forward. Why, then, do you delay? Why do you not think about the reform of such men? Why do you not restrain the monks’ wicked pursuits? Their ill repute confounds us; their examples scandalize us, since the more numerous they are, the more [unclear]. Now then, father, come to the aid: they are your sons, your instituted ones.
