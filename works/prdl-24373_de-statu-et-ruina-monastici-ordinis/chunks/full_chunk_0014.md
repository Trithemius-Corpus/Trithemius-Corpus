For thus Saint Benedict says in the Rule: Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd falls upon him, if the father of the household has been able to find any lack of profit in his sheep. Therefore, abbot, whoever you are, if you are wise, if you understand, if indeed you believe in the judgment to come, if you desire to escape the torments of hell, do not yield to the depraved desires of monks; do not consent to criminal dismemberments. What does pleasure profit you in this most brief life, for which you are compelled to receive eternal punishments? Do not, I beg you, do not inflict so great an injury upon Saint Benedict; do not take from him the monasteries which neither you nor all your forefathers built. Let it be enough for you that you are abbot, because you live from his patrimony. Do not forsake the order; do not consent to devilish suggestions; do not make your monks secular; do not resign the monastery entrusted to you into the hands of others. Know yourself to be a man subject to death, of brief life upon the earth. Do not do so ruinous a thing; do not commit this crime; do not perpetrate so immeasurable a sacrilege. Remain in the holy order, and do not forsake your profession, if you desire to come to eternal life. For the Lord says: He who puts his hand to the plough and looks back is not fit for the kingdom of God. Behold, you have put your hand to the ploughshare; behold, with Lot you have gone out of Sodom. If you look back, if you corrupt your profession, if you return to the world, you are not fit for the kingdom of God, and you will by no means deserve to receive it. But if you persevere in your renewal, if you keep your good will, if you have the charity of God in your heart, believe me: even if in some respects you have lived negligently, you will find Saint [Benedict?] a kindly intercessor with God, for love of whom you did not wish to forsake the order when you could have done so. Remember, I beseech you, the covenant of the Lord which you made with him when you entered the order; and do not be unfaithful to your Redeemer; do not break your vow; do not violate the pact with so wicked a perjury. Consider the pact; attend to the condition; and take care that you not prove a liar to God. Before you promised him, you were free; but after you promised, you were no longer free, but were made a servant of God. It is not lawful for you thereafter to abandon this servitude voluntarily assumed; nor is it lawful for you anywhere after profession to return to the world. But if you corrupt your profession, if you deny your vow, if you return to the world, God will not have you in that rank in which he would previously have had you, before you entered the monastery. For before your entrance into religion you were, as it were, of the household of the Lord, among his subjects and laborers. You were, I say, a creature of God; you were a servant; you were a slave; but you were not a friend, an officer, an intimate, a secretary. But after you became a monk, you promised fidelity to the Lord, that you would cleave to him continually, that where the Lord is, there you too would be, his minister. He chose you as a friend and, so to speak, as guardian of his body, that in his sight you should always minister to him and fulfill his will in all things. For this you promised. Therefore, if you withdraw from him to whom you vowed yourself, if you are found unfaithful and a despiser of your vow, he will not have you of his household as before, before you became a monk, but certainly as a fugitive, unfaithful, a transgressor, perjured, and lying. For the monk who denies his vow denies Christ. For blessed Jerome says: I, who seem to be a monk, if I corrupt my profession, have denied Christ. But you transgressors, who, despising the sacred order, have from monks been made canons: you, I say, have not only corrupted your profession, but, what is more wicked, have utterly destroyed it. Now therefore either you have denied Christ, or what Jerome says is false. But because what so great a man taught cannot be false, it remains that you are deniers of Christ, perjured and unfaithful. Would that at least, though late, you would at last undergo the remedies of penitence, you who are placed in such great dangers. If he has denied Christ who violates the monastic profession, what does he deserve who defrauds the whole order, and finally all the saints, of what is due? If he always burns who unjustly occupies the professions [unclear] of men, of what punishment is he worthy who usurps goods dedicated to God and the saints for himself?
