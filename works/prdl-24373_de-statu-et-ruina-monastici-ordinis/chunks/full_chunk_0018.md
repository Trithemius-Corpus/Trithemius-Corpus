You desire to walk in the pleasure of the flesh alone. All the causes and reasons that you related in your petition are fabricated and false. How will the dispensation be lawful? How will the authority of the supreme pontiff excuse you, which you did not fear to circumvent by falsehood, fraud, deceit, and importunate prayers? What should be thought of you, your conduct, once examined, has declared. For the Lord says in the Gospel about ravening wolves: By their fruits you shall know them. Never is true joy seen among you because of it; never can the conscience be secure, but it is always anxious over its guilt, always troubled, since they have the devil as an inciter to despair, by whose prompting you have withdrawn from the order of necessity. Hence it is that you are not at all solicitous about the love of God and the salvation of souls, but, intent on earthly vanities and carnal desires, you think of nothing less than eternal life. By what spirit this transfer was made among you, your way of life shows. For those who are led by the spirit of God have crucified their flesh with its vices and desires. But you are not so. For as soon as you cast off the habit of religion, like wanton oxen, released from the yoke of subjection, you plunge yourselves, as if lawfully, into every disgrace; you cast off all honor; you make a pact with vices and wage war against virtues. The charity of God is not in your hearts. You despise mercy and piety. You love vanity, and you have become vain. You are evil to all good men, worse to monks, worst to yourselves. A transfer that has opened an entrance to vices has not been made for the better. Deserters of the love of God and of the order, enemies of honor, adversaries of piety: if you love God, if you are good, if you love religion, why do you hate monks? But you hate especially those who have persevered in the order from which you have fallen away, because, when you see those men now standing firmly in their vocation, you fear their testimony against you in judgment. Therefore all monks persevering in holy conduct will stand in the strict examination, about to bear testimony against you; by their works they will show that all the accounts and causes of your apostasy, already here cloaked in fraudulent words, are false and empty. You hate the monks of your order because, in truth, as you know yourselves to be, so you judge others to be; and the good that you do not see in yourselves, you also envy in others. We do not wonder at the madness of your heart against monks, when we consider the injury and insult that you have brought upon God and upon your holy lawgiver. For one who does not love God, who breaks the vow solemnly made to him, who casts off the habit of religion, is not to be wondered at if he hates the monks who are his servants. For every apostate is an adversary and despiser of his order. But I beg, but I exhort, but I urge all of you who have deserted your order: abandon fury, soften your rage, cease to rave against monks, lest those whom you now persecute with hatred you have in judgment as witnesses and judges against you. Do penance for this impiety of yours while you have time, lest swift death seize you unprepared. Do not, I beseech you, vent the corruption of malice against Saint Benedict; do not wish, by your transfers, to destroy his venerable order; do not despise the opportunity for repentance, if you desire to attain eternal life. You have built no monastery for Saint Benedict. I ask and exhort you not to transfer to the uses of secular men those built for him by others. Remember how brief the present life is, how certain death is, how strict the judge is; and do not place upon your necks so heavy a burden of sacrilege that you will never be able to restore it worthily by any satisfaction. Do not stir up Saint Benedict as an adversary against you, if you desire to find the friendship of the other saints. For when one has been offended, none of the rest will be a friend. Consider the end of those who long ago transferred themselves from the holy order, and likewise fear for your own. Where are the abbots and monks who once withdrew from the order and handed over the monasteries of the holy father Benedict into the hands of secular men?
