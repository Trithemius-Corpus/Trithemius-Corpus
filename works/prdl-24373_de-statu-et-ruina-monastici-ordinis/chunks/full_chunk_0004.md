And indeed, to speak without envy, the holy religious order of Benedict easily surpasses all, if you wish to consider dignity, if its broad dominion throughout the world, if men distinguished for learning and holiness, if finally their multitude. For this venerable plant brought forth [unclear]; archprelates, one thousand six hundred; bishops, three thousand five hundred. But if anyone calls this total of illustrious prelates too great and casts doubt upon it, let him turn over the chronicles and deeds of the ancients, and he will find that few have been named out of many. But let no one think that the former splendor of the order should be measured by the present, since there is no equal comparison of our men with it, when in our times monks do not shine, as formerly, either in knowledge of the Scriptures or in holiness of morals. Perhaps even now you will find devout monks, though few, but simple. But for the rule of souls in this time devotion does not suffice, if knowledge of the Scriptures is lacking. But if our monks are neither devout nor learned, they are rightly judged unworthy for the rule of churches. Many subjects, indeed, walk well, who, if they have been raised to the governance of others, have never remained in the tranquillity of their former life. And, to say briefly what I think: neither are monks worthy of churches, nor churches of monks, at this time. The cause is not hidden from the prudent; it is to be brought into the light hereafter, in its own time. Now, if it please, let us inquire briefly what the present state of our order is.
