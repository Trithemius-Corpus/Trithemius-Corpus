Therefore let them know that they are to be condemned by him whom they mock. For they are guilty of a double sacrilege, because they unjustly take themselves away from God and monasteries away from Saint Benedict. And whoever supplied occasion, favor, counsel, and aid to these [dismemberments?] has done this irrecoverable harm to the order and to Saint Benedict. For the rule of law says: He who gives occasion for damage is deemed to have caused the damage. Therefore such men are liable to the holy lawgiver of monks and bound to restitution. Let them see how they will answer at the last judgment to God and his saints, whose temporal goods they unjustly usurp for themselves or for others.

Woe to those who unjustly take what belongs to others, which they can never restore either in this life or in the future. Woe also to you, apostate and renegade monks, who confound right, despise equity, and violate faith and covenant. Woe also to you abbots, who do not correct the evil pursuits of your monks, who consent to their apostasy, and who yourselves apostatize from the order with them. O abbot, shepherd and idol, who make wolves from sheep and secular men from monks, what answer will you have to give to the supreme shepherd when you are compelled to render account of your stewardship? What will you say to Saint Benedict when he demands back his sheep from you? He will require monks from you, not canons. What then will you answer him? Are canons monks?

When that day comes, to be feared by all mortals, on which the just judge will render to each according to his works, Benedict, the lawgiver of monks, doubt not, will accuse you gravely. Against you he will bring forth as witnesses all the true monks of his order, who will cry out unanimously to the judge: Just judge, Jesus Christ, who know all things, behold the madness, crime, sacrilege, and cruelty of this apostate, who destroyed the holy order, called monks back to the world, broke faith, and with unheard-of presumption violated the vow made to you and to us. Wretched man, what will you do, accused so gravely? Where will you flee from the face of the fury of the Lord? To whom will you have recourse? There will be no place to flee, but you will stand before the tribunal of Christ to receive a harsh sentence, unless here, through repentance and tears, you have taken care to come before the face of the Lord.

No place will be granted for excuse when you have been accused in the judgment of him who knows all things. For whatever you may wish to say will be nothing, because, accused outwardly and inwardly by the testimony of conscience, you will be judged worthy of condemnation. You seize the things that belong to divine right, you who had been appointed their guardian; therefore you will hear the Lord saying: Pay what you owe. That is: give back the monks whom I entrusted to your faith; give back the monastery which you received to govern; give back faith; give back the vow; give back holiness; give back gain; give back merit; give back honor. Pay what you owe.

I appointed you shepherd of my sheep, so that you might preserve them in holy religion, teach them, instruct them, amend them, and correct those who sinned. But you hated discipline and cast [my?] words behind you; if you saw a thief, you ran with him, and with adulterers you placed your portion. Intent on the pleasures of the flesh, you did not guard my sheep; therefore pay what you owe. After monks came from the world to religious life, they became subject to my right, and it is not lawful for them to return any further to the world. But you were appointed minister of my sheep, not lord, nor was it lawful for you in any way to alienate, usurp, or plunder anything belonging to my right. You were guardian of my things, not owner; you ought faithfully to preserve what had been acquired and to seek what had been scattered.

But you, forgetful of me, squandered my inheritance, scattered the sheep, indeed made beasts from sheep, enemies from servants, and faithless men from allies. Now therefore pay what you owe. I entrusted monks to you, whom you ought to have preserved in their holy purpose: to raise up the fallen, care for the sick, restore the failing, kindle the sluggish with charity, rouse the negligent, rebuke sinners, correct the disobedient, restrain the dissolute, and hold back the perverse from their evil pursuits. But you took care of none of those things which [unclear] by office; given over to greed, lust, and gluttony, you not only permitted monks to return to the world, but, utterly forgetful of pastoral care, you also became the leader and companion of their apostasy. Now therefore give back what [was entrusted?] to your faith, not to say to your treachery. But because you will not be able to give it back, you will be cast into a dark prison covered with the gloom of death; from there you will not come out until you pay the debt, which you will never pay.
