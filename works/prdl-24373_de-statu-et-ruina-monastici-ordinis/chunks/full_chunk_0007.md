The cloisters of monks become dwellings of clerics, and from evil pursuits monks are transferred to the worst. You err, O wisdom of the world; power of the age, you are deceived: never from a bad monk will you be able to make a good canon. But you also, transgressors, take heed; apostates from the order, listen. What madness drives you, that you destroy for Saint Benedict monasteries which you did not build? Or do you think yourselves free from damnation if, after casting off the monastic garb, you sin as clerics? In the strict examination, will a presumptuous license to sin save you? Perhaps in another habit you will be unknown to [unclear]? We shall see whether God approves your transfer, when in that dread judgment, where no rank but perpetual horror dwells. For what other dwelling he is to receive, having become a worse canon from a bad monk, I do not sufficiently understand. Senseless men, and enemies to yourselves, who will teach you to flee from the wrath to come? Who will be able, or will dare, to defend your unjust cause against Saint Benedict in that dread examination? Whom will you have as advocate for your rashness? Saint Benedict, prince of monks, will stand as witness against you; he will accuse you and condemn you: who will excuse you? Do not flatter yourself, wicked contemner of the regular vow; do not soothe your conscience. If you have Benedict as your adversary, you will find none of the saints your friend. For he who offends one citizen of the heavenly fatherland [unclear]. Where there is the highest and blessed tranquility, there also will be the indivisible unity of souls. Who, then, will be able to deliver you from the hand of the Lord, if all the saints have stood against you? Call upon those who now deceive you, who take from you the habit of the order, who lead you away with flattering speeches, who stand as collaborators in your apostasy, that they may defend you from the tribulation that is at hand. For if the righteous will scarcely be saved, where will the apostate and destroyer of the holy order appear? The justice of the saint will not deliver the wicked man on that day, nor will he bring aid to himself or another. Rather, those whom you now win over to yourself with gifts and presents as such great collaborators in your presumption, then you will have as witnesses and companions of your wickedness.

There will be no defender there for a corrupt action, no advocate of iniquity, since where all things will be laid bare, there the accusing conscience will be enough for each one unto damnation. There the army of saints will stand against those who have wickedly profaned the dwellings of monks, who have impiously destroyed the order of the holy fathers. But the authors of these things, which I seem to myself justly to have called unjust, strive to excuse their deed in this way, saying: The irregularity of monks, driving monasteries to extreme poverty, cannot be corrected and amended unless it is transferred into another state, on account of the contrived reasons which they invent for themselves; these also, God granting, we shall show in what follows to be empty. They say, then: To correct bad monks by transfer is not only without sin, but is also a great part of justice and piety. But where can these things be discerned? In what manner will you teach as true the things you say? To convert bad monks to goodness is praiseworthy and holy; but to make them canons is not to amend monks, but to have corrupted them. A bad monk will never be a good canon; but a good one will not strip off the order that is his, but will cherish, honor, and love it. I do not now wish to contend in words; I do not wish to be too vehement for the hearing; I do not wish to depict the effect of this transfer, which I do not doubt will gradually come to light. If you wished to make bad monks into canons, why did you not put good men in their place? There is no change without transgression of the vow, when it is presumptuously changed for the worse. Woe to you deserters, who, so that satisfaction may be made to your pleasures, despise the order, deny the vow, and confound the rule of equity. We shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ, says the Apostle, so that each one may receive according as he has done in his body. What will the destroyers of monasteries receive there, who show no deference to the honor of the Almighty, who violate the covenant, who nullify the wills of saints, who despise the decrees of law? If it is good to found monasteries, it is evil, let no one doubt, to destroy them. A contrary action will receive no reward except a contrary one.
