Now the greedy man is believed learned; now he is called religious who joins field to field, who keeps watch over earthly gains, who loosens his appetite for gathering riches. But believe me, fathers, since I am going to speak the truth: temporal riches do not make discipline stable in the order. O what a terrible sentence abbots will undergo: those who neglect the Rule, who do not go before their monks in word and by the example of holiness, who despise spiritual exercises, who devote themselves to earthly gains, who scorn the discipline of monastic honor. Would that they were wise and understood, and foresaw the last things; and either governed carefully the burden of so great a rule, or, if they could not, humbly laid it down. Happy is he who stands solicitous upon his watch, who always does the will of the Lord, who lives without pretense according to the purity of the Rule.

But for the present these things which we have said concerning the state and reformation of our order, as I believe, are sufficient; let the reading of them also obtain its end at the same boundary as the chapter. Moreover, all and each of the things which I have written in this treatise or in others, or shall write, I submit to the examination of the sacrosanct Roman Church, whose faith, doctrine, and definition, so long as I live, I wish always and everywhere to observe, teach, and defend. But if anything, which God forbid, has been said by me otherwise than she holds, teaches, and believes, I now by these presents simply revoke it, and wish it to be regarded as not said. By this my protestation I wish and hold the supreme pontiff, the vicar of Christ, to be in all things the judge and moderator of my faith and doctrine.

The present treatise was read in the provincial chapter of the order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz, celebrated at Hirsau at table, in the year of the Lord 1493, on Jubilate Sunday and on the following days until the end. With all the abbots gathered there wishing and consenting, it was decreed that it should be printed and in future provincial chapters always be read publicly at table, as is clear in the statutes.
