*That visitors ought to be solicitous for the reform of our order. Chapter 10.*

But the chief force of reform is placed almost entirely in diligence. Therefore it is said in the constitutions of the fathers that suitable men should be deputed to the office of visitation, who by word and example can effectively instruct those to be visited in honorable conduct. For a visitor must be learned in divine law; he must be holy and irreproachable in life, so that by word he may teach the teachable, and by example instruct the obstinate. For one without the other will not suffice in a ruler of souls. For if he is ignorant, how will he instruct others? If his conduct does not provide those to be visited with an example of good action, how will he compel them to reform? He will indeed hear the objection of the proverb, which says: Physician, heal yourself. And not undeservedly. For by what presumption are you led, O wordy visitor, that you attempt to reform others, while until now you lie in your own deformity? You are polluted with the filth of vices, and will you be able to cleanse others? Believe me: the Holy Spirit will not give power to your words, when by a contrary life you offend his grace. Speech lacks efficacy in the heart of the hearer when the conduct of the preacher condemns it. What shall I say? I lie if it is not often the disobedient man who rebukes the disobedient, the foul man who defames the foul man, and he believes that he has escaped the witness, as though conscience were not enough.
