Now therefore my discourse must be directed to you, most reverend fathers: you who, by apostolic authority, celebrate the order; you who receive the power of compelling rebels to discipline; you who have zeal for our order according to knowledge. You, therefore, I address; to you I shall try, according to my powers, to persuade that you come to the aid of an order almost falling; and I most earnestly exhort you to do this.

Consider, fathers, the miserable condition of our religion, and, so far as you are able, come to the aid of its calamity, if indeed you wish to endure to the end. The wickedness of many is not hidden from you: in order that they may be able to escape the discipline of the order, they labor to dismember themselves from it. Their rashness must be firmly opposed, lest the unpunished malice of a few give many the boldness to offend. And the wickedness of a few, when deferred, always increases. Although the public good ought not to be corrupted by the evil of one person, nevertheless the ignorant crowd blames all for the fault of a few. Therefore, those evil men who by their own deformity cloud the reputation of the good are strengthened by superiors who do not punish them.

It therefore belongs to you, fathers, to correct deformed monks as far as possible; it is the duty of your office to preserve the order in its vigor. And because a fool is not corrected by speech, what our hierophant set down against the incorrigible, as it were monastically, should generally be referred to [unclear]. Rise up now, fathers, for it is time. Arm yourselves with zeal for regular discipline. I beg you, defend the inheritance of the blessed father Benedict. Drive out the proud; correct the rebellious; rebuke the hard. Fight bravely for the preservation of our order. Arm yourselves with the armor of God and with love of the order, about to fight not so much by your own labors as by the help of Christ. His grace will furnish you aid, if with purity of heart you pour out devout prayers to him.

Why then do you dissemble? Why do you not defend the portion of Saint Benedict? How long will the efforts of the wicked prevail against the holy order? Truly, fathers, truly, the great necessity of the order demands a swift remedy. Consider the conduct of irregular abbots and monks, and weigh how wickedly they kick against the discipline of the order. Apart from the habit, and that itself in many cases dissolute, they show nothing honorable or religious. But reserving these things for their appointed places, let us hasten to what we have intended.

*On the Most Holy Primitive State of Our Order. Chapter 2.*

I somewhat doubt whether I shall do a thing pleasing to all if I commend in a few words the former state of our order, since I judge that by the praises of the ancients I have paid little regard to the monks of our own time. For there are some who think themselves either the best monks, or dare to compare themselves with those best fathers who preceded us. However they measure themselves, let them not be angry with me if I do not dare to equate our observance with that of the ancients. For if we contemplate their life by a right light, we shall scarcely be able to compare the best among us, in habit, with a good monk of that time.

Indeed, their holy way of life, bright with virtues, approved by signs, and illustrious through instruction in all the Scriptures, won favor with God and with men. For hearing the voice of the Lord saying, “If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me,” they flew to monasteries, despising the world; and, placing their necks under the yoke of the rule, they humbly took up the norm of obedience. Loving God with their whole heart, they sought nothing worldly in this life; but, all perishable things despised, and tested in the furnace of poverty, they happily arrived at the heavenly kingdom.

They were mature men, zealous, and most learned: firm in faith, strengthened in hope, shining with twofold charity; pure in mind, outstanding in devotion, and adorned with the honor of every virtue. They had no care for earthly things, no enticement of pleasures was in them; but, turned toward God with all their powers, they conquered the world with its vanities. Hence kings and princes of this age, devout men and dear to God, because of the most holy way of life of the monks, became lovers of the order, and magnificently exalted it with gifts and largesses.
