Do not suppose, you vilest knaves, who taste nothing spiritual, that Christ will come again from heaven to speak to you the things which he entrusted to his preachers to say. Or have you not heard Paul saying, “Christ, rising from the dead, now dies no more; death shall no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God”? Once Christ was seen on earth; once he appeared visibly among mortals; once he preached the way of eternal salvation; he will not add a second time.

He appointed shepherds and vicars over his household to teach the ignorant, and, wishing to make their authority plain, said in the Gospel: “He who hears you hears me, and he who rejects you rejects me.” Therefore hear those whom Christ has set over you as rulers, to whom he entrusted the dispensing of spiritual wheat, as he said in the holy Gospel: “Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward whom the Lord has set over his household, to give them their measure of wheat in due season? Blessed is that servant whom, when the Lord comes, he shall find so doing.”

Therefore we exhort you, brothers, in the Lord Jesus, and with all the earnestness we can, we beg you not to consume your days in vanity, but consider the excessive brevity of this life, and bring forth worthy fruits of repentance while the opportunity is granted you, lest, if you neglect to repent, the time be cut off. For you know what the divine Scripture says to us: “The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night.” Beware, I pray you, of putting off the good, because none of you knows when he is going to die. Rather, attend to that true saying of the wanton poet Naso, who warns thus: “But hasten, and do not put yourself off to the coming hours; he who is not ready today will be less fit tomorrow.”

Let no one, brothers, promise himself a long life, but let each instead think that death is near at hand; and therefore let him live so prepared as if he were to depart from the body tomorrow. For the hope of a longer life has deceived many, and, holding them back from the fervor of a holy purpose, has suddenly plunged them down to the lower regions. Therefore fulfill your warfare, which of necessity will end before long, in such a way that you do not fear to appear before your king when he comes.

Always remember that you have here no lasting city, nor can you remain long in this mortal life; but death will come quickly and compel you to depart. For whoever is a true monk and desires to be a faithful soldier of Christ contemns all the delights of this world, always thinks of his Lord as present to him, and, having cast aside every hindrance and every little burden, cares above all how he may please him. He ought not to be anxious in seeking the necessities of life, since the providence of the shepherd provides all these things for those subject to him. Let the whole care of the monk be to keep himself unstained from this world, so that, free and released from all things, he may seek nothing but God and think of nothing but Jesus crucified for us. O happy soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised, if he remains faithful, prudent, and watchful in the love of God to the end.
