Two judgments are impending, neither of which we shall be able to escape; rather, necessity compels us to appear at both. One is called the particular judgment, in which, immediately after death, the soul is judged according to its merits, either to grace or to punishment. The other is named the general judgment, which will be at the end of the age, where we shall all be judged publicly by the only-begotten Son of God.

Both judgments are exceedingly terrible and greatly to be feared by all mortals. Hence the prophet Joel says: “Great is the day of the Lord, and very terrible; and who shall endure it?” Likewise in Ecclesiastes King Solomon says: “All things that are done under the sun God will bring into judgment, judging every error, whether it be good or evil.” In Isaiah also it is read thus: “The Lord will come to judgment with the elders of his people and its princes.” And in the book of the prophet Amos it is written: “Woe to those who desire the day of the Lord. What is it to you? For that day of the Lord is darkness and not light.” Zephaniah also, the prophet of the Lord, foretelling the bitterness of the future judgment, said: “The voice of the day of the Lord is bitter; the strong man will be troubled there. That day is a day of wrath, a day of tribulation and distress, a day of calamity and misery, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and whirlwind. In the fire of the Lord’s zeal the whole earth will be devoured, because he will make a consummation with haste upon all who dwell on the earth.”

This day, exceedingly terrible and fearful to all mortals, which you believe will certainly come, dearest brothers, you should always keep in memory. For the most holy bishop Ambrose says: “There is nothing that profits more toward an honorable life than that we believe there will be a judge whom neither the hidden things of minds deceive nor shameful things delight.” For all of us, as holy Christophilus says, must be made manifest before the tribunal of Christ, so that each may receive the things proper to the body, according as he has done, whether good or evil.

How much that future day of terrible judgment is to be feared, the holy priest Jerome indicates, saying: “Whether I eat, or drink, or do anything else, that voice always seems to sound in my ears: Rise, you dead, come to judgment. Whenever I think of the day of judgment, I tremble in my whole heart and body.” Therefore, if there is any joy in the present life, it must be carried on in such a way that the bitterness of the future judgment never withdraws from memory.

What shall we wretches say on that day, or how shall we stand before the strict judge, if, as the divine apostle Peter says, scarcely will the just man be saved? If Jerome, a just and most holy man, feared the future judgment, O good God, how will Trithemius, an unhappy sinner, appear? If the just man fears, how will the sinner stand? For what shall we say, or what shall we answer, accused heavily on every side? All the elements will stand against us as witnesses; earth and heaven together will accuse us. But even if all things were silent, each one’s own conscience will be a heavy accuser. He who knows all things is ignorant of nothing, for there all things will be manifest to the judge. The evil things we have done will be there, accusing us heavily; they will be present all around as witnesses [unclear].
