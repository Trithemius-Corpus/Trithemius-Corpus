How Sin Is to Be Fled.

Since he is good, in him alone are the soul’s rest and salvation; without him there is no salvation, no rest, no blessedness, no happiness, neither in heaven nor on earth. For since God is the cause, maker, and beginning of all things, he alone is the object of the soul’s blessedness; to his everlasting enjoyment the soul comes only through charity, and to the love of him it comes only through purity of mind.

Therefore, since God is the soul’s one and highest good, he is to be loved by us for his own sake with all our strength. Outside God there is nothing good, nothing holy, nothing that can gladden the soul forever. For God alone is the center of the soul’s blessedness.

Second, we are commanded to love the Lord our God because he deigns to show himself our most loving father. For thus he taught us to pray: Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name. Truly God is the father of us all: he created us, nourishes all, feeds all, and preserves us in being, lest we perish. There has never been any father on earth so indulgent that he loved his only-begotten son with so great a charity as God almighty, our father, loves, cherishes, guides, and follows us with loving affection. And therefore we are bound to love him with a pure heart above all things, for he gives us both being and life. He indeed is our father; and Adam, who was created first of all men, did not know us.

Third, we ought to love God above all things because of likeness, since man was made in God’s image, following his creator by a certain likeness in his intellect. But God is perfect, infinite, and everlasting intellect; and our intellect, sharing in his goodness through grace, is made blessed in him who created all things. For although there is no comparison between God and man, nevertheless there is, from the nobility of its condition, a likeness of dignity not to be despised between the rational soul itself and its principle. For the Prophet says: I said, You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High. The Lord God also said in the beginning: Let us make man to our image and likeness. Great is the condescension of divine loving-kindness toward our race, which it befits us at all times to remember with a pure mind of charitable reverence.

Fourth, we ought to love our God above all things because of his inestimable generosity, for we acknowledge that he has bestowed such great benefits on us though we did not deserve them. When we did not exist, he first, by his goodness, established us in being, and ordained us to a share in his glory, which will never end. For Scripture says this of the nobility of man: Thou hast made him a little less than the angels; with glory and honor thou hast crowned him; and thou hast set him over the works of thy hands.

Do you wish to know, brothers, in what respect man is less than the angel? Attend diligently to what we say. Two intellects were created from the beginning, the angelic and the human.
