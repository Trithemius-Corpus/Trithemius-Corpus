Homily I.

Consider your calling: the Lord has called you not to idleness, not to the pleasures of this world, nor to the pursuit of the desires of the flesh, but rather to continual labor, to abstinence from all delights, and to the constant mortification of the flesh under the image of the Crucified, so that you may bear his wounds in your hearts. Surely you have now put your hands to the plough of the holy heavenly husbandry, in which you are commanded to sweat continually in labor, and not only never to look back, but, by advancing always from virtue to virtue, to strive toward the summit of highest perfection.

For to live as a monk is nothing other than to withdraw from the company of men and, in solitude, actively to please God alone through the desire of most fervent love. Hence Scripture says: It is good for a man when he has borne the yoke of the Lord from his youth; he shall sit alone and be silent, because he shall lift himself above himself. Therefore, if you wish to be perfect, you who by your regular institution are bound to love perfection, begin from the way of life that Christ taught; and since you already despise all things for love of him, do not neglect humbly to conquer, overcome, and despise even your very selves.

The servant of Christ has nothing except Christ, loves nothing, cares for nothing, seeks nothing except Christ, so that he may be perfect and please him whose service as a soldier he has voluntarily professed. But the monk who has anything besides Christ cannot be perfect, because true love is not divided, nor is the mind truly united to things diverse and contrary. Therefore the Lord says: If you wish to be perfect, go and sell all that you have and give to the poor, and come, follow me.

You, my most beloved brothers, have already, as I trust, left the world and whatever you had in the world; and not only all the things of the world that you possessed and could have possessed, but you have also laid aside every desire of having. You have come to this monastery of the holy bishop Martin, in which the poverty of temporal goods is great, and this alone has been given to us in place of riches: not to find occasions for avarice.

Yet just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so want does not make a blessed poor man, but voluntary patience in that very poverty for the love of Christ. For in the Gospel the Lord did not proclaim all poor people blessed and destined to receive the kingdom of heaven, but he foretold that the poor in spirit are blessed. For contempt of riches is not enough for salvation unless the one who renounces gold also strives with all his strength, through patience and humility, constantly to imitate the Savior.

For it is easier to despise the world than to scorn and deny oneself. Yet whoever wishes to imitate the Son of God and attain evangelical perfection must become a despiser not only of the world and temporal things, but also of his own self. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: Unless someone renounces all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple. And lest we should think this sufficient for perfection, he again bears witness more openly concerning contempt of oneself, saying: If anyone wishes to follow me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his life will lose it; but whoever loses his life for my sake and for the Gospel will save it.

Christ became obedient to the Father even unto death. If, as you have promised, you wish to imitate him, brothers, it is necessary that you deny yourselves to yourselves. For no one will be able to be a monk who has not first renounced his own will entirely. You who before God and all his saints have promised to live according to the monastic rule may no longer claim your own will for yourselves, because what has once been consecrated to God must no longer be recalled to human uses.

For you serve as soldiers not for yourselves but for Christ the King; hence you do not retain in your own power either to will or not to will. For you are dead, as the holy Christophilus says, and your life is hidden with Christ in God; but when Christ your life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory. If you desire to rejoice with him without end, you must now, in this present time, fight bravely for love of him. For no one will be crowned with Christ unless he has persevered to the end, striving lawfully, since he cannot conquer who does not know how to persevere.
