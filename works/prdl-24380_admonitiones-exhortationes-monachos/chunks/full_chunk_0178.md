Sermon II.

...he had established for the good of the reform, he obtained through the Apostolic See. The first abbot of Bursfeld in the reform was appointed John Monk of Reinhausen; the second was the John of Hagen already mentioned; the third was Theodoric, whom we have seen; and the fourth is John, who has now recently been appointed to govern.

We have therefore, dearly beloved brothers, briefly touched upon the origin of our reform, so that you may know with how much labor it began, and may persevere constant unto death in holy regular observance. For among all the reforms of our order that have preceded it, there has never been one more conformable and agreeable to the Rule of the blessed father Benedict, nor more alien from foreign inventions. We have read the statutes of almost all the other reforms, but we judge none more agreeable than ours to the Rule according to the purity of Benedict’s intention. This is the most holy Bursfeld reform in the order, which both teaches true obedience and preserves subjection in humility. In this holy religious life there live monks who are poor and without private property; they live humble, they live obedient, they live chaste.

O venerable union of monks, in which doubled charity commands, obedience serves as a soldier, humility complies, prayer keeps watch, perseverance fights, holy chastity triumphs. This is the school of regular discipline, the teacher of Christian humility, which directs conduct toward uprightness and restrains the vices of the flesh. In this arena of virtues no one is permitted to follow his own will against the Rule; no one is forbidden to do good. In this holy union life is lived according to the purity of the Rule, and if anyone begins to stray from the path of uprightness, he is soon recalled by the diligence of the elders to the observance of justice. The Rule of the most holy father Benedict is read publicly three times a year, so that no one may try to excuse himself from observance under the guise of ignorance.

There are two things that promise me great hope for the perseverance of this holy reform: namely, the annual chapter and the careful visitation of the monks every two years. If either of these is omitted at any time, the reform is at once endangered. Therefore consider now, dearly beloved brothers, and take the utmost care, as far as you can, that you do not uselessly occupy this monastery of the blessed bishop Martin under the title of so venerable a reform; for unless you live according to the Rule and the statutes of the order, purely, holily, and blamelessly, you trust in the name of God in vain.

We shall give you a few admonitions for your salvation; by the zealous observance of them you will deservedly possess both the name and the dignity of our Bursfeld reform. First of all, direct your striving with all your desires toward monastic perfection; and whenever you feel it growing lukewarm, renew it at once by attentive remembrance of the Lord’s Passion and most innocent death. For even if we are not bound by necessity of salvation to be perfect, nevertheless we are bound to strive for perfection with all our desires. For as soon as a monk casts aside the effort to make progress, he falls into the pit of falling away.
