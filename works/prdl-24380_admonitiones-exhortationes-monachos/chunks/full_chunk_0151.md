Brotherly charity is formidable to demons, since it makes victorious all whose courtyard of the heart it surrounds. Charity extinguishes the fire of lust and covers the whole multitude of sins. Charity makes a man most dear to God and makes him a companion of the holy angels. Charity preserves the Church in the unity of the saints; outside it no one obtains eternal salvation. Brotherly charity orders the conduct of cloistered men and causes God to dwell in the midst of monks. Charity does injury to no one, but ministers help to all through compassion. Charity drives out every vice, because it is the one nurse of all virtues. Charity keeps the reform of brotherhood in vigor among cloistered men and works great salvation for souls. Mutual charity strengthens obedience and makes brotherly correction fruitful. Charity nourishes true concord among the brothers, and preserves a sweet-smelling chastity of both minds and bodies. Charity makes monks of one mind in the house of God and praiseworthily harmonious in every good work. Mutual charity lightens the rigor of the order, and all things that seem hard and rough to carnal people it changes through habit into nature, as though they were very light. Charity is the most beautiful beauty of monks, adorned with all virtues. Charity utterly drives hatred and envy from the monasteries of God’s servants, and by its presence drives far away the other crimes of death. The charity of the brothers is a fitting dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and a fortress of serene conscience. Charity is the servant of justice and the sole preserver of regular discipline in the monastery. Charity keeps the mind harmless from cruelty and supplies to all a peaceful gentleness of spirit. Brotherly charity seeks the salvation of all and does not allow the one who possesses it to grow sluggish [unclear]. Charity does not loathe want, but is ready, for love of the brothers, to endure every poverty. Charity is the water of heavenly sweetness; if anyone drinks it in the fervor of the Holy Spirit, he does not fear the everlasting thirst of hell. Brotherly charity does not seek the enticements of the flesh, but directs pure, holy, and undefiled affections toward one’s neighbor. Charity bears the name of chaste beauty, which cannot be shared with the things or actions of most impure pleasure. Charity loves purity and cleanness, and admits no impurity. In the work of love, charity does not consider the flesh of one’s neighbor, but the soul, which it does not doubt was created in the image of God. Brotherly charity raises up a sinning neighbor with pious admonitions, and keeps all its actions pure and harmless from every malice. Charity walks in peace and tranquility of mind, and saddens one’s neighbor neither by word, nor by deed, nor by sign. Charity humbles itself in all things as far as it can, and most diligently [unclear] for a neighboring brother who is weak, as for a son of God.
