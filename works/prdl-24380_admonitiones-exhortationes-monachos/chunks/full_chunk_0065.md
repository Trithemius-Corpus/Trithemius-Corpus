It is a sign if a Christian man is struck by various adversities in this world, one who, serving God with a pure conscience, lives in true humility without offense. For the merciful God often strikes in various ways in this world those whom he loves, for whom he has prepared eternal rewards in heaven. Some, indeed, knowing that they are very capable of sinning, he forestalls by bodily illness so that they may not sin, and mercifully scourges them for their salvation, seeing that it is more useful for their souls to be broken by sickness unto salvation than to live unharmed unto damnation. Others he wills to be poor, whom he foreknew would not be able to use riches for salvation. For the wealth and riches of this world have provided many with a cause of eternal damnation.

Hence holy Pope Gregory again says: Their own prosperity has laid many low more grievously; long-lasting peace has rendered many conquered; and the unexpected enemy strikes them the more severely because he finds them negligent through long habit of quiet. Hence holy men, when they consider that they are making great progress by the prosperity of virtues, rejoice that by a certain moderation of heavenly dispensation they are also exercised by temptations, because they guard the glory of the virtues they have received so much more sturdily, the more, shaken by the assault of temptation, they humbly acknowledge their own weakness.

The way of tribulation is a short road to Christ, and he is found in it more quickly than in tranquillity. For the holy bishop Ambrose says: There are many who seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and there are those who seek him in persecutions and quickly find him. He is present in the dangers of his faithful, just as he speaks of the righteous man in the psalm, saying: I am with him in tribulation; I will deliver him and glorify him. Let purity of conscience be guarded in adversity; let the inviolate integrity of life always be preserved; and whatever adversity there is will undoubtedly be for us a merit of salvation.

The ancient enemy, by contending, can overcome no one except one whom he has first found to have fallen away from himself. He seeks one sluggish in leisure; he trips up the unwary soldier; and he most easily casts down the one whom he finds stripped of arms. Therefore, my brothers, always keep watch in holy prayers; always be intent upon good works, so that whenever the tempter comes, he may never find you idle. Remember the words of our Savior Jesus Christ, saying in the holy Gospel: Let your loins be girded, and lamps burning in your hands, and be like men waiting for their lord, when he returns from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may open to him at once.

He keeps watch who, standing firm in the fear of the Lord, never dozes in sluggishness of mind. He keeps watch who, through the tireless constancy of a holy purpose, preserves his soul against any adversities of this world. He keeps watch, whoever does not cease daily to advance in the love of the Lord. He carries a lamp in his hands, whoever adorns the purpose of holiness with fitting works.
