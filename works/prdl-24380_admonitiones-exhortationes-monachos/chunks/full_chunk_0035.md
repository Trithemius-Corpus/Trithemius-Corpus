There are two intellects created from the beginning, which I know is hidden from none of you, dearest brothers: namely, the angelic and the human. Holy Scripture does not plainly disclose when the angelic intellect was created, but the judgment of the Fathers has counted the creation of the angels as a work of God and among the works of the six days. There are, however, some who write that the angels were created long before this visible world; and just as we cannot prove this by the testimony of the divine Scriptures, so neither do we rashly oppose it. For there are many weighty and holy men who placed the creation of the angels long before the beginning of the world.

The angelic intellect was made from the very beginning in act with respect to all knowable things: it knew everything that it could know, and thereafter admitted nothing further for its own perfection. For, created by the Lord in the natural perfection that it could have, it required no further means of increase by which it might understand. Indeed, since it is joined to God without intermediary, it was made a pure intellect by nature, and does not, as we do, need exercise in order to understand; its being admits no corruption. And so, cleaving to its object through the affection of love, it immediately and unchangeably contemplates and loves God, whom it understands to be the center of its blessedness; it is blessed, and fears no alteration of itself.

But the human intellect, which our teachers have called the soul, was indeed created from the beginning in complete ignorance, and by nature knows none of the things that the angelic intellect understands. These two intellects differ as act and potency, light and darkness, knowledge and ignorance, habit and privation, beginning and end, simple and composite, substance and accident, nature and art, truth and likeness. For all the things which, as divine Scripture testifies, are in the angelic intellect by nature and from the beginning, the human intellect will attain through art at the end, as the Lord Jesus says in the Holy Gospel: In the resurrection of the dead they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but will be like the angels of God in heaven.

For as the mind is trained toward understanding through many labors, as though through art, it has been placed for a short time in the prison-house of the body; and if there, turned toward God, it has duly completed its craft through love, then at last, departing from here, it becomes equal to an angel. But if, consenting to the motions of the flesh, it has not kept its order, disturbed and wretched, like the falling apostate spirits, from the body into eternity
