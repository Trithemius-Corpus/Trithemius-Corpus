We have heard, my dearest brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ commanding in the holy Gospel: “Whatever you wish that men should do to you, do likewise to them.” It is indeed a brief, but solid commendation of brotherly charity, without which no one can ascend to the love of God. For no one can love God who is proved not to love his fellow Christian. Hence the blessed apostle John says: “Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him.” Therefore, if anyone says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar.

For this reason holy Paul, the lover of Christ, exhorts us, saying: “Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.” And Peter, the prince of the apostles, admonished us in this way: “Before all things,” he says, “have constant mutual charity among yourselves, because charity covers a multitude of sins.”

How necessary brotherly charity is for salvation, Jesus Christ, the Lord of all, made clear when he said: “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind. This is the first and greatest commandment. The second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang the whole law and the prophets.” For whatever is commanded anywhere in the whole sequence of the Scriptures is without doubt referred to these two commandments.

Here we understand “neighbor” not as someone joined by kinship of blood, but as every human being joined by the fellowship of reason, and especially a Christian, whom each person is commanded to love as himself, since he is seen to await eternal salvation together with him.

But it especially befits you, brothers, to love one another in the true charity of Jesus Christ the Savior, since the monastic profession enjoins you to strive with all your strength toward the summit of perfection. For just as covetousness is the root of all evils, so charity is the root of all goods. First you must love God for his own sake, because he is the highest good, above all things with a pure heart; and then you must love your neighbor with sincere charity for the Lord’s sake.

These two precepts of charity have been joined together by Christ’s authority, so that neither can in any way be fulfilled without the other. For one who truly loves God cannot hate his neighbor. If he hates his neighbor, he does not truly love God. For the love of God brings forth love of neighbor, and through love of neighbor the love of God is nourished. If one of the two loves is lacking, the other does not grow strong. For one who does not perfectly love his neighbor shows himself empty of the love of God. Therefore we truly love God when we love our neighbor, that is, every Christian human being, more earnestly with sincere intention.
