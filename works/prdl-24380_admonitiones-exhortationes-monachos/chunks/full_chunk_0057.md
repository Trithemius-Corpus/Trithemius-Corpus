Or if a useless word is judged liable to the reckoning of a strict examination, what will he answer who so readily speaks what is evil and destructive?

Consider, brothers, what an idle word is; consider your speech, and let no one speak a word that does not pertain to the matter, or to rightful usefulness, or to necessary reason. For you have heard the fearful sentence of the Lord, and you are not ignorant of the command of our holy lawgiver in the Rule, when he says: “Scurrilities, idle words, and words provoking laughter we condemn everywhere to perpetual exclusion, and we do not permit the disciple to open his mouth for such speech.”

Here the holy father Benedict forbade monks three kinds of words, and condemned them everywhere to perpetual exclusion: namely buffoonery, idle words, and words provoking laughter. What buffoonery is, the explanation of the grammarians has made clear to us. For the vice of buffoonery is opposed to courtesy and modesty; it is a kind of wanton joking that shamelessly and dishonorably provokes immoderate laughter in hearers by words and gestures that do not accord with reason, do not befit a good man, and are not fitting for a wise man.

Buffoonery is so called from the buffoon, who follows the houses of the rich, provoking his hearers to laughter with wanton and indecent words and gestures, either [unclear] or for the sake of a gift. This kind of speech the holy father Benedict forbade to monks and utterly condemned, since those who serve God are rightly commanded to abstain perpetually in his sight from shameful and indecent words.

Between buffoonery and much speaking there seems to be this difference: the former goes beyond measure by reason of indecency, the latter by reason of superfluity. The former is shameful and indecent; the latter is superfluous and without usefulness. Foolish talk is rightly joined to these, since it is forbidden not only to monks but also to all Christians. Hence the holy apostle of Christ says: “But fornication, and all uncleanness, or covetousness, let it not even be named among you, as befits saints; nor filthiness, nor foolish talk, nor buffoonery, which is not fitting, but rather thanksgiving.”

Likewise an idle word is forbidden to all monks, since it is prohibited by the Lord, as you have heard, in the holy Gospel. Words that provoke laughter also do not befit Christian men, and much less are they suitable for those in the cloister. For the monk, who is dead to the world, is commanded always to think on his last things, and is commanded everywhere, for the love of God, to despise laughter and every insolence of this present life.

Laughter is a confused sound, immoderately announcing the inward gladness of the soul. Nothing that exceeds modesty and temperance is permitted to be present in monks, whose life and conduct belong not to flesh and blood, but to angelic purity.
