What do you have that you did not receive from his Rule? Who endowed the monastery entrusted to you? Was it not the devotion of princes and the alms of faithful peoples? Why, then, are you ungrateful for such great benefits? See, you think of nothing less than regular observance: you reject discipline; you spurn God, despise his precepts, and presume upon security. See, with the birds of the sky you sport against the Rule; see, with unclean dogs you hunt against the law of clerics; see, in the pleasures of the flesh you delight against decency; see, what is most shameful, despising your oath of chastity, you consume the property of Saint Benedict with prostitutes and pimps. You are not afraid to spend thirty or forty gold coins at the baths, and yet you refuse to offer even one to Saint Benedict? I lie if I have not known abbots who spent more than fifty florins at the hot springs, some of whom are foremost among those who resist the subsidies of the chapter. Others also among those who wish to be called Observant, unable to endure seclusion and quiet, and unable to remain long in the monastery, judge every occasion suitable by which they can migrate here and there at great expense.

What shall I say about buildings, both curious and superfluous, which are liable to blame no less than to expense? Walls are adorned with paintings, pinnacles gleam with gold, windows are marked with monuments; the poor cry out at the door, the sacred order, being in straits, asks for help, and no one deigns to assist. O abbot, servant of pleasure, cultivator of vanity, enemy of piety, you who shamefully pour out the substance of the monastery, you who drink wines from gold, you who deny aid to your order, how will you render an account of your stewardship to Saint Benedict? If the rich glutton was buried in hell because he did not give Lazarus help from his own goods for God’s sake, where will the abbot be buried who denied to his lord what belonged to another? If he who did not give alms from his own goods will burn forever, what does the abbot deserve who has committed robbery?

What do you have, O abbot, that you did not receive? For everything you possess belongs to the right of Saint Benedict. It is a moral principle for all that he who lives by the support of justice should dare to deny no one what is just. The definition of justice is to render to each what is owed to him by right. But we who are established in monasteries have nothing of our own, but possess another’s goods in good faith. What belongs to another is judged liable to restitution. For all things that princes and faithful peoples bestowed on monasteries for the sustenance of monks are remembered as having been handed over to Saint Benedict. If, then, you have squandered his substance, if you have despised rendering to him what is his, you will be subject to a twofold condemnation, since from your unfaithfulness you will be judged sacrilegious, and from your denial of trust, criminal.

Render therefore to each what is his, and do not usurp for yourself what is not yours. Give to your lawgiver what he rightly demands; give aid to his order, that it may subsist. Why do you not spend the subsidies owed to your religion? Why do you not render a part to him who bestowed the whole on you? Why do you deny a little to him from whom you have received much?
