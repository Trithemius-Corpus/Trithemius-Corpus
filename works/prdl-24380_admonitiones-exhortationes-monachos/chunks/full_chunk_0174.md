Courage of soul receives its strength from the love of Christ, and stands firm in the desire for virtue, no less in adversity than in prosperity. Courage walks the path of uprightness without fear, and to its enemies, by a certain holy pride, appears terrible and formidable. Courage of soul despises the injuries of the little and easily overcomes all the pleasures of the flesh. Courage lies hidden in prosperity, is shown in adversity, and is plainly manifested through patience. Strong in soul is not the man who avenges injuries inflicted on him, but the man who patiently endures them; and if he has brought his adversary into his power, he counts it vengeance enough that he could have taken revenge.

The courage of monks is to despise the world, conquer the flesh, spurn all the honors and pleasures of the world, and place all hope and confidence in God alone. The measure of courage is to be neither timid nor rash, since it advances on the straight way and declines neither to the right nor to the left. That monk is to be judged courageous who is temperate in all things, moderate, and just. Hence the most holy Pope Gregory says: The strength of the just is to conquer the flesh, to resist their own pleasures, to extinguish delight in the present life, to love the hardships of this world for the sake of eternal rewards, to despise the allurements of prosperity, and to overcome in the heart the fear of adversity. They became strong in battle, because they tame the flesh, shine with virtues, strengthen the spirit, despise earthly things, seek heavenly things; they can be killed, but they cannot be bent; nor do they, through weakness, fear to endure falsehoods, nor, when injured, are they ever silent about the truth.

Therefore the monk who is strong with true virtue neither dares rashly nor fears without judgment. For courage together with constancy is the royal road; from it the rash and obstinate man declines to the right, but the fearful, fainthearted, and timid man to the left. The monk constant and strong in soul never falls away from his purpose of holiness, but is neither crushed by adversities nor lifted up by prosperity. Hence also the most holy bishop Ambrose says: The fool is changed like the moon; but the wise man is not broken by fear, not changed by power, not puffed up by prosperity, not sunk by sorrows. For where wisdom and virtue are, there is constancy and courage of soul. Therefore the wise man is always the same in soul; he is neither diminished nor increased by changes of circumstances, nor, like a little child, does he waver, so as to be carried about by every wind of doctrine, but remains perfect in Christ, founded in charity and rooted in the perfection of faith.

Finally, my dearly beloved brothers, perseverance is necessary for you in every good and virtuous work; without it no one ever reaches the crown of glory. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: He who perseveres to the end will be saved. Take away perseverance, and virtue will have no reward, obedience no recompense, nor does [unclear] merit grace.
