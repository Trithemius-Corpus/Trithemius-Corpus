But you who are younger, considering their gray hair, show reverence with humility to your elders; and rejoice to have as your instructors those whose company you know to be more fervent in the observance of regular discipline. Beware of those who either walk restlessly or observe the purity of regular discipline too negligently.

Not all who are called elders are worthy of imitation by the young; nor do years make a monk venerable, but morals grounded in the love of God with prudence make him worthy of reverence. Therefore whoever among you, dearest brothers, desires to be a true lover of God, whoever desires to advance in the holy monastic way of life that he professes, let him always flee the company of undisciplined brothers, and seek good fellow-soldiers who agree with his purpose. For the prophet says in the Psalm: With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man you will be innocent, and with the chosen you will be chosen, and with the perverse you will be perverted.

For evil company has harmed many; and he who delights to dwell with the reprobate will not be free from the contagion of sin. Indeed, everyone who keeps company with the wicked and the reprobate must have the integrity of his life somewhat corrupted by their perversity. For either he loves them and becomes evil like them, or he will hate them and be the enemy of many, and for that reason necessarily restless. But if, overcome by the multitude of the wicked, he once begins to delight in their company, then he too has already been depraved, and little by little he will be depraved still more. But if he detests the works of the wicked, he will labor under a grave trouble of mind, and his desire will be so much slower in advancing toward the better, the more he is disturbed by the company of the perverse.

Yet I give thanks to Almighty God for you, because, even if not all of you are fervent in the love of God as I would wish, nevertheless, as I trust, you all live far removed and estranged from great sins and from the rashness of pride. My exhortations and fatherly admonitions, which I give you as I am bound to do, you willingly receive every day, although you do not yet all display them perfectly in deed. But I hope in the Lord Jesus that he will bring to perfection in you the good that he has begun, so that my joy may be fulfilled and I may see you all, with one zeal, one purpose, one blaze of holy fervor, delighting and exercising yourselves in the workshop of divine love.

But since some of you delay this desire of mine, not yet taking up the spirit of fuller amendment that I desire, I am compelled by just necessity to admonish those who are making progress, that, rising up with a manly spirit, they flee the private and harmful associations of the lukewarm. For the fire of divine love is still small in their hearts; the flame of holy fervor that burns in them is still new and weak, and unless it is fostered by daily increases, it will easily suffer loss.
