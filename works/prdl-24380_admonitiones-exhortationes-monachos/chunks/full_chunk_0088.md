Homily XIII.

On the True Penance of Monks. Homily XIII.

Although our whole way of life, my most beloved brothers, may rightly be called penance, since we seem both dead to ourselves and already buried to the world, nevertheless, because we are surrounded by mortal flesh and live as pilgrims in the midst of a sinful nation, it cannot happen that, driven by our own frailty, we do not offend daily in some matter. For the expiation of this, satisfaction must be made by penance not only generally but also specifically.

For penance is necessary for all mortals: by it they both lament the evils they have committed, with a purpose of amendment, and resolve thereafter, as far as can be done, to live without sin. It is proper to monks to do penance without interruption and continually to pray both for their own sins and for those of others. For a monk does not have the office of one who teaches, as Saint Jerome says, but of one who laments: either, if weak, he mourns himself, or, if perfect, the world, and in fear awaits the coming of the Lord.

If you are a perfect monk, do penance for the sins of others; but if you are weak, you must do penance all the more for your own. Therefore a monk must always do penance, for he is appointed before God as an intercessor not only for his own sins but also for the offenses of others.

But who among you, brothers, is not weighed down by his own fault? Who among you, I ask, does not need penance? I see you offending daily in many things, and I grieve; I perceive how you neglect penance for past sins, and I tremble. Do you not recognize that you are mortal? And why do you not live up to the name of monk, so that you do the necessary penance while there is time?

Remember, I beg you, the threatening Scripture, which says: “Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day. For his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you.” For death will not pass you by; it is at the door for you old men, and lies hidden in ambush for the younger. But when you least expect it, the spirit, driven from the body, will depart.

Therefore, if you acknowledge that you are sinners, if you desire to come to the mercy of God, from now on, I beg you, take up penance, and amend your conduct in all things according to the purity of the rule. Let it be enough that until now you have neglected true penance and have continued for so long in a way of life that is far too lukewarm. For God, who overlooks the sins of men for the sake of penance, has not promised a prolonging of life to those who defer repentance.

Consider the long-suffering of God, who has mercifully put up with you for so long while you sin daily, and do penance, so that you may find the remission you desire. For the Lord says through the prophet: “I do not will the death of the wicked, but that he be converted from his evil way and live.” He also says in the holy Gospel: “Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near.” Penance is a great good [unclear].
