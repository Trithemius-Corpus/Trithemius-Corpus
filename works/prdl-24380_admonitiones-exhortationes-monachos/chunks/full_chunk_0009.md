Whenever, dearest brothers, the recollection of that moment comes to mind, when you made me, protesting and resisting, your unworthy abbot over you, just so often I am wholly shaken within myself by the bitterness of my heart, and I am compelled, even unwillingly and against my will, to shed tears. For I know that it was not because of my own merits that I was set over you; but whether it was because of yours, I do not know. May almighty God grant that this appointment of me was not made because of my demerits or yours.

Why did you wish it so, brothers? You raised me up, young, unlearned, and in no way fit for the warfare which we profess, to be your leader, so that now it is altogether necessary for me both to teach and to learn, since neither the perfection of my life commends my teaching, nor does regard for my age yet obtain for a shepherd the necessary authority. In these three years now I have experienced enough how great are the miseries in which I am entangled, since I alone am compelled to bear the weaknesses of you all, and I, unhappy man, am supported by no one.

Who will deliver me from the vanities of this world, into which I have been recalled, I who entered the monastery in order to flee every care and anxiety of this present life? But since indeed it is necessary to bear the burden laid upon my shoulders, I beg you, brothers: diligently stretch out to me the helping hands of your prayers, so that in your midst, by the Lord’s bounty, I may deserve to conduct myself in such a way that I do not transgress the condition of the spiritual warfare which the Rule prescribes for us.

For we are professed soldiers of the same warfare, and the vow does not distinguish the duty by which, at the beginning of this life, solemn profession made me, shepherd together with the sheep, bound. Therefore, when I speak to you about the spiritual warfare which, by God’s mercy, we have professed, my intention is, so far as the Lord Jesus will deign to grant, both to shake myself out of the feathers of sloth and lukewarmness into the fervor of holy newness, and to stir all of you, according to my strength, to the keeping of military order. For we are frail and weak, and we do not remain long steadfast in our holy purpose unless by daily exercises we continually renew the fervor of the spirit.

By reason of my office, I am bound to set before you in common those things which can serve your salvation. It belongs to you to listen patiently in all things to me as I speak, as the shepherd of your souls. For although, being young, I am not fit to teach so great a multitude of elders, nevertheless, because you appointed me your shepherd, I am compelled, under necessity of salvation, to feed the flock which I have undertaken to govern. What is mine to do, I both do and will gladly do according to my strength, as long as I live in this solicitude of pastoral care: young though I am, I will teach you elders, and without ceasing I will show you how you ought to serve as soldiers for him into whose army you have sworn.

For the dreadful warning of our lawgiver does not escape me, when he says: “Let the abbot know that whatever profit the father of the household has been unable to find in the sheep will be laid to the fault of the shepherd.” And what follows concerns you, my brothers, if, while I preach and admonish, contempt for the word of God should arise. “Only then,” he says again, “will he be free, if all diligence has been applied by the shepherd to a restless or disobedient flock, and every care has been shown for their diseased actions; then their shepherd, absolved in the judgment of the Lord, may say with the prophet: Lord, I have not hidden your justice in my heart; I have spoken your truth and your salvation. But they, despising, have rejected me. And then at last, for the sheep disobedient to his care, let death itself, prevailing over them, be their punishment.”

Here no covering of excuse is left; no place for escape is granted, but the danger of both parties is openly described. For I, holding the place of abbot, if I do not announce to you the justice of God, if I do not correct you when you sin according to the Rule, have denied my office; and whatever holiness is found lacking in you will be wholly imputed to me. For by reason of the pastoral office it is mine to tell you what is expedient for the salvation of your souls; but it is yours humbly to hear the things which I shall proclaim in the name of the Savior, and, as far as each one is able, faithfully to fulfill them with a great heart.

You have heard my danger if I do not cry out; you have also heard yours, if you despise me when I preach the word of God to you. But so that, by the Savior’s mercy, we may all escape in safety, let each one of us diligently pursue what belongs to him in this warfare [unclear].
