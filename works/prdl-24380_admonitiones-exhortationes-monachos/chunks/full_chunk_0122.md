God’s providence is consulted, so that by benefits they may be stirred toward better things. “I was young,” says the prophet, “and now am old, and I have not seen the just man forsaken, nor his seed begging bread.” “Cast,” he says, “your thought upon the Lord, and he will nourish you; he will not allow the just man to waver forever.”

The Lord Jesus knows what is fitting for the salvation of each one of his little servants, and therefore he distributes the gifts of the present life unequally, according to the capacity of each individual. For not everything is suitable for everyone; therefore among the just in this world some are poor in temporal goods, while others are wealthy. Concerning the poor the Lord Jesus says in the holy Gospel: “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” And through the prophet he speaks, saying: “Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.”

For it is not conducive to eternal salvation for everyone to possess the riches of this world; rather, it is expedient for some to be poor and for others to be wealthy, according as the Lord of all has provided what is more useful for each. Concerning just and good rich men Solomon says in the book of Proverbs: “The blessing of the Lord makes men rich, and affliction will not be joined to it.” But if evil and reprobate rich men have abundance in this age, good men ought not be moved to indignation, but should rather fear him who leaves no good unrewarded and no evil unpunished.

For the evil, whom because of their iniquity he cannot reward in heaven, he rewards in this life for their good deeds; and for the good, whom he nourishes here in poverty, he preserves eternal rewards in heaven for the merit of their justice. Yet just as not all the poor in this world are just and good, so not all who are rich in temporal things in this life are evil. The reward of the evil on earth is rightly to be borne without indignation, since not the love of God but his justice willed it to be present.

Therefore, just as the evil have nothing in heaven, so you, who have professed monastic poverty, ought to have nothing on earth; but in hope of that good toward which you are hastening, you ought to bear most patiently whatever adversity befalls you on the way. For, as Paul, the holy lover of Christ, says, “the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which shall be revealed in us.” Let those who are rich in Christ join mercy to humility; but let those who are poor take up patience with innocence for the love of God.

For the soldier of Christ is neither cast down by poverty nor lifted up by riches, but, standing firm in himself in spirit, serves the King of heaven in tranquility and cares nothing for transitory things. So too, he who is a true monk is neither puffed up by praises nor broken by blame; he does not swell with an abundance of necessary things, nor is he constricted by poverty; he despises both joyful and sorrowful things alike, because, as the prophet had said, “the sun shall not burn him by day, nor the moon by night.”

But we, dearest brothers, let us make virtue and a good will out of necessity; and from the hardship of poverty, which we suffer of necessity, let us make for ourselves something good and useful [unclear].
