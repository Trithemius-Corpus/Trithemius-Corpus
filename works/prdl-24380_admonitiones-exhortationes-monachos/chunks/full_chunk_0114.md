A certain other of the saints wished two or three hours to suffice a monk for sleeping, so that he might commend sparingness in food also by moderation in rest. But the more recent reformers of our order, who under the name of the Bursfeld Union handed down to us throughout Germany a manner of living according to the purity of the Rule, considering the weakness of the human condition, arranged the measure of vigils very moderately. For they mercifully allowed the monks in community a space of five hours before the nocturnal praises, and likewise added two, or two and a half, after matins for sleep. Whoever does not find this time, namely seven hours, sufficient for rest shows himself to be an excessively sluggish and slothful monk. Hence we are admonished to keep watch not only in mind but also in body, because he who disdains to keep bodily vigil in the service of God can scarcely preserve his heart in true purity.

Let us therefore keep watch, most beloved brothers, especially in these holy days of Lent, in which we have declared war on vices and are strongly prepared to conquer and overcome the ancient enemy, the devil. For you hear the holy Prince of the Apostles admonishing us in these words: “Brothers, be sober and keep watch.” Why did he warn us of this? “Because,” he says, “your adversary the devil, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking whom he may devour; resist him, strong in faith.” And so, my brothers, let us keep watch as long as we are set in battle, lest at some time, weighed down by sleepiness or idleness, we be overcome by the adversary of our salvation. For as long as we live in the flesh, we must fight bravely against the enemy by vigilance and by chastising our body.

Do not let the labor of the struggle frighten you; for although our adversary is troublesome, yet he is so weak and feeble that he can overcome no one except one who is willing, and one who has utterly cast aside all guard over himself. But the Lord Jesus Christ, our helper and protector, is strong: he grants strength to those who fight eagerly, and will bestow on the victors an unfading crown with all his saints in heaven. As dust is scattered by the wind, so our adversary the devil, confounded and terrified by the voice of those singing psalms with humble devotion, is driven to flight.

Therefore let none of you, most beloved brothers, act negligently; let no one grow dull at the time of prayer through sluggishness of mind and sleepiness; let no one fear the devil’s contest with disordered dread. Stand manfully for the salvation of your souls, fighting continually against a weak adversary; and regard your own flesh with no less suspicion than the demons. For you know, as not only Scripture bears witness but long experience also teaches, that the flesh lusts against the spirit; and therefore the monk who is not harmed by the wantonness of his own flesh is rarely overcome by demons in the struggle.
