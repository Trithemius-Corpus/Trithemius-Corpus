What the Armor of Spiritual Warfare Is. Homily III.

Dearly beloved brothers, today we have heard the holy Apostle writing in his epistle to the Ephesians: Put on the armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil, for our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places. Therefore take up the armor of God, that you may be able to resist on the evil day, and, being perfect in all things, to stand. And what the armor of God is, he adds: Stand therefore, having girded your loins in truth, and having put on the breastplate of justice, and having shod your feet with preparation for the Gospel of peace; in all things taking up the shield of faith, with which you may be able to quench all the fiery darts of the most wicked one; and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God; praying at all times in the spirit, with every prayer and supplication, and in it keeping watch with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints. Likewise he says to the Thessalonians: But we, who are of the day, let us be sober, having put on the breastplate of faith and charity, and for a helmet the hope of salvation. These are the arms, brothers, with which the soldiers of Christ, when clothed in them, defeat the savage adversary; these are the monastic badges of virtue, without which no one attains victory over the vices.

The instruments of human warfare, which many mortals pursue with excessive zeal, are the helmet, shield, breastplate, sword, dagger, coat of mail, belt, lance, and many other things of this kind, with which men who are about to go forth to war arm themselves most carefully; and unless they have been most diligently protected over their whole body, they are certainly judged less fit for battle. But the arms of our spiritual warfare are faith, hope, charity, patience, humility, obedience, chastity, together with the holy virtues of the soul enumerated by our divine teacher Benedict in the Rule under the fourth chapter, where he makes a beautiful mention of the instruments of good works.

And would, my brothers, that we were as attentive and diligent about these spiritual arms of our warfare as we see worldly men to be concerned about their perishable armor. But now, as I say with too much sorrow, since we shamefully cast aside the arms of salvation with which we ought to have defeated all our adversaries, what remains except that we fall before our enemy in the struggle? But perhaps you will say: If the Christian faith, as the Apostle testifies, belongs to the armor of spiritual warfare, we certainly have never cast it aside. For we firmly believe whatever Holy Mother Church has set before us to be believed, and in that profession of faith we steadfastly intend to remain until death.

But be it so. What then does Saint James say in his epistle? What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith, but does not have works? Will faith be able to save him? For just as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead. Therefore, whenever Scripture speaks of faith, it must always be understood not as a common and dead faith, but as one formed by good works and illumined by the sweetness of divine love. For no one will be saved by faith alone, as James says in his epistle; rather, the works of Christian devotion must be joined to faith.

Do you believe in one almighty God, Creator of heaven and earth, Father and Son and Holy Spirit? You do well. The demons also believe, and tremble. What then? Where are the works through which your faith may be shown to be formed in Christ? Idle and dead is the faith that is not commended by holy and necessary works. We believe in Christ: that is good, if we follow Christ’s examples. For if we do not follow Christ in works of devotion, we flatter ourselves in vain about faith alone. You, I say, believe, and the demons believe. Faith with love is the faith of a Christian; without love, it is the faith of the devil. You believe rightly, if with all your heart, according to your strength, you love God. But if you do not truly love God, you have an idle and dead faith. The proof of love, as Saint Gregory bears witness, is the performance of the work. For thus the Lord speaks in the Gospel: If anyone loves me, he will keep my word.
