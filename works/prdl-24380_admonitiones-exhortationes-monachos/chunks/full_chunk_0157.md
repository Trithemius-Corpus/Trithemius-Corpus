On the charity of man toward God.

And happily to triumph with him forever. Let none of you seduce his own soul with empty words, so that he believes he truly loves God while neglecting to keep his commandments. You hear the Lord Jesus saying in the holy Gospel: “Whoever loves me will keep my words.” And, as the blessed Pope Gregory says, “the carrying out of the commanded work is the proof of the love that has been declared.” For then we truly love the Lord our God with our whole heart, when, having despised worldly pleasures, we keep all his commandments perfectly even unto death. Likewise, then we love him above all things when, for love of him, we walk innocently, not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit.

He truly loves God who, always intent upon good works, keeps himself pure and unstained from this world. But he rightly loves the Lord God who desires absolutely nothing in this world. He truly loves God with his whole heart who, for love of him, bravely despises the honors, riches, pleasures, and all comforts of the flesh belonging to the world. He loves God with his whole soul who, always solicitous in observing his commandments, omits to do none of the things that are pleasing to him. For the love of God neglects nothing, but is accustomed most diligently to keep and fulfill everything that he has commanded. He who loves God in true charity has fulfilled the law, the prophets, and the Gospel. Therefore by your works you will give testimony whether you truly love God. For everyone who has deserved to receive a ray of divine love always shows great and manifest signs of that love.

Indeed, the fire of divine love makes the one who possesses it pious, chaste, meek, and most humble in all things, and always tranquil in the same constancy of mind, without disturbance, both in adversity and in prosperity. The monk who truly loves the Lord God, according to the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, cheerfully obeys the elders’ commands in all things, embraces patience in hard and contrary matters, and preserves true humility everywhere. The love of God makes its possessor exceedingly rich in every good work, and renders him wholly alien to every kind of vice. The lover of God does not murmur at the command of obedience; in rebukes he does not rashly excuse himself; he is not touched by the bitter gall against his neighbor, nor is he disturbed even when injured.

He who is a true lover of God preserves his life blameless in all purity of mind and body, and shows within himself a charity fervent not only in word, but also in deed and in truth. Whatever is seen in the world as beautiful, whatever as pleasant or delightful, whatever flatters the insolence of wanton flesh, the true lover of God most steadfastly despises it all, and desires nothing other than to please Christ and to live with him as soon as possible. Great and incredible to those who do not know it is the power of divine love, which always impels a man to work well and never allows him to live idly.
