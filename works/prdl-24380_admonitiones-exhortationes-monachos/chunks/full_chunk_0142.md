Yet let the judgment of your own self-assessment be suspect to you, unless the judgments of others also confirm you. Always remember that hour when you first entered the monastery for the sake of conversion, so that, whenever you find your soul growing sluggish from the fervor of holiness, you may renew it according to the former training of your novitiate.

You will quickly be able to become a good monk if you always and everywhere preserve obedience and humility in all things. True obedience in a monk gathers many virtues within him; humility, however, preserves those virtues once gathered, lest they flow away. Be ready at all times to obey the commands of your shepherd, and however much progress you have made in the struggle of holy combat, do not lift up your soul in any estimation of your progress. For what are our righteousnesses before the Lord? Or what good can we do that he has not given? Since God, who is sufficient to himself in all things and alone perfect, has no need of our good deeds, for he is the highest and unchangeable good, in comparison with whom all our justifications are less than nothing.

Therefore a monk placed under an abbot, in the humility of one truly obedient, walks securely; and if he perseveres to the end, fighting and conquering, he will be gloriously rewarded. Truly, brothers, the humility of your obedience delights me, which you show faithfully to the almighty Lord. In this respect I do not regard myself as your prelate, but as your companion and the humble minister of you all, though a more useless one. Therefore, in those matters that concern your salvation, I beg you, do not look at my person as I speak, but rather at the common Lord of all, for love of whom you have set me, an unworthy man, over you as abbot. For I will not be troublesome to you while you are running well; but if anyone turns aside from the way of truth, I am compelled, even unwillingly, to oppose him as far as I can.

Let a monk always remember his entrance into the monastery, and never forget the first resolve of his training. In all your works too, O monk, always remember that you are not lasting long upon the earth, but will shortly depart from this life, and that you will die perhaps when you do not expect it. Therefore keep inviolate your purpose of holiness, and always be watchful in good works, so that when the Lord comes and calls you, he may find you prepared. Remember that you are a pilgrim on earth, and have here no abiding city; but just as a traveler hastens quickly toward the end of his journey, so death approaches you daily without interruption.

Therefore, while you are healthy, set the house of your conscience in order, so that you may not fear death, since he rarely dies well who is not prepared as one who will die each day. O how securely that monk departs from this life who, keeping his conscience clean in all purity, conducts himself at all times, without reproach, according to the full integrity of the rule of our most holy lawgiver, even to death. Let your mind continually [keep before it] our Lord Jesus Christ as present, indeed as nowhere absent.
