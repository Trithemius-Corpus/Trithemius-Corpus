For humility is the ladder to heaven, beautifully divided into twelve degrees; concerning its ascent our lawgiver Benedict says in the Rule: “If we wish to reach the summit of highest humility and swiftly arrive at that heavenly exaltation, we must set up for ourselves that ladder which appeared to Jacob in his dream, by which angels were shown to him ascending and descending.” Without doubt that descent and ascent are understood by us to mean nothing other than descending by exaltation and ascending by humility, as divine Scripture shows us: “Everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled, and he who humbles himself shall be exalted.” The ladder itself, set upright, is our life in this world, which, when the heart is humbled, is raised up by the Lord into heaven. For we say that the sides of this ladder are our body and soul; into these sides the divine summons has inserted the different steps of humility and discipline that must be climbed. He then follows and enumerates those twelve steps in order, with a sufficiently beautiful arrangement; since they are altogether very well known to you, it would be superfluous to spend longer on reviewing them this time.

It is enough in this place to remember the holy canon, in whose words you all long ago swore voluntarily, so that you may know that humility is no less necessary for salvation than love of God and of neighbor alike. For humility is said to be the monk’s greatest virtue, and it is so necessary to his profession that without it he cannot be saved. A monk who is puffed up in spirit and proud is not the servant of Christ, but the slave of the devil; he will have no share with the Son of God in heaven, but will be tormented without end with the demons in hell. For Christ, the teacher and forerunner of humility, detests all the proud and admits none into his fellowship except the humble, the meek, and the gentle. But the devil is the head and inventor of pride; all who follow his pride and arrogance are his servants and disciples. For he himself, the king of the proud, cannot endure humility; he flees the meek and does not dare to stand in the sight of the gentle in heart.

For Christ dwells in the assembly of the humble servants of God; but in the midst of the proud the devil stirs up contentions and the quarrels of those at odds. Where humility is, there is true peace and the highest tranquility; but where pride holds sway, there resentment, anger, disturbance, and discord confound everything without ceasing. Therefore, my brothers, beware of pride, because if it cast down the exalted angels from heaven, when would it not condemn poor cloistered men? It made demons out of angels, and without doubt it makes companions of demons out of monks. Follow humility at all times with all your strength, and flee every pride and display as much as you can, because unless you greatly humble your souls, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven.
