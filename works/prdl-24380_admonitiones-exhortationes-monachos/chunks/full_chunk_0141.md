Homily XX

Whoever truly loves obedience undoubtedly reaches the crown of martyrdom. It is an arduous work and an exceedingly laborious struggle for a free man to despise himself, and forever to surrender himself wholly, and all his own will, under the command and power of another. But let us look with faithful eyes upon the reward of this struggle, and we shall reckon all the hard and bitter things of this life as a kind of childish game, which is seen for an hour and afterwards appears nowhere. For, as the Apostle, the lover of Christ, says: the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which shall be revealed in us.

The crown which martyrdom once bestowed upon the saints for Christ, humble obedience will today prepare for the true lovers of Christ. For the monk who is truly humble and obedient for Christ dies secure and without fear, as one who, faithfully serving under the command of his superior, is freed from every demand for an accounting. For he who, living at the nod of his shepherd, walks rightly in all the commandments of God, can commit nothing that may be brought before the judge. He is defended by the testimony of the abbot, under whose direction he has lived rightly and without complaint.

Consider, brothers, the law of obedience according to which you promised to live, and know that you have made necessary what had been voluntary, when you solemnly consented to the judgment of monastic tradition. It is no longer permitted you to withdraw from the holy purpose, nor can you revoke a vow once uttered. If you do all things according to the holy Gospel of Christ and the rule of Father Benedict, which you vowed, let there be no doubtful hesitation in you: you will be blessed, and you will receive from the Lord the reward owed to the saints. But if, forgetful of your promises, you violate the vow of holy purity made to God, if, despising the spiritual life, you live according to the flesh, I now declare to you from your own law that you are most certainly to be condemned by our Lord Jesus Christ, the judge of all, whom you have mocked and despised. For it is far better to promise nothing than to violate a vow.

Therefore the condition of your salvation is placed in this one thing: that, just as you once promised God, you persevere until death according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, living in all purity of mind and body. For a Christian man who has not vowed monastic conversion can be saved by living rightly according to the Gospel; but he who has once become a monk will not be saved unless he has kept until death all the things required for the monastic institution.

Listen, then, O monk, to a brief, solid, and true word. You are a monk: if you live as a monk, you will be saved; but if you live otherwise, you will not be saved, but will receive your portion among the apostate spirits, because you have mockingly despised God, to whom you lied.
