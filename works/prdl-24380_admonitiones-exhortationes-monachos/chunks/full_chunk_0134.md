Daily he calls back to memory the evil he remembers himself to have committed, and groans deeply that he has grievously offended the Lord. He walks with great caution, and so laments past sins committed that he in no way commits further things to be lamented.

The monk who is truly humble follows the Lord Jesus with a pure conscience, and fears nothing in this world more than offending the master whom he imitates. Whatever he does, and wherever he goes, he always keeps the Lord Jesus present in his thoughts, and by continual meditation examines how he may please him alone. For this is true and Christian humility, and humble Christianity: to wash away sin in true compunction, and to preserve the conscience in all purity at every time, not only through fear of punishments, but rather through love of Christ.

True humility of monks continually does whatever good it can for the honor of God, and intends to do nothing that it knows displeases Christ. True humility of monks neglects none of the things that God has commanded, and always flees as deadly the things that he has forbidden. The penitent monk, humble in the compunction of tears and serving the Lord in the fervor of charity, is the companion of the holy angels, the friend of the citizens of heaven, and very fearsome to demons. For demons fear and flee nothing more than the humility of the servants of Jesus Christ, because, since they themselves are most proud by their vicious will, they are most violently tormented by the devotion of the humble. For where true humility is, demons cannot remain; for just as ice is dissolved by fire, so malignant spirits are scattered by the presence of a humble monk.

O humility, venerable mother and guardian of all virtues, yours is the kingdom, yours the power. You bestow the kingdom on the humble; you grant the glory of eternal happiness to the meek. Without your guidance no one comes to heaven; he who follows you does not fear hell. He who gathers works of virtue without you is like one who carries dust into the wind. You are the seasoning of all good works, because just as food in the mouth of the eater is not savory without salt, so without your companionship no one is made a friend of God.

Humility made the Son of God man; humility will liken the servant of Christ to a holy angel. An angel, through pride and by his own choice, was made a devil; and a Christian man, through humility, becomes an angel. Humility opens heaven to the meek servants of God, and pride plunges arrogant men down to hell. Humility makes friends of God, and pride makes companions of demons. Humility enlightens the mind to true wisdom, and pride blinds the understanding of the one whom it has possessed.
