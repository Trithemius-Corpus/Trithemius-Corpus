The second thing which we believe to be necessary for a monk walking on the way of eternal salvation is that he strive to know himself. For self-knowledge is the best of all sciences, and therefore deservedly to be preferred to all the pursuits of mortals. Yet the beginning of this saving knowledge is exceedingly difficult, because there are very few who do not love the world. For everyone who follows the world and loves the vanities that pass away cannot be one who knows himself. And nothing hinders a man more from knowledge of himself than man himself. He who is fed on the pleasures of his flesh, who is lifted up into pride by the vain knowledge of this world, or by riches, or by honors, is not permitted to come to knowledge of himself. Nevertheless, to know many things and to neglect oneself is not wisdom, but rather madness. It belongs to the heap of a heavier damnation to learn many things and shamefully to be ignorant of oneself.

Let each of you therefore consider what he is, whence he is, and what he is going to be. Man, certainly, of himself is nothing; but whatever he is, he is from God and for God. What is man but a traveler and a pilgrim, as it were of one day upon the earth, who today is seen alive in the world, but in the morning, snatched away by death, is seen no more? All our life, every action, every day, every hour, shows how nothing we are: we die by living, are corrupted by acting, are extinguished with time, and are changed. O tearful race of men, unstable, miserable, and weak, how windy, blind, and vain I see you to be, when I make a comparison between ourselves in the present and those who have gone before us to death. He spoke truly who compared you to the leaf of a tree. The wind, snatching a leaf from the tree, casts it to the ground; in its place another new leaf afterwards grows up, subject to a like fall. But what has fallen to the ground is resolved into the earth, and never thereafter returns to the tree. Hence a certain wise man of the Greeks says:

As is the race of leaves, so also is the race of men.

The race of men is very like the race of leaves, because it grows green for a time and is resolved into the earth; and neither does the leaf return to the tree, nor man to life.
