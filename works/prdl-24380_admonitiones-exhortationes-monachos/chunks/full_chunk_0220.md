Although your request far exceeds the powers of my talent, and that of your brothers, most beloved Father, I have nevertheless preferred to undertake the arduous burden rather than fail to satisfy your charity as far as I can. For the love of God, zeal for the Order, and fervor for discipline make you worthy that I should deny you nothing when you ask for the honor of God. You asked me when I was present by word of mouth, and more often when I was absent by letters, and in a certain manner you compelled me by the authority of your presidency, to write you something concerning the calamity of our Order, so that you might stir up the fathers to be assembled with you in chapter to have compassion on it. The request is holy, but the difficulty of obeying is great.

I shall be torn by the teeth of the envious. I know that some will judge me either presumptuous or excessive; but with my ears stopped I shall pass securely by their reproaches, provided that the burden I bear is pleasing to holy and learned men. I do not remember having written anything that rivals can justly tear apart, but things true, manifest, and open to the eyes of all; and if they do not wish these things to be written, let them also grieve that they have been done. In this publication alone charm of speech will be lacking; on that point I do not fear being reproved among religious men. For what? Is truth therefore despised because it is not clothed with the ornament of eloquence? Far be it. For plain and true speech is better than adorned and false speech. But these things are nothing to the point.

I therefore submit this little treatise to your examination, so that, if you judge it worthy, it may be read to the brothers; but if unworthy, it may escape the eyes of the lords. Farewell. In the year of the Lord 1493, on the Kalends of May.

The Pentichus Book of Lord Johannes of Spanheim, Abbot

On the ruin of the Order of our most holy father Benedict in the regions of Germany.

Chapter 1

When I recall to mind the former beauty of our Order, I grieve deeply over the deformity that we now endure, so that I would rather shed tears than compose a lamenting discourse. For who would not weep over its ruin, if he weighed its ancient glory? O, if we were ever to call back to memory the golden ages once possessed by this most sacred Order, when like a river of paradise it watered the whole Church throughout the world with doctrine and holiness, we would easily perceive how little its present state is.

Indeed our observance, which we call regular, when compared with the institutions of the ancients, is found not only lesser, but also in many respects more lax. How has the gold been darkened, the finest color changed? The splendor of surpassing holiness, once shining bright, has been obscured by desire for earthly things. The rule of honor has yielded to the enticements of pleasures. Therefore let my eyes pour forth tears day and night, and let them not be silent, because the religion of my people has been crushed with a great crushing, with a very grievous wound. If I go out into the fields, behold the idle and vain; if I enter the monasteries, behold those thinned and weakened by pleasures. For I find many professing the rule of Father Benedict; I see few walking according to his tradition.

“You have multiplied the nation,” says the prophet to the Lord, “but you have not increased the joy”; because, while the holy exercises of monks fail in many, corrupt hearts do not admit the true joy that is born of virtue. Would that we might awake, even late at last, after the destruction of so many souls, which, having despised the yoke of regular observance, have descended into the abyss of damnation. Would that we might fear the sentence of Benedict our lawgiver, who so often strikes the madness of a harsh abbot by summoning him to render account to God.

To you now, therefore, most reverend Fathers, my speech must be directed, you who receive the power to compel rebellious men to discipline, you who have zeal for our Order according to knowledge. Thus I address you; to you I shall try, according to my strength, to persuade that you come to the aid of an Order close to falling.
