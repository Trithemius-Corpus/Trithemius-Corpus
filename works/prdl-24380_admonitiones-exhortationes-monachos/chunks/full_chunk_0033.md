Unless you urgently prepare your [heart], you will never see the face of God in glory. Acedia is the root of despair, and not only its root, but also its nurse and mother; if once it has oppressed the mind of a person dedicated to God, it immediately closes off every road to salvation. For the kingdom of heaven is not promised to sleepers, nor is the blessedness of eternity prepared for those who grow sluggish in leisure and idleness; rather, the crown is laid up for those who manfully rule the motions of the flesh, and those who live by the spirit, not by the flesh, are granted the glory of eternal happiness.

No virtue is acquired without labor; no enemy is overcome without battle. If you wish to be crowned, conquer; if you desire to conquer, you must fight bravely and perseveringly. “How long will you sleep, O sluggard?” Solomon cries to you; “when will you rise from your sleep?” If you are diligent, your harvest will come like a fountain, and poverty will flee far from you. For he who gathers in harvest is a wise son, and he who snores in summer is a son of confusion. For he who works his own land will be satisfied with bread; but he who pursues idleness will be filled with poverty.

Now is the time for doing good, brothers; now the opportunity of earning eternal blessedness is granted to us by the Lord God, an opportunity that is suddenly cut off in death. For Scripture says: “Whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly; for neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge will be in the underworld, to which you are hastening.” If the good of eternal blessedness that is promised delights you, then the labors of the struggle that are set before you must not frighten your mind. For no one will be crowned by the Lord unless he has competed lawfully; no one will reach the summit of eternal loftiness unless by fervor of spirit he has overcome the unlawful motions of the flesh.

We must always fight, brothers, against the devil’s threefold battle-line as long as we live in this pilgrimage; for no one here becomes so unconquerable that, if he grows lukewarm, he is not gradually overcome. Here Saint Augustine proclaims: “No one ought to feel secure in this life, which is wholly called temptation, lest one who was able to become better from being worse should at some time become worse from being better.” Hence Solomon too says in the book of Proverbs: “Desires kill the sluggard.” For just as constancy gives perfection in every good work, so acedia is the destroyer and stepmother of all religion and of the progress of the spiritual soul. For, as blessed Jerome says, nothing is worse in a holy purpose than idleness, since it not only fails to acquire new things, but also shamefully consumes what was previously acquired.

The order of holy spiritual life advances and grows by continual progress in joy; but by cessation it grows sluggish and fails. Therefore, brothers, the mind must be renewed by daily and fresh increases of virtues, and the measure of this path of life must be taken not from what has passed, but from what remains. Therefore flee idleness first of all, for it is the mother and nurse of all vices; but overcome acedia not by fleeing it, but by resisting it bravely. Scripture says: “Idleness has taught much evil.” For in vain and pernicious desires is every [unclear].
