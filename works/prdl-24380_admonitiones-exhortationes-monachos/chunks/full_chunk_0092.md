And which of you lives with such purity as monastic perfection requires? It is a grave sin for a monk if he abandons his effort toward perfection, if he lives negligently in the monastery, if he transgresses even one commandment of the rule and of the Gospel. And who among you, brothers, loves the Lord God with such sincerity, such charity, and such fervor of love as a man is bound to love his maker and redeemer? Or whom will you give me from among all of you who does not sometimes go too far either in taking food or in drink, beyond what befits the integrity of monks? Which of you does not offend in word? Or who is not sometimes troubled by evil thoughts? Whom among you has acedia not struck? And whom has gloomy sloth not disturbed? Who is there in this small congregation of yours whom pride does not touch, and whom impatience does not more often make a murmurer?

Let each one of you consider the paths of his manner of life, and he will find that in many things, even at any moment, he is at fault. For what if a monk has committed no evil? What if he has kept himself unstained from this wicked age? He will not be able to be perfect unless he has both turned away from evil and done the good that his perfection requires. For one without the other is not enough, but from both together perfection arises in a man.

Therefore, brothers, we must always keep watch with the greatest zeal, so that we may satisfy the institution of the holy fathers, according to which we solemnly bound ourselves by public profession to live, in order that we may direct our effort without interruption toward the perfection set before us. Let not the consideration of our own weakness draw us back from holy effort, because we are helped no less by the grace of the Creator than we run by our own desire. Just as every day, by living rather negligently in many things, we fall into fault on any occasion, so also at every moment, rising again from the sluggishness of mind, let us renew our holy effort in our good purpose.

Let us join tears to holy prayers, and always call upon the help of God with devout supplications. Let us keep charity inviolate in all things, since it is charity that covers the multitude of sins. Each day the good soldier of Christ who desires to be faithful to his Lord should examine his conscience by searching it at least once, and should weigh carefully how much he has advanced in holy desire, or fallen short in it, on that day. And whenever he finds that he has sinned in anything, let him at once do penance, and with tears ask mercy from the Lord. Let today’s salvation not wait for the uncertain morrow; and he who can immediately find pardon must in no way insert delay. Let him at once grieve in heart that he has sinned against the Lord; let him also renew his holy purpose with new fervor, and let him judge that he has utterly lost that day on which, by making progress, he has not become better.

Let prayer always be on the monk’s lips; let continual compunction be in his heart; let tears of devotion flow continually from his eyes, and [unclear] of inward love.
