They relate false causes as true ones; they invent necessity; they lie about usefulness; they put forward the appearance of honor, which they in fact flee. The root and cause of this whole evil is founded in pleasure alone and in hatred of discipline. But when the cause stated in the petition is false, the dispensation does not free the one who obtained it, but condemns him. The Supreme Pontiff, after hearing the accounts of petitioners, is accustomed to reply cautiously: “If it is as it is related,” he says, “let what is requested be done, in the manner and form that follows.” He replies cautiously, grants indulgence discreetly, and dispenses within fixed limits. But unbridled monks, as soon as they have received the bulls, do not remain within the limits established in them, but transgress the manner of the dispensation, considering not what they have obtained, but what they wished to obtain.

O wretches, O senseless men, O unhappy men, why do you deceive your very selves? Why do you voluntarily destroy your souls? The intention of the one dispensing does not absolve you from what you promised to Saint Benedict. But you claim for yourselves more than the Pope granted; indeed, you take entirely without permission what you unlawfully desired to obtain. What a certain man, still living, said of some of you is true, when by apostolic commission he had stripped them of the monastic habit: “Although,” he said, “I have taken the habit from them, nevertheless, according to the tenor of the bull, they are still monks before God.”

Unhappy apostates, who will deliver you from the hand of the living God? Who will be able to defend you from the accusation of Saint Benedict? What will it profit you that here, without challenge, you have been made canons from monks, if at the final judgment of God you should be numbered with the reprobate? Indeed, unless the almighty judge approves your transfer, unless Saint Benedict, prince of monks, consents, no dispensation excuses you. Your transfer does not look to its proper end, and therefore it will be inexcusable.

Question your consciences, and weigh with what intention you withdrew from the order. If you took off the habit for love of God, from desire for the heavenly homeland, for the salvation of souls, for advancement in virtues and a holier state of life, then God approves the deed you have done. But your intention was contrary to all these things; therefore your transfer from the order is rightly judged alien to the will of God. For you cast off the habit in order to live without law; your first intention was to live without obedience, without humility, without poverty. There is no fear of God before your eyes; you think nothing of the salvation of souls; you understand nothing of eternal life; you desire to walk in the pleasure of the flesh alone. All the causes and reasons that you stated in your petition are fabricated and false. How, then, will the dispensation be legitimate? How will the authority of the Supreme Pontiff excuse you, when you did not fear to circumvent it by lying, fraud, deceit, and importunate prayers?
