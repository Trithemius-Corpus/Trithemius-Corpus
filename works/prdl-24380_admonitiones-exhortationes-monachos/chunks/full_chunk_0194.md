Now follows the third thing, which I think very necessary for a monk walking in the way of God. Every man who understands himself to be a traveler in this life of very brief duration truly shows himself humble in all things. For why should earth and ashes be proud? He who truly knows himself cannot be exalted. My brothers, we have no cause for exaltation and pride, but much and continual cause for humility, since we are earth, a certain compacting of dust, and after a few days are to return again into dust. But because true humility belongs to few, therefore the way of salvation is called narrow and is found by few. By humility one comes to the kingdom, because, as the Lord says in the holy Gospel, everyone who humbles himself for God’s sake will be exalted, and he who exalts himself will be humbled.

Fourth, for the monk traveling to heaven it is supremely necessary that he make himself a stranger to every sin, because no one is admitted into the kingdom of God unless he is commended by the highest purity of conscience. Therefore the conscience must continually be adorned with holy virtues and good works, because heaven is opened only to those who despise the world for love of God. Hence Peter, the most holy chief apostle of Christ, says in his second epistle: Applying all care, minister virtue in your faith; in virtue, knowledge; in knowledge, abstinence; in abstinence, patience; in patience, godliness; in godliness, love of brotherhood; and in love of brotherhood, charity. If these things are present with you and abound, they will make you neither empty nor without fruit in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. There is one virtue, as Firmianus testifies, and one way of the good, which leads and carries souls stripped of the flesh not into the fabulous Elysian fields of the poets, but into the choirs of angels: namely, the Christian faith adorned by works of true charity, which flees every sin.

Fifth, the traveler on the steep road of the heavenly kingdom is instructed that, by keeping God’s will, he should follow the commandments in all things. Whatever he is about to say or do, let him first examine diligently whether it agrees with the ordinances of God and of the Church. But if he finds it contrary either to God or to the Church, let him flee from it at once, lest he fall into sin. For it belongs to a wise man to commit nothing unexamined, but to consider all things diligently by the counsel of foresight before he acts. But whoever wishes to be safe and secure on this steep journey should, to the best of his strength, imitate the life and conduct of our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the light for all unto eternal life.

The sixth thing that we judge necessary for our traveler is that he love the saving study of Scripture. Let him not be curious in reading, nor given to vain superstitions, but simple, pure, and upright in soul. It does not befit a monk to cling to writings.
