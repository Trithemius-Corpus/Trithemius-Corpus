The company of the wicked has drawn many back from the way of the Lord and of our Savior Jesus Christ. Therefore, my most beloved brothers, we do not cease to admonish you to walk with the good and holy in the way of the Lord, so that by their teaching and example you may daily become better. For as the divine Apostle of Christ says, imitating the verse of a certain Greek named Menander, bad conversations corrupt good morals. He does not truly desire to be good who takes pleasure in the company of the wicked. For no one speaks wicked things unless he is first found in his heart to have been corrupted and depraved against the purity of the evangelical tradition. As each person is, and as the things are which he turns over and considers in the secrecy of his heart, so the speech that proceeds from his mouth is more often. For speech is the truest witness of the speaker’s heart, as our Lord and Savior says in the holy Gospel: “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” For a good man brings forth good things from the treasure of his heart, and an evil man likewise brings forth evil things.

Since, therefore, he who speaks evilly is not good, we must beware of all who use wicked, scurrilous, and destructive speech, in whose talk shameful things often resound, while words of holiness are either never heard or heard very rarely. Foul speech is the sign of an unchaste heart, even if deeds do not follow. Therefore, my dearest brothers, you who promised, by a profession both implicit and explicit according to the rule and the ecclesiastical statutes of the divine fathers, to preserve chastity in deed and every kind of modesty even to death, and who ought also to be far removed from every unclean thought, flee the company of wicked men and the gatherings of the malicious, and at all times avoid those who speak unjust, shameful, or idle things.

Remember the Scripture which says: “He who touches pitch will be defiled by it, and he who has fellowship with the proud will put on pride.” Hence holy Pope Gregory says: “The weak ought to avoid the company of the wicked, lest they take pleasure in imitating the evils which they often see and are unable to correct.” And the divine Bishop John says: “If a good man is joined to a bad one, either they are made alike, or they are quickly separated from one another. For friendships either seek equals or make them.” For the nature of things is such that, whenever a good man is joined to a bad one, the bad man is not made better by the good, but the good man is contaminated by the bad. For diversity of things can never have concord, and wicked company tempts many.

There are some among you, brothers, I say it with groaning, professors greatly forgetful of regular discipline, for whom the cell is a prison, the choir a siege, prayer nausea, silence a punishment, and the reading of the Scriptures a weariness. The love of God either never or rarely lifts upward the hearts of those whose whole way of life consists in lower things. Whenever I happen to be absent, they soon come together among themselves, not to speak of Christ, nor of the sweetness of the heavenly kingdom, nor of the integrity of regular observance, but they bring forth a long discourse about the vain and useless affairs of this world; and by conversing they involve themselves in tales and in the wars of kings, matters about which it is not lawful for a monk to think. Whatever I say to them, whatever I declare, whatever penance I impose, it profits nothing for those whom evil custom has depraved from the beginning of their instruction.

Nothing is worse than an evil custom; for the monk who is neglected as a novice and a young man spends all the days of his life in torpor and sloth. For Scripture says: “A young man walking according to his way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” Therefore I say to you, venerable elders, I beg you, I warn you, and by the mercy of God I ask you with all the urgency I can: live henceforth in such a way, conduct yourselves in such a way, walk in such a way, that the habits of old age may correspond to your years, and that you who have already advanced far into old age may provide an example of holiness for the instruction of the younger men. Without doubt, whatever is found lacking in the conduct of the younger men is ascribed to you. For you ought to go before the younger men in all holiness, in word and in deed; you are commanded to show them an example of all perfection, so that when they behold in you the perfect works of all the virtues, they may be stirred, as if by a certain impulse of shame, toward imitation [unclear].
