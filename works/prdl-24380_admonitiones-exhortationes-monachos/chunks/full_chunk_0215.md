On the nine degrees of ascent.

We must attend to ourselves. But those who, by the Lord’s mercy, daily make progress toward better things in this degree fall into sin more rarely. Yet whenever they have fallen, so often do they rise again through repentance. Still, their state is close to danger, because just as a garment often torn is at last rendered useless, so a person who slips by sinning more frequently is often smothered by evil habit. Yet if any from this degree depart this life in the Lord’s mercies, they will be saved, but not without the very grievous pains of purgatory. We must therefore hasten to higher things, so that not only mortal sins but also venial sins may be avoided as far as our strength allows.

Those are believed to ascend the second degree who strive, as much as they can, to avoid venial sins as well as mortal crimes; but because of too much commerce with the body’s weakness, they do not persevere in their purpose, but frequently fall into the various negligences of this life, although they do not commit mortal sins. We, the lukewarm, trust that we have in some measure ascended this degree, who, being placed under the observance of regular discipline, guard our minds, with the Lord’s help, from the more serious sins. But because we are exceedingly lukewarm, we live very negligently and are slow to perform the exercises of holy virtues.

There are many placed in the region of this second degree whom servile fear possesses; and if this fear stands alone, it by no means draws near to the kingdom of God. For to obtain the glory of heavenly blessedness, servile fear without charity is not enough, since he is a servant who does not sin from fear of punishment, but a son does no evil from love of God. Yet it is good to begin from servile fear, so that a person may gradually be able to arrive at filial fear, which is called chaste. For even if withdrawing from evil through fear of punishments is by no means enough to obtain the kingdom of God, still it prepares the one who acts for filial fear. Hence the lowest degree of those making progress is not to offend through fear of punishment, and not to sin lest one be condemned forever.

For charity alone, discerning the merits of all, is admitted as fit to enter the kingdom of God; and whatever has been done outside it is judged unworthy of God’s kingdom. Therefore, whoever you are who have deserved to ascend to this second degree, whoever you are who have learned not to sin through fear of punishments, if you wish to reach the joy of eternal blessedness, take care to hasten as quickly as possible to higher things. But since, as the proverb bears witness, no one suddenly becomes highest, we shall indeed rightly allow you to begin from the lower things; but we judge it dangerous to stand long in the valley of darkness. Ascend, therefore, as much as you can each day, and do not persist with the beasts in the dung of stinking deeds, because if you do not advance, you fall back; if you do not ascend, you descend.

The third degree is attained by those who, persevering in a good purpose, keep themselves from every mortal sin and also withdraw from venial sins as far as their strength allows. They love purity of conscience, and in no way commit anything that could stain it. Yet in one respect they are lower than those who follow, because they do not simply act for the love of God.
