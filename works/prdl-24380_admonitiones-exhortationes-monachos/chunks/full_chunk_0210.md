For his will is subjected to the divine will with the affection of a peaceful mind, and is set far apart from all desires that war against the spirit. Whoever has received this gift of the grace of the Holy Spirit should first give thanks with humility, and should not vainly exalt himself because of the gift; rather, the more he has advanced, the more he should learn to humble himself in all things.

Often, however, a man deserves to lose God’s grace when he neglects to preserve it in the vessel of true humility. Therefore almighty God sometimes withdraws the grace of sensible devotion for a short time from those who are advancing, so that humility may remain in the mind and the fervor of desire to seek further may burn more intensely. But since there are many causes in human beings for which the grace of God is sometimes withdrawn, it was worth the effort to state them in order.

First, then, the grace of God is withdrawn from a man because of sin, when the conscience is stained by the foulness of vices. For our God, as Scripture says, is jealous: either he alone is loved, or he is not loved together with any companion. Therefore, as often as the mind turns back, either upon itself or upon some other thing, with disordered desire, it is estranged and removed from its source, which is God. Hence it deserves to lose the grace it had, because by consenting to sin it was unwilling to keep it inviolate. For this reason the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, exhorts us not to receive the grace of God in vain; and it is indeed received uselessly and in vain when the mind of the recipient is defiled by the filth of vices.

The little vessel into which we receive God’s grace is our conscience. When it is cleansed from vices and adorned with holy virtues, it becomes a worthy dwelling place of the Holy Spirit and produces great progress in us. But if the little vessel of our conscience has been befouled by the corruption of sin, at once the Spirit of God departs with all the gifts of grace. Is not our Lord Jesus Christ the lover and faithful bridegroom of the soul, which he purchased for himself by the shedding of his own blood? With what face, with what rashness, O man, can you despise so great a lover and your savior, and love anything else in this world? God loves you supremely: he is your creator, and by his death he freed you from everlasting destruction. What will you repay him, O mortal man, from whom you have received such great benefits? He asks nothing of you, seeks nothing, demands nothing, except gratitude, love, and charity. Guard the innocence and purity of your conscience, lest you lose the prompting of grace.

The second cause for which grace is withdrawn from a man is called the pride of ingratitude. For God, as Scripture testifies, resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. By the just judgment of God, the grace of the Holy Spirit is lost whenever the one who has it is lifted up into pride. For everyone who humbles himself will be exalted, and whoever exalts himself will be humbled. Hence almighty God again says: Upon whom shall my Spirit rest, if not upon the humble and quiet man, and upon him who trembles at my words?
