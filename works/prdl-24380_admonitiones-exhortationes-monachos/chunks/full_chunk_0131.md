No good work pleases God if it has been offered apart from true humility. There are, moreover, many things that are accustomed to stir the servants of God to true humility. For some are humbled before God within themselves by consideration of their own vileness, because, when they consider that they have now often experienced themselves to be wretched and weak, they place all their hope and confidence, in humility, in God. Others, carefully weighing the multitude of God’s benefits toward them, fear the judgment due to ingratitude, and therefore they always walk humbly in the sight of God with great fear and reverent gratitude.

And which of us, brothers, is there to whom the mercy of almighty God has not granted many benefits, even in a special way? If we wish to be grateful to him, we must above all be humble. Others, after a multitude of sins, having been turned by divine kindness to repentance, diligently recall to memory how many evils they have committed against the law of the Almighty, and for these, humbled in bitterness of spirit, they detest their impieties with horror.

But we offend in many things; therefore it is necessary that we humble ourselves always in mind and also outwardly in body, so that grace may abound where sin previously abounded. Often, indeed, the providence of divine mercy permits certain people to sin grievously for a time, so that afterward, when they have been converted to repentance, they may learn to remain in true humility. Better is a humble penitent than a proud man who has not sinned, since humility justifies penitents, and pride condemns the arrogant forever, even if they have done nothing else wrong than this alone, that they are proud.

Others, bearing in their heart through daily meditation the bitter passion of Christ Jesus, humble themselves all the more, the more they know that they are far too insufficient as debtors for such great and wondrous benefits of their Redeemer. The Son of God humbled himself, becoming man for us, and, filled with many reproaches, voluntarily underwent the most shameful death of the cross, so that he might free us from the destruction of perpetual death. What Christian man, and especially what monk, will not greatly humble his soul before God when he recalls to memory such humility of his Creator and Redeemer?

Indeed, the man whom the humility of Christ does not stir to meekness will not be helped by any admonitions of men. We are called monks in vain unless, according to our strength, we have humbly followed Christ. For this reason he said in the holy Gospel that he himself is the way, so that we may imitate his humility, and so that the way of life of so great a Master may become the spiritual pattern of the disciple. Therefore we, brothers, must strive with all our strength, as much as we can, so that humility, conceived through meditation on the most bitter passion of Jesus Christ, may level, restore, and reform everything in us that pride has made swollen.
