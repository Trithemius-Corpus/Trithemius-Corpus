How blind we are, though we keep our eyes open; how forgetful and sluggish travellers we are, who know most certainly that we are to die, and yet put our trust in the vanity of the present life as though we were not mortal. But what man is there who shall live and not see death, shall he deliver his soul from the hand of hell? Behold, Scripture says, we all die, and like water we flow away upon the earth.

Therefore be mindful, my brothers, that death does not delay, and everything that passes will be reckoned brief; for, as the holy Christ-bearing Paul says, it is appointed for men once to die, and no one will be able to escape this common law of necessity. O, if now we set daily before the sight of our mind the hour of approaching death, which each one of us must necessarily undergo shortly, and so ordered our life and conduct that we might always be found ready, how happy and secure our departure could become.

Did not Christ himself, our Lord, king of the living and the dead, wishing us always to be prepared for death, warn us beforehand in the Holy Gospel, saying: Therefore you also be ready, because at the hour when you do not think, the Son of Man will come? How much frequent meditation on death profits toward purity of mind, the wise man warned in Ecclesiasticus, saying: In all your works remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.

The thought of future death brings fear; fear necessarily shakes the mind and, like a nail driven by a hammer, fastens all motions of proud flesh to the wood of the cross. In view of this consideration, many of the pagan philosophers used to say that the whole life of a wise man was a kind of continual meditation on death. Hence also Saint Jerome says: Nothing will profit you so much toward temperance in all things as frequent meditation on this brief and uncertain life, which passes away like a leaf carried off by the wind, and, when it is not feared, falls into death.

Therefore remember your death, which you can escape by no plan, and you will easily be able to overcome all motions of the flesh. For he who thinks daily that he is going to die despises present things and, unless he is utterly foolish, will prepare himself for future things. For I know, and in part have learned by experience, that the will of the human mind cannot long remain uniform in the holy fervor of spiritual life; and therefore it has pleased me to recall the memory of death more often before your eyes, so that, while the mind is shaken by fear of things to come, the fervor of holy purpose may be renewed each day.

For the human soul, bound to the body for its exercise, is very changeable and unstable; now it is carried off to this, now moved to that. Therefore the Wisdom of God says through Solomon in Proverbs: My son, give me your heart, and let your eyes keep my ways. As if he were saying: Unless you have entrusted the whole love of your heart to divine wisdom, you will never be steady and confirmed in keeping the commandments of the Lord.
