If by reading you desire to attain true knowledge, take care first of all to lay aside every swelling of pride, all proud exaltation, and rash perfidy, and then to cleanse the chamber of your heart from every uncleanness of carnal pleasures. For God, as canonical Scripture says, resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.

No proud, puffed-up, arrogant, or rash person, no one carnal in mind, lustful, or restless, is held fit for the study of sacred Scripture, but only one who is humble, gentle, meek, modest, chaste in body, and tranquil in mind. Hence Prosper says in an epigram: “A mind cannot search out the commandments of God unless it is at rest. Therefore, that religious study may be practiced, the quarrels of the malicious must be cast away. Hearts that are in some measure quiet can search the law, if, free from the noise of the world, they have leisure. Therefore, that the mind may be found intent on holy studies, let divine love conquer carnal battles. For the temple of the Lord grows through sacred silence, and the house that will not fall is built in quiet.”

Therefore, with your souls cleansed, and your heart pierced in the love of God and humbled in true contrition, proceed to the study of the Scriptures, because the Lord will not enlighten you unless he has first made you purified from every iniquity. For, as Scripture itself says, perverse thoughts separate from God, and tested virtue rebukes the foolish, because wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sins; for the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit, and will withdraw himself from thoughts that are without understanding. And therefore we must pray very often to the Lord with holy David: “Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within my inward parts.”

For no one profitably understands the sacred Scriptures of God who does not turn himself with his whole heart to the Lord through repentance. And therefore, if you wish to understand the divine utterances both rightly and healthfully, take care first, with the utmost zeal, to keep yourselves, as far as you can, clean from every pollution of sin.

I beg you, do not let the mad folly of certain monks seduce you, who try to excuse their ignorance with a worn-out proverb, saying: “What need have we of knowledge of the Scriptures, since we have no office of preaching? We embrace the counsel of the wise man, who says in the book of Proverbs: ‘He who walks simply walks confidently.’ It is enough for us to live simply, because the servant who knew the will of his lord and did not do it will be beaten with many stripes; but he who did not know and did things worthy of stripes will be beaten with few.”

O foolish and utterly ruined champions, who despise saving knowledge and love ignorance of the divine Scriptures more than understanding: will ignorance deliberately chosen make you liable to fewer stripes? Or will you, when you sin, be innocent before God because you are willingly ignorant of the commandments of God? You will have to be tormented with a double punishment: one because you despise knowing the way of God’s commandments, the other because you do not keep his words.
