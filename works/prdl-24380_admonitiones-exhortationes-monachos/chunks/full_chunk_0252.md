For without spiritual things, temporal things do nothing toward eternal salvation; without the aid of temporal things, spiritual things do not long subsist in this life. Therefore, if you wish to be a good shepherd, know that the care of both kinds of feeding has been laid upon you, namely spiritual and bodily.

I had said that your sins must be discharged by repayment of a debt. Of what debt? Of that, namely, by which you are deemed bound to St. Benedict. What is that debt? Everything that you have. For from him you have it that you are a monk; from him, that you are wealthy. And now he from whom you have all these things knocks at your door; for the necessity of his order he asks help; he desires to receive back the least part of the very great things he has entrusted to you. Why do you deny him his own goods? Why, when they are demanded of you, do you not at once restore what was entrusted to you?

Divine Scripture commands and says: If you see the naked, cover him, and do not despise your own flesh. Behold, your order stands naked; behold, your torn religion cries out, begs alms, asks back what is its own, seeks help. If there are in you any bowels of pity, any heart of sweetness, any love for your order, any love of God, do not despise your own flesh; do not scorn so great a public kinship. Honor him who first honored you; give alms to him who raised you up; bestow a little on him who made you rich in many things. However much you give him, nothing at all is diminished for you, if only you apply a good will: you will give a little and receive much; you will generously bestow earthly things and hope for heavenly things.

Nor should it escape you, since, as is commonly said, whatever is withdrawn from honor is reserved for shame. For the legal maxim says: The treasury takes what Christ does not receive. He is not free from the guilt of crime who sets greed before the order of piety.

But why do we strive in vain? The word of piety has no place in wicked hearts. If they despise the words of God, what wonder if they scorn ours? Though I may speak many things, say many things, urge excellent things with words for contributing to the order, I shall never bend the rebellious man. Indeed, unless your authority, lord presidents, intervenes, the speech of one who admonishes labors in vain. It therefore belongs to your office to bend the rebellious; to you is committed the humbling of the disobedient; ours is humbly to obey your decrees. Therefore, if you desire to defend our order from the assault of the wicked, take care first of all to recall rebellious abbots to obedience, because unless the unity of the order is restored, the impiety of its adversaries will easily prevail. For the Lord says in the Gospel: Every kingdom divided against itself will be laid waste, and house will fall upon house. Division alone confounds the order.
