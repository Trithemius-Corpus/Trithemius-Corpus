What the signs of true humility are.

In reproving the sins of others, he is slow out of love for humility, and is not found quick to do so. For one who is truly humble always considers his own weakness, and silently weighs in secret how many evils he himself has committed against God’s justice. Hence he greatly fears to reprove the deeds of others, because he recognizes himself as a sinner and therefore unworthy to judge others. We except the prelates and rulers of monks from this rule of humility only insofar as we know them to be obliged to correct their subjects.

The seventh sign of true humility is that he gladly accepts corrections and amendments for his faults and negligences, and is not embittered by any resentment against the one who reproves him. Whenever the humble man is corrected by someone, he rejoices in spirit with the Prophet, saying: “Let the just man correct me in mercy, and let him rebuke me; but let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head.” But of the proud Scripture says: “They hated the one who reproved them in the gate.” He who is truly humble loves the one who corrects him, but flees the flatterer.

The eighth sign of true humility is shown clearly when, after being corrected, he does not defend the fault, does not deny it, does not palliate it, does not excuse it, but confesses it with simple shame. A truly humble monk, when he is corrected, does not murmur, does not complain that the measure of fairness has been exceeded; but at once, falling to the ground before the judge, he cries out most meekly: “I have sinned; I confess the fault; I beg pardon; I promise amendment.”

The ninth sign of monastic humility makes the one who has it gentle, sweet, and modest in speech, and not loud in voice. A monk whom you see loud and hasty in speaking you will judge not humble, but proud and darkened. The humble man speaks modestly and almost under silence, and is always found readier to listen than to speak before others. It is shameful for a monk to raise his voice too much when he speaks, since one crucified to the world is commanded to love silence.

The tenth sign of perfect humility is possessed by the man who is accustomed to interpret in the better sense everything whatever he has heard about his neighbor. For our Savior says in the Holy Gospel: “Do not judge, and you will not be judged.” And again: “With whatever measure you have measured, it will be measured back to you.” The holy Apostle also says: “Do not judge before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both illuminate the hidden things of darkness and make manifest the counsels of hearts.” “Who are you, who judge another’s servant? To his own lord he stands or falls, not to you.”

The eleventh sign of holy humility is that he despises no man in his heart on account of any transgressions whatever, but places all before himself by merit of holiness. He even thinks that all who sin publicly are better than he, and therefore condemns no one. What evil he himself has done he always considers with himself in his heart; but he humbly weighs the good deeds of others as outweighing his own.
