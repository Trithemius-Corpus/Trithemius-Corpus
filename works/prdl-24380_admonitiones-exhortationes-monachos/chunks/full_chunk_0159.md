He dies who has always been solicitous in the exercise of divine love and at all times zealous in the observance of God’s commandments. For only the love of God is admitted into the kingdom of heaven; and the more fervent the mind has been in loving, the greater will be its reward in the kingdom of heaven. He who loves much receives much; and he who now loves less will then also receive less in glory. Therefore, as much as each one can, with all his heart, and with all his soul, and with all his strength, so much let him love, because our God is not to be loved lukewarmly or sluggishly, but fervently, wisely, and sweetly.

The love of God gathers strength and grows through spiritual exercises, just as a burning fire is enlarged by a supply of wood. Indeed, if the wood is taken away, the fire is extinguished; and if spiritual exercises are interrupted, charity is soon shipwrecked. True love of God is never idle, but at all times is delightfully occupied in those things that nourish and preserve charity. For the lover of God never forgets his beloved, but always meditates on his presence and beauty; now in most devout prayers he speaks to Jesus, now in the holy Scriptures he marvels at him speaking to him. The true lover of Christ meditates day and night on the law of the Lord, and with the greatest diligence does everything that he understands to be pleasing to God.

O how sweetly he burns whom the love of God truly inflames! How pleasantly he glows whose mind the flame of the Holy Spirit sets on fire! Blessed is that monk who, full of divine love, withdraws himself wholly from earthly vanities and rests in the shade of heavenly desire. O if only we had love for God as strong, whole, and vehement as mad and foolish lovers have for their corruptible mistresses, with desire for whom they often burn, shamefully go mad, and fear no scandal of this world. But the love of God is stronger than all things, if it has been truly rooted in us.

Whoever truly loves the Lord God always keeps in his memory his most bitter Passion, because nothing so perfectly causes love of God in the human heart, nothing so induces true compunction, as the never-interrupted remembrance of God’s benefits. Therefore let the written sequence of Christ’s Passion always be in your memory, and let this word never depart from your mouth: O crucified Jesus, my love, by the power of your most innocent death have mercy on me, and make me your faithful servant.
