I cannot [serve you], because I am too much compelled to be occupied with secular affairs. Hence only on feast days, and in that part of the night which after the night lauds I am accustomed to spend sleepless, do I serve you by exhorting you, setting down in little pages what occurs to me to say.

And so I wished to introduce today’s discourse from the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, who speaks thus about the Lenten observance: “Although at every time the life of a monk ought to have the observance of Lent, nevertheless, because this virtue belongs to few, we therefore advise that in these days of Lent they guard their life with all purity, and together wash away in these holy days all the stains and negligences of other times. This is then done worthily if we restrain ourselves from all vices and devote ourselves to prayer with tears, to reading, and to compunction of heart.”

If this virtue belongs to few, that they should at every time lead lives of Lenten observance, it is now plainly clear that few monks live in religion according to the purity of the Gospel, since all who profess the warfare of Christ are bound to direct their effort toward the height of perfection. For a monk, according to the interpretation of his name, is solitary; and he is believed then to live rightly in the monastery according to the Gospel and the Rule which he has professed, when, having despised worldly things and honors for the love of God, he lives before God and men in all purity of heart, vigorous in work, clean in thought, sparing and peaceable in speech.

This is the life of a monk that has Lenten observance at every time: to chastise the body for the love of God through abstinence, and to nourish the mind through inward compunction. He lives rightly who lives justly, who loves God above all things, who with the highest zeal and the highest diligence observes his commandments in words and holy works. For he is a true monk of Christ who imitates Christ with all his strength.

But if you wish to imitate Christ, O monk, observe most diligently what I am about to say. Flee the world and all comforts of the flesh, so that you may deserve truly to be solitary. Never allow vain, useless, foul, and harmful thoughts to remain in your heart, but always think on something good, and let the memory of the Lord’s passion never withdraw from your mind, so that each day with thanksgiving you may contemplate the Lord hanging on the cross. Consider God as present to you everywhere, who sees, knows, and understands whatever you have either thought or done, since nothing is hidden from him.

Keep your heart clean from every stain of sin, and always have a pure conscience burning with the true love of Christ. Let no idle time fly away from you, but let thought always find you engaged in good works. Do not grow sluggish with much sleep, and make yourself far removed from every acedia of lukewarmness. Embrace moderation in food and drink, and live as sparingly as is permitted, so that the body, moderately sustained, may always serve the spirit in humility, and by no means against the command of reason [unclear].
