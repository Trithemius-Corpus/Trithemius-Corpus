That one must pray without interruption.

Let him continually carry his memory in his breast, always giving thanks to his Redeemer, always blessing, always adoring, and every day, indeed as much as possible at every moment, let him ponder with tears, in compassion, the bitterness of Christ’s wounds. Every day, after each of the regular prayers, which the Greeks call canonical, with knees bent and mind lifted up to heaven, let him say:

I give thanks to you, and, blessing you, I adore you, O mediator of God and men, sweetest Christ Jesus, God and man, for your most holy Incarnation, for your Nativity from the Virgin, for your Circumcision, for your flight into Egypt and the seven-year exile which you endured for us. I adore you, Lord Savior Jesus Christ, for your labors, for hunger and thirst, cold and nakedness, for your journeys, for your teaching and preaching, for your miracles, for the calling of the nations, and for the justification of sinners. I give thanks to you, Lord, Son of the living God, for the pain of your wounds; I bless you for the shedding of your most holy Blood; I adore you for your death, which you endured in all innocence; and I ask you, through all your Passion, your goodness and mercy, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death.

This very short prayer the devout monk shall say at least seven times in the day; and as often as he passes before an image of the Crucified, in like manner, with fervor of mind, he shall adore Christ. Whoever is a true lover of Christ always thinks of Christ, speaks of Christ, and venerates Christ; he does not doubt that Christ is present to him everywhere. The Passion of Christ is never absent from his memory, and because of the greatness of his love he forgets himself and is wholly occupied in Christ in mind and desire. Whoever burns in spirit with the love of Christ despises earthly things, conquers the flesh, and longs only for heavenly things. Waking, he always thinks of Christ with desire; sleeping, he dreams of Christ; and sighing continually, he cries out with Saint Ignatius: My love has been crucified.

He joins sighs to sighs, and Jesus, whom he loves with all his heart, he beholds present not with the eyes but with the spirit. Whenever, under the urgency of loving desire, he looks upon the crucified one, at once he melts into tears, and, burning with the fire of charity, cries aloud: O love stronger than all powers, how sweetly he burns who is inflamed with longing for Christ! He spoke rightly who said: Love is strong as death. For what can be stronger than the love of Christ, by whose ardor the holy martyrs, despising so many thousands of kinds of death, overcame them? O my crucified love, have mercy on me; grant me to feel that sweetness of your love of which I speak, so that I may always carry in my heart your most bitter Passion.

I know, my brothers, I know that there is no more effective way of praying, nor any more useful for us who are still weak, than in the memory and power of the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. Therefore, since it is clear that without a mediator there is no ascent of the mind to God, every good work must be begun in the name of Christ; for then we pour out our prayers to God profitably, when with a purified conscience we pray through his only-begotten Son.
