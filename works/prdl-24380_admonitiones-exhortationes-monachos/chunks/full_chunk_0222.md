Loving God with their whole heart, they sought nothing worldly in this life; but, scorning all passing things and being proved in the furnace of poverty, they happily reached the heavenly kingdom.

They were mature, zealous, and most learned men, firm in faith, strong in hope, radiant with twofold charity, pure in mind, outstanding in devotion, and adorned with the beauty of every virtue. They had no care for earthly things; no enticement of pleasures was in them; but, turned toward God with all their strength, they overcame the world with its vanities. Hence the kings and princes of this age, devout men and dear to God, because of the most holy way of life of the monks, became lovers of the order and magnificently exalted it with gifts and largess.

They rejoiced that in their times the holy pursuits of the monks were being spread abroad and extended with glory to the farthest regions of the world. For the more the holy monks loved God, the more they were loved by men; and the less they sought earthly things, the more they abounded in the necessities of the body, by God’s providence. For to those who love God, the whole world is riches.

O how fair was the face of your youth, holy religion of Benedict; how beautiful your appearance; how adorned with the splendor of all virtues your majesty. Who could suffice to weigh your worth duly? You show the way of three days to the people of God, freed from the servitude of Pharaoh in the desert, when you persuade men, snatched from the darkness of the world, to come for the love of God to the desert of cenobitic life through the three substantial vows of religion. Blessed are your professed members, who, departing from the Egypt of this age, long with all their desires for the land of eternal promise by this way of three days.

This is the threefold cord of holy religion, which is not easily broken, which is adorned with the brightness of obedience, chastity, and poverty; which calls back its lovers to the heavenly homeland; which presents its worshipers joyful to the supreme Emperor, Christ Jesus. For even if many things are necessary for regular observance, I would nevertheless dare to say that whoever has kept these three things inviolate stands secure in the order, like a threefold cord. For all the statutes of the order were made for the preservation of these substantial things.

The holy monks of old, carefully considering this, observed most zealously this way of the three substantial things. Poor in spirit through contempt for all worldly things, continent through chastity of mind and body, obedient through humble subjection, and rich in every virtue, they shone forth most beloved in learning and holiness, and both by word and by example converted many from wickedness to righteousness. Hence, as their merits increased, many of them were promoted to the government of churches; and the higher they were placed, the more illustrious they became known in virtues.
