On the nine degrees of ascent.

It is found there. Here all things are clean, all things pure, all things holy, all things most sweetly burning with the fervor of charity. Oh, if only we would strive to ascend to this degree! Oh, if only we might someday deserve to reach this harbor of tranquility! The more one, standing in this holy region, has begun to taste the sweetness of inward delight, the more, hastening toward what lies ahead, he desires still further.

Oh desires of holy love, always to be proclaimed; oh exercises of venerable perfection, where the clean conscience of one who loves God shines without sins, where the wicked contests of vices have ceased, and great tranquility has succeeded them.

Those already perfected in the eighth degree ascend to the ninth, who keep all the things prescribed purely and simply for the love of God, and desire nothing other than to praise, bless, and glorify God. Whoever has ascended this degree of perfection, as though forgetful of himself, resigns himself wholly to God and humbly submits. He regards himself as unworthy of all God’s benefits; he has no care at all for himself, because he has already learned to entrust himself wholly to God and humbly to be subject in all things to his ordering. Hence he neither asks for miracles nor seeks any other consolations in this life, but with the holy Apostle of Christ truly cries out: “For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.” “For the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.”

For the true monk who loves Christ has offered himself wholly to God out of charity, and has despised the world with all its riches, honors, and vanities. Hence the world is said to be crucified to him, since there is nothing in it that seems able to penetrate his mind. Happy are those monks who with pure conscience serve the Lord God in true charity; who run without offense under the rule of humility and obedience; who ascend this last degree of perfection. These are true monks, who seek nothing other than the honor of God.

Indeed, my brothers, although monks are not bound by necessity of salvation to be perfect, for otherwise very few would be saved, yet we are all bound with good will to strive toward perfection, and with all the strength we can to yearn for this ninth degree. For it is very contrary to the monastic profession to be unwilling to make progress; it is a kind of apostasy, after a vow of perfection, to wish to remain in imperfection. For the vices that attack us are enemies of our salvation, and until they either conquer or are conquered, they do not cease to persecute us. Let us imitate the history of the ancient battle, when the children of promise destroyed their enemies not in one day, but successively. For it is difficult to conquer all things at once, unless the charity of God has suddenly overshadowed the mind. Against all the vices that in any way attack us, let us wage war. Let us conquer gluttony today; when it has been duly laid low, let us overcome sloth; and let us deal so with the rest, that at last we may come forth victors over them all. The Lord will be with us as we fight, and God will give victory to those who love him. Amen.
