For no one is devout by his own merits; no one is holy by his own powers. Let us recognize that all the causes of the withdrawal of God’s grace which we have mentioned belong to our weakness.

But as regards those who are already walking in the way of perfection, another explanation is given, so long as they persevere. For God’s grace is never withdrawn from them, although the sensible fervor of compunction is sometimes taken away for a time. And let us set out in order the causes of this taking away.

First, Almighty God sometimes withdraws from his lovers for a time the grace of sensible devotion, so that, while he seems not to be present when called upon, the desire of inward love may grow. All who have felt the fire of divine love, and who are true imitators of the holy fathers, know this alternation of holy ardor. For whenever the bridegroom withdraws from the bride, the desire of the lover grows. And while she seeks in burning longing the one whom she loves, she groans from the desire of love.

Second, this grace is withdrawn from the devout for a time lest frailty be lifted up into pride. When we lose the grace of compunction even against our will, we judge, by humble examination, our weakness, which can do nothing of itself. Therefore, in order that the devout worshipers of God may remain humble in their exaltation, they must recognize their own frailty.

The third cause concerns merit: because to seek inward consolations belongs not to the strong, but to the weak. The Lord must be served freely, because he is good; but those who seek sensible sweetness in their devotion are too delicate, and seek the Lord Jesus for little consolations. The Lord must be loved manfully, and all womanish and soft affections must be driven far from the soul. Therefore, so that the Lord may increase merit for his worshipers in holy compunction, he sometimes denies them, when they ask, the grace of sensible devotion. But lest, affected by the bitterness of weariness, they grow wholly sluggish in their purpose of holiness, the consoler of the sorrowful does not withdraw far, but remains nearby. He suddenly returns to his friend; and the one whom he had seemed for a time to leave in sadness, he consoles with the sweetness of his spiritual presence.

We join to these a fourth cause, by which we are warned to walk more cautiously. For if we fear losing the grace of compunction, let us learn to guard more diligently what we have received. For a rare and precious treasure, the more it is loved, the more diligently it is guarded by its possessor. Therefore the monk who has deserved to receive from the Lord the grace of devotion must keep watch with the utmost care, lest he lose it through his own negligence.

And so that you may know how you ought to preserve the granted grace of compunction, lest it perish, the order of what is to be said makes it clear to you. First, he who does not wish to lose the grace of inward compunction granted to him strives, as far as he can, to keep himself clean and pure from every sin.
