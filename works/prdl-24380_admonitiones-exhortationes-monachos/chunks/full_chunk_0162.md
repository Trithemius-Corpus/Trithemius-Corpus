But the proud, luxurious, impure man, entangled in great or public crimes, darkens his conscience by deliberate perversity, and often shows, both by words and by deeds, how wrongly he thinks about the future repayment of merits. A malicious disposition perverts the judgment of uprightness, and the disordered desire of the flesh darkens the understanding. Sometimes, moreover, excessive and immoderate faintheartedness makes the conscience err; this evil is born partly from ignorance, and partly generated from one’s own sensuality.

In this scrupulosity, however, a monk should follow the counsel either of the abbot or of some other more experienced elder, and should not rashly defend the opinion of his own head. Excessive scrupulosity is dangerous, because it hurls the conscience into error and unhappily overthrows many. For everything that is done against conscience is said to build toward hell. Excessive perplexity of mind has also harmed many and cast them headlong into various errors. But he who is truly a humble monk submits himself to his superior’s examination. For whoever makes use of his superior’s counsel in doubts that arise is not easily overthrown by the snares of demons. For the more the consciences of monks have been exposed to the inquiry of a wise prelate, the more secure and the brighter they will shine.

Therefore, dearest brothers, you must take care first of all to have consciences free from every error, then pure from all vices, and finally adorned with the holy fragrances of all the virtues. Therefore let the monk who truly desires to make progress guard his heart with every watchfulness, and, as far as possible, set a cloister over his senses, and not allow anything deadly to enter. But if by any occasion he recognizes that he has fallen into some sin, let him at once strive to wipe it away by true repentance, and not grant time to the wicked seed, by which it may grow strong into something worse. While the wound is small, let the remedy be applied; for if it is neglected, it grows beyond measure.

Therefore, my brothers, resist bravely the first movements of demonic suggestion, and overcome the enemy when he is small and weak. But if the sensuality of the body has become altogether wanton, let it be brought back to obedience by harsh mortification, just as we know our holy father Benedict did when he rooted out the slippery insolence of the flesh by the stings of nettles and brambles. Finally, if you diligently recall to mind the dreadful punishments that await the wicked, you will easily overcome the illicit movements of the flesh. For there is no one who does not fear pain more than he loves pleasure.

Also often bring back to mind the joys of heavenly happiness, to which you desire to come, because if you faithfully consider them, you will strongly lessen the fire of the flesh. Whatever delights in the world is corruptible and momentary, but the joys of eternal delight remain forever without end.
