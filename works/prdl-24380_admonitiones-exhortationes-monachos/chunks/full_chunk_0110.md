By the remedy of contrition and confession wash away your past sins, and by persevering in prayers with tears strengthen your mind, with God’s help, against all temptations. Toward God, as much as you can, be fervent in the exercise of love; toward yourself, be well at peace in a pure conscience; toward your brother, be rightly ordered in true charity. Always be foremost in humility, firm in constancy and obedience, shining in chastity of mind and body, gentle in meekness, a monk in deed, and continually kindled with desire for perfection. If you commend your abstinence by this kind of cleanness and purity, you will fast rightly.

Diligently exercise your mind in meditation on the Passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, so that no day may pass on which, with thanksgiving, you do not at least once run through his life and death in meditation with tears. For crude and exceedingly undevout, not to say ungrateful, is the Christian who is so immersed in temporal affairs, or so occupied with the pleasures of the flesh, that he does not daily, at least once, remember with thanksgiving the most bitter Passion of his Redeemer. But the monk who forgets the benefits of our Lord Jesus Christ, who does not once each day recall his Passion with thanksgiving, who does not every day meditate on the evangelical law of the Lord, is rightly judged the most ungrateful of all men.

For nothing is more powerful than that prayer which is offered to God the Father in union with, and in the power of, the Lord’s Passion. Most justly is that spirit to be condemned which, redeemed by the most precious blood of Christ, is led astray by the pleasures of its flesh so as not to remember such great benefits of God toward it. For there was no cause for the Son of God to become man except that he willed to justify us sinners: born in the world for us, suffering in the world for us, and cruelly dying on the wood for our crimes. He, the just one, came into the world to justify us who were sinners; the merciful one came to the impious, so that we might learn mercy, humility, and patience from him. The physician came to the sick, life to the dead, compassion to the despairing; it was not our merits that drew him down from heaven into the world, but our sins.

It is fitting, therefore, my brothers, that with all devotion we celebrate this Lenten fast dedicated to his most sacred Passion, since he first deigned to do such great things for our salvation and, in order to rescue us from the destruction of death, did not hesitate to give himself over to death for us. Let us do, for love of Christ, whatever good we can; let us endure, in view of his Passion and death, every adversity that befalls us in this world, since the Lord Jesus, who out of charity willed to suffer for us, has truly shown himself most worthy that we should love him with all our heart. For if, brother, you acknowledge yourself a sinner, Christ saved you by his death. Contemplate him hanging on the Cross as you ought, and you will be healed from every bite of sin.
