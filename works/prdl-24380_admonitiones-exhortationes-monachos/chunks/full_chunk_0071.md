For just as water extinguishes the fire into which it is poured, so the holy purpose of novices is damaged by the examples of the lukewarm. Beware of taking part in the conversations of those whom you see to be negligent in the observance of God’s commandments and of monastic purity; for since they are driven by carnal passions, out of the abundance of the heart they speak nothing but vain and carnal things. For the evil conversations of old men corrupt the good morals of the young. Vain are all their pursuits, and useless, indeed poisonous, are the words of those who, being carnal, do not know the exercises of divine love.

Indeed, just as one diseased sheep gradually corrupts the whole sheepfold, so one slothful and perverse monk in a congregation is often the cause of many transgressions. As Saint Christophilus testifies, a little leaven corrupts with its odor the whole mass of flour with which it is mixed; so young monks and novices who associate with lukewarm and acedia-ridden elders are imbued with their depraved habits, and thereafter are never restored to the integrity of holy fervor.

Therefore I say to you elders: be, by good works and by chaste and always modest speech, a model of life for the young, so that whenever they are corrected for their faults, they may not defend themselves by the examples of their elders. For it is most shameful that elders should be guilty in those matters for which juniors are deservedly rebuked. Live, I pray, you old men, in such a way that in your presence the younger men presume neither to say nor to do anything that seems blameworthy. Let them fear you as judges of evils, not as men acting the part of patrons; since all who have sinned by your example are reckoned to have perished through your instruction.

Let there be among you no one lukewarm or idle, for desires kill the lazy man, and vanity destroys every wandering-eyed monk. Let there be among you no talkative man, no roving monk, no gadabout, no murmurer and restless man, because whoever delights in such pursuits both ruins himself by evil conduct and kills his brother by his examples. Let there be among you no mocker of the simple, no despiser of the humble, no rash critic; for he who is quick to laugh will be consumed in his own folly and will not be glorious. He opens his mouth against the humble, always seeks quarrels, and rejoices that he is talkative about the deeds of others. He will not prosper in his counsel, and if he has been raised to some honor, he will quickly fall into the depths. His pride is hateful to God, because, though he is foolish and stupid, he boasts that he is learned and wise. His foolishness will soon be laid bare, and a grievous destruction will seize him. His heart will meditate folly, and there is no peace around him. He will be hated by those near him and by his neighbors, and no faithful friend will attend him.
