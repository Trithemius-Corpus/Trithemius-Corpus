Let the body be chastened by discerning abstinence, lest it kick against the rule of the mind; let the concupiscence of the flesh be broken by holy exercises, so that it may always obey the counsels of reason. If ever it begins to assail the mind with wanton impulses, let it even be restrained with blows, because it is better that the bruise of stripes should humble the back than that the insolence of the flesh should cast the soul down into hell.

For Scripture says: He who rears his servant delicately from his youth will afterward find him rebellious. The flesh is the servant of the mind, and must always obey the command of reason. But if the flesh has been reared too indulgently, being now accustomed to pleasures, it will refuse to bear the rule of the spirit. For a servant becomes rebellious against his lord whenever he is habitually reared by him in softness. And it is difficult to bring back a rebellious and insolent servant to humble obedience after he has long been accustomed to kick against the goad. For it is written: A servant cannot be instructed by words, because he understands what is said and disdains to answer.

Therefore the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: Fodder and a rod and a burden are for an ass; bread and discipline and work are for a servant. He works under discipline and seeks to rest; slacken your hand toward him, and he seeks liberty. The yoke and the strap bend a stiff neck, and continual tasks bow down a servant. If we begin to turn these testimonies of Scripture from the literal sense to the moral sense, we shall find how rightly everything said of the servant and the ass applies to lazy and delicate flesh.

For truly the flesh is the servant of the soul, and it always rises up against its mistress unless it is wearied by daily discipline and work. He who at all times rears it delicately in idleness and pleasures will necessarily find it rebellious against spiritual promptings, because while the flesh is lifted up in pride, the spirit, languishing in idleness and lukewarm, is weakened. Therefore unless you continually humble this servant of yours, namely the flesh, O brother monk, by work and abstinence, unless you place upon its unlawful impulses the bridle of continence and sobriety, you will go wholly, with it leading, into the abyss.

For we know, as the holy Apostle says, that the flesh lusts against the spirit, and unless by the spirit we mortify the works of the flesh, we shall die in its corruption. Nothing casts the soul more deeply into the pit of sin than the insolence of the flesh, which, when it is permitted to live by its own will, struggles rebelliously, a servant against its lord. Unhappy flesh, which by the concupiscence of vanity casts down the soul: it will not go unpunished for you if, at your prompting, it commits any evil, since God will judge both. Woe to you when it has sinned, because when Adam, the transgressor of the commandments, is expelled from paradise, Eve, the instigator of evils, necessarily accompanies the wretched man.

Be consenting to the spirit, O flesh, because the Apostle says: Those who are Christ’s have crucified their flesh with its vices and concupiscences. And you, O men and brothers, who profess the warfare of Christ, who have voluntarily sworn yourselves to the law of monastic life, who have promised to imitate the footsteps of the Savior, consider your calling.
