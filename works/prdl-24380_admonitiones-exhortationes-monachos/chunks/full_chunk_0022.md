Homily IV.

Having been instructed, we should gladly devote ourselves to the reading of divine Scripture, and direct all study of secular literature, in which we once labored, to the keen vision of this contemplation. For in the divine Scriptures whatever saving knowledge requires is found most abundantly expressed, so that what you do not find there is by no means necessary for salvation. For the majesty of the divine Scriptures far surpasses all the knowledge and teaching of the whole world; the Holy Spirit infused it into the men of God so that it might be written, and, so that it might be understood by studious readers, bestowed the grace of his illumination.

My brothers, diligently turn over this Scripture with purity of mind and the fervor of twofold charity, since whoever truly loves God spends all the time he can find for leisure in sacred readings. Let the frenzied actions, so to speak, of carnal lovers be an incentive to you, I pray, toward the wholesome study of the Scriptures: they receive letters and tokens from their beloved women and do not cease to read them, and often read them again and kiss them; and, what is a greater sign of love, they either hang them from their neck or place them on their breast, so that the cherished memory of the one they desire may never depart from their mind.

Consider, brothers, the madness of carnal men in love, and be ashamed of your own lukewarmness in the love of God. Which of you is moved toward the holy Scriptures of God with as great a love of heart as a blind lover, raving with desire for one little prostitute, is inflamed toward filthy and utterly vain love letters? Because of love for the absent woman whom he desires, he follows her letters and little gifts with frequent kisses; he examines each letter with excessive love and searches it again and again; in everything he does he is always thinking of the absent woman, and anxiously hastens to come into her sight.

O if we who are called Christian monks, who serve the supreme and immortal King Christ Jesus, who are commanded to love our God above all things and are instructed to love nothing in this world except him, if, I say, we were to pursue our Creator with as much love as carnally minded men attend to and love their corruptible and mortal women friends. Holy Scripture, read daily, makes God known to us, reveals his will to us, instructs us what we ought to do, and discloses what we ought to avoid. Through it the Almighty speaks with us, and, forming us by invisible instruction, comforts us, as the holy Apostle says: Whatever things were written were written for our instruction, that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope.

Therefore the true soldier of Christ, and especially the cloistered man, who loves God with his whole heart and desires to please him alone, embraces the divine Scriptures with affection, and especially the holy Gospel, and is continually occupied in meditating upon its reading. But whoever neglects the readings of the divine Scriptures plainly shows that he is not a true lover of God. But what does Scripture itself say?
