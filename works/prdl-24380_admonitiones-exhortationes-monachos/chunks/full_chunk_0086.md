Therefore in this matter I do not fear the hatred of murmuring subjects, knowing that I have taken on the care of sickly sheep, to whom, whether they wish it or not, I am bound to apply the remedies of salvation.

If it were possible, brothers, I would greatly prefer to disturb none of you in anything, even slightly, but to live among you in the greatest tranquility and cheerfully serve the Lord our God in peace of mind and body. But because not all of you truly seek the things that are God’s, I must rebuke and at times disturb the restless, lest impunity for small offenses provide boldness for graver ones. For I know what dangerous necessity I undergo in the pastoral office, and therefore I neither can nor ought to be silent when you sin, lest I be judged by some to have consented.

I willingly endure it if on this account I happen to be hated. But since this cannot happen without the gravest danger to your souls, we beseech you through our Lord Jesus Christ, crucified for us, that for the love of God and for the remission of your sins you always endure our fatherly and necessary rebukes patiently and with true humility. Let each one think within himself that there is no day on which he does all things well and does not offend in something; and let him know that he will shortly receive the reward of his works: good things for good deeds, and likewise evil things for evil deeds.

Therefore let him strive, as much as he can, to make satisfaction for his faults in this life, and humbly expiate all his iniquities by daily chastisement of the body. Know, my brothers, that there is no sin so small that the justice of Almighty God lets it go unpunished. Therefore, since there is none of us who does not offend in many things, arm yourselves, I pray, with patience and humility, so that whenever you are corrected for your sins, you may bear it with all meekness. For in this time of free will you can make satisfaction for many debts with a small expiation, whereas after death in purgatory the soul will be tormented for a long time and most grievously even for small sins.

Indeed, voluntary satisfaction is worth much; but that which is made from necessity earns less toward grace. Altogether, brothers, it is fitting and necessary for the soul to be cleansed from fault if it ought to reign in heaven and rejoice with Christ. But the soul that has neglected the cleansing of its faults in the present life will be punished more severely in the life to come. Let none of you, my dearest brothers, say what many ignorant people are accustomed to say: “It is enough for me if my soul reaches the punishments of purgatory.” Believe me, brothers: whoever says this from the heart has not considered what purgatory will be. For he does not know what he asks who desires purgatory. Just as a man is foolish who, when he could pay his debt while free, despises this and willingly goes to prison, so he must deservedly be judged the most foolish of all men who, when he can make satisfaction to God for his sins in this life, delays.
