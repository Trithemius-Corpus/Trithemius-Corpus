If you wished to make them canons, why did you not put good men in their place? A change is not made without transgression of the vow when it is presumptuously changed for the worse. Woe to you, deserters, who, so that your pleasures may be satisfied, despise the order, deny the vow, and confound the rule of equity.

“We shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ,” says the Apostle, “so that each may receive according to what he has done in his body.” What will the destroyers of monasteries receive there, who do not render honor to the Almighty, who violate the covenant, who nullify the wills of the saints, who despise the decrees of law? If it is good to found monasteries, it is evil, let no one doubt it, to destroy them. A contrary action will receive only a contrary reward.

Finally, if no one dares to break the last will of a dead man in matters of bequests, even though made to a human being, why do lovers of the world presume to usurp what has been offered to God and the saints? If he is guilty of a crime who violates a man’s testament, what punishment does he deserve who unjustly diverts to alien uses the foundations of monasteries made by holy bishops and princes?

Indeed, kings, princes, prelates, and the other faithful who founded monasteries for the honor of God are proved to have wished above all that the foundations of this institution should obtain an inviolable effect. If they had wished to have canons in monasteries, they would not have placed monks there. But the blind ambition of men has grown so strong that it thinks of nothing less than the future, and loves nothing more than what is perishable.

O wretched condition of human frailty, O always damnable forgetfulness of the commandments of God! The laws have consented to sins, and what is public has begun to be lawful. Crimes blaze everywhere, and everywhere, through wicked minds, in manifold kinds of sinning, a harmful poison works deceits and frauds. This man confounds the order; that man utterly destroys it. This man withdraws the will of the saints; that man writes false allegations of dismemberment. Here monks are driven from monasteries; there outsiders lord it over the goods of the order. An enemy accuses the just, a slanderer attacks, a false witness defames. Now, among the guilty, to be innocent is a crime.

What love of honor can be there, what integrity could exist in minds, where the purpose of the order is cast down, where the calling made by God is revoked? What shame concerning affairs can remain there, what virtue could stand, where divine right is violated, where there is no fear of the laws, no dread of the judge? What can be called more inhuman, what more cruel, than to wish to usurp for oneself what is divine, to confound right, to break a vow, to withhold from God the worship owed to him?

O that dreadful day, terrible to all mortals, when the wrath of God, which has long endured these evils against the monks, will be avenged; when apostates will feel their due punishment; when the adversaries of the holy order will pay the penalty for their rashness. What then will it profit you to have denied the vow made to God? What will the destruction of the sacred order profit you? What will that brief pleasure in the flesh, shamefully bought, profit you, when the Lord begins to review his worshipers and to give rewards to true monks?
