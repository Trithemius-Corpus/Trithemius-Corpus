Nothing can be so just, nothing so holy, nothing in human actions so honorable, as always to love God above all things with the whole heart, with the whole mind, and with the whole strength, and for love of him to fear neither death nor any kinds of punishments. There is no greater cause for any action in us than divine love, because, since God is the author of all things, nothing is more justly loved than he; and since he is the greatest of all goods, nothing is loved more fruitfully than he. God first loved us: the highest loved the lowest, the greatest loved the little, the almighty loved the weak.

Therefore, my brothers, let us love the Lord God with our whole heart, our whole soul, and our whole strength, by whom we are most sweetly loved, who not only created us in his image and likeness, but, as a proof of the greatest love, mercifully called us to the kingdom of eternal happiness through the teaching and death of his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. No fleshly mother ever loved her only son so tenderly, sweetly, and strongly as almighty God loved us, who not only willed to become man for our instruction, but also deigned to undergo death in the flesh he had assumed for our salvation.

No love has ever been greater, if we weigh rightly who it was who endured such things for us. For out of love for us, he who was free from the debt of death voluntarily dies; and he who was always immune from every sin is willingly condemned, innocent and just, for our sins. Therefore with the most just reason we are commanded to love the Lord our God with our whole heart, who, when we did not exist, created us from his goodness alone, and when we had perished, mercifully delivered us.

The love of God is the greatest of all virtues, since whatever good, whatever virtue is done in this world, if it is not done from charity toward God, is deprived of reward. Charity toward God purifies the conscience from every fault, because he who truly loves the Lord his God with his whole soul, with his whole heart, and with all his strength undoubtedly grants no place in his conscience to vices. For no uncleanness of vices enters a heart occupied by the love of God.

The love of God is an unfailing treasure: whoever possesses it is richer than all the wealthy of the world; whoever lacks it is more wretched than all the poor. Hence the holy Apostle of Christ says: “For those who love God, all things work together for good,” because nothing is lacking to those who fear him. Happy is the man in whose heart no bond of earthly affection dwells besides the love of Christ, who sighs after the remembrance of no human being, in whose conscience purity resides, and divine love always burns most ardently.

Love him, my brothers, who for love of you descended into the Virgin’s womb. Always carry in your hearts him who by his death opened heaven to you. Let the love of Christ be in you at all times, brothers, strong as death, so that your life may be conformed to Christ’s teaching, and, if it should ever be necessary to die for love of him, you may not fear, just as he himself first deigned calmly and kindly to undergo death for us.

For the love of Christ makes a monk near to God, and whoever is always fervent in the charity of the Lord will obtain the fellowship of angels. The love of Christ makes a man most strong against all assaults of demons, and no less patient and tranquil in adversity than in prosperity. The love of Christ teaches one truly to despise the world and all its delights, and to count all comforts of the flesh as nothing. The love of Christ quickly suppresses the unlawful movements of the flesh and brings the purest chastity into the mind. The love of Christ sweetly raises the mind with desire to heavenly things, and preserves it, separated from earthly things, in spiritual exercise.

The love of God smooths all hard and rough things, because where Christ is truly loved, tribulations and any adversities are reckoned as delights. The love of God is always constant in good works and is never idle. The love of God despises present things and strongly raises the mind to eternal things. The love of God neither seeks honors nor looks for riches, but, joined to Christ through desire, covets absolutely nothing except him.
