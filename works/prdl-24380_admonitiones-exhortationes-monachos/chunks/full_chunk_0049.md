52

That one must pray without interruption.

Cast from yourself all malice and unrest of heart in prayer; and, as we often say, forgive if you have anything against your neighbor. For unless you forgive your neighbor every debt for love of the Savior, you will obtain nothing from the Lord by your prayers.

It is said that there is a certain kind of serpent which, when it wishes to go into the water to drink, first vomits out all its poison. You too, monk, imitate this serpent in this respect, and vomit out all the poison of anger and bitterness, forgiving your brother the hundred denarii, so that the Lord may forgive you the ten thousand talents. Matthew 18.

As you desire the Lord to be toward you, so also show yourself toward your fellow servant. Call upon the Lord with all your heart, and you will find him. Let your prayer be burning and most pure, so that your mind may be joined to him in his most holy love. Strive for this throughout your whole life: to offer pure prayer to the Lord God at every time. Never fail in prayer, nor despair of the Lord’s mercy. For what God is going to grant, even if he delays it, he does not take away. He does not act from fickleness, nor is he easily moved from his decision; what he has promised is fixed. If you pray persistently, you will see a miracle.

Remember that you stand under the gaze of God, who understands all the secrets of hearts and knows the hidden things of the mind. Therefore stand watchfully in the sight of him who knows all things, and pray with as much fervor of spirit as you can, so that you may deserve to obtain mercy. For by holy prayer the conscience of the penitent is cleansed, and the prayer of one who lifts up his heart to the Lord in true purity does not return empty.

O dearest brothers, if you knew how pleasing to God are the prayers of a pure mind, and how great a benefit they bring to the soul, you would allow no time to pass without prayers. For our prayer, if it is pure and burning in fervor of spirit, is a most faithful messenger to God; it speaks to God on our behalf, and obtains everything necessary for our salvation while it prays.

Therefore I warn you and exhort you in true charity to pray without interruption, and never to cease from the fervor of the Holy Spirit. Let no time be empty for you, or idle in sluggishness of mind. I beg you, always be watchful and attentive in the fear of the Lord, so that you may be able bravely to conquer and overcome the temptations that threaten you. As soon as foul thoughts begin to strike the mind, flee back to the practice of prayer, and they will by no means be able to prevail over you.

For prayer is a great fortification of the soul; in its presence all the contrivances of the opposing side fail. Through most pure prayers, all useful things are granted to us by God, and all adversities are driven far away. By pure prayers the mind is powerfully kindled in the love of God, and whatever stain of lighter fault occurs is cleansed by praying. By holy and continual prayers the world is conquered; the plagues of the soul are healed by devout petitions; by tears and weeping the devil is overcome; and he for whom a thousand cohorts are not enough for battle is weakened by the prayer of one poor man. Just as wax melts and dissolves before the face of fire, so at pure prayer [unclear].
