On the Lenten fast of monks.

Taking upon himself in his own heart the position of the petitioner, let him say: “What would I wish to be done for me in this matter, according to the right judgment of reason, if I were in the position of the petitioner?” Indeed, if the abbot imagines himself a subject, he will not send anyone away from him saddened.

We have already said, brothers, what sort of men you ought to be toward us for the love of God, so that, being good monks, you may truly please the Lord Jesus. Nevertheless you have heard what kind of shepherd I ought to show myself to you; whether I do so or not, you yourselves will judge better than I. For we shall fast in vain, and shall find no fruit of Lenten observance, unless we all remain in true brotherly charity, with purity of heart, in the love of God.

What does it profit eternal salvation to afflict the body by abstinence and fasting, and not to be of one accord before God in the true charity of ecclesiastical unity? Or what good will it do to abstain from richer foods and defile the conscience with evil works or thoughts? For, if the fasts we have begun are to be pleasing to God, they must not only be alien and far removed from vices and sins, but also adorned with the fullness of all virtues.

For holy Ambrose says: “What does it profit to fast with the belly and to indulge in hunting? to abstain from foods and to wander in sins? Do you think, brothers, that he fasts who at first dawn does not keep vigil in the church, does not seek out the holy places of the blessed martyrs, but rises in the morning, gathers his servants, sets out nets, brings out dogs, searches through groves and woods, and, I say, drags his servants along with him, who perhaps are more eager to hasten to church? To his own pleasures he adds the sins of others, not knowing that he will be guilty both of his own fault and of the ruin of his servants.”

And Saint Leo the Pope says: “The whole substance of our fast does not stand in abstinence from food alone. For food is not fruitfully withdrawn from the body unless the mind is called back from every iniquity.”

The blessed Jerome also says: “What does it profit for the body to be made thin by abstinence, if the soul swells with pride? What virtue is there in not drinking wine, and being drunk with anger, hatred, and the other poisonous cups of the soul?”

Therefore let none of you promise himself holiness from abstinence from foods alone, since whatever is required for true repentance is judged necessary also for fasting. For fasts profit unto salvation for those who are contrite for their sins in a true purpose of amendment, who fervently devote themselves to good and holy works. Therefore those who fast from food and drink must also abstain from all vices, so that the mind, adorned with the beauty of virtues, may sweetly burn in the love of Christ and by no means grow lukewarm in the progress of inward conduct.

For abstinence is no virtue if it has not been adorned with works of charity. Therefore hear my counsel, O monk, if you wish to make your fast fruitful before God: first cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin, so that no desire for evil may remain in you, so that your mind may become fervent in the exercise of divine love and be laudably adorned with all virtues.
