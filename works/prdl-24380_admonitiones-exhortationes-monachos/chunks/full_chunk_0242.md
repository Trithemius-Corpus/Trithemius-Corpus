Woe to you, perverse monk, who presume to inflict so great an insult on the order that you destroy monasteries which neither you nor your parents built. Woe also to you, who provide patronage in evil matters against God and his saints, who entangle yourself in the sins of others, who sell your tongue, who for a paltry price incur the indignation of God and of all the saints. Woe to you, I say, advocate of reprobate monks, who do injury to God, who mock his saints by a wicked deed, who deny the right of equity, who encourage monks by words and deeds to return to the world.

O what will you answer to almighty God when an account of all your works is demanded from you, when you are accused by Saint Benedict as the destroyer of his order? What will you then set against the accusation of the saints? What will you answer? How will you excuse yourself? Who will make a plea before the strict judge for your deliverance? What advocacy will you then be able to have, you who now are an advocate of the wicked against God? Would that you were wise and understood; would that you foresaw the last things; you would by no means entangle yourself in the sins of others. But inflamed by the torches of greed, you think only of the profits of this life, and show plainly that you have no understanding at all of future calamities. Therefore, just as birds are caught in a snare, so you will be caught, when you do not expect it, in your own sins and in the sins of those whom you have made apostatize from the order.

O how harsh a sentence they will receive by whose counsel monks have despised their order. You have falsely claimed great usefulness in your petition, you who intend this alone: that you may shamefully empty the order of its honor. Is the worship of God increased in this way? Is this that peace promised to men round about? You will never make bad monks into good canons. But he who makes men bad, what does he deserve except the reward of the bad? Bad are the monks who, despising the order, return to the world. For unless they were bad and sinful, they would by no means abandon the holy order. For no one hates the good except a bad man; no one withdraws from the holy purpose which he vowed except a perverse man. And he who offers himself as a patron to the perverse in evil causes makes himself a sharer in the sins of others.

To be a canon in the Church of God is good and holy; but to be a monk for the love of God is of greater perfection. A canon is permitted to pass over to the monks as to a higher degree; but a monk is forbidden to descend to the canons. Nor does a dispensation make lawful what has been obtained by false representations. If there is necessity in the petition, integrity in the intention of the petitioner, and honorableness in the use of the dispensation, I say boldly that the pope can dispense with a monk so that he may become a canon, yet in such a way that he is not, without necessity, released from that by which he is known to be bound to God.
