Homily IX.

He says: “Do you not know that your members are the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have from God, and that you are not your own?” And elsewhere: “For the temple of God is holy, and that is what you are.” And concerning Paul himself, in the Acts of the Apostles: “The Lord said to Ananias: Go, for this man is a vessel of election for me.”

The monk who serves in priestly order in the Church of God is bound for two reasons to the observance of most holy purity. For a soldier of Christ, because of the vow that he has made to God, must be pure, holy, and always tireless in good works; and by reason of the priestly ministry he must be circumspect and most diligently watchful. For it is written: “Cursed is the man who does the work of God negligently.”

You are ministers of almighty God, who daily offer his Son upon the altar; his work is committed into your hands, and therefore great purity of mind and body is required in your manner of life. Therefore, so that the chaste purity of the mind may remain, the instability of thought must be restrained. The senses, through which the integrity of the mind is corrupted, must be guarded at all times.

For you have died to the world, and have now begun to live for Christ. It is not lawful for you to think anything except what is holy, nor is it fitting for you, ministers of God, to love anything earthly. It is God whom you serve, O monks; it is the Almighty whom you minister to, my brothers. Live pure, holy, and innocent lives, because it is not fitting for you to be occupied with the business of the world, you who have been called to the service of God. Let love of God and purity of heart grow in you with time, so that the longer you live in this world, the holier you may become.

Always turn your sight away from every evil, so that you may be able to remain without failing in good thoughts. Always lift the eyes of your mind to heaven, and do not occupy the sight of the flesh with perishable things. Close your hearing also at all times against every useless, vain, and foolish word, and always think with fear upon the future judgment of God. Do not defile your hands with unlawful and shameful contact with any things whatsoever, so that you may be able to offer the Son of God with a clean body and a pure conscience.

In food and drink it is not for monks to distinguish flavors, nor is it fitting for soldiers of Christ to love bodily pleasures. Nature requires few things, and simple, rustic foods by which it may be nourished; but appetite, unless it is restrained by the command of reason, is carried off toward unlawful things, and through it the simplicity of nature is violated. Avoid gluttony and drunken excess in all things, and keep temperance in your regular diet.

Let none of you patiently listen to one slandering his brother or speaking evil of his neighbor. Let each one of you restrain his tongue from useless speech, and let a good monk be ashamed to speak evil of Christian people. Never let that warning of the Lord in the holy Gospel depart from your memory, where he says: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.
