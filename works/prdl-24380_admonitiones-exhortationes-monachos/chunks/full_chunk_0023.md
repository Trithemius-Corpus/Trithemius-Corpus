Blessed indeed are those who search the testimonies of the Lord; with their whole heart they seek him.

And so, my brothers, if you truly love the Lord Jesus with your whole heart, if as true soldiers you desire to please him above all things, if, finally, you long to advance toward the perfection of the monastic life, you ought always to occupy yourselves with fervent affection in the reading of the divine Scriptures. How useful the reading of the Scriptures is, blessed Jerome indicates when he says: Love knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.

For after God nothing in this life is taken up more pleasantly, nothing is felt more sweetly, nothing separates the mind more powerfully from love of this world, nothing so greatly strengthens the soldier of Christ against all the temptations of his adversaries, nothing disposes and confirms a monk so much for every good work, as frequent reading and ardent study of the Scriptures. Indeed, if by reading throughout the whole wide range of Scripture we recall with studious devotion the benefits of the Almighty which he has bestowed on the human race, they will provide us with the greatest matter and occasion by which we may advance, burning ever more in the love of God. For what else does the whole sequence of the divine Scriptures contain than a certain ordered text of the good things which God has either already bestowed on human beings or has promised that he will yet give?

Or what is there that can so strengthen us to endure evils and make us willingly patient as if we read in the Gospel that the Son of God suffered innocently for us, and after him the victories of the holy martyrs? For Sacred Scripture contains the things that are necessary for us for saving knowledge, and it warns us what, as soldiers in this pilgrimage, we ought usefully to seek, and what also to avoid.

Hence again the divine Jerome says: Our riches are to meditate on the law of God day and night. Therefore let us prepare strength for ourselves; let us take up perfect power, so that amid the darkness of errors and the confusion of night the light of the knowledge of Christ may appear, and days and nights may have a protecting light, so that, with Scripture as guide, we may be able to reach the holy land.

What is better than this learning? What is sweeter than this discipline? What is more delightful than our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the true salvation and sweetness of faithful souls? Let Christ be our banquet; let sacred reading be our food and drink. For what can the life of a monk-priest be without knowledge of the Scriptures? We know Christ not by sight, not by hearing, not by touch, but by the reading of the Scriptures; and the more we advance by reading toward true knowledge, the more we delight in the holy Scriptures.

For what is sweeter than to know the will of God and not to be ignorant of the rewards of the promises? For as Jerome says: The field of the divine word is exceedingly rich; it has all delights within itself, and whatever you wish is born from it, just as the Jews relate that when the manna was eaten in the desert, it tasted in the mouth of the one eating according to his will and desire.
