If you look back, if you corrupt your purpose, if you return to the world, you are not fit for the kingdom of God, and you will by no means deserve to receive it. But if you persevere in your calling, if you preserve your good will, if you have the love of God in your heart, believe me: even if in some matters you have lived negligently, you will find Saint Benedict a gracious intercessor with God, for love of whom you were unwilling to abandon the order when you could have done so.

Remember, I beseech you, the covenant of the Lord which, when you entered the order, you made with him; and do not be unfaithful to your Redeemer, do not break your vow, do not violate the pact by so wicked a perjury. Consider the pact, attend to the condition, and beware lest you prove a liar to God. Before you promised him, you were free; but after you promised, you were no longer free, but were made a servant of God. It is not permitted to you henceforth to abandon this servitude voluntarily assumed; it is not permitted to you, nor to anyone, after profession to return to the world.

But if you corrupt your purpose, if you deny your vow, if you return to the world, God will not have you in that rank in which he would formerly have had you before you entered the monastery. For before your entrance into religion, you were as it were of the household of the Lord, among his subjects and laborers. You were, I say, a creature of God; you were a servant, you were a bondsman, but you were not a friend, an attendant of his mysteries, an intimate, a secretary. But after you became a monk, you promised fidelity to the Lord, that you would cling to him continually, so that where the Lord is, there you also, his minister, might be. He chose you as a friend and, so to speak, as guardian of his body, that in his sight you might always minister to him and carry out his will in all things. For this is what you promised.

Therefore, if you withdraw from him to whom you vowed yourself, if you are found unfaithful and a despiser of your vow, he will not have you as one of his household, as before you became a monk, but certainly as a fugitive, an unfaithful man, a transgressor, a perjurer, and a liar. For the monk who denies his vow denies Christ. Blessed Jerome says: “I, who seem to be a monk, if I corrupt my purpose, have denied Christ.”

But you transgressors, who, after despising the sacred order, from monks have become canons, you, I say, have not only corrupted your purpose, but, what is more wicked, have utterly destroyed it. Therefore now either you have denied Christ, or what Jerome says is false. But since what so great a man taught cannot be false, it remains that you are deniers of Christ, perjurers, and unfaithful men. Would that, even late at last, you might take up the remedies of repentance, you who are placed in such great dangers. If he has denied Christ who has violated the monastic purpose, what does he deserve who defrauds the whole order, all the saints, and God?
