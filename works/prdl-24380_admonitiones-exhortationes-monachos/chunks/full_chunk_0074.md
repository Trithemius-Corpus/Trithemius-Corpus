Chastity is corrupted when a monk too often wanders outside his cell with desire. He who leaves the privacy of his cell without the elder’s order does not strive toward the perfection of his holy purpose. Beautiful is the fellowship of good men who fear God; and he who has become accustomed to dwell with them will not feel confusion. The heart of a young monk is a clean tablet, on which the conduct of elders is written, never to be erased.

A very bad elder is a writer on a fair heart when he either does or says shameful things before a young man. He who infects the minds of the young with rash speech is like wicked Herod, who killed the children. The harm that a young man receives from a foolish and negligent old man he will not amend even when he has become a priest. Every vessel that is filled with vinegar is spoiled; and a monk who is neglected in his novitiate is not amended when he has grown old.

Cursed is the old man by whose evil conduct the young perish, since he who leaves evil footsteps for those who follow is like one who has cast a blind man with his own hand into a pit. Before the inward Judge each person is worthy of as many deaths as the innocent who have perished by his depraved example. Young man, seek an old man to be the guide of your life: not one who has completed many years in his lax and lukewarm way of life, but one whom the fear of the Lord and knowledge of God’s Scriptures commend.

Better is the fellowship of a young monk who fears the Lord than the company of an old teacher whose life lacks the light of divine love and the integrity of regular observance. An old man disordered in morals, lacking cleanness of mind and without discipline, is like one injured in the leg, led lame to the grave. An old and lax monk is like a foolish man who, not knowing the end, is caught with robbers at a drinking banquet. An elder without maturity is like an elderwood pipe crushed under the feet of those playing. Vainly does he boast that he has been a monk for many days, if he has not perceived the beginning of inward conversion.

He who loves the cleanness of his heart guards himself at all times from the company of undisciplined brothers. As a pitch stain clinging to the garments of dancers saddens virgins, so a good young man blushes at the shameful words of elders. Wanton speech does not belong to a man who fears God, since out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks; and he who utters shameful words is not clean before the Most High.

If sinners begin to hate you because of the fear of God, do not be saddened, because unless you displease the evil, you will not attain the perfection of the good. For to displease the evil for righteousness’ sake is praiseworthy, and a clear sign of divine love. If you love your soul, flee the counsels of the undisciplined, and do not associate with scoffers. Walk with the good, and let one who fears the Lord become your counselor.
