For we are soldiers of God, fighting against a threefold adversary: namely, the flesh, the world, and the devil. It is a doubtful war and an exceedingly hard contest to suffer as an enemy one whom nature has given as a familiar companion. Therefore, dearly beloved brothers, let us arm ourselves with the adornments of virtues, since whoever is adorned with the splendor of good works is not easily conquered by the adversary.

First, let us fortify ourselves with the most steadfast faith of the Holy and Undivided Trinity; the devout and sincere confession of it in mind and mouth drives far away all the wicked schemes and deceits of demons, and, overcoming the greatest enemy, casts him down and utterly weakens him. Then let us take up for ourselves the most fervent love of God and of neighbor, which is more fearful to demons than all else, especially if it has been rooted in true Christian humility.

Let us not give confidence to our soul, since blessed is said to be the man who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil. Let us follow Christ our leader, who said in the Gospel: “I am the way, the truth, and the life, for no one comes to the Father except through me.”

Now therefore, brothers, attend to these things which are read in the holy Scriptures concerning our Savior, since he who says that he remains in Christ, that is, in the way of truth, should strive to walk just as he walked. But it was necessary for him first to suffer the reproach of the cross and so to enter into his glory, so that we too, his soldiers, however unworthy, might be warned that we must enter the kingdom of God through many tribulations. Therefore the Lord himself, King of all things, admonished us in the Gospel, saying: “Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Behold, Christ sets before us the way to eternal life: though he was Lord of heavenly and earthly things, for our sake he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant; in that form, willingly and not under compulsion, he bore our weaknesses, becoming obedient to the Father even unto death, death on a cross. And so, if it was necessary for the Lord of the whole universe first to suffer, it certainly cannot be that we, his imitators, wicked and most vile sinners, should not have to suffer many things, we who justly endure, by our own desert, everything that we suffer.

For no one comes to the glory of the heavenly city unless he has first been justified here from sin; but since the tribulations of the just are many, a man will by no means be able to be just if he has refused to endure adversities for justice in this world. Therefore, my dearly beloved brothers, let us imitate in great patience and humility our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who, having become man for us, willingly endured the greatest things, and in this world was never without the bitterness of persecution. For from the hour of his most sacred birth until the departure of his spirit from his body, he was always in the bitterness of the present life.
