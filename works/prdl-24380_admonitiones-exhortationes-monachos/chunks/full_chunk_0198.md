Sermon V.

We shall see countless demons around us as witnesses against us; below us we shall see the horrible open chaos; above us we shall behold the judge, angry; and on every side the utmost danger will be hanging over us. My brothers, always turn over in the memory of your heart these dangers of the examination to come, and diligently consider how dreadful it is to fall under the future sentence of the judge. In that terrible hour pure hearts will have more power than the crafty words of rhetoricians; a clean conscience will help a man more than the money of the whole world, because he who is to judge all things is not deceived, indeed is changed neither by price nor by prayers.

O what incredible horror will seize all the wicked, what fearful sentence they will hear: “Depart, accursed, into eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels.” O worst of all evils; O deepest sorrow of all sorrows, to be separated from the fellowship of the saints and to be tormented with demons forever. Truly the day of judgment is horrible and to be feared by all, because it is exceedingly bitter: there the worm of conscience does not die; there one once damned to punishment is never set free.

Therefore the monk who desires to attain eternal salvation should always keep himself, as far as he can, clean from every sin, and that voice, “Depart, accursed, into eternal fire,” should never depart from his memory. Hence the third thing which we judge to be of great help in turning away from sin is to think diligently on the bitterness of the punishments of eternal damnation. For no temptation of fleshly pleasure is so violent that it will not at once fail, overcome, if the person tempted recalls in earnest meditation what the damned are now suffering in hell.

What profit now to the damned are the riches they once possessed? What do past pleasures bring to the lustful who have been placed in hell? What do the emptiest honors bring to the proud? What does the power once so broad bring to the arrogant? Think, brothers, what the damned are now suffering in the lower world, and lest similar things befall you, persevere continually in good and holy works. If you fear hell, flee sin. For hell will do no harm if a man has remained in the faith of Christ, free from sins; nor will he burn in punishment who has not first burned here in guilt.

The place of hell must be feared, where there is no redemption; but whoever, once damned, has entered there will no longer go out to salvation. They cry out in torments, wretched in anguish, placed in everlasting horror, saying: “What does our pride profit us now, our riches, honors, dignities, luxury, gluttony, and all past fleshly pleasures? What do they profit us now, damned forever? Behold, all things have passed like a shadow, and everything has vanished like the brief dream of a single night. But we have been assigned to eternal torments, in which we know most certainly that there is no redemption.”
