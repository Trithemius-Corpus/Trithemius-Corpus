He loves discord; he will be caught in the sloth of his own mind, and there will be no one to grieve with him. He will be crushed by a double affliction, because he did not allow peace to those who possessed it. The devil has made a nest in his tongue, and Satan has fixed his dwelling in his mind. Therefore there is no love of the brethren in his heart, because he sows discord between the shepherd and the sheep. He boasts that he is a friend of God and accepts no one as his equal in regular observance, yet he does not understand humility, the greatest ornament of a monk. He does not open his mouth for words of spiritual edification, and from his heart nothing proceeds except vanity. All day long, dissolved in mind and wandering in body, he runs about the monastery, gives himself to useless and entirely idle talk, thinks nothing of the salvation of his soul, and cares for his belly as his god.

Guard your mind from his speech, and do not set your foot on the path of his words. Whoever you are who desire to show yourself a monk and minister of God, whoever you are who truly love the Lord Jesus with your whole heart, be cautious and watchful in all your works, and always flee the company of those in whose life no heavenly teaching is seen. Be the companion and associate of humble monks who pursue perfection; and the one whom you have known to be grounded in the fear of God, gladly accept as instructor and master. Walk gladly with those who love discipline and purity of mind, and do not be like a man who, shaken, yields to every wind, but strengthen your soul in a good purpose, lest you be deceived by the counsel of the wicked. For it is best for the heart to be strengthened by grace, and neither to be able to be deceived nor to wish to deceive.

Have as your friend a disciplined brother; and love and venerate as a father the one who corrects you from evil by word and instructs you toward good by example. Have no familiar dealings with a restless brother, lest, strengthened by his wicked example, you ever fall into the rebellion of the proud. Let no monk who is angry and full of murmuring be at your side; and never consider as a friend the one who kicks against his superiors. When you see someone violating the rule of fasting without the blessing of an elder, flee him as an enemy of salvation; and one who is quick to chatter, when there is no cause, overturns peace and stirs up quarrels.

Flee a rebellious monk, disobedient to elders, as from the face of a serpent, because he burns full of the poison of malice, and in the pride of his mind he has lost his soul. He who does not obey the command of an elder is perverse; and he who, being corrupt, is not humbled, is an apostate. Let one who disparages his brother with slander not be in conversation with you, because he is far worse than a thief. The one whom you see wholly zealous in regular observance you shall love as a father and venerate as a most loving instructor. Confidently enjoy the familiarity of the one whose friendship always makes you better in the love of God. Imitate good men with all zeal, and follow the conduct of those who live in conformity with monastic perfection. Have as a friend a brother of good will; but from the one who is hard of heart, turn away your ears.
