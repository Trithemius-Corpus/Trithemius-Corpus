Take care, my brothers, not to admit any error in faith, and strive with the utmost care that there be no wickedness in your conduct. Follow the faith which holy Church commends to us; live as the Gospel commands, and as the monastic rule commands. Then have at all times a most certain hope in the mercy of God, yet never one estranged from the fear of the Lord.

Hope is the certain expectation, through the grace of the merciful God, of future goods, and a solicitude to merit, through the exercise of charity, the crown of righteousness. Hope is the certain expectation of future blessedness, which the grace of God works in us, accompanied by the uprightness of a good will. Hope is that by which a person hopes to come to the life of eternal happiness, which he desires, and, through the mediation of God’s grace, also arranges and adapts his own will to meet it. Hope is of good things, difficult, future, and possible, for no one hopes for the contrary. For no one hopes for evil, nor for what is slight, nor for what is present or past; nor are we unaware that hope is not concerned with impossibilities.

Hope is the moderator of all labors and the sole consoler in the midst of tribulations. For hope of future goods brings rest to those who labor for Christ in this life, just as, for those contending in the arena, the expectation of the crown through continual victory lessens the pain of wounds. Hope strengthened the martyrs amid torments and made them, in the power of Christ, stronger than all their persecutors. Hope of eternal blessedness preserves the Church, and gave to the holy confessors and virgins true charity in Christ. Hope powerfully raises up a spirit cast down by grief; and the higher it lifts itself through love, the less it feels the adversities of this world. Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord, because hope does not confound, as the holy Apostle promised, by which we have been saved.

But so that hope may not break out into presumption, the fear of the Lord is necessary for our salvation, so that, while we believe him to be kind and merciful, we may not doubt that he is also just and upright. For the most blessed Pope Gregory says: Nothing is safer for us than to fear under hope, lest an unwary mind either cast itself down into the depth of vices by despairing, or fall presumptuously by exalting itself over gifts received. For the more it trembles humbly under hope before the eyes of the strict judge, the more firmly it stands in him.

The fear of God corrects the mind and keeps it pure from all malice. The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, because in the way of God one begins from fear, so that little by little one may come to the strength of charity. The fear of the Lord orders the monk and makes him zealous in the observance of regular discipline. The fear of the Lord guards the rule and preserves true purity of mind and body. The fear of the Lord neglects none of the things that have been prescribed, but guards them all with care. The fear of the Lord admits no idleness, but is continually occupied.
