Charity counts others’ troubles as its own, and weeps for brothers in tribulation as though it itself were suffering. Charity has compassion for all who are in need, and is saddened no less by the misfortunes of others than by its own. Charity reproaches a neighbor with nothing, but for the love of God always bears the weaknesses of all with equanimity. Charity is constant in good works, and so long as it remains in the heart, it is never idle. Charity neglects none of the things to which it is bound, but keeps all things as diligently as possible.

Fraternal charity is the fulfillment of the law, since whatever is commanded after the worship of God is referred to love of neighbor. Hence the holy lover of Christ says: “He who loves his neighbor has fulfilled the law. For, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not kill, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, You shall not covet your neighbor’s property, and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this word: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. Love of neighbor works no evil. Therefore love is the fullness of the law.”

And so, my dearest brothers, we beseech you through our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead, that you always preserve fraternal charity unharmed; without it you can neither truly love God nor fulfill any commandment of his. For the preservation of fraternal charity is as necessary for your salvation as the nourishment of food and drink is for bodies to preserve life. For the body dies without necessary nourishment, and all the virtues of the soul are scattered without fraternal charity.

Indeed, fraternal charity preserves a multitude of monks in unity of spirit, because without the bond of mutual love no cloistered man perseveres without disturbance of the community. The monk who pursues his brother with hatred is driven far away from the charity of God. For the holy Evangelist John says: “He who hates his brother is in darkness, and walks in darkness, and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.”

But the chaste and prudent lover of his neighbor walks securely in the light of the Holy Spirit, and is neither disturbed by anyone himself nor disturbs anyone else. For true love of neighbor is gentle, sweet, and most dutiful, and in all its common life meek, peaceable, and tranquil. Yet among rulers the vigor of discipline guides meekness, and the clemency of fraternal consideration beautifully tempers vigor. For one must be so tempered by the other that vigor is not harsh, nor love lax. The lover of fraternal charity does not abandon the way of justice; and he who rightly loves his neighbor admits no corruption.
