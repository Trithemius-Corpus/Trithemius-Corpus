But when I consider the end of those who long ago severed themselves from the order, I judge that this longsuffering patience of God has been avenged by a sufficiently severe punishment. For, as we have learned from the report of those who knew many of them, few of those who cast off the monk died a good death. Rather, called suddenly from this life without signs of repentance, without ecclesiastical sacraments, they gave a clear sign of their unhappiness. What then shall we say to these things? Shall we rejoice in the perdition of the dying? Far from it. It is the Lord who long tolerates sinners here so that they may be converted; he is also the one who condemns more harshly those who are not converted. But we fear his sentence with the prophet, saying: “The righteous man, by whatever death he has been overtaken, will be in refreshment.” But of the wicked the Prophet says: “The death of sinners is most evil.” We recall the destruction of the depraved to mind for this reason, that by fear at least we may call the rest back from an evil undertaking.

But how will they receive our words, who deride the things above and the things below? Unless deeds are added to words, exhortations accomplish nothing among malicious minds. Still the stiff-necked people murmurs in the desert; still the impious nation blasphemes its lawgiver; still the wicked people deliberates about returning to Egypt. They fear dying in solitude, when this alone they ought to have feared: dying outside solitude. O hard mind of men, O destructive liberty of monks, how long does your cruelty rage, which does not even know how to spare those whom it has as authors and friends? He who is wicked to himself, to whom will he be good?

O unheard-of crime of monks, O immeasurable offense: to mock God, to deny him what was promised, to break faith, to withdraw the service owed. What is more cruel than this impiety? What is more wicked than this sacrilege? O perverse monk, O unjust apostate, O most wicked perjurer, how long do you set God’s kindness against him, how long do you deride the mercy of the Almighty? You are a monk; you will also be judged a monk, even if you have laid aside the skin along with the habit. Why do you depart from the holy order? Why do you lie to God? Believe me, the Almighty will not give his honor to another; he will not forgive you forever for what, once solemnly received, you offered. Before you promised God, you were free and of your own judgment; but after you promised, you began to belong to God. Now it is not lawful for you to let go what belongs by right to God. Your voice bound you to God; you will be saved as a monk, or otherwise you will not be saved.

Behold, before your eyes are the way of life and the way of death; choose whichever you wish. If you keep faith with God, if you fulfill what was promised, you will have a reward; if you deny it, you will receive punishment for contempt. Hence Paul, admonishing you, says: “Brothers, do not err; God is not mocked. For whatever a man has sown, this also will he reap.” What do you presume you will receive, you who sow in the flesh? For he says: “He who sows in the flesh, from the flesh will also reap.” But the fruit of the flesh has no share in the inheritance of the saints. Why then do you deceive yourself? Why in vain...
