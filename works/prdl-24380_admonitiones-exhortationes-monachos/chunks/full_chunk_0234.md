You have the Rule; shape your life according to its precepts: no one demands more of you, nor requires more. The exercises of monks, which you condemn in those who observe the Rule, the Rule itself teaches, itself commands, itself enjoins. If you refuse to imitate them, you have destroyed yourself by the sentence of your own mouth. For it imposes upon its professed members the conversion of morals according to the Rule of Saint Benedict: fasting, abstinence, vigils, disciplines, obedience, patience, humility, poverty, and the other exercises of monastic discipline, which you judge hard and burdensome, are so proper to the Rule that without them no one deserves to be called a monk.

Tell me, I beg you, transgressor of your vow and of your order, tell me: do you still wish to live blessedly? Do you still hope that you can come to eternal life? You will answer: “That question you ask is foolish. Who does not wish to live blessedly? Who does not hope to come to eternal life?” Therefore I both desire to live blessedly and trust that I can come to the heavenly homeland. If then this is so, if you trust that you will receive the blessed life, why do you abandon, scorn, and despise the way that leads to it?

You are mistaken, wretched man; unhappy man, you are deceived. One does not come to the kingdom except through humility; to rest, except through labor, through abstinence, through obedience, through poverty. For Scripture says: “Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.” But you, unhappy apostate, who reject the order, scorn humility, refuse obedience, hate poverty, despise patience: by what rashness do you presume to such heights? If the mouth that lies kills the soul, unless you do penance, you have no share in the kingdom of Christ and of God, you who lie not to man but to God, who violate the covenant, who deny your faith, who take back the vow of religion made to God.

The dispensation of the Roman Pontiff will not save you, I dare say, which you seek by fraud, describe with falsehood, and extort by importunate petitions. For the four things which we said make a due dispensation do not seem in the least to have come together in your frauds. For that transfer which you make from the holy order is neither useful nor necessary, and for that reason is unfitting and dishonorable. In it, indeed, will stands in place of necessity, pleasure in place of usefulness, carnality in place of fitness, and in place of honor [unclear].
