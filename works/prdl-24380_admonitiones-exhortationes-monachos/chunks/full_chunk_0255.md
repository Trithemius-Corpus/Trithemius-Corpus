For a chapter is the preservation of order, the increase of regular discipline, the uniting of unity. In it, indeed, integrity is given to morals, purity is increased in minds, and the equality of regular life is restored. But hear what is required for it to be celebrated rightly; hear what its sincere celebration demands. It requires zeal, it requires knowledge, it requires piety: without these three the celebration of a chapter is worth nothing. For if zeal is lacking, who will correct offenders? If knowledge is lacking, what fruit will excessive piety bring forth? Again, if piety is lacking, neither does zeal receive love, nor knowledge discipline. For piety avails for all things: zeal sharpens, knowledge directs, piety produces fruit. Without zeal, knowledge remains cold; without piety, fruitless. Likewise zeal without knowledge will be excessive and undiscerning; without piety, hateful. But piety avails for all things, although without zeal and knowledge it is not sufficient for the governance of souls. For it pleases all, and benefits only the humble when set apart.

Therefore the whole matter of celebrating a chapter consists in the disposition of the presiding officers, which requires men who are zealous, learned, and gentle. For if they are lax, inexperienced, cruel, and impious, how then will they reform others to piety? If the man who sits to punish crimes admits them himself, whom like himself will he amend? Therefore this fault must be referred to you, Fathers, if you choose deformed and vicious abbots for the presidency. With the greatest care, then, see that you appoint as presiding officers over the chapter such men as an irreproachable life commends. Let zeal for holy religion sharpen them; let instruction in saving knowledge temper them. For one who presides over others must be a man upright in morals, mature in conduct, fervent in zeal, instructed in knowledge of the Scriptures, adorned with gentleness. Such a man, O Fathers, is rightly to be preferred over us, whose life offers an example and whose teaching gives an increase of virtue. If the presiding officers of the chapter are such, those subject to it will easily be brought back to obedience. For those will be fit to preside who can instruct the teachable by word, the rude by example, and the obstinate by authority. In these ways, indeed, the chapter will be rightly celebrated, and the souls of the subjects will be built up.

The other things that make presiding officers suitable I am not permitted to describe on this occasion, since I believe none of them is ignorant of what sort of men they ought to be. Therefore I turn again to you, O Fathers, who have gathered in this place for the celebration of the chapter. I address you; I exhort you to direct your intention to the restoration of the order, and to consider with careful mind why the chapter was instituted. Do you wish to know why chapters were instituted? Seek out the decrees of the supreme pontiffs, and you will know the cause of their institution. But lest either laziness or occupation hinder the understanding of this matter, I shall briefly explain the cause for the establishment of the chapter.
