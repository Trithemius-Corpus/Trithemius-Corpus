Homily XV

Let us restrain ourselves. Let us fast from every sin; let us take no food of malice; let us devour no feasts of pleasure; nor let us be drunk with the wine of luxury. Let us fast from all depravity; let us abstain from evil works. Let us not speak useless, vain, idle, buffoonish, and harmful words; and let us always beware of harmful and perverse thoughts, which separate us from God. Let us keep most diligent watch in prayers to God, knowing that he whom we call upon with our prayers is everywhere most present to us. Let his praise be always in our mouth, and, since we cannot praise his magnificence in speech, let us adore him in our heart with the desires of a most chaste love. For then our fasts are pleasing to him, when they have been adorned with holy works and continual prayers. For the prayer of the righteous person, as sacred Scripture bears witness, pierces the heavens; and it becomes the more efficacious, the more it is found adorned with charity and fasting. Let us join to it, as it were, two wings, fasting and charity, and it will obtain for us from the Lord whatever we ask that is conducive to salvation.

On the Lenten fast of monks. Homily XVI.

Since, by the bounty of our Lord Jesus Christ, dearest brothers, we have begun the Lenten fast, reason demands that we say something briefly about its observance. For although you have many writings of the holy fathers concerning this most sacred exercise of fasting, nevertheless, because I find that my sermons among you are not wholly fruitless, I gladly share for your edification what the Lord has granted.

For, as blessed Jerome bears witness, the act of a living voice has a certain hidden operation within, which in the adorned Greek language he called energy, and a discourse spoken by the mouth of one who goes before is stronger for persuasion. But I, set apart in your midst by the name of shepherd alone, confess myself to be a young man, unlearned, and lacking at once the experience and discretion necessary for the governance of souls. Hence it comes about that I speak with a certain blush and modesty: a young man among elders, an ignorant man among the learned, a disciple among masters. Therefore I beg that none of you despise the simplicity of my speech, that none take it hard that I speak, since even if I am not fit for speaking, nevertheless by reason of the office of pastoral care committed to me I acknowledge myself bound before God to admonish you.

Your charity gives me boldness to speak, and the more ready you show yourselves to listen, the more you stir me to speak. Nor will any labor be burdensome to me, however great it may seem, by which the salvation of your souls is brought to completion. Help me with your prayers, that the Lord Jesus may speak in my mouth and grant strength to my small talent to bring forth into the midst whatever can produce in you an increase of holy fervor.
