A secure conscience can exist; but it is always anxious over its guilt, always troubled, since you have the devil as instigator toward despair, by whose prompting you withdrew from the order. Hence it is that you are not at all concerned for the love of God and the salvation of souls; but, intent on earthly vanities and carnal desires, you think of nothing less than eternal life. Your way of life shows by what spirit this transfer of state has been made in you. For those who are led by the Spirit of God have crucified their flesh with its vices and desires. But you are not so. For as soon as you cast off the habit of religion, like wanton oxen freed from the yoke of subjection, you plunge yourselves into every shameful thing as though it were permitted; you cast aside all honesty; you make a pact with vices and wage war against virtues.

The charity of God is not in your hearts. You despise mercy and piety; you love vanity and have become vain. You are evil to all the good; worse than the worst monks to yourselves. No transfer has been made for the better when it preserves access to vices. Love of God and hatred of one’s brother do not dwell in the same breast. But you, violators of your vow, deserters of the order, enemies of honesty, adversaries of piety, if you love God, if you are good, if you love religion, why do you hate monks? And you hate especially those who persevere in the order from which you have fallen away, because, when you now see them standing firmly in their vocation, you fear their testimony against you at the judgment.

Therefore all monks who persevere in holy conduct will stand against you in the strict examination to bear testimony, and by their works they will show that all the stories and reasons for your apostasy, already cloaked here in deceitful words, are false and empty. You hate the monks of your order because, in truth, judging others to be what you know yourselves to be, you envy in others also the good that you do not see in yourselves. We do not marvel at the madness of your heart against monks when we consider the injury and insult you have offered to God and to your holy lawgiver. For if someone does not love God, if he breaks the vow solemnly made to him, if he casts aside the habit of religion, it is no wonder if he hates monks, his servants. For every apostate is an adversary and despiser of his own order.

But I beg, I exhort, I urge all of you who have deserted your order: abandon your fury, soften your rage, cease to rage against monks, lest those whom you now persecute with hatred become witnesses and judges against you at the judgment. Do penance for this impiety of yours while you have time, lest swift death snatch you away unprepared. Do not, I beseech you, raise the horns of wickedness against Saint Benedict; do not wish, by your transfers, to destroy his venerable order; do not despise the opportunity for penance, if you desire to reach eternal life. You have built no monastery for Saint Benedict; I beg and exhort you not to transfer to secular uses those founded for him by others. Remember how brief this present life is.
