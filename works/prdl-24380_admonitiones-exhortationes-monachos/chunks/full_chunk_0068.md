Homily X

You, I say, my most beloved fellow-soldiers, always keep watch in good works, mortifying your bodies, which are upon the earth, for love of Christ; be constant in fasts and prayers, and continually occupied in meditation on the divine Scriptures. Always stretch yourselves forward to what lies ahead; every day renew with fresh zeal the fervor of your holy purpose. Flee idleness and every sluggishness, and diligently consider how brief is the time of this life.

Let not the short and moderate toil of the hard contest terrify you; let not the wickedness of the enemy make you faint-hearted, because every labor is quickly overcome by willing perseverance, and the devil, however much he rages, is defeated as soon as you have invoked Christ. Remember the words of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who says in the holy Gospel: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me. But what is our cross, my brothers, except a life full of temptations and adversities, and the burden of regular discipline, under which we are wearied day and night?

But let us gather our hearts, dearest brothers, and gladly bear for the love of God whatever he wishes us to bear, so that at his judgment we may deserve to say: We passed through fire and water, and you brought us into refreshment. O how glorious a reward is laid up in the kingdom of heaven for the servants of God, who, contending in this brief course, are now wearied for love of him. Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, the immensity of that good which almighty God has prepared in eternal happiness for those who love him, to remain without end forever.

The highest happiness of this good is acquired by humility, and possessed by purity of heart and the fervor of divine love. Let us hasten there, dearest brothers; let us run there with as much devotion of mind as we can, where the spirit is joined to its creator through the enjoyment of sweetness, where the intellect is perfected in the knowledge of the highest good, which is God. There is the joy of everlasting happiness, which is not diminished; there is gladness which has no end; there is perpetual rest from all labors; there is the glorious reward for tribulations and injuries suffered for Christ. Whatever good we now do, there without doubt we shall find laid up; whatever adversity we have patiently borne for the love of Christ, there we shall receive it rewarded with most abundant payment.

O monk, monk, you who fruitlessly spend the time mercifully granted you by the Lord God for doing good, what will you reap, or what wages will you receive in that terrible hour, you who are now so lazy and unwilling to sow? Have you not read what is written about the labors of the saints? Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming, they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves. Now is the time to work the field of your heart, O monk; now is the hour to sow good seed with the blessing of cheerfulness, because he who now sows sparingly will also reap sparingly in that future repayment of reward.

Now we must labor, and never cease from good work, because whatever we have done here now, there without doubt we shall receive. Therefore if anyone among you is a faithful monk, if anyone is a zealous lover of his own salvation, let him always think of the hour of the future reward, which no one can escape; let him always prepare himself to render an account of his stewardship. Let no day ever pass in which he has not done some particular good work, to send ahead for future repayment. Let him judge that he has spent uselessly the day on which he has not sent ahead a good work for which he may expect to be rewarded.

A good monk strives at all times to cultivate the field of his heart, and to root out completely, as far as he can, all the thorns and thistles of evil affections. Let him learn to love the Lord Jesus above all things with a pure mind, and to keep most diligently whatever he has commanded. Let him sow in the tears of devotion and constancy what he may reap with all the saints in exultation. Let not the continual difficulty of laboring frighten his mind; let not the hard struggle of the flesh break his spirit, because he who labors little gathers little fruit at harvest; and he who now sows much in tears for the love of Christ will carry back most abundant fruit on that day.

For this is the destination of our journey, brothers, which we undertake in the present pilgrimage: that, purified by continual exercise in labors and adversities, we may depart from here clean and pure to our homeland.
