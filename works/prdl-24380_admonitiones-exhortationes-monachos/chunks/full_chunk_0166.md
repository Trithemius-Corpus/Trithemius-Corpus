We are all taken from the midst as the fates press on,
and we are drawn where life, once completed, calls us.

Therefore, wherever we can do good, my brothers, let us do it while we have time, lest, when death’s command forestalls us, we grieve too late that we have been negligent. Time alone is ours; all other things are another’s. The labor of our spiritual warfare, even if it seems somewhat hard to lukewarm minds, is nevertheless very brief, and will soon be ended with life; but the reward of good works, which will be given to us, remains forever.

Let meditation on death soon to come never leave you; but by consideration of the strict judgment, which no one can escape, let it continually pierce your minds with love of God. Behold how many monks in this monastery of Sponheim have gone before us, living and dying, through almost four hundred years from its first foundation; of them no memory at all remains among us today. What we are, they were; what they are, we shall soon be. They were mortal men, as we are; they died and returned to the dust of the earth, as we shall.

I am the twenty-fifth abbot of this monastery of Sponheim; unless twenty-four had gone before me to death, I certainly would not have come as the twenty-fifth to this abbacy. I too shall shortly give place to another, so that the number of the twenty-sixth abbot may be fulfilled. After him another again will be appointed; and the death of one becomes the institution of another; and while one succeeds another, all are subjected to the command of death.

Where, then, brothers, are those who went before us in this monastery? Just as there is no memory among us of those who went before, so we too shall be in everlasting oblivion among those who follow. Let us think on these things beforehand, dearly beloved brothers, while we live, lest we fall upon death unprepared. As daily we hasten toward death, so daily let us prepare ourselves according to our strength; for he dies secure who in life is adorned with good and holy works.

Do not let the hope of a longer life deceive you; for the longer you live in this world, the shorter is the time you will still live. Behold, daily tending toward death, we die; daily one part of our life is cut away from us; and the longer our life is drawn out, the more the remaining time for living is shortened. All the time that we have lived we have wholly lost, because it has passed away; the present moment in which we live today we share with death; how much time for living remains to us in the future, we do not know. Why, then, do we trust in the vanity of this life, we who are soon to die?
