I set before them the example of the man already mentioned, who do not heed Jesus Christ, the most patient of all men. For indeed, whoever does not embrace patience and humility after the example of the King of heaven will not be recalled to gentleness by the imitation of any man. For the Son of God is the highest and supreme model of patience and humility; whoever is not bent toward endurance by the remembrance of his passion is rightly judged hardened and a companion of demons.

No good Christian doubts that the words of our Lord Jesus Christ are true. Now Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the earth.” If the meek are to be called blessed, what prevents the rancorous, the wrathful, and the unmeek from being called accursed? Certainly nothing. And if, as our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ himself bears witness, we possess our souls in patience, there is no doubt that through impatience we lose them.

It is therefore especially fitting that monks be meek, gentle, and patient, since they have solemnly devoted themselves to the imitation of Christ and the apostles. But Christ, the meekest of all, commands his disciples and imitators in the Gospel, saying: “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you shall find rest for your souls.”

Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: If you wish to reign with Christ in heaven, first learn from him to be meek, patient, humble, and gentle, since unless you have lived in this world in imitation of his life, you will by no means reign with him forever.

Saint Ambrose also says: When I have laid aside every sin, stripped off every malice, and, content with simplicity, have been poor in evils, it remains that I temper my conduct. For what does it profit me to be free of crimes, unless I am meek and gentle? But he is meek who yields to wickedness and does not resist in evil, but overcomes evil with good.

And, as Chrysostom bears witness, he is gentle who, when provoked by injuries, neither thinks of doing evil nor does it.

Cassiodorus, moreover, says: We call the gentle “accustomed to the hand,” that is, patient and meek, those who bear the wrongs of others and do not presume to burden anyone. Of them it is said in the psalm: “My soul shall be praised in the Lord; let the gentle hear and rejoice.” He did not say, “let those skilled in the law rejoice,” nor “those who fast,” nor “those who sing psalms,” but “let the gentle rejoice,” those who, together with charity, have especially been accustomed to possess temperance in all things.

But those are truly meek, gentle, and patient who harm no one, wrong no one, offend no one at all, but, standing firm in their simple humility, always bear with evenness of mind all injuries, insults, and mockeries for the love of Christ, and are moved by no grief of heart against those who harm them, just as we have already said that monk was, whom the raging boys at Bingen treated with great insult.
