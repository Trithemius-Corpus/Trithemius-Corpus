The insolent demon of fornication is never better conquered than when true humility of heart is preserved in the fullness of all virtues. For just as almighty God, resisting the proud, gives grace to the humble, so the devil, fleeing the humble, hurls the proud down into fornication. Therefore anyone who is gluttonous, eating and drinking to satiety, and desires to conquer the spirit of fornication, is carrying oil to the fire. And anyone who strives to extinguish the impulses of lust by abstinence alone is like a man seen swimming with one hand in the midst of the waves. Let continual prayer to God be joined to abstinence, and he will sail securely in the ship of holy humility.

Let prayer always be in the monk’s mouth, and let the memory of the passion and death of the Lord Jesus never depart from his meditation. For nothing so strengthens the mind against the demon of fornication, and the soul, as devout meditation on the passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Therefore, as often as you feel a disordered movement of the flesh, as often as you perceive that the invisible enemy is present to do harm, at once flee to the Son of God hanging on the cross, and, with your knees bent to the ground, let such a prayer be formed to him:

O Jesus crucified, my love, through the pain of your wounds have mercy on me; through the shedding of your most holy blood help me in this struggle; and through the power of your most innocent death free me from every defilement of mind and body.

Nothing is more wholesome than this manner of prayer, in which the memory of Christ’s passion is honored and all petitions are strengthened in the power of the Savior. Nothing more weakens the snares of the ancient enemy, nothing more quickly enfeebles the forces of his malice, than if the devoted servant of Christ humbly perseveres in meditation on the most bitter passion of the Lord. Just as some thief flees before the face of the pursuing judge, so the devil swiftly vanishes from the sight of a monk meditating attentively on the passion and death of the Savior. The movement of wicked thought is quickly extinguished where the bitterness of Christ’s passion is recalled to mind. Just as snow melts when the sun grows warm, so the devil is put to flight by meditation on the Lord’s passion.

The greatest safeguard of chastity is always to invoke divine aid. And unless you run back to the Lord at once from the beginning of temptation, you will gradually feel the harm caused by delay. For if you allow delay amid pernicious thoughts, the demons soon introduce an imperceptible blindness of mind. For unless the mind has first been blinded, the will does not succumb to the demon. But the spirit of fornication first heavily darkens the mind; afterward, until it has hurled it down into sin, it fights against the blinded one. And after it has hurled the monk into the mire of unspeakable crime, lest he rise again to repentance, it sets itself in the way in two ways. For either it subtly tries to lessen the gravity of the sin, so that he may not fear a great punishment; or it brings back to the fallen man’s memory the strictness of judgment, so that he may fall into the pit of despair.
