On this law we all entered the world: that by fighting we should conquer. On this condition we were made human beings: that, having overcome the enticements of the flesh, we should become equal to the holy angels.

Combats are never lacking to monks, since, as long as we live in the flesh, we are necessarily exposed to temptations. For Scripture says: “Many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these the Lord will deliver them.” But in all our temptations let us diligently call to mind that saying of the prophet Isaiah: “You who have been weaned from milk, you who have been drawn away from the breast, expect tribulation upon tribulation, hope upon hope, yet a little while, because of the malice of lips, because of the malignant tongue.” Those weaned and drawn away from the breast are men advanced in age, whose part it is to undergo tribulation upon tribulation for God, so that they may deserve to receive hope upon hope, as sacred Scripture says: “But we also glory in our tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, and patience proof, and proof hope. And hope does not confound, because the charity of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us.” For through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.

Indeed, through temptations we become known to ourselves, and we discover whether we truly fear God. Hence the Lord speaks through Moses to the people, saying: “The Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him with all your heart and with all your soul.”

This world, dearly beloved brothers, must be compared to a furnace, in which the fire of temptation and adversity burns continually, through which we must necessarily pass. In this furnace of tribulation the chaff is burned up, but gold and silver are cleansed from every stain. The chaff is turned into ashes; the gold is reserved for the ornament and beauty of the royal palace. The furnace, I say, is the world; the fire is temptation or tribulation; the gold is righteous men; the chaff is the unrighteous and sinners. The craftsman who purifies the gold is almighty God himself, who favors our progress and cooperates by grace toward the merit of virtue.

Therefore let us do what God wills; what he lays upon us, let us endure patiently for love of him, since, as holy Christophilus says, “The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which shall be revealed in us.” Hence Saint Jerome says: Who would not wish to be troubled, who would not desire to be spoken ill of, so that he may deserve to be praised by the voice of Christ and rewarded with a heavenly and abundant recompense? Would that, because of the name and justice of my Lord, the whole multitude of unbelievers might persecute and afflict me. Would that this whole world might rise up to my reproach, provided only that I may deserve to be praised by Christ and hasten to the reward of his promise.
