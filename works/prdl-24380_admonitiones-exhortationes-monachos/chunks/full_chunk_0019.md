Let certain overly superstitious and foolish cloisterers be ashamed, who value their rules and statutes more than the Gospel of Christ, who keep human traditions even to prisons and chains, but pay no heed to the ordinances of God and of the holy universal Church. To these the words of our Savior rightly apply, when he says to the Jews in Matthew: “Why do you also transgress the commandment of God because of your tradition?” For the teaching of the Gospel is to be preferred far above all the constitutions of the whole world. After the Gospel, the statutes of the universal Church hold the first place of authority, and no constitutions or rules of monks are comparable to them in dignity. Let us take care to live in evangelical purity, and we shall be perfect in the monastic way of life of the fathers. Let our feet always be shod for the preparation of the Gospel of peace, so that we may mortify in ourselves the desires of the flesh, and through love of God and neighbor may have peace with all. For without the peace and concord of brotherhood, the chastising of the flesh profits nothing, as Saint Jerome says: “Whoever afflicts the body but abandons concord does indeed praise God on the timbrel, but he does not praise him in the choir.” Hence Solomon, praising the peaceful man, says in Proverbs: “Better is a dry morsel with joy than a house full of victims with strife.” And again he says: “It is an honor for a man to separate himself from disputes, but all fools become entangled in insults.” Saint Christophilus, too, a lover of peace, admonishes us, saying: “Pursue peace with all, and holiness, without which no one shall see God.” Hence also the prophet Zechariah says: “Love peace and truth,” says the Lord Almighty. And our Savior says in the holy Gospel according to Mark: “Have salt in yourselves, and have peace among yourselves.” “There is no peace for the wicked,” says the Lord. But he is truly a soldier of Christ who has peace with God, with his neighbor, and within himself. A man is said to have peace with God when, moved by good will and turned toward him through a continual desire of love, despising transitory things, he directs the sharp gaze of his mind to heavenly things, and most diligently keeps all that the Lord commands. For when the Son of God was born in the flesh, the holy angels sang glory to the Lord, who dwells above all in the highest, and announced peace to men of good will. But wicked and unjust men cannot have this peace with God, because they have turned their wills away from the Lord. He has peace with his neighbor who does injury to no one, who, when injured, does not return vengeance, but, overcoming evil with good, never abandons brotherly charity. If anyone among his neighbors murmurs unjustly against such a man, the soldier of Christ has not for that reason abandoned peace, because perhaps another, without just cause, has begun an unjust murmuring. A just man, who has peace with God and desires to harm none of his neighbors, will rightly be called peaceable, even [unclear].
