Indeed, just as from knowledge of oneself the fear of God comes into a person, and from knowledge of God the love of God is born, so from ignorance of oneself comes pride, and from neglect of the knowledge of God come contempt and despair of salvation. Nothing is more wretched than an unlearned monk, who either through sloth omits the study of the holy Scriptures or despises it; for he can never remain pure within himself in true tranquility of heart, but is forced by his own restlessness, contrary to the integrity of monastic life, to occupy his mind with outward and useless things.

We see among you certain men ignorant of the Scriptures, unstable, difficult in mind, and wandering; and we blame their ignorance the more because they are the less inclined toward discipline. It is shameful not to know what you are commanded to know; more shameful not to learn; but most shameful of all to despise it. And there are among you some who do not know saving knowledge; there are also some who neglect to learn. What if I add that there are others too who utterly despise the knowledge of the divine Scriptures? Certainly I am not lying; you know that what I say is true.

For some of you have opposed my studies bitterly, even to the point of envy, quibbling that they wanted ploughmen rather than men of prayer as their prelate. Did they not also try to draw the visitors over to their opinion, desiring, by their urging, to bring me to a renunciation of the Scriptures, so that they might make me a tiller of fields and a keeper of pigs rather than of souls? But they strive in vain to hinder the divine oracle; under its impulse I have resolved, as long as I live, to set the study of the Scriptures before all the honors and riches of the world.

I am your abbot for as long as he who called me shall will it. If one of the two must be done, I would rather give up the abbacy than renounce the holy study of the Scriptures. For I truly know that nothing more wholesome can befall a cloistered man in this life than if he begins truly to love the divine Scriptures. Whoever among you wishes to follow this instruction of mine will have me alike as guide on the way and as companion. But whoever scorns to follow me as a learner will nevertheless be compelled to hear me as a teacher, even against his will.

I have said these things against those who, minding earthly and perishable things, cast away from themselves the fear of the Lord, who, though ignorant of almost everything, despise learning. I know the face of my flock, and I believe that I am not wholly ignorant of the inclinations of each of your hearts. Therefore I shall not cease to exhort you to the holy studies of the Scriptures, knowing that a lover of sacred reading cannot be a worker of fleshly vices. For the more zealous a monk has been in thoroughly learning the Scriptures, the less he is inflamed by the burning desires of fleshly pleasures.
