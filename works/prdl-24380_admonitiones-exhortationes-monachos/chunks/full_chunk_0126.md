Take up a firm purpose of complete amendment, and understand that the kingdom of heaven is promised not to those who begin well, but to those who persevere to the end. Know also that heaven is not reached by idleness and sluggishness, but by great labor, by the strongest effort of the will to make progress, and by tireless zeal in spiritual exercise.

Therefore, wishing to serve and please the Lord, always stand in the truth within yourself, fearful in awe, and prepare your soul for temptation. The disordered impulses and desires of the flesh must be strongly resisted; all the enticements and vanities of the world must be fought against with a most steadfast spirit; through prayers and greatness of soul the devil, the adversary of our salvation, must be driven away.

No sleepy, lazy, slothful person ever reached the kingdom of God; no one without continual labor and much sweat has obtained heaven. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “The kingdom of God suffers violence, and the violent,” that is, those who have learned steadfastly to resist the evil desires of the flesh, “seize it.” Review the deeds of all the saints, and nowhere will you find anyone sanctified without great and continual labor. For even if no one becomes holy by his own powers, nevertheless the gracious mercy of the almighty Lord, which grants to those who strive both the will to do good and the strength, sanctifies no one without the works of a good and upright will.

Since, then, God wills all men to be saved and no one to perish, we, my brothers, must take the greatest care not to receive his grace in vain, but rather strive with all our strength, so that, like fertile soil, we may always answer the free gifts of so great a sower with fitting fruits from the works of a good will.

Let us therefore arm ourselves with the shield of faith, and put on the breastplate of steadfastness and saving patience, so that, prepared against everything that can happen to us, through humility and the fervor of the Holy Spirit, with the help of our Lord Jesus Christ, we may overcome. In all these things patience is very necessary for us; without its support there never has been, nor could there ever be, perseverance in good and holy works. For there was no other way to heaven for the saints than patience; nor could anyone ever be sanctified who cast patience aside.

Indeed, all who have pleased almighty God from the beginning of the world down to this our age have triumphed over the prince of the world in no other way than through patience. Hence all of them could most rightly say that verse of the psalmist: “For your sake we are put to death all day long; we have been reckoned as sheep for slaughter.” The most holy apostle of Christ, Paul, also recalling the patience of the saints of old, says among other things: “They went about in sheepskins and goatskins, needy, distressed, afflicted, of whom the world was not worthy, wandering in deserts, in mountains, in caves, and in the caverns of the earth.” For all who wish to live piously in Christ suffer persecution, because, as the prophet says, “Many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these the Lord will deliver them.”
