On the Daily Struggle of Temptation. Homily X.

In yesterday’s sermon, dearly beloved brothers, I warned your Charity to resist the beginnings of evil thoughts, and to prepare yourselves with all your strength, by good and holy works, for the reward of the future recompense.

But since, as Scripture bears witness, the life of man upon earth is temptation, I have thought it necessary to speak in your hearing, as far as the Lord grants, about this very subject, so that, forewarned, you may be able to fight more strongly against the invisible adversary. You know the words of the wise man, who says in Ecclesiasticus: “My son, when you come to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear, and prepare your soul for temptation.” Let no one think himself free from temptation, brothers, as long as he walks in this mortal way of life; rather, the more each one has advanced in the fear of God, the more cautiously he should know that he must live. For the holy Pope Gregory says: “Our enemy, the more he sees that he is being resisted, the more he strives to attack. For he neglects to strike those whom he feels he possesses by undisturbed right.” The devil pursues the one who flees from him, and tries to overthrow the one who rebels against his rule.

O men, the more you advance in the spiritual life, the more earnestly you must keep watch against the adversary of your salvation. For, as Saint John Chrysostom says: “Our adversary never sleeps, but is always awake for the destruction of the human race; and none of us can resist him except the one who has learned to keep watch at all times in the fear of the Lord.” And Saint Leo, pope of the city of Rome, says in a certain sermon: “The ancient enemy does not cease to spread everywhere the snares of temptations, so that in whatever way he can he may darken the faith of believers by corrupting it. For he knows in whom to implant the enticements of the flesh, into whom to pour the strength of envy, before whom to set the incitements of lust. He knows whom to trouble with sorrow, whom to deceive with joy, whom to crush with fear, whom to seduce with admiration. He examines the conduct of all, he searches the affections of all. And there he seeks occasions for doing harm, wherever he sees anyone to be occupied with particular zeal.”

There are no works of virtue without the trial of temptations; no contest without an enemy; no victory without conflict.
