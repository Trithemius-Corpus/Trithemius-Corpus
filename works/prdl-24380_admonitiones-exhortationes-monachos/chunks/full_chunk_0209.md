On the recognition of spiritual grace.

If he has received the blessing, he understands, more than before and above others, what the merits of true charity are, and likewise the exercises of piety. The state of the mind is also set in the uprightness of heavenly desire, and is enlightened toward the true knowledge of future blessedness. It has this familiar indication as a sign of attained truth: because the mind, when God enlightens it, begins to understand the loftiest things in the Holy Scriptures and beyond them; and the more it understands, the more deeply it strives to humble itself. For the Holy Spirit joins humility to his gifts; and if humility has been cast away, it is already plainly evident that the Spirit has withdrawn.

The fifth sign of God’s grace is received if the whole mind of a man is resigned to God’s will, if daily it is kindled in love for him through desire, if the conscience, so far as human weakness permits, is preserved in all purity. For the love of God, through the received grace of the Holy Spirit, is at once born in the mind, and is strengthened daily by good works and spiritual exercises; it shudders at sin, detests it, and flees from it, and admits nothing except what is pure, holy, and deifying. Wherever this sign is present, there will be no sin. If sin is present, the sign was not true.

Let those receive the sixth sign of divine prompting who are always growing stronger in soul toward what is better. For the Spirit of God makes the mind of the man whom he inhabits by his grace patient and willing to endure injuries, and renders it most ready to do good. Whoever among you thinks that he is being moved by a divine impulse, whoever thinks that he is walking in the way of uprightness, let him examine his conscience, whether for the love of Christ he becomes daily more ready to endure injuries; whether, that is, he rejoices in tribulation, and whether the devotion of his soul grows more in adversities than in prosperity. But if he finds himself unwilling and impatient in enduring injuries, he easily recognizes how far he is placed from the grace of perfection. For charity, the gift of the Holy Spirit, is patient and kind.

Those receive the seventh indication of spiritual grace who for the love of Christ despise all the consolations of the present life. The soul that has God present within itself through love despises all comforts of the flesh, and longs to come to its Lord through hard and rough ways. It does not love the riches of this world; it rejects passing honors and dignities; it strongly despises the pleasures of the flesh. It meditates always on the law of the Lord, and never forgets the most bitter passion of Jesus Christ. Which of you, brothers, does these things? Who detests all the little consolations of this age? Who thinks of nothing, seeks nothing, desires nothing except the charity of God? Whoever has obtained this sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, if he perseveres to the end of life, will be saved.

We receive the eighth indication of a good course, and the true sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, when in all things we humbly conform our will to the divine ordinances. This degree of spiritual progress frees the monk from all cares.
