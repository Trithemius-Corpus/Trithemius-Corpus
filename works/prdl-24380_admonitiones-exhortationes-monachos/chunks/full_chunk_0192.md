To love God above all things, to exercise oneself continually in good works, to keep one’s conscience in all purity, and to do injury to no one provides the soul with true provision for the journey to its everlasting homeland in heaven.

Fourth, travelers are necessarily advised not to forget the purpose they have undertaken before they reach the end of the journey. For he must be called a foolish traveler who is called back from his road by children’s games and forgets where he had meant to go. And we, dearest brothers, who have taken up the way of monastic religion, must always keep our end in mind, because even when we do not notice it, death hastens daily and presents both the unprepared and the prepared alike for judgment.

Meanwhile let us do good, while we may, as much as we can, and let us not delay turning to the Lord with our whole heart. Christ is the way by which we go to the kingdom of God. Christ is the truth, in which, if we remain, we do not go astray. Christ is the life for which we hope. Hear, brothers, what he himself says in the holy Gospel: “If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.” This is the way upon which you entered from the beginning of your monastic profession. If you wish to proceed on it rightly and praiseworthily, you must live purely in regular observance and love Christ above all things. It is yours, brothers, to love nothing in this world and to chastise the flesh for the love of God. It belongs to monks to despise all the delights and pleasures of the present life and to desire nothing but the eternal banquet. It belongs to monks to imitate Christ with all their strength and to shape their life according to evangelical purity. To imitate Christ is the highest perfection. The faithful minister of Christ follows his Lord; and where Christ is, there also will his minister be. Therefore whoever among you loves his own salvation and desires to come after Christ to the kingdom of heaven, whoever wishes to guard and preserve to the end the things he promised on entering religion, let him also keep these things.

First of all, as far as he is able, let the monk learn through faith and devotion to know God in sincerity of mind, and at all times to occupy himself piously and soberly in the holy Scriptures. Whatever holy mother Church of the faithful has established either to be believed or to be observed, let him faithfully and firmly both believe and keep, and let him in no way depart from her determination. The flood once destroyed all who had remained outside the ark; Noah alone, with those who were in the ark, escaped safe. The ark presented the figure of the Church, and the deed that was done prefigured a mystery. Hence there is no salvation for men outside the Church, which our Lord Jesus Christ built upon the firmest rock. Every unbeliever is not in the Church, and therefore will necessarily perish, as once did those who were not in the ark. For all who do not believe in the Lord and Savior of the world, Jesus Christ, Jews, pagans, Gentiles, Saracens, likewise heretics and schismatics, who have withdrawn themselves from the unity of the Catholic Church, are placed outside the ark of salvation; and if they persevere in error, they are without doubt to be damned forever with the demons.

Since, then, faith is the foundation of salvation, without which it is impossible to please God, the monk must know what he ought to believe; and lest he happen to go astray, let him learn the Gospel and the constitutions of the Church that concern him. For he who is ignorant will be unknown. It is most shameful for a monk not to know the rules of the Christian faith, and to be utterly ignorant of what he is bound to believe for salvation. We do not say that the things proposed to you for belief should be examined by curious argument, since, as the holy Pope Gregory says, faith has no merit when human reason supplies proof for it. But we admonish you in this: that a monk ought to be studious in the divine Scriptures, so that he may know what holy mother Church commands him to believe, and may be able to guard against all errors contrary to the faith. Therefore let every monk, as a soldier of Jesus Christ, be firm and steadfast in the Catholic faith; let him be sober and studious in the Scriptures, and constant in good works.
