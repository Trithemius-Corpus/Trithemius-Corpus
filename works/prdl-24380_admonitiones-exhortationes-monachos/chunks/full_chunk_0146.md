But a monk zealous for his own salvation can easily extinguish the fire of lust, if, fleeing idleness and repletion, he is willing to occupy himself continually in spiritual exercise. For among all the vices, none attacks chastity more greatly or more dangerously than repletion and idleness. If a monk has overcome these two evils, he will easily guard chastity. Hence the most holy fathers of monks, wishing to cut away the causes of lust, established for us a sparing and scanty diet in fixed measure, so that the body may be sustained for necessity and not fattened for lust. They also ordained restriction of sleep as useful for monks, because immoderate rest inflames the flesh no less than gluttony. But they wished idleness, as the most destructive plague of the soul, to be far from cloistered men, most wisely dividing the time so that a monk would never be found idle.

Hear these things, brothers, and grasp them in your mind, you who carry a great treasure in a fragile little body. Flee idleness; always do some good thing, so that as often as the devil the tempter approaches, he may find you usefully occupied. For truly, he who is idle lies open to the snares of the tempter; but he who is occupied in good work has closed every entrance to snares. Therefore the mind must be occupied with continual exercise, so that it may never be found idle.

Blessed is that monk who occupies his mind at all times in good exercises, and never sleeps in the slumber of torpor and sloth. Blessed is the spiritual soldier of Christ who always keeps watch in the fear of the Lord, and immediately restrains from the beginning all the disordered movements of the flesh. For it is not enough to preserve true chastity unharmed if one plays, as though deliberating, with unclean thoughts. One must resist the first stirrings of wicked thoughts, since where delay gives rise to danger, one must immediately fight bravely. One must always keep watch against [unclear] of salvation.
