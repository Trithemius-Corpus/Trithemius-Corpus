Justice, as Scripture testifies, is everlasting and immortal; injustice, however, is the acquisition of death. But in what ways the Christian soldier ought to put on this breastplate of justice, our Savior himself declares in the Holy Gospel, saying: “All things whatsoever you wish that men should do to you, do you also likewise to them.”

Therefore, my brothers, keep this manner of living in the monastery: let each one do to his fellow-soldier in all things as, according to the right judgment of reason, he desires at all times that others should do to him. For this is the first duty of justice: that no one harm anyone; then, that he use common things without detriment to anyone else; and, as our further warfare requires, that he do for his brother, as for himself, what by just reason conduces to salvation.

O what great peace mortals would enjoy if they followed the exhortations of the Apostle, by which each is commanded to bear another’s burdens. “Everyone,” says Scripture, “who does justice is born of God.” This is the breastplate of justice, brothers: to keep God’s commandments, to love one’s neighbor, and to love God most fervently above all things. This is the strongest garment of our warfare: to show worship and devotion to God, holiness to ourselves, and brotherly charity to our neighbor.

We all serve Christ under the trophy of justice. If anyone is unjust, he shows that he belongs not to Christ’s army but rather to the fellowship of demons. For justice is the greatest virtue, which distributes to each what is his own; unless you guard it most diligently, you lose the name both of monk and of soldier. Therefore render worship and devotion to God; obedience, together with reverence, to your superior or ruler, the one belonging to action and the other to the heart. For it is not enough to obey superiors outwardly unless, from the inmost affection of the heart, we likewise think rightly of them.

This indeed, brothers, is the true justice of monks: by it each person lives without offense according to the prescribed rule of the law, giving to each his own proper dignity: reverence to his superior, concord to his equal, discipline to his inferior, worship and obedience to God, holiness to himself, patience to his enemy, mercy to the poor, and brotherly charity in the Lord to all.

Therefore holy Christophilus says: “We are debtors, brothers, not to the flesh, so as to live according to the flesh. For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” Therefore he rightly serves God as a soldier who distributes to each what is his own.

Next we are commanded to have our feet shod in preparation for the Gospel of peace, so that the good which we ourselves, by the Lord’s bounty, have come to know, we may also gladly share with others. For our law, brothers, according to which we are commanded to serve the Lord, is the Gospel of Jesus Christ, to which all the rules and constitutions of cloistered persons are ordered. For the Gospel was not made for the sake of monastic constitutions, but monastic statutes were made for the sake of the Gospel.
