Others, although they did not offer themselves of their own accord, nevertheless willingly received the martyrdom offered to them, always ready to endure for the faith of Christ whatever the cruelty of tyrants could have inflicted.

The most radiant Confessors rightly hold a rank nearer to these; even if the cruelty of the impious did not take them away by persecution, still they in no way lost the glory of martyrdom. For by crucifying their flesh in this world for the name of Christ, they endured a voluntary and long-lasting martyrdom: through abstinence and humility they were made glorious, steadfast in soul, always most patient, and most ready to undergo martyrdom bodily.

Take care, O monks, to be imitators of them according to your strength, so that, since enemies who kill the body for Christ are now lacking, you may show yourselves martyrs in mind and spirit by resisting the desires of the flesh and patiently enduring adversities for the love of God. In the peace of the saints, the best kind of martyrdom is to afflict the flesh for God’s name through abstinence, to desire nothing in this world, always to advance in good works, and, if any evil happens, to bear it in all things gladly and patiently with cheerfulness of mind.

Live henceforth according to this rule of patience, dearest brothers, if you desire to reign with Christ forever. Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ, so that each one may cheerfully give to his brother what he desires to be done for himself.

In this way all Christians owe martyrdom to Christ.

On the True Humility of Monks. Homily XIX.

It is the judgment of almost all the teachers, dearest brothers, that the highest and chief virtue of monks is no other than humility, because it is itself the mother and nurse of all virtues. For humility makes the monk; pride destroys and corrupts him. Hence the Lord says through Isaiah: “To whom shall I look, except to the poor little one, and the contrite in spirit, and the one who trembles at my words?”

For God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble, because pride is the beginning of every sin, and without true humility no one can attain salvation. Since this is so, I think it necessary, brothers, to exhort you without ceasing that, fleeing pride, you always preserve deep humility in all your works. Indeed, no good work is believed to please the Lord God if it is presented without the seasoning of humility.

For humility is a most healthful medicine for the souls of the faithful, helping all, because it presses down the swollen inward parts of the spirit, restores what is wasting away, corrects what is depraved, and removes what has grown excessive. O humility, mother and parent of all virtues: you strengthen patience, stir up compunction, illumine charity, adorn chastity, make prayer fruitful [unclear].
