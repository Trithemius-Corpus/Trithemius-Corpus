But let us return to him with our whole heart, I beg you, and do penance, because we have withdrawn far from the way of salvation, serving the flesh rather than the spirit.

Let no one deceive himself with empty hope; let no one wait for the final hour of this life, when he can no longer sin, as the time for penance, because neither the length of life is certain nor delay in repenting safe. He who does not repent while healthy is foolish; and he who does not amend his vices when he can, by the just judgment of God loses the power he had put off using. For penance is fruitful when it has been voluntary, by freedom of choice; but when the danger of death is already pressing, it seems to be necessary rather than undertaken by the penitent’s own choice.

Let no one say in his heart, my brothers, let no one think that the labor of repentance is hard and therefore to be put off until the very last particle of this life. That thought is exceedingly dangerous, since the certain limit of life, the one which divine providence has fixed, just as it has willed it to lie hidden from all, so it will prolong it for no one who deliberately defers repentance. If we wish to be safe and secure in conscience, let us do penance while we are healthy, while we are able to sin, while we are not yet pressed by any necessity of approaching death.

For it is a very doubtful matter to do penance when necessity already compels, and the pressure of death makes you no longer able to sin: that is, when it is not you who have left your sins, but they that have left you powerless over them. O how many, deceived by the hope of a longer life, go down to the pit of eternal damnation, who in health despised doing penance. For the longer you put off penance, the less disposed to penance you will always be found. For malice gains strength with time, and there is no sin so small that it does not grow when neglected.

Therefore embrace penance while you are healthy, and do not put it off from day to day, because you understand what you can do today, but you do not know what tomorrow will bring. For each new day brings its own troubles, and he who today is not disposed to penance will perhaps be less fit for it tomorrow. But let none of you say, “What have I sinned, that penance should seem necessary for me, when my whole life is nothing other than a certain hard and prolonged necessity of doing penance?”

You have heard, with Scripture as witness, that there is no man upon earth who does good and does not sin, since no one is clean before God. For even if you do not commit the great crimes of human impiety, such as murder, theft, adultery, fornications, and the like, nevertheless, because you have been placed in that vocation in which you ought with all your strength to raise every effort toward the state of perfection, sins which are small for mortals living in the world are certainly imputed to you as more serious.
