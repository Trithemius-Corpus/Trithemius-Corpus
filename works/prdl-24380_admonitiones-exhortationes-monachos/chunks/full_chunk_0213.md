On knowledge of spiritual grace.

He who despises the smallest things gradually falls away; and he who does not beware of many small things often, by habit, makes one great thing. The second caution necessary for the preservation of grace is said to be this: that all boasting be avoided. If you have merited to receive from the Lord the gift of compunction, strive, as far as lies in you, to hide it humbly. For the grace of the Holy Spirit flees publicity and detests every heart proud with boasting. For, as blessed Gregory says, he desires to be robbed who carries his treasure in public.

Third, if you do not wish to lose the grace of inward compunction, strive to love the quiet of solitude. For divine Scripture says: Upon whom shall my spirit rest, if not upon the humble and quiet? And again it says elsewhere: In silence and in hope shall your strength be. For the grace of inward compunction is quickly lost when the secrecy of solitude is not loved. Let the monk, therefore, learn gladly to remain with himself in his cell, if he wishes to possess the grace of God.

Fourth, one must take the greatest care not to despise in one’s heart any person, however evil; for the grace of compunction is withdrawn by the just judgment of God whenever one does not beware of rash judgment of one’s neighbor. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned.

Fifth, the monk who does not wish to lose the grace of inward devotion should strive to exercise himself in the practice of all the virtues. Let him always keep watch in the fear of the Lord, always occupy himself in the exercise of divine love; let him be constantly diligent in good works, and, as far as he is able, devoted at all times to the memory of the Lord’s Passion. For intent meditation on Christ’s Passion strengthens a person against all vices and preserves the grace of compunction.

Sixth, be constant in holy prayers, because he who interrupts the practice of prayer is not able to guard the grace of compunction. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: Watch and pray, that you may not enter into temptation. The help of the Lord is near to those who call upon him; but he who neglects to pray loses grace.

But if you have lost the grace of devotion in any way, you will quickly recover it by the following six remedies. First, consider that without the grace of God you cannot make progress toward salvation, but that in all the good things you are about to do you have the greatest need of his mercy. Recognize, therefore, that you are unworthy of the grace of God for doing good; but although you know yourself unworthy because of the multitude of your sins, nevertheless call upon him, because he is near; and see that all the time granted to you as free from the common exercises of the brothers is spent usefully and fruitfully. The grace of God is necessary for you in doing good; it is acquired by the breadth of charity, guarded by humility, and recovered by the practice of saving repentance. The Lord is with you as long as you are with him in good will. But in order that you may be able to recover the lost grace of devotion, you must submit yourself to God in true humility.
