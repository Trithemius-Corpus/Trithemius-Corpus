Who was higher than he in our whole congregation? Who went about filled with greater insolence? Because he seemed to have greater experience than we in temporal affairs, he therefore judged himself superior to all. The Almighty permitted him to fall, so that the man who, through self-regard, refused to acknowledge his own weakness might at last, confounded and not without the scandal of many, understand that God is the adversary of the proud. I grieve deeply, more than can be said, and the fault of the one who fell seems heavier to me because, when he falls, we are all confounded. May God grant him true repentance, so that those who had been scandalized by his fall may be built up by his satisfaction before God and men.

Pride has always been destructive to monks, and once it has invaded a man’s mind, it plunges him into the pit of every vice. Beware pride, my brothers, for it is the beginning of every sin; it is pride which, through the devil’s envy, brings death into the whole world. Pride is the death of all virtues, and the fountain and source of all vices. But humility, the nurse of the virtues, most befits monks, because it makes them gentle and meek. For every proud man is impatient, and every arrogant man is an enemy of meekness. He who is humble of heart has patience in adversity for the love of God; and he who remains calm in spirit under insults deserves a double crown. A patient man in adversity is near to God; but one who is proud and harsh gladdens his enemies. For Scripture says: “A patient man is better than a strong man, and he who rules his own spirit than a conqueror of cities.” The holy bishop Augustine also says: “A just and devout man must be ready to endure patiently the malice of those whom he wishes to become good, so that the number of the good may rather increase, and no one by equal malice add himself to the number of the wicked.”

Patience is greatly necessary for us, brothers, so that, doing the will of God, we may receive the holy promise in Christ. Each day adversities are present to us by which we may be exercised; therefore, just as a cause for endurance is never lacking, so, I pray, may necessary patience always be present. We are oppressed by want and poverty in necessary things; we are burdened by the commerce of the flesh’s weakness; besides this the world tempts us, to allure us, and the devil tempts us, to seduce us. Among so many adversaries of our salvation it is necessary, brothers, that we keep watch in all patience and humility, because in this pilgrimage, as long as we live, we are placed, so to speak, in the midst of evils. Our poverty ought to be enough for us, because it does not belong to monastic religion to be exalted in temporal things, but rather to be brought low, so that poor monks, imitating Christ, who was made poor for us, may deserve to glory with him forever in heaven. What if we see certain wicked men and sinners in the world abounding in riches? If good and just men are rich in this world, it is granted through [unclear] to their weakness, not to their strength.
