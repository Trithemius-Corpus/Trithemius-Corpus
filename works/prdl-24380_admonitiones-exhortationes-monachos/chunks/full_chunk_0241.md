Each man loves his own purse, and the state of the community falls. But if, according to the rule which you have professed, you were to live without private property, with the Lord providing, you would abound in all riches. But you, neglecting spiritual things, devote your efforts to the pleasure of the flesh; for that reason you have come to poverty in temporal goods.

I have said these things against those who suffer want of temporal things because of their contempt for spiritual things. But those who have transferred themselves from the order, if they had been poor, would have remained in it. For many expenses, outlays, and gifts are needed, which the poor could never have furnished. Therefore they lie who take poverty as the reason for their transfer. But as for their alleging wars, battles, and the devastations of enemies in defense of their dismemberment, no prudent man fails to know how frivolous, vain, and insufficient this is. For everything they recount can be no less hostile to canons than to monks.

And as for their calling the rule of abbots the cause, the blame is not undeservedly ascribed to those by whose election a bad ruler is placed over the monastery. Nor undeservedly: as the monks are, so is the abbot of the monks. For bad monks are judged unworthy of a good abbot. Finally, if the abbot is bad, how will the provost be good? Other remedies should have been sought by you from experienced men, by which you might have made progress in the order without complaint. But he who wishes to withdraw from a friend seeks an occasion; he who desires to dismember himself from the order invents countless causes. Now he says the monastery is burdened with debts; now he falsely claims the inconvenience of hostility. Now he invents a harsh lordship; now he blames the sloth of the prelates. He seeks everything, invents everything, assumes everything, so that he may either disparage the whole order or show that the place is unfit for regular observance.

“If such a monastery,” he says, “Most Blessed Father, is transferred into a secular church, divine worship will be greatly increased thereby; it will make princes always devoted to the Roman Church; it will provide peace for the peoples round about and the greatest honor for the Christian name.”

O deceitful tongue, O lying lip, O contriver of fraud, O inventor of deceit, O minister of iniquity, how false are the things you tell as true, how evil the things you tell as good, how sacrilegious the things you tell as holy. Kings and princes have been accustomed to build monasteries, not to destroy them; you ought not deceitfully to have alleged them as patrons of your treachery. For if they seek the praise of God, they never consent that monasteries be transferred from the holy order. But if they wish to consider their own advantages, they know that they receive more profit from monasteries than from collegiate churches.

Whence, then, comes to you such unheard-of presumption, that before the Vicar of Christ you falsely claim as pleasing to princes a thing which is known to be contrary to them? Thus you act, thus you deceive the Vicar of Christ, master of evil, contriver of fraud, inventor of falsehood, author of crime, craftsman of iniquity, you who live by deceit, who fortify yourself with impiety, who attack religion, who gape after profits, who are fed by pleasure, for whom everything rests on crime and nothing on the love of God.
