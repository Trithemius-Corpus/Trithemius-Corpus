Tribulations, adversities, and illnesses, together with poverty or want, have also made many humble, who perhaps in prosperity and good fortune would have perished through pride. And we trust, as we shall often say, that poverty contributes to our salvation, since we have in it a great cause for humility, while we find no matter at all for self-exaltation and pride. Others, through the continual practice of virtues and the remembrance of death, which they must shortly and inevitably undergo, have come to great humility.

Whom, however proud, would not earnest meditation on death humble? Or who is so fierce in spirit that he would not little by little lay aside his swelling pride, if he considered more carefully that he would die in a short time? Therefore the conscience of a monk must never drive away from itself the memory of future death, so that he may remain both humble and sorrowful: that, standing firm in humility, he may not grow proud, and that continual grief may forbid every wantonness to the heart.

Some also the gracious mercy of God forestalls, and by the indwelling of the Holy Spirit makes humble. Everyone who is led by the Spirit of God utterly detests pride. He who truly knows himself is most wise, and cannot depart from the meekness of humility. But every proud and arrogant man is ignorant, even if he seems great and renowned among mortals. For true wisdom rests in the dwelling-place of holy humility, and is shown more clearly through the proof of patience.

From the knowledge of God and of oneself humility is born, and this is rightly called true wisdom. He who has truly known himself now has nothing from which to grow proud, because, knowing his own vileness, he thinks nothing lofty, but, set in sorrow of soul, cries to the Lord: “What is man, that you are mindful of him, or the son of man, that you regard him? Man has become like vanity; his days pass away like a shadow.” Why then is earth and ashes proud? Why does the spirit of frail and mortal man swell up against God?

Be humbled, therefore, my dearest brothers, as it is written, under the mighty hand of God, so that he may exalt you in the time of visitation. For humility with chaste fear is highly pleasing to the Lord God in his servants, since without it he approves no virtue of man for salvation. Therefore, if we desire to climb the throne of the lofty kingdom of heaven, we must be very humble, because there is no other way to heaven except that of deepest humility, by which the Son of God himself and all the saints ascended. But all who have tried to enter the kingdom by another gate, striking the head of pride against the threshold of humility, have been wholly unable to enter. For the gate of humility does not admit the proud, and the narrow way of meekness does not receive lovers of avarice and self-exaltation. Therefore whoever desires to come to the height of the heavenly kingdom must ascend by the steps of humility; without them, whoever begins to ascend falls into the depth of perdition and death.
