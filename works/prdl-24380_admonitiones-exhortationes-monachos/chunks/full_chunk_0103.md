It is left to the judgment of each person’s discretion, that he should take only as much as necessity demands, not as much as the pleasure of appetite commands. This above all must be guarded against: that immoderate fullness of food not creep in, which defiles the soul when either disgust follows refreshment, or heavy acedia follows the mind. Vomiting that proceeds from fullness is for dogs rather than for monks. The Christian religion especially detests a man who is a glutton, since the delights of the servants of God are not food and drink, but piety and justice, because God is worshiped by purity of faith, and to each person what is his own is justly administered.

Therefore, whoever you are who desire to please God with your whole heart, whoever you are who hasten to arrive at the joys of eternal happiness, appoint for yourself a measure of fasting according to the measure of your strength, and apply the greatest care that, while fasting, you be constant in prayer, just in judgments, outstanding in brotherly love, patient under injuries, humble under rebukes, circumspect in speech, clean in conscience, fervent in the love of God, and in all your actions, with prudence accompanying you, filled with fear. Flee useless and idle words, and be persevering in good works at all times, having constancy in adversities, watchfulness in temptations, continence in refreshment, and simple purity in heart.

Let fasts be moderate and holy in all humility of mind, so that they weaken the body in such a way that they do not lift the mind into pride, lest the exercise of humility become material for vices. Fasting detests idleness, does not love wordiness, loves solitude, and applies itself to prayers and tears. Fasting, as holy Ambrose bears witness, is the death of guilt, the destruction of offenses, the remedy of salvation, the root of grace, the foundation of chastity. By this step the righteous Moses came to God; by this gift Elijah ascended into heaven in a chariot before he died.

Those who walk in the spirit love fasting; and those who profess chastity necessarily flee an abundance of delights. For hunger is a friend to virginity, an enemy to wantonness. Fullness, however, betrays chastity and nourishes wantonness. For fasting extinguishes the burning arrows of the devil, the urgency of prayer restrains them, and the continuance of vigils in the love of Christ repels all the snares of demons.

Hence holy Pope Leo says in a certain sermon: What can be more effective than fasting, by whose observance we draw near to God, and, resisting the devil, overcome a flattering life? For fasting has always been food for virtue, and abstinence the nourishment of holy chastity. From abstinence, indeed, chaste thoughts proceed; it begets reasonable desires, modest counsels too; and through voluntary afflictions the flesh dies to concupiscences, while the spirit is renewed in virtues.

Therefore, my brothers, let us love fasting with the love of virtue, for the sake of him who did not refuse to undergo death for us; and let us abstain not only from illicit things, but also, as far as possible, from permitted things, so that we who have offended in many ways against the commandments of salvation may, in compensation of satisfaction, henceforth keep ourselves from every pleasure.
