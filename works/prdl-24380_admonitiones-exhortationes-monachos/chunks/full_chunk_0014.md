Certainly nothing is happier than the good monk, to whom the Son of God promises himself as reward; yet nothing is more laborious, for, set in the arena of combat, he daily endangers his salvation. Nothing is stronger than a monk who conquers both the world and the devil together; yet nothing is weaker than one who is overcome by his own flesh.

Therefore, O monk, you must always keep watch against the hidden enemy of your salvation, who is never more easily conquered than when he is kept far from the entrance of the mind. So if you desire to come forth victorious against him, do not allow him to bring against you the devices of his own tricks and deceits. Guard your senses above all, lest death enter through the windows; and beware the enemy’s snares most of all when for a time he has allowed you to feel secure in your devotion.

Never forget the admonition of the wise man, who says: If you do not hold yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, your house, that is, your conscience, will soon be overthrown. Therefore Solomon said in Proverbs: Blessed is the man who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil. For as soon as a monk presumes upon security, he is already, without doubt, tending toward destruction. Therefore the soldier of Christ must always be prepared against his adversaries, so that, as long as he lives in this mortal body, he should not trust that he has perfectly overcome them. For the flesh always lusts against the spirit, and lest the soul ever be overcome, it is necessary to fight as long as life lasts.

Whenever you have conquered your invisible enemy, he rises up again and attacks you with greater savagery, so that, since he cannot obtain victory by strength, he may at least bring on despair by persistence. But you, most valiant soldier of Christ, beware lest you fail in the arena of this very brief combat; beware lest, before the battle is completed at the end, you lay down the arms needed for defense. For the Savior says: He who perseveres to the end will be saved. Give no rest to your soul as long as it has been granted you to remain in this miserable pilgrimage, but always do something good, lest the devil ever find you idle.

Always remember the admonition of Scripture, which says: Whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly, because neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge will exist in the lower world to which you are hastening. For there is no one who lives forever; rather, we all die, and like waters we flow down into the earth, and they will no more return to their former state. Then there will no longer be time to do good for the merit of eternal happiness, but whatever a man has sown here now in time, he will then receive as his reward. Therefore holy Scripture again says: In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening let not your hand cease.

And Saint Paul, admonishing Timothy, says: Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No one serving God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself. For even among those who contend in the arena, no one will be crowned unless he has competed lawfully. Everyone who contends in the arena abstains from all things. In another place also the same lover of Christ says: I want you to be without anxiety.

You therefore, soldier of Christ, who contend in the arena of daily combat so that at last you may appear glorious forever before your God, imitate the mortal contestant, who, competing in the arena for a perishable crown, abstains from all things lest he be defeated, so that, naked and free, he may run more swiftly. And so, if you desire to walk the way of God in true purity, if you long to conquer the enemy in the arena of combat, cast away from yourself all the garments of earthly cares, and put off all the hair of carnal pleasures. For whoever, with hair and garments cast away from himself, has learned to run naked, carries about nothing except the bare body by which he could be held by the adversary. For he who runs naked has stripped himself of all anxiety over the things clamoring around him, and thinks of nothing but victory, nor does he care what meanwhile is going on with his wife or his children. Nothing present calls him back from the race; the speed of his companions does not frighten him; among many he contends as if alone; he hastens breathlessly toward the prize as if certain of it.

Imitate him, soldier of Christ, in spiritual combat; contemplate the naked Savior hanging naked on the cross, so that you may be able to overthrow the adversary the more easily, the less he seems to have hold of you through worldly vanities. Therefore fight naked, light, and unencumbered on every side; naked and freed from all things, fly to heaven. Naked you came into this world; naked you will return to the earth. Diligently considering these things, my brothers, so run that you may obtain eternal life with all the saints. Amen.
