Homily XI.

your [unclear]. One who is fickle in mind and dissolute in conduct should be to you like a man who tears down hedges in a field. Do not be the companion of one who despises the rule and silence, who does not distinguish places or times, who without the prior’s blessing multiplies words everywhere. Avoid the monk who often exceeds the common canon of the monastery in wine and drinking, as fuel for lust and a spring of all vices.

A monk who relies on the counsel of his own head against the command of an elder is near to destruction and hastens breathlessly into danger for his soul. Do not share in the works of an obstinate monk, and do not have friendship with one who, in his own rashness, laughingly speaks against the deeds of his superiors. Be continually in the midst of disciplined men, and let your soul gladly walk with the humble. For he who is joined to good men shares in their virtue; and he who walks with the wicked will fall into the pit.

A monk of perverse will leads very many astray, and he who despises obedience will never be exalted. He who raises his voice when speaking and cries out with loud shouting cannot hide the folly of his heart, and he will glory in his vanity. The friendship of good men is the firm salvation of the soul, and in the company of the wicked the innocence of the simple is corrupted. The company of those is best whose fear of God has confirmed examples of good works. Nothing is more precious than a friend who fears God, and nothing more dangerous than a companion who is of perverse will.

A brother who fears God with his whole heart will be like a wall of strength for his friend; and he who is joined to such a man in soundness of mind is sanctified at all times by the blessing of the Spirit of God. He who walks in the midst of those who fear God is upheld, lest he fail, as though by a very strong wall round about. From his words the servant of the Most High is known, and works of holiness reveal a good monk. No foul, buffoonish, or idle word comes from the mouth of one who fears the Lord; and he who is a true lover of Jesus does nothing that offends goodness.

In the mouth of a monk who loves God, Christ always resounds; and he who is a true worshiper of God shows the speech of righteousness both in his mouth and in his work. He who fears God is the best counselor; and he who walks according to the canon is a prudent judge of thoughts. An elder who fears God, and who has been tested and proved for a long time in regular observance, is a guide of the young; and he who has no stain on his garment should be a good instructor of novices.

Receive gladly all the words of the mouth of one who is an understanding teacher of divine things and an interpreter of the Scriptures, keeping his soul in humility, as though they were golden, because they have the sinews and solidity of future eternal happiness. He who is truly learned has compassion on the weak, and he who loves Jesus exhorts the wavering in mind to strength. He who walks with a talkative man will not escape contention; and he who delights in falsehood will never be a friend of wisdom.
