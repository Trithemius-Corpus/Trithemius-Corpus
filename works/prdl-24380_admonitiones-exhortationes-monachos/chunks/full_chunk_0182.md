For the profession of monks, under sentence of eternal damnation, commands them to live without property, under obedience to their shepherd, and in perpetual chastity. If one of these three is taken away, the integrity of monastic life has been violated, and transgression condemns the one who despises it.

In the ninth place, therefore, we admonish every person dedicated to God always to beware of his own will as of a venomous serpent, since unless he keeps it suspect at all times, he easily falls into the pit of damnation. Consider the Rule, which permits monks power neither over their body nor over their own will. No one sins unwillingly. If you have perfectly submitted your will in all things to God and to your abbot, you will easily avoid sin.

Tenth, what can preserve in you, my dearest brothers, the integrity of regular observance is that in all adversities and oppositions you have the most steadfast patience for the love of God. For patience is a great virtue, which by another name we call tolerance; without it no one will emerge victorious over his temptations. Therefore the Lord said in the holy Gospel: “In your patience you shall possess your souls.” What is patience, if not to bear calmly, for reverence and love of God, both one’s own injuries and those of one’s people, and all other adversities whatever? Believe me, brothers, I speak the apostle’s sentence: patience is very necessary for you, so that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise. Let the Lord Jesus be for you the living example of patience; follow him with all your strength.

Eleventh, my brothers, beware lest your hearts be weighed down with crapula, because a monk ought not eat and drink to satiety, but through love of abstinence always to stop with a certain appetite and hunger. For the wise man says: “He who loves feasts will be in want, and he who loves wine and rich foods will not become rich.” Love fasting; cherish abstinence; preserve continence and frugality in all things. These are the arms of cloistered men; these are the standards of the spiritual warfare of monks, without which the flesh is not conquered, nor are demons overcome. Abundance of foods is accompanied by useless talk; and a belly filled with wine stirs up unclean suggestions of flesh and lust. If you wish to be chaste, be abstemious. It is most disgraceful for a monk to serve his belly and his gullet, when for the love of God he has renounced all the pleasures of the world together.

The twelfth thing that makes a monk useful and vigorous in regular observance is that he be most diligent in the service of the divine. For truly, he who is a true servant of God, as all monks ought to be, is always fervent and diligent in the work of his Creator; and because he truly knows that God is present everywhere, he prepares himself with complete devotion for obedient service to him.
