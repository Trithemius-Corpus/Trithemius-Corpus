Obedience is the voluntary mortification, alive in the mind, of the members; and the merit of its dignity is so great that a man has nothing greater to offer in honor of God. Let each person offer for the love of God what he can: beyond doubt, he has offered more than all who has slain his own will for God’s sake through obedience. It is indeed a great good to distribute one’s possessions for the love of Christ to the use of the poor, and to relieve the want of the needy with alms. But as much as the whole outweighs the part, as much as the free choice of the will is nobler than necessity, as much as heaven is distant from earth, so much more has he offered who, for the love of God, has of his own free will denied himself forever.

For whatever temporal substance you have offered to God is not yours, but another’s, and is of less value, both in estimation and in reward, than what is your own. You have one thing of your own which, when offered, pleases God above all things. What is this but free will, which the almighty Creator has placed in your power? And a good will is greater than all riches: nothing richer is offered to God, nothing more acceptable is consecrated to him.

This most precious gift, my brothers, you once offered to God when, denying yourselves and all worldly things for his honor, you put on the monastic habit. Remember, I beg you, the words of your holy profession, and let that solemn promise never depart from memory, which each one of you made in the sight of God before us, saying: “I, brother John, promise stability, conversion of my ways, and obedience according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, before God and his saints, in this monastery, which has been built in honor of blessed Martin, in the presence of Dom John, abbot.”

Behold the bond by which we have voluntarily bound ourselves to obedience; behold the service we have professed. For these are the vows which our lips uttered; these are the sacrifices of devout dedication which we offered to God. Certainly, my brothers, great are the things we have promised, and the necessity laid upon us to keep what we have professed is greater still. For unless we faithfully observe everything that the rule of our holy father Benedict commands us, the almighty justice of God will judge us severely as perjurers and wicked transgressors.

For it is very dangerous not to render to God what has been promised, and through forgetfulness of the holy institution to violate one’s purpose. Therefore, having been charged with the office of pastoral care, I am accustomed daily to admonish you to the observance of regular discipline; nor henceforth will the necessary word of salvation ever be lacking to you, so long as my spirit shall remain in this prison-house of the flesh. But you, I beg you, take care that you are not fruitless hearers, since the matter concerns the soul, which once lost is never recovered.
