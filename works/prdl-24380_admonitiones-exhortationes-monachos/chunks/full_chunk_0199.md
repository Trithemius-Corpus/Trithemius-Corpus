How sin is to be fled.

The flame burns those whom carnal pleasure now defiles; and each person is burned all the more in torments, the more he takes delight here in the flesh against God. In hell, as Gregory bears witness, there will be intolerable cold, inextinguishable fire, the immortal worm of conscience, unbearable stench, palpable darkness, immeasurable pains, scourges of those who strike without intermission, the dreadful sight of demons, confusion over crimes committed, and despair of all good things. But of all the punishments in hell, by far the gravest must be judged to be this: that those damned ones are forever deprived of the vision of eternal light.

Hence the most blessed bishop John Chrysostom says: “Gehenna is an intolerable thing, and all men shrink from it. For who does not know that the punishment of hell is very dreadful? Yet if someone should set down a thousand, indeed ten thousand gehennas, all that he says is nothing in comparison with this: for a soul hated by Christ to be driven away from the glory of the saints, and to hear that terrible voice from the mouth of the Judge of all: ‘Depart from me, you workers of iniquity, for I do not know you’; and: ‘Go, you cursed, into the eternal fire which has been prepared for you and for the devil.’”

O voice by far the most terrible of all terrible things; O sentence to be feared, and exceedingly dreadful to all mortals: to be hated by Christ for one’s deserts, to be reproached by the Judge, to be separated from the fellowship of glory, and to retain no further hope of recovery. Think now, brothers, on the punishments of the wicked that most certainly will come; and compare all the torments of this passing age, and every imaginable torture of this world, with those everlasting punishments of the damned, and they will be as nothing compared with something else, imagination compared with truth, a dream compared with waking. You will easily overcome the temptations of the flesh and the law of sin if you continually keep the thought of these things in memory.

O eternity, what an inconceivable span of time; an age without end, a world without time. Blessed is he who now foresees in time how he may fare well in eternity after this very brief life.

The fourth thing that can draw a person back from sin is to keep continual remembrance in the heart of future and everlasting happiness. Truly, the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which will be revealed in the saints, where there will be no discord, no disharmony at all, but the highest peace of all and the everlasting concord of the rejoicing saints. In the heavenly blessedness of the saints there is true joy without sadness, peace without discord, goodness without malice, gladness without end, light without darkness, everlasting life without pain. There is sweetness without bitterness, rest without labor, praise without blame, love without fault, brightness without cloud, satiety without disgust. Eternity remains without weariness; goodness abounds without defect; happiness continues without misery. For where God is all in all, perfect and full joy will be present to everyone.
