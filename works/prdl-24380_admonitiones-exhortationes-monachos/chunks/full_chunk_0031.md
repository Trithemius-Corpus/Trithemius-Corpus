Therefore whoever already experiences the love of God within himself, whoever has received heavenly charity in his heart, must take the greatest care not to fall back into the negligences of his former life, but must apply the necessary fuel of spiritual exercises, so that he may be able to preserve the advancing fervor of God within himself. Let him beware not only of great sins, but also of small negligences, and, as far as he can, the very least; for whoever despises small things gradually falls, and whoever does not always fear danger will quickly feel the loss of spiritual fervor and of his purpose. For Scripture says: If you do not hold yourself constantly in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown.

Blessed, therefore, is the man who is always fearful, who does not presume upon the virtues he has received, who firmly judges that he is failing whenever he has not found himself advancing at every moment toward higher things. But I fear, brothers, and I am deeply afraid, unless my estimate of certain works deceives me, that in some of you the fire of this holy fervor is not yet present, since you seem to love the true pursuits of monks only a little. Since this is plain from their way of life, none of you can be ignorant of it. I have not thought it altogether superfluous to admonish them, although so far I seem to have made little progress, since the hand of the Lord has not been shortened so that he cannot save the penitent. For I trust in the mercy of the living God that, because of my charity toward them and the exercises of devotion of those among you who walk according to the purity of the rule, he will at some time grant them to come to their senses and to love the pursuits of holy fervor more ardently.

What is this, brothers, that you do not live monastically in the monastery, and, having professed a way of life of the spirit, you walk according to the flesh? Do you not know how dangerous it is for a monk to live secularly in a community, and to violate by a lukewarm way of life the vow of holy purity which we have all made? Know, I beg you, that the last things of all cloistered persons are close to the extremes: so that good monks, zealous lovers of their own salvation, receive in heaven not ordinary rewards, but the greatest rewards of the saints; and those who conduct themselves badly or lukewarmly in monastic religion are punished not with a moderate or common torment, but with the gravest torment, that of demons. For the higher the state in which someone sins, the heavier also will be the punishment of the offender in it.

Therefore, if anyone among you has already learned to walk the way of divine love with fervor of spirit, he must go forward, so that he may make greater progress every day. But whoever still lies in the torpor of a sluggish mind should strive as quickly as possible to rise again; for just as punishment is promised to those who lie fallen, so the crown of victory is denied to those who do not persevere. Beware, therefore, lest destructive lukewarmness overthrow the mind.
