The Fifth Book of Johannes Trithemius.

...that you should preserve them in religion, teach them, instruct them, amend them, and correct those who did wrong. But you hated discipline and cast my words behind your back: if you saw a thief, you ran with him, and with adulterers you placed your portion. Intent on the pleasures of the flesh, you did not guard my sheep.

Therefore render what you owe. After monks have come from the world to religious life, they have become subject to my right, and it is not lawful for them afterward to return to the world. But you were appointed servant of my sheep, not their lord, and it was not lawful for you in any way you wished to alienate, usurp, or tear away anything belonging to my right. You were guardian of my possessions, not their owner; you ought faithfully to have preserved what had been acquired and sought out what had been scattered. But you, forgetful of me, squandered my inheritance, scattered the sheep, indeed made beasts out of sheep, enemies out of servants, and faithless men out of allies.

Now therefore render what you owe. I entrusted monks to you, whom you ought to have preserved in their holy purpose: to raise up those who had fallen, heal the sick, restore the failing, kindle the sluggish with charity, shake the negligent awake, rebuke sinners, correct the disobedient, restrain the dissolute, and keep the perverse from their evil pursuits. But you cared for none of the things that belonged to your office. Given over to greed, lust, and gluttony, you not only permitted monks to return to the world, but, utterly forgetful of pastoral care, became the leader and partner in their apostasy.

And so now render what you owe; restore what I entrusted to your faith, or rather, not to say, to your faithlessness. But because you will not be able to render it, you will be cast into a dark prison, covered with the gloom of death, never to come out from there until you render the debt, which you will never render. There you will receive punishment not only for your own deeds, but also for the sins of all those whom, though committed to your rule, you neglected to correct. For thus Saint Benedict says in the Rule: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd falls upon him if the father of the household can find any lack of profit in his sheep.”

Therefore, O abbot, whoever you are, if you are wise, if you understand, if at last you believe in the judgment to come, if you desire to escape the torments of hell, do not yield to the wicked desires of monks; do not consent to criminal dismemberments. What does the briefest pleasure in this life profit you, for which you would be forced to receive eternal punishments? Do not, I beg you, inflict so great an injury on Saint Benedict; do not take away from him monasteries which neither you nor all your parents built. Let it suffice you that you are abbot, because you live from his patrimony. Do not desert the order; do not consent to diabolical suggestions; do not make your monks secular; do not resign the monastery entrusted to you into the hands of strangers.

Recognize that you are a man subject to death, of brief life upon the earth. Do not do so ruinous a thing; do not commit this crime; do not perpetrate so inestimable a sacrilege. Remain in the holy order, and do not desert your purpose if you desire to arrive at eternal life. For the Lord says: “No one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back...”
