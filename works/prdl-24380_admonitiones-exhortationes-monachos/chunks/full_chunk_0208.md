For as soon as the Holy Spirit has entered a man’s mind, he either lessens every blaze of vices or extinguishes it entirely. Therefore let each one of you diligently examine himself and consider by whose prompting he is moved. For the prompting of God is accustomed to lessen sin, but that of the ancient enemy to increase it. Hence, in those who are led by the Spirit of God, sin is daily diminished; but those who follow the prompting of the devil are rarely truly amended.

The second sign of the divine prompting is recognized from the evidence of virtues, because the grace of the Holy Spirit always works great virtues in a man. Therefore, when you feel yourself advancing daily in the love of God and in good works; when you experience in yourself that the habit of sinning and all vices are prevailing less from day to day, you ought humbly to acknowledge that the grace of God is present. For the love of God, which grace brings, is never idle, but is always usefully occupied in good works, because it both covers the multitude of sins and, by exercising the soul in virtues, does not permit it to sin. For the grace of God cannot stand together with sins, and it is not accustomed to be in a man without the operation of virtues.

The third sign of the Spirit of God is shown in a man when the mind does not waver in its purpose of holiness, but, growing stronger from day to day, is more firmly strengthened. For there are many who, touched by a momentary compunction, sometimes receive the first two signs, but, despising perseverance, do not reach the third. Without perseverance, however, there is no reward for a good work, as the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “He who perseveres to the end will be saved.”

The devil knows how to feign absence for a short time, and to dissemble toward one advancing according to the two aforesaid signs of the Spirit of God, as though he did not know him or feared him. Then, bursting forth from his ambushes, he attacks the unwary devout man, and, casting the wretched man down into the former pit of negligence, wounds him more severely. O evil habits, what a heavy yoke, what unjust and dreadful dominion, and rarely to be ended before death. Let anyone whom a long habit of sin binds fear and tremble. For nothing is more difficult than to conquer an evil habit strengthened over a long time. Hence Solomon says in Proverbs: “A young man according to his way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” Nevertheless, if we do not violently conquer an evil habit, we shall always find it harder and less conquerable.

The fourth sign of the presence of the Spirit of God is rightly added to perseverance in good, because, when the affection has been purified, the mind is enlightened by grace into the knowledge of the divine light. For this is the anointing of the Holy Spirit, which no one understands except the one who receives it; this is the fullness of heavenly doctrine, which excludes both vices and errors from minds.
