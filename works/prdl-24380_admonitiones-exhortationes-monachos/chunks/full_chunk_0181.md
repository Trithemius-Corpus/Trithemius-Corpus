The fourth thing which we have judged necessary for you for regular observance is that you keep watch in guarding the outward senses, because no one can adequately guard the city of his heart who neglects to close its gates. Love silence, and flee every tumult of this age. Apply holy prudence in all that you do, and love the secrecy of quiet. For the monk who loves public and wandering excursions has never been a true lover of observance.

Fifth, we admonish you to flee destructive idleness of the soul, since this pestilential poison nourishes the gravest vices and always gives birth to impure thoughts. Therefore whoever is a lover of virtues and loves his own salvation, whoever wishes to arrive at eternal happiness, should flee idleness as from the face of the devil, if he desires to avoid sin.

Sixth, for the purity of regular observance it is required by the necessity of salvation that the renunciation of ownership be kept inviolate by all until death. For a monk who has private property prepares for himself a road to hell. But the one who receives money and spends it in the monastery should be a public minister; no private person dares presume to do this. The possession of riches does not befit monks, who are commanded to imitate the Lord Jesus in poverty of spirit. Let us hear the holy Apostle of Christ saying: Having food and clothing, let us be content with these. By God’s mercy we provide you with necessities, not as much as the desire of disordered pleasure demands, but as much as nature, content with little, requires. And so, just as it is not lawful for you to have anything without the consent of the abbot, so, since we supply everything, it is not necessary either.

The seventh thing that makes a monk is humble obedience to superiors. For he cannot be a true monk who despises obedience and denies to his superiors the humble subjection that he promised. This disease of disobedience arises from arrogance and pride of mind, just as pus is produced from an evil ulcer. But what does Scripture say? Pride is hateful before God and men, and is the beginning of every transgression. The warfare of monks is the imitation of Christ, who obeyed the Father even unto death.
