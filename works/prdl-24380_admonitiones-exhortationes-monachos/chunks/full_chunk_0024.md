For if, according to the Apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, then he who does not know the Scriptures does not know God’s power and wisdom; and how will he know Christ? For ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ; and he who does not know Christ will not belong to his fellowship, as Saint Christophilus says: if anyone is ignorant, surely he too will be ignored.

Moreover, the more constant anyone has been in the study of the divine Scriptures, the greater understanding and sweetness he gains from reading them. But it especially befits you, brothers, to turn over the divine Scriptures daily by reading and learning them, since you are granted both abundant time, free from occupations, and a place sufficiently suitable because few people come there. Therefore I exhort and admonish you, if you love your salvation, always to have divine readings in your hands, so that all the time allowed you without labor after the service of prayer may be spent on the study of sacred Scripture. Let no day pass, my brothers, on which a monk fighting for Christ does not read something from the sacred books that may make him better in his holy purpose.

Rather, let him follow the counsel of the divine Jerome, who says: Let the divine Scriptures always be in your hands, and let them be continually turned over in your mind; and do not think it enough for you to store God’s commandments in memory, unless you also strive to fulfill them in deeds. For God requires two things of us, my brothers. First, he wants us to know his most holy will; second, that once it is known we should do it. But we cannot know his commandments if we are ignorant of the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Nor does he do God’s will who is ignorant of it, nor does he know it who has not learned it. Learn, then, what God’s pleasing will is, so that you may do it; do it once known, so that you may understand it more fully. For no one understands God’s will better than he who, once he has known it, does it out of love.

And so, if you desire to become a most learned interpreter of the divine Scriptures, learn to begin your pursuit of knowledge not from words but from holy works. For then you will grasp the rich knowledge of sacred reading, if you keep your heart clean from every sin and, fervent in the love of God, continually turn over holy Scripture with compunction. For the knowledge of the divine Scriptures is a high mountain, on which Moses speaks with the Lord; and a man polluted with the filth of vices is forbidden to climb it. Thus we read in Exodus: Take care not to go up the mountain, nor touch its borders. Everyone who touches the mountain shall surely die. Whether it is a beast or a man, it shall not live.

Indeed, a man touches the mountain and dies whenever someone, swollen with secular learning, takes offense at the simplicity of the divine Scriptures. But a beast touches the mountain and is overwhelmed with stones whenever a man, plunged in the fury of carnal passions, advances to the study of sacred Scripture without a purified mind.
