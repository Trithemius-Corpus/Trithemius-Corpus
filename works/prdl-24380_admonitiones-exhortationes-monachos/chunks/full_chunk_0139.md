For monks’ judgments are often blind whenever an opinion is sought about prelates; and those who by their conduct corrupt the integrity of the order rarely judge truly and rightly about pastors. Because of the ingratitude of subjects, rulers are often changed for worse ones; and monks who despise a good and useful pastor while he is present in this life have received a foolish and useless successor by the just judgment of God.

Those who are subject in simplicity walk rightly; and those who truly imitate Christ do not pass judgment on the one who presides. For we have one whom we may follow, the shepherd of faithful souls, our Lord Jesus Christ; looking faithfully to him in mind, we shall not rashly judge the visible shepherd. “The scribes and Pharisees,” he says, “have sat on the chair of Moses: do what they tell you, but do not imitate what they do.” Hear these things, O monk, and you who are a zealous lover of your own salvation, consider the pattern of obedience that you should follow.

The abbot bears Christ’s vicarship in the monastery; whether he is good or bad, he deserves the obedience of all his subjects not by his own merit, but in the name of Christ. For we understand the saying, “He who hears you hears me; and he who despises you despises me,” not of the apostles alone, but of any prelate in the Church of God who has been appointed guardian and ruler of souls. Therefore, if you have an abbot who is good, suitable, and holy, hear his voice as the command of one who stands in God’s place, and whatever he commands you, be obedient in all things. Let his teaching be for you the rule of living, and his holy conduct an example of purer formation. For he is a friend of God and the vicar of Jesus Christ: by the one he deserves that you love him; by the other, that you obey him in all things when he commands.

But if you have obtained a pastor who is crude, inexperienced, or certainly less suitable, beware, I beg you, of wishing rashly to examine his actions; rather think that your sins deserve to have a useless pastor. Consider him not as what he is, but as what he is called; and honor the pastor for love and reverence of him whose vicarship marks him. Remember at all times that the obedience you promised to God must be shown purely and simply to his vicar, since, if the abbot is evil in his conduct, he nevertheless does not lose the vicarship of Christ unless he is lawfully deposed. Nor does the reprobate life of your pastor release you from the obedience promised in your religious profession.

The sentence of our customary proverb is to be feared: “Reprobate monks do not deserve to have a good abbot.” Do not suppose, dearly beloved brothers, that this example has been hurled at you; for I do not trust that I am a bad pastor, though I cannot deny that I am crude and inexperienced. Nor have I ever admitted any suspicion of evil about you, since we seek nothing more than to nourish and enlarge fraternal charity in all things.
