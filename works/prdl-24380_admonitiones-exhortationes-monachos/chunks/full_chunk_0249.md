Let it be put into practice: look at the other orders, and from their diligence you will recognize what you must do. They appoint procurators from among their own men in the city of Rome, who vigorously resist the beginnings of evils and allow nothing to gain strength against their order. And you, Fathers, unless you do the same, will suffer serious losses in the future. Therefore, if it can be done, if it is pleasing, let someone be chosen from the order: a zealous, devout, learned, and experienced man, who will fight no less by influence than by zeal, who will not prefer his own interests to the common good, who will not flee labors, who will attempt nothing rashly beyond the commission to be entrusted to him, and who, once appointed procurator of the order in the city, will always keep careful watch on its behalf. I believe that, if such a man were available, he would forestall many dangers to the order.

For it is the greatest foresight if the procurator in the city can denounce before the Pope the false and fictitious allegations which perverse monks falsely present as true for the sake of their own dismemberment. And this is not a work of great difficulty, for all the causes they allege are full of fear and falsehood. But the Vicar of Christ, who grants transfers of monks more because of importunity than out of favor, when he has seen their allegations refuted by the diligence of our procurator, will rejoice that he has found an occasion for denying what he was unwillingly granting. For he too fears the judgment of his Lord. Therefore, if we were watchful, we would have him as defender and advocate of our order. Our negligence alone is accused. Our stinginess alone is condemned.

Against those who uselessly consume the goods of monasteries and disdain to assist the order even in any measure. Chapter VIII.

But now I am pleased to turn the force of my words against those who, amid such great necessities, do not deign to assist the holy order even in any measure. These are worldly abbots, useless men, given over to pleasures, who, to use the words of Mantuan, neglecting the worship of those above and despising the command of the Thunderer, indulge Bacchus and serve Venus. They carry sacred things with gold, build altars with coins; with gold they pour libations of wine, with gold their paneled ceilings gleam. They cover whores with jewels, set banquets before dogs; adorned with the spoils of the poor and enriched by plunder, puffed up by success, they laugh at heaven and Acheron alike.

They have no care at all for the salvation of souls, no love of God, no reverence for the saints, no practice of the virtues. These are the abbots rebellious against your chapter, who neglect the statutes, spurn the sanctions, and despise the Rule. From the rejected they have been raised to greatness, but they impiously despise the order that honored them. Saint Benedict exalted you, ungrateful abbot; he made you rich, received you as a monk, and appointed you shepherd of his brothers. Why, then, do you not acknowledge the generosity of your lawgiver? Why do you not repay his benefits in return?
