That monks must live cautiously.

For perseverance alone in good works deserves to receive the crown of praise; without it, it profits nothing to have begun well. Virtue has no merit where perseverance has not given increase. The kingdom of eternal blessedness is promised not to those beginning good, but to those persevering in good until death; for not in the morning, not at midday, but when evening had come, the denarius will be paid to those laboring in the vineyard. For it is not the one who began well who is to be praised, but the one who continued well to the end who is to be honored.

Therefore, my brothers, I am accustomed earnestly to admonish you, and I must never cease, that you attentively consider your calling and walk cautiously in all religion and holiness, as befits sworn soldiers of Christ. For now is the time for doing good, because when the hour of death arrives, it will utterly cut off every ability to work. And so, as long as you live sound and unharmed, do good; always think of the future recompense, and daily grow in spiritual progress. Seek the Lord while he can be found. Let the life of Christ the Savior be your imitation; by the very foundation of your institution you are bound to follow him according to your strength, not by the steps of your feet, but by likeness of affections.

Walk cautiously in all things; always place the fear of God in true humility before the sight of your mind. For you need great purity, you who have consented in mind and body to the holy warfare of Christ. It now belongs to you, brothers, for God’s sake to despise the world and all things that are in the world with perpetual courage, to overcome every movement of carnal pleasures, to flee sin, and to occupy yourselves continually in good and holy works. Your life also, as far as human weakness allows, must imitate angelic purity and flee and detest every perversity. The holy angels are never idle, but are always intent upon the praises of God without weariness or tedium. The holy angels love God above all things, and do not withdraw from his charity even for a moment.

In imitation of them, the monk must be constant in the service of Christ and always occupied in good works according to the Gospel and the rule. The monk must have a most fervent charity toward God, and a true, ordered, and sincere love of neighbor. For fervent love for God perfects the monk and does not allow him to be idle. For the most holy Pope Gregory says: “The love of God is never idle.” For it does great things if it exists; but if it refuses to work, it was never true love of God. For if the foolish poet spoke truly concerning carnal love, “Let the man who does not wish to become idle love,” how much more powerfully does the most chaste love of God drive the mind it possesses toward difficult things.

Love God, brothers, with your whole heart, as far as the strength of desire can extend itself, and you will be able most easily to observe all the things that have been prescribed for you. For nothing is difficult to one who loves; rather, fervent love makes all hard things, all heavy things, and all difficult things very easy.
