Indeed, whoever already thinks himself to be something is still farther from true humility, since true humility does great things and thinks the least of itself; and the more it has advanced, the more deeply it recognizes its own weakness. For so Scripture has instructed us, saying: “The greater you are, humble yourself in all things, and you will find grace before God.” And again it says: “Humble your spirit greatly, for the punishment of the flesh of the ungodly is fire and the worm.”

Therefore, my brothers, take up true humility: not that which is sometimes displayed outwardly in gesture and voice before men by pretenders, but Christian humility, which is possessed by the pure affection of the heart. For it is one thing to have the virtue of humility, and another to have the appearance of its likeness. True humility is always patient with a tranquil heart toward every injury and insult; it is obedient without dispute to commands, not only of superiors but even of equals; in adversities it preserves constancy, in bodily necessity moderation, in conduct a good conscience, in common life honorableness. It is cheerful, not dissolute; serious, not melancholy; generous in acts of mercy, not wasteful; compassionate toward the weak, not lax.

Christian humility is unceasingly pierced with compunction in the love of God; fervent in the spirit of devotion, it is continually watered with tears; it always longs to see its prince, Jesus Christ, and, following his admonitions in spirit, sighs from grief. For whoever embraces true humility grieves that he is a sinner; whoever imitates the Son of God groans that he cannot advance as much as he desires.

Nothing is more excellent than Christian humility, nothing more splendid than self-knowledge, which, though always set in a higher place, is never exalted, and, though subject to all, rules over all. For he who is small in his own eyes becomes great before God; and he who thinks himself to be something great in this world is judged before God to be of no account. For each person will be the more precious before the inward judge, the more, while doing good and never failing, he has been contemptible in his own estimation.

Humility is the stable foundation of all virtues, since every spiritual building that is raised up to the height of Christian perfection is supported by the rock of humility alone. Indeed, if true humility is lacking, the gathering of the other virtues will be nothing but the collapse of a house built without a foundation on sand. For in the spiritual building humility lays the stable foundation; charity orders the breadth of that house; the wall is raised on high by the continuation of good works in fervor of spirit; the roof is set at the top in faith in our Lord Jesus Christ and in firm hope of the promise of eternal protection and happiness; and the length is arranged in the long-suffering of patience with most beautiful order.
