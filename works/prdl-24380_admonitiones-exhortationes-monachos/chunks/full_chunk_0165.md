Paul says: “It is appointed for human beings to die once.” Continual meditation on necessary death makes a person never trust in earthly things, but await his departure in every place and time with foresight. Meditation on death always expects the end of life in every place, because it is wholly ignorant of the moment of dying. Whoever meditates daily on the coming of future death prepares himself for dying at every time with all the diligence he can. For nothing is more certain for us than death, although we all are ignorant of the hour of dying.

Therefore, brothers, with all the urgency we can, we admonish your charity always to think that you are going to die, and with such foresight diligently to prepare yourselves for dying that, when the hour comes, you may not fear to depart. Let no one promise himself a long life in this world, because nothing deceives mortals as much as the hope of a longer span. For what does a longer age produce in human beings except that it makes them liable to more sins and always less prepared for dying? The longer we live in this world, the more we fear death. A longer age piles up greater debts of spiritual obligation; and an old man fears all the more to come before the sight of the strict judge, the more he recognizes that he is bound before the Lord in many matters to render an account. For the soul departs unwillingly from its body when it is tormented by the heavier stings of its conscience.

But whoever is pure from every evil of iniquity and adorned for salvation with the beauty of the necessary virtues does not fear to die, because through death he goes to everlasting life. Therefore, O monk, live now as you wish to die; for the death which has been preceded by the integrity of a good life cannot be evil. O how securely he dies, whoever loves the Lord Jesus above all things and, despising all the goods of this world for love of him, faithfully keeps his words. He departs securely from this life who always lives in the love of God without stain of conscience. He dies securely whose life is defiled by no pleasures of this world. For the death of the saints is a most sweet sleep and a peaceful entrance into eternal life. But the death of sinners is worst, whose evil life has gone before it, contrary to the divine ordinances. For one who lives in sins, opposed to God, death becomes for him a dreadful entrance into hell.

He does not die badly who has lived well; and rarely does he die well who is shown always to have lived badly. He dies securely who loves God above all things with a pure mind and at all times keeps himself from this wicked world. Death is desirable for the good and upright in heart, because it sets an end to exile and restores life in heaven. Death is the leaving of the body, the overthrow of the dark prison, and the laying down of a heavy burden. Death is the end of present evils, the finish of sadness, and the grave of every earthly pleasure. A good life in the faith and charity of Jesus Christ makes death precious and especially desirable to the servant of God.
