Life of Abbot Johannes Trithemius, written by himself in the Book of Ecclesiastical Writers.

Johannes, abbot of the monastery of Saint Martin the bishop at Sponheim, of the order of the divine Father Benedict, of the diocese of Mainz, a German by nation, a Mosellan by homeland, born in the village of Trittenheim in the diocese of Trier. Although I am not worthy for my name to be placed among ecclesiastical writers, because of the poverty of my talent, nevertheless, urged by friends, I was compelled to place at the end of this catalogue the titles and beginnings of my own writings as well, following the example of the divine fathers Jerome, Gennadius, Honorius the priest of Autun, and Sigebert, monk of Gembloux, who also placed themselves at the end of their treatises published on these same matters. Therefore, besides this work, I have also written the following. And first, by command of the presidents of the general chapter, I abridged:

The Statutes of the same Chapter, one book: “Since they wish.”
On the Visitation of Monks, one book: “Your brothers.”
On the Manner of Observing the Chapter, one book: “Since in heaven.”
On the Rule of Saint Benedict, two books: “Come, children, listen.”
An Exhortation to Monks, two books: “Of Christ’s warfare.”
On Temptations, two books: “The human race.”
On the Life of Priests, one book: “You ask of me.”
On the Vice of Possessiveness, one book: “All who come to life.”
On the Misery of This Life, one book: “Since there is nothing.”
On the Ruin of Our Order, one book: “Since of the order.”
On the Illustrious Men of Our Order, four books: “As I was considering.”
Praises of the Order of the Carmelites, two books: “By prayers and entreaties.”
On the Praise of Scribes, one book: “To the venerable father.”
On the Lights of Germany, one book: “Since there are.”
Letters to Various Persons, one book: “You ask that copies.”
On Saint Anne, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.

As for conferences, whether delivered in general chapters, in observantial chapters, or elsewhere, they are without fixed number; some are extant, and some have been taken away. These, then, apart from those which still lie unfinished in my hands, are the works I recall having written up to the present: in the thirty-fourth year of my age, the tenth of my assuming the office of abbot, and the twelfth of my conversion, under Maximilian, King of the Romans, and the Supreme Pontiff Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which I have now for the third time corrected, enlarged, and completed the catalogue of ecclesiastical writers, having labored on it for nearly seven years: 1493, in the twelfth indiction.

Letter

Johannes Trithemius, called abbot of Sponheim, to the Reverend Father and his preceptor, Lord Johannes de Colenhausen, abbot of the Monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter, martyrs, in Seligenstadt, formerly abbot of Sponheim, my predecessor: reverence and honor.
