Let these exercises never depart from his memory. Let the most bitter remembrance of the Lord’s Passion often be in the mind of the cloistered man, and let us offer all the prayers that he makes to the Father in the power of the blood and wounds of our Lord Jesus Christ. Let there always be on his lips words bearing before them the mark of a contrite mind, and let either prayer be offered to God or meditation on his words be turned over in the mind. Let the devout monk turn away from evil and do the good he can, with cheerfulness and with the discernment of the Spirit of God, since, as has been said, for doing penance it is not enough to change one’s conduct for the better and withdraw from evil deeds, unless one also strives to adorn one’s conscience with good works.

For true penance, then, four things are required, as you know, my brothers. The first is true contrition for sins, which is a sorrow of heart voluntarily undertaken in detestation of the evils one has committed. Of this Scripture says: “A contrite and humbled heart God will not despise.” And again: “Those who sow in tears shall reap in rejoicing.” For the companion of penance is the sorrow of contrition, and the greater it is, the more fruitful the penance will be. True contrition, indeed, is that which both sorrow of mind commends and continual cleansing by tears bears witness to.

Great is the usefulness of tears, because there is no sin so grave that contrition, accompanied by tears, does not blot it out. Hence the holy teacher Augustine says: “O humble tear, how powerful you are, and how full of virtue, for yours is the kingdom, yours is the power. You do not fear the gaze of the judge; when enemies accuse, you impose silence. Alone you enter to the king, but alone you do not depart. You conquer the unconquerable and bind the omnipotent.” The same man also says: “The holier anyone is, and the fuller of holy desire, the more abundant is his weeping in prayer.”

True compunction has sorrow; sorrow, however, is shown by tears, and by these penance is adorned as though with certain ornaments. True compunction makes the soul shudder at sin; and when it has begun from fear, increased by its own growth it gradually rises into love. Great is its virtue, and truly admirable its power, since all enormity of sins soon yields to it. For whomever true compunction of heart has made a suppliant, the merciful Lord at once hears him. O happy tears, O saving affliction, and truly sweet bitterness, which the devil fears, through which the pierced soul rises up to the mercy of God. For, as the holy Pope Gregory says, the more abundantly guilt is wept over, the higher is the knowledge of truth that is reached, because a conscience once polluted, baptized anew with tears, is renewed for seeing the inward light.

The second thing that belongs to the truth of penance is a firm purpose of amendment, since whoever has the word of confession in his mouth and does not have the purpose of amendment in his heart is either deceitful or empty.
