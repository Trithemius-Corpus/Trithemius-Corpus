Sing sweetly, wisely, fervently, and cheerfully in the service of the highest and unchangeable God, dearest brothers; and with all the devotion and purity of mind you can, diligently praise your creator and savior. Let all sloth be far from the hearts of those who sing psalms; let no drowsiness be admitted while praising, since, when God is present in the midst of those who sing psalms, it is altogether fitting that fervor of devout souls be present. During the time of divine service let all things be clean, all things pure, all things most full of the fervor of charity, so that wicked thoughts may have no place and all things may be done as they ought.

When the day is finished and Compline has been sung according to custom, as soon as the monk has reached the privacy of his cell, let him examine his conscience, considering with what business he has occupied himself. This is the custom of the brothers of the Bursfeld reform, excellent and praiseworthy: that no one should presume to lay himself down on his bed after Compline unless he has first taken the greatest care to examine his conscience concerning the faults or negligences committed during the day. But if, after examination, you find that during the day you have offended in anything, you know, taught by long custom, what must be done. Hidden faults must at once on the next day be wiped away by contrition and humble confession; manifest faults, however, must be brought to the chapter of faults, which is held daily. This is the custom of the reformed order; this is the institution of holy reform: that we never allow the faults of offending brothers to grow, but as soon as they begin to arise, we take care to cut them off. The soul must be cleansed daily; the purpose of holiness must be renewed daily; because, just as we cannot remain in the same state without change, so it is necessary for us, by continual exercise, to rouse the mind more frequently to its former fervor of charity.

What are the signs of true humility. Sermon III.

Let none of you, most beloved brothers, deceive himself by his own estimation of himself; let no one seduce his heart by foolish and empty presumption. Not all who desire to seem humble are humble before God; nor are those who keep silence under injuries always truly patient. For the roots of true virtues lie hidden in the secrecy of the heart, and not in the mere outward display of presentation. We see certain men among you who desire rather to be regarded as humble than truly to be so, and who show many signs of pride, but either no signs of humility, or certainly very few. We have deep compassion, as indeed we ought, for those who err so greatly, who desire to have the name of virtue but despise virtue itself. Whom, I ask, do you deceive, wretched men? Do you not shamefully destroy yourselves by misleading yourselves, who, despising truth, love falsehood?
