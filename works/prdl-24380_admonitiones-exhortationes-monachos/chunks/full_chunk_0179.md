The memory of the Lord’s Passion, with thanksgiving, to be made daily.

What the ordering of a good monastic life should be.

According to what the holy custom of our reformation has established for you to observe, firmly preserve your effort to make progress in the desire you have conceived. Therefore in the morning, when you are awakened and rise for Prime, do these things in secret. Examine and consider the course of the coming day that lies before you, and prudently think about what useful thing is at hand to be done. Set before yourselves in your hearts how carefully you wish to live the whole day, and arm your minds with patience against all things that may happen adversely. Whatever you do, complete it in the fervor of charity, and always order all words and deeds to the praise of God.

Third, we judge it useful and necessary for you always to strive to implore divine help in all things through most devout prayers. And it seems very fitting to us, as we have often said and always admonish, that each of you at least once a day should commemorate the Lord’s Passion and, for the many benefits received from God, should say an act of thanksgiving even once. So that this may be done uniformly by each one of you, listen to this brief form which we have arranged, and, if you wish, copy it out for yourselves.

I adore, praise, bless, and glorify you, Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, Savior and giver of life to all faithful souls, and I give you thanks from the depths of my heart for your most holy Incarnation and your Nativity from the most pure Virgin Mary, for your Circumcision, for your flight into Egypt, for your seven-year exile there, and for your later return to the land of your temporal Nativity.

I give you thanks, sweetest Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for all your labors, journeys, and weariness, for hunger, thirst, cold, nakedness, poverty, and want, and for the necessities, injuries, insults, mockeries, and slanders which throughout your whole life you endured for us willingly, not under compulsion. And as often as you moved your hand to work, your foot to walk, or as often as you opened your mouth to speak, so often I bless, praise, glorify, and humbly adore you, and with all the strength of my heart I always give you thanks, mediator of God and men, sweetest Jesus Christ.

I give you thanks, Lord Jesus Christ, most merciful King of Heaven, for your most holy teaching, for the evangelical institution of your faith and sacraments, for the calling of the nations, for the justification of sinners, and for all your wonders and powers, and for all your benefits, which you have bestowed either on me particularly or on all others together with me generally.

I give you thanks, Lord Jesus Christ, most kindly Savior, for your charity which you had toward the human race, and for your whole most bitter Passion and all the sorrows which you endured for us wretched people. I give you thanks, Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for the grief and sorrow of your most innocent heart, which you endured for us when the time of your impending Passion was near, when you said to your disciples who followed you: My soul is sorrowful even unto death.
