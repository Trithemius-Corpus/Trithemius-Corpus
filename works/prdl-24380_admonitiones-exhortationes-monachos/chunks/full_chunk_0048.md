For without the justice of truth every prayer will be fruitless, since we know that God does not hear sinners, and that salvation is far from sinners, as Scripture says: He who turns away his ears so as not to hear the law, his prayer will be abominable. The holy prophet also says in the psalm: If I have regarded iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me. For just as no medicine does any good in a wound unless the iron is first drawn out, so the prayer of one in whose mind deceit still remains, or hatred in whose breast, does no good.

But in order that God may hear your prayer, first of all your conscience must be cleansed from every stain of mortal sin, and the mind must take on a true purpose of amendment. To pray much at the same time and offend in many things, what else is it than to mock God? For prayer is intercepted in two ways, so that it is not heard by the Lord: one, when the one praying still commits evil by sinning; the other, if he does not forgive his debtor the debt for God’s sake. Therefore you who desire to have fruit from your prayers, who long to come with the penitent to God’s mercy, beware lest you do any evil against God’s will, and take diligent care that you truly forgive from your heart all things committed against you by men. For you know that the Lord said: Forgive, and you shall be forgiven.

But after you have cleansed your conscience from evil and forgiven every offense to your neighbor, then you will be fit for prayer, if by the fervor of a holy purpose you have been rightly ordered toward God. Now then approach confidently to beseech the Lord; cast yourself humbly in his sight; and pray with purity of heart and fervor of spirit, and do not cease from prayer as long as you live in the flesh. Let your prayer be pure, simple, clear, and plain; let it be full of seriousness and weight, and adorned with the beauty of true humility. Do not ask anything in prayer that is contrary to the salvation of the soul; nor should you pray too importunately for a temporal thing, since it is shamelessness to ask God for riches, which provide the greatest occasion for pride and often stain Christian purity. It is not shamelessness if you ask for daily bread, since it is one thing to ask for material from which you may grow proud, and another to ask for the necessary food by which you may live.

Do not justify yourself when praying, lest like the Pharisee you go away condemned; nor ask anything from the Lord as though it were owed to your merits as a debt, but humble yourself in all things, that you may deserve to receive grace. Remember the humility of the publican, and pray with such great humility that with him you may find forgiveness of sins and justification of soul. For God’s grace is more abundant than the prayer of the suppliant, and he grants us more when we pray than we ask. You will not pray to him who knows the hidden things of the heart with a cry of the voice, but with fervor of spirit and with the cry of the inward mind knock at the ears of the divine majesty.
