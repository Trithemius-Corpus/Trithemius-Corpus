Consider now, therefore, your calling, O monks, and do not corrupt your purpose of holiness, if you desire to arrive at the glory of God. Woe to you, faithless monk, who, having become a despiser of your calling and a transgressor of your vow, live in the monastery not as a monk: you who were called to endure tribulation patiently for the love of God, and yet ask about refreshment.

For your profession commands you to be humbly content with all mean and very poor clothing, and you are not afraid to devote your zeal to finery in dress. You were called to thirst, and you, despising the drinking of water, delight in drinking wine to satiety, against the monastic rule. You were made a monk to fight bravely against the enemy of your salvation, and you, throwing away from yourself the weapons of the virtues, go about lazy, idle, and naked; indeed, what is worse, with a troop of vices accompanying you. You were invited to watch in holy prayers, and you are dissolved in sluggish sleep and shameful idleness. You were called into the subjection of humble obedience, and you, swollen with pride, despise obedience to the precepts of your elders. You were called into charity, and you do not fear to carry hatred against your brother in your breast. You were called to weeping and tears, and you pour out laughter with disorderly guffawing.

The Lord called you to repentance in the monastery, and you, loosened into wantonness, join sin to sin. You were called to meditate day and night on the law of our Lord Jesus Christ, and you, walking after vanity, devote yourself to tales and perishable things. You were called to heaven, and you, loving the world, pursue earthly things. The Lord called you into the meekness of humility, and you go about full of rancor and pride. You were called by the Lord to the perfection of the purest monastic way of life, and you rot away like a beast in the dung of vanity and negligence. You were called to the observance of God’s commandments, and you, given over to the pleasures of the flesh, think of nothing less than Christ. You were invited to love the Lord Jesus above all things, and you walk after the evil desires of your heart, showing yourself ungrateful to your Redeemer. You who ought to have lived by the Spirit savor earthly things, and, drowned in pride, you despise all the holy exercises of the spiritual life.

And what will you answer on that day of judgment to the Judge, who will say to you, most ungrateful one: I, who am Lord of all things, humbled myself for your sake, taking the form of a servant, and, being found in appearance as a man, poured out my blood for you; for you I endured scourges, and for you I underwent a most bitter death of my own free will. I, O monk, loved you with my whole heart; I redeemed you by my blood from everlasting death; I called you out of love to the heavenly kingdom, if you had kept my commandments.
