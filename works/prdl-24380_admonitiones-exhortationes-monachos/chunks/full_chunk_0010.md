It is enjoined in the spiritual life: let us take the greatest care that, having become deaf hearers of the truth, we do not lie to the Lord. Let us diligently remember the vow we made, and let us preserve inviolate until death the holy order which we profess. Let us hear our King saying in the Gospel to his soldiers: “If anyone serves me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also will my servant be.”

We are ministers of the eternal King: priests and Levites, all cloistered monks, bound for a twofold reason to great holiness, since we have both taken up the order of clerics and profess the rule of monastic purity. Therefore let us follow our instructor, for whom we serve as soldiers, along the harsh path of all the virtues; and let us arm ourselves with right faith, firm hope, and most fervent charity toward God and neighbor, in which the perfection of all holiness is comprised.

For whoever is girded with the defense of charity is never overcome by the adversary. Truly, in charity the warfare of Christ grows sweet, and when its fervor begins to burn in the soldier’s heart, he fears no adversity. For charity, as Saint Gregory says, is never idle, but does great things if it is true; but if it refuses to work, it was not perfect charity.

Therefore, cloistered soldiers, you who have pledged yourselves to serve the eternal King forever, cast away now the works of darkness and put on charity toward God and neighbor, which is the strongest of all armor of salvation. If anyone is clothed with it, he will fear no labors, however hard, no contrary things of this world. He will not fear hunger, nor thirst, nor persecutions by men, nor reproaches, nor mockeries, nor prisons, nor chains, nor anything hard and adverse; but everyone who has once perfectly tasted the charity of God will remain unconquered against whatever raging things of the world.

The true lover of God rejoices in tribulations, is glad in adversities, and exults in persecutions for the sake of justice. He follows his King, seated in the heavens; there he dwells in mind and in the desire of his soul, where he does not doubt that his Savior reigns. Whatever pleasure and delights are seen anywhere in the world produce stench and horror, like the vilest dung, for the servant of Christ burning with true charity, because he loves nothing earthly, nothing transitory, nothing perishable, but with the whole intention of his mind loves him alone to whom he has devoted himself.

For the true soldier of Christ thinks nothing of the things of the world; he cares not for the flesh but for the mind, since he is not ignorant of what is written: “No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself.” Indeed, all who serve an earthly emperor always obey his commands in everything, and at once go without contradiction wherever he has ordered. They all follow one commander, and whatever he commands in the king’s name, no one in the whole army contradicts.
