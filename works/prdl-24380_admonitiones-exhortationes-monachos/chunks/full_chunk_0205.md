It is the regard of his supreme kindness toward us: that, after leaving so many thousands of people throughout the wide world in error, he mercifully called us in Europe, with a few others, to the knowledge of the Christian faith. Of all his benefits after the death of Christ, I think this is the chief: that he made us Christians in the faith. For outside the faith of Christ no one will be saved, whether Christ is proclaimed to him or whether he is utterly unknown to him. How many kingdoms today in Asia and Africa, how many peoples, how many islands of the sea, have no knowledge of Christ and no faith, but all their religion is in error and without any merit for salvation, even if some outside Christianity seem to adore and worship one God? For Christ is the savior of faithful souls; whoever has not believed in him is already judged, and without doubt will be condemned. Therefore we shall never be able to render to God worthy acts of thanksgiving, because he ordained that we should be born, so to speak, in the Catholic faith, and by the blood of his only-begotten Son he freed us from the destruction of death. Let us therefore be mindful of so great a benefit, and take care to live in such a way that we are not judged for ingratitude. Let us bless God the Father at all times, who has poured out the gifts of the Holy Spirit upon us and has not permitted us to be born removed from the unity of Catholic unity.

The seventh thing believed able to produce the love of God in our hearts is considered in the promise of everlasting happiness. For if we consider how great are the good things in heaven that he has promised he will give us, his love will deservedly burn sweetly in our hearts. He has promised his whole self to us as a reward: he alone is the highest and utterly unchangeable good; and although he is indivisible, he offers himself to be enjoyed by all in such a way that nevertheless he is possessed whole by each one. He loves all by whom he is loved, and he loves each one by whom he is possessed. The more, then, we love God now with a pure mind, the more reward we shall receive there.

On the Knowledge of Spiritual Grace. Sermon VI.

The holy apostle of Christ, John, teaches us that not every spirit is always to be believed, but that impulses must be carefully examined to see whether they are from God, who is the true light, lest perhaps something rise up against us from the side of darkness. For our adversary, since he is crafty, never sleeps, but devises a thousand ways by which he may catch us unawares. Yet he attacks those already making progress in one way, and deals in another way with those who are still living in the sluggishness of negligence. The more we have advanced in God’s commandments, the more necessary it is that we keep watch against the devil lying in wait for us. The demon pursues with importunity those who try to flee, and rages with envy against those who desire to please God with love. Lurking in secret under the appearance of good, he is accustomed to attack those who do not know him, whom he knows he cannot overcome in open conflict. He is accustomed to [unclear] many things.
