Sermon V.

There is no contradiction found here, since both statements are true: no one is without sin, in that no one is without the law of sin; and he who is born of God does not commit sin, because through the law of the mind, that is, through charity, which is the seed of God, he does not commit sin. For charity covers a multitude of sins; without it there is no good work, and it alone has power to blot out every sin. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: No saint and no just man is without sin; yet on account of this he does not cease to be just or holy, if he holds fast to holiness with affection. But these sins of the just are not to be regarded as mortal or enormous, but as small and venial sins, without which the present life is scarcely lived; for every mortal sin must be far from the just man, if he is to retain the name of justice.

For mortal sin separates man from God, kills the soul, and condemns it eternally with the demons. By the grace of God we can avoid this destructive kind of mortal sin, and through uprightness of will we must do so diligently. For this is why we have been made monks: that, living most cautiously under the command of saving obedience, we might not fall into the pit of mortal sin. Therefore a monk must always keep watch in good works, so that he may live free from mortal crime.

But whoever, on the way of God, feels his flesh troublesome and contrary to him must make it subject to the spirit through remembrance of punishments, and obedient, as it were, through fear. First of all, then, a monk who loves his own salvation should always attentively consider the deceitful condition of this world and its deception, and should weigh how quickly every delight of the flesh passes away. He can easily draw his soul away from sins who has carefully considered that the pleasure of the flesh is very brief, but the punishment owed to sinners is eternal.

The world passes away, and its desire with it; all human beings too pass away by dying, and no one is permitted to remain long in this life. For what is the world but a great sea, deep and restless? In it there are as many creeping things of evils as there are evil desires of human beings [unclear]. O deceitful, deceiving, and unclean world, you shamefully deceive those who cling to you; you plunge all who follow you into the depth of hell. How happy are those who despise you for love of Christ, and, making a useful separation from your vanities, depart from you in time.

Whatever we see in the world is vain, and every lover of the world becomes vain and will soon be corrupted. Think that you will soon die, for nothing draws a person back from sin as frequent and intent meditation on death does. For Scripture says: Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever. Thought of the necessity of dying shakes the mind with fear and restrains it from vice.

The second thing that persuades one to flee sin is fear of the future judgment, at which all human beings must necessarily be presented. No one can escape this terrible judgment; rather, we must all appear at it. Two things must be feared by us.
