Augustine says: The devil is permitted to tempt only so far as benefits you, so that you may be exercised, so that you may be tested, so that you, who did not know yourself, may be discovered by yourself. For the devil, if God permits it, can stir up the impulse of temptation; but it is in your own completely free power whether you wish to give consent to the tempter or to refuse it. Hence, when he tempted the Son of God in the desert, he did not drive him, did not touch him, did not compel him, but only said, “Cast yourself down,” so that you may understand that he cannot conquer unless one is willing. Therefore the divine chief apostle of Christ said: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” For the devil can suggest evil; he cannot compel, nor can he overcome unless you consent.

Our life, as Saint Augustine bears witness, cannot in this pilgrimage be without sin and temptation, because our progress is increased through temptations; nor does anyone come to know himself unless he is tempted; nor can he be crowned unless he has conquered; nor can he conquer unless he has fought; nor can he fight unless he has had an enemy and temptations. For Scripture says: “He who has not been tempted, what does he know?” For no one will be crowned except the one who has fought lawfully.

Therefore, as you approach the service of God, dearly beloved brothers, stand in justice and fear; and so long as you can be overcome and subdued by the adversary, do not boast of security. Always keep watch, always fear, because the enemy with whom you have entered into combat never sleeps, does not rest, does not cease from persecution; and as he is always laying snares for you, he is never wearied. But if at any time he has been overcome, at once he rises up to a new battle, and he does not desist from attacking the good until, utterly defeated, he gives way, or else by troublesome perseverance he conquers the one who is fighting against him.

Hence Saint Pope Gregory also says: The ancient enemy, when he sees himself giving way in the first contest, renews other wars of temptations. The crafty adversary of the human race observes each person’s character, and whichever vices they are nearer to, these he sets before the face of the heart; and he uses great strength to overthrow the condition of the mind.

Stand in the fear of the Lord, firmly rooted, and you will easily overcome all the temptations of the enemy. For it is written in the book of Proverbs: “In the fear of the Lord is confidence of strength.” And again: “By mercy and faith sins are purged; but by the fear of the Lord everyone turns away from evil.” In Ecclesiasticus, too, the wise man said: “The fear of the Lord drives out sin. For he who is without fear will not be able to be justified; for the wrath of his temper overthrows him.” Therefore, by Solomon’s testimony, that man is called blessed who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil.

Prepare your souls also for temptation through the practice of holy conduct, so that when it comes you may be able to resist and stand all the more strongly. For you will easily overcome the devil if you always come prepared to fight. Behold, your adversary, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking someone to devour; you must resist him in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ if you desire to obtain the victory. There is no place here for fleeing.
