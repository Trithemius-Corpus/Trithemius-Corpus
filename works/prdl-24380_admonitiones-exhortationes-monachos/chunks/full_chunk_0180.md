Sermon II.

I ask you, by this sorrow of your heart, O Lord God: have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death, and take from me all melancholy and disordered grief, granting me at all times a saving sorrow for my sins. I give you thanks for the sadness of your most holy heart, when, placed in agony, you prayed to the Father even a third time that he would remove from you the chalice of the Passion, and, having poured forth a wondrous sweat because of your anguish, you resigned yourself wholly to his will for our sake.

I give you thanks, O King of heaven and earth, for your captivity, for the insults, wrongs, and mockeries that you endured throughout the whole time of your Passion for our salvation and that of all. I give you thanks, O Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you, bound to the column for the harsh scourging of your most tender body, for the shedding of your most holy blood, and for the immeasurable pain of your wounds. I adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, crowned with thorns, and I give you thanks for the piercings and pains of your most holy head, for the blows, spittings, and all the mockeries cruelly inflicted by the impious upon your Majesty.

I adore you, Christ Jesus, Son of the living God, carrying the most heavy weight of your cross. I adore you, stripped of your garments before the multitude. I adore you, stretched out by your hands and feet with iron nails upon the cross, and most cruelly fastened to it. I adore you, Jesus Christ, eternal King, hanging and dying for me upon the cross; and I give thanks for the pains of your wounds. I give thanks for the shedding of your most precious blood; I give thanks for your most innocent death, which you willingly endured for our sins and the sins of the whole world.

I give thanks to your boundless goodness, because you called me to your faith, because you caused me to be baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity, and because you deigned to call me from the vanity of the world to serve you, in true tranquility of mind, in this holy congregation. I ask you by your most holy name, O sweetest Jesus, mediator between God and men; I ask you by the pain of your wounds and by the merit of your most bitter Passion; I ask you by the shedding of your most holy blood and by the power of your most innocent death: grant me forgiveness of all my sins, pour into my heart the sweetness of your most fervent love, give me perseverance with growth in all virtues, and have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death, guarding me today and at all times in your mercy, loving Jesus, from every sin and danger of mind and body, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Which of you, brothers, cannot say this brief prayer, or one like it if he has a better, once each day? For it is not lengthy, nor wrapped in foreign expressions, but brief and plain, embracing the life and Passion of Christ in a concise summary. A Christian man is exceedingly ungrateful to God, and especially a cloistered man, if he does not at least once a day make remembrance of the Lord’s Passion, devoutly rendering acts of thanksgiving. But if this prayer is too long for any of you, in the eighth homily of our exhortations we recently gave you a shorter one, to which one who finds this tedious may have recourse.

Above all, I beg you, take care that you are never ungrateful for such great benefits of God. Let this thanksgiving be made after matins, because that is a very suitable time for prayer, and the silence of the night’s rest is accustomed to increase devotion. For I say what those who have experienced it bear witness to as true: there are no prayers more effective for obtaining what is sought than those that are offered to God the Father with remembrance of the Lord’s Passion and with gratitude.
