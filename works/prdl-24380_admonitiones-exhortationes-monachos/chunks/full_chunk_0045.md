Therefore, if there is any gladness in the present life, my brothers, it must be pursued in such a way that the bitterness of that dreadful judgment never withdraws from memory.

O that great day, terrible, exceedingly dreadful, and very bitter, on which the sun and moon will be darkened, not by any lessening of their light, but by comparison with his majesty; where every argument of excuse will cease; where all things will be manifest, and nothing will be able to lie hidden from the Judge; because he who knows the hidden thoughts of minds will then judge openly; he who is not anticipated by favor, nor bent by mercy, is not corrupted by gifts and presents, will not be softened by prayers, nor will any satisfaction thereafter appease him.

Therefore, placed in this life, brothers, let each of you do as much as he can for his soul, because repentance for sins performed here alone is useful and fruitful, so long as there is room for pardon and the sentence of justice is still deferred. He who puts off doing good and doing penance until death is near to damnation, because the good which he neglects when healthy and sound, he will rarely restore when sick and already about to die. For holy Pope Gregory says: Whoever does not fear to defer the fruits of repentance until the hour of death is himself a witness against himself that true repentance was never in his mind. Now, therefore, turn yourselves to our Lord with your whole heart, doing penance before him; and so live, I beg you, that when you must depart from here, you may not fear to appear before his face.

That one must pray without interruption. Homily VIII.

Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, in the Gospel, brothers, according to Saint Luke, wishing us to be constant in prayers, gave this warning, saying: “One must always pray and never give up.” This counsel of the Lord befits monks above all mortals, and all cloistered persons in whatever religious life: their every action and their whole life, indeed whatever they do, ought either to be prayer or at least in conformity with prayer. For not without reason are all who profess the monastic warfare called spiritual, so that by the very word they may be reminded to despise the pleasures of the flesh for love of God and to walk through true charity in the ardor of the spirit.

For the holy Apostle of Christ says: “Those who are in the flesh cannot please God,” because “if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” And knowing that without persistence in prayer the fervor of the spirit cannot long remain in us, he again says to the Thessalonians: “Always rejoice; pray without interruption; give thanks in all things, because this is the [unclear].”
