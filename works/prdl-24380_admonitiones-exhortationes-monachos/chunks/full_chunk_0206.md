Against his snares the best remedy is for a monk to submit all the thoughts of his heart to the examination of the elders. For demons hate the public light, and are ashamed that the thoughts of the young which they suggest should come to the knowledge of spiritual elders.

Therefore, whoever among you thinks that he stands in good, let him always be careful not to fall through the devil’s overthrowing him. There are some among you, young both in years and in conversion, who consent to their sensual inclinations more than is fitting; demons mock such men in various ways, as though under the appearance of good. They seek holiness without merit; they desire to have merit without the practice of the necessary virtues. They seem to themselves great before God in devotion, and exceptional, yet in their animal passions they have not been mortified.

You are mistaken, O wretched men, and you shamefully deceive yourselves, when you think of building the tower of holiness without the foundation of most humble subjection and obedience. First the disordered desires of sensuality must be mortified; the passions of the animal man must be restrained: anger, murmuring, the spite of indignation, and all impatience. For as long as vices rule over you, whatever gifts of private singularity you offer are not pleasing to God. All things are vain where there is not a pure conscience.

The conduct of a young monk is suspect to us, however devout it may seem to be, if the humility of voluntary subjection does not commend it. For a monk without humility and obedience cannot be good, even if he hunts for holiness every day by fasting and prayer. The devil deceives many in the hiddenness of elation and pride, whom he allows continually to keep vigil, fast, and pray. For just as no works are pleasing to God without humility and charity, so the fasting, vigils, prayers, and studies of monks without these are not fearsome to the devil.

The devil is not moved by anything that does not torment him. He does not greatly value the vigils of the self-exalted, nor does he attend to the fasting of the swollen with pride, nor does he fear the prayers of the proud. But if he finds you humble; if in all things you obey the commands of the elders cheerfully; if he sees you doing great things and thinking humble things about yourself; if he perceives the charity of God and neighbor burning in you, then surely he is stirred against you, because his envy is tormented by your virtues. He turns against you all the weapons of his cunning that he can, and with all his strength tries to overturn your purpose of holiness.

For nothing displeases demons more in a monk; nothing torments them more violently than if, for the love of God, he has truly humbled himself in all things and has always, with a cheerful spirit, kept the discipline of the monastic way of life under the guardianship of obedience. You must beware the impulses of demons, you who think yourselves to be something great without the merit of true virtues.
