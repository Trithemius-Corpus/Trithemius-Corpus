Therefore no labor should seem burdensome, no time long, no observance hard to a monk, by which the glory of eternal blessedness is acquired.

Consider, my brothers, the shortness of this life, and do good while it is permitted; for he who now sows sparingly will also reap sparingly after death, and each person will receive the fitting reward for his own work, not for another’s. The time for labor is very brief, but the reward owed to good works is everlasting.

Consider, my brothers, men entangled in the world and all lovers of this age: how strong they are in earthly and perishable affairs, but weak and feeble in heavenly ones. Take care to please God as much as they continually labor to appear rich and glorious in the world. For many do not shrink from sweating even to death for temporal glory; but for the hope of future happiness they scorn to do or endure even a little. For earthly gains they bear any injuries with the greatest patience, yet for a heavenly reward they refuse to endure even the insult of the slightest word. They can stand daily, strong and steadfast, before an earthly king, prince, or judge; but in prayer before the almighty Lord they cannot persist for the space of one hour. They willingly bear many adversities to acquire riches that will perish; but they neglect heavenly goods all the more, the more delightfully they are occupied in love of earthly things.

Let their way of life, dearly beloved brothers, warn you to flee all the cares of this world so much the more, now that it is permitted you to handle the business of earthly things so much the less. Always devote all your solicitude and care to the acquisition of heavenly goods, and labor most diligently at all times for the reward of future blessedness. For, as Scripture bears witness, your life on earth is warfare/service, and whatever a man has sown, that also he will reap.

Know that death hastens daily, and at every moment a part of life, never to return again, is extinguished. Watch, therefore, my dearly beloved brothers, watch, for you do not know when the Lord our God will come for the judgment of each one’s death: evening, midnight, cockcrow, or morning; lest, when he comes suddenly, he find you sleeping. Therefore watch at all times, praying that you may be deemed worthy to escape all the evils that are to come and to stand with confidence before the sight of the Son of God, who judges all things. For, as the blessed Christophilus testifies, we shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each may receive what is proper to the body, according as he has done, whether good or evil.

Blessed is that monk whom the Lord finds watching in good works, who has prepared himself for the wedding-feast of the heavenly Lamb in true purity of heart, who will not fear to meet the Judge.
