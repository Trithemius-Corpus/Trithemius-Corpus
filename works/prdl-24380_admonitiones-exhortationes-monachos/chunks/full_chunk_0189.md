But the monk who, according to his strength, follows Christ in true humility is accustomed to flee every sin and, as much as he can, to exercise himself in the pursuit of virtues. Humility confesses its sin, and whenever it offends even in the smallest matters, it does not rest until it has amended everything through true penitence. Whoever is truly humble is always found zealous to preserve the purity of his conscience. For charity is the nourishment of humility, and it does not allow the one who has it to be prying.

The thirtieth sign of true humility shows a monk confirmed in the purpose of holiness, and makes him subject to all the brothers, even the younger ones. Whatever his superior commands, he obeys simply, and he never debates the commands of his elder; he does not ask the reason why he orders it so. If impossible things are commanded, he is ready in spirit; for whatever is hard and arduous, he is prepared without contradiction. He is directed by the counsel of his superior; he believes him, trusts him, obeys him; he is willingly governed by his command.

You have now heard, my dearly beloved brothers, the thirty marks of true humility. O good God, where are the works? Which of us will boast that he has true humility, so that he suffers no defect in these signs? These, shame to say, are our times, in which the charity of many has grown cold, the integrity of holy religion is perishing, and the devotion of almost all has breathed its last. Our monks seek holiness without the merit of virtues; they wish to have merit without the exercise of labor; therefore today no saint is found in the monastery. The fervor of holy compunction in many is either very rare or lukewarm, because where one lives for flesh and blood, the fervor of inward charity is not possessed.

It is not enough for you, O monk, who boast of observance, to have nothing of your own, not to touch a woman, to reside in the monastery under obedience to your superior, not to eat meat, and in the other outward matters of the discipline of the Rule to live according to monastic institution, unless with the greatest diligence you also strive to adorn your inner life through true charity toward God and neighbor. For charity is the end of regular observance, for the acquisition of which all the other things were principally instituted. Therefore without the charity of God all things profit you nothing, since it alone, and no other, distinguishes between good and evil works. O monk, monk, whoever you are who live lukewarmly in the monastery without the fervor of charity, do you think that the outward apparatus of discipline can save you? It is charity that sanctifies the one who has it; charity commends the other virtues and all good things to God. Have charity, and all the works of piety that you do will be pleasing to God.
