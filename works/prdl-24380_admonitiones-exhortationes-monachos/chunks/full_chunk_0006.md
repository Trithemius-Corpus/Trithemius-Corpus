He proved most patient for us, and the bitterness of his future passion, foreknown by him, never departed from his foreknowledge. For although he was almighty God, having become man for us, he knew all things, understood all things, foresaw all things; nor could anything of what he was to suffer be hidden from him, who, in order to suffer in the world, was made capable of suffering, while remaining impassible as the God he was.

Born of the Virgin Mary without intercourse with a man, in order to fulfill the law, he was circumcised on the eighth day; and he who had no sin was wounded for our offenses. At night, before the face of his persecutor, the child was led into Egypt; and he who was Lord of all, poor and needy, lived unknown in exile among the nations for seven years. Finally, throughout all the days of his life in this world he endured continual labors with the greatest patience, and never refused to undergo any adversity for the glory of the Father. For the just and innocent one voluntarily suffered all hard and bitter things for us in the most unworthy ways; by the most bitter death of the cross he redeemed us from the ruin of eternal damnation, and in his own blood mercifully reconciled us to the Father.

Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, as the Prince of the Apostles forewarned, let us, my brothers, arm ourselves with the same thought: so that, sharing in compassion for the innocent one, we may diligently recall to memory the things he endured for us. For if we suffer with him, we shall also reign with him as he triumphs in glory.

Behold, Christ calls us to the glory to which he has gone before; he gives us the weapons with which he himself first overcame the prince of this world; he has prescribed the pattern by which a soldier must follow his king to the crown. Christ calls his soldiers to the palace of heaven, whom hardships lead onward with the gifts of virtues accompanying them, and rewards receive as victors to reign forever [unclear].

Therefore, dearest brothers, as we always pursue our warfare with the greatest zeal, let us continually declare war on vices and desires, so that, just as the flesh fights against the spirit, so we may fight in return, ever watchful in prayer and fasting against the inciter of vices. For as long as we live in this world, we are pilgrims away from the Lord, and like athletes we contend in the stadium, carrying out the necessary course of life in continual struggle.

But we fight not by our own strength, but upheld by the mercy of our King, who said to his imitators in the holy Gospel: “Without me you can do nothing.” And lest we flee the battle of the warfare set before us as impossible, he said elsewhere: “In the world you will have tribulation; but take courage: I have overcome the world.” And so that we might know that the Lord Jesus is always present with his little servants in this world, he promises us thus in the Apostles: “Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the age.”

Having received this promise of security, O soldiers of Christ, my dearest brothers, why do we hesitate? Why do we not stand bravely against all the insolence of this world? We always have our King and protector present while we fight; and do we wretchedly fear to struggle against a weak enemy, who overcomes no one unless he is willing?
