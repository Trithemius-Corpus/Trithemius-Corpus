Rise now, Benedict, most holy prince of monks, and see your order, which you established, and your inheritance, which you planted. Look upon the monks who have forgotten your profession, sunk in earthly things, given over to pleasure, full of every spring of vices. You once gave them the best precepts for living; you commanded them to be strangers to the cares of worldly business; you ordered their hearts to be stretched out toward the love of God alone. Rise, I say, most blessed Father, and see the desolation of your order, because within it is confounded by its own sons, and without it is scattered by enemies. The monks despise the institutions of your piety; strangers seize the monasteries of your order. There is no one to defend the honor of your name; there is no one to protect the beauty of your most holy order.

Awake also, great Pope Gregory, you who under anathema forbade places sacred to God for monks to be transferred to the uses of secular men, and see monks driven from their monasteries and worldly clerics thrust in. You, once prince of all the churches, used to apply the greatest care to monastic honor, guarding with immense solicitude lest anything be presumed in the sacred order against the Rule of Benedict. Behold now, most reverend prelate, the conduct of monks, bare of every honor, full of every shame. See how the monasteries of your order, dedicated to God, are handed over into the hands of clerics, and those places which once could be compared to angelic choirs now become brothels of harlots.

Awake, most glorious of emperors and unconquered defender of the Christian faith, Charles, glory of the Germans, and see the calamity of the monks. You once made the order of Father Benedict glorious, and through your zeal spread it through almost the whole world. See now how the monasteries which you once founded are all exposed to the plundering of enemies. You once venerated the monastic order as the camp of God; now, holy prince, behold it as though despised by all, stripped of its goods, [unclear]. You once used with immense care to bring back abbots and monks grown lukewarm to the observance of regular discipline, and, once reformed, to defend them with great solicitude from every molestation by your own men or by any others.

Rise also, Berno, abbot of the monastery of Cluny, with your successors, you who in ancient times magnificently restored the holy order when it was collapsing and brought it back to its former beauty throughout the whole world. Rise, I say, now, and see how the iniquity of monks abounds, fury rules, crime overflows, and pleasure makes almost all men mad. The beauty of your reform has been taken away; discipline has collapsed; honor has fled; zeal for holy conduct has perished; humility has breathed its last.
