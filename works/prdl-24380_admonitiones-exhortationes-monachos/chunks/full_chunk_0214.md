Sermon VI.

For the recovery of lost compunction it seems necessary that you in no way lift yourself up against the Lord, but conform your will in all things to the divine ordinance. “That his will be done,” we say in the Lord’s Prayer; it is fitting that in mind and in the affection of the will we should always freely agree with that very prayer.

Third, whoever desires to recover the lost grace of devotion must firmly despise every pleasure of this world for the love of God. For it cannot happen that one should receive the spirit of inward compunction while he opens the dwelling-place of his heart to earthly joys and pleasures. Therefore you must cast away every consolation of the present life, if you desire to have the spirit of divine love with its gifts.

Fourth, you are commanded to withdraw yourself perpetually from the vain cares and occupations of this world, and to occupy your mind delightfully at all times in the service of God. For, as the Lord says, no one can serve God and mammon; but whoever desires to serve the Lord in truth of heart must despise all the occupations of the world.

Fifth, we advise you to remember always to occupy your heart in meditation on the passion of Christ, since there is no shorter way to recover the grace of compunction, nor any manner of prayer more effective for obtaining from the Lord whatever we ask.

Sixth, you should strive daily to cleanse your conscience through true penitence; and whenever you have fallen into any fault, immediately come to your senses again through contrition. If you take care to observe these things with a good will, by God’s mercy you will recover the lost grace of compunction.

On the Nine Steps of Ascent. Sermon VII.

We describe for you, dearly beloved brothers, a ladder of nine steps, by which the human spirit ascends into heaven. And although one step is higher than another, nevertheless all of them, each and every one, direct us toward the kingdom of God, and they distinguish the merits of those ascending according to their merits. Therefore attend diligently to what we are about to say, and consider how much you have advanced in spiritual progress.

All, as many as have reached the kingdom of God, have ascended by the steps of this ladder. And the more anyone has advanced in the love of God, the higher he has already ascended. The first step is nearest to hell, and farther removed from the height of the heavenly fatherland. For people placed on it begin to withdraw from vices and to make progress in the fear of God; neglecting venial sins, they withdraw from mortal ones. Their minds, indeed, longing for the practice of virtues, flee the more serious sins; but, weighed down by the weakness of evil habit, they pay no heed to the smallest ones. Hence they more often fall through frailty into the pit of mortal sin, because they have not guarded against venial sins as occasions of graver ones. For no sin is so small that it does not become greater when neglected.
