You give [unclear], and commend fasting and abstinence. O holy humility, truly beloved of God, through repentance you justify the ungodly; you preserve the just man in his resolve of holiness; you powerfully open heaven to those who love you; you close hell to the penitent.

O venerable humility, exceedingly pleasing to God, you compelled the Son of the eternal Father to descend from heaven into the womb of Mary, the most chaste and perpetual Virgin. You made him appear visible to us; you laid him, wrapped in poor cloths, in a manger, so that he might adorn us in heaven with the ornaments of everlasting happiness. You drove the Son of God to undergo circumcision of the flesh, so that we, circumcised in mind, might be able to enter the promised inheritance of the saints.

O humility of great power, you made the King of heaven and earth withdraw before the face of one most contemptible little man, and dwell in seven years’ exile in the land of Egypt. You made Christ Jesus, the only-begotten Son of God, whom you compelled to assume a human body for our salvation, undergo also his most bitter passion and death by his own free will.

Great is your dominion, O venerable humility of holiness, since the kingdom of heaven is promised to you, the gift of everlasting blessedness is granted to your worshipers, and whoever keeps to your footsteps is raised up to the height of the angels, as Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted.” The King of heaven taught you, when he said to his imitators: “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” The most blessed Mother of God also, the untouched Virgin Mary, speaks thus of the Lord God in her sweetest song of praise: “He has cast down the mighty from their seats and exalted the humble.” And the prophet says in the psalm: “The Lord has regarded the prayers of the humble, and has not despised their petitions.” For the prayer of one who humbles himself pierces the clouds; until it draws near, he will not be comforted, and he will not depart until the Most High looks upon him.

Humility makes a monk who is pure in his conscience most pleasing to God; and the more he casts himself down by humbling himself for God’s honor, the more glorious he will appear in the kingdom. The monk who truly humbles himself becomes a son of the Most High through participation in grace, and will rejoice forever with the King of heaven, Jesus Christ.

He who truly desires to imitate Christ should also take up true humility; girded and protected by it as by a breastplate of strength, he will do great things, thinking nothing of himself except what is humble, and will not incur the stain of vainglory. For this is the true perfection of monks: always humbly to recognize their own imperfection, and diligently to consider rather what good is lacking to them than what they possess.

For true humility always stretches forward toward the things ahead through the desire of charity; mindful of what is past, it fears lest it lose what it has, and, desiring to acquire through the zeal of devotion what it does not yet have, it does not grow lukewarm in the fervor of holiness. It considers most carefully how greatly it is still deficient, and in what respects it has made progress [unclear].
