Sermon II.

What the good ordering of monastic life is. Sermon II.

We have said that the institution of our most holy father Benedict, which by the usual term we call a religion or order, shone forth as most holy at the beginning of its foundation, and was spread very widely and in many ways throughout the whole Church of the Latins. Hence, for very many years in this same order there shone many wise, learned, and most holy men, who magnificently illuminated the whole Church. For there are those who write that from this monastic religion of ours fifteen thousand six hundred men and nuns have been enrolled in the catalogue of the saints. This multitude of saints in the order is set before us, brothers, for imitation, not for empty boasting. What can their multitude contribute to our salvation if we do not imitate the manner of life by which they went before us?

Whenever we desire usefully to praise the most holy men of our order, let us hasten by holy conduct to come into their fellowship. For then we shall glory fruitfully in the multitude of the saints of our order, if, by living rightly, we strive diligently to increase their number. For this reason we set before you so great a multitude of saints: so that, when you see that many before you in this same order conquered the world and the flesh, you may at last rise up from your lukewarm way of life. The virtue which God’s mercy bestowed on them he will not deny to you if you ask in true charity.

They conquered the world because they loved Christ with complete devotion, despising all things for love of him. They despised all things, and the Lord Jesus provided for them whatever seemed necessary for the sustaining of life. This fervor of devotion among the monks endured for many years after the first institution of the order in great constancy, and converted the souls of many thousands of men to the Lord. But nothing among human things is stable, nothing among perishable things is lasting. Holy religion bestowed riches on the monks, until in time the daughter suffocated her mother. For, as Scripture says, all who wish to become rich fall into the temptations of the devil.

The beloved grew fat and kicked back: made fat and enlarged with earthly possessions, he abandoned the holy pursuits of the first institution and went after vanities and false insanities; the abundance of temporal things made him poor and miserable in spiritual riches. This lamentable deformity of our order began about two hundred years after the passing of the holy father Benedict, and grew more and more with time, until at last the harmful poison spread through the whole order. Yet this deformity did not seize the order suddenly, but over the course of many years at last occupied almost the whole of it; although almighty God never wholly deprived it of good men, but always left for himself some who had by no means bent their knees before Baal.
