Homily XXI.

It is not enough unless he who created nature and all things has given it. For whenever nature is overcome by chastity, God is manifestly working together with man. Chastity itself has a difficult beginning in the young, until the movement of unlawful thought is brought into contempt. Chastity overcomes fornication by fleeing rather than fighting, because lust is girded about with so many subtle weapons that it must be avoided before it is engaged.

Therefore, whenever the soul is touched by the movements of lust, let divine help be invoked without delay. Let no time for deliberation be given to an unclean thought, but as soon as it begins to be felt, let it be brought into contempt by the remedy of forgetfulness. With a deceitful enemy, one who always uses ambushes, no conversations at all are to be mingled; and with the unclean thoughts of the flesh no counsels are to be discussed. For unless you crush the serpent’s head, it slips away; and unless you immediately repress the movements of lust from the beginning, they grow strong.

The holy apostle of Christ, Paul, warns that one must fight against every vice, but he commands that fornication alone be fled. We carry about with us the material for victory against ourselves; therefore he wills that we flee the enemy. Chastity is nourished by fasting; by gluttony it suffers shipwreck. Therefore, if you love chastity, love fasting, so that it may have its necessary nourishment.

He who is chaste follows the diligence of the bee; and he who is truly a monk, as if adorned with every flower, is adorned with all virtues. Not everyone is chaste who does not know lust, but he who, in pure and clean flesh, for the love of Christ alone, also obtains holiness of mind. Otherwise, what does the purity of the dwelling profit, if the inhabitant is foul and unclean?

Blind paganism once had its virgins sacrificing to Vesta; but because they did not offer the chastity of their bodies to the true God, nor hold right faith in their mind, they went down into the depth of hell with their demons, whom they worshiped. For he is chaste who, for love of the Creator, restrains the flesh from every lust, and offers the mind, adorned in every way with holy virtues, to God himself. Outside faith in Jesus Christ today there is no true chastity, nor any that merits eternal salvation.

Live chastely, O monk; conduct yourself pure and holy; and as long as the spirit remains in the body, do not presume to have confidence in the chastity of clay. The food of chastity is fasting and abstinence, and in every action the strengthening of wholesome continence. But when you have attained all things, do not trust in yourself, because he fell from heaven whom we know to have eaten nothing. The mortification of the body is very necessary and useful for preserving the beauty of chastity; but however much you have advanced in both, do not trust in yourself, but always keep watch, hope in the help of the Lord, and fear.

For unless you continually and most urgently keep yourself in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overthrown. He runs in vain who presumes to guard chastity by his own strength. For unless the Lord has built the dwelling of chastity, he labors in vain who builds the wall of abstinence. And unless you have become, with all your strength, a co-worker with divine grace, you will by no means obtain the beauty of chastity.

For God will not make you chaste without your will, nor will you attain this great gift unless he comes to the aid of your weakness through his mercy. Therefore, O monk, it will be yours, with a good will, to offer the desire for chastity, and with humble confidence to await sure increase from the Lord. Apply what is yours, and the Lord’s help will not be lacking. Chastity is a gift of grace, not of nature; and to live in the flesh as though beyond the flesh belongs to angelic strength, not human.

Yet it is not difficult for a man of good will to preserve chastity, especially one who is separated from the tumult of secular people and lives solitary in the midst of monks. A monk also who, young and innocent, enters a monastery of good reform, if he continually occupies his mind in the fear of the Lord, is less attacked by lust. For experience of desire is destructive, since, as someone has truly said:

Venus wants to conquer souls, not satisfy them.
