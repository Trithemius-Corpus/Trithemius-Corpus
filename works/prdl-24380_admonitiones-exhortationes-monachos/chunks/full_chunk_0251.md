Will you be able to plead the poverty of your monastery, you who so shamefully squander so much? I am ashamed to say what many abbots are not ashamed to do: sunk, or rather maddened, in the pleasures of the flesh, they preserve nothing of monastic honor and omit nothing of worldly baseness. They think everything is permitted to them, since they consider not what is fitting, but what pleases them.

See: one, abhorring monastic quiet, seeks comfort in the world by wandering about; another, impatient of the seclusion required by the rule, goes out pompously in public; another, despising honorable discipline, sits all day as a drunken dinner-companion among frivolous people; this one, scorning the meekness of the saints, entangles himself in the lawsuits of litigants; another sits playing at the gaming-board; another foolishly dances to the drum.

What do ambitious abbots not presume to do? What do they leave untried in their pleasures? When they dine, the knees of servants are bent before them; when they pass through churches, carpets are spread beneath them; when they go forward in public, a huge crowd of servants follows. You will find, I am not lying, an abbot among them, even among those who boast in the name of reform, whose ambition is so great that he does not wish to be called inferior to a bishop; who sits at table with such display that the abundance of vessels, the variety of food and drink, and the multitude of attendants would suffice for any prince. In short, if you met him as he proceeded through a city, you would see him surrounded by such a crowd of servants that you would think not an abbot but an archbishop was passing by. If they did not pretend to be poor, but were poor, vanity would by no means pour itself out in such great solemnity. For I am certain that this crowd follows prey, not a man. Who could find prey among the poor, or would wish to?

O abbot, fugitive shepherd, ungrateful and faithless steward of the goods of your Lord, who spend the substance of the monastery so uselessly, who live voluptuously and shamefully: who will help you at the final examination? Whom will you appoint as advocate when you must render your account? Behold, you consume the goods of Saint Benedict in pleasure; from them you aid your relatives; you lavish gifts on base men; you distribute presents to your flatterers; day and night you serve gluttony and drunkenness. You eat the richest things of the earth and drink the best wines; poor monks live in want, humble brothers eat the bread of sorrow; the sheep entrusted to you are fed on worthless fare.

O vain shepherd, who neglect the Lord’s flock, who despise the necessities of your brothers, remember to whom you are going to render an account; call to mind how dreadful it is to fall into the hands of the living God, who does not regard the person of any man, who repays each one according to his works. But hear my counsel: do penance, redeem your sins with alms, indeed discharge the repayment of what is owed, so that before the eternal judge you may deserve to find mercy. Lay aside your arrogance; cast off pride; abandon vanity. Be the abbot of your brothers according to the rule; be a shepherd in solicitude, a father in charity. Feed them by word, feed them by example, feed them...
