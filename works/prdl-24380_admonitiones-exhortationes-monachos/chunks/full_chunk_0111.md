Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: “Brothers, that we, wounded by sin, may be healed, let us look upon Christ crucified. For just as those who looked upon the bronze serpent in the desert did not perish from the bites of serpents, so those who with faith and devotion look upon the death of Jesus Christ are healed from the bites of sins.”

But if you trust that you have been justified from sin, then all the more, and more fervently, must you look upon Christ hanging on the cross, without the merit of whose passion no one lives righteous on earth. For outside faith in Christ there is no righteousness; and however religiously a man may live, if he does not believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, he will in no way attain eternal salvation, but the wrath of God remains upon him, and he will necessarily perish forever. Christ’s death opened the gate of salvation, through which no one else can enter except one who faithfully believes in Christ. For this reason Christ humbly submitted himself to the condemnation of death, that he might free believers from the yoke of perpetual damnation.

O infinite charity, O mercy without measure, who among those who believe in you, eternal Majesty, would not marvel at such astounding benefits granted to us? For your Passion, Lord Jesus, is the price of our salvation, the final refuge of sinners, the singular remedy of infirm minds, the fountain, origin, and end of all good things. Therefore now, dearest brothers, let us recall and diligently bring back to memory the bitterness of the Lord’s Passion, so that, since we necessarily recognize ourselves to be sinners, we may at last be justified through the merit of that same Savior of ours.

For meditation on the Lord’s Passion surpasses all exercises of this world, if it is continued for a long time with the fervor of inward devotion. The mind of that monk which, kindled by zeal for divine love, has been accustomed daily, with tears, to keep the memory of the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ for at least a short hour, does not easily lie open to the suggestions of demons. For the heart which the love of Jesus possesses always remains inaccessible and closed to all evil thoughts.

Happy indeed is that monk, and never to be named except with honor, who meditates day and night with loving zeal on the teaching and Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ; who never forgets his Savior; who, whether he eats or drinks or does anything else, always beholds with the eyes of the mind the Lord Jesus hanging on the cross. No destructive thoughts move about in his heart; no evil or idle word proceeds from his mouth; he thinks of nothing but Jesus crucified for us; and whatever he says sounds of Jesus or of righteousness. As soon as he sees a painted image of Jesus crucified, he dissolves into tears, and, made as it were senseless by the exceeding desire of his mind, he is filled with sweetest bitterness.
