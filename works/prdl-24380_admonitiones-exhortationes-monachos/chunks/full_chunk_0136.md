Humility of monks, if it is true and perfect, begets the whole fullness of holy virtues. But if it lacks the splendor of virtues, true humility was never in the mind. For first of all it begets true obedience, and in all things a profound subjection for the love of God. For the monk who sojourns in the flesh truly humble shows himself in all things an eager disciple of our Savior Jesus Christ; for love of him, content with every lowliness and extremity, he glories in being subject, obedient in all things to his abbot. Humble obedience for the love of Christ is a great virtue: it opens heaven to the monk and by its strength shuts hell. Obedience restores the way of paradise and reforms peace between God and man.

This, however, is the true obedience of a monk: that which is rendered to the commands of superiors not from servile fear, but from the affection of inward charity; that which, persevering unto death not through fear of punishment but through love of justice, works good in gladness of mind. There are two most beautiful wings of the mind by which the monk flies up from earth to heaven: one of them is called humility; the other is named the obedience of subjection. The monk who has not been adorned with these wings has utterly lost both the name and the merit of his dignity. Nor should he be called a monk, but a liar and most deceitful apostate, whoever lives and dwells in a monastery without the obedience of humble subjection. For a monk without obedience is a monster of the devil, and he who resists the commands of his elders will inherit the punishments of hell.

The disobedience of the first man lost from the beginning the life of eternal happiness, which the obedience of Jesus Christ repaired. For he himself was made obedient to the Father for us unto death, and by his most innocent passion restored to us the happiness we had lost. Hence humble obedience is the road to salvation, as our holy lawgiver Benedict cries out in the Rule: “Listen, O son, to the precepts of the master, and incline the ear of your heart, and willingly receive the admonition of a loving father and effectively fulfill it, that by the labor of obedience you may return to him from whom you had withdrawn by the sloth of disobedience.”

Obedience is the tree of eternal salvation, which grows in the garden of monastic humility. Obedience is the dwelling place of the Holy Spirit and the quiet tomb of one’s own will. Obedience is the merit of heavenly happiness and the one sure lesson of monastic discipline. Obedience is the extermination of pride and all arrogance, and the persecutor of all vices. Obedience conquers every malice and extinguishes all the disordered pleasure of the flesh. Obedience makes the conscience pure and clean, and preserves true humility in the mind. Obedience is the remedy of the wounded soul and the foundation of all monastic observance. Obedience is the faithful guardian of all good works and the discipline of a sincere mind. Obedience is the beginning of mortification and the glorious ornament of a monk. Obedience is the preserver of peace and concord, without which the vigor of monastic discipline perishes. Obedience always directs the keenness of the mind toward God; and he who is truly mortified in spirit despises all the customary things of this world. Obedience makes the monk near to God and far removed from all stains of the soul. Obedience is the adornment of the soul and the garment of beauty, without which no one will come to the kingdom of eternal brightness. Obedience is the mother of victory, and he who knows how to be humbly subject for the honor of God will obtain the crown of martyrdom in heaven. Obedience distinguishes the monk from the world, and he who follows it with his whole heart will be great before the Most High. Obedience is the consummation of Christian perfection; in its humble workshop, the more a monk has advanced, the holier he becomes. Obedience is sure security in the hour of death, because the monk who is about to depart from here will fly away the more securely, the more ready he has been for obedience to commands. Obedience is the rewarder of the humble and the chief sign of merits. Obedience is the rule of Christian meekness and the strongest nourishment of inward charity. Obedience is the shade of chastity and the ornament of innocent purity.
