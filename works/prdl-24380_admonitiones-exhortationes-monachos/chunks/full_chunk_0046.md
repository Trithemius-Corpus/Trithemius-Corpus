The will of God in Christ Jesus in all of you. But what the manner of praying is, the Savior taught us in the Gospel, saying: “But you, when you pray, enter into your chamber, and, having shut the door, pray to your Father in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you.” And in another place he said to the Samaritan woman: “Woman, believe me, the hour will come when neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you worship the Father. But the hour comes, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father also seeks such as worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth.”

From Christ’s teaching it is plainly established that prayer is of the heart rather than lengthy speech of the mouth, because, since God is spirit, he must be worshiped only with the inward fervor of the spirit. For the prayer of the heart in the fervor of the spirit has great power, but the multiplication of words without attention of mind has no usefulness at all. Hence Saint Jerome says: “God must be prayed to not only with words, but with the heart. Therefore the chanting of five psalms with purity and serenity of heart and spiritual cheerfulness is better than the singing of the whole psalter with anxiety and sadness of heart.” And the blessed Pope Gregory says: “True petition is not in the sounds of the mouth, but in the voices of the heart, that is, in holy thoughts. For it is not words, but desires, that make stronger voices in the ears of God.”

Therefore, except for those prayers which we are bound to say either by decree of the Church or by the institution of the monastic order, in the rest, lengthiness and the multiplication of words contribute nothing, or little, to true and inward devotion of mind, because where there is no fervent desire in the love of God, an extension of words is introduced superfluously. For holy Augustine says: “Let much speaking be absent from prayer, but let fervent attention persevere; for to speak much in praying is to conduct a necessary matter with superfluous words.” Indeed, prayer comprehends many-sided realities in few words, so that the simplicity of faith might quickly learn the sufficiency of its salvation, and the wisdom of the gifted might be amazed at the depth of the mysteries.

But for those who, because of hardness of heart, lack the compunction of inward fervor, when they prostrate themselves for prayer, there is need of more prolonged speech, crying out, and tears, so that by a long stirring up of the mind they may at last strike forth within themselves the burning fire of divine love, until they rise up in the Spirit of God into the affection of holy prayer. But after the fervor of divine love and compunction has penetrated the mind, there is thereafter no need for the exercise of words; rather, the continuation of spiritual fuel is required, lest the fervor of the spiritual fire be extinguished. To this end our most holy father wished prayer in the community to be shortened, so that if anyone wished to pray in the Spirit, he could do so apart. There are, however, some who are so hard of heart that in praying they find little or no spiritual compunction; for such people liberty brings ruin, and solitude often gives birth to despair.
