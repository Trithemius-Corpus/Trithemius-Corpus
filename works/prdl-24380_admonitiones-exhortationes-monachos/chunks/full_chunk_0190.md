Sermon IV.

On the difficult way that leads to life. Sermon IV.

You have heard, dearly beloved brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ saying in today’s reading from the holy Gospel: “How narrow is the gate and confined the way that leads to life, and few are those who find it.” Is not Christ himself the way, who had said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life”? And how does he now say that it is confined and narrow, when he is the same one who had said, “Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart. For my yoke is sweet and my burden light”? What else is the yoke of Christ but the confined and narrow way to the kingdom of God? How then is it both light and narrow? Let your charity hear.

The yoke of the Lord Jesus is sweet to the good, but hard and unbearable to the reprobate. The way of Christ to life is broad and pleasant to the humble who are trained in charity, but narrow and bitter to the proud. For there is nothing so hard, nothing so terrible, nothing so difficult, that it is not easy and pleasant to one who loves God. Again, there is nothing so gentle, nothing so easy, nothing so small, that it is not heavy and burdensome to an evil will.

Therefore the Lord said, “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart.” If you wish to carry my yoke upon you, first learn the meekness and humility of my heart; for unless you are meek and humble, you will by no means be able to carry my pack as if it were a heavy weight and an unbearable burden. I am a humble way to the proud; I am a narrow gate; I do not admit the swollen with pride. Unless you despise in your mind all things that are worldly, unless you bring necessary force against the pleasures of the flesh, you cannot enter through me into the kingdom like an animal loaded with sins. I am the humble way, and I receive the meek and humble. I am the narrow door; I admit the poor in spirit and the pure in heart.

To eternal life, brothers, we make our way by the narrow way of truth; therefore we must procure in due time all the things necessary for so difficult a journey. Now there are four things especially necessary for travelers, without which the journey is dangerous and the goal is rarely reached. The first thing that we say is necessary for travelers, we believe to be knowledge of the roads. For one who tries to walk far without prior knowledge of the roads is sometimes entangled in dangerous errors. Therefore let the prudent traveler either first learn the way, or take for himself an experienced companion for the journey, lest he either fall into the hands of robbers or depart from the right path of the journey.

Therefore, since we desire to return to the homeland of the heavenly kingdom by a way exceedingly narrow and known to few, it is necessary that we have as guide and companion of the journey Jesus Christ himself, who called himself the way and the truth. Outside Christ there is no true knowledge; and whoever neglects to have Christ as the companion of his journey runs in vain and without fruit. Therefore let us learn the way of life, dearly beloved brothers, without knowledge of which [unclear].
