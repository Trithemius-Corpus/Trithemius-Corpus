The monk should always avoid every reading of vain superstition; and whatever does not contribute to knowledge of the divine Scriptures must at all times be despised by our traveler.

Seventh, we judge it very necessary for our traveler that he prudently direct both his senses and his thoughts, and guard them from every disordered desire. Our stubborn little ass must be restrained by the bridle of discipline, lest it cast its rider, the spirit, headlong into hell. He who always considers that he walks in the sight of him who sees all things preserves his conscience pure and clean with the greatest diligence. He always keeps watch in the fear of the Lord; always, in fasting and abstinence, he chastises his poor little body for love of the heavenly homeland. The monk who knows himself to be a pilgrim and a stranger in this world never forgets the heavenly homeland, because he sees nothing on the road of this present life in which his soul may take delight. For the prudent traveler pays no attention to the spectacles along the roads; nothing among passing things delights him in his pilgrimage, but at all times he sighs for the end of the journey. He always thinks of the sweet homeland, he bears it heavily that his pilgrimage is prolonged or delayed, and therefore he desires to have nothing in this world except the Lord Jesus, into whose presence he eagerly hastens to appear.

Eighth, tireless practice of divine charity is necessary for our traveler. For no one will be able to come to the heavenly homeland who departs from the path of charity. The charity of God is the way to God; if anyone has neglected to have it burning within himself, he has miserably strayed from the true road of the heavenly homeland. God, says Scripture, is charity, and he who remains in charity remains in God, and God in him. But he who is not in charity has already withdrawn far from the way of salvation, and, walking in the darkness of sins, will never come to the kingdom.

How Sin Must Be Fled. Sermon V.

You have all heard, my dearly beloved brothers, what Saint John the Apostle said about sinners: He who commits sin is of the devil, because the devil sins from the beginning. A sentence to be feared and greatly dreaded. If all who commit sin are of the devil, and we all offend in many things, how many shall we find immune from sin? Nevertheless he says immediately after: Everyone who is born of God does not commit sin, because his seed remains in him; and he cannot sin, because he is born of God. But he had said before: If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. What is the meaning of this contradiction: that none of the sons of God is without sin, and yet he who is born of God does not commit sin?
