Homily X.

Whoever of you, my brothers, desires to reach the kingdom of heaven, whoever takes care above all things to please God, let him learn willingly, for love of him, to accept the trials of temptations, and let him joyfully endure whatever adversity may befall him. For there is none of us who does not need daily purification, none who does not offend almost at every moment. Therefore, if we love our own salvation, if we ask that our offenses be forgiven us and that we be cleansed from every stain of heart, let us patiently endure whatever adversity happens to us; for the evils of tribulation, which the soul patiently bears for love of God, purify it more than the good works which it now performs of its own accord in this world.

A great occasion for suffering has been left to us in this place: need presses us heavily, poverty weighs us down, and debt also binds us. What adversity do we not experience in this monastery? What squalls and storms do we not suffer, brothers? By what hardships are we not shaken? None of you can be ignorant how desolate all the goods of this monastery were when regular observance was introduced fifteen years ago. But the tribulation of our common need rages more fiercely against me, since I must provide you with the necessities of life, and often I do not sufficiently know from where I shall pay.

Yet your patience consoles my spirit, and the hope which you have conceived in the Lord, the savior of the humble, so that I do not doubt that his mercy will be present in time; and I hear him saying to me through Isaiah the prophet: Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I chose you in the furnace of poverty. Gladly, then, will I have patience in the poverty which, God so ordaining, we now suffer, so that after death we may deserve to come to the riches of eternity. For the more, in this our pilgrimage, we have been afflicted by persecutions, poverty, need, labors, and tribulations, the greater rewards, by the Lord’s bounty, we shall obtain in the future recompense of the just.

Therefore, my brothers, patiently endure with me whatever almighty God has foreordained that we should suffer, since, as the blessed Pope Gregory says: Trust in almighty God is certain when adversity is added to a good work, and when the scourge of God is received with joy in this world, so that a more beautiful reward may be kept for the future recompense of the saints. Again he also says: Those whom the adversity of temporal life humbles take joy from the hope of an everlasting inheritance, because divine dispensation would not restrain under the rule of discipline those whom it did not see were to be saved forever. When afflicted, we lose earthly things; but by humbly sustaining affliction, we multiply heavenly goods for ourselves. For the present life is a road by which we journey toward our homeland, and therefore we are often touched in it by disturbances, lest we think that our homeland is here.

Let the monk, then, rejoice in his adversity; let him be glad when tempted, he to whom the world has been crucified. Let him trust that he belongs to the fellowship of God, who with a pure conscience always does good and receives the contrary in tribulation. For it is the truest [unclear] of divine compassion.
