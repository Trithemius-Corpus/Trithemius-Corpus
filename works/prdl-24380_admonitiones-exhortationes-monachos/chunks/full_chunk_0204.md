For the perfect consummation of nature, which is present in the angelic intellect from the beginning, will be given to the human intellect at the end. Hence it is clear that between these two created intellects there is only this difference: the angelic intellect is what it can be, while the human intellect is to receive the likeness of angelic perfection at the end, that is, after bodily death.

For when the angelic intellect was first created, without any action of its own, immovably, substantially, and by nature, it came forth full of all knowledge and perfect. And among the angels, those who adhere by love to their beginning, namely God, have been confirmed in eternal happiness; but those who, glorying in themselves, withdrew in affection from God, are forever wretched and unhappy. The human intellect, however, since it was created in the beginning in ignorance and privation of all things, must necessarily come at last, by action, motion, and art, to the knowledge of things set before it.

As long as we are in the body and are pilgrims away from the Lord, our intellect, because of its excessive commerce with the body, is confused as though placed in the darkness of ignorance, and is by no means illumined with perfect knowledge of future things. But after the soul has been separated from the body, then the intellect will rejoice in angelic perfection. Therefore we differ from the angels only insofar as we share in bodily impurity.

Fifth, God must be loved by us above all, because he first loved us and washed us from our sins in the blood of his only-begotten Son. The Creator supremely loves his creature, whom he made from nothing; it is fitting that the creature itself should supremely love its Creator, by whose goodness it was brought into being. God loves us with the greatest charity, than which no greater can be spoken or thought, and he loves us so much that he neither ought nor could love us more; for in God all things are perfect, and in the unchangeable one there is no capacity for change.

Now consider, my brothers, with what fervor of mind and with what desire of heart a man ought to love his God, by whom he is loved even without merits with the highest degree of love. Let us stretch our soul toward love of him above all things, as much as we can; for he is worthy to be loved by us with all our strength. For however much we may love God who loves us, we shall nevertheless in no way reach that measure by which he loves us. For he, as we have said, loves us supremely, and his love toward us cannot increase further, since it is certain that he loves his creature most perfectly. But however much we have loved him, we can always make further progress in loving and love more ardently until death.

O we who are weak, we who are lukewarm, we who are bound over to a muddy prison: what shall we repay to so great a lover, who surpasses us in loving by infinite degrees, since there is no comparison between the thing made and its maker?
