A city without the protection of walls is quickly captured by enemies; and a monk who does not have fear of God is easily overcome by demons.

For fear of God alone corrects minds, drives away crimes, preserves innocence, and makes a monk persevering in his purpose of holiness. From fear charity gradually arises; and when, with God’s help, it has been truly rooted in the mind, overcoming servile fear, it now casts it out. Charity is the root of all good things, because whatever good is done in this life in order to please God is governed by charity alone. Charity is the greatest of all virtues; without it all mortal deeds are nothing.

For the most holy Apostle of Christ says: If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not charity, I have become like sounding brass or a clanging cymbal. And if I have prophecy, and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so that I could move mountains, but have not charity, I am nothing. And if I distribute all my goods as food for the poor, and if I hand over my body so that I burn, but have not charity, it profits me nothing.

Consider yourselves carefully now, my brothers, and question your consciences, whether you have true charity of God, without which all other things avail nothing for salvation. For you have heard the clear judgment of the Apostle, in which he says that charity is necessary for salvation. Let no one trust in the wisdom of this world; let no one vainly exalt himself over natural gifts received; let no one presume upon bodily continence, because, if charity is lacking, the observance of the rest contributes nothing to salvation.

But lest anyone should presume a false estimation of himself, the holy Apostle set forth certain signs and works of charity, saying: Charity is patient, is kind; charity does not envy, does not act perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, does not seek what is its own, is not provoked, does not think evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices with the truth. It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Charity never fails.

Have the charity of God within you, my brothers; unless you keep it, you labor in vain by struggling. We have often told you that humility is wholly necessary for you: it is not only a great virtue, but also the nurse of the other virtues and the chief guardian of all. For the great Pope Gregory says: He who gathers virtues without humility carries dust into the wind.

For humility is the greatest virtue of monks, because they are bound to be humbler than others living their life in the world in the same measure as, by profession, they are obliged to strive and tend toward greater perfection.
