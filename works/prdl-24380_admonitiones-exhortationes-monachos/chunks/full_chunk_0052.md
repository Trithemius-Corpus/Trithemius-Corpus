In their morals they are lax and disorderly, in their words frivolous and noisy, unwilling for obedience, and always slow in good works and full of sloth. They neglect the common exercises of the order; they devote themselves to private ones according to their own will. And as long as they remain in their rashness, they do not rise up to the true love of God.

Let monks who love their own will hear what the Lord says: “I came not to do my own will, but the will of the Father who sent me.” Attend to this example, brothers; unless you live in imitation of him, you will pray without fruit. For the Lord gladly hears the prayer of the one who truly keeps his commandments. But the prayer of one who transgresses God’s command, who follows with desire the pleasures of the flesh and the consolations of the world, is not heard.

For God, just and upright, is believed to hear your prayer gladly when both mind and good life plead together. But if a man’s conduct displeases God, how will his prayer please him? For God is spirit; and the man who walks not in the spirit but in the flesh certainly draws very little near to God.

Therefore you, monk, who do not walk according to the purity of the Gospel and the rule, who love your own will and not God’s, know that you will not feel the efficacy of your prayers unless you first amend your life. For unless the manner of your life agrees with your prayer, you will not obtain what you ask. Then you cry to the Lord with a loud voice when, even with the tongue silent, you cry out by good works. Good prayers and an evil life merit no good.

Do, therefore, what a certain wise man advised: “And if you have sinned, do not add again; but also pray concerning your former sins, that they may be forgiven you.” Before prayer, prepare your soul, and do not be like a man who tempts God. Let the mind be pure and burning in love of the Lord; let the conscience be clean and strengthened in the purpose of holy conduct, and the prayer will be pleasing to divine ears. If you pray otherwise, you will obtain nothing.

He prays well who lives well; and he easily obtains his request who prays with holy works. Finally, prayer is rendered very efficacious and pleasing in the sight of God if at all times it is in harmony with the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. For Christ is God and man, the mediator between God and us, through whom we have safe access to the Father, as he himself testifies in the holy Gospel: “No one,” he says, “comes to the Father except through me.”

Therefore let there be no day on which, in prayer, you do not give thanks to Christ at least once, for by the pain of his wounds he healed the sicknesses of your soul, shed his blood for you, and by his most innocent death redeemed you, wretched man, from the destruction of everlasting death. Surely he is a most ungrateful man, and unworthy of the Christian name, who is so forgetful of such great benefits of Christ that he does not render thanks to him daily, at least once.
