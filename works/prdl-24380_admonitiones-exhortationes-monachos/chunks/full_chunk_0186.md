The twelfth perfect sign of true humility is that he does not desire to be praised by men; rather, whenever he is commended by another, he blushes, groaning with sorrow of mind. But when he hears the praises of others recited in the assembly, he rejoices in his neighbor’s praises and grows cheerful with exultant spirit. One who is perfectly humble cannot hear his own praises without bitterness of heart, because one who has placed his hope in the Lord Jesus despises all the glory of this life.

The thirteenth sign of true humility is not only to despise praises and favors in the truth of one’s mind, but also prudently to avoid those by whom one sees oneself being praised. Those who are truly humble flee their praisers; and whenever they are praised, they fall silent and answer nothing. The estimation of a great reputation is burdensome to the humble man, and the more he is proclaimed by men, the more he is confounded within himself. For true humility, when blamed, casts off the blame; when praised, it runs into danger.

The fourteenth perfect sign of monastic humility is that in corrections and detractions, if he suffers any, he conceives spiritual joy and a great increase of divine love. When injured, he is pierced with compunction and is stirred all the more to contempt of the world. Whenever he is wrongfully reproved by another, if he is humble, he judges that he has been justly corrected. For he does not consider his own innocence in the accusation, but humbly and contritely weighs how gravely he has offended God in other matters.

The fifteenth sign of perfect humility is that he never kicks back against the one correcting him, but preserves silence in all tranquility of spirit. He does not throw even the best-known faults of his reprover in his face, nor does he seek other grounds for argument. He does not complain that an insult is being done to him; he offers no excuse against the rash man; nor is he accustomed to repay words with words, but remains silent in everything as though mute.

The sixteenth sign of deep humility is that he despises all the consolations of the world and seeks no friendships among mortals. For true humility, undertaken for the love of Christ, takes delight nowhere except in Christ alone. No one can abide in the love of Christ unless he has learned to deny both his own will and the world with all its disordered pleasures. For thus the Lord says in the holy Gospel: If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, and even his own soul besides, he cannot be my disciple.

The seventeenth sign of humility is that he does not divide himself among many things, does not greatly seek men’s support and benefits, but remains humbly in his own moderate condition, or even in extremity. For benefits received from men he is not accustomed to applaud much, but shows himself grateful with few and simple words. The little gifts of the weak dissolve spirits, and often render the unwary useless in speech [unclear].
