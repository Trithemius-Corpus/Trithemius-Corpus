Abstinence with fasting is a great and necessary good for a monk, dearly beloved brothers: namely, that in mind he not sin through desire of the flesh and evil thoughts, and that in body he abstain from foods and drinks that beget wantonness of soul. For the desire of the mind is quickly kindled into lust if the body is nourished too carefully.

Considering the very great usefulness of this good, the most holy Fathers of old, who handed down fixed rules of life to monks, all with one judgment established for us a definite measure of fasting, under whose moderation the body should take only so much refreshment from foods as nature required, not as much as the excess of the will desired. Holy Scripture also commends fasting to us in many ways, and mentions it so often with praise. For the Lord God says through the mouth of his prophet Joel: “Return to me with all your heart, in fasting, lamentation, and weeping.” In the book of Judith also it is read thus: “Know that the Lord will hear your prayers, if you remain steadfast in fastings and prayers before the Lord.” Likewise we read in Tobit: “Prayer with fasting and almsgiving is good, more than storing up treasures of gold, because almsgiving delivers from death, and it is this that purges sins.” Our Lord Jesus Christ also said to his disciples in the holy Gospel: “Blessed are you who hunger now, for you shall be filled.”

But the best fasting of monks is to abstain from everything that can pollute the soul: that is, not to utter idle speech, to admit no vain, useless, or foul thoughts, to commit nothing unbecoming to a servant of God, and to omit nothing that belongs to the perfection of righteousness. For unless the mind fasts from vices and sins, bodily abstinence will profit nothing toward the soul’s salvation. Hence the holy priest Jerome says: “Your abstinence and fasting are the more pleasing to God, the more they are offered together with holy conduct, so that things which in others are coverings for vices may in you become ornaments of virtues.” For what good is it for the body to be made thin by abstinence, if the soul swells with pride?
