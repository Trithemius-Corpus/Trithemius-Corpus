Do what the Gospel says. Hear what it commands, and consider how you ought to serve as soldiers in the observance of regular discipline.

“He who ministers to me,” he says, “let him follow me.” Listen, monk; attend, you who bear the cloistered warfare. Christ, whom you serve as a soldier, says this to you; the heavenly Emperor, whom you serve at the altar, addresses you. If you wish to be my minister, you must follow the virtue of patience and humility which you have learned from me. For I have given you an example, O my soldiers, that as I have done, so you also should do likewise.

Charity, O monk, compelled me to be born as man in the world from a virgin; may that same charity, for love of me, always preserve you pure and humble in the cloister. The work of human salvation which the Father entrusted to me, I strove always to accomplish for your sake with unceasing meditation; and you, if you love me, take care to preserve inviolate until death the holy purpose of monastic life which you have undertaken for your sins.

I was always voluntarily subject and obedient to my heavenly Father in all things, even to the most bitter death of the cross; you also, if you wish to come to me, must always obey humbly in all things, according to the Rule, your abbot, who has been set in my place. I never transgressed in anything the commandment which my Father gave me; and you, unless you keep inviolate until death the three principal vows of monastic institution, are in no way to be numbered among my ministers and soldiers.

Though I was rich, for your salvation I became poor and needy; but you, unless for love of me you despise and leave behind all things, cannot be my disciple. Poverty, hunger, thirst, heat, cold, and continual labors in the world I endured for your sake; and unless in these things, according to your Rule and to the best of your strength, you have followed me patiently and willingly, you certainly will be neither my minister, nor my soldier, nor my monk. For in your patience you will possess your soul.

In this world I desired nothing, possessed nothing, loved nothing earthly, but, going about poor, humble, and meek, I subjected myself most perfectly and always in all things to my Father’s will, ready to receive injuries and blows. But you, O cloistered monk, who profess the monastic Rule under an abbot or prior, if from now on you follow the desires of your flesh, if you possess private property or anything of your own in the monastery, if you love anything besides me, if you despise the commands of your superior, if you do not persevere in serving me as a soldier in humility, know for certain that I will not hold you as a soldier, nor count you as a monk, minister, or disciple, but, judging you to be a fugitive, an apostate, faithless, a liar, a mocker, and a perjured unbeliever, I will condemn you without mercy forever, as you have deserved, with my betrayer Judas and the [unclear] angels.
