Penance for mortals cleanses filthy souls and calls minds back from the destruction destined for them. Hence Saint Augustine says: Penance heals sicknesses, cures lepers, raises the dead, increases holiness, preserves grace. Penance restores walking to the lame, sight to the blind, abundance to the barren, firmness to the weak; it drives away vices, adorns virtues, fortifies and strengthens the mind.

But that is true penance, brothers, whose necessary parts truly commend it as a whole: where contrition pierces the mind; where humble and pure confession discloses hidden sin; where obedience makes satisfaction according to its powers; where the penitent so grieves for past things that he truly guards himself at all times against things to come. True penance is that which the love of God adorns with groans and tears, where the sinner grieves more over the offense against God than he fears the torment of hell.

And who among us would not grieve, most beloved brothers? Who among the monks would not groan that he has sinned against the law of the Most High, from whom we have not only life, but also await the promise of eternal happiness? Woe to us, brothers, if in this life we have neglected to do true penance, for the continual practice of which we became monks. For our way of life ought to be occupied in Jesus Christ alone, crucified, and altogether alien to the consolations of the world.

Worldly consolations do not befit you, O monk; not pleasures, not delights, not laughter, not guffaws, not any comfort of the flesh, but sorrow and bitterness with tears. If you do not continually give yourself to these, you have lost the name of monk. It is not lawful for you to rejoice with the world, you who are dead to the world; nor ought you to seek any consolations of the present life, you who promised the Lord to serve in solitude according to the monastic rule. It is your part, monk, always and everywhere to despise all the joys of this world for the love of God, and to refuse to undergo no adversity or hardship for the honor of your Savior. For so the canon admonishes you, so the monastic institution commands, so the necessary reasoning of salvation advises.

You are commanded to be content with every vileness and extremity, and always fearful toward all things. But I see very many of you perverting the rule of salvation and living in the monastery otherwise than you ought. Monks now do not fear to seek delights in monasteries, though they came from the world to conversion poor and beggars. For the things they could not have outside, if they are now lacking in the monastery, they become rebellious against humility, as though they had suffered a most serious insult. Woe to the monks whom not the love of God but poverty has made cloistered, whose god is the belly, and who think of nothing less than poverty.

It is not for you, O monks, henceforth to seek delights in the monastery, which the rule commands you wholly to renounce. It befits you to despise all the pleasures of the present life and to love absolutely nothing in this world except God. For your whole way of life ought always to be bound to divine services, you who promised to live by the spirit and not according to the flesh. He who is a true [unclear] never lives without penance.
