Hebrews 1: concerning him. Therefore faith is the origin of all virtues, from which all pursuits of salvation arise. True and right faith in God begets fear of the Lord, since if we believe God to be just, we shall rightly confess that he is to be feared. Right faith produces hope and confidence, because, when we believe God to be merciful, we at once hope that we can obtain salvation from his goodness. Faith begets charity, because if we believe God to be the supreme good, we undoubtedly love him. For who would not love what he does not doubt to be supremely good?

Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the proof of things not seen, and the one foundation of our salvation. Faith joins the soul to God and, in the midst of charity, makes it the friend of the eternal King. For the most holy bishop Augustine says: There are no greater riches, no treasures, no honors, no greater substance of this world, than the catholic faith, which saves sinful men, enlightens the blind, heals the sick, baptizes catechumens, justifies the faithful, restores penitents, increases the just, crowns martyrs, preserves virgins, widows, and married people in chaste modesty, ordains clerics, consecrates priests, and places believers with the holy angels in the eternal inheritance of heaven.

But where sound faith is not present, there can be no justice, because the just man lives by faith; and whoever lacks the merit of justice will have no share with Christ. Faith is the beginning of human salvation; without it no one can reach the number of the children of God or possess any part in the kingdom of God. Faith is the foundation of all virtues: whatever is done outside faith is without doubt built for Gehenna. Christian faith is a most beautiful lamp, enlightening the soul and, through observance of the commandments, leading it to eternal happiness.

Faith first brings the mind back to the knowledge of God; afterward it gives the precepts of living for obedience. And when a person has kept God’s commandments, hope of obtaining salvation is raised up; then charity is strengthened in the heart. Faith gives knowledge to the soul; knowledge brings forth action in charity; and to these two blessedness is promised in eternity. One must take the greatest care that there be no error in knowledge, because when right faith is corrupted by the falsehood of heresies, no action brings one to the harbor of salvation. Therefore let there be rectitude and truth in faith, justice and sincerity in action, and no iniquity will have dominion.

For faith without works is dead and merits nothing toward the salvation of eternal happiness. To have faith without the works of charity is to imitate the demons, who, though they are wholly estranged from all charity, nevertheless both believe that God is just and tremble. Hence the Lord speaks through the prophet Isaiah, and afterward through himself in the holy Gospel, concerning believers who do not show the works of charity, saying: This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Whoever praises Christ with his mouth without works is like a man who doubts in faith. For if he truly believed, he would surely fear God. Faith
