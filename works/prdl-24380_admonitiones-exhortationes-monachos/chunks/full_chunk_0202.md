But the prudent and devout man is not moved by the sloth of the proud, since he proves and understands that a certain effective power of exhortation lies in the histories of the saints. For, to confess meanwhile what is true about myself, I am stirred more to devotion of inward fervor by the life of one saint than by many curious sermons, because, as the most holy Pope Leo says, examples are stronger than words, and it is more to teach by deed than by voice.

But just as not all things please everyone, so not all things are suited to everyone. We advise each person to read more often and more eagerly that by whose reading his mind is stirred to greater progress.

The eighth thing that restrains a man from sin is charity, concerning which the holy Apostle says: Charity is patient, is kind; it does not act perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, does not seek the things that are its own, is not provoked, does not think evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth. It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Charity never fails.

He who loves God flees sin; but he who is the slave of crimes certainly does not love God. The lover of God does not commit sin, and he who truly loves his Lord and always beholds him as present does not work iniquity. God is the highest and unchangeable good; out of love for him sin must both be fled and detested, because he who fears to sin only from fear of punishments does not rise to the rewards of charity. For a slave does not sin from fear of blows; but a son withdraws from evil out of love for his parent, lest he offend his father’s heart.

Let us beware of sin, my brothers, and with as much purity of soul as we can let us live in the sight of the Lord, because just as deadly poison, when taken, kills the body, so sin kills the soul of the offender. Hence the wise man admonishes us in Ecclesiasticus, saying: Flee sins as from the face of a serpent, because if you approach them, they will take hold of you. The teeth of a lion are the teeth of sin, killing the souls of men. Like a two-edged sword is the iniquity of man; for its wounds there is no healing. In Ezekiel too it is said: The soul that has sinned, that soul shall die.

With all care we must always keep watch, so that we may not, through the mediation of sin, be separated from the Lord. Nothing is stronger than perfect charity toward God for overcoming every sin, because, as the most holy evangelist John says, everyone who is born of God does not commit sin; but he who sins does not imitate God, but the devil, the reward of sin. Let us love God, and sin will not rule in us; let us love Christ, and we shall do nothing contrary to our salvation. Nothing is better, nothing stronger against sin, nothing safer, than to love God, who first loved us and washed us from all sins in the blood of his only-begotten Son.
