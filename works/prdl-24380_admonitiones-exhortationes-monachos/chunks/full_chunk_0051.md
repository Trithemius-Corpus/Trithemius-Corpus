That one must pray without ceasing.

...that devout and fervent prayer cannot obtain. But then, when we pray, we receive from the Lord confidence of obtaining what we ask, when no perversity of life contradicts our prayers. For the Lord is the hearer of a righteous prayer: that is, when integrity of life commends the prayer, and no guilt of conscience makes the one praying hesitate. Friendship will not stand between those who are unlike, and between God and a sinner there can be no communion.

If you wish to be heard when you pray, take care first of all that you do not differ from the will of God. Live in spirit most like God, and you will suffer no rejection in prayer. Indeed, purity of conscience in the fervor of divine love makes a person like God in understanding; likeness causes love, love introduces familiarity, familiarity confidence, and confidence easily obtains the granting of the petition. For just as union does not occur between unlike things, so between God and an evil person prayer avails nothing.

Therefore, whoever among you desires to obtain the effect of his prayers should first cleanse the dwelling of his heart from every fault through true repentance; then he should strive to inflame himself by the exercise of holy compunction; afterward he should continually feed his mind in the pursuit of the love of God, so that at no later time he interrupts the good that he begins. But afterward, when, with the desire of divine love always increasing in him, he has brought the practice of holy purity by long effort into habit, and his mind has conceived the necessary firmness in its good purpose, then for the first time a likeness of divine communion is gradually introduced, and prayer is formed into its proper state.

For the more the mind, stirred up in the love of God, has advanced, the more quickly prayer will obtain from the Lord what it asks. For the love of God alone makes prayer powerful; and the more fervently the mind burns in it, the more easily it will obtain the effect of its petitions. For if you love much, you can do much. For God is charity, and he who abides in charity abides in God, and God in him. But in the one in whom God abides, nothing is stronger than he, except God alone.

There are among you certain men weak and feeble in soul, who already think themselves strong and perfect in the way of the Lord; theirs is a rare and momentary devotion, and they boast that without the wedding garment they have come near to the kingdom of heaven, though in fact they still lie far below. To these I say again what I have often said: no one suddenly becomes the highest, and unless a monk has subdued all his animal passions by the mortification of the flesh, he does not reach the most lofty mountain of divine contemplation.

For whoever, placed in a monastery under authority and obedience, follows his own will against monastic discipline is not worthy to be heard by the Lord in his prayers. But those of whom we speak are great and wise in their own eyes; loving their own will, they grumble at everything, and whatever they themselves have not arranged they think badly done.
