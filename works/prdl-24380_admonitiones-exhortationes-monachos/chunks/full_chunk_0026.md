Hear what Solomon says to you in Proverbs: “Where there is no knowledge of the soul, there is no good, and a man’s folly trips up his steps.” Of you also, O most foolish children of darkness, that saying of Saint Jerome is understood: that ignorance is no longer excusable where it is known what the thing is that is not known. But you must take still greater care lest in the end you be counted with those who said to God, “Depart from us; we do not want the way of your sciences.”

You misunderstand Solomon’s word, thinking it was spoken simply about ignorance. “He who walks simply,” without any deception of his neighbor, walks confidently in keeping the commandments of God; but he who corrupts his ways cannot remain hidden, but will be made manifest. Hence again he says, “The lips of the righteous will instruct many; but those who are untaught will die in poverty of heart.”

What is more pitiable than an untaught priest, who, even if he does not have the office of preaching, is nevertheless, by reason of the order he has received, bound to knowledge of the scriptures? For God wills that every man fulfill his holy will, which no one who is ignorant of it is able to do. Therefore the Lord first commands that you learn what is pleasing to him, and secondly that you do it. How then will you think ignorance of the law excusable, through which you have been made transgressors of the first will of God?

Why do you think almighty God handed over to men the books of his scriptures? Was it so that they should be read by them and understood, or so that, unread, they should be despised? Certainly the scriptures of God’s commandments were given to mortals so that they might read them, and afterward so that they might fulfill them in deed. But you who with deliberate mind wish to be ignorant of God’s scriptures, how can you do his will, which they make known? Or how will you be able to be doers of the law, when you have not first been hearers? For it can happen that one who knows the will of God either observes it or neglects it; but it cannot happen that one who is ignorant of it fulfills it. Indeed, someone will more easily fail to do the good that he knows than fulfill in deed what he does not know. No one does the good that he does not know.

Saint Augustine says in a certain place concerning the ignorant: “Not everyone who is ignorant is free from guilt. For that ignorant man can be excused from punishment who did not find what he might learn. But those cannot be pardoned who, having someone from whom they might learn, did not apply themselves to instruction.” Hence Saint Leo the Pope also says: “If ignorance seems intolerable in laypeople, how much more in those who preside is it neither worthy of excuse nor of pardon?”

Therefore, brothers, ignorance of the divine scriptures does not make you excused before God, but doubly guilty, since, in order that you may not have to do God’s will, you desire to be utterly ignorant of it.
