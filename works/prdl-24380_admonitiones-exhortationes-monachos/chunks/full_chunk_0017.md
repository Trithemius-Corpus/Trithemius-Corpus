Let us consider, then, brothers, how necessary for salvation is faith formed by charity; without it, as we fight in this warfare, neither victory is granted nor confidence preserved. For faith is the shield with which we most easily overcome the enemy of our salvation, if we add to it the handle of divine love. For blessed Pope Gregory says: We are truly faithful when we fulfill in deeds what we promise in words.

In this armor, since faith is the foundation of all good works, it ought rightly to precede all our actions, so that the mind may be able to come to love. For unless faith is first held, one in no way reaches spiritual love. Charity does not precede faith; rather, faith precedes charity, because no one can love or hope for as good what he refuses to believe. Since, then, you now faithfully believe what it also commands, you have set the foundation of the spiritual building upon firm rock; now you must also do without delay the other things that remain for perfection.

Love peace, therefore, and truth, so that in the faith of Christ, as true soldiers, you may be able at all times to remain steadfast and most firm. For, as Scripture bears witness, truth is great and stronger than all things, and there is nothing unjust with it. He who loves truth is a true disciple of Christ and loves Christ himself, who says in the Gospel: I am the way, the truth, and the life. All the ways of the Lord also are mercy and truth; from this truth Satan fell through pride, because he did not remain in everlasting truth.

Therefore the holy apostle Paul commands the soldiers of the Lord to gird themselves with truth; in this he wished to show the state of perfection toward which cloistered men ought especially to strive. For what else is it to love truth than for a man to be what he is commanded to be, and so to live according to the rule of justice, true and solid, such as holy perfection requires of nature through grace?

Hence Firmianus Lactantius says: He who devotes himself to truth casts away pleasures hostile to it and full of anxiety, which bind the soul to themselves and do not allow it to be free. He always prefers true things to false, eternal things to brief, useful things to pleasant. Let nothing be pleasing to the sight except what can be done justly, piously, and religiously; nothing sweet to the hearing except what makes the soul better.

Thus soldiers have their loins girded in truth when, having despised the enticements of the flesh and the vanities of the world for the love of God, they strive with all their strength so to conduct themselves and live that they may be able in all things to please the highest truth, which is God. After this the soldier of Christ is commanded to put on the breastplate of justice, without which no one will be able to serve the Lord rightly and fruitfully. Justice, indeed, is to preserve and distribute to each person what is owed to him according to law or nature. And this is the most excellent of all virtues; without it there can be no piety, no religion, no holiness, and no discernment of good and greater things.
