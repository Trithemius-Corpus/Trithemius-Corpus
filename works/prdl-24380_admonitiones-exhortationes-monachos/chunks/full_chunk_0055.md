For the house is the conscience, of which Scripture says: Unless you steadfastly keep yourself in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown, that is, your conscience. This house is very fragile, and is quickly overthrown unless it is fortified by the continual exercise of the fear of the Lord. For unless the Lord guards the house, he who guards it keeps watch in vain. Therefore the house of conscience must be fortified by the fear of the Lord.

The senses also must be most carefully restrained; for it is not enough for a man to guard his thoughts if he does not turn his ears and eyes away from the occasion of evil. Therefore, if you wish to be a prudent guardian of your thoughts and a true gatherer of your heart in the holiness of the Lord, bury your ears with the thorns of compunction and fear of God, lest they hear evil, and turn away your eyes, lest through sight they bring in sin. What it is not lawful for you to desire, do not look upon with desire; and what it is not expedient to have, do not think about.

Many have perished through the service of the senses, and those who go on incautiously looking at external things have very often shamefully lost their inner goods. He who does not fear danger is surely already near destruction; and he who presumes upon his own virtue runs hastily toward the ruin of sin. He who flashes his eye at the gleam of lightning will be blinded for a time; and he who contemplates the beauty of a woman will not be free from danger.

I praise the prudence of that monk who is alone in the midst of crowds and sets a censor over his senses, lest he admit any evil to the mistress. He stations guards by the gate, who with sword and club drive back those rushing in disorderly. For the holy fear of the Lord never deserts the mind of a wise man, but, like a strong and watchful fighter always standing at the prince’s side, so it guards the mistress of the heart. He turns his ears away from all scurrility of words, and closes his eyes lest they see evil. He always walks according to the twelfth degree of humility, with his gaze fixed on the ground, praying with the prophet to the Lord: Turn away my eyes, lest they see vanity; in your way give me life, O Lord.

Thus I admonish you, brothers: direct your senses in the fear of the Lord, so that you may always be able to keep the dwelling of the heart clean and inviolate. Remember the Scripture that says: Be cleansed, you who carry the vessels of the Lord. Consider, I beg you, with how great a cleanness and purity of mind you ought to shine, you who not only, by reason of the priestly order, carry the vessels of the Lord, but, what is more, are yourselves also vessels of the Lord.
