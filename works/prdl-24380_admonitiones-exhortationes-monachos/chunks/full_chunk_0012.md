When I come, have no doubt, I shall judge. But if you have persevered faithfully in your holy purpose even unto death; if you have kept inviolate, under the authority of my vicar whom I have appointed as your shepherd, the will which you solemnly consecrated to me; if, for love of my name, you have observed obedience, poverty, and chastity with humility to the end; if you have always striven to please me alone in all your actions; if you have taken care to follow me without ceasing along the arduous path of the virtues, know, know most certainly and without doubt, that, as one made from a servant into a most dear friend, I shall have you as my soldier, to be crowned with me in my kingdom; I shall love you, I shall cherish you, I shall set you in glory forever in the midst of my saints.

Hear me now, my servant: you who, out of reverence and honor for my name, have enrolled yourself in the knighthood of monks, follow the footprints of my feet with a great and perfect heart, just as Benedict, that former teacher of yours and my friend, taught you and strove with all his efforts to imitate. The enticements of the flesh will not deceive you, O brave soldier. Let not the vain and ruinous dealings of this world seduce your mind, which has been dedicated to me. Never yield to the exceedingly deceitful suggestions of the unclean spirit, who rages wholly for your destruction; but remember that your dwelling in this age is very brief, and that death stands before the doors, which no mortal has ever been able to escape.

Let those last things which you must necessarily undergo always remain in your memory; and from the very swift passing of those pleasures of the flesh which have gone by, and from the thought of punishments to come, take up for yourself a necessary horror and contempt of this sojourn. Just as a brief labor endured for me in the world with patience and charity prepares eternal happiness for my soldiers, so the desires and pleasures of the flesh, which suddenly pass away, plunge the soul of the sinful man down to hell, to be tormented without end.

Therefore let all your desires be directed to me, monk, you who serve me and follow me along the arduous path of regular observance. Offer me your heart, if you desire happiness; seek me alone, if you love your own life and salvation. When you have loved me, you will be tranquil and holy; when you have followed me, you are blessed and glorious without end.

What would you do, my brothers, if it befell you to hear these words which we have spoken from the mouth of our Savior and king as he spoke? What, I ask, would you do? I certainly believe there would be great compunction in your hearts if you heard Christ speaking in person. But we, bearing the place of Christ in the monastery, speak the words which he has given, and you do not heed them? We proclaim to you the necessary training-ground of salvation, and you show no signs of compunction?

If you do not know what cause there has been for saying these things, now, I beg you, understand. I see that some of you shrink from sacred readings, are given over to vanities, and care for nothing less than the perfection of spiritual warfare; their god is the belly, and their glory is in confusion.
