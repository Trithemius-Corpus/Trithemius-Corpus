Flee idleness, O cloistered ones, and shun gluttony and fullness of the belly with all your strength, because the monk who fills his belly with food and drink gives the demon of fornication material for doing harm. Take on true contrition of heart, and restrain the unlawful motions of the flesh through the tears of prayer. For no one is able rightly to conquer his body unless he has first taken up the goad of contrition. But he cannot attain the grace of holy contrition who has not perfectly despised and denied himself. Yet whoever has perfectly denied himself for the love of Christ, already overcoming the assault of the flesh, will more easily obtain the grace of contrition. For one who has been mortified in will desires nothing carnal, nothing shameful. But which of us, brother, is there who would dare to say that he has reached this grace of mortification? Which of us does not know himself to be weak? We run in the arena of spiritual warfare, and in daily struggle we fight against vices. We have received no assurance of peace, even if at times we have peace. The more familiar to us the weakness of the flesh is, the harder are the battles of chastity. Therefore we must be especially watchful, since we carry about with us a familiar enemy and endure an evil impulse as though it were domestic. Therefore, as long as we live in this mortality, let us always fear the corrupter of chastity. Let us beware of everything that stirs up lust, and as much as we can, let us flee sloth and idleness. For the strongest enemies of true chastity are fullness of bread and idleness; unless they are driven far away by abstinence and by the work of salutary exercise, they threaten chastity with imminent shipwreck. Guard your hearts with every care, my dearly beloved brothers, and always keep watch in mind against your adversary, who never sleeps. Arm yourselves with the breastplate of perfect faith in Jesus Christ, so that in deed and in belief you may be true Christians. Always be of one mind in brotherly charity, and without pretense be united in the true observance of regular discipline. Love fasting and continence; always pray without ceasing. Flee idleness as a deadly plague of the soul, and continually exercise yourselves in holy works. Love silence, and do not flee monastic solitude, if you desire to obtain true purity of mind and body. Keep continual guard over your eyes, ears, and speech, lest death enter the soul through the windows. Flee conversations with women, and avoid even the touch of holy women as a blazing fire. No one safely looks willingly at what, when desired, brings the sting of death. Always behold with the faithful eyes of the mind, through love, our Lord Jesus Christ dying for you on the cross; occupy yourselves each day for your salvation in his evangelical teaching; humbly give thanks for his passion and death alike. If with continued perseverance and in true simplicity of heart you do these things until death, we trust that you will be rewarded with the fellowship of the saints.
