Therefore, as the holy bishop John says, we have been baptized into one body of the Church, so that we may not be dissolved, but may preserve forever, in that harmony which exists toward one another and in the friendship of one body, charity and mutual concord. Just as no one outside the unity of the catholic Church can obtain salvation, so no one is believed able to be saved without brotherly charity.

Charity, therefore, is necessary for you before all things for salvation; without it the remaining works of the virtues are utterly fruitless. For there can be nothing good, no saving merit for life, which has not taken its origin from it. Charity is a certain short path to virtue; if anyone enters upon it by the right track of the journey, he will need no direction from a guide. Charity, pure, simple, and upright, is not accustomed to harm one’s neighbor, nor to inflict evil on anyone. Charity is accustomed to bestow on all the affection which it gives to its own advantages; and whatever it hopes will conduce to its own salvation, it desires this to be common to all people in Christ Jesus. Charity exercises the office of love without any disturbance, and although it loves all people greatly, nevertheless for no one is it provoked to a zeal of bitterness. It is always solid, always constant, always stable and firm, because it is neither touched by passions nor shaken by movements of unrest. It shows itself common to all, and so bestows its love on each one that nevertheless it seems nearest to all. Charity does not neglect itself in those things which pertain to the reason of the mind and soul; but in those things which regard the advantage of the body, it often places the causes of others before its own benefits. Great is the command of true charity, which compels a person to make another’s disadvantage his own.

You, therefore, my brothers, who have solemnly girded yourselves for the warfare of Christ, who through the arduous way of obedience pant after eternal life, if you do not wish to run in vain in the arena of so laborious a contest, take care, we exhort you, before all things always to keep brotherly charity inviolate among you. For unless you have sincere and perfect charity toward one another, you labor without fruit in regular observance. Abstinence from eating meat will contribute nothing to the salvation of your souls; the renunciation of all private property will profit nothing toward the adoption of eternal happiness, if brotherly love does not hold a place in your hearts. Therefore, desiring to be saved with Christ, love one another in truth, and, as Christ loved you, so we beg you, love one another. Whatever each one of you desires to be done to himself, let him bestow that very thing on his brother without envy, for the love of God, at all times. Let divine love overcome in you all quarrels of the flesh and every kind of contention; and let no pernicious dissension of the flesh find a place where the fullness of brotherly charity ought continually to dwell. For if all of you in the monastery live according to the law of Christian instruction, you necessarily always abstain from injuring one another. But if through contentions and wrongs you injure one another, you are certainly neither monks nor true Christians.

For it befits cloistered men and all monks to be outstanding in the observance of charity, and to be far removed from the rancor of envy and hatred. Therefore let the monk be stable in his purpose of holiness even to death; let him be faithful in the service of God, so that he may both sweetly burn in love of the Creator and never depart from purity of conscience. For whoever perfectly loves the Lord God with his whole heart both preserves his conscience clean from every pollution of sin and always shows pure and sincere charity to his neighbor. But the one whom either envy burns or hatred stirs up against his brother, it is clear that he neither truly loves God nor has a conscience pure from sin. Therefore everyone who pursues his brother with hatred and indignation is neither a true monk nor worthy of the name of Christian, but by merit and conduct a servant and companion of demons.

Therefore let hatreds depart far from this holy congregation of yours; let there be no contests of pernicious contention among you; let no zeal of bitterness be found here; let no speech of displeasing pride be heard. Let the concord of brotherly unity flourish here continually; let the innocence of Christian purity adorn the consciences of each one, so that we may live in this brief time in such a way that after death we may be glorified with Christ in eternity.

On the Charity of Man toward God. Homily XXIII.
