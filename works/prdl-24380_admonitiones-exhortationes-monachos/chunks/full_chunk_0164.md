Homily XXV.

He does not fear the poverty of this life, because with his whole desire he longs for eternal riches. Meditation on death endures all the adversities of this world most patiently for love of Christ, because it hopes for nothing other than the recompense from above. Meditation on death makes the monk humble and obedient in all the commandments of righteousness. Meditation on death makes the monk quiet, stable in the congregation, and in good concord with everyone in Christ. Meditation on death makes the monk calm and peaceable, and tranquil without any disturbance of mind. Meditation on death exterminates all dissoluteness and levity, and preserves the monk mature and healthfully sorrowful in all things. Meditation on death makes the monk solitary and continually devoted to holy prayers.

Meditation on death hastens penitence and brings in true compunction. Meditation on death withdraws desire from every vanity, and keeps the conscience serene and most pure. Meditation on death makes a man despise all the comforts of this world, and prepare himself daily for his departure. Meditation on death teaches him to flee idleness, and makes a man keep continual watch in the fear of the Lord. Meditation on death does injury to no one, because it always fears the strict accounting of judgment. Meditation on death mitigates anger and all disordered impulses, because, while a man considers that he will soon die, nothing external disturbs his mind. Meditation on death makes the heart contrite and humble, because attentive remembrance of things to come permits no one to be proud.

Continual meditation on death makes one’s departure safe at the end, because it has made the meditator prepared over a long period of time. Meditation on death is especially fitting for cloistered men and monks, whom the renunciation of all earthly things and the very profession of the rule have already made dead and buried to the world. Meditation on death makes the monk run prudently in the course of spiritual warfare, and renew daily the purpose of holy fervor. Continual meditation on death is very useful for cloistered men, because it both powerfully restrains the vices of the flesh and grants no entrance of the mind to perverse thoughts.

For the holy bishop Augustine says: Fear of future death necessarily shakes the mind, and, like a nail of the flesh, fastens all the motions of pride to the wood of the cross. And the holy champion of the Church, Jerome, says: Nothing will help you as much toward temperance in all things as frequent thought of this brief life, whose end is certain, while nothing is more uncertain than its hour. Therefore meditation on death corrects all intemperance, and arranges the habits of the one who meditates in stable order with beautiful variety. Meditation on death gives a man true wisdom, and perfects the highest philosophy among cloistered men. For Plato too held the opinion that the whole life of the wise is meditation on death, because we are all born on this condition: that from here we must depart again through death.
