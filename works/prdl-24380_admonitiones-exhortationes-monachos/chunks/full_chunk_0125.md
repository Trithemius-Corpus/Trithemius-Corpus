Therefore give an account of your stewardship. What then will you answer, O lazy and idle monk, who now spends the present acceptable time so fruitlessly, and despises living in true monastic purity according to the rule? What, I ask, will you answer? Will you deny the works of wickedness that you have committed, intending by deceit to show good works that you now disdain to perform, so that, deceiving the Judge by a lie, you may thus escape the punishment due to your demerits?

But you must not be ignorant that everything you have done, said, or thought throughout your whole life, good and evil alike, is manifest in the sight of the Judge, and nothing can be hidden from him who alone knows all things. For all things are written in the book of God, and your conscience will bear open testimony against you concerning all the evils you have done, so that not even the smallest thought will be able to lie hidden. For the Scripture of the prophets says to God in Psalm 75: “For the thought of man shall confess to you.”

You are to be judged before the tribunal of the all-knowing one, before whose terrible judgment every creature will stand in great fear and trembling, where no excuse whatever is admitted, but each person will receive the sentence due to his merits, with appeal set aside. Thousands of thousands and ten hundred thousand holy spirits will stand beside him, and do you trust that you can deceive the Judge by a lie?

But hear my counsel, and, rising from your destructive inertia, at last wake up, and by true repentance flee in time from justice to the mercy of God. Do not put it off from day to day, because it is extremely dangerous for one remaining in sins to be confident, and to reject the remedy of salvation that is offered. Remember that you are going to die, and when you do not expect it, you will be summoned to judgment.

Consider, I beg you, how the uncertain time of life slips away, and how quickly all mortal things decay. Nothing among human affairs is stable, nothing fixed, nothing firm; rather all things that we see are corruptible and change with time. Consider also where our brothers Valentinus, Martinus, and Ioannes are, who in the present year were living with us healthy, safe, and joyful; they spoke with us, ate, drank, and labored together with us, thinking of nothing less than that they would die this year. Behold, when they did not think it, death came, snatched them away, and set their souls before the tribunal of the dreadful Judge, who rendered to each one according to the works of his hands.

Behold, now learn from the example of your brothers that you too are going to die, and perhaps you will pass from this world much sooner than you can estimate. Come to your senses, wretched man, while you still can; do penance in the time of your health, while the Judge promises mercy, and do not defer today’s good into tomorrow’s uncertainty. Rise up, even now, late though it is, at last from the sloth of your more negligent past way of life, in which until now you have shamefully rotted away; cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin; wipe away with tears the evil you have committed, and from now on live innocently, as befits a monk.
