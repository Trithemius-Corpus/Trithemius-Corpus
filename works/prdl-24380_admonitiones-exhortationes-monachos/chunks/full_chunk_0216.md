Those who withdraw from evil do good, but for this reason: because they trust that they will receive the kingdom of God for their merits. Those who serve God for the sake of reward are not in the state of perfection, but, together with the weak, still stand far from the summit of holiness in lower things. Whoever truly loves God with a pure mind serves and waits upon him neither from fear of punishments nor with a view to reward, but only from love of him. And therefore all the degrees set forth above are proposed to beginners, so that, ascending gradually through them, they may at last be joined to the perfect.

It is good to withdraw from evil through fear of punishments; it is good to do good from love of reward; and although this is by no means sufficient for perfection, nevertheless it provides a beginning and a way of advancing for those who desire to make progress. For servile fear, if continued, begets filial and chaste fear; from its spring perfect charity flows forth. But after charity has once kindled the mind, it thereafter casts out every servile fear and dread.

The fourth degree strives to show the way of perfection to those ascending, and therefore it is rightly judged greater than those previously described. Whatever good is not seen in them is possessed more perfectly in this one. For those who ascend this fourth degree guard themselves with all zeal not only from mortal sins but also from venial sins, as far as is possible for them; they apply themselves most diligently to good and holy works, and above all desire to please God. In one respect they surpass those who came before: they turn aside from evil neither from fear of punishments, nor do they perform good from hope of reward, but they do all things for the love of God alone, and seek nothing other than the honor of God.

This is the way proposed to those striving toward perfection; this is the true rule of holy justice: that we love God with a pure mind above all things, and do all our works for his honor. For the true lover of God neither fears punishment nor thinks of reward, but, walking in largeness of heart, commends his will to the divine ordering. For God, because he is supremely good, is to be loved not for the reward of the heavenly kingdom, nor for anything else, but for his own sake; and in every good work he must always be held principally in one’s intention.

Hasten, my dearest brothers, to ascend this degree of the spiritual ladder as quickly as possible, so that you may one day be able to reach the summit of perfection. For if you, who have been placed under the obedience of monastic perfection, have advanced to this fourth degree, you will then ascend the fifth without difficulty. For those who are placed in the fourth degree do the good works of justice by their own will, because they have not submitted themselves by vow or profession to monastic constitutions. And in this one respect they are inferior to those who follow: because a good work is so much the more acceptable to God, the more it is commended by the merit of obedience.

Therefore those ascend the fifth degree who guard themselves with all their strength from sins and, placed under obedience, always strong and steadfast, persevere in the purpose of holiness. They love God with a pure mind above all things.
