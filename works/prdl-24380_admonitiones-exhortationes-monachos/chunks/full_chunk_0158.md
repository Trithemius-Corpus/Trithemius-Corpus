Through it a man is carried away from all the consolations of the world and, as though he felt nothing of what mortals are doing, feasts at the banquet of the Holy Spirit alone. The monk who is a true lover of God has laid aside, for Christ’s sake, all curiosity about the world and anxiety over worldly things, and, as though he did not feel them, has risen above all the functions of the soul’s cares. With the Apostle he cries out to me, “The world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.”

O wine of divine love, how sweetly you intoxicate, how you satisfy the innocent, how wisely you make them foolish, how chastely you inflame them. For everyone who has been intoxicated with your taste, separated from every tumult of carnal pleasures, rests sweetly in the shadow of the Holy Spirit. Because of the excessive intoxication of the spirit, he speaks things foreign to the flesh and to the world, and nothing contrary to God is heard from his mouth. This is the immaculate satiety of spiritual wine, which is enlarged by desire the more it is continued.

O holy and venerable simplicity of the lover of Christ: although to many who savor earthly things it seems to be foolishness, before God and all his saints it is nevertheless the greatest and truly the highest wisdom. Let the world have its prudence, let it have honors, dignities, and riches; but I would choose the merit of one simple monk who truly loves God rather than the rule of the whole world. The love of God is both the highest delight of the soul and always the most chaste; its fire illuminates the mind and also sweetly inflames its affection. The love of God is a heavenly fountain of grace, because the grace of the Holy Spirit makes fruitful with the fruits of all virtues the mind which the charity of Christ purifies by plowing. The love of Christ wins for human beings the friendship of almighty God and reveals to mortals the will of the supreme majesty. The love of God makes a monk acceptable to God, because he does not require from us knowledge, learning, or eloquence, but charity.

Therefore the monk who has publicly offered himself to the Lord God ought to care for nothing except God, lest he seem to have a divided heart; for just as God is one, so love for God ought not to be divided. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: “He loves you less, Lord, who loves anything with you that he does not love for your sake.” Just as God is perfect, so also he must be loved by us with all our strength, because he is not rightly loved whenever anything besides him is loved together with him. Love of God advances rightly when nothing is mixed with it that could weaken it. Let each one of you, as much as he can, strive to love the Lord his God above all things with the whole strength of his mind, because love for God alone is what will open for you the gate of paradise. For the more ardently you now love God, the greater glory you will receive in the heavenly fatherland. Blessed is that monk in whose conscience, apart from the love of Christ, no imagining of any occupation resides; who, awake, thinks of nothing but Christ; asleep, dreams of Christ; living and dying, sighs most constantly for Christ.
