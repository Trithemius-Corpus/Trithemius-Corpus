Do you trust in vanity? Why do you transgress the ancient boundaries which the holy Fathers set? You deny the vow you solemnly made to God, and yet you expect a portion with God’s elect as though you were righteous? The ungodly man has no share with the sons of God. Behold, you squander the Lord’s inheritance; you enrich strangers from the goods of holy Father Benedict; you deny your promise; and, so that you may sin freely, you heap up falsehoods in place of truths.

If you are a good monk, do not abandon your profession; do not forsake the enclosure of the monastery. But if you are a bad monk, you will never be a good canon. For he who is not good is bad; and he who is bad is not as he ought to be, and therefore he is unjust. The unjust have no share in the kingdom of God; and it matters little where you are, so long as everywhere you are bad, everywhere unjust, everywhere wretchedly tormented. Unless perhaps you wish to be liable to a double damnation, so that, while you are a bad monk and an unrighteous canon, guilty in both states, false in both, you may demand the reward of both.

Nor should you set against me the dispensation of the Roman Pontiff, which, unless God approves it, will in no way excuse you. Not everything that is done on earth through the Supreme Pontiff is pleasing to God. Now if you cry out and are angry with me, as though I were judging the deeds of Christ’s Vicar, I answer: I embrace, honor, and venerate the Supreme Pontiff as the true Vicar of Christ, and I in no way censure his deeds; but I know that he is a man, who, as a man, can both be deceived and deceive. Nor is this surprising. For amid such great care for all the churches, amid such great occupation with affairs, when false things are reported as true, a mind distracted among many matters is easily deceived.

But you, apostate from the holy order, who think yourself secure because of a bull of dispensation, beware lest after death, as is commonly said, you meet an illiterate demon who does not know letters, who does not defer to an appeal, who despises the allegations of your immunity. Believe me, there is no cunning that can deceive God.

O wretched man, O unhappy man, O senseless apostate. What will you answer Saint Benedict concerning the profession that you despise? What will you answer almighty God concerning the vow that you contemn? Whom will you be able to appoint as advocate for your cause in that dreadful judgment? There nothing will be able to lie hidden; all things there will be bare, all things open. There no place for saving repentance will any longer be left; there every opportunity for excuse will be denied to all. What good will that dispensation rashly presumed here do you there, when it will not be permitted either to prove it by testimony or to defend it by law?

Then you will see, O perverse monk; then you will understand what strength a license fraudulently obtained contains in itself, when you have felt the vengeance of the eternal Judge in punishment. Why do you allege dispensation to me? Why do you proclaim a license to sin? Do you think that by the authority of the Supreme Pontiff you can escape the wrath of God? I dare to say: the Pope’s dispensation will profit you nothing if it was obtained by fraud, deceit, lying, or injustice. What is acquired by evil arts is not good, is not just.

For a dispensation to be just, four things are required, without which the dispensation will in no way save you. What are they? Necessity [unclear].
