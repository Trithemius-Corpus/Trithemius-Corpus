For the holy Apostle says: The animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him; nor can he understand, because it is examined spiritually. But the spiritual man judges all things, and he himself is judged by no one. For the love of Jesus is not given to carnal persons, since those who burn with vices and carnal desires show themselves utterly unworthy of divine love. But let us leave those who have a carnal mind, and return to those who live spiritually in the love of God. The love of God makes a man despise death and pant, with great and continual sighs, for the joys of heavenly felicity. Let us bring forward the holy martyrs of Jesus Christ, and they will be the confirmation of this judgment. For so great a force of divine love, so great a fervor, so great an elevation of mind toward God had kindled them that for the love of Jesus they feared no kinds of punishments, they dreaded no torments of death. They had been absorbed in the love of Jesus; therefore they did not care about the destruction of their bodies. For the love of Jesus by its power incomparably surpasses all sufferings of the body, since it does not refuse to subject itself to a thousand deaths. O how savage the torments and pains which the holy martyrs endured in their bodies for the love of Jesus Christ; all these, although they appeared grievous enough to weak flesh, nevertheless seemed light to the spirit because of the magnitude of its immense love. For there is nothing so arduous, nothing so hard, that the love of God does not make it pleasant. For he who loves Jesus with his whole heart above all things fears no adversities of this world, no torments of death. And what the holy martyrs once offered to God in sacred service by dying for love, this true lovers of Jesus even now offer in desire. For the soul, kindled by divine love, desires with many groans and sighs to leave the prison of its body; with its inmost affections it hastens to come to him whom it loves; but the longer it is afflicted by delay while remaining here, the more it is enlarged through desire into love. Often too the fervent lover of Jesus is raised toward Jesus by such great desires that he seems to himself no longer able to live; and hidden in secret he offers loving prayers to the beloved; because of the greatness of love he sends forth his voice with tears: O most loving mediator of God and men, Jesus, why do you not rescue me, placed as I am in this prison? Why do you leave me so long an exile in this misery? I do not desire to increase merit by the prolongation of time; I do not await a reward. You alone, O my sweetest lover, suffice for my enjoyment, you who do not despise a spirit that loves you. Be my reward, you who have given to me, your undeserving creature, the fire of your love. O good Jesus, love raises hope, [unclear] confidence. Nevertheless may your most holy will always be fulfilled in me, since, although I vehemently desire to come to you, I do not refuse to remain in the flesh for as long as you shall wish. In these ways, indeed, and by many more secret exercises, love lifts the mind to heavenly things; through them it shows itself not idle, but working many wondrous things in man.

*Eighth,* divine love makes the soul of its host, although still placed in the flesh, known and familiar to the citizens of the heavenly fatherland, because whoever is loved by the king is venerated by the whole court. But our heavenly king loves the one who loves him, as he says in the holy Gospel: He who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me. But he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and will manifest myself to him. Therefore the lover of Jesus is loved by him, and is honored by those holy spirits who are in the fatherland; and although man, according to his understanding, cannot always comprehend this familiarity of the angels while in the flesh, nevertheless the mind itself can wondrously perceive insensibly.
