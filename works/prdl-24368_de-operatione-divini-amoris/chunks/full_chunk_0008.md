But let us hear what the holy apostle next enjoins upon us, he who, free in spirit, groaned under the law of the flesh. “Therefore,” he says, “brethren, we are debtors, not to the flesh, so as to live according to the flesh; for if you live according to the flesh, you will die. But if by the Spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” And of himself he says in another place: “I chastise my body and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, after I have preached to others, I myself should be made reprobate.” This is the wonderful working of divine love: it both subjects the flesh to the spirit and does not break the law of the flesh. For if love were wholly to abolish the law opposed to itself, it would not be expedient for man. For there must be movements in the flesh contrary to the love of the spirit, so that victory may have merit and virtue may have exercise. And therefore love is often withdrawn for a time from the minds of the saints, so that exercise may be tested.

But the love of God suddenly consumes the love of the world, and the heart that has burned with mystical fire desires nothing of carnal love. It suddenly changes desire and directs the affection toward other works. What shall I say of you, O holy and wholly ineffable fire? You suddenly made apostles out of fishermen; you raised the unlearned into most learned men; you taught your guests to despise the riches and honors of the world and to fear no torments of the malicious. O holy power of divine love, how quickly the onrush of carnal concupiscence is extinguished if the mind is set aflame by your fire. You suddenly made the persecutor defend the faith of Jesus Christ, which without you he had previously been accustomed to attack terribly. For as soon as he was able to feel you working within, he no longer yielded to flesh and blood, nor did he care to revisit friends once beloved, after he had deserved to taste the kernel of your sweetness. You so intoxicated him with the sweetness of inward love that thereafter, because of the greatness of that sweetness, he could scarcely contain himself; with a free voice he confounded the enemies of the cross of Christ; he no longer feared to undergo any adversities of this world for love of Jesus.

O how many souls you have suddenly snatched from the peril of this age; how many sinners you have brought without hindrance into the joys of paradise. You made the holy angels of God steadfast in cleaving to their principle; you marked out the innocent from the origin of the world; you chose the patriarchs; you ordained the prophets. Finally, you made the holy disciples of God despise the world; you wonderfully strengthened the martyrs; you purified the confessors; you prescribed for holy virgins the rule of living. And why should I use many words? Without you no one ever pleased God; no one, strengthened by your aid, ever displeased the supreme and immutable good. Through you faith obtains merit; through you religion receives increase; through you the rust of sin is consumed; through you alone every elect person is sanctified.

Just as a great fire consumes the little water cast into it, so the mighty power of divine love restrains carnal passions in a person. For whoever has truly conceived the love of God in his heart will burn with such desires and such affections toward God that, while the exercise of love continues, he feels no disordered affections of the flesh and desires nothing besides God. And plainly it is a wonderful change of affections, since one who before could scarcely tame the passions of his flesh through many labors, now, set aflame by divine love, has overcome them in the silence of contemplation. But to experience this is not granted to all, because it is reserved for few. For many think that conceptions placed in their senses are divine love, and the higher they raise themselves through imagination, the more grievously, for the most part, they fall. For Scripture says: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be stoned.” If you desire to ascend without peril the holy mountain of divine love, you will have to slay bestial affections, because the soul is then made one through love with that one principle of things, when, stripped of every multiplicity of affection, it is raised alone and, lifted above every imagination, reason, and understanding, [unclear] from all things that can be said or thought
