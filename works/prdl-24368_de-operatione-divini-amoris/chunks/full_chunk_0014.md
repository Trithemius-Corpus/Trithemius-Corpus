For the love of God prepares an open way for the soul to the sight of the supreme emperor; it calls the citizens of the heavenly country to meet the one who is departing; it does not allow demons to draw near; it removes all impediments. The spirit of a man who loves Jesus above all things has as many familiar friends in heaven as he has had companions in the love of God. There the angelic spirits, confirmed in the loftiest charity of God, love most ardently the soul of a man who loves Jesus; they recognize their fellow citizen and familiar friend; while he remains in the flesh they guard him; as he goes forth they meet him with the greatest joy. There the order of the patriarchs attends the lover of God with wondrous favor; it rejoices with inestimable joy that a soul which loves the divine majesty is arriving. There the holy seers, foreknowing things to come, joyfully receive the soul, receiving with inestimable joy the spirit of the lover of God. There the holy martyrs, clothed in white robes, come to meet him; they lead the soul, fervent with divine love, to the feet of the Most High. There the most holy virgins will be present; they will receive him with incredible exultation; they will lead him to the throne of glory to be crowned with immortal beauty. We read that many saints in this life had the companionship of angels, and that they often came to meet the dying, because where they see the love of their king Jesus Christ dwelling with sweet fervor, they go more frequently as if to his dwelling-place. For Jesus dwells in the heart of one who loves him; he prepares a throne for himself there; he drives away on every side all the snares of evil affections; he establishes peace and concord in his kingdom. The holy angels also often render invisible service to the one whose lordship over themselves they love. Therefore the soul burning with the fire of divine love is familiarly known to the spirits above, singularly loved by all the saints, and will happily pass into their company without impediment.

*The Ninth.* Divine love produces an inestimable union between God and the soul by the adhesion of union, when the spirit of the lover, not by nature but by grace, is dissolved into the abyss of divine contemplation, and, failing from itself, is wonderfully transformed into a participation in divinity. This adhesion of union by no means annihilates the human spirit, nor changes it into another essence; rather, while remaining what it is, it is rapt above itself, and, while its first essential act remains, it is deprived of the exercise of the second. For entering the new darkness of divine contemplation, it leaves far behind itself both itself and all things that have been created or can be created; it ascends above every imagination, reason, or understanding; and there, when it has failed from every action of a created or creatable being, exercising itself in God alone, it rests insensibly. For the mind of one who loves God must rise through affection to this experiential knowledge, and, having abandoned every creature, enter through darkness to the Creator, who makes darkness his hiding place. And first the mind itself abandons every operation of sense; then it ascends affectively above the unknown intellect; afterward, failing even from itself, it enters the divine darkness, in which, resting through ecstatic love, it is lulled to sleep. But to see God face to face as he is is granted to no soul, however holy, so long as it is contained in the prison of the flesh. For God says to Moses: No man shall see me and live. And that disciple whom Jesus loved says in a certain epistle: No one has ever seen God. The holy Paul also, contemplator of divine things, says: No man has seen God, nor can see him. For the true and immediate perception of God is promised to the lover in this life, and bestowed in the life to come. But true love, raising the mind into that divine darkness, does not wholly satisfy it, nor does it disclose the vision of God, but ineffably refreshes it through the inward sweetness of divine delight and the taste of supernal happiness/
