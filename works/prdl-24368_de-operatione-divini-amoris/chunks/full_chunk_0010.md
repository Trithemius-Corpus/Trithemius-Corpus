O shepherd of monks, diligently consider yourself, and see whether your mind is in all things resigned in this way to almighty God. If you find it so disposed, you are near the fire of sweetest love. But if your will has taken delight in the office of rule, you show most plainly that there is more love of self than of divine love in you. For love loves nothing in this world except God; it desires nothing, but despises all honors, spurns riches, and at the same time contemns all things.

Fourth, the love of God begets wondrous compunction and devotion in its host, because the heart which it has kindled with its own sweetest ardor it carries away, as it were, outside the man. For divine love itself is said in a certain way to imitate the nature of fire: for just as fire always by nature directs the flames of its burning upward, so this love always ascends to heavenly things. What the inward state of a man loving God is, what compunction, how many sweetest sighs of the heart, no one can understand except one who has deserved by experience to taste that very sweetness of inward love. Love raises the mind from earthly things, discloses heavenly things, manifests secrets. O burning of holy love, how sweet is the presence of your most blessed operation, since the mind which once you have entered you do not leave idle. It always burns with the love of the sweetest Savior; it always cries sweetly to the King of glory; it directs desire toward the mystical embraces of the bridegroom; because of the greatness of love it pours out tears. As often as it thinks of its crucified Lord, it weeps most bitterly from an excessive feeling of compassion. Weeping, it goes with him through all the places of the Passion; in spirit it drinks the blood flowing from his body; it embraces Jesus hanging on the cross; with tears it kisses the wounds of him as he dies. These, O faithful soul, are the first preludes of divine love; these are the excellent exercises of spiritual zeal: that you often recall to memory the Passion of your lover, that, giving thanks, you humbly bow yourself before him. Behold the sorrowful soul of Jesus, even unto death; from affection suffer with your suffering lover; give thanks for his most bitter wounds. By these holy exercises divine love is acquired; by these pursuits devotion is begotten, since whoever has learned wisely to exercise his soul in the Passion of sweetest Jesus ascends quickly and easily to the dwelling-place of divine love. This that holy lover of God, Ignatius, knew very well, who, from the vehemence of his compunction, used to say: My love has been crucified. O good Jesus, how sweet is your love; how gently it penetrates the soul. Seeing you in spirit, the soul longs to embrace you; if you command, it hastens to come to you as quickly as possible. Indeed, fathers, he who loves Jesus forgets himself; he meditates on nothing other than that sweetest presence of his. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves Jesus except Jesus; nothing at all is found that the soul desires. O with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is distressed; with how abundant tears the face is watered in secret, because as often as it thinks of the face of Jesus seated in heaven, so often it weeps that it is desolate on earth. Meanwhile the mind, languishing with love, often tries to say something before men about its most beloved Jesus; it even strives to provoke others to love him; but as soon as it begins to utter the sweetest name of Jesus, because of the greatness of love it is struck dumb. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, explained by no letters. In secret it instructs the soul concerning each thing; it hands down the most effective manner of praying; it enlightens the understanding of the lover for asking; it denies nothing to one who prays through love. In the secret of divine love, the devotion of the mind toward God overflows; the innermost compunction of the heart reigns; the spring of sweet tears flows around the lover’s face without injury to the head; the wall of perseverance guards the bridal chamber of love. A true lover of Jesus is always fruitfully pierced with compunction in his meditation; pierced with compunction, he weeps because of the sweetness of love; he is seen to live in body among men, but in mind he dwells in heavenly things. The prayer of one who loves God penetrates the heavens; whatever he has sought from Jesus he easily obtains; he casts his whole self into the bosom of the sweetest lover. There is no true divine love in a man’s heart without burning compunction. O how sweet, how gentle, how divine are the thoughts the mind burning in divine love conceives in psalmody and the other readings of prayers; how deep and mystical are the meanings of the Scriptures it comes to know, which, when it strives to express them in words, it is overcome by their greatness.
