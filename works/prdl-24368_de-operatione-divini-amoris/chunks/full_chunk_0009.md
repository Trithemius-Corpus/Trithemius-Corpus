Nor is it necessary in this place to instruct the true lover in the conditions of false love, since the true love of God brings with it so many most certain signs of itself that it does not permit the one who has it to doubt. And although a man may not know whether he is worthy of love or hatred, nevertheless I say with confidence that one into whom almighty God deigns to pour the sweetness of his inward love will be able to know many things unknown to us.

*Third*, the love of God makes the one who has it despise all things and love absolutely nothing in this world. He is not drawn to honors and dignities; he delights in no riches or joys of the world; he always thinks of the Lord Jesus; he longs to come as quickly as possible to his sweetest embraces. Whatever is seen to be delightful in the world is bitter to him; he delights in nothing except in his lover. For the mind, gathered together and united to itself, despises both itself and all things, so that, without the hindrance of multiplicity, it may stretch itself through love toward that one ineffable principle. O wondrous sweetness of divine love, how powerful is your burning, how sweet your bond, how blessed your experience. Whoever has you has all things; whoever does not have you has nothing. But no one can acquire you, O sweet love, who has wished to love anything in this world besides you. For you either inhabit the human heart alone, or, if not alone, you refuse to enter. Therefore whoever desires to have the fellowship of you within himself must despise all things besides you, for to the extent that he has wished to love anything besides you, to that extent he drives away from himself your sweetest love. The soul cannot exist without love: for either it loves God and is truly blessed, or it leans upon transitory things and is always wretched. But no one can at the same time love God and the world, for if [unclear], he necessarily despises the other. Yet there is a great difference between the love of God and the desire of the present age. For the love of God makes a man always pure in conscience, joyful in heart, honorable in conduct, distinguished in action, tranquil in mind. He covets nothing; his own condition suffices for him; he rests without ambition. But the love of the world pollutes its possessor in conscience with many crimes; it makes him always anxious and sad; it renders him disturbed in conduct, sluggish in action, turbulent in mind. The end of divine love is everlasting happiness; but the end of worldly affection is calamity that will remain without end.

The love of God always has joy; the love of the world is full of infinite miseries. The love of God does not love the vain honors of the world; it does not desire to be over others; rather, for God’s sake, it desires to be subject to others; it does not refuse to lay aside even honors already possessed. For whoever truly conceives the love of God despises all things, both prosperous and adverse, equally. You are placed in some honor, O man: you are an abbot, you are a provost, you are prior of monks. If I ask you whether you love God with all your heart, what will you answer? Whatever you answer me outwardly, I shall appeal inwardly to your conscience. If you truly love God, tell me with what intention you are pastor of monks. You will of course say, if I am not deceived, that for the honor of God, whom I love, I bear the burden of governing monks. Therefore either you love God, O abbot, or you do not love him. If you truly love God, you will be ready at every time, for love of him and at the counsel of good men, to yield the office of governance, and with an even mind to be subject to another. But if you do not truly love your God, you unworthily occupy the place of governance. For the true lover of God orders his conscience at every time in such a way that he does not depart from his love through affection for any other thing or office. Hence the ruler of souls who loves his Lord with all his heart, at every time resigned to him, says in his prayers: Lord Jesus, who are ignorant of nothing, you know that I desire to be over no one; you know that I do not refuse to be subject to another. For even if I am not fit to carry out your duties, nevertheless I desire always to be subject to your most holy will; and for love of you I am prepared both to feed your sheep according to my powers, and humbly, as insufficient, to resign the office of pastoral care. I subject myself to your will; I commit myself to the judgment of your servants. But if [unclear] command me to abandon the abbacy, for love of you I wish to obey without pretense.
