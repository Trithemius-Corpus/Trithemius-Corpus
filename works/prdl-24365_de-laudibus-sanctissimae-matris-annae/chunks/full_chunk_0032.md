Anne, take courage; behold, your daughter stretches toward your milky breasts. Hail.
Your daughter seeks your tender hands and your neck. Hail.
Anne, you break off delays; baring breast and bosom, Hail,
you receive her tender little limbs in your tender lap. Hail.
Anne, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast the sweet juices, Hail,
she draws with rosy mouth, sounding pleasing murmurs. Hail.
Anne, if you knew what great sorrows your daughter will bear, Hail,
and how many deaths she will bear, you would now be no more. Hail.
Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, it is dear to your heart to adorn your daughter with approved, Hail,
virtues, and to fit her for the highest God. Hail.
Anne, you prepare to carry your daughter into the lofty shrine, Hail,
but the kindly girl of her own will reached the upper step. Hail.
Anne, with slow step and effort you follow the swift girl, Hail,
and the eager little virgin consoles her weary mother. Hail.
Anne, why do you marvel at strength in the tender virgin? Hail,
you see not human things, but holy signs of God. Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to give gifts to the needy and to sacred temples. Hail.
Now you give that than which no woman has given more. Hail.
Anne, where you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell, Hail,
she gladly entered the cloisters and the virginal house. Hail.
Anne, about to send away the holy one, your cheeks and eyes with weeping, Hail,
you consume; deep love holds you unmoved. Hail.
Anne, return home; send your daughter to the Thunderer. Hail.

Anne, it is enough for you that two Marys serve you. Hail.
They too will honor you with no small piety. Hail.
Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, by your own weeping you have learned to aid one who weeps. Hail.
Hence you too readily favor the wretched man with prayers. Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to overturn the fixed laws of nature, Hail,
and so you will: let the barren woman now be a joyful mother. Hail.

Anne, you drive away disgrace; you give the gifts of fair fame. Hail.
You relieve grief, and love yields to you. Hail.
The savage chains of a dark prison hold fast. Hail.
Anne, be present as a star of relief to suffering mortals. Hail.
With you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing. Hail.

Anne, mother, the sailor calls on you amid the fearful wave, Hail,
if winds and water toss his shattered ship. Hail.
Anne, mother, you are ever to be feared by the black tyrant. Hail.
As ice flees fire, that harmful one flees you. Hail.
Anne, mortal hearts cannot ask anything of you in vain; Hail,
whatever you will, your daughter and God will will. Hail.
Anne, therefore, blessed in merits and in so great an offspring, Hail,
great mother Anne, look upon our prayers. Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary*

Almighty and most loving God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as mother of the mother of your Son, graciously grant that we who honor her memory with faithful devotion may, by her merits and prayers, obtain the helps of eternal life. Through [the same].

*Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beisselius, professor of both laws and patrician of Aachen, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim of the Order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1493.*

*Poem of Adam Wernher of Themar on the most holy Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot*

In praise of the illustrious grandmother we compose; dear Jesus, grandson, move the plectra of the heart,
nor you, daughter, chaste mother, be absent from the singing choir.

Anne, offspring of kings, long remains barren to her husband;
weeping, she laments herself as fruitless;
weeping, she prays for offspring.

Heavenly messengers promise a child;
pregnant, joined to her husband, then as a woman in childbed,
she is proved a mother by the offspring
through whom our salvation comes.

Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green;
he becomes worthy, removing the reproaches of the priest.
Thus, Anne, prepare us to carry pure prayers
to your grandson.
