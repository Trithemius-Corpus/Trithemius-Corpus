Turn over the writings of the aforesaid fathers. She was dignified in gait, calm with a countenance of shining grace, mature in counsel, composed in speech, most holy in worship and deed. On the law of the Lord she meditated day and night, firm in faith, strong in hope, filled with twofold charity. She had learned always to persist in prayers, and with the greatest longing she awaited the coming of the Lord. She was honorable in deed, sober in conduct, humble in mind. No one ever found her running about through streets and squares; no one heard her chattering uselessly in the houses of neighboring women; no one ever found that she had stood by at dances and spectacles. She learned from her youth to remain at home. She was accustomed to work at something with her hands. She did not seek to be seen outside by men. She strove to keep concord in good and peace with her neighbors. She did wrong to no one; by word or deed she disturbed no one. She was rarely seen in public. She did not sit in the squares with little women chattering, and she did not stand in the conversation of slanderers. For that most prudent woman knew that sin is not absent from much speaking; therefore with all watchfulness she guarded her heart, lest she ever offend in speech. Thus, intent upon holy studies and exercises, from being barren she deserved to become a mother, just as almighty God had ordained from the beginning. Therefore the holy woman conceived by a holy husband; she conceived without original stain; she gave birth without fault. O holy and formerly unheard-of conception. She conceived by a man, but this conception was never liable to any crime. She conceived according to the order of nature, but beyond the custom of nature. Holy conception, to be celebrated with continual praises, which prepares a mother [for the Son?], over which the choir of angels rejoices, and the whole world throughout the earth exults. Truly an ever-honored conception, which was pleasing to almighty God before it was fulfilled. Truly worthy conception, which, with the Holy Spirit preserving it, took its beginning in such purity that it was disposed to conceive and bear God. Therefore Anne, the most holy parent, is to be revered by us with the highest devotion, she who conceived the mother of God without stain. What a worthy offering she offered to God when she presented her most holy daughter in the temple. Finally, it pertains to declaring the merits of the holy mother that the daughter whom she conceived and bore she consecrated to the Lord immediately from infancy. As was the mother’s way of life, so was the daughter’s training. No one can teach another what he himself has not learned; no one badly trained trains children well. Often children advance according to the habits of their parents, and what they have seen practiced by them they embrace more willingly. A mother worthy of the most holy daughter: so that from her from whom she had drawn the material of her flesh, from her she might suck the teachings of piety. The grace of God had anticipated both and had filled both; but it was earlier in the mother, more abundant in the daughter. The holy mother taught the daughter what she herself had first learned from her parents, although she had no need of human instruction, whom the Holy Spirit filled at conception. Nor indeed did she need maternal instruction, since she was full of heavenly grace. Learn, all of you, to train your daughters for the honor of God: not to love the pomps of the world, to flee public sight, to remain at home. For the holy bridegroom hates publicity and loves the secrecy of a pure mind. Why do you run about through streets and squares, if you love modesty, virgins? The archangel came to Mary not in public but in secret; not to her speaking in the street, but to her silent in her chamber. The apostles received the Spirit not in the forum, but shut up in the house.
