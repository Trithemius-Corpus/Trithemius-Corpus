If the Lord Jesus, king of heaven, loves those who love him, it is rightly to be believed that he loves his own parents before all others; nor can they be in the heavenly fatherland without special glory, whom almighty God on earth had filled with such extraordinary grace. Therefore we believe Anna, after the Virgin Mother, to be nearest to the Son of God, and a powerful advocate for the wretched, to whom the king of heaven denies nothing, but in all things for which she asks honors her face as that of a most beloved parent. The whole Trinity also loves her; the angelic choir honors her; all the citizens of the heavenly fatherland venerate her with the greatest reverence as the honorable mother of their queen; and not undeservedly. For if they love the king, they rightly venerate his grandmother. The honor that is paid to parents is shown to the children. But he proves that he loves the king less who does not venerate his father and mother and his whole lineage. We are also accustomed to grieve more over injuries done to our parents than over our own. Why is this, unless because an insult inflicted on them groans within us? For we believe that not only our parents but also we ourselves are marked by grave injury in the same way; just as we believe an insult to our parents to be our own, so we reckon the honor shown to them as ours. Whatever honor, therefore, we pay to the divine mother Anna, let us believe without doubt to be shown to God. For the Lord says to us in the Gospel: Whatever you have done to one of the least of mine, you have done to me. If he asserts that everything paid to the least who believe in Christ has been done to himself, how much more must it be believed to be done in those who are not merely least with him, but greatest and most powerful? His parents are not last with the Son of God, not least but greatest, not small but most powerful. Whatever veneration, therefore, we pay to them, let us by no means doubt that it is doubled before God. And if revelations are to be believed, the most blessed Virgin appeared to a certain devout man and, among other things, said to him: If you love me, honor my mother, and whatever reverence you pay to her will be doubly pleasing to me. Now therefore, brothers, if we love the Mother of God, if we desire to please the queen of heaven, let us honor her venerable mother with diligent devotion, and we shall find them both pious helpers in every tribulation. Behold, the angels of God and all the saints venerate and love Anna, and shall we be silent and mute from her honor? The intercessions of the saints are necessary for us, not for those who already exult in the fatherland; and therefore it is fitting that we should always be devoted in their veneration. We, I say, who still live in the flesh and are pilgrims away from the Lord, must honor those citizens of the fatherland above with especial devotion, so that, aided by their suffrages, we may be able to come to the joys which they inhabit. But among all the saints we believe the Mother of God to stand preeminent, and Anna, her venerable parent; if we are devoted to them and their worshippers, no adversities will harm us.
