And I believe I have argued sufficiently about this above; by these arguments I do not doubt that satisfaction has been given to the devout worshippers of Anna. Moreover, to wish to satisfy the curious and the proud is superfluous, since piety does not move them, devotion does not stir them, charity does not inflame them. Therefore let the devout worshippers of blessed Anna believe, and hold without any hesitation, that her feast is ancient and was customarily celebrated by the ancients with the greatest reverence, as befitted the mother of the most holy Mother of God. For in Judea and the regions overseas her commemoration was very famous among faithful Christians, until at length, when the land was occupied by unbelievers, it was for a long time too much neglected. But in the regions of Europe her feast was not so celebrated, because many of these came to the faith later. Yet in certain places of Europe there was always a solemn remembrance of Anna, which had been instituted by the faithful coming from Palestine. This is proved by the altars erected in her honor in various churches from ancient times. This is proved also by very ancient oratories and by various treatises of the fathers on the praises of Anna. But why do we try to defend the antiquity of this feast, which no good and devout person will wish to attack? Against the rebellious we are not at present to dispute, since we shall never be able to persuade them by words to the worship of most holy Anna. Therefore, whether the commemoration of so great a woman is judged by them to be new or ancient matters little; this alone must be labored for by us, that it be celebrated by faithful and devout Christians with all reverence. For if we honor with due devotion the feasts of other saints of Christ, why should we not celebrate the feast of Anna, most beloved by God? For you know that she is inferior to the rest neither in merits nor in glory, since she was of such dignity while still mortal that she bore the mother of him who, immortal, lives forever in heaven. And just as we make some commemoration daily of our special patrons, so also concerning Saint Anna, as right devotion shall suggest, let us offer something of praise to God every day. And if devout worshippers ought always to be ready for her honor, nevertheless on her birthday especially they ought to be solicitous, since her commemoration is observed not only on earth but is also honored in heaven by the votive praises of the saints. It also agrees with devotion and befits piety that, just as the Sabbath day is observed by clergy and people with masses and prayers in honor of the most holy Mother of God, so the commemoration of her mother should be celebrated at least once a week. For by solicitude for household affairs we are quickly distracted from devotion, to which it is necessary that we return at least once a week, lest, if we put off the honor of venerable Anna too long, in our lukewarmness we lose her patronage. For celebrating this weekly remembrance, Tuesday seems sufficiently fitting, on which she is read to have been both born and to have died. Finally, in many places a laudable custom has prevailed that, with no less reverence, the commemoration of Saint Anna is celebrated every Tuesday than the Sabbath day is observed in memory of the Mother of God in the church of the faithful. For we have seen among the Sicambri and [Grunes?] that every Tuesday masses are sung in honor of blessed Anna, lamps are lit, offerings are made, and the poor are more abundantly refreshed. We have also known among them certain devout worshippers of the blessed woman who, in honor of Jesus, Mary, and Anna, every Tuesday lit three candles on her altar, gave three special alms, and received as many acts of pardon or disciplines. This custom took its origin from a miracle of Saint Anna that happened in Pannonia, where she advanced a desolate and very poor young man to great honors and riches. If anyone desires to have knowledge of this matter, let him read the said miracle, which is now so widely known among the worshippers of blessed Anna that it can be hidden from no one. And we have recognized that these things are done by many not only on Tuesday, but every day, as the devotion of each person requires. Therefore priests of the Lord who desire to be devout worshippers of the most holy mother should take care to celebrate devoutly for her commemoration at least once a week. Whoever has adopted that devotion as his custom, we believe that he will have Anna as a pious advocate in every necessity.
