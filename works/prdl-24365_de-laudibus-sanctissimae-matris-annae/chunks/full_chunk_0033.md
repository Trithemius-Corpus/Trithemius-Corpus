Commend [us] to the boy whom holy chastity
bore of his mother; we ask in prayer,
O blessed matron.
Christ denies you nothing.
To Christ, who, sharing rule with the Father and the Spirit,
reigns, be glory through all ages;
whom your prayers, O Anna,
make reconciled to us. Amen.

This hymn is sung to the tune of that one which, on the Nativity of Saint Mary, is used in many churches, and begins, “Rejoice, mother, in your inmost heart,” etc. From the castle of Heidelberg, 3 days before the Kalends of February, 1493.

*The Poem or Hymn of John of [unclear] on Saint Anna, to the reverend lord father John Trithemius, abbot.*

Rejoice, Anna, refuge for the wretched.
We run to you, we sing, we bring forth songs.
On the feast, be gracious with your offspring;
now we speak praises to you.

Rejoice, earth shining, fertile with grass,
and wet with rain, with divine dew,
from which was born, purer than the ether,
the Virgin innocent of sin.

Rejoice, you who had been of royal seed,
now conceived, given to a husband, you had led
a chaste marriage, long without offspring,
made a mother by vow.

Rejoice, you who, reddened by the priest’s reproach,
are confounded; you call upon God with tears.
Heard, you receive your husband coming to meet you
at the gate called Golden.

Rejoice, you who, joyful, embraced Joachim,
your husband coming to meet you, and taught by the heavenly
deity; and thus the mother of your Jesus
is conceived in your little womb.

Rejoice, wise angelic simplicity,
you who, praised by the choir and [unclear] threefold,
divide your goods among the temple and the poor,
and a part among your servants.

Rejoice; the people of the faithful celebrate your feasts,
so that, as their patroness, you may render the Spirit with the Father,
and Christ the Son, by your prayers,
propitious, Anna. Amen.

This hymn takes the melody of the one sung for the Ascension of the Lord, namely “Now the celebrated feast,” etc., and of that one for martyrs, “The illustrious merits of the saints,” etc. From Heidelberg, the third day before the Nones of February, in the year 1493.

*An elegiac poem on Saint Anna by Rutger of Venray, canon regular in Heinau, of the diocese of Worms.*

Hail, splendid mother of the Virgin Mother,
of the Father’s splendor, of the brightness of untouched light.
You, holy one, bore for the Lord the divine maiden,
from whom came forth Jesus, the kindly light of the world.

Constantly tell your daughter’s son to [unclear] the groaning,
although he himself made the heavens and the earth.
Your grandson in the flesh, Jesus himself, joined
highest divinity to flesh, for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus always existed, nevertheless he desires
and deigns to be called your grandson.
Do not hesitate to strike such a grandson with prayers;
Anna, we ask, you who will obtain all things asked.

If he has not bent easy ears to the first requests,
Anna, quickly turn again to your daughter.
Let your daughter show her breasts to Christ her son;
at once the kindly parent will grant what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to obtain by prayer,
let the daughter with her mother seek help.
My faith is firm, nor do I doubt that immediately
the Lord will hear both as they pray with gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers
one must seek the help of Anna and Mary together.
It is certain: if you honor the mother of the Lord’s mother,
you yourself cherish the titles of the Virgin Mother.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it is clear [unclear].
Let praise, virtue, and honor be to the daughter, to the son, and to Anna,
and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*A Sapphic poem of John Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the day of Saint Anna.*

Let us celebrate the feasts of holy Anna,
of the exalted mother, renowned for merits,
who brought forth for the world the parent
of God the Father’s Son.

Her life, ever to be venerated by us,
is deservedly praised as blessed;
by the gift of signs she increases relief
for wretched minds.

She heals the languid, purges all evils,
brightens the hearts of the sorrowful with joy,
sets right what is apt, rules and governs,
levels rough things.

Sitting in the palace of the all-powerful
highest King, she wipes away stains, sins;
she confers grace, and leads faithful souls
to the stars.

We brothers, here frequenting your feasts,
bringing vows, praying good things from you:
help your servants, refresh, govern us,
renowned mother.

May honor and virtue be to the thundering one above,
who, God simple and likewise threefold,
granted the crown of great glory
to Anna forever. Amen.

This Sapphic hymn is sung to the melody “with loosened...” etc.; “Life, offspring of the holy Virgin”; “Because the choirs of bards”; “This confessor,” and similar hymns which are Sapphic only. From Sponheim, in the Nones of July, in the year of the Lord 1493.

*An elegiac poem on Saint Mother Anna by John Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.*

How great the rewards are that Anna gives back to her worshippers,
no one grasps in mind nor can speak with the mouth.
She drives away hardships, obtains pardon, increases the ornaments
of honor for her own, and makes them be in heaven.

By contrast, many very bitter things accompany the scorners
of Mother Anna; look out for yourself, wicked one.
She crushes the proud, casts down the powerful from honor,
and permits them often to suffer great scandals.

This is clear and remains firm: Anna offers joyful things
to those who worship her, but brings back many sad things to her enemies.
