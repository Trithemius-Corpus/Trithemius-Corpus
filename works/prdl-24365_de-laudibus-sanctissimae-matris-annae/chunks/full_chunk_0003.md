With what devotion I can, I shall sing the praises of the most blessed mother Anne. For if we are commanded to praise God generally in his saints, we are without doubt urged to honor his parents more especially. For he who commanded us to honor our parents by no means excludes his own according to the flesh. Therefore, dearest brothers, it is holy to offer the diligent praises of devotion to those from whose blood we believe the Son of God took flesh. Among them the most blessed Mother of God rightly holds the first place; so great are the merits of her holiness that she has been exalted in heaven above the choirs of angels. After her, the most holy Anne, her mother, of whom we have undertaken to speak, just as she is nearest in the flesh to the Son of God, so by the merits of her virtues she is believed to be acceptable before the other saints. Nor may it be permitted us to doubt her holiness, since we believe that she bore the Mother of God without the stain of original guilt. For she could not have been the mother of so great a virgin unless she was holy, through whose virginal birth we received the remedies of eternal salvation. Anne is the holy root; Mary the Virgin, the most holy tree; the fruit is the Son of God, the medicine of souls. A good tree, a holy tree, a tree most fruitful without human cultivation, which took its beginning from a holy root and its growth from the richness of the heavenly rain. Never does a good root produce a bad tree; never does a good tree bear bad fruits. A good tree cannot stand without a root, nor bear good fruit unless it is good. As a tree is known from its fruit, so what sort the mother is is declared in the daughter. In the most holy Mother of God we understand what we ought to think about the holiness of her mother. A birth free from the guilt of original stain did not befit any mother except a holy one; to conceive and bear the Mother of God was fitting for none except one most pleasing to God and, above all then living in Israel, devout. For that she loved God above all things is shown by the first instruction of her daughter. As a little child, she is read to have been entrusted to the Lord; she is recorded to have served God in the temple; conceived without any guilt, she is believed always to have lived holy in the world. What more do you require? Is such an excellent life of the daughter from infancy not sufficient to declare the merits of the mother? If Anne had not been most holy, she would never have instructed her daughter so carefully and devoutly for the honor of God. We know that such as mothers are, such also are their daughters as instructed by them. But the most blessed mother Anne, just as in merit and holiness she surpasses all women, so she took care to instruct her daughter from youth, more diligently than all the saints, in the love of God. If, therefore, we love the Son of God, if we honor his most pure Mother, why do we grow lukewarm and keep silence from the praise of his most holy grandmother and mother Anne? If we seek the honor of the highest king, why do we not love his parents? He who despises the lineage of the emperor does injury to the king.
