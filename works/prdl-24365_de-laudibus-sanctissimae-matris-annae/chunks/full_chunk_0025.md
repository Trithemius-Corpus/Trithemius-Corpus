There are also many other practices of holy exercise, which are placed in each person’s discretion and devotion. A devout mind will always find a way to honor the most holy Anne with due praises. In fasts and tears, in alms and prayers, in genuflections and disciplines, in song and readings, the most blessed mother is to be honored by us. Yet it offends us no little that we find no suitable history of the holy mother, one that would in all respects befit her honor and feast. For the history that is already in use among many, in various forms, is variable and, as it seems to many, not sufficiently elegant; I pass over the fact that it is less worthy of such merits, and in very many places seems apocryphal. Nor is there only one, but several are found, as each person has seen fit, with additions that are less approved. What shall I say of the office of the Mass, which we see confused in many places by similar variety? An office is sung by very many which some devotee drew out from the Saturday office of blessed Mary, changing the meanings of the words but retaining the melody; this seems less conducive to devotion, since hearers, deceived by the similarity of the melody, think that the celebration is not of Saint Anne but of the Mother of God. If the matter is pleasing, let at least the melody be changed. Yet all these things could easily be corrected if the authority of some pontiff intervened; relying on it, men learned in these matters might compose a history and office, regulated in reading and melody and approved by the Scriptures.

The frequent display of miracles merits reverence for the saints. Blessed Anne is most worthy of the greatest honor, since she bestows daily so many and such great benefits upon her devout worshippers that there is no one who could recount them all. We indeed know many miracles of Saint Anne, both new and old, but it is not our purpose now to direct the mind to these things, which we have learned are called into doubt by many: not because they contain any falsehood in themselves, but because modern men, most of whom seem learned to themselves, do not believe miracles but disparage them. We therefore do not wish to give an occasion for disparagement, nor to expose the present treatise to suspicion, lest they say that we write not true things but apocrypha. For no one understands, no one believes, no one grasps how great are the benefits that God daily bestows upon the lovers of Saint Anne, unless devout experience instructs him.
