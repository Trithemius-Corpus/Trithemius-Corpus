If we are instructed to interpret in the better sense those things whose intention we do not know, how much more ought we to receive with piety the hidden works of God, whose reasons we do not know? None of us was present at the counsel of God; no one has known the manner of the conception. But, induced by many reasons, we, from pious belief, believe that she always existed most pure; the adversaries, led astray by consideration of nature, defend the contrary opinion with rash presumption. But it is better to render an account for piety toward the Mother of God than for rashness.

If we err, piety, indeed God himself, is the cause; but if you err, O adversaries, say for what cause. What consideration moves you to say so boldly that the most pure conception of the Mother of God was subject to original guilt? You will answer, I know: love of truth. But show that your opinion is true, and I shall believe that you are lovers of truth. Yet you will not show it, because you do not understand. You do not understand because you do not believe. You do not defend the truth, which you do not know; for no one properly defends what he does not know.

Consideration of human nature deceives you; while you carefully turn over its custom, you do not consider that God is the establisher of nature. He who made nature is above nature. God, the maker of the law, does not confine himself under the condition of human law; but he who willed that a virgin should give birth also preserved her from sin beyond the order of nature. Therefore the cause of the opposing opinion takes its beginning from rashness, and for this reason it does not so much deserve reward as punishment. For just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so the error concerning the conception does not condemn [unclear].

One must believe the examples and testimonies of the ancients. We read that very many have most miserably perished who attacked the purity of the glorious conception; but we find that no defender of it was ever struck by God. For the supreme dignity of the Mother of God, when considered, urges us to the defense of the most holy conception; it shone forth such and so great that it was most worthy of every honor.

If we are mistaken, we are excused by holy piety. But if you err, who will defend you? Truth? But you do not know it. Justice? But it condemns errors; it does not excuse them. Mercy? But you do not have the bowels of mercy. Who, then, will free you? The Virgin Mary? But you despise her honor. The Son of God? But you do not love his mother. The prayers of the saints? But you detract from their queen. Therefore you will have no excuse, because you offend God and all his saints. You arm yourselves with scholastic arguments, and you are naked of piety.
