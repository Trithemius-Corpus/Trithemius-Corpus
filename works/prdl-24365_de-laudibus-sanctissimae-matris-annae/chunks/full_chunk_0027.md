So many and so great miracles concerning our blessed Anne have become known to me that, if I wished to write them all, it would compel me to enlarge this treatise too much. The greatest of divine miracles is this: if by her merits we may deserve to receive compunction and pardon for our sins; if to overcome an evil habit; if to attain eternal life. For this end, and no other, she is to be venerated by us.

*Chapter XV*

*On the brotherhood of the most blessed mother, how useful and necessary it is, especially in our times.*

For in these last days, as charity grows cold among many, iniquity abounds. It is very necessary for us, my brothers, that just as we fall almost daily by sinning, so also through frequent devoted exercises of holy practice we may be renewed every day in every good will. For there has never been any institution so holy among human customs that it has not, with time, grown lukewarm from its first fervor. Thus all the orders of religious, declining with the passage of time, have departed from their first institution; unless by the diligence of their superiors they had often been reformed to the paths of regular life, they would always have remained in their deformity. The same thing is also done among Christians, who today are found to have fallen far away from the first institution of the faith.

Where now is the former [unclear] of our faith? Charity has grown cold, and all the pursuits of religion have likewise perished along with it from among people, for the most part. Who would believe how great a loss evil morals have brought upon the Christian faith? Behold, with God permitting it because of the crimes of men, the unbelieving Saracens, Turks, Tartars, and the other enemies of the Catholic faith, after slaughtering Christians, have brought Asia, Africa, Greece, the Hellespont, and a great part of Europe under their power, so that a twentieth part of the world which once was Christian is not Christian today. And truly, unless the compassion of almighty God preserves the remnants left in Europe, there will soon be an end of the Christian name.

Since, therefore, weighed down by our sins, we are not sufficient to obtain God’s mercy, the intercessions of the saints must be implored for us with diligent prayers, so that, appeased by their merits, the eternal judge may grant us pardon and peace, which we do not deserve. And lest devotion fail in time, as is customary, we must work with great care so that through the mutual exhortation of the brothers it may attain perseverance.

Hence a praiseworthy custom has grown up in the Church of God: that the faithful establish congregations in honor of certain saints, which, for the increase of devotion, they call brotherhoods. Thus knights act in honor of Saint George; archers, of Sebastian the martyr; pilgrims, of James the Apostle from Galicia.
