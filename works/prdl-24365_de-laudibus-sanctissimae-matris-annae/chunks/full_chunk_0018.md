*That the holy mother Anne is held in the greatest honor with God and all the saints. Chapter IX.* We return, however, and hold without any hesitation that holy Anne, the parent of the Mother of God, is of singular merit and honor before God; by this she is not only sufficient for her own glory, but also becomes fit and powerful to bring us aid. For if we trust that the apostles and the other saints are powerful with God, we must necessarily believe that the parents of God are most powerful. For Anne, a most holy woman, pleased almighty God to such a degree that he wished the mother of his Only-begotten to be born from her; he would never have dedicated a womb for his mother unless he had found in it the merit of great holiness. For Scripture says: “The holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit, nor will it dwell in a body subject to sins.” In the most blessed mother Anne there was nothing deceitful, nothing that could have offended God, but all things were upright, clean, and holy, and worthy of the dwelling of the Holy Spirit. Therefore she is rightly believed to be next after her daughter in glory before God, since she was able to please him so greatly in this life. Let no foolish disputant object to me that the saints of the Old Testament were of lesser merit than those of the New, since time does not bestow holiness, but the grace of faith. I except Anne, whom I acknowledge as a lover not so much of the Old Testament as of the New. For she herself established the ark of the New Testament, and carried it for nine months in her womb. Nor does time diminish the holiness that God’s special grace has granted. So great a grace had gone before the most blessed Anne that in Israel in her time she had no equal in virtue and holiness. Accordingly it seems fitting that she should prevail before God in glory more than the rest, she who shone before others in life. Indeed, just as the Son of God surpasses his mother [unclear] where virtue abounds, affection does not cease where love is continued; the love of kinship does not perish where glory is owed to merits. Carnal affection ceases where there is no generation of the flesh. But love never ceases where it loves; affection, where love is continued, does not perish; the love of kinship does not perish where glory is owed to merit. I confess that in the blessed carnal affection ceases, and the generation of the flesh also ceases; but the love of kinship does not cease, since among them all it is common. For no one ever hated his own flesh. Nor is the Son of God so harsh that he does not love his holy parents, his own flesh, his own bowels. Or how would he not love his own parents, who loves all his saints and servants? For he says: “I love those who love me.” Who could ever love God according to the flesh more than his parents?
