The merits are rightly preferred to the gifts of Elizabeth. Saint Elizabeth bore a soldier; Anna, the mother of God. There is no comparison where there is no likeness. If Elizabeth was full of grace because of John, much more do I believe that Anna obtained grace because of Mary. Anna was holy before she conceived the mother of God, yet in the conception she was more fully sanctified, since she deserved to become the tabernacle of her who, above all creatures, was full of grace. We call a casket or ark in which the pledges of the saints are kept sanctified and honorable; how much more ought we to venerate as venerable Anna, in whom the mother of God lay without stain, honored and sanctifying. Truly holy is she who bore that one without the fault of original stain, than whom [unclear] of the saints will be confessed, because no one was ever holier. Truly holy is she who was chosen to be the mother of the mother of God before she was born. O womb never to be named without honor, in which the ark of God deserved to be fashioned without stain. Blessed the belly that carried the lady of heaven; happy the breasts that deserved to nurse the mother of God. Run, peoples, from every side, and from the merits of so great a patroness receive blessing. For indeed, if the holy devotion of the faithful is directed toward her, we believe that almighty God bestows great gifts upon mortals, and not less than he has been accustomed to give through daughters. Therefore you, O faithful peoples, who believe the daughter of Saint Anna to be the mother of God, who rest beneath the shadow of the holiness of both, if you desire to advance in the love of God, if you wish to come to the heavenly fatherland, do not neglect the memory of the most holy mother. For after the queen herself she is the most powerful in the hall of the heavenly emperor, because from her blood the Son of God took flesh from the virgin. From whatever tribulation you cry to her, you will find help. She has power to grant you all things that you can ask in faith. She heals the sick, cleanses crimes, gladdens the hearts of the sorrowful with holy exultation, calms sad things, smooths rough things, drives away the phantasms of demons. As ice is dissolved by fire, so by the merit of the most holy mother Anna the ancient enemy, fleeing, is thrown into confusion. Anna, ark of piety, drives away disgrace, preserves fair fame, and marvelously removes base love from the minds of her faithful. Therefore honor Anna, you who lead the toilsome life of this world. Anna obtains pardon for sinners, roots out vices, plants virtues. Anna cures illnesses, heals wounds, exterminates pestilence. Therefore honor this mother, O children, who is powerful to bring you into the sight of God; whoever has her as advocate before God will fear no adversities of this world. Whoever, therefore, desires to lead a tranquil and holy life on earth, whoever earnestly asks pardon for his sins, should never depart from the service of this most holy mother. For she rewards her worshippers in many ways even in this life, and prepares eternal gifts for them if they persevere. I was younger than I am, and I have never seen a servant devoted to Saint Anna abandoned by God, nor his reputation stained by shameful reproach.

Accordingly I urge you, O men distinguished by learning and knowledge of letters, not to despise the devotion of the simple, nor to criticize the cult of the most holy mother Anna as something new, but rather to imitate it as far as you can. For I was not persuaded in vain to say these things. For there are among you, if I may say so with the peace of good men, some who, swollen with the stamp of pride, despise all the devotional exercises of the simple, scorn the miracles and examples of the saints as ravings, and think that nothing holy should be admitted which they do not prove by the arguments of the philosophers. All revelations shown by God to devout people they regard as lies or women’s dreams; they call the legends of the saints fables; and while they rashly proclaim their own learning, they shamelessly attack the great works of God. They also reject the treatises of the holy fathers and of devout people, because they do not bear Ciceronian eloquence in their defense, as though they were lacking in learning, and they despise the words of heavenly instruction because of the instruction of the simple. O fools and subverters, why do you deceive yourselves? Why, though you are Christians, do you despise ecclesiastical simplicity? The grace of God is not known in eloquence, but is acquired in holy simplicity. At the final judgment of God, Quintilian eloquence will not be required of you, but Christian life. Ornate speech is not rewarded by God, but humble devotion. There sophistical arguments will cease, when the secrets of hearts will be manifest. Then the simple and devout will appear in glory, and the learned proud in punishment. Behold, the unlearned simple arise and receive the kingdom of God in their devout simplicity, and you with your doctrines descend in pride and arrogance to hell.
