God had chosen her before the foundation of the world and had pre-elected her, and what he had ordained without time he fulfilled in this presentation. How great, we think, was the joy then in heaven, when Mary, a young virgin, was joined to the heavenly bridegroom. The most holy virgin is led by her mother into the temple, she who was to bear the Lord of the temple; and now, as a little child of three years, she is made ready for divine nuptials. Who will be able to recount your joy then, O mother Anna, when you devoutly handed over to the temple, to serve there, your daughter received from the Lord, indeed the one who was to bear the Lord of the temple? No one ever offered so pleasing a gift to God; no one ever presented to the temple a treasure of so great a price. The holy parents marvel in their daughter at qualities beyond her age; they marvel at her grace; they marvel at her eagerness. For she was full of grace from the womb, nor was she ever subject to any stain, however slight, she who was the temple of God. Why do you marvel at your daughter, mother? The grace of God is not enclosed under the law of nature; the power of the Holy Spirit does not require age to accomplish what it wishes; the gift and will of the Most High is not constrained under many years. The Most High sanctified his tabernacle, and the ark in which he had resolved to place the true manna he most beautifully adorned within and without with the purest gold of virtues. How perfect the mother was in the love of God she declared in this offering of her sweetest daughter. For unless she had been steadfast in the fear of the Lord, she would never have allowed her daughter, beloved as her own life, tender and noble, to be torn from her. But love of the Creator overcame a mother’s heart, and the pledge most dear above all created things she offered with cheerful countenance to the Lord, to be consecrated. Nothing here is done by chance; custom works nothing; all things without time were ordained by the Most High; all things by the counsel of God were foreordained from eternity. O Joachim and Anna, most blessed parents, who were worthy to bring forth her who, without male seed, deserved to beget the Son of God. Truly an offering always to be honored with fitting praises, which provided a mother for God, a queen for the angels, and a savior for the world long lost. Moreover we believe this offering to have been adorned with such purity that under God no earlier one could exist; for she was made queen of the angels, mother of God, lady of the world. In the greatest glory before God, too, these most blessed parents must be believed to be, who begot a mother for the Son of God. Learn, O parents, to set the fear of God before your children; learn to form them when young in the love of God, lest, if you neglect them as little ones, you later find them obstinate. Anna offered her sweetest three-year-old daughter to the Lord; she taught the little child to fear God; she presented her to the temple to be instructed in the fear of the Lord. But you do not do so. You instruct your daughters for dances and the love of worldly vanities; you tell them nothing of the fear of the Lord; you persuade them of nothing concerning eternal life, since indeed you have learned nothing good from your parents. Let not a woman glory in the multitude of her children if she has neglected to instruct them properly in the charity of God, for it is not multitude of children but uprightness that commends women.
