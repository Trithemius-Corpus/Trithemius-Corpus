Therefore, dearest brothers, let us choose as our advocate her whom we have known from eternity always to have pleased holy God, and, venerating her daily, let us honor her with fitting praises. Let married people honor her, because by her most holy childbirth she consecrated marriage to the Lord, for whom she bore a mother without stain. Let widows and the continent praise her, because she prefigured the rule of that continence. Let virgins love her, because she bore a virgin of such holiness that no one holier ever shone forth. Let the righteous honor her, because by her own most holy manner of life she showed them the rule of righteousness. Let sinners venerate her, because she bore the mother of him who makes the righteous from sinners. Let all venerate her with the greatest love, because she brought forth the mother of him who came as redeemer for all. Let no one grow sluggish in her praises; let no one fail in love for her; let no one become lukewarm in her worship. Let all love her as a common mother; let all honor her as a public advocate. By her gift all received the common Savior. For she will obtain for us pardon of sins, from whose blood the fountain of mercy flowed forth bodily. Rise up before her, brothers, because she does not abandon those who hope in her; she relieves the oppressed; she sustains those who have been relieved; she protects widows; she mercifully [defends?] orphans and wards; she [unclear] the weak; she raises and heals the feeble; she confirms those raised up in virtue. It is impossible that she, praying for her devotees, should not be heard, who built from her own breast so precious a bridal chamber for almighty God.

*How just, holy, and acceptable to God was the life and conduct of the most blessed mother Anne. Chapter VI.*

Whoever has read through the memorials of the holy fathers with diligent devotion can discover with how great holiness the life of the most blessed mother Anne shone. Concerning her, the holy prelate Epiphanius composed certain things; he records that her life shone with such righteousness, innocence, and purity that she had no equal in Israel. Honorius too, priest of Autun, a holy and learned man, published in the *Speculum Ecclesiae* certain things in a lucid discourse on the praises of the holy mother Anne, and showed in brief summary how holy she always lived. Saint Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, once a cultivator of Mount Carmel and of the Carmelite order, when he wrote to Pope Celestine I concerning the birth of the blessed Mother of God, Mary ever virgin, inserted many proclamations of praise concerning the life and conduct of Saint Anne. But what need is there for me to cite many unknown doctors, when blessed Jerome the priest, named throughout the whole world, is said to have written certain praiseworthy things on this very matter, which are held by many?

Therefore the most blessed Anne, so far as her generation according to the flesh was concerned, was sprung from the tribe of David, from Bethlehem, with Stolanus as her father and Emerentia as her mother, parents religious and devout according to the decrees of the law, who nourished her, together with her sister Esmeria, in the fear of the Lord from infancy. Moreover Esmeria, her sister, bore Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist. Joachim of Nazareth, of the tribe of Judah, took Anne as wife; she bore a daughter, Mary, afterwards Mother of God and ever virgin. O Joachim and Anne, parents worthy of eternal memory, who, serving the Lord God in simplicity, offered an inestimable treasure. Who could suffice to praise these parents worthily? For truly, unless their life had been most holy, they would never have brought forth offspring so pleasing to the Lord.

They were of moderate but sufficient substance, obtaining what was necessary from the pasturing of sheep and from agriculture. From these things they are said to have made three equal portions: one they gave to the temple, another to the poor, and the third they kept for themselves for domestic uses. As for their character, they were most diligent in the observance of the law, approved in the fear of the Lord, perfect in love of God and neighbor. They adorned the marriage-bed with the fullest virtues, in which they advanced so far that they pleased God and men. In concord, innocence, and piety they were of one mind, and rich in every virtue. Both derived their origin from the house of David; both were perfect before the Lord; both awaited the redemption of Israel with longing. Both from their earliest age had been instructed in the fear of the Lord; both were dear to God; both were chosen to be the parents of the Mother of God. Anne, however, is believed to have been filled with greater grace, without disparagement to her husband, in whose venerable womb the most holy Mother of God was being formed without any stain. In her breast the love of God found for itself a gracious dwelling; from her blood the Holy Spirit built for the Son of God a most pure tabernacle.
