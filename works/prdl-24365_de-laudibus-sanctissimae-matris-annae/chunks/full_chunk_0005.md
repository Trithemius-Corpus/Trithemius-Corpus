I, who am not worthy to lift up my face to the heavens because of the multitude of my iniquity, how much less should I dare to touch the praises of most blessed Anne with impure lips. Yet by your grace I am what I am, and all the good that I will and am able to do. For you, Lord, enlighten the will by your grace, that it may will good things and be made good. You also are he who grants us to work for a good will. In all things we acknowledge your grace going before our will, and in the good things that we will and do we proclaim your gifts, without any merits of ours. Nothing comes from us except evil; nothing proceeds from you except good.

Since, therefore, I can do nothing good of myself without your grace, I devoutly flee to you; I appeal to you in humble subjection, that what my merits impede, you may mercifully bestow by the gift of your grace. Give me, Lord, wisdom, the attendant of your seats, that she may be with me and labor with me at all times, and especially in this work in which, with slender powers, I have resolved to praise your name in holy mother Anne.

You also, Anne, most holy mother, whose memory is being treated, deign in your wonted kindness to be present with me, that I may be made fit to set forth your praises, and may be found a faithful herald of your merits. My lady, my hope, my advocate in every tribulation, now mercifully be present to one who desires to praise you; and since by manifold kindness you have stirred up the will to write, obtain by your merits ability for my poor talent, that it may be sufficient to praise you worthily. I seek nothing except your honor. I desire nothing except to please you. I intend to write nothing about your praises except what is right and true. Therefore, I pray, bring help; give the hand of assistance; obtain grace.

You also, immaculate Mother of God, who taught your servants to honor Anne, your venerable mother, be present to me, who desire to set forth her praises, by your merits, so that the devotion of your servant may obtain in the work the due effect which the grace of God conceived in the mind. You also, blessed spirits of the heavenly fatherland, who exult in the most sacred fruit of mother and daughter, the virgin, our Lord Jesus Christ, make me by your prayers a worthy praiser of Anne, and obtain for me the grace of the Holy Spirit. If you honor mother and daughter in heavenly joy, it is fitting that you assist their praisers in this pilgrimage.

You also, most blessed Anne’s devoted worshippers, do not detract from our pious studies, but if in anything we have failed through weakness, excuse it through charity. Nothing is written by me through contention or vain glory. Nothing of apocryphal doubtful character is alleged; nothing is assumed except what has been approved. I receive nothing that opposes the divine ordinances. I write nothing that stains the Christian faith. I introduce nothing that does not accord with holy morals.
