Look to both: mothers to the mother, virgins to the virgin. Believe me, it does not befit your honor to delight in public; it is not expedient for virgins to be greeted in the marketplace, because he who frequently goes into public diminishes the reverence of honor. When virginity is modest, it flees the public. Without modesty there is no sincere virginity, nor can one who is too public remain pure.

*That holy mother Anne conceived her daughter, the most blessed Mother of God, without original stain. Chapter 7.*

Since it greatly contributes to declaring the merits of the sanctity of the most blessed mother Anne, that she bore the Mother of God without the stain of original guilt, the prerogative of so great an honor must by no means be passed over by us in silence. And indeed we know that this most pure conception was set as a sign, against which many contradict down to the present day; while they wish to defend the custom of nature, they are not afraid to attack the grace of God. Why do the foolish bark? Why do the unlearned murmur? Is it not permitted to God to do what he wills? Behold, the Church venerates the conception of the Mother of God as pure, without stain; behold, it celebrates the feast with annual devotion, and contentious men labor to stain it with rash presumption. Holy and most learned men, past and present, in various kinds of writing, proclaim in their lucubrations that this conception was never subject to original stain; and still the sons of Adam rashly attack it. Almost all religious orders of diverse kinds confess, believe, and preach that she always existed most pure; to wish to condemn them as erring is extreme madness. Behold, secular men, religious, monks, devout and learned, celebrate in verse and prose the ever most pure conception; and lukewarm and unlearned men, for whom it seems enough to know the name of philosophy, strive to besmirch the holiness of the Mother of God. Many, I say, most learned men have published various treatises in its defense. What learned man has ever written the contrary? But you, indeed unworthy of your name, [Nicator?], who, setting your mouth against heaven, have impiously blasphemed against the most pure Mother of God, will not have done this without God’s grave notice, since you were moved to write not by piety but by impiety. Finally, we know that more than enough answer has been made to your foolish rashness by learned men in various styles of writing. And you depart confounded not by our arms, but by the power of God. Nor do I condemn your error, but your arrogance, your rashness, your presumption.
