Truly a foolish consolation, if you are praised where you will never be, and are tormented where you will remain always, without end. Therefore I admonish and counsel you: put away haughtiness, drive out madness, cast aside fear, so that you may be able to escape the torments of hell. For God resists the proud; he regards not learning, but humble devotion; he rewards not words, but deeds; not knowledge, but life; not doctrine, but conscience. Rather, change your purpose, and do not despise the devotion of the simple, but imitate it. No one understands the house of God except him who receives it. What, then, you do not understand, with what rashness do you tear apart? The gift of God is not enclosed under the reasoning of philosophy. Devotion is rare among the learned, because, while they trust in the profundity of their acquired doctrine, they withdraw farther from simple devotion.

For the grace of God is not well received when it is examined by human curiosity. While savor is sought in speech, inward sweetness is lost. The Holy Spirit despises a proud dwelling. Therefore shut your rash mouths, restrain your badly eloquent tongues, and do not speak falsehood against the Lord. Do not tear apart the devotion of the simple, lest it be counted against you as a grave scandal. Let no one think me, as I say these things, an enemy of the good arts; let no one believe me a slanderer of the learned, since I have always cultivated and loved letters, have always been accustomed to honor learned men, and have sometimes defended them even to the point of hatred and persecution by the unskilled. But let those who seem learned in their own eyes beware, lest, by despising the devotion of the simple, they think that they ought to be called back from reverence for Saint Anne. But if any such men are to exist, I would believe no learned man to be among their number, since true learning is accustomed to assist devotion, not to rebuke it. Many very learned men also, still living today, exercising their talents magnificently in verse and prose, have dedicated them to the merits of Saint Anne.

*Chapter Four.*

*Against those who tear apart, deride, and attack the feast and memory of Saint Anne.*

Hear and understand, whoever you are, who disparage the festive memory of the most holy mother Anne, who attack her honor with impious talkativeness, who blaspheme her devotees with rash presumption. What displeases you in the most holy mother of the Mother of God? Do you prove her degenerate? She is holy; she is beloved by God. Foolish and unwise man, who blame as faults the good in the servants of God which you ought to imitate; who withhold the honor owed to the saints; who persecute the devotees of most blessed Anne without cause. For you reproach them and say: Why do you take up new feasts, you who do not celebrate the ancient ones? Listen, profane man. Does the feast of Saint Anne seem new to you, which is proved from the ancient monuments of the old to be most ancient? If it is new, how could the holy fathers have celebrated it in antiquity? It is not new, in whose honor Saint Augustine is said to have composed an elegant sermon a thousand years ago. That cannot be new about which very ancient songs exist. Or how will that be able to be new whose golden name is found inserted in the most ancient calendars? But to you it is both new and rare, you who have never celebrated her memory. Nor can the memory of the most blessed mother Anne be new, whose house, in which she had been accustomed to live in antiquity, is read to have been consecrated as a church under her name not long after the Passion of the Lord.
