This fraternity of the most blessed mother Anne is not superstitious, as you say, O impious slanderers, but it is true, upright, holy; in it the worship of God is not diminished but increased, obedience is not despised but commended. Nor is the Catholic faith weakened through this holy fraternity, as you think, but renewed and defended, while through the exercises of holy charity it is illumined as being formed.

But as for your lament that parish churches are deserted, this is to be imputed in no small measure to your own offenses: you exact milk and wool from the sheep of the Lord, yet you do not feed the sheep themselves as you especially ought, nor do you guard them. Why do you pretend that offerings are denied to you, when you abound in many riches? Christ is the vicar of all pastors; if he has wished the faithful to be free in hearing masses, there is no one who can prevent him. You have not seen all the privileges of the monks, nor will you see them. Who does not know that you wage war against holy fraternities for the sake of your purses, and that you tear at monks not from love of God but of gold?

Behold, the supreme pontiff has confirmed this fraternity; behold, Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, than whom there is no other more excellent, has approved it by ordinary authority; and you foolish men still murmur. Cease, I beg you, cease from this folly, and do not censure what you know such great pontiffs to have approved. Believe me, it is not good to wish to forbid so greatly the devotion of men toward holy Anne. He approaches the greatest confusion who opens his mouth against holy Anne. If you do not believe my words, observe those who are such, and by experience you will know that I have spoken truly.

It is rash to censure a good that is seen to redound to salvation for many. Many good things come to devout men from the celebration of this most blessed fraternity, of which those who are strangers to it are for the most part empty. It also draws its worshipers to love of God and neighbor; it creates concord of brotherly love; it calls back from vices and sins; it inflames desire for the love of heavenly happiness. I pass over how many masses, how many prayers, how many alms, how many [unclear] it kindles in the minds of all, with how great devotion, how great peace and how great concord might redound in the Catholic Church. For indeed, if all were to come together into this fraternity, it could happen that, for love of holy Anne, they would desist from mutual injuries and be made more ready in observance of the commandments of God. For true fraternity cultivates and nourishes brotherly love, nor does devotion allow those to be unlike whom the observance of so great a congregation has made concordant in the love of most holy Anne.
