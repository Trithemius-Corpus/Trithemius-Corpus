Anne, parent, venerable mother of the highest parent,
you who first open for peoples the path of salvation:
from you all diseases flee, and pallor and pain,
and all harmful things that settle in soul and body.
Whatever there is among human beings that wearies cares and hearts
flees you, as clouds driven by the winds are wont to do.
Rutgerus [Venrai?], regular canon of [Hayn?],
to the image of Saint Anne.

Here pour out deep prayers, devoutly, with knee bent,
where Anne the parent sits with her blessed offspring.
Do not doubt: you will obtain whatever you ask of her,
for Anne continually shines, powerful in prayers.

*Distich of the same author on Saint Anne*

Hail, Anne, mother of the mother of Jesus Christ,
and, we ask, drive every wickedness from us.

*The rosary of Jodocus Beissel, patrician of Aachen, on Saint Anne, to the Reverend Father Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict.*

Whoever wishes to read this rosary out of devotion in honor of the most blessed mother Anne should place before it the Lord's Prayer with the salutation of Saint Anne, which he should always repeat after two verses. That salutation is as follows.

O venerable Anne, mother of the Mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women: blessed are you by the Lord, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, and Jesus Christ, born of the offspring of the Virgin. Amen.

Our Father. Hail, [mother] of God.

Anne, may I, wretched as I am, be allowed to utter your praises,
for to you, if you permit it, I shall bring garlands and roses.
Anne, you shine, venerable offspring of the kings of Judah,
and also renowned through your pious husband Joachim,
the sole glory of married women, and their one honor.
Pious Anne, you seek offspring not for vain uses,
but so that with holy posterity she may worship God.
"Rejoice," he says; "behold, exulting mother, you shall have offspring."
Anne, you will bear offspring blessed with such great virtue
that, though a mother, she will gleam with virginal honor.

Anne, you will [bear?] the gracious one whom the world does not contain;
God himself willingly enters the chaste womb. Hail.
Anne, at last from your blood will arise that blood
which, when poured out, will bring hope forever to wretched sinners. Hail.
Anne, so great an expectation befitted your childbearing;
it is by no means easy to be held the mother of the mother of God.
Anne, the praises of your offspring will fly above the stars,
and all ages will proclaim you happy also.

Our Father. Hail, [mother] of God.

Anne, you are believed to have conceived Mary pure from evil,
and soon to have had her holy for God. Hail.
Anne, in your womb you carry her whom figures marked as coming,
whom the holy fathers sang of. Hail.
Anne, you carry the enclosed paradise, the happy tree of life,
and one sacred with all the honor of the field. Hail.
A burning cherub guards it night and day.
Anne, you carry a flower that surpasses white lilies,
and lowly violets, and the crimson rose.
And the golden urn of heavenly bread lies there. Hail.
Anne, you carry the bride of the true Solomon,
adorned with every jewel, within and without the house.
Anne, you carry the temple whose inner chambers sweet sounds
and all rejoicing music have filled. Hail.
Anne, you carry the veil under a beautiful image,
holding in itself the deeds of the fathers, which covers the deep things of God. Hail.
Anne, you carry the offspring whose head Phoebus' golden
flame clothes, and whose snowy feet the shining moon clothes. Hail.

Our Father. Hail, [mother] of God.

Anne, now made possessor of longed-for Mary,
you scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have sprung up. Hail.
Anne, all the stars applauded Mary newly born,
and an unwonted radiance flowed from the pole.
Anne, it is right to believe that angelic choirs around you
celebrated the birthday of your child. Hail.
