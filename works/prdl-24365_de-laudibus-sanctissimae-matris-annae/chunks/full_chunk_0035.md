I have noted beforehand concerning the mother of God and virgin, so that no one may take it otherwise than my intention has it; I thought the reader should be warned. For I know, and it does not escape me, how many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the stain of original sin, men who flourished long before the Council of Basel. Their opinion, making allowance for the time, I pass over wholly untouched, and I in no way intend to say or write anything against them. Indeed, if anything spoken by me seems rather harsh, let it be referred not to those who think otherwise than we do, but only to the obstinate, who, prattling without learning, do not hesitate to call the defenders of the other opinion heretics. And my words indicate this, since they denote the present time and not the past. For when I say what the foolish bark, what the unlearned mutter, that belongs to the present time, not the past, and today it reproves the obstinate, not those of old who held a different opinion. But when I ask what learned man has ever written the contrary, I am not speaking of the ancient fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the aforesaid council, hold a different opinion. Thus I censure only the obstinacy of certain unlearned men of this time, not the opinion of any learned men whatsoever who think differently, since I am not ignorant of the declaration of Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff. But it has seemed to me in no way alien to the holy Roman Church to hold the faith of the most pure conception, which she believes and venerates, as is shown today by many proofs. Finally, together with the doctors of the universities of Paris, Cologne, Heidelberg, Erfurt, Vienna, Tübingen, Ingolstadt, Basel, Mainz, and many others, and with almost all orders and the faithful of Christ spread throughout the whole Christian world, I do not fear to venerate as pure the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary. Yet I do not admit obstinacy, but I wish in all things to obey the Roman Church as the most reverend mother of all churches. This is the pure and sincere intention of my aforesaid chapter, in which I think no one who holds an opinion soberly and without obstinacy is disparaged. Farewell, reader.

This letter of the aforesaid lord Johannes, abbot, is lacking in certain copies through carelessness and negligence; nevertheless he ordered it to be appended to all copies, and decreed that it be regarded as appended to each.
