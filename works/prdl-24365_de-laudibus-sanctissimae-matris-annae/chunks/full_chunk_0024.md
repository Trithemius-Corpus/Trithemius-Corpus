Likewise let the leaders and brothers of the other religious orders do the same as far as they can, and let them never at any time grow lukewarm in the worship of so great a mother. But you, O professors of the Carmelite order, who by the most ancient privilege are specially distinguished by the title of the most blessed mother of God and Virgin Mary: you ought to venerate and embrace most holy Anne with singular devotion, from whose daughter you are called venerable. Take care, therefore, that you never be lukewarm in the worship of the mother, you who from her daughter have obtained the name of so great an honor. You also, all others chosen into the lot of the Lord, extend your souls to the honor of the most holy mother of the mother of God; bestow on her whatever praises you can, and instruct those subject to you, as far as possible, to do the same. You too, O men distinguished by learning in letters, in whom the vein of composition abounds: dedicate your talents to the divine mother Anne; play in verse in her honor, and, as much as you are able, praise her also in prose. But the simpler, whose abilities are slower and not sufficiently suited for composing, should copy out with devotion the things that have been published by others; they should read them more often, and for the edification also of others should affix them to the altars of the holy mother. Let them read prayers suited to her honor; let them read due praises; let them read hymns, songs, and proses. Let no one be free from the praises of Anne; let no one be silent; let no one grow lukewarm. Let all nations redeemed by the blood of Christ celebrate the memory of Anne, from whose merits a great hope of salvation is promised to us. But lest matter for prayer be lacking to the devout worshippers of Anne, three years ago we made, in her honor, a brief prayer in the form of the angelic salutation, which is now both had and read by many. And it seems to us sufficiently fitting that, just as in the rosary of the mother of God we pay five Lord’s Prayers and fifty angelic salutations, so also we should do in honor of Saint Anne, yet in such a way that in place of Ave Maria our prayer which follows should be said.

*Prayer.*

O venerable Anne, mother of the mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women: blessed are you in the Lord, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, and the virgin-born son of that offspring, Jesus Christ. Amen.

Many appoint this prayer in honor of Saint Anne in place of the angelic salutation in the rosary, because it is both brief and by no means useless. But if our prayer for the rosary of Saint Anne does not please someone, let him read the salutation, as is the custom, angelic; and at the end, after he has said “Jesus Christ,” let him add this clause: And blessed be your most honorable mother, Saint Anne, from whom your virgin flesh proceeded without stain. Amen.

The holy mother of God is said to have revealed this clause to a certain recluse devoted to her. If this too does not please someone, let him make, according to his devotion, some other prayer suited to the honors of the most blessed mother Anne, as best he can.
