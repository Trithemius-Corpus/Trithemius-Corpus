If you love God, if you love Jesus Christ, the Savior of all, [do not] do so great an injury to his most holy Mother, concerning whom no one is permitted to think [anything but] most holy things. To believe that her conception was always most pure belongs not to necessity but to piety; not to believe it belongs to human slowness. But, by not believing, to attack it, and to wish to defend the contrary opinion, is not only not without fault, but is also a matter of great rashness and folly. For indeed rashness is rightly reproved where piety is rewarded. Let no one think that sin a small thing which is committed against God and his Mother. But he who loves the Son of God does not open his mouth against the honor of his Mother; he does not detract from her purity; he does not stubbornly defend an uncertain opinion. And because where the devotion of the humble receives a reward, the presumption of the proud will find torment. For the Mother of God is believed to have been worthy to be conceived without the original stain, she who, as a virgin, was to bear him who would wipe away the sins of the whole world. Blessed Mother Anna too was worthy of such a conception, whose renowned way of life was most pleasing to God. I do not wish you to wonder that the Mother of God, contrary to the custom of nature, was preserved from original sin, she whom God, the infuser of grace, had prepared as a dwelling for himself. Consider the one born as Mother of God, and you will not wonder at a conception without stain. Great are the things that are said, but true, but holy, but fitting for the Mother of God, whose conception grace anticipated; and at this nature marvels. We too marvel, because we faithfully believe. But the adversaries, not believing, do not marvel, but are confounded. If you can grasp these things, believe; if you do not grasp them, do not examine them, if you do not wish to sin. For it is better to keep reverent silence concerning the purity of the most blessed Mother of God than to speak unworthy things. Take care, therefore, always to honor with humble silence those things which you cannot understand.

*How the most blessed mother Anna presented her sweetest daughter to the Lord in the temple.*

*Chapter 8.*

In the beginning of the book of Kings we read that Anna, the wife of Elkanah, offered her son Samuel, whom she had asked from the Lord, to God’s service at the temple after the years of weaning. Indeed, she became so pleasing to God by the offering of Samuel that he not only chose him as priest, after Eli had been rejected, but also filled him with the spirit of prophecy and wisdom. What, brothers, is presented to us by Samuel’s offering by his mother? O how pleasing to almighty God was that offering, in which already then a most worthy dwelling was being prepared for the Only-begotten.
