*... he chose Anna as the mother of his mother before the foundation of the world. Chapter 5.*

Just as almighty God, before the foundation of the world, appointed the time at which he would be born in the flesh, so too from eternity he preordained the parents from whom he would be born. For the Son of God did not find his parents, but sent them before: he who, without time, ordained them for time did not come upon them by chance accident, but by a wondrous dispensation chose them beforehand from eternity. Therefore most blessed Anna could confidently say that which is written of the eternal wisdom of God: “The Lord possessed me at the beginning of his ways, before he made anything from the beginning. From eternity I was ordained, and from ancient times.” Before the foundation of the world she existed in the divine mind, made not by nature but by grace pre-[unclear].

Worthy to be [unclear] and honored is she whom the grace of God, by going before, appointed as [unclear] of the mother, and by always preserving made most holy [unclear]. Truly holy and most worthy of every honor is the parent who provided for us a rule of holier life and brought forth from her own breast the fountain of inexhaustible piety. She could not have been anything but most holy, who bore so precious a treasure in her womb. She was the most noble ark of the covenant, shining with the adornment of all virtues, in which was hidden that vessel which was the true manna, the virgin who was to give birth. With how great honor must Anna’s womb be judged worthy, which, made God’s treasury, carried a treasure of inestimable price.

There must be no doubting, dearest brothers, but it must be believed with firm devotion, that blessed Anna was then filled with ineffable joy, when she bore in her most blessed womb for nine months the future mother of God, made worthy by the Holy Spirit from her conception and preserved clean from every stain. O who can narrate the joys of that most holy mother Anna, which she sensibly perceived in her breast from the presence of the Holy Spirit? What voice can express, what tongue relate, what mortal mind of a wayfarer comprehend, with how much gladness and exultation the inmost parts of most blessed Anna were filled, when she merited to feel the grace of the Holy Spirit in the heart of her daughter not yet born?

If Elizabeth is said to have been filled with the Holy Spirit at the presence of the soldier, that this happened more abundantly in venerable Anna is proved by many arguments. For the former bore in her womb the herald of the highest king; the latter, the mother. The former had conceived the soldier; the latter, the queen. John, conceived in the womb, was sanctified; Mary also, in conception, was preserved. Finally, the Holy Spirit, choosing Mary from the hour of conception as his most pure bride and dwelling in her, never afterward abandoned her, not even for a moment; but, always guarding and directing her, he illuminated her with a special gift above all mortals. We believe that the most blessed mother of this virgin shared in this most sacred illumination, not only when she was bearing the virgin still enclosed in her womb, but also at all times afterward [unclear].
