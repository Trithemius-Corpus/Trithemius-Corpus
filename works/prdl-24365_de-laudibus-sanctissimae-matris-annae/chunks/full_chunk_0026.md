Who could enumerate how many have been healed, by the aid of the most dutiful mother Anne, from destructive sadness of mind and melancholy? How many also have escaped secure amid adversities, fearless amid enemies and robbers, safe amid the dangers of the sea? The most holy mother Anne has been accustomed to rescue many devoted to her from various [unclear], to free them from the contagion of pestilence, and also to cure them of various infirmities. Who could tell how many she has brought back from the worst despair to the hope of mercy; how many she has recalled from the hardest habit of sin to the paths of penitence; how many, living lukewarmly in religion and outside it, she has inflamed with the fire of divine love? By her merits and intercessions women in childbirth, otherwise near to death, have been delivered, and many have even been snatched, as we read, from the sentence of eternal damnation. Finally, it is said that she has guarded some of her worshippers from the reproach of infamy; she is remembered to have delivered others from a desperate illness, some from chains and prisons, and certain others from the dangers of imminent death. We also read that by her merits some have been recalled from death to life; others, at the point of death, have been most mercifully visited through her, and before they left the world were already made secure concerning heaven. Often, too, she is said to have illuminated the dulled wits of some, so that those who before had been despised for lack of learning afterward became an object of admiration to many. Orators also, often faltering in speaking, she is said, when invoked, to have strengthened so greatly that they found themselves made not only steadfast but also readier in delivering a speech. Thus, finally, the [unclear] through Anne is immense. For if I had a voice of iron and a hundred tongues, I could not run through all Anne’s benefits by which she is accustomed to aid her worshippers. Indeed, as many are her benefits as there are hardships of human life. In whatever tribulation anyone cries to her with devotion, he can obtain help. She extinguishes the flames of carnal desire; she turns destructive sadness of mind into saving joy; she grants what is necessary for life; she preserves a good and fair reputation. She delivers women in danger in childbirth, mitigates pains, drives away demons. In tribulation it is permitted for a person to seek [her]. Only let devout faith commend the one who prays. But concerning all the things which, in recalling them by category, we have merely touched on, we have at hand particular examples of deeds done, by which we could write not uncertain things, but relate deeds in truth.
