MAGIC.

The figure of a bearded man, having a long face and curved eyebrows, sitting on a plough between two bulls and holding a vulture in his hand, carved in stone: know that it has power in the planting of trees and the discovery of treasures, and makes a man victorious in battle. Serpents are put to flight from the face of the one who wears it; it heals epileptics and removes every vexation of evil spirits. It ought to be set in an iron ring and worn.

The figure of a man holding a sickle in his hand above his head, with a crocodile beneath his feet: when it has been carved in stone and set in a leaden ring, and a little root of squill is beneath the stone, whoever carries this ring with him will be safe from enemies, and from robbers on a journey.

The figure of a man holding a sword in his hand and sitting upon a dragon: when it has been carved in amethyst and set in a leaden or iron ring, and is worn on the finger, spirits will obey and will reveal to him any treasures whatever.

The figure of a flying eagle: when it has been carved in an aetites stone and set in a leaden ring, it has the power, for the one who carries it with him, of catching many fish; nor will any wild beast harm him, and he will be loved by men.

The figure of a man having a palm in his hand [unclear], if it is carved in any stone, makes the wearer pleasing and lovable to the powerful and to princes. These things are from Hermes.

TRACTATE IV.

THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF THETEL.

Thetel, a very ancient teacher, treating of carvings on stones, says that these carvings, when found in suitable stones, have very great power.

The image of a man carved in jasper, having a shield in his left hand and in his right an idol or something warlike, and with vipers in place of feet, and in place of a human head the head of a cock or a lion, will give power against enemies and makes the wearer victorious; it is effective against poisons also, and checks blood from whatever part it flows.

The figure of a man having a bundle of herbs on his neck, when carved in jasper, has the power of discerning or recognizing illnesses; it restrains blood from whatever place it flows. They say that Galen had this stone.

They say that a cross carved in green jasper has the power of freeing the wearer from drowning in water.

A woman having in one hand a bird and in the other a fish: when carved in chrysolite, it is of great value for doing business.

The figure of a wolf carved in jasper is effective against [unclear] and prevents the utterance of insane words.

The figure of a stag carved in jasper has the power of healing and freeing lunatics and the frantic.

The figure of an emperor carved in jasper, with head raised, makes the wearer lovable to every creature, and whatever he asks he will obtain.

The figure of a virgin with flowing garment, holding laurel in her hand, when carved in jasper, makes the wearer safe from drowning in water; nor will he be troubled by a demon. It makes him powerful and able to obtain all things.

The figure of a man having a palm marked in his hand, when found in jasper, makes the wearer powerful and pleasing to princes. Thus far Thetel.

TRACTATE V.

THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF SOLOMON.

A very ancient little book was found in the desert among the children of Israel, which, because many works of Solomon were found in it, was ascribed to Solomon. The title of the book was: On the Seals of Stones According to the Name of the Lord and According to the Course of the Stars.

The figure of a man sitting on a plough, having a small neck and a long beard, with four men lying on his neck, and holding in one hand a fox and in the other a vulture: know that this seal, hung from the neck, is effective for all plantings and all discoveries of treasures. You shall make trial of it in this way: take pure black wool without grease, wrap the stone in it, place it among wheat chaff, and put it under your head; then in sleep you will see all the treasures of that region in which it is. It also has the power of healing all the illnesses of beasts, if they drink the water in which it has been washed.
