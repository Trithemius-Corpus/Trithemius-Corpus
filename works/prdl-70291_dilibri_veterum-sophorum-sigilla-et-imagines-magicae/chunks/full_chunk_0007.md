A man carved in green jasper, having a shield hanging from his neck, and above his head a helmet with an upright sword, and trampling a serpent under his feet: place it on your neck, and you will fear no enemy; in all things you will be victorious, especially in martial affairs, and it ought to be bound in [unclear].

A horse bearing a crocodile on its back, carved in white hyacinth, makes one lovable to humans and beasts.

The figure of a seated man and of a woman standing before him, having hair stretched down to the thigh, and raising her eyes upward, when it has been carved in cornelian: know that its power is to render every person, male or female, obedient; and it ought to be bound in as much weight as the stone itself has. Put betony and amber beneath it.

A foaming horse, with a man upon it holding a scepter in his hand, carved in amethyst: know that this seal is useful for all the affairs of the one who bears it, and all princes and chief men will be obedient to him. It ought to be bound in gold or silver in double the weight of the seal.

If the image of a man’s head with a long beard and a little blood around the neck is found carved in diamond, know that it confers victory and boldness, preserves the body from every injury, and is effective for obtaining favor from princes.

The figure of a man wishing to play instruments, carved in sapphire, makes one most pleasing to everyone.

The image of a cock, or of three girls, when found in agate, makes a person pleasing to God and to men.

The figure of a lion carved in garnet gives riches and honors, gladdens, and drives away sadness.

The image of a stag or a dove carved in onyx gives the bearer courage against demons.

The figure of a man in the likeness of a merchant carrying wares to sell, or of a man sitting under a centurion, when found in emerald, gives riches, makes one victorious, and lifts him out of straits.

The image of a bull or calf found in lodestone gives the bearer fortunate journeys through all places, and is effective against all enchantments.

The figure of a horse or wolf found in jasper drives away fevers and stops blood.

The figure of a crowned man in topaz makes the bearer good, well-mannered, and beloved by God and men, and provides honors and dignities.

When the image of an armed man with a sword in his hand is found carved in sard or amethyst, it gives the bearer a perfect and good memory, and makes him prudent.

The figure of a stag or goat found carved in chalcedony gives the power of increasing riches, when it is kept in a money-box.

The image of a hare in jasper does not allow the bearer to be harmed by any demon.

A sea-locust carved in beryl reconciles quarrels and joins marriage in love.

The figure of a night-raven, because it is the bird of Pallas, carved in a stone that conduces to knowledge, makes a person most learned and eloquent.

The image of a peacock carved in a suitable stone makes the bearer rich.

The figure of a man killing a lion or another beast with a sword makes the bearer elevated, victorious, and lovable.

The image of a man holding laurel or a palm in his right hand makes the bearer dear, victorious in battles, lovable, and powerful in his region.

The figure of a winged man having a serpent under his feet and holding its head in his hands makes the bearer rich, prudent, and lovable to all.

The image of an ant having an ear of grain or a grain, carved in some stone, is effective for acquiring riches.

The figure of a cock holding a crown or belt in its beak will give victory in a duel, especially if it is in cock-stone.

When you find carved in some gem the figure of a standing man whose head is that of an ox and whose feet are those of an eagle, and impress it in wax, no man will speak ill of you.

When you find a basilisk and a dragon carved in cornelian, fighting with each other, and there is also the head of an ox there, apply it to your neck when you are about to fight, and you will conquer all beasts, both marine and terrestrial.

The figure of a naked and crowned man, holding in one hand a cup and in the other a branch of an herb, carved in jet and bound in a ring of any metal, when a feverish person wears it, immediately frees him from fever.

The figure of a man sitting upon a fish, in red jasper.
