*Seals*

And this is the method, from the powers of the heavens, of sharpening the intellectual power and the memory. From plants also and from animals, I hope that I shall enter into no small favor among those who have a fragile and fleeting memory, though they are quickened for understanding, if I bring forth certain remedies for strengthening the memory, which I know that very famous and very learned men have used successfully, and which until now have lain hidden among secrets, not grudging what can be of public use. You should know, however, that there are many things in them which adhere neither to temperament, nor to matter, nor to any manifest quality, but must be attributed to some occult property alone.

Take hearts of swallows, still beating, number 7; of turtle-doves, number 2; dried mint, pennyroyal, vervain, eyebright, prepared coriander, [galitrichum], of each 1 ounce; flowers of rosemary, 1 ounce; aromatic calamus, cinnamon, of each 2 ounces; cloves, 1 1/2 ounces; musk and muscat galls, of each 6 grains; anacardine honey, storax calamita, of each 1/2 ounce. Let those of these which can undergo grinding be reduced to a fine powder, and with a sufficient quantity of fennel-water let them be reduced into pastilles in the form of a small date-stone. The user will observe this order. With the body purged, for [50?] nights he will eat nothing for supper except raisins with a few almonds and a little bread; his dinner, however, will be roast hen, or a young chicken, turtle-dove, partridge, or something similar; his drink, wine of one year, in moderate measure, [unclear]. And on each night, near every hour, a pastille is to be inserted into the right nostril and stopped with cotton, and you will keep it so until you have recited no more than twenty verses of Virgil. After some days have elapsed, insert a pastille, moistened with rose-water, into the left nostril, and you will retain everything that you are about to read, or will quickly attain understanding of it.

But whoever altogether desires to preserve memory in this way must abstain from drunkenness, sexual intercourse, and especially from crudity of the stomach, from every satiety, green fruits, vegetables, salty cheeses, and all things that generate wind. Lastly, prepared coriander seed is to be taken. Care must also be taken above all that it be prepared diligently and properly. It will also not be harmful, at intervals, to hold one pastille in the mouth and swallow it. It will also help to throw a pastille onto coals, so that the smoke may be well received into the nostrils and, once received, retained, the nostrils being stopped with cotton so that it does not exhale; or also to dissolve some pastilles in lavender-water or rose-water or anise-water, and frequently, morning and evening, to massage the forehead, temples, nostrils, and occiput around the seat of memory, and to insert into the nostrils clean little linen cloths imbued with it. And this remedy has been made known to few.

But, to return to celestial matters, in precisely the same way and form as that of Mercury, the powers and influences of the remaining planets too can be brought into accord; for example, for obtaining honors and dignities, and the favor and goodwill of princes and magnates, an image of the Sun when it is in Leo and in the tenth house, in trine with Jupiter or the Moon. This year 1608, however, the solar influence is impeded by the opposition of Mars from Aquarius, as the notable and unusual changes of the very air openly demonstrate. Hence, in forming these figures, this alone must be considered: that the planet whose virtue and influence someone desires to bring into accord for himself should be as free as possible from every impediment.

But why and whence rings, images, and figures engraved on a certain metal or stone have the powers credited to them is not to be assigned to temperament, nor to manifest qualities, nor to the whole substance, but to a divine and magical cause, that is, to a certain higher and more sublime cause, which some not inappropriately call a spiritual vehicle and bond. By this mediation, body and soul, substances distant from one another, are coupled with one another; by this the magnet draws iron, and chicory follows the motion of the Sun.

By the interposition or intervention of this vehicle and bond, Mercury is efficacious in quicksilver, in sharpening or weakening the memory and the cogitative faculty, in the alectorius stone, in mixed color and flavor, in Egypt, Greece, Flanders, and Silesia.
