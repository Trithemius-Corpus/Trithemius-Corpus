MAGIC.

TREATISE III.

THE SEALS OR IMAGES OF HERMES.

Hermes, in book 14 of the Quadripartite work, records these images.

An image of a man, carved in [unclear: diadochocstantis], and of great stature, holding in his right hand an obol and in the other a serpent, and likewise above the man’s head the figure of the Sun, and having a lion prostrate beneath his feet: when it has been placed in a lead ring with a measure of artemisia and root of fenugreek, and you have it with you on the bank of a river, and call upon the aquatic spirits, you will receive from them answers concerning the things asked.

The figure of a standing man holding a bundle of the herb [unclear: colto] hanging down, with thickness of the loins and breadth of the shoulders: when it has been carved in green jasper, know that it gives help to those suffering from fever, and frees them from fever; and if anyone practices the art of medicine and carries it with him, it gives him very great favor in distinguishing illnesses, recognizing medicines and herbs, and giving potions; and in every hemorrhage and flow of blood it helps, and immediately checks it.

The image of sea turtles, if it is carved in a sea-stone and is worn bound in a lead ring, the wearer will not be able to be harmed by another, and he is loved by [unclear] and by the lords of lands.

The figure of Aquarius, if it has been carved in green jasper, and someone carries it with him, gives profit in selling and buying things; and merchants seek counsel from him, and will bring merchandise to his dwelling. A bird which holds a leaf in its mouth, and in its sight the head of a man looking at that bird: if it is carved in pangonus-stone, and you bind it in gold and carry it with you, you will be rich and wealthy, and venerable to all.

The figure of Jupiter, which is that of a man sitting upon a four-footed chair, and before Jupiter there are four men standing, and Jupiter’s hands are raised toward heaven, and on his head he has a diadem: if it is carved in hyacinth, and bound in gold, and someone carries such a ring with him, or hangs from his neck the figure of this ring impressed in wax, he will obtain from kings and wise men whatever he wishes.

An image of a man having the face of a lion and the feet of an eagle, and beneath his feet there is a dragon, and its tail is stretched out; and in the hand of that man there is a staff, with which he strikes [unclear].
