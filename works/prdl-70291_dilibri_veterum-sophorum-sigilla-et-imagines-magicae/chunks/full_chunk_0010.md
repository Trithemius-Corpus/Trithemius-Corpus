…sometimes in the likeness of a man sitting on a throne, with scattered hair and flowing garment, and also in a chariot, as if drawn by a team of four horses, so that round about the chariot are the 12 signs of the Zodiac. But the virtue of this seal, carved in some stone, renders the bearer commanding, and pleasing to the great. The images of Venus likewise are many, [though] more frequently carved in [certain] stones: a woman with a great garment and stole, holding a laurel in [her] hand—the virtue of this is to give agility in [the] doing of affairs, and to bring all [things] to the desired end, [and to give] fear… .

*SIGILLA.* [page 36]
…[and] honors, and to furnish, in all [the] doing of affairs, speed and ease, and to bring all [things] to the desired end. But it is to be carefully observed (as also was advised above) that the Planet, whose image carved in a fitting ring you desire, ought to be best disposed—that is, not combust, retrograde, or oppressed [or touched] by the rays of the malefics, but in a good place of the heaven, [the] Ascendant or Mid-heaven, with the Moon or Venus, in [its] essential dignities; otherwise the image stands vain and of no moment.

Moreover, the image of the Bear the ancients formed thus: They carve two bears wrapped round by a serpent—the greater bear contained in the head of the coil, the lesser in the tail; which image, carved in some stone, renders the bearer knowing, cunning, and powerful.

The image of the Crown has the likeness of a king's crown with several stars; sometimes it is figured in the likeness of a king crowned on the head; and it exists in the northern part and the sign of Sagittarius, and is of the nature of Venus and Mercury; and when this image is carved in a stone, it gives the bearers honors and knowledge, and royal favor.
