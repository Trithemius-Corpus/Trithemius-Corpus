Seals and Magical Images of the Ancient Sages, according to the names of God and the constellations of the stars, with the signature of the planets established.

Treatise I

The Seals of Raphael

The image of a handsome dragon, if it is engraved in ruby or in another stone of similar nature, know that its virtue is to increase the goods of this world, and to make the wearer cheerful and healthy.

The image of a falcon, if it is in topaz, serves to acquire the goodwill of kings, princes, and magnates.

The image of an astrolabe, if engraved in garnet, will have the virtue of restoring the wearer to health and keeping him free from all sicknesses and adverse accidents on a journey.

The image of an ass, if it is in chrysolite, will have the virtue of foreknowing and predicting future things, that is, so far as this can descend into human weakness; for truly to predict future things belongs to God alone.

The image of a ram, or of a human head with a beard, engraved in sapphire, has the virtue of healing and freeing from many infirmities, from prison, and from every trouble; and it is a royal image, for it gives dignities and honors and raises one on high.

The figure of a frog, if engraved in beryl, has the virtue of reconciling enemies and bringing friendship among people, if you touch the discordant persons with it.

The image of a camel’s head and of two goats between a myrtle tree, if it is in onyx, has the virtue of summoning, gathering, and constraining demons, if someone carries it with him; and in sleep it makes terrible and marvelous things appear.

The image of a vulture, if it is in chrysolite, will have the virtue of restraining and gathering demons, of defending the place where it is put from evil spirits and from their infestation, and it makes demons obedient to the wearer.

The image of a bat, if engraved in heliotrope, gives the wearer power to resist demons.

The image of a well-adorned man, holding something beautiful in his hands, if engraved in cornelian, has the virtue of stopping blood, and contributes to honors.

The image of a lion or Sagittarius, if it is in stone, is effective against poisons and frees from fever.

The image of an armed man with bow and arrow, if engraved in iris, has the virtue of protecting the wearer and the places where he stays from evil.

The image of a man with a sword in his hand, if it is in cornelian, has the virtue of preserving the place where it is from lightning and tempest, and it guards the wearer from vices and from fascination.

The image of a bull, if it is in prasius, is said to help in sorceries, and in works of mastery it grants favor.

The figure of a hoopoe with the herb dragonwort before it, if it is in beryl, will have the virtue of invoking water spirits, compelling them to speak, and receiving answers from them concerning questions asked.

The image of a man with his right hand raised toward heaven, if it is in chalcedony, will have the virtue of winning civil lawsuits, restoring the wearer to health, and preserving him on journeys and from harmful causes.

If the names of God are engraved in ceraunia, it will have the virtue of preserving the places where it is from storms, and it gives power and victory to those who wear it against their enemies.

The image of a bear, when it is in amethyst, has the virtue of putting demons to flight and of defending and preserving a person from drunkenness.

The image of an armed man engraved in magnet makes those who wear it victorious in war.
