When you find carved in hyacinth, garnet, or crystal the figure of a woman with loose hair or bare breasts, and in her sight a man looking at her face, and bind it in a gold ring of the same weight as the stone, with amber, aloes, and polypody beneath the stone, it will make the wearer lovable to all; and when it is placed under his head, he will see in sleep whatever he wishes.

When you find carved in pyrite the figure of a man riding on horseback and holding a bridle in one hand and a bow in the other, with a sword girded on, and bind it in a gold ring, it makes the wearer victorious in battle, so that no one will be able to resist him. And if anyone immerses this ring in musked oil, all will greatly fear him; if he anoints his face with that oil, they will not be able to oppose him.

When you find in magnetite the figure of a standing man who is naked, and at his right a naked girl standing, with her hair bound around her head, and the man holds his right hand over the girl’s neck and his left over her breast, and the man looks at the girl’s face while she looks at the ground, set it in an iron ring of the same weight as the stone. Enclose beneath the ring the tongue of a hoopoe, myrrh, alum, and human blood, as much as the tongue weighs. For the one who carries this ring, no enemy will be able to resist him in his sight, whether in war or elsewhere; neither thief nor harmful beast will be able to enter the house in which this named stone has been. And when an epileptic drinks water in which it has been washed, he will be cured. Also, if red wax is sealed with it and hung from a dog’s neck, it will keep the dog from barking.

Know that a soldier running on a horse, with a horn at his neck and a tree before him, carved on some precious stone, is effective for every kind of hunting.

Know that a woman holding a bird in one hand and a fish in the other, carved in crystal, is effective for catching birds; it ought to be bound in silver.

Know that the figure of a man with a goat’s head in place of his own is effective for acquiring riches.

The figure of an eagle carved in crystal makes the wearer rich, victorious, and eloquent.

Know that a horned animal bearing a horse beneath it, while the horse draws behind it the half of a goat, carved on some more precious stone, is very effective for taming any beast; it ought to be bound in a lead ring.

When you find the figure of a turtle carved in pangon and carry it in a lead ring, you will never be wounded, nor suffer harm, but will be honored by all, especially by elders.

When you find carved in gold the figure of a bird holding a leaf in its mouth, and in its mouth and sight the head of a man or of a vulture, the wearer will gain profit from everything he is going to buy and sell, and he will be sought out by buyers.

When you find carved in crystal the figure of a lion’s or dragon’s face having two heads with a delicate tail, or of a man in whose right hand there is a staff and who strikes the lion’s head, and you set it in bronze, with musk and amber beneath the stone, and carry it with you, all will obey you and you will enlarge your property. Wax sealed with it will have the same virtue.

When you find carved on some stone the figure of a winged horse, called Pegasus, it is very good for soldiers, and in open battle it gives boldness and eagerness.
