Treatise I The Seals of Raphael The image of a handsome dragon, if it is engraved in ruby or in another stone of similar nature, know that its virtue is to increase the goods of this world, and to make the wearer cheerful and healthy.

The image of a falcon, if it is in topaz, serves to acquire the goodwill of kings, princes, and magnates.

The image of an astrolabe, if engraved in garnet, will have the virtue of restoring the wearer to health and keeping him free from all sicknesses and adverse accidents on a journey.

The image of an ass, if it is in chrysolite, will have the virtue of foreknowing and predicting future things, that is, so far as this can descend into human weakness; for truly to predict future things belongs to God alone.

The image of a ram, or of a human head with a beard, engraved in sapphire, has the virtue of healing and freeing from many infirmities, from prison, and from every trouble; and it is a royal image, for it gives dignities and honors and raises one on high.

The figure of a frog, if engraved in beryl, has the virtue of reconciling enemies and bringing friendship among people, if you touch the discordant persons with it.

The image of a camel’s head and of two goats between a myrtle tree, if it is in onyx, has the virtue of summoning, gathering, and constraining demons, if someone carries it with him; and in sleep it makes terrible and marvelous things appear.

The image of a vulture, if it is in chrysolite, will have the virtue of restraining and gathering demons, of defending the place where it is put from evil spirits and from their infestation, and it makes demons obedient to the wearer.

The image of a bat, if engraved in heliotrope, gives the wearer power to resist demons.

The image of a well-adorned man, holding something beautiful in his hands, if engraved in cornelian, has the virtue of stopping blood, and contributes to honors.

The image of a lion or Sagittarius, if it is in stone, is effective against poisons and frees from fever.

The image of an armed man with bow and arrow, if engraved in iris, has the virtue of protecting the wearer and the places where he stays from evil.

The image of a man with a sword in his hand, if it is in cornelian, has the virtue of preserving the place where it is from lightning and tempest, and it guards the wearer from vices and from fascination.

The image of a bull, if it is in prasius, is said to help in sorceries, and in works of mastery it grants favor.

The figure of a hoopoe with the herb dragonwort before it, if it is in beryl, will have the virtue of invoking water spirits, compelling them to speak, and receiving answers from them concerning questions asked.

The image of a man with his right hand raised toward heaven, if it is in chalcedony, will have the virtue of winning civil lawsuits, restoring the wearer to health, and preserving him on journeys and from harmful causes.

If the names of God are engraved in ceraunia, it will have the virtue of preserving the places where it is from storms, and it gives power and victory to those who wear it against their enemies.

The image of a bear, when it is in amethyst, has the virtue of putting demons to flight and of defending and preserving a person from drunkenness.

The image of an armed man engraved in magnet makes those who wear it victorious in war.

MAGIC. TREATISE II. THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF CHAELOS.

Chael, one of the descendants of the children of Israel, a most ancient teacher, when he was in the desert, observed and caused many figures to be carved according to the course of the signs and planets, and recognized a very great effect in them; and so that the powers of these images might be manifest to posterity, he composed a book from which the following things are excerpted. Blessed be God, who gave such powers to the earth for the salvation of the human race. These words are found in the very book written by Chael, immediately at the beginning.

The figure of a man sitting behind a plow, with a long beard, a long face, and curved eyebrows, on whose neck there are four men lying, and who holds in his hands a fox and a vulture: if such a stone, on which these things have been carved, is hung from the neck, it is effective for all plantings and for finding treasures. For if, when going to bed, you place it beneath your neck, in sleep you will see treasures and the manner of extracting them. It also has another power, namely, to help the illnesses of brute animals when they have drunk water in which this stone has been washed. It should be carved on the day and hour of Venus.

SEALS The image of a man having a shield at his neck and a helmet on his head, holding a sword in his hand and trampling a serpent under his feet, carved in a red stone: if it is hung from the neck, he will overcome everyone in battle, especially if the engagement has taken place on the day of Mars.

The image of a horse having a crocodile above it, carved in hyacinth, has the power to make the wearer victorious in civil cases; it makes him loved, compliant, and amiable. It should be set in gold, because its power is increased by gold.

The figure of a seated man, and of a woman standing before him with her hair spread toward her loins, and of a man looking upward, carved in carnelian: its power will be to acquire goodwill from all who are touched with this stone. A little amber and terebinth resin should be placed beneath the stone.

The figure of a foaming and fiery horse, upon which a black man sits with a scepter in his hand, carved in hematite, grants the power of ruling and restores lost favor. It should be set in an equal weight of gold and silver.

The image of a seated man with a lit candle in his hand, if carved in chrysolite, makes the wearer rich, and it should be set in the purest gold.

A carved figure of a stag, or a hunter, or a dog, or a hare has the power of restraining demons, lunatics, and frenzied persons.

Know that the carved image of a woman having a bird in one hand and a fish in the other has power in catching fish and birds. It should be set in silver.

An image whose front part is a horse, but whose rear part is a goat, if carved in some stone, gives fortune in feeding and equipping animals or beasts of every kind. It should be set in lead.

The figure of a woman seated with a trumpet on a horse, or of a soldier running with a horn at his neck and with trees before him, carved in some stone, grants hunters success in hunting if they carry it with them.

The figure of a man with bent knees looking upward and holding a cloth, if carved in stone and carried by someone, gives favor and riches in buying and selling goods.

The figure of a scorpion and Sagittarius fighting each other, if carved in some stone, will make the malicious and discordant harmonious, and enemies friends. It should be set in silver.

The image of a vulture with an olive branch in its mouth, if you carve it in pyrite and carry it with you in a silver ring, you will be invited to many banquets, and all will look upon you with admiration and astonishment.

The half-figure of a ram and a lion, when carved in some precious stone: when you touch discordant people with it, they will become amiable and harmonious. It should be set in silver.

The figure of a woman in the upper part, but a fish in the lower part, holding a mirror in one hand and a branch in the other, carved in hyacinth and worn on the finger in a gold ring, makes one invisible. The ring with the stone must be turned toward the palm, and the palm must be closed.

The figure of a basilisk, one part of which is female and the lower part serpent, when carved in some precious stone, will have the power of driving away all poisonous animals.

The figure of a basilisk fighting with a dragon, with the head of a man above it, carved in carnelian: when it is hung from the neck, it will give the wearer power to overcome every beast, both terrestrial and marine.

The figure of a naked, swollen man, together with another figure of a well-dressed man holding a cup in one hand and a branch of an herb in the other, when carved in jet, know that it has the power of freeing from all [unclear].

From [unclear] fevers, when he has carried it with him for three days.

A statue of a man having an ox’s head and eagle’s feet, carved in some stone: when you have carried it with you, no person will speak ill of you.

A man bending his knees, looking upward, and holding one cloth, carved in turquoise: know that it is effective for buying and selling goods.

A figure of scorpions and archers fighting one another, carved in some stone, set in an iron ring, and impressed in wax, immediately makes discordant with one another whomever you touch with it.

A figure of a ram with half an ox, carved in some gem, know to have this power: when it has been set in a silver ring, whomever you touch with it will immediately become reconciled.

A woman, in the upper and lower part the figure of a fish, holding a mirror in one hand and a branch in the other, when carved in hyacinth and set in a gold ring, renders one invisible whenever you wish; but the seal must be covered with wax and worn on the finger toward the palm.

A figure of a man ploughing, and above him let there be the hand of the Lord making a sign, and beside it let a star be carved: if it has been carved in some gem and you have worn it most honorably, in whatever region you are, you will not perish by storm, and likewise the fruits of the earth in that place will not perish.

The figure of a young man wearing a crown on his head and sitting upon a four-footed throne, and beneath each foot let there be a man standing, holding the chair with his neck; and let there be a circle around the seated man’s head, and his hands raised to heaven: if you find this figure carved in white hyacinth, you may set this stone in a silver ring of equal weight to the stone, and put beneath the stone a little mastic and turpentine, and make a seal in wax, and wear it at your neck; you will obtain from kings and princes whatever you wish. It must, however, be worn most justly and most chastely.

A figure of a serpent having a man upon its back, and above the same a raven: if you find it carved in some stone, whoever carries this stone with him will abound in all things and will be clever and prudent.

The figure of a man standing upon a dragon, holding a sword in his hand: if you find it carved in hematite, place it in a lead or iron ring, and all subterranean spirits will obey him and will reveal to him all treasures with a light incantation, and moreover will show him the way to extract them.

The figure of a man standing and wearing a cuirass, with a helmet on his head and a drawn sword in his hand: if you find it carved in some stone and set it in an iron ring of the same weight, no one will be able to resist the bearer of it in battle.

The figure of a bearded man with a long face and curved eyebrows, sitting upon a plough between two bulls: if you find it carved in some stone, it is effective for plantings and for every kind of cultivation, for finding treasures, and for fighting. It will turn enemies into friends, and is effective in many illnesses; and if anyone carries it, serpents will flee from his face. It also heals epileptics; and if an infant carries it at the neck, it drives fear from him, and the vexation of evil spirits; and if a sick person carries it, he will recover health. And so that it may have greater power, it ought to be placed in an iron ring of double weight to the stone.

A figure of a standing eagle: if you find it carved in aetites and place it in a lead ring, the bearer of it will be accepted and loved by people, and animals will obey him.

The figure of a ram and likewise of a lion: if you find it carved in one of the precious stones and set it in a silver ring, if you touch discordant people with it, they will be reconciled.

The sign of Capricorn carved in cornelian, or in another stone, set it in a silver ring and carry it with you; for you will be harmed by enemies neither bodily nor by word, nor will a judge pronounce an unjust sentence against you. You will abound in business and honor, and will acquire the friendship of many; and all enchantments made against you will be destroyed. In the field too no enemy, however strong he may be, will be able to resist you. Hac Chael.

TRACTATE IV.

THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF THETEL.

Thetel, a very ancient teacher, treating of carvings on stones, says that these carvings, when found in suitable stones, have very great power.

The image of a man carved in jasper, having a shield in his left hand and in his right an idol or something warlike, and with vipers in place of feet, and in place of a human head the head of a cock or a lion, will give power against enemies and makes the wearer victorious; it is effective against poisons also, and checks blood from whatever part it flows.

The figure of a man having a bundle of herbs on his neck, when carved in jasper, has the power of discerning or recognizing illnesses; it restrains blood from whatever place it flows. They say that Galen had this stone.

They say that a cross carved in green jasper has the power of freeing the wearer from drowning in water.

A woman having in one hand a bird and in the other a fish: when carved in chrysolite, it is of great value for doing business.

The figure of a wolf carved in jasper is effective against [unclear] and prevents the utterance of insane words.

The figure of a stag carved in jasper has the power of healing and freeing lunatics and the frantic.

The figure of an emperor carved in jasper, with head raised, makes the wearer lovable to every creature, and whatever he asks he will obtain.

The figure of a virgin with flowing garment, holding laurel in her hand, when carved in jasper, makes the wearer safe from drowning in water; nor will he be troubled by a demon. It makes him powerful and able to obtain all things.

The figure of a man having a palm marked in his hand, when found in jasper, makes the wearer powerful and pleasing to princes. Thus far Thetel.

TRACTATE V.

THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF SOLOMON.

You shall make trial of it in this way: take pure black wool without grease, wrap the stone in it, place it among wheat chaff, and put it under your head; then in sleep you will see all the treasures of that region in which it is. It also has the power of healing all the illnesses of beasts, if they drink the water in which it has been washed.

SIGILS On the Method of Making Magical Sigils and Images Treatise VI Having set forth magical sigils and images, and the method of making them, at least insofar as it has been handed down by the most ancient writers themselves, it remains that in this place there should also be added a more exact method of making them, and that the preceding matters be explained more carefully.

And indeed, before all else in their composition the time must be considered: I mean the time of election, which must be diligently observed in writing or engraving figures on stones. For they are then thought to receive greater power if they are engraved at appointed and suitable times; for in this way they accord better with the stars. Hence one who wishes to arouse love uses favorable aspects, but one who wishes to kindle hatred uses unfavorable ones. Therefore they wish the image of Saturn to be fashioned when Saturn is in Capricorn or Aquarius; that of Jupiter when he is in Sagittarius or Pisces; that of Mars in Aries or Scorpio; that of the Sun in Leo; that of Venus in Taurus or Libra; that of Mercury in Gemini or Virgo; that of the Moon in Cancer. Thus the image of Leo or Cancer should be impressed on a ring or stone when the Sun is passing through Leo, or the Moon through Cancer, yet in such a way that each is free from every impediment, especially from the rays of Saturn or Mars.

It must also be held that any sign, as long as the Sun is in it, becomes living, rules over the others, and has its effect felt before the rest; and therefore the planet must always be directed to the place, that is, to the sign, face, and degree which the Sun illuminates.

So much concerning the magical images of the ancient sages, which it has pleased us to list here chiefly for this reason: that it may be plain that they were constructed from magical philosophy, that is, from true and natural magic, not from the principles of goetia and necromancy, although it is certain that superstition has crept into many of them through abuse. Use has praise; abuse has blame. For if anyone should wish to direct them, with superstition, toward evil action, he will be contrary to the divine order and to the principles of the art itself from which they flowed; and he must beware lest, surrounded by mad superstitions, he become wholly mad and superstitious.

Moreover, from almost the same foundations and causes, even now people have begun to prepare gilded and silver plates, round, square, or triangular, on which certain signatures and characters of the planets are engraved at certain positions and configurations of the heavens, or constellations, for conciliating the favor and influence of each planet. For it is agreeable to reason and experience that the influence of the planets is impressed, received, and retained in sigils and images of this kind, marked with a certain form and with images.

Meanwhile this must be observed with the greatest care: that the planet whose influence you seek be strong and fortunate, that is, in its own domicile, exaltation, or triplicity; in the angle of the East or the midheaven; free from the evil rays of Saturn or Mars; supported by a trine or sextile of Venus or Jupiter, or joined with them; direct, swift in motion, not combust, and ascending in the circle of the apogee.

The Erection of the Signature of Mercury, for Sharpening the Memory Let Mercury serve as an example. If anyone desires to win for himself Mercury’s influence for sharpening the intellect, wit, and memory, let him observe at what time of the year Mercury is in Gemini or Virgo, in which signs he has his domicile, and is strong and fortunate in the East or midheaven. When the hour and minute of the hour have been found, let a single celestial figure be made, with a careful delineation of the aspects and diligent and artful engraving on pure silver. Afterward it is to be enclosed in white silk and kept; and, with a few grains of the best musk added, it is sewn into the cap on that part precisely where it touches the cerebellum. You should undertake the operation at night, and more evidently at that hour and time when the engraving was both begun and completed.

In this year 1608, Mercury was found strong and fortunate around May 11, at the second hour of the morning. Again he will be most strong and most fortunate around the 14th and 15th day of the month of September, a little before and after the fifth hour of the morning, at which time he precedes the Sun on the eastern cardinal point, in his own domicile, exaltation, and the fortunate trine of Jupiter.

To seek the principles of this hidden thing, therefore, from its influencings, from principles and manifest qualities, from temperament or from the whole substance, is not so much absurd as ridiculous, since the actions of those from whom these powers flow forth are not corporeal, and are poured in from heaven without a body. Therefore here there is no need of any measure drawn from the senses, no ambitious battle of words and vain attempt at demonstrations. That the beginning of every philosophical demonstration cannot be sought from the senses and qualities, but must be sought elsewhere, is clear even from this common example: if someone asks whence a white rose draws its color, and wishes to assign the cause to a colder temperament, what, I ask, will he say of a red one? Will you say that species of endive pour forth a milky juice for the reason that they are cold? Yet very many very hot plants, such as the spurges, swell and drip with the same juice. And is aloe bitter for the reason that it is hot? Yet in opium, which kills by its destructive coldness, there is remarkable bitterness. And although it be granted that such things flow from the property of the whole substance, nevertheless a more sublime and more excellent maker, engraver, and fashioner above that substance must be placed and sought in our mystery. In vain, therefore, have the followers of Alexander attempted to refer everything to manifest qualities,

But so far be it from me to attribute any powers in themselves to these images, or to affirm that characters or so-called constellated names have virtue by reason of their figure, since figure, number, magnitude, position, and the division of the continuous are principles of no action, that, unless there is something else [signifying?] and admonishing, by whose power the characters are effective, I plainly deny it. God, through the prophets, heals human beings by the mere utterance of a word; indeed God is living, and the living name of the living God is also living, and the letters of the living name are also living. God lives on account of himself; his name lives on account of him; and the letters live on account of the name. And just as God has life in himself, so also he gave to his name to have life in itself, and further gave the name to the letters.

Therefore images or characters are effective by reason of the virtue or office ordained by God or nature for such a name or character. For there is no virtue either in heaven or on earth which does not descend from God. Thus medicines are visible bodies; words are invisible bodies. Whether an herb or a word heals, God is the cause, that is, the spirit of God, nature being aided by the word “Let it be.” Hence the herb itself is not medicine, but this lies hidden invisibly in it, namely implanted by God. Therefore things that are more remote from the senses and have hidden causes are not immediately to be condemned all the way to Orcus, nor stupidly mocked.
