But you will say: “Contrary custom has abrogated this law, and after a long time it must be regarded as law.” But iniquity lies to itself, and by foolish reasoning is deceived in itself. A custom contrary to good morals can never either remove or establish a law. And what is so contrary to priestly honor as to take pleasure in the companionship of young girls? Chastity is endangered where the fellowship of priests passes among little women. For the first and strongest temptations of clerics arise from frequent access to women. Foolish presumption has overthrown many who, so to speak, handed over to their enemies the instruments of victory with their own hands. Danger is avoided with the greatest difficulty if the occasion is not avoided. Indeed, while a woman stays with a cleric, and sits alone with him alone, prepares food for him as he eats and arranges the little bed for him as he sleeps, speaks to him, smiles at him, she supplies the kindling of lust. However holy a woman may be, she is always to be feared by a cleric. For just as sparks leap from coals, so the poison of concupiscence is spread from the constant familiarity of women. To a chaste man a familiar woman is the gate of the devil, the door of hell, the way of iniquity, the dreadful stroke of a scorpion, and a harmful kind of deception. When she comes near, she stirs him; when she speaks, she inflames him; when she touches, she burns him violently. We do not condemn the sex, but we warn of the danger to chastity. We read that Saint Augustine fled women so greatly that he did not permit even his own sister to enter his episcopal house. We too have known several priests who governed both the house and the people entrusted to them harmlessly for many years without the service of a maidservant. Nor will it be difficult for you to imitate what you see others have repeatedly done. Beware therefore, and do not approach the company of young girls, since even among old women it is difficult to live chastely. Remember with whom you speak at the altar, and do not pour out in wanton kisses the mouth consecrated to divine ministries. For it is most shameful, with hands consecrated by the oil of holy anointing, by which the Son of God is held and sacrificed, to touch a woman with polluted touchings. Do not give a stain to your glory, and your name as a reproach to all. All will tear you apart fiercely in public; in the field, rustic plowmen and vine-dressers will tear you apart daily, if, being yourself young, you presume to live with women, especially younger ones. You will become a proverb and a mockery to all the people if you often enter the dwellings of young women. Against your invisible enemy, who always flies around your side by lying in wait, keep watch at all times, and, according to Naso, resist the beginnings of evils, when the temptations are still weaker. For, as we see, after a cleric has once presented his body to the embraces of whores, rarely or never does he return to perfect amendment. For all who have lain sick with the perverse love of luxury return to health of mind with so much greater difficulty. Vice strongly inveterate fixes roots in the human heart. O how many priests there are today in the Church of God who, sunk in the depth of luxury, despise the long-suffering of God, as it is written: “When the impious man has come into the depths of evils, he despises.” There are many, without doubt, among clerics befouled with lust, who, although from the beginning of their promotion they proposed to live chastely, deceived by negligent custody of the senses, now without any fear of God, as if licitly by custom, mingle with whores. Nothing is worse than the custom of sin, by which it comes about that the gravest crimes are esteemed as the least. The strongest bond of sin is the custom of sinning, which grows with time and by delay takes increase of strength. Therefore he counseled most excellently against the fires of the flesh who said in verse:

“Resist beginnings; medicine is prepared too late,
when evils have grown strong through long delays.
But hasten, and do not put yourself off to the hours to come:
he who is not ready today will be less ready tomorrow.”
