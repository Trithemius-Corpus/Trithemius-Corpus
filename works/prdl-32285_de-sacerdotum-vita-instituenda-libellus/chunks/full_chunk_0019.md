God accepts [nothing] as good except what the flame of charity has kindled on the altar of the heart; for charity holds the primacy among all the virtues. Humility follows it, which is called the guardian of the virtues. Yet such is the eminence of this virtue that every other virtue quickly perishes unless it is strengthened by the foundation of humility. For the holy Pope Gregory says: “He who gathers virtues without humility carries dust into the wind. For by the very thing from which he is seen to carry something, he is the more grievously blinded.” Therefore the greatest adornment of a priest is charity, and the most precious pearl in his garment is humility. You too, brother, show forth these twin virtues in yourself, and you will be a fruitful preacher in the Church of God. Whatever you say, you will easily persuade your hearers, if you have charity in your heart and diligently show it in deed.

Let cursed ambition have no place in your heart; it so blinds the mind which it has once invaded that it brings forgetfulness of God and of one’s own salvation. Be a Christian lover of men and an enemy of vices. Keep the commandments of God, and do not neglect ecclesiastical sanctions. Honor all men for God’s sake; never consider anyone worthy of contempt. What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another. Let not the sun go down upon your anger. Flee the flatteries of the rich; never at any time stroke sinners. Deny yourself to your desires, so that you may become a disciple of Christ. Chastise your body, bringing it into servitude according to the Apostle; despise delicacies; love chaste fasting. Be compassionate toward the poor: feed the hungry, clothe the naked, visit the sick, free the captive, console the sorrowful, bury the dead. Do not love the world; do not seek riches; do not run after vanity. For no one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs. Do injury to no one, but learn patiently to bear injury inflicted on you, after the example of your Lord, who, hanging on the cross, prayed for his persecutors, that they might not perish. Have peace with all men, but concord and unanimity only with the devout. Do not be proud and arrogant, not given to wine and much eating, not a backbiter nor one who rashly judges anyone. Guard your actions at every hour, and let the memory of things to come always dwell in your thought. For Scripture says: “Son, remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.” Therefore always think that you are going to die; fear the day of judgment; dread the torments of hell. If you do these things, you will easily overcome all temptations of the flesh. Despise the pleasures of the world, so that you may be able to enter rejoicing into the joys of heavenly happiness. Do not deceive your soul: no one can rejoice here with the world and afterward reign with Christ. For through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God. Therefore, despising present things, let us hasten with every desire toward eternal joys. Guard your heart with every watchfulness, because life proceeds from it. If you have been overtaken by some fault, do not delay in seeking the remedies of penitence and cleansing yourself in confession through true contrition. Strive to enter into life by the narrow way, that is, not to fulfill the desires of the flesh, to hate the world, to live by the spirit. But in this life there is no certain victory, but continual battle. He who has persevered to the end will be saved. A man will be proved at his end. The life of man upon earth is warfare. For you are a soldier of the highest King; it is not permitted you to abandon his contest. A good soldier follows his king wherever he goes. Where then is your King? In heaven. Why do you not follow him? What are you doing on earth, if you are a soldier of the heavenly King above? Why do you not follow your Lord? For he says: “If anyone ministers to me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also my minister shall be.” Let him follow, he says, not with steps of the feet, but with the paces of affections.
