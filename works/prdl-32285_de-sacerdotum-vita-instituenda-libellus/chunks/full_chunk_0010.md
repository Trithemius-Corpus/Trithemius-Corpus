But to what exercises you ought to devote your time, receive in a few words, if you please. In the morning accustom yourself to rise from sleep before daylight, and to spend a little time in both the canonical and the votive prayers. And when the assigned task of service [unclear], you will thereafter devote yourself to sacred things, until you understand that you have been called by duty to celebrate Mass. After Mass one must again devote oneself to prayers, and apply oneself as devoutly as possible to holy meditations. Then one must work with the hands, attend to the affairs of the house, and, if anything must be arranged out of necessity, let it be disposed with the fear of God. The manual work of a priest is to write, to bind books copied by himself or by others, or printed ones, and to apply himself to other honorable labors according to his condition or necessity.

Then, after food has been taken, thanks must be rendered to God, who feeds us here on earth, and again one must pray that after this life he may feed us without end in heaven. When these things have been duly completed, one must not immediately go off to stories, but must again devote oneself to sacred letters. Then one must work with the hands until evening, which should not extend beyond the fourth hour in manual work. But when, with the day now declining, one must sleep, first let recourse be had to most devout prayers. When these have been completed, crying out before [him] that you are guilty of your acts, you should demand pardon and indulgence; for if you have judged yourself, you will be absolved by God. And unless you have constantly kept yourself in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overturned. But if you have been overtaken in any fault, immediately have recourse to the lamentation of penitence, and choose, if it can be done, such a confessor as both knowledge of the Scriptures proves suitable and conduct commends. For he who lives in blameworthy conduct rarely opens in truth the way of salvation to those who confess.

But if, when the enemy suggests them, you feel temptations of the flesh in your soul, drive them out as quickly as possible by prayers and holy exercises. Slippery is the serpent of lust, and unless it is crushed in the head, it is not easily destroyed. While the adversary is still weak, he is easily overcome. Nor, if you have sometimes conquered, should you think yourself secure, because the victory is not complete where battle is waged daily. Therefore, as long as you can be subjected, do not believe that you have conquered. Do not trust in your own strength, do not arrogate victory to yourself, but know that your hope consists in the aid of God alone. For no one is chaste by his own powers. For good will, aided by the grace of God, conquers the flesh. One must, however, pray without interruption to the Lord against temptations of the flesh, and say: Pierce my flesh with thy fear, for I have feared thy judgments. Do not love the delights of this world, which will soon make you vanish away and easily kindle you with the heat of lust. In your food let excess be avoided, and let not variety of dishes move appetite, but hunger. Let not incentives to lusts be set before you, but things that nourish the body, not inflame it. Let wine, in which there is luxury, always be suspect to you, lest you be inflamed by a double heat, that is, of drink and of youth. He fosters the fire of luxury in his bosom who makes constant use of wine. The exposure of Noah's shameful parts and the incest of Lot should warn you always to fear wine. For drunkenness is the burial of a living man: it destroys the senses, extinguishes reason, binds the mind, wounds the conscience, diminishes the health of the body, kills the soul. Drunkenness, a spring of evils, excites the flesh to luxury, pollutes the heart with the worst thoughts, and loosens the tongue into the most shameful words. It is the saying of the most holy Jerome: A full belly easily foams into lust. Therefore let refreshment be moderate for the chaste man, let drink be sparing and thin, let speech be modest. Let him beware familiarity with women; let him direct his senses with every guard, lest he fall into the pit of lust. As often as a base thought begins to knock at the mind, let the memory of the Lord's Passion come upon him; let him set his thought in the wounds of Jesus Christ.
