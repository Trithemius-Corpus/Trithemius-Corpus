The faithful flock is sustained by threefold food:
the Lord’s body, by which salvation is increased;
the compendium of the word, which is to be given discreetly;
worldly food, lest it fall into peril.

You are bound to preach the truth to them;
but what, to whom, how, where, and when
you must carefully reconsider, why,
lest anyone say that you err in your office.

It belongs to the office of your dignity
to give freely the gifts of grace to those who seek them;
but if ever you sell the gifts of faith,
know that you will incur the leprosy of Gehazi.

Administer the Eucharist freely to the people;
hear confessions freely, baptize freely;
according to the Apostle, give all things to all;
preserve only that which is your own.

Let your conduct be religious,
your conscience clean, your life virtuous,
your habit regular, and your mind gracious;
let no criminal stain defile you.

Let no pride oppress the sign of [unclear] mind;
let your gaze be grave, let your habit be a witness;
let nothing entangle you in dishonorable cares,
you to whom the keys of the heavenly kingdom have been entrusted.

Be brief in speech, lest loquacity,
nurse of vanities, draw you into guilt;
let the word you set forth be shortened,
for in much speaking sin is not lacking.

Be kindly, sober, prudent,
just, chaste, simple, pious, patient,
hospitable, humble, teaching your subjects,
consoling the wretched, correcting the wicked.

Would that you could so bear the pastoral care
and lead the spiritual life
that, when you have put off the fleshly cloak,
the Lord may clothe you with the eternal stole.

May he who sits on the throne of highest majesty
purge you of vice, cleanse you from sins,
come to your aid in your piety,
so that at last you may sit in Abraham’s bosom. Amen.

*A Poem on the Eyes.*

*From Lamentations, chapter 3.*

“My eye has plundered my soul.”

If youthful strength is a care to you,
if the welcome repose of a serene mind is so,
with anxious zeal keep your wanton eyes
safe everywhere.

Remember that, though they are small little eyes,
they are the greatest enemies of the fragile mind;
you must always restrain them with a bridle,
unless you wish to perish.

For whoever delights to wander this way
and does not know how to hold back his sight
will feel great ruins of the soul,
captive in love.

For Love, fearsome with his hidden bow,
first striking the slippery little eyes,
creeps into the veins and passes into the very
ark of the heart.

The restless demon, ever watchful at the windows,
keeps guard, and suddenly burns the mind
with his breath if he sees the eyes
badly at rest.

Therefore, whoever seeks eternal rest,
fearing the fires of Gehenna,
keep watch over the door; be ever vigilant
in guarding your eyes.

What madness, evil as it is, compels you to see
what it is not lawful for you to enjoy,
and from which you feel the inmost parts of your heart
wrapped in sudden darkness?

While you trust too much in yourself
and do not diligently labor to cut away the causes of the disease,
afflicted by a swift and willing wound,
you burn wickedly.

Turning slippery images over in your mind,
wretched man, you are burned within by a blind fire,
cherishing a silent wound in your veins,
by which you will perish.

Indeed, already you have perished before the Lord,
burning violently with putrid heat,
while in your madness you wickedly desire the crime
and love the wound.

Why do you look with wandering eyes through all the streets
at whatever comes in your way,
when youthful heat burns even one unwilling
more than enough?

The venom of the basilisk is less to be feared
than deceptive beauty and an adorned woman:
by sight the former kills the body, the latter the mind
with everlasting death.

The prophet of the holy Lord is witness,
the royal psalmist, who, seeing a woman’s naked body,
suddenly fell
into grave sin.

Who after this, I ask, will not fear for himself,
when he sees that such a man fell?
Whom will that pious David,
made an adulterer, not terrify?
