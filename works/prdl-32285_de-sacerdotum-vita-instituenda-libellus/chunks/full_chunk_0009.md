They love a wicked concubine more than eternal blessedness. What repentance do you suppose these men perform at the end, when they are pressed not by will but by necessity? Those who have spent the whole time of their life in lust rarely attain true repentance in the final moments of their life. For even if the late repentance of such men, by God's mercy, sometimes becomes true, nevertheless they will not be able to reach the kingdom of heaven without the most grievous torments of purgatory. Finally, if revelations are to be believed, a very great multitude of clerics among those below suffers, because of the vice of incontinence, immeasurable punishments that will last for eternity. You see whither incautious familiarity with women drags a cleric: it condemns him to the eternal torments of hell.

Therefore one must resist the beginnings of temptations, because lust is never better conquered than if it is avoided by flight. For if once it has entangled you through habit, it never lets you go free without the special grace of God. If you hesitate to put faith in our words, read the examples of the elders. But there is no need to seek examples in books, since you can find proofs before your eyes. Behold, today we know many aged priests whose life we know to be incontinent. We see age growing old in them, and the vices of youth by no means ceasing. And when they come to extreme old age, they do not give up their depraved ways, but as they have always been from the beginning, so too they go to death. You would see among them priests already decrepit who have drunk in the poison of lust to such a degree that, when because of old age they can no longer be wanton in deed, they burn for their accustomed things in thought, word, and gestures. Of such men the Lord says through the Prophet: If the Ethiopian can change his skin, and the leopard his spots, then you also will be able to do good, when you have learned evil. In old men, however, intercourse with women may cease, but the desire for the act remains as before. Therefore, such as you desire to be as an old man, live as such now also while young. For it is difficult for an old man to correct his ways and to think new things in an old mind. Indeed, old age has its ways; it does not receive them. He who wantons with a woman as a young man raves as an old man. Therefore it is good for a man when he has borne the yoke of the Lord from his youth, because if anyone has been evil in youth, rarely will he be good in old age.

*On the Remedy against Lust. Chapter Three.*

Physicians are accustomed first to show the seriously ill the gravity of the disease by subtle disputation, and only then to apply the aid of a remedy; for once the seriousness of the illness is known, the bitterness of the potion terrifies less. Intending to do this with you, I first disclosed to you by manifold demonstration the danger of living together with a woman, and, as far as was permitted, I declared what the end of this disease is. It follows, therefore, that we now consider the remedy. For the flesh always lusts against the spirit, and unless it is restrained by a salutary exercise, [unclear] and casts itself headlong into that abyss of damnation. Hence the poet aptly warns, saying:

If you take away idleness, the bows of desire have perished,
and the torches, scorned, lie without light.

For Holy Scripture bears witness that every idle person is in his desires. Therefore he who desires to extinguish the vice of lust must continually exercise himself in some holy work. Idleness, indeed, is the enemy of the soul. Hence blessed Jerome admonishes someone, saying: Always do some good thing, so that the devil may find you occupied. The best remedy against evil thoughts is the occupation of some holy work. Indeed, while the mind is delightfully intent upon some good exercise, it does not think of anything else. Access will not lie open to the devil if the mind has been occupied in some exercise.
