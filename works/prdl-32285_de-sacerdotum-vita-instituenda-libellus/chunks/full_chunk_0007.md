I suppose indeed that you are thinking of repenting in the future, but you by no means consider how very near death is, the cutter-off of repentance. For the merciful God, who promised pardon to the penitent, did not pledge tomorrow to the one who delays. Moreover, a cleric whom a woman living with him has once carried off captive she never, or rarely, releases as free, unless death intervenes. I pass over the fact that many, through the tricks and sorceries of concubines, are very often so maddened that they cannot in any way shake themselves free from their most shameful love for them. The hearing shudders at the crimes of [unclear], nor does the purity of chastity permit one to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by the concubines of priests. They make a stew for their lords from menstrual blood; and while they desire to entice them to their love, they turn them into colic and madness. By dreadful sorceries and superstitions they entangle priests in their lusts, until, their strength gradually exhausted, they render them utterly useless. Blessed is the man whom God has kept safe from an evil and lustful woman. There is no wickedness above the malice of a woman who enjoys evil loves. Whatever she devises, she attempts; whatever she can do, she dares. No one believes what efforts, ways, and inventions she uses, so that the man whom she plans to deceive she may lay low for her pleasures with an indissoluble bond. She joins herself to her lord according to her wishes, not because she loves him, but because she desires him. For the crowd of concubines follows the prey, not the priest. So long as he is rich, she abounds in everything and does not leave her lord’s side. But if he has begun to lack bread, the concubine turns herself to another. Sometimes, however, on account of the children, the priest and the concubine are so glued together that they cannot by any means be separated from one another, except by death.

It also often happens that some prefer to give up their prebends rather than their concubines. You are not ignorant of what happened to your first teacher. He, most miserably led astray by the savage love of that harlot whom you know, since because of fear of her husband he did not dare live with her openly, despising his homeland, his relatives, and the benefices he had, in order that he might freely enjoy her, departed, fleeing secretly with her. And to this present day no one knows where he went. I know a certain priest from among the concubinary priests; when, for his sin, I rebuked him privately, I finally brought him to this point, that he was willing thereafter to promise perpetual continence into my hands. And when I pressed him to cast out his concubine at once, he answered, “I cannot.” “Why?” I asked. “If I cast her off,” he said, “who would take care of my little children?” For he already had two boys by her. “I am poor,” he said; “I cannot put them out for hire. Therefore I will not cast out their mother until the little children grow up, lest I become guilty of blood. Yet I shall be chaste with her as chaste, nor henceforth shall I be joined to her in any mingling.” “And what will you do,” I asked, “if, before those children have grown up, others arise in their place?” He swore that he wished to take care that this should not happen. “You will not amend the fault,” I said, “unless you cast out the concubine. It is difficult to leave accustomed things, and to abstain from former vices while the occasion is present. No one will cherish a serpent in his bosom unharmed.” The man went away to his house, and has pursued his domestic crimes down to this day.

I considered the works of God, since what he has despised no one can amend. So difficult is it for a priest once fallen into fornication to be able to rise again to true chastity that out of all of them you can scarcely find one. For according to Scripture: “He who commits sin is the slave of sin”; and he who is subjected to shameful love is scarcely ever wholly freed. For fornication is more easily fled than amended. Therefore, I warn and entreat you to flee the company of women, lest you lose the reputation of a good name. For once a priest has been made infamous for fornication, he will never recover the honor he has lost. Avoid conversations with women, decline frequent greetings in the marketplace, because through light words one comes to the worst deeds. For incautious speech with women has deceived many.
