He is always feverish for greater profits of money; he always pants for an increase of gold; and the desire to possess grows in proportion as his cash happens to have grown. Thereafter one benefice, however fat, is not enough; the avaricious priest runs about even to obtain others, and strives, by right and wrong, to acquire what he improperly covets. Meanwhile he runs to the Apostolic See, states the grounds of his petition, which, if it is examined according to the truth, is at once found false. Then one man obtains many prebends, hires out the care of the sheep to mercenaries, receives wool and milk from the sheep, but scorns to feed the flock. “Woe,” says the Prophet, “to the shepherds who feed themselves.” They think themselves free from fault who, by dispensation of the Supreme Pontiff, hold many benefices; but they do not weigh what is required for that obtaining.

But you, stand firm, and do not transgress the ancient bounds which your fathers set, but set a limit to desire, lest you fall into sin. I wish you to be content with one benefice, which suffices you according to the modest measure of your state, lest, if you accept several without just necessity, you be judged more severely for it before God. Do not take as an example the multitude of those acting otherwise; do not walk on the broad road of sinners, if you wish to arrive at life. He who lives by the altar is worthy to receive support; but he who neglects to feed the sheep, with what rashness does he demand wool and milk from them? Ecclesiastical benefices were instituted for the support of those who serve them, not for their avarice. But he who flees the burden of solicitude does not receive the profit of the milk without fault. Therefore, if you refuse to feed Christ’s sheep, by no means seek wool or milk from them. For the sheep were not appointed for the shepherd, but the shepherd for the sheep. Nor is the care of souls to be sought so that food may be had; rather, food is to be sought so that due care may be applied to the sheep. But now the order is perverted by many, who seek prelacies and benefices for this reason, that they may have money in the chest, not that they may feed souls for the love of God.

O idol shepherd, how miserably you deceive yourself. You take care only that you may eat, though you ought to have eaten so that you might be able to care for souls. Cupidity is the root of all evils, the mother of transgression, the teacher of doing harm, the charioteer of malice, the inventor of deceit, the promoter of falsehood. It commits sacrileges and thefts. It carries out plunders and burnings; it wages wars and murders. Finally, cupidity alone is what taught men to buy and sell simoniacally; it seeks and receives unjustly, trades unjustly, relies on deceits, and is fostered by falsehood and perjury. It is this that breaks faith, dissolves agreement, corrupts testimony, and violates oath. Cupidity also extinguishes charity between God and man, interrupts brotherly love, disturbs concord, makes counsel foolish, perverts the judgment of equity, destroys unity. Therefore, if you desire to show yourself a worthy minister of God, tear this root of all evils out of your heart as quickly as possible.

Brief is every possession of earthly riches; brief is the action of mortal life; daily pressing on is the procession of the human race toward death. Why, then, do you delay, for the love of God, to despise what, overtaken by death, you will shortly be forced to leave behind, even unwillingly? For we have here no abiding city, but we seek one to come. Where your treasure is, there also will your heart be. Do not seek the perishable riches of the world; do not bind your heart with the chains of avarice. For it befits the priest of Christ to be a gentle and merciful disciple, who does not covet the things of others, but distributes his own generously among the poor.

*On the Honorable External Conduct of Priests. Chapter Six.*

Consider now what kind of conduct you ought to establish for yourself, and what kind of life you ought to live, so that you may be able to please almighty God, whose minister you are, with your whole heart. But as regards internal conduct, it is necessary that you shine clean and pure in conscience, you who minister before the Lord. For the Prophet of the Lord says to the priests: “Be sanctified, you who carry the vessels of the Lord.” And the Lord says: “Wash yourselves, be clean; remove the evil of your thoughts from before my eyes; cease to act perversely; learn to do good,” says the almighty Lord.
