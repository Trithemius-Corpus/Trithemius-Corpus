Do not neglect frugal hospitality; assist poor strangers mercifully with what diligence you can, knowing that this is proper to a priest: to have care for the needy. Have gold not so that you may keep it, but so that you may spend it in alms for the poor. Let the bellies of the hungry poor praise you, not the banquets of belching rich men. Let your table be with the poor and needy, because in such persons Christ is received and the heavenly court is honored. Avoid the banquets of proud rich men, especially of the powerful, in which nothing is done concerning the salvation of souls and nothing at all is discussed concerning spiritual conduct. Let your clothing be respectable, not costly. In giving alms do not seek glory from men, but expect praise and reward from God alone.

Be a father of the poor and protector of orphans. Let no poor man depart from you without consolation. Do not be a striker or quarrelsome among the people; injure no one wrongfully, but do good to all, so far as you are able, even to enemies. Never [flatter?] sinners, but rebuke them as one who has obtained authority from God. Do not be a striker of the consciences of the people entrusted to you, nor utter the word of God indiscreetly, but with the moderation of piety. Be merciful to penitents, and do not break the bruised reed, knowing that you have undertaken the care of the weak, not of the strong. For it is better to send the penitent with kindness to purgatory than, by the austerity of the confessor, to consign him in despair to hell. Do not neglect to pray your canonical hours with reverence and devotion at the times appointed by the Church. Likewise, do not omit to read the Hours of Blessed Mary ever Virgin, the vigils of the dead, with the other things due according to the priestly order. Have care for your household in the fear of God: if you have a family, persuade them by word and example to observe modesty and honorable conduct. Indeed, it damages the reputation of a priest among the people when the people speak ill of his household. Let your speech be strengthened by the testimony of the Scriptures, because whatever is taught apart from the holy Scriptures is despised with the same ease with which it is proved. I do not want you to imitate those who preach in the Church without learning, who frequently cite fables and phantoms of dreams, and are ignorant of the mysteries of the divine Scriptures. Ranting and garrulous priests, speaking without reason, do not edify the people, but labor to draw them into admiration of themselves by empty speeches and Aesopian fables. For it is a foolish and damnable abuse, with the Scriptures and the Gospel set aside, to teach the Christian people the fables of Aesop and the invented deeds of the Romans. And do not wonder if the unlearned people hear these things more willingly than the Scriptures, since you read that the Apostle foretold this. “There will be a time,” he says, “when they will not endure sound teaching, but according to their own desires they will heap up teachers for themselves, itching in their ears; and they will indeed turn their hearing away from the truth, and will be turned to fables.”

Hence Jerome says: To roll out words and, by speed of speaking, to create admiration for oneself among the ignorant crowd is the mark of unlearned men. A worn-out brow often interprets what it does not know, and when it has [unclear] its own things with others, it also claims for itself knowledge which it does not have. Nothing is so easy as to deceive the base and unlearned little crowd by fluency of tongue; whatever it does not understand, it admires the more. But you, speak the things that befit sound teaching, and do not seek the applause of the unskilled if you wish to find a place among the learned. Do not imprudently desire the care of souls, unless you have first learned, in accordance with ecclesiastical sanction, to govern yourself and others justly. For the office of teaching the people is subject to many dangers and is troubled by daily anxieties. For the ruler of souls must be without reproach, as a steward of God, not angry, not greedy for shameful gain, sober, just, kindly, embracing the faithful word which is according to doctrine, so that he may be able both to exhort in sound teaching and to refute those who contradict.
