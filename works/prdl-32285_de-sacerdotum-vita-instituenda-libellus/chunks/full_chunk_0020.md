You, therefore, who await the unfading crown of victory, labor like a good soldier of Jesus Christ; let no pleasure of the flesh touch your mind with its enticement. For nothing is happier than the soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised; nothing more laborious, since daily he is in danger of his life; nothing stronger, which is overcome by the flesh. Let no house call you away, let no kinship hinder you, let no flesh constrain you. I want you to be without anxiety, so that you may freely think on the things that are God’s. Naked and unencumbered, fly to heaven, for naked your mother bore you, and naked the earth will receive you. Love that which [unclear]; seek that by which you may rejoice happily forever. Live so that you do not fear to die. A good conscience does not fear death, because it knows that through death it will come to the joys of the eternal heavenly homeland. You now have, most beloved brother, what you asked from me; you have some sort of rule of good conduct, to which you may conform yourself; you have a standard of holy exhortation, to which you may diligently have recourse. I have done what I could. For the rest, live according to the Gospel and the constitutions of the holy fathers, in which the whole form of Christian perfection is seen. Farewell, mindful of us.

From Spanheim, on the Kalends of April, in the year 1486.

*Poem to the Clergy, exhorting them to purity of life.*

*1 Corinthians, chapter 3.*

Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwells in you? But if anyone violates the temple of God, God will destroy him.

*1 Corinthians, chapter 6.*

Taking the members of Christ, then, shall I make them the members of a prostitute? God forbid.

Hear, sacred house of Melchizedek,
order of priests, clergy sacred to God,
company of the key-bearer of the starry pole.
God has established you like a great radiance,
and has given you to the peoples as a shining globe:
that, irradiating the world with a bright lamp,
you may dispel the darkness of the lands with light;
that, pure, you may sacrifice to the terrible God,
and give the sacraments with reverence
to the nations purchased by crimson blood.
You hold the seat of the Lord, you hold his throne,
so that you may express the light of faith, which cannot deceive,
and the law, in speech and conduct:
as the sun makes the year fruitful with its rays,
so may you make the world fertile with light.
You hold exalted the royal glory of magnificent Jove,
so that you may remember, by fruitful governance,
to feed the nations,
and bravely rescue those whom the adamantine bonds
of the tempter hold entangled.
God has anointed you with sacrificial oil,
rendering you bright with every kind of gift.
Since, therefore, you shine with manifold good,
if perhaps you displease others, you do not displease me.
But I would have you carefully warned,
that you look into yourself and ponder well
how much many things defile the sacred seat
of the pontiffs and of the heavenly ones,
and especially the foul filth of lusts.
How displeasing it is, and how hateful,
to the holy lover of virginal bloom,
he cannot fail to know who unrolls the sacred Bible
with vigilant care.
Now therefore attend to my sad song.
Since the purity of the flesh and the brightness of the mind,
unstained, and chaste joys,
ought to belong to the high servants of the prince and of God,
why do I everywhere see so many little prostitutes
now ruling over the clergy and priests?
Why do I see men otherwise learned and celebrated,
illustrious in riches and wisdom,
so utterly losing their senses
that they do everything at the whim of whores,
and do not refuse to be ruled by a vile little woman?
O royal seat of the Lord, you cherish
very many in your gentle bosom, as a mother does,
who allow themselves to be drawn by feminine enticements,
like brute animals,
to whatever impiety, disgrace, or crime.
How vile a snare, how evil a servitude,
when a wise man allows himself to be ruled
by women’s counsels, at the whim of a harlot;
and, shamefully forgetful of God and of himself,
closes the way to fruitful admonitions.
How blind, foolish, and miserable
are all those who, bound in heart by tenacious snares,
raise at home, with no small effort and expense,
a pestilential serpent,
indeed a perpetual evil more harmful than death.
Unhappy clergy, open your eyes,
so that at last you may see how grave a crime it is
if you defile the temples of your flesh, consecrated to God,
with the meretricious filth of the flesh,
or if the mind burns with illicit fires.
At least let some shame rebuke you,
