But as regards the outward man, the Apostle, instructing a bishop, says: A bishop must be irreproachable, sober, chaste, kindly, adorned in conduct, hospitable, teachable.

Whatever is enjoined upon a bishop in this passage is also commanded to the other prelates and priests. And first, when he says that he must be irreproachable, he declares him free from all vices and from the slanders of the people. For the life of a priest is set before the people as an example, and is compared to a lamp, just as light shines in the darkness. For the Lord says in the Gospel: Let your light so shine before men that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven. Then, moreover, the priest walks worthily before God when he daily adorns his life with good and holy works and, for the love of Christ, mortifies his members that are upon the earth. For he is the servant of the Lord, and this above all is required of him: that in words and deeds he carry out the will of God. But no one fulfills the will of God except he who, having driven out the vices, makes his heart a dwelling-place of the virtues. Beware lest you stain the Church, the bride of Christ, through the foulness of your life; beware lest by an evil example you scandalize anyone, because however many people you lead into sin by your fault, for that many you will be tormented in punishment. Be seen rarely in public; accustom yourself to remain at home, to devote yourself to readings. Be calm in mind and body; do not run about through the streets and the houses of neighbors; do not take part in the tales of peasants. For presence diminishes reputation, and familiarity with the priest breeds contempt among the people.

But rare going out in public will display for you among the common people a great reverence of maturity. Live in such a way that many may be edified by your example. In the midst of elders speak little, because talkativeness is often reproved where silence is praised. Indeed, among old men it is fitting for a young man to listen, not to speak. Do not desire to seem wise in the company of priests, lest before them you be confounded as foolish. Do not be wordy and arrogant; when questioned, answer with humility and goodwill; and do not, in the manner of wranglers, be clamorous in your voice when you speak. Do not love unrestrained laughter, and always and everywhere keep yourself from every dishonorable levity. Avoid useless conversations and empty words, knowing that for every idle word that you have spoken you will have to render an account to God. If your speech is moderate and sparing, let every lie be far away, and let no oath be taken in vain. Do not enter taverns with peasants, because that is not permitted to your honorability. For it is very unfitting that he who ought to rebuke sinners should wish, by his own presence, to show himself a patron of sins. For entry into taverns is forbidden to priests by the holy fathers, unless they are on a journey. It is shameful for a priest of God to enter a tavern with peasants, and for a mouth consecrated to God to be befouled with impure speech. The discourse of one who preaches is easily despised if it is poured out familiarly among peasants. From the habit of familiarity they will begin to despise you and to regard you as one of their own. For no reverence is paid to superiors if their life is narrowed among their subjects. One who presides must surpass all by a certain reverence of maturity, and wisely decline excessive familiarity with his subjects. Indeed the preeminence of authority is broken when it is indiscreetly mingled with the actions of those subject to it. Speak ill of no one, nor take up a scandal against anyone. It is disgraceful for a priest to pollute with detraction his tongue, which, placed at the altar, is to pray for all. Nor should you lend an ear to detractions, because the detractor and the willing listener are worthy of equal condemnation. For the one carries the devil on his tongue, the other in his ear. Let one who is of this sort learn not to detract readily, when he sees that you listen unwillingly. Do not be hasty in tongue, and do not stand in the company of detractors, because they are hateful to almighty God. Hedge your ears with thorns, lest you hear the words of detractors.
