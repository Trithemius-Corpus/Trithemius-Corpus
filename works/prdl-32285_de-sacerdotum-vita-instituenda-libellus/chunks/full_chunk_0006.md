See the senseless folly of foolish priests, who are led captive by vile concubines, so that they do not do whatever they themselves wish, but what is commanded them by harlots. Meanwhile you would see the stupid cleric sitting beside his concubine and gazing at her open-mouthed: if she smiles at him, he laughs; if she has become angry, he flatters her until she is restored to favor. The unhappy priest is led captive by a woman, and he who ought to have ruled others is overcome by the dominion of his maidservant. Oh, who would believe what groans the wretched man utters amid these things, how often he thinks of casting off this most harsh yoke of the arch-devil, what pains he carries in his heart. At times, indeed, he is touched by such displeasure against his beast that he plans to drive her away from him on the morrow. Meanwhile he gives and receives abusive words, and finally after words it comes to blows: he strikes and is struck, he curses and is blasphemed.

O astonishing spectacle: the head of a priest, which it is not lawful for a prince to touch, the hands of a whore tear, strike, and batter. At last, after curses and blows, the woman turns herself to weeping; with the devil pouring them in, she sheds tears until at length she recalls the unhappy captive to mercy and renders him placable toward herself. You would now discern the cleric suddenly changed, so moved by the tears of his Abra that he who had received the injury is the first to beg pardon. Then the table is set out, the banquet is renewed, sighs are mingled with words, dealings of new marriages are discussed. Amid these things the [stolid?] priest sits, disputing about patience and gentleness, sighing that he has sinned against his mistress, laboring with all his strength to recover her favor. What is done after the friendship has been renewed, it will be permitted you rather to think than for me to say.

There is no fear of God before the eyes of a priest bound by the chains of a concubine, no dread of the punishments of hell, no thought of priestly dignity, no memory of public honor. This vice of incontinence has now prevailed so greatly among the clergy that it has almost begun to be lawful, because it is public. Lamentable condition of the human race: those who ought to have corrected the crimes of others commit graver ones. For shame, priests sit publicly in villages and cities, defamed by intercourse with their concubines, so that if some prelate should wish to correct such men in his diocese, overcome by their sheer multitude, he is frightened from his purpose. If you entered the house of any one of such men, you would see him carrying in his arms his little child, witness of his father’s fornication, and while reading the Canonical Hours consoling the crying child with words and songs; you would also see the unfortunate father rising from bed, taking the child upon his knees and, after giving him kisses, placing him on his right knee and the Breviary on his left. While praying he smiles at the laughing infant, flatters him when he cries, and addresses him when silent with frequent words and nods. Meanwhile the fond mother comes up; from the father’s hand the sweet pledge snatches the book, handles the leaves, tears them, and, with saliva flowing from his mouth, stains them with the parent’s permission. Amid these things there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention is applied to the words, but all concern is consumed in consoling the child as it cries. The words fall confused from the mouth of the one praying and are interrupted by the child’s frequent kisses, so that, when the prayer is completed, he scarcely remembers that he has prayed.

Meanwhile the sacristan pulls the bell, the altar is prepared for sacrifice [unclear], the priest is awaited by the people in the church. The priest hurries up with his hands washed in the child’s urine, puts on the sacred vestments with as much haste as he can, and completes the sacrifice of God with a distracted mind. O conscience, where do you meanwhile remain, where do you lie despised? O wretched man, you who handle the Son of God with polluted hands, how long will you wound and tear your soul [unclear]? With what rashness, then, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul? Unless the great compassion of almighty God spared you for a time, the provoked angels would strike you at the altar with heavy indignation. Where is the vow of perpetual chastity which you promised into the hands of the bishop when he ordained you? Where is the reverence for the priesthood you have assumed?
