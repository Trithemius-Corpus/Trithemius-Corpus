Ecclesiastical authority has now come to such misery that we are ordered to embrace the words of priests, not their deeds. For the unhappy priests say of themselves, bearing witness to their own defilement: “Attend to the words of our instruction, O faithful of Christ; beware of our examples.” O words most worthy of everlasting confusion: as much as they ought to instruct the people of God by doctrine, so much do they scandalize them by perverse works. The unlearned and ignorant now come to the priesthood without discernment of merit, and by their most evil morals they unhappily kill Christ’s sheep. No holiness of life is now required in clerics to be ordained; no learning in letters is demanded; purity of conscience is not considered. It is enough among them if one can construe a running Sorites, if he can answer what part of speech “Lord” is, if he can understand “yes” and “no.” Woe to the bishops of our age, who, intent on secular business, do not consider the ordinations of clerics, but entrust the care of examination to the unskilled. How strict a sentence they will undergo, who show themselves to be the cause of all the evil in the Church. Indeed, since it is fitting for a priest of the faithful to be learned and holy, with what rashness do the unskilled come to the priesthood, who can instruct neither themselves nor others? For without learning, a good life in a cleric is not sufficient; nor does learning without [unclear] instruct the people healthfully. For [unclear] kills the teacher; life without learning makes him useless. For the madness of wolves is to be driven away by the barking of dogs and by the shepherd’s staff. Therefore the ecclesiastical shepherd should, through a good life, offer his sheep an example of righteousness, and through salutary doctrine feed the minds of his hearers, lest, if he does one without the other, he either neglect himself or carry out the care of the flock fruitlessly. Alas, what shall I say about our priests, unlearned and vicious, greedy, luxurious, and most wicked? I am ashamed to speak, and I cannot be silent. Look to yourself, dearest Nicholas, lest you become an imitator of those who, having despised the books of Scripture, spend the whole day at games and drinking, who defile themselves by intercourse with women, who by their depraved morals scandalize many in the law of God. Be an example of honor among the people of God in all things, so far as can be done, so that you may remove every occasion for evil suspicion. For it is necessary to have testimony even from those who are outside, because the word of one who preaches is easily despised when his life is marked among the people by evil suspicions. Therefore show yourself a cultivator of good works, so that you may be able to instruct the people of God no less by example than by speech. In order that you may be able to accomplish this salutarily, take care to avoid the company of the undisciplined; for he who does not flee the company of the wicked can scarcely manage to live without reproach. For Scripture says: “With the holy you will be holy, and with the perverse you will be perverted.” And again: “He who touches pitch will be defiled by it.” Everyone delights to have the companionship of his own way of life. For if you have not kept your foot from the assembly of evildoers, you will at all times be subject to the slanders of your subjects. A youthful mind is easily deceived by the company of the perverse. You are young, and [unclear] flexible toward either side. Therefore you must watch over yourself with the greatest diligence, lest you be depraved by the company of the undisciplined. For nothing harms the young so much as the depraved examples of elders. The younger you are, the more carefully must you take care to guard your life in the sincerity of the fear of the Lord. For it is written: “A youth according to his way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” What you learn as a young man, you will practice in old age.

*On Avoiding the Company of Women. Chapter Two.*

But what shall I say about familiarity with women, which is most pernicious to the priests of God, since the decrees of the holy fathers forbid this with sufficiently strict prohibition? If I set before you the admonitions of the most blessed father Jerome, you will immediately cry out: “I cannot manage a household without the service of a maidservant; I am not able to prepare food for myself; I do not wish to clean dirty clothes.” “Why,” you say, “do you cite Jerome’s precepts to me, which bind only the will?
