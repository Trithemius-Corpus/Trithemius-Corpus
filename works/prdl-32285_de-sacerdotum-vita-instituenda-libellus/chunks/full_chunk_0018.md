He who burns without wine with the heat of youth, and is inflamed by the warmth of the blood, if he is strong in body, should flee wine as poison, lest, suffocated by a manifold fire, he fall into the mire of lust. A stomach heated with wine stirs up lust, dulls perception, and weakens the head and the whole body. It is also said in a proverb: A fat belly begets a thin understanding.

Therefore, when you are invited and come to banquets of priests, do not forget clerical propriety, but be modest; beware lest either your tongue or your ears itch, that is, lest you either disparage others yourself or lend a hearing to those who disparage them. Know that all the things you say about others must be turned back upon yourself; perhaps you will be reproved in the very same matters that you were censuring in others. But perhaps you will say: I cannot do wrong to those who report it, because whoever plans to stop the mouths of everyone strives in vain; what, then, is another person's disparagement to me? Surely I cannot prevent people from speaking? But, as we said above, so present yourself before the disparager that he may learn to disparage no one, when he sees that you do not willingly listen to what he says. Do not associate with disparagers, says Solomon, because their destruction will come suddenly; and who knows the ruin of each? Namely, both of him who disparages and of him who lends his ear to the disparager. Be prudent in conduct, discreet in silence and taciturnity. Be diligent in examining the mysteries of the Scriptures, and if any matter of doubt occurs to you as you read, know that it must be referred to the interpretations of the elders. Nearby you have the school of the very ancient city of Trier, in which there is an abundance of learned men, to whom you will be able to have recourse in the uncertainty of questions. Let gold not be dearer to you than knowledge, because the fruit of wisdom is eternal, while the use of riches is brief and momentary. Let there be no one who prevents salutary study, not mother, not kinsman, not sister: do the work of an evangelist, learn what you are to teach, lest you prove to be an idol shepherd. You are one and have no second; for whom, then, would you heap up riches? Live temperately; marriages strip the mind of virtues and render the purse empty of coins. Buy for yourself, from the alms offered, codices of divine Scripture; taught by assiduous reading of them, you may worthily instruct the people committed to you and raise yourself by contemplation to heavenly things. Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and it will exalt you before God and men. Do not consent to the pleasures of the flesh, restrain the impulse of fury, and never forget death.

*What Sort of Person a Priest Ought to Be in His Inner Conduct*

*Chapter Seven*

Since, however, it is not enough for a minister of Christ to lead an honorable life before men, unless he strives to appear before God clean from every crime, I exhort you to direct your heart in the fear of the Almighty and never admit any thoughts against God. For it is necessary [unclear]

for a priest of God to be holy in mind and body, and clean in thought. Hence the Lord says: Be holy, for I am holy, the Lord your God, who sanctify you. And Peter the arch-apostle says: You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition, that you may proclaim the virtues of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light. Let charity hold first place in your heart, for it is the fullness of the law and the completion of the prophets. For the greatest of all virtues is charity, which consists in love of God and neighbor. God must be loved for his own sake with the whole heart, and one's neighbor for God's sake.
