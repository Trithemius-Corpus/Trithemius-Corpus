For temptation is never lacking to a man so long as he lives in this life. But if you undertake domestic care without the service of a maidservant, take on one who is respectable, from whose living with you no suspicion can arise among the people. Nevertheless, above all take care of this: that you not take a married woman as your maidservant. For what God has joined together, let man not separate. The cohabitation of another man’s wife is not free from suspicion of evil. I wish you, so far as can be, to have a good testimony from all. Let the dangers of others make you cautious, and where you see that many have fallen, do not wish to walk incautiously. Remember that the strongest and wisest men, from the beginning of the world down to this day, have perished through the company of women. A woman deceived Adam, the first-formed, and, once he had been overthrown, slew us all. From the beginning of the created world, through women the sons of God sinned, and by their crimes brought the flood upon the whole world. Women of the foreigners also laid low in the desert the children of Israel as they came out of Egypt. Why do we recount these things, when a lowly little harlot, having captured Samson, the strongest of all men, with her charms, handed him over to death and disgrace? Let no one presume on his strength; let no one trust in his former chastity, since he who often today [unclear] seems to fall into fornication. The very wise Solomon could not guard against the deceits of women; who now will be safe? The strong and wise man falls by the sword of women, and do we, frail and foolish, presume upon security? For he who fell says: The wickedness of a man is better than a woman doing good. It is safer to live with a wicked man than with a good woman. For the adversary of chastity, the devil, never ceases to set snares for the chaste man. If he can find material for deceiving him, since he is cunning, he knows how to dissemble for a time, until at last in one hour he can overturn all the labor and reward of past continence. He inserts himself little by little among those who love, and under the appearance of Christian charity sows sweet words about virtues and vices, until he draws the incautious into the snare of crime. Then they begin thereafter to speak more familiarly; each opens his more secret matters to the other; little by little they grow accustomed to touching one another with their hands. Finally, through mutual laughter, future marriages are prepared: while from habit they live familiarly, they suddenly fall into the pit of the devil. But after they have once fallen into mutual guilt, they seem to themselves to abhor the deed somewhat, but they cannot easily withdraw from what they have begun. Therefore they proceed on the path on which they had begun, and although in the beginning conscience abhors the crime, nevertheless from habit it gradually bears it more lightly. And at first, indeed, they begin to act wantonly with a certain bashful fear, but afterward, blinded by depraved habit, they are no longer ashamed. Meanwhile the belly, conscious of the secret, begins to swell, and to disclose publicly the crime that had been hidden. At last a little child is born; for a brief time the father and mother are ashamed, and immediately they return to their accustomed crimes. Then thereafter all shame is cast far away, the eye of conscience is blinded, the fear of God and of hell is utterly driven from memory. Now they live freely in sins, joined by a diabolical glue; they live defamed before men; they live alienated from God. Soon the inveterate crime is turned into despair; the remedy of repentance is mocked; the long-suffering of God’s patience is despised. Meanwhile the priest is made a slave to lust to such a degree that he seems unable to live without a concubine. He can indeed amend his sin, but he refuses, because he feels himself delightfully entangled in the bonds of luxury. He promises himself that he will undertake a resolution of repentance in the future; he promises to amend his life. But while amid so many crimes he postpones the will to repent, he loses both time and ability alike. Hence it comes about that healthy priests who keep concubines rarely come to the remedies of saving repentance, because they hope that life will still be prolonged for a long time.
