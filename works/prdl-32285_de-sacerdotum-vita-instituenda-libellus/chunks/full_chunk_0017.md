He is a delicate teacher who debates fasting with a full belly. Therefore life must be sought before doctrine, because one teaches more fully by deed than by speech. For the most excellent kind of teaching is the demonstration of example. And indeed the Lord Jesus first acted, afterward taught. For in teaching he uses the greatest authority whose speech is confirmed by the attestation of his work. The word of a ruler is easily carried out by subjects if it is supported by uprightness of conduct. But he who teaches well and lives badly destroys himself by the sentence of his own mouth, and does not turn his subjects away from bad instruction.

At the same time consider how dangerous the care of souls is, especially in these times, when iniquity abounds everywhere and the charity of many has grown cold. For the days are evil, and men are slow to justice, and the whole world is set in wickedness, and faith is rare on earth. Amid these things the word of God is despised by the people, the discipline of the Christian religion is neglected, priestly authority is scorned. Lay life, liable to many sins and crimes, neglects the remedies of penance, gives itself to deceits and frauds, and scorns the admonitions of priests. And unless the ruler of souls diligently announces to the people committed to him their crimes and the punishments owed to sinners, before God he too will be guilty of their blood. And after these things God will bring all things into judgment, where the shepherd of souls is compelled to render account for each one; if he has been negligent in this world, he is condemned more heavily. Therefore it is safer humbly to flee the care and rule of souls than arrogantly to usurp it.

For if each person scarcely suffices to render account of his own life in that dreadful judgment, how will he answer for so many thousands committed to him? O how well he lives who lives for himself. It seems good and pleasant among men for a bishop, abbot, pastor, or any prelate to live in this age; but who will be able to think how terrible is the end of the negligent man? For judgment most severe will be made upon those who preside, and the powerful will powerfully suffer torments. He passes well who lives simply, to whom no earthly honor is entrusted. For pastoral care is never without danger so long as one lives in this world. The greatest danger of all is when, the soul separated from the body by death, the earthly shepherd must be presented before the supreme Shepherd, God, to render account. Here now the moment of final condemnation is imminent, when an account of badly administered stewardship is demanded, and the blood of many sheep is required from the hand of one shepherd. What then will the unskilled shepherd be able to answer to the charges, he who was negligent in the care of the sheep? He must fall silent, because he has nothing to answer.

Therefore, brother, if you consider yourself fit for the care of souls, if you are preparing to rule the church of Mernice, know that you are to be brought into judgment for these things, and that according to the merit of your life you will receive glory or torment. But believe me: he dies secure who, putting aside care for others, lives for himself alone in the fear of God. For to whom much is entrusted, much also will be required of him. The priest, especially one having the care of souls, is God’s herald, so that he may announce to the people the will of the Almighty, and may heal those who sin against the law of the Lord by words of rebuke. Therefore he must be learned and most steadfast, lest either through ignorance he fail, or through inconstancy he fear the hatred of the perverse. For it is shameful, because of fear of the people, to keep silent the words of God. The priest must use a little wine, lest, if he gives himself to drinking-bouts, he become a reproach among the people. For the Apostle condemns drunken priests, and the old law forbids them access to the altar. In the Council of Agde too it is read thus: Before all things drunkenness is to be avoided by clerics, since it is the tinder and nurse of all vices. Therefore anyone who is proven to have been drunk, as his order permits, is either to be removed from communion for the space of thirty days, or subjected to corporal punishment.
