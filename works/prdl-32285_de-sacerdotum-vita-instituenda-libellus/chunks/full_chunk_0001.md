*A Little Book Concerning the Life of Priests,*

formerly written by the most pious father Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim;

but now, by the command and at the expense of the most reverend father in Christ and lord, Lord Christopher Andreas, bishop of Gurk, newly published for the use of his diocese.

In this little work priests, ministers of God, have a certain fixed manner by which they may order the actions of their life, useful both to themselves and salutary to the flock committed to them.

At Brixen, Donatus Faetius was printing it. In the year of the Lord 1577.

The righteous man by virtue shall tread upon savage lions;
with unharmed foot he shall crush the bones of the asp.
Therefore, because monsters submit themselves to the sacred tiara,
Christopher, these are monuments of your praise.

*Christopher Andreas, by the grace of God bishop of Gurk, to the whole clergy of his diocese, grace and salvation in the Lord.*

Since we have fallen upon those times in which, especially, if ever before, there is need of very frequent exhortations to the clergy, we have thought that we would do something worthwhile if what we have already, for a second time, performed by living voice in our annual synod, so far as lay in us, we should now diligently attempt in writing as well, so that we might admonish all of you with fatherly zeal concerning your office. For although, in some way I know not, the things that are taken in by the ears affect us wonderfully, nevertheless those things also usually move us no less which we ourselves have received from mute interpreters, after the reasons have been considered and carefully weighed.

For this reason it seemed good to select from our Strasbourg library a little book of Johannes Trithemius, formerly abbot of Sponheim, a man, as appears from his writings, most distinguished both for life and for learning, which we should see to having printed with renewed type, so that, when copies had been multiplied, each of you might frequently have it in his hands and use it, as it were, as a guide of life through the rough places of this our age. It transmits the best precepts, and those most suited to the more corrupt morals of our time, concerning the pious and holy ordering of the life of priests. Everything has been drawn from the clearest fountains of theological doctrine. It contains nothing except the most salutary admonitions, nothing, finally, except the straightest and most pleasant road to attaining salvation.

But since there is scarcely anything so new and unheard-of that it can be devised or said by us without having already been said and heard more often before, we neither can nor wish to deny that some other theologians of most celebrated name have treated the same matter. For in the time in which he lived, already a hundred years ago, either the same crimes were flourishing as today, or he foretold that this tempest of ours would be polluted by so many crimes, so vividly does he depict the morals and life of most men; and indeed he sets excellently before our eyes what is to be done by us and what is to be fled. This was the principal reason why we judged that his most pious little book should be commended to you above the rest.

To this is added that we hear Urban, our predecessor, whose memory we honor and whose footsteps we gladly follow, was accustomed to commend this same treatise to you. Since, therefore, it is our duty to go before you, to call you, to stir you up, be persuaded that we do this all the more willingly and more often because we see that our exhortations among you are not empty; and we ourselves already perceive no small fruits from them, and our whole flock will shortly receive far richer ones, if you do what you do of your own accord
