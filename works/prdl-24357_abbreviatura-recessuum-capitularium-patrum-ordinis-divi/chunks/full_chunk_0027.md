But what is set forth concerning the provincial chapter to be celebrated every three years, in order to remove every scruple of hesitation, we wish to be referred to a sound understanding: namely, that when the provincial chapter has been celebrated in one year, in the next following year it may be omitted, and it shall take place in any case in the third year following.

Indeed, since it would be superfluous to issue sanctions if their execution were neglected and there were no one to protect them, we ordain and will that the said [common place?] or place of the religious estate suitable for this, if it exists in the same province, ought to be used for the celebration; otherwise it is to be celebrated in another fitting and secure place. Concerning this, and concerning the day for celebrating the first chapter of this kind, and concerning the other things to be done opportunely about the same matter, those who shall have been deputed for this by the Apostolic See shall provide on the first occasion.

And so that, concerning the same chapter and through it from then onward and perpetually, a reasonable ordinance may be had, we decree that in the said chapter first to be celebrated, and always thereafter successively, those presiding over the individual chapters to be celebrated, or the greater part of them, shall provide and take care to ordain by whom in the following chapter the sermon is to be delivered and Mass celebrated; and they shall choose the place for celebrating the next occasion of the aforesaid chapter, and who ought to preside in it. And thus let it be done thenceforth in successive provincial chapters.

They shall also provide which abbots, and how many, and how often, ought to visit the monastic cathedral churches, monasteries, and other places of the same order or religious estate up to the next provincial chapter itself. If it seems necessary to the lords presiding, the following chapter from the same constitutions of Benedict XII, *On subventions and collections*, may also be read. *Likewise from the Benedictina, chapter 5.*

Since in the often-mentioned constitution of the general council, among other things, it is expressed that those who have assembled in the triennial chapter are to lead a common life and proportionally meet the common expenses to be incurred, we, considering that besides expenses of this kind other expenses are necessarily required not only for the provincial but also for the aforesaid annual chapters, decree and ordain that in the same chapters, notwithstanding the contradiction or absence of anyone, for liberties, necessities, utilities, common expenses, and other customary matters, since a burden divided into parts is borne more lightly, collections, subventions, or contributions are to be made, imposed, and proclaimed by apostolic authority, according to the judgment of the presidents or definitors with regard to a provincial chapter of this kind, or of the abbot or other person presiding over the annual chapter itself and of the definitors with regard to the aforesaid annual chapter.
