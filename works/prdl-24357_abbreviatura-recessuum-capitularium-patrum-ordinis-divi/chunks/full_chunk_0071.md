LXVII

*The sixteenth provincial chapter, celebrated at Erfurt for the third time, in the year of the Lord 1456.*

In the year of the Lord 1456, on Jubilate Sunday, the sixteenth chapter was celebrated at St. Peter’s in Erfurt. In it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Berthold of St. Stephen’s, Würzburg; Johannes of St. Michael’s, Hildesheim; Udalricus in Blaubeuren; Rubertus of St. James’s, Mainz.

*The election of future presidents and visitors belongs to the presidents.*

We decree and declare that the election of succeeding presidents and visitors belongs to the presidents for the time being, just as the constitution of Pope Benedict XII defines.

*That secular persons may not attend capitular acts.*

We will that henceforth no secular person be admitted to the deliberations and acts of chapters; but when some act must be reduced to public form, then notaries and honest, trustworthy witnesses are to be summoned.

*Concerning conformity of habit and tonsure among those coming to the chapter.*

We also will and ordain that henceforth the prelates coming to the chapter, and the proctors of absent prelates, should strive for conformity of manners, especially in habit and tonsure. And those who use birettas should not wear them bare and openly, with the hood of the cowl lowered, as some are accustomed to do, but should place the hood of the cowl itself under the biretta for the sake of conformity, since this too seems to be a sign of humility. And we will this to be firmly observed henceforth in every place, especially by the simple conventual brothers of our order, except in the divine offices.

*That the names of the abbots and proctors present at the chapter be inserted individually in the recess.*

We have also decreed that henceforth, in the recess of the chapter celebrated for the time being, the names of the fathers present and of the proctors of the absent be written down individually.

*Concerning not requiring an oath in the act of visitation.*

The visitors of the provincial chapter, in the act of their visitation, shall not constrain either prelates or subjects, by oath or otherwise indiscreetly, to disclose or publish hidden matters, especially criminal ones, unless the order of fraternal charity has been observed.

*That provostships and priories be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter.*

Certain abbots who have provostships, priories, and deaneries subject to themselves or to their monasteries have, as we have learned, hitherto been negligent in visiting them. Therefore we decree that the same provostships, priories, and deaneries are henceforth to be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter. The abbots to whom they are subject, however, may likewise attend such visitation if they wish. And such persons visited shall be bound for the time being to pay only half the levy.

*Monks and nuns ought not to devote themselves to disorderly feasting.*

It has become known to us that there is an abuse among certain persons of our order: that after dinner, and also after supper, they hold in turn disorderly and excessive feasts and drinkings, mixing vain and scurrilous talk with them. This abuse we remove by the present statute and forbid it to be done henceforth, ordering that those who shall be found transgressors in these matters be duly punished by the visitors with abstinence on bread and water, or otherwise according to the nature of the offense.

And because the aforesaid abuse is said to be fostered by a certain other abuse, altogether more detestable, namely that in certain monasteries they usurp special revenues and arrange for them to be dispensed not through the cellarer but through whomever they themselves wish: we likewise will that this abuse be abolished entirely, and that all things be brought into common use and the necessary things be supplied to all by the cellarer according to the Rule.

*Concerning the visitation of monasteries of nuns.*

The monasteries of nuns of our holy order are to be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter.
