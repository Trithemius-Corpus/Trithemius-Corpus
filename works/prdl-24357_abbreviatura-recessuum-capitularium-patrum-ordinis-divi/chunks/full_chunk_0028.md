But those which shall be made in the individual churches, monasteries, or other places of the same order or religious body shall be made by three upright and faithful monks of each church, monastery, or place in which, as stated above, they shall be made; of these, one shall be appointed by the abbot or other prelate of that place, another by the prior or priors and administrators or officers, and the third by the chapter of the church or the convent of the monastery or place of this kind. They shall be diligently kept in one chest locked with three locks, and shall be applied only to the use of those for whom they were made, and to no other uses. For faithfully observing and fulfilling these things, the said three abbots shall take a bodily oath in the presence of the presidents, and the aforesaid monks in the presence of the abbot and chapter, and also of the convent of the church or monastery, or of the prelate of the other principal place where the said annual chapter shall have been held. Nor may another contribution be made so long as anything sufficient from the first contribution happens to remain.

And let the aforesaid presidents, and each of them, who [unclear] to provincial chapters of this kind, as also abbots and superiors or the other already-named persons, have the power, by the authority applied above, of demanding, collecting, and levying collections, subsidies, and contributions of this kind from all and singular abbots subject to them, if they have or shall have any, and from priors, prelates, administrators, officers, and beneficiaries of whatever kind who shall be subject to that annual chapter, or to the church or monastery or other principal place; and of compelling all and singular the aforesaid persons, by the same authority and without appeal, through ecclesiastical censure and the other remedies of law and of the order or religious body itself, to pay in full the aforesaid collections, subsidies, and contributions, as shall have been ordained in such provincial and annual chapters, with terms assigned concerning this; and freely to publish and execute the aforesaid things, and processes and sentences in cathedral churches, monasteries, and other places of the same order or religious body, whenever and as often as it shall seem expedient to them; and also to absolve from the said sentences, provided that fitting satisfaction has first been made; and to relax and remove all processes and sentences of suspension and interdict; and to dispense with them concerning irregularity, if, being so bound, they shall have incurred any by profaning the divine offices, or otherwise involving themselves in them, yet not in contempt of the keys.

When these things have been read by the promoter of the order, other things also from the constitutions of earlier chapters may be read at the pleasure of the lord presidents. When all these things have been completed, one of the presidents, beginning the provincial chapter, shall say: “Bless ye.” Let the response be: “The Lord.” And again let him add: “Let us now speak, venerable fathers; from the sacred canons concerning the order [unclear] and from the constitutions of the supreme pontiffs you have heard the authority of this sacred provincial chapter, to celebrate which God has gathered us together into one; supported by that authority, we begin this provincial chapter itself in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” Then let him add:

“We protest, therefore, first, that we wish this place and the other places of this monastery necessary for us for celebrating this chapter of ours to be assigned to us, and we assign them as a capitular place. And we wish this protestation to be held as repeated as often as, being assembled capitularly in the same places or in any one of them, we shall ordain, establish, or discuss anything.” Then the same president shall receive from the notary a manual promise that the acts of the chapter will be kept secret and not revealed publicly. And let the promoter say:

“We protest further that if anyone excommunicated, suspended, or interdicted is here, we do not intend to celebrate this chapter in his presence; therefore, if anyone knows himself to be such, let him leave this capitular place, and let him not presume to be present unless absolved.” And, turning to the notary, the same promoter shall add:
