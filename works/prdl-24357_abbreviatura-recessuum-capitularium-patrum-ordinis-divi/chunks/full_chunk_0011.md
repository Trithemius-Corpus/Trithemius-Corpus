This sacred chapter decreed and established that, when visitors come to any monastery for the purpose of visitation, the abbot and convent of that monastery shall have the horses and belongings of the visitors guarded with the greatest diligence. But if anything of their goods or belongings should happen to be lost or taken away, before all else the abbot and convent of that monastery shall without any contradiction pay at once to those visitors the value of the goods or belongings lost. And nevertheless such negligence shall be punished in chapter at the discretion of the presidents. This was also often established afterward in other chapters.

*Of the penalty for visitors who neglect to visit at the due time. Chapter 16.*

Since the increase of holy religion and regular observance arises in no small measure from frequent and diligently performed visitations, lest so wholesome an institution of the fathers be brought to nothing through the torpor of negligent visitors (for where the governance of discipline is despised, what remains is that religion suffer shipwreck: De statu monachorum, Attendentes, in Clem.), the fathers in almost all chapters celebrated up to now have imposed penalties on visitors if they neglected their office. And first, indeed, in the constitution of Benedict XII, chapter 3, concerning visitors, it is said thus: But if the visitors themselves have neglected to carry out their office, we will that they incur automatically the penalties incurred by those who do not come to the provincial chapter without having a canonical impediment. And in chapters 4 and 5 and certain others it was established thus: This sacred chapter further decreed that those appointed to visit, if they have neglected the time appointed for them, namely within the year, shall pay the penalty like those who neglect to come to chapter, that is, thirty Rhenish florins, with moderation and just assessment reserved to the discretion of the presidents in the provincial chapter. And in chapter 19, celebrated at Bamberg for the second time, it was established that all visitors who have not completed their visitations as enjoined on them shall be bound, for each monastery not visited, to pay three florins to the chapter as a penalty, especially in the case of monasteries of men, unless they have a legitimate cause excusing them. This same thing was likewise established in chapters 20 and 21. Besides these things, however, the presidents, whenever it seems necessary to them, can also impose other penalties. Concerning these matters also the constitution of Honorius III, Extra, De statu monachorum, chapter Eaque, speaks thus: Let the following visitors diligently investigate the traces of the earlier visitors, and let them report their negligences and excesses to the following general chapter, so that according to their fault they may publicly receive the penalty due.

*When the visitors first come to any monastery to be visited. Chapter 18. How they should begin.*

Therefore, when the visitors have gone to visit some monastery, if it can be done, on that same evening they shall summon the abbot and convent, the monks and professed lay brothers, to the chapter place, or on the following morning at an hour that they have decided is suitable; when these have been gathered, they too shall enter. And when all have sat down, one of the visitors shall say: Benedicite.

*When they have answered, Dominus, let him add these or similar words.*
