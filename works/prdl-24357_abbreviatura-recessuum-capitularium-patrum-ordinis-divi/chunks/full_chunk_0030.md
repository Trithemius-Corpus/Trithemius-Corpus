And if the presidents wish to give any further admonition, let them do so, especially if there are any who differ from the others in their tonsure, so that after dinner they may see to it that they are properly shaved. Then the presidents shall conclude: “Our help is in the name of the Lord.” The others shall answer: “Who made heaven and earth.” And so let them go. But before they are dismissed let all be admonished that, at the first or second signal of the bell, all are again to be present in the chapter-place; and, if it seems good to the presidents, some penalty shall be imposed on those who arrive late.

*After dinner.* When the fathers have assembled together at the ringing, let them say the customary prayer, the president beginning it, which ought to be said on bended knees before every capitular act, as follows:

“Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love, who through diversity, etc. Alleluia, alleluia. Kyrie eleison. Kyrie eleison. Our Father.” One of the presidents, adding, shall say: “And lead us not into temptation.” The others shall respond: “But deliver us.” President: “Save your servants.” Response: “My God, who hope in you.” President: “Send them, O Lord, help from the holy place.” Response: “And from your Zion [unclear] them.” President: “Be to us, O Lord, a tower of strength.” Response: “From the face of the enemy.” President: “O Lord God of hosts, turn us.” Response: “And show us your face, and we shall be saved.” President: “O Lord, hear my prayer.” Response: “And let my cry come unto you.” President: “The Lord be with you. Let us pray.

“Stretch forth, O Lord, to your faithful the right hand of heavenly aid, so that they may seek you with all their heart, and may be worthy to obtain what they worthily ask. O Lord, by your inspiration go before our actions, and by your help carry them forward, so that every operation of ours may always begin from you, and, once begun, be ended through you. O God, who taught the hearts of the faithful by the illumination of the Holy Spirit, grant us in the same Spirit to know what is right, and always to rejoice in his consolation. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.”

These things shall be said by the president who gives the address, the others responding. After this the same president shall say, “Bless.” The others shall answer, “The Lord.” And he shall add: “If any one of you, reverend fathers, wishes to propose anything for the common good of the chapter, of the order, and of the persons of the same, let him set it forth confidently, with fear of the Lord, reverence, and humility; and we shall grant him a hearing with all patience.” But if anything necessary has been proposed, let the presidents, together with the definitors, have mature deliberation as to what is to be done in each matter. The presidents also sometimes customarily ask all the fathers in order, according to the requirement of the matters that arise: “If any fathers have complaints to propose against anyone, which could be remedied by us, let them propose them humbly and without disturbance, and in like manner we shall patiently hear them.” And he shall then add: “Likewise, if there are any brothers sent on behalf of their own people with testimonial letters to propose anything against anyone, let them present the letters and explain the reasons; we shall also hear them patiently and kindly.” After this, if there are any matters to be handled capitularly, let them be handled; and let one of the fathers be appointed who on the morrow shall sing Mass for the brothers. Then a brief exhortation can be given to the fathers, how they ought to conduct themselves in lodgings and in every place, in this or a similar manner:

“Venerable fathers, as you heard today when it was read from the Rule of Saint Benedict, according to which we are bound to live: the abbot who is set over the brothers ought to instruct them with holy doctrine and to go before them with examples of good works; and unless he presents both to his disciples, without doubt he will receive a fearful sentence in the strict examination of God’s judgment. For he is believed to act in Christ’s place in the monastery; therefore he must follow Christ.
