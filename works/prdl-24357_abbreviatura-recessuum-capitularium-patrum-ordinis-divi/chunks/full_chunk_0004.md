Moreover, they must be gentle, not cruel. 10, q. 3, *Cavendum*. For this office men are to be chosen who are mature and experienced in many matters, not neophytes, masters before they have been disciples. 16, q. 1, *Si clericatus*; 61 dist., *Miserum*. Yet I do not say that mature young men are not to be preferred to dissolute old men, since they are deservedly called presbyters, that is, elders, by wisdom and not by age. 84 dist., *Porro*. But those gray-haired in morals should be appointed as visitors. What this grayness is is stated in Job 28: “Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom, and to depart from evil is understanding.” 26, q. 2, *Qui sine*. And prelates are also called elders because of their honor. 21 dist., *Cleros post me*.

Let the visitor be a companion to the good through humility; but toward the insolent and those who despise discipline, let him show the severity of his authority, because governance is diminished if too much humility is shown by prelates toward subjects. 86 dist., *Quando*. And yet this severity must be tempered with kindness. 45 dist., *Disciplina*, and c. *Sunt*. Therefore, from among the fathers, if it can be done, let the better men always be appointed to this office, men who know and are able to take counsel for the needs and cases of individuals; men who first do in their own affairs what they try to do in those of others. For thus in c. 6 our provincial fathers said, c. 5: “Visitors appointed by the sacred provincial chapter shall first and before all things visit and reform themselves and their monasteries, with those subject to them everywhere, lest, when they direct their eyes to the reform of others, the weak mutter under their breath and say, ‘Physician, heal thyself.’” Luke 4. Therefore let them first begin with themselves; then let them visit and reform the other monasteries committed to them, both of monks and of nuns, with such diligence as they can. To this pertains *De censibus*, *Romana*, book 6, and 2, q. 7, *Iudicet* and the two following chapters; 25 dist., *Primum*; 61 dist., *In sacerdotibus*. 83 dist.: “No one rightly assumes the office of a monitor unless he condemns errors by his own actions and shows love of innocence by his manner of life.” Thus there.

Therefore let visitors strive first to visit themselves, and so proceed to the correction of others. For it is written in Job 5: “Visiting your appearance, you will not sin.” For he who neglects himself cannot amend another, as the Lord says in Luke 6: “If the blind lead the blind, both fall into the pit.”

*III*

*Lastly, That Visitors Should Have Zeal for God and for Souls. Chapter 4.*

Finally, the visitors themselves must, according to knowledge, be zealous for the salvation of those to be visited, so that they do not conduct their office negligently and in passing, lest, with the lazy servant, they be judged useless by the sentence of condemnation. And let them have zeal for regular discipline and for the constitutions of the fathers, knowing beyond doubt that they will be richly rewarded for their labors by the giver of all good things. Let them carry out the office of visitation committed to them as if the care of all whom they visit belonged to them alone. Yet let their zeal be discreet, lest, if, which God forbid, they correct sinners indiscreetly, they themselves incur a graver fault. 24, q. 3, *Non imperpetuum*. And where they find things worthy of some correction, let them correct not from love of vengeance nor from hatred, but from zeal for justice. 23, q. 4, final section. And the just and holy are indignant at sinners not through hatred but through zeal for discipline. 45 dist., *Vera iusticia*. Nor should they be remiss where fault has deserved discipline, because if they leave the faults of offenders unpunished, they sin gravely. 11, q. 3, *Si is*; 23, q. 5, *Qui viciis*. And a prelate who does not correct crimes is called not a prelate but a shameless dog. 83 dist., *Nemo*. He has the guilt of the doer who, when he is able to correct, neglects to amend. 86 dist., *Culpam*; 83 dist., *Error*. And not to correct the wicked but to foster them is to kill them. 23, q. 5, *Qui viciis*; 45 dist., *Sed illud*.
