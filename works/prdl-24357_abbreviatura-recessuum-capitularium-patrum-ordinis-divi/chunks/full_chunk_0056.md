Likewise, provisions are not to be supplied to monks in money. And concerning a prudent elderly porter. Likewise, monks are not to be received for a price. Women are not to enter the cloister of men. And abbots and each of the officials are to render an account to their visitors. And silence is to be observed at its proper times by all and in all places. And concerning nuns, as above. And the other things that were issued in the preceding chapters were also here enacted as though newly decreed.

*Concerning the claustral prior, what sort he ought to be.* This sacred chapter has decreed that a prudent and discreet prior be appointed in every monastery, as the more suitable one can be found in the congregation, who is able in deed and speech and diligent regarding regular discipline, and observes the articles of the order concerning him or the brothers. But if he is found to be a transgressor or blameworthy in any respect, let him be admonished once and again. If he does not amend, let him be removed by the abbot from the office of the priorate, and let another who is suitable be substituted in his place. This is to be understood not only of the prior, but also of all officials of the monastery.

*The lord presidents, visitors, and treasurers are to come before the day of the chapter.* We further enact and ordain that the presidents, both past and future, of the chapter shall henceforth come before the appointed day together with the treasurers and visitors, so that the treasurers are there on the penultimate Saturday before the Sunday on which the chapter is to be celebrated, and the presidents and visitors on the following Tuesday, so that they may report opportunely to the presidents concerning their visitations. Therefore these same presidents and treasurers are also ordered, as has been said, to come before the day of the chapter to be celebrated, so that they may inform one another about each particular matter, and render account to the new presidents and to the chapter concerning receipts and expenditures. This statute had also been issued in the earlier chapters, namely in the first and following.

LVI

*Concerning visitors.* We further decree and ordain that the visitors appointed in chapter shall, immediately after the chapter has been celebrated, if it can conveniently be done, or as soon as possible, begin and complete the act of their visitation, having God alone before their eyes. We also wish that these appointed visitors, and those to be appointed, not omit to visit also the monasteries of nuns in the dioceses in which they are to visit.

*Concerning the penalty of visitors who neglect to visit at the proper time.* If the visitors neglect to visit within the time appointed for them, namely within the year, they shall pay the penalty, like those who come to the chapter neither in person nor through suitable proctors, namely thirty Rhenish florins, saving moderation and just assessment at the discretion of the presidents in the provincial chapter.

*Concerning the procurations of the visitors.* Commanding also, we wish that every abbot and convent, when their visitors have come to them for the sake of visitation, shall satisfy them within three days with the sum of three Rhenish florins.

*Concerning the confessions of monks.* We prohibit and forbid all monks, conversi, and brothers subject to us that anyone henceforth should dare to confess to anyone inside or outside the monastery without the permission of his superior.

*Concerning the celebration of masses.* Concerning celebrations, let them observe those things that are contained in the constitutions of lord Pope Benedict XII and in the statutes of the preceding chapters; and we perpetually forbid them all games and unseemly exercises, lest they become [unclear].
