The visitors themselves also, if they find any monks contumacious and rebellious, shall, in our stead, strike them with regular censure according to the measure of their fault, without respect of persons, not sparing rebels, because of their obstinacy, through the power of friends, but rather casting out the diseased sheep lest it infect the sound sheep. But if abbots are found negligent in correcting, according to the mandate of the visitors and the regular statutes, either themselves or their monks, let them be denounced and reproved, and so punished publicly in the general chapter that their punishment may be an example to others. But if an abbot who is not exempt is found by the visitors excessively negligent and lax, they shall without delay report this to the diocesan of the place, and by him let a faithful and prudent coadjutor be given to him until the general chapter. But if he is found to be a dissipator of goods, or otherwise deserving to be removed, after this has been reported to him by the visitors he shall be removed by the diocesan, without the din of legal proceedings, from the government of the abbey; and meanwhile let a suitable administrator be provided for the monastery, who shall take care of its temporal affairs until provision has been made for the monastery itself concerning an abbot. But if perhaps the bishop is unwilling to fulfill these things or neglects them, the visitors or those presiding over the general chapter shall not delay to make known the bishop’s default to the apostolic see. We command that these same things be done concerning exempt abbots by the visitors or those presiding over the general chapter, the deposition of those abbots being reserved to the apostolic see, so that, when an abbot who seems fit to be removed has meanwhile been suspended from administration by the visitors or by those presiding in the chapter, a suitable administrator shall be assigned to the monastery. The presiding officers, however, shall report to us, through faithful and prudent messengers, their excesses and the other matters which shall have seemed fit to be made known to the chapter; and to these messengers expenses shall be sufficiently supplied from the common contribution of the abbots, according to each one’s means.

The following visitors, however, shall diligently inquire into the traces left by the earlier visitors, and shall report their negligence and excesses to the next general chapter, so that they may publicly bear the penalty due according to their fault. We command the same to be observed also concerning the abbots presiding over the general chapter. And after a few words: We command that all these things also be observed in monasteries which do not have their own abbots but priors, and also in monasteries of nuns, so far as concerns the articles suitable for abbesses and nuns.

*Then, if those presiding have ordered it, also the following.*

*From the constitution of Pope Benedict XII, on provincial chapters, chapter 2.*

We also decree and ordain that in the order or religion of the aforesaid black monks, in each of the provinces deputed by us below, there shall be held every three years, in a place to be deputed for this purpose, saving the right of the diocesan bishops, a common or provincial chapter of abbots and of priors who do not have their own abbots, and of other priors of cathedral churches of that same order or religion, or of others who are greater in those churches after their prelates, by whatever name they are called, and of the other prelates who, as was said above, do not have their own abbots, exempt and non-exempt, of the same order or religion. To this chapter, unless a just impediment prevents them, all the aforesaid abbots, priors, and other prelates shall assemble, and in regard to the reformation of such order or religion and to regular observance they shall strive to fulfill the things set forth in the same constitution.

But those who shall not be able to come in person to that chapter, in order to allege the cause of a just impediment, shall send a monk of their own from the same church or monastery, if this can conveniently be done; otherwise another suitable man, with a sufficient mandate, a sufficient cause of excuse, and also power [unclear].
