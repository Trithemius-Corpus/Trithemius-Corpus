*That the procurators of absent persons may be able to be present at chapter discussions.*

It pleased the lord presidents and the chapter that monks who are procurators of absent fathers and have sufficient procuratorial authority may be present at chapter discussions, just as they have hitherto been present.

*Also concerning the procurators of absent persons.*

The procurator of absent persons must be fully informed about the visitations of those who send him, if there have been any visitators, and must make a report about all these things to the lord presidents; he must take upon himself, in the place of those who send him, penalties and humiliations, and within the space of two months must send the recess of the chapter to those by whom he was sent. But if he has not done this and is found negligent in the aforesaid matters, if he is an abbot let him be punished by a penalty of two florins for each monastery; if he is a simple monk, by suspension from divine offices.

*On the propriety and quality of clothing.*

We also ordain and decree that abbots and religious, whoever they may be or to whatever offices they may have been deputed, both inside and outside the monastery shall wear their habit decently, or a pluvial garment over their scapulars, with cowls sewn on and birettas covered, and shall go about with a fitting household also, all affectation being set aside.

*If an abbot, in excuse of himself, [sends] letters of princes to the chapter.*

No abbot or prelate shall henceforth presume to obtain letters of princes to be sent to the chapter concerning his absence, under penalty of three Rhenish florins to be paid to the chapter without any remission. [unclear]

*What sort of person ought to be elected as abbot, provost, or prior.*

The lord presidents, together with the fathers, decreed that no one henceforth be elected or postulated as abbot, provost, or prior unless he has stood, without pretense, for at least the space of a year in observance and regular life, and has held and observed it. But if otherwise has been done, there shall be done concerning such a person what the lord cardinal legate ordered in such matters to be done by his letters.

*Whether a general letter of license should be granted to monks by their prelates.*

We most strictly forbid any prelate of our province or chapter henceforth to grant to his brothers, to be sent to whatever monastery, a general letter of license; nor shall he permit those already so granted to be used, but rather let him prudently revoke as soon as possible what he imprudently granted to the peril of souls.

*Concerning surreptitious apostolic indults.*

We have also salutarily decreed that apostolic indults already obtained, or perhaps to be obtained in future without the knowledge of the presidents, especially those which tend to the detriment of observance and regular discipline, are henceforth not to be used, as being useless and surreptitious.

*The twenty-first provincial chapter, held at Nuremberg for the fourth time, in the year of the Lord 1473.*

In the year of the Lord 1473, in the sixth indiction, on the Sunday Misericordia Domini, the twenty-first provincial chapter was held at Nuremberg, at Saint Giles, for the fourth time. At it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Theodericus of Bursfelde, Guntherus of Saint Peter in Erfurt, Johannes of Saint Giles in Nuremberg, and Johannes of Holy Cross in Werdea. These statutes of the elders were repeated here.
