[Correction?] ought not to be indiscriminate, but, according to blessed Gregory in the *Moralia*, judging justly, holding the balance in its hand, it should carry justice and mercy in either scale-pan. Dist. 45, *Omnis*. For the harshness of excessive rebuke receives neither correction nor salvation. Ibid., *Cum beatus*, and c. *Disciplina*. Therefore, lest zeal be deemed indiscreet, let it be tempered with mercy and gentleness.

*That visitors should be discreet in their inquiries. Chapter 5.*

Those also who are appointed as visitors should be wisely discreet, imbued with the divine Scriptures, and experienced in this business either in themselves or in others, lest they rashly believe things that ought not to be believed, or impudently conceal things that ought to be believed. For either is an evil. For he who believes quickly is light of heart. Ecclesiasticus 19. And we ought not quickly to believe evil things about others. Dist. 86, *Si quid*. And evil things concerning one’s neighbor must first be proved before they are believed. 2, q. 1, *Deus*. And 3, q. 8, *Iudicet*. 11, q. 3, *In cunctis*, and c. *Quamvis*. For it is written, Ecclesiasticus 19: Do not believe every word. 11, q. 3, *Non solum per* [unclear]. But to wish to believe no one is the mark of an impudent man. And indeed the innocent man always believes well and judges well of others. Proverbs 14. 22, q. 4, *Innocens*. Therefore visitors should be discreet and prudent, so that, considering the zeal, life, and merit of those making depositions, they may be able to know whom they ought to believe. For witnesses are to be weighed according to worthiness of faith and seriousness of conduct. 4, q. 3, § *Item in testibus*. And there throughout. Therefore, insofar as it can be done, visitors ought diligently to examine the intentions, motives, and causes of those making depositions, lest perhaps they be moved by no just reason. Finally, our fathers decreed thus on this matter: Visitors ought especially to be zealous for religion, mature and discreet in character, and also very solicitous and circumspect in carrying out the act of their visitation. And a little later it is added: But visitors should not readily believe every [unclear].
