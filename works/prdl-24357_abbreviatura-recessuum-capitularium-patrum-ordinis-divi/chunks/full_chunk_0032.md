And we so condemn them in these writings, and declare that they are to be compelled to pay by ecclesiastical censures, and we compel the same persons. The assessment of these monies we have fixed at 2 Rhenish florins, to be paid without remission to our treasurers within [unclear] under penalty of excommunication, which, once that term has elapsed, they shall incur by these writings unless they have made satisfaction truly and effectively before the appointed day.

From these words the proper form for the process is to be taken. Or, if the lord presidents wish to act more mildly, then the last clause should be changed into this or a similar one: The assessment of these sums or quotas of money we reserve to ourselves or to our visitors. The presidents may, however, first cite them to hear the assessment made, which does not seem necessary, since the Benedictine constitution simply entrusts it to the lord presidents.

Then the promoter of the order should accuse the rebels and say: Reverend fathers, in the second place I accuse of contumacy a, b, c, abbots, who do not appear, neither by themselves nor by lawful proctors, although they have been sufficiently awaited, and who do not show that they have obeyed the concluded reform and have desisted from their contumacy and rebellion; and I ask that they be deemed contumacious, as they are, and that in their contumacy they be declared to have fallen under the sentence of excommunication contained in the processes of the past provincial chapters; and that appropriate letters be decreed thereupon, with further penalties, censures, and sentences, even to the invocation of the aid of the secular arm.

Then the same president should add: We deem the aforesaid absent persons contumacious, as they are. And he should add this sentence:

Having invoked the name of Christ, having God alone before our eyes, by this our sentence which, while residing in this place of presidency, we deliver in these writings with the counsel and assent of those learned in the law, we pronounce, decree, and declare: that the lords a, b, c, abbots, and a, b, c, conventuals, contumaciously absent, because of their contumacy and open rebellion, in that they damnably refused in the chapters to receive and effectively enter into the concluded holy and due regular observance, and, not desisting from this rebellion of theirs, did not appear, nor did they take care to allege lawful or reasonable causes why they should by no means be bound to this, have fallen under the sentence of excommunication according to the tenor of our processes in the past chapters, or in the last chapter celebrated at N.; and that they as such are publicly to be denounced and are denounced. And we command that our necessary letters be issued thereupon, also under other censures and sentences, decreeing by these presents even inclusively to the invocation of the aid of the secular arm.

It should be noted that the lord presidents, against absent contumacious persons, may also, if it pleases them, proceed otherwise: namely, that in the recess they set down that within a certain time they are to come to terms with them, or with the visitors, or with others to whom they have given authority, a term being given as canonical warning. But if they neglect this, once the appointed term has elapsed, they are excommunicated by the fact itself, to be absolved by no one except by the presidents themselves or by the visitors. But if it has been done thus, then they should take care to bring a process of this kind to their certain notice.

After the sentence has been passed, the absent persons should be cited again to hear and see the expenses assessed on the morrow, under this or a similar form, and let it be affixed in the evening:
