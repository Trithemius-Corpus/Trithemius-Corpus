Let each one sleep clothed in a separate bed, in the same place, if it can be done, or at least in groups of ten or twenty.

But not in chambers, especially if they have not been suitably latticed, so that the bedding in them can properly and clearly be seen; bedding according to the manner of life, at the abbot’s disposition. And those who have feather mattresses are not to use them.

If, however, chambers are found in the dormitory, we command that they be destroyed by the visitors or by their order, or at least suitably latticed. In these places and at these times silence is to be commanded to be kept according to the Rule. We further will that silence be observed at all times in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and cloister, and that after Compline no one be allowed to say anything, except as is contained in the Rule. Before the said Compline, however, let the Collations of the Fathers, or another edifying work, be read.

*On the collation.*

*On the porter of the monastery.*

At the gate of each monastery one prudent and discreet porter or doorkeeper is to be appointed, so that without the permission of the abbot or of another presiding officer in the monastery no freedom of going out from it may be granted. But if anyone goes out without such permission, let him be subjected to the penalties of the constitution of Benedict XII, *On Permission Not To Be Given for Going Outside the Monastery Without Reasonable Cause*. The porter is also to be attentive that he permit women in no way to enter.

*That a sufficient master of novices be had in the monastery.*

We ordain that abbots place in their monasteries sufficient masters who will introduce their novices to the elementary sciences and, once they have been introduced, send them on to the general studies, assigning them a pension according to the form of the statutes of the often-mentioned Pope Benedict XII.

*An inventory is to be made at the assumption of each abbey.*

Let an inventory be made at the assumption of each abbot, as is contained in the Benedictine constitution. And so that the condition of the aforesaid monasteries may be known, we command, by virtue of holy obedience and under penalty of excommunication to be imposed by the visitors, that each abbot be bound to deliver distinctly to the visitors, in writing, the ancient and modern condition of his monastery.

*On grants and leases at farm, and on sales and loans.*

Let them not give places of the aforesaid order at farm unless the Benedictine constitution has first been consulted concerning places not to be given at farm; this we will to be firmly observed. We also will that when [unclear] loans are to be made, the constitution concerning loans and sales likewise provide for it, and that it likewise be observed.

*As many monks are to be maintained in the monasteries as can be conveniently supported.*

We will and ordain that the ancient established number of monks be maintained, if the resources suffice for this; but if not, let them maintain as many as can conveniently be supported. Nor may anyone defend himself in any way under pretext of a privilege or custom, which should rather be called a corruption, by which they say that they ought to receive no one unless he is noble, especially if they have no nobles whom they may receive or who wish to enter the aforesaid monasteries; yet if such are found, other things being equal, they are to be preferred.

*Necessary things are not to be supplied to monks in money.*

Victuals and other necessary things to be supplied to monks are in no way to be supplied in money or revenues, notwithstanding any constitution or ordinance whatsoever. And in this let the constitution of the aforesaid Benedict be observed, chapter [unclear], *That Victuals Are Not To Be Supplied in Money*. We will, however, that the said victuals and garments be supplied to the aforesaid religious persons at the [unclear] appointed in the Benedictine constitution, *On Censuses and Revenues To Be Paid*, chapter 21.
