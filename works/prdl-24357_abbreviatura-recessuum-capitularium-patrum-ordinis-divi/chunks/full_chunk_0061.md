We command all and each of the prelates of our province and those subject to the chapter, that each of them, immediately after notification of the present letters, in his monastery, should strive, as far as he can, to correct and reform himself and his monks according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the canonical sanctions, and the statutes of the provincial chapter and of the order, especially in the three substantial matters: obedience, continence, and poverty; in the eating and abstaining from meats; in divine worship and the other monastic disciplines, trembling at that terrible but truthful sentence of our blessed father: “Let the abbot know that whatever the master of the household shall find less useful in the sheep lies as the fault of the shepherd.”

*The seventh provincial chapter was held on the Monks’ Mountain near Bamberg, in the year of the Lord 1429.*

In the year of the Lord 1429, in the seventh indiction, in the twelfth year of the pontificate of lord Martin, pope the fifth, on the tenth day of the month of April, which was the Sunday *Misericordia Domini*, our seventh provincial chapter was held in the monastery of Saint Michael of the Monks’ Mountain near Bamberg. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Georgius in the castle of Saint Peter of Erfurt, Heinrich of Saint Giles of Nuremberg, Hugo in Alpersbach.

*On the statutes of the ancients approved.*

First of all we approve and renew the ancient statutes, and we will that all of them, just as they were duly and lawfully issued, be inviolably observed by those subject to our chapter.

*That those coming to the chapter should live communally during those three days.*

The provincial sacred chapter further decreed that those coming to celebrate the provincial chapter in future times, according to the constitution of Innocent, pope the third, of happy memory, *Extra, De statu monachorum*, chapter *In singulis*, should lead a common life and be refreshed together during those three days. And only two dishes of fish should be served, one of eggs or of fried things, and two good pottages, with cheese added, and some fruits if they can be had.

*On the [unclear] of the lords.*

We establish and ordain, by the authority of the presidents, with all the fathers consenting, that each of the presidents shall have full power and authority jointly and severally to carry out execution and to proceed, according to the tenor of the Benedictine letters, against all and each persons of either sex who oppose those visiting them, or do not admit them, or hinder them, or obstruct them in any other way whatsoever; and against those who offend monasteries in persons or in goods in any way whatsoever. And that they should compel the visitors to exact, for the chapter, according to the Benedictine letter, from the monasteries the visitation or the penalties for contumacy from abbots who neglect to come to the chapter.

*Visitors. On fulfilling the time of visitation.*

Within the year they shall faithfully complete the office of visitation entrusted to them, and as quickly as they can they shall make a report of it to the lord presidents. Those rebellious against the visitors shall be denounced to one of the presidents, the nearer one, so that he may proceed against such rebels, and also against those who do not obey the visitors, by apostolic authority through ecclesiastical censure according to the measure of the faults and of the rebellion.

*On certain statutes placed in the preceding chapter and repeated in this one word for word.*

In the immediately preceding chapter certain statutes were issued, which in this one also were precisely repeated and inserted word for word in the recess, namely: that the visitors should first strive to reform themselves and their own; likewise that, after the word of God has been set forth, they should proceed without the exaction of an oath; and concerning the full reform to be introduced by them; likewise the mandate made to the prelates concerning the reform of themselves and their own, all of which are contained in the preceding chapter.
