Monks may neither make spiritual co-parents for themselves nor have familiarity with women. 18, q. 2, *pervenit ad nos*.

Monks are not permitted to live with nuns or to dine with them. 18, q. 2, *diffinivimus*, and in the two following chapters.

Monks are not permitted at their own pleasure to expel abbots or appoint others. 18, q. 2, *nullam*.

Women are forbidden to enter the monasteries of monks. 18, q. 2, c. *pervenit ad nos*.

A monk is not permitted to possess anything of his own. 12, q. 1, *non dicatis*, and c. *quia tua*; but all things ought to be common to them, c. *scimus*.

Incontinent monks are to be confined in work-prisons for severe penance. 27, q. 1, *impudicas*. 12, q. 1, *non dicatis*.

A monk is not permitted to receive anything without the license of his prelate.

A congregation of monks, however holy it may seem, is rarely found without an admixture of wicked men. 47, dist., *quantumlibet*. And this is a saying of Augustine to Vincent the Donatist, where he also says: “As I have found it difficult to find better men than those who have advanced in a monastery, so I have not found worse men than those who have fallen away in a monastery.” Hence I think it was said in the last chapter of the Apocalypse: “Let the just man be justified still, and let him who is in filth be filthy still.” Thus there.

A monk who seeks to live delicately in religion, though he was poor in the world, is to be detested. 12, q. 2, *gloria*.

A monk has the office not of teaching but of lamenting, that he may mourn either himself or the world, and wait fearfully for the coming of the Lord. 16, q. 1, *monachus*.

Monks who fail in religion are the worst, and those who make progress are the best. 47, dist., *quantumlibet*.

True and religious monks, if good, are to be honored before the rest. 16, q. 1, *qui vere*.

Monks are not to be received into a monastery for a price, nor are they to be allowed to have private property. *De statu monachorum*, c. *monachi*.

A monk who has private property is to be removed from communion of the altar; and if such a man is found at the point of death, he is not to receive burial among the brothers. Ibid., and c. *super quodam*, and c. *cum ad monasterium*.

Monks are bound to keep continual silence in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and cloister. Same chapter, *cum ad monasterium*.

Healthy monks are not to eat meat. Ibid., § *in refectorio*.

*From the Summa of Brother Angelus, on religious persons.*

Whether religious are bound to be perfect. It is answered that they are not, except with that perfection to which they have bound themselves; but they are bound to tend toward perfection, according to Thomas, Second Part of the Second Part, question 186. Contempt of perfection is contrary to this. Hence they are not liars, although outwardly they bear the state of perfection, because they do not assume it as though professing themselves to be perfect, but as professing that they are tending toward perfection. Hence they would indeed be liars if they turned their mind away from the intention of perfection, according to Astesanus, book 6, title 47.

*On obedience, collected from the Decrees.*

Obedience alone possesses the merit of faith and is rightly preferred to sacrifices, because by sacrifices another’s flesh is slain, but by obedience one’s own will is slain. 8, q. 1, *sciendum*.

Obedience ought to be promised in writing, which is by custom and by the precept of the Rule of Saint Benedict. Likewise, he who knows that he has been set over others should not take it hard that someone has been made prelate over him; but let him himself also render the obedience that he demands.

It is often not expedient to obey commands. 11, q. 3, *non semper*. It is not always evil not to obey commands: when a lord orders those things that are contrary to God, then indeed one must not obey. And the following chapter, and c. *Iulianus*, c. *si is*.

One must obey superiors promptly. 93, dist., *si inimicus*.

God must be obeyed chiefly. 8, dist., *quae contra*.
