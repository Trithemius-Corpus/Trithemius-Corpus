*Bull of the Council of Basel concerning the reform of the Order of Saint Benedict throughout the province of Mainz.*

The most holy general synod of Basel, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit, representing the universal Church: to the beloved sons of the Church, all and singular presidents and visitors appointed anywhere, and to be appointed in future, for future provincial chapters of the black monks, greeting and the blessing of almighty God.

Among the manifold cares by which we are distracted by the varieties of affairs and business arising in the universal Church, that one especially stirs and moves our minds by which regular orders and places devoted to the yoke of holiness may flourish again in spiritual things; whence we willingly expend effective solicitude for this, that those orders and places themselves which have incurred the reproach of deformity may be led back to the paths of due reform, and, with the Lord continually directing them, may receive increase in those same spiritual things and also in temporal things.

Indeed, a petition lately presented to us by the beloved sons of the Church, religious men of the order of the black monks of the [unclear] nation, contained that, although some time ago the fathers had included salutary articles concerning the reform of that order, nevertheless some of the subjects of this order and nation, when provincial visitors or reformers come to them according to the form of those articles, do not permit themselves to be reformed, <[unclear: about one line damaged]>

taking occasion from the fact that such articles did not proceed from us, and that, if we should ordain any reform, they would receive it.

Wherefore, on behalf of the religious men of this nation it was humbly supplicated to us that we would deign to command that reform be made in all monasteries in which the greater regular observance does not flourish, of the nation and order, according to the content and form of the aforesaid articles.

We therefore, whose principal concern this is, and who have been gathered into one by divine providence for this purpose, that the salutary reform of the Church of God, in head and in members, may be steadily made and may persevere, considering that articles of this kind are conformable to written law and to the Rule of blessed Benedict, and that they ought to have place and to be observed not only in the aforesaid nation but in all and singular other nations, monasteries, and places of the said order, as regards such reform, and wishing also to provide opportunely concerning the premises, by these writings command your discretion that you and each one of you, as it pertains to the office of each and shall pertain according to the time, whenever it shall seem expedient to your consciences, which we burden as to this matter, should faithfully and diligently exercise the office of visitation and reform in all monasteries and places of the often-said order in which the greater regular observance does not flourish, according to the content and form of the said articles, the texts of which we have caused to be entered below word for word, by the authority of the universal Church.
