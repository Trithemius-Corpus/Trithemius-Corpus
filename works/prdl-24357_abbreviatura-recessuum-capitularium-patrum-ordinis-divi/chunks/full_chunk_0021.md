11 q. 3, *Imperatores*.

A monk ought to obey his abbot more than the bishop in those matters that pertain to the Rule. 18 q. 2, *Quod sit*, § *Nullus*, and c. *Abbates*, 9 q. 3, *Conquestus*.

The commands of superiors must be obeyed even if they themselves act otherwise, unless they have erred in the faith. 2 q. 7, *Sacerdotes*.

Never should evil be done through obedience; but sometimes, rather, a good may be omitted because of obedience: [unclear]. 11 q. 3, [unclear].

*Likewise, collected from the Decrees concerning the correction of sinners.*

Correction ought to be made out of charity, according to the diversity of faults. 24 q. 3, *Corripiantur*.

Correction ought to be made with the intention of healing, not of raging. 86 dist., *Odio*; 23 q. 4, *Ita plane circa me*.

Correction ought to be mixed with kindness and severity. 45 dist., *Disciplina*; 12 q. 2, *Fraternitas*; 45 dist., *Omnis*.

For excessive severity on the part of one who rebukes receives neither correction nor salvation. 45 dist., *Cum beatus*.

Many things on these matters are found scattered above in the formula of visitation.

Before God he curses who consents to sins. 11 q. 3.

He who consents to sinners and defends another who offends will be accursed before God and before men, and will be corrected with the severest rebuke. Hence also a certain most holy father says: If anyone defends a sinner, let him be restrained more sharply than the one who sinned. These things are said there by Isidore, and c. *Ita corporis*, and in the two following chapters.

He who does not resist errors consents. 83 dist. Error which is not resisted is approved; and truth, when it is not defended, is oppressed. Indeed, to neglect to disturb the perverse when you can is nothing other than to foster them; nor is he free from the scruple of hidden fellowship who ceases to oppose manifest crime. It is a saying of Innocent, and c. [unclear], Pope Pius.

For what does it profit him not to be polluted by his own error, who [unclear] stands in agreement with error? And in the same chapter Gregory says:

You see that he consents to error who does not hasten to cut away the things that ought to be corrected.

The faults of inferiors are imputed to the negligence of rulers. 86 dist.

The faults of those under orders are to be referred to none more than to idle and negligent rulers, who often nurture many pestilences while they pretend not to apply the more austere medicine.

Men are to be loved, sins to be hated, [unclear]. Let them hate sins, not men. Let the proud be corrected; let the weak be borne with; and those whom it is necessary to chastise more severely are to be punished not with the spirit of one raging, but of one healing.

Everyone is to be pitied; the sinner is to be angered at. 23 q. 4. These two names, when we say “man” and “sinner,” move us; in vain do they say: because he is a sinner, correct him; because he is a man, pity him. Nor will you wholly free the man unless you have persecuted the sinner. These things are Augustine’s, on the Psalter.

XXI

*The manner and form of celebrating the provincial chapter of the fathers of the Order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz.*

Since, in celebrating provincial chapters, many occupations are accustomed to confront the lord presidents, lest, when those well-worn and experienced have departed or are absent, those less experienced and not accustomed to celebrating chapters of this sort should happen to be distracted by those very many occupations, I have taken care to commit the present form in writing for the knowledge of those who follow. I saw and heard it in those very chapters at which I was often present when they were being celebrated. Although in it we do not have all the things that are to be done in chapters, nevertheless many things, and the more necessary ones, will be found. For I judge that this is useful both for those present, in the exercise of discipline, and for those to come, by the example and regard of their predecessors.

*That the presiding officers, visitors, and treasurers should arrive before the day of the chapter.*

Therefore the presidents, past and future, together with the visitors or treasurers, should arrive some days before the appointed day for celebrating the chapter, being meanwhile also supported at the expense of the chapter, in order to discuss and mutually advise one another on the things that arise to be done, as was ordained in the fifteenth chapter, celebrated for the second time at Seligenstadt in the year of the Lord 1454.

On the Saturday preceding the day of the chapter, let one citation be made of those absent and of those who have not yet made satisfaction concerning the imposition of the contribution. The promoter of the order, by mandate of the lord presidents, shall have this citation affixed by the notary of the chapter to the doors of the monastery under vespers or compline. And he shall have it similarly done in two other churches, if need be. The citation may be made under this form.
