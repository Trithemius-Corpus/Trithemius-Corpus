*That foodstuffs are not to be given in money. Article 34.*

We forbid foodstuffs, clothing, and other necessities to be supplied to monks in money or revenues in any way, notwithstanding any constitution or ordinance whatsoever. And in this matter the constitution of the aforesaid Pope Boniface XII is to be observed. We also will that the aforesaid clothing and foodstuffs be fittingly supplied at the appointed times to the religious of the Benedictine order.

*Concerning the vice of incontinence. Article 35.*

The visitors of the chapter also, with exact diligence, are to make inquiry whether anywhere there are persons marked by incontinence; and wherever they find that this is so, especially superiors, or those who connive at the incontinence of others, they are to take care with all zeal to purge out that vice with due punishment. And if, perhaps because of favor shown to persons, they cannot do this, let them report it to the provincial chapter. The said visitors are to inquire whether abbots are excessively carnal in distributing the offices and benefices of their monastery, so that they have more regard to the advancement of their blood-relatives, and because of them the monastery suffers ruin or harm in spiritual matters and is damaged in temporal matters; and they are to rebuke the guilty very severely and report them to the provincial chapter. They are also to inquire whether the abbot, in pride of heart, treats his monks harshly and not in brotherhood, as though he were not one of them; and whether he does not permit them to say anything, even charitably, for the good of the monastery and of morals, if he himself by his own fury is overturning his house; and all whom they find guilty they are to take care diligently to correct, or to denounce them to the provincial chapter.

*Concerning the obligation of these [constitutions].*

By these things we do not understand that the aforesaid matters bind more to guilt than they formerly bound, but only to penalty.

*Concerning the constitutions of the supreme pontiffs. Article 37.*

We also will that the declarations of the Roman pontiffs issued concerning the aforesaid Benedictine constitutions shall have place in the foregoing matters.

*That visitors are to be diligent in all things. Article 38.*

In all the foregoing articles it is entrusted to the visitation of the visitors that they be solicitous for the gain of souls, and according to the condition of persons and likewise of places they are to act prudently in all things.

*That secular persons are not to be appointed over the necessities of the monastery.*

Secular persons are not to be appointed over offices, Article 39, nor monks to receive and collect the goods of the monastery and to distribute them, except insofar as they are subject and servants of the officials themselves of the monasteries, and are bound to render account to them concerning what has been received and distributed. Given at Basel, on the tenth day before the Kalends of March, in the year from the Lord’s nativity 1439. Here ends the bull of the Council of Basel for the reformation of the order, transmitted to the chapter.

*Concerning the life and morals of the visitors, especially while they visit.*

Since formerly and below. Therefore, first of all, we will and, under attestation of the promise made to us and of the strict examination of the Last Judgment, enjoin upon you that at all times, and especially in the time of your visitation, in all conduct, life, morals, and discipline, you study to bear and conduct yourselves so religiously, sacredly, and honorably that to the others to be visited by you, not only by word but also by example, you may present the form of all perfection; proceeding in the time of visitation in fitting habit, with household and mounts appropriate and honorable, not excessive, so that each of you be content with a chaplain and servant, with three horses. *Moderate*
