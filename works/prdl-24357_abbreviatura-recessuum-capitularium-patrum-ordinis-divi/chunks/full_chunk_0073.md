*Concerning the preacher.* The one who is to give the sermon should arrive somewhat before the aforesaid appointed day, and should in good time submit the sermon itself, in the form in which it is to be delivered, to the correction or alteration of the presidents.

*If one of the presidents should happen to be absent.* If at such an advance meeting any one of the presidents is absent for any reason, the rest shall discuss and arrange all things that are necessary, with power to call in one of the fathers present if need be, until the one arrives who ought to have attended this advance meeting.

*Concerning the death of a president.* Concerning the death of any president, or of another failing in any way, the bull of Pope Martin V, which is placed above at the beginning of this volume, is to be observed, with this declaration added in this chapter: that the authority of the former presidents lasts up to, but not including, the day for beginning the chapter, and on that day the authority of the new presidents begins.

*That schoolboys not take part by singing in the choirs of monks.* Since the association of schoolboys and secular clerics in the divine offices is recognized as a corruption of maturity in the minds of religious, the lord presidents decreed that those same schoolboys and clerics are to be sent away to their parishes. But if anywhere they could not be sent away without notable scandal, the visitors of those monasteries, after hearing the reasons, shall determine how far and in what manner they ought to be tolerated.

*The eighteenth provincial chapter, celebrated at Wuerzburg for the third time, in the year of the Lord 1464.*

In the year of the Lord 1464, in the twelfth indiction, on Jubilate Sunday, the eighteenth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of St. Stephen at Wuerzburg. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Berthold of St. Stephen in the same place, John of St. Michael's in Hildesheim, Melchior of St. Ulrich's in Augsburg, and Eberhard of Monks' Mount. In it it was ordained that serious resistance be made to those who wish to transfer themselves from the order to the secular state or to that of secular canons, and that procurators be appointed against them in the Roman curia.

*In difficult and important cases the votes of the fathers are to be sought.* We wish above all and ordain that in the great and difficult cases of the chapter and of the order, henceforth during the time of the chapter itself, the votes of the individual fathers present be canvassed in order; and each of them, according to God and his conscience, when asked, shall say with humility and reverence what he has judged better.

*Concerning the multiplication of constitutions.* Noting that, although in earlier chapters many useful things were decreed, from which as yet, alas, little fruit is found to have followed because of the negligence of very many, since it is superfluous to enact laws, however celebrated, if they are not duly committed to execution, and lest the multitude of statutes increase the material for transgression, we do not now wish to establish new ones, but command the observance of the old constitutions to be kept by each more diligently than hitherto.

*The nineteenth provincial chapter, celebrated at Monks' Mount for the first time, in the year of the Lord 1467.*

In the year of the Lord 1467, in the fifteenth indiction, on the nineteenth day of the month of April, which was Jubilate Sunday, the nineteenth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Monks' Mount at Bamberg. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots John of Bursfelde, Melchior of Augsburg, Heinrich of St. Michael's in Hildesheim, and John in Werdea.
