But at the beginnings of this kind of new arrangement they shall summon two neighboring abbots of the Cistercian order, to provide them suitable counsel and assistance, since from long custom they are more fully informed in holding chapters of this kind; and these, without contradiction, shall associate with themselves two from among them whom they shall see to be expedient. But these four shall preside over the whole chapter, so that none of them thereby assumes for himself the authority of prelacy; whence, when it is expedient, it can be changed by prudent deliberation. Let a chapter of this kind be held continuously for some fixed days, according to the custom of the Cistercian order; and in it let there be diligent discussion concerning the reform of the order and regular observance. And what shall have been decreed with the approval of those four is to be observed inviolably, all excuse, contradiction, and appeal being removed; provision nevertheless having been made where the chapter ought to be held at the next term.

And those who have assembled shall live a common life and shall all meet the expenses together proportionately, so that, if not all can lodge in the same house, at least several may lodge together in different houses. Also in the same chapter let religious and circumspect persons be appointed, who, according to the form prescribed to them, shall take care in our stead to visit each abbey of the same kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, correcting and reforming what they shall see to need the office of correction and reform; in such a way that, if they know that the ruler of a place should be removed altogether from administration, they shall notify his own bishop so that he may take care to remove him. But if he does not do so, let the visitors refer this to the examination of the apostolic see. We wish and command this same thing to be observed by regular canons according to their own order. But if, in this new arrangement, any difficulty whatever should arise which cannot be settled by the aforesaid persons, let it be referred without scandal to the judgment of the apostolic see, while the other things that have been provided by concordant deliberation are to be observed without opposition.

Moreover, diocesan bishops shall take care to reform the monasteries subject to them in such a way that, when the aforesaid visitors come to them, they may find in them more that is worthy of commendation than of correction; taking the utmost care that through them the said monasteries are not burdened with undue charges, for we wish the rights of superiors to be observed in such a way that we do not wish the injuries of inferiors to be endured. To this end we strictly command both diocesan bishops and the persons who will preside over the chapters to be held that, by ecclesiastical censure, with appeal removed, they restrain advocates, patrons, vice-lords, rectors and consuls, magnates and knights, or any others whatever, from presuming to injure monasteries in persons or property. And if perchance they have injured them, let them not fail to compel them to make satisfaction, so that they may be able to serve God more freely and peacefully.
