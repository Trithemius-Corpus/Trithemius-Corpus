But those who do not come, when a lawful impediment has ceased, or who do not send to it, as has been stated above, an excuser and procurator, their fellow monk or another suitable person, with a sufficient mandate, are bound to pay, within a term to be fixed by the same presidents, double what they would have spent in coming to the said chapter, remaining in it, and returning from it, to be estimated according to the judgment of the same presidents or of the greater part of them, and to be converted to the use of that provincial chapter; and the presidents themselves may coerce them, by ecclesiastical censure and by the authority [apostolic?] applied, to pay this double amount, and may publish, or cause to be published, in the places of the same order or religion, the proceedings to be carried out against them in this matter and the sentences to be pronounced. And no grace may be granted or remission allowed concerning this double payment, either in whole or in part. And nevertheless proceedings are to be taken against the aforesaid absentees, especially if they maliciously withdraw themselves, or in order to avoid correction, or so that the condition of their church or monastery may not be known, or otherwise as shall seem good to the said presidents or to the greater part of them, their absence notwithstanding.

Concerning the presidents also and the visitors to be appointed in the same chapters, and all other things to be carried out in them, let those things be observed which are contained in the constitutions of the said general council and of Honorius, pope of happy memory, our predecessor; the statutes, ordinances, and additions of ours written above and below being nevertheless observed, in the manner in which they are modified and restricted in the same.

And because the instruction and salutary observance of the prelates and monks of that order or religion is involved, we decree that the priors of cathedral churches of the same order or religion, or those who are greater in those churches after their bishops, and after those bishops have regular care of the monks, by whatever name they are called, are to assemble in those provincial chapters under the modes and penalties designated above; and also that the same priors or greater persons and chapters, and all the monks of those churches or monasteries, even if in those churches or monasteries, or outside them, wherever they have priories, offices, or administrations, or have taken up residence, both exempt and non-exempt, even if those non-exempt persons are subject to certain exempt bishops, are included and also comprehended under the constraints and ordinances of the said provincial chapters; and that the aforesaid priors or greater persons, by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure and other appropriate remedies, with the obstacle of appeal removed, are to compel them to observe these ordinances under the penalties which the said presidents shall think fit to impose on them, and also to compel any monks whatsoever of the aforesaid churches, holding monastic benefices pertaining to those same churches, to contribute to the moderate and reasonable expenses which the said priors or greater persons have incurred in coming to those chapters, staying in them, and returning from them.

And that any ordinaries of places, exempt and non-exempt, should place no impediment upon the aforesaid persons or any single one of them, nor permit one to be placed, but, when lawfully required at the proper times, should cause all and singular these things to be committed to execution and inviolably observed.
