When these things have been finished, or as much has been read as shall please those presiding, immediately from the book of the monastery let the names of the dead be recited, if any occur. Then, from the slips brought by the priors, let the names of the brethren of the order who have died since the last chapter celebrated be recited by the same reader of the martyrology. When these have been finished, let the presider add: “May the souls of the brothers, familiars, and benefactors of our order rest in peace.” And when the others have said “Amen,” let them read, as is customary, the psalm *De profundis* to the end, concluding with *Requiem eternam*. And when each has said silently the *Pater noster*, let the official add: “And lead us not,” etc., as is contained in the proceedings of the daily chapter. When these things have been completed, let one of those presiding enjoin upon the abbot of the house that after vespers the brothers perform vigils for those dead in choir. And let him appoint from among the priors one who on the morrow shall solemnly sing the high mass for the same dead. After this the promoter of the order shall read chapter *In singulis* from the Decretals, thus: Innocent III in the general council.

*In each kingdom or province*

In each kingdom or province, every three years, with the right of the diocesan bishops preserved, let there be held a common chapter of abbots and priors who do not have their own abbots and who have not been accustomed to celebrate such a chapter; to it let all come who do not have a canonical impediment, at one of the monasteries suitable for this purpose, with this moderation applied: that none of them bring more than six mounts and seven persons. But in the beginnings of this innovation let them call in two neighboring abbots of the Cistercian order, to furnish them suitable counsel and aid, since from long custom they are more fully informed in celebrating chapters of this kind; and these, without contradiction, shall associate with themselves two from among them whom they judge expedient. Let these four preside over the whole chapter in such a way that from this none of them assume to himself the authority of prelacy; and, when it is expedient, one already provided may be changed by deliberation. Let a chapter of this kind be celebrated for certain continuous days, according to the custom of the Cistercian order; in it let there be diligent discussion concerning the reformation of the order and regular observance. And what has been enacted, with those four approving, shall be inviolably observed, all excuse, contradiction, and appeal being removed; nevertheless provision having been made where the chapter ought to be celebrated at the next term. And those who have assembled shall lead a common life and shall all together meet the common expenses proportionally, so that, if not all can be together in the same house, at least several may dwell together in different houses.

Let them also ordain in the same chapter religious and circumspect persons who, according to the form prescribed to them, shall in our stead take care to visit each abbey of the same kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, correcting and reforming what they shall see to need the office of correction and reformation; so that if they recognize that the ruler of a place must be removed entirely from administration, they shall report him to his own superior, that he may see to removing him. And if he does not do so, let the visitors refer this to the examination of the apostolic see. The things that follow in this chapter are not customarily read consequently. The following chapter shall continue: Honorius III to the abbots and monks established in Lombardy and the March of Treviso.
