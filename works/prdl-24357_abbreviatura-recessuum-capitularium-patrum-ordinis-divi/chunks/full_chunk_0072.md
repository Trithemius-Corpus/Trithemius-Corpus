*With What Caution Nuns Are to Be Visited by Prelates. LXVIII*

Since the sight of women is contagious to spiritual men, let him to whom the care of souls is committed take care never to conduct a visitation of nuns out of a desire to see them, but only for the sake of necessity; and when that has been completed, let him depart all the more quickly. Nor should he presume to enter the enclosures of the monastery unless great necessity or advantage demands it.

*On Appointing a Confessor for Nuns*

Let a confessor for nuns not be appointed without the consent of at least one president. But if anyone presumes to attempt otherwise, whether prelate or subject, let him be punished most severely by the visitors. Moreover, entry into the enclosures of nuns shall be wholly forbidden to the confessor.

*[That Monks Are to Be Received Without Regard to Persons or Nobility]*

Furthermore, it is reported that in our province there are certain monasteries of both sexes in which only knights and nobles have customarily been received to profession. From this proceeds every dissolution and abandonment of regular observance, and the total ruin of those same monasteries in spiritual and temporal matters. Let the visitors examine the reason for this custom; and if privileges are alleged, let them examine them diligently, so that, once the truth has been discovered, an abusive practice of this kind, being elated, may be abolished; and thereafter persons, otherwise suitable, may be professed there according to the Rule without regard to nobility.

*Let No One from a Reformed Monastery Be Received Elsewhere Without Letters from His Abbot*

We forbid anyone from among the monks, converts, or donors who has gone out from a reformed monastery to be received in another.

*Let No Guest Monk Be Permitted to Govern a Parish Cure. Let a Guest Monk Not Govern. Incorrigibles Are to Be Brought Before the Presidents to Be Corrected.*

*On the Proclamation of the Fathers in Chapter*

Because the fathers of the Order ought, among other things, rightly to come together for this purpose and be gathered in the Lord, namely to celebrate provincial chapters and to clear themselves before the presidents concerning their various negligences and faults, the lord presidents decree and establish that henceforth, in celebrating chapters of this kind, each of the fathers must proclaim himself concerning his faults, excesses, and negligences, with a faithful and humble mind, ready to accept punishment according to the judgment of the presidents.

*On Supplying the Negligence of the Visitors*

If any of the visitors, because of a legitimate impediment, is unable to fulfill the office assigned to him, let him signify this to the nearest president, who will supply his place, or else will appoint some other one of the prelates to supply it.

*That the Presidents and Visitors [unclear] the Day for Celebrating the Chapter at Least Four Days [unclear]*

We wish the visitors, together with the president, to hasten to arrive at least four days before the day for beginning the chapter, to report to the presidents the things discovered, done, and completed by them in their visitations, so that those presidents, once the fathers have gathered, may be ready to correct the excesses of the rebellious and disobedient, or also to punish the negligence of those same visitors if any has been found.

*The Seventeenth Provincial Chapter, Celebrated at Nuremberg in the Third Year, A.D. 1459*

In the year of the Lord 1459, on the Sunday *Misericordia Domini*, the seventeenth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Giles at Nuremberg. In it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots George of Saint Giles in that same place, Berthold of Saint Giles in Brunswick, Eberhard of Saint James at Mainz, and Henry of Blessed Mary in Ettenmunster.
