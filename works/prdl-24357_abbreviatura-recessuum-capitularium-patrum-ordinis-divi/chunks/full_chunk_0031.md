For the teaching of a pastor is useful to all; but examples of virtue are of greater benefit to the weak and untaught. Indeed, examples are more powerful than words, and it is better to teach by deed than by speech. We say these things for this reason: that, shining forth in good morals, we may furnish a good example in all things to those who see us. And, to come briefly to the point, we exhort your Paternities that in every place and time, and especially in lodgings, you do good, scandalizing no one; because the suspicion of the weak is so ready for the worse that by the scandal of one or a few the reputation of many is corrupted, which nevertheless, even with the praises of many, scarcely knows how to repair the infamy of a few. And the presidents shall instruct the notary to write the chapter proceedings. After this, at a suitable hour, the presidents shall withdraw to some more private place and hear the visitors one by one concerning the office enjoined upon them and performed by them, and concerning the conditions of the monasteries which they visited, both in spiritual and in temporal matters, and whether anywhere they were not admitted; and they shall produce copies of the letters which they left in the monasteries visited by them. But if any visitors are not present, they shall make such a report to the fathers either in writing or through their proctors. If any abbots have been notably accused in any matter by their visitors before the presidents, they shall be called, if it seems useful to the presidents, and admonished to amend themselves henceforth in the matters reported. Likewise, if the lord presidents cannot hear in person, let them appoint three or four fathers who shall hear and receive from the treasurers themselves an account in writing of what has been received and distributed; once reduced to writing, they shall send it to be deposited in the chest of the chapter. Then, when it is opportune, the promoter shall again accuse the contumacy of the absentees, and shall ask that they be condemned in expenses according to the constitutions of the order, under this form of words:

Reverend fathers, today, ex abundanti, I caused another citation to be affixed to the doors of the monastery against the abbots and other absentees who are bound to be present at this provincial chapter, so that they might still appear before your Paternities; otherwise they should know themselves bound to certain penalties. None of them has appeared or does appear. Therefore, lest they gain advantage from their disobedience, and that their punishment may serve as an example to the rest, I require you, reverend lord presidents, and ask that, according to the force and form of the constitutions of Benedict XII, you deign to proceed against the same absentees as contumacious, disobedient, and rebellious, according to the order of law, and to condemn them in expenses according to the named constitutions of the aforesaid Benedict.
