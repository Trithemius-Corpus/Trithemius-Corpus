*Concerning the enclosure of monasteries.*

With regard to the enclosure of those monasteries, careful and diligent attention is to be applied; nor shall it be lawful for any religious, except certain officials, to pass beyond the bounds of the immunity of his cloister without the superior’s special permission to go out. Let all observe the fasts instituted both by the Church and by the Rule.

*Concerning the divine office.*

Monks shall reverently, as is fitting, and distinctly perform the divine office, both by day and by night, at suitable hours. When this has been completed, they shall devote themselves to readings or to the works of their hands, according to the demand of the time and of their duties, and according to the direction of the Rule of blessed Benedict. Let books be distributed to the brothers at the appointed time. Let the brothers’ beds be inspected from time to time on account of private property.

*Concerning the daily celebration of the chapter of faults.*

Let the chapter of faults be held daily; in it, after the readings of the martyrology and of the Rule have first been said, let them proceed to the exposition of the Rule and to the examination and correction of faults.

*Concerning the celebration of masses, confession, and communion of monks.*

Let the abbot celebrate or hear mass every day. Monks who are priests and reside in monasteries shall prepare themselves to celebrate at least once a week. Those who are not priests shall confess at least once a week, and shall receive communion on the first Sunday of each month.

*That sick brothers and guests be provided for.*

Let monks be suitably provided with food, drink, clothing, and other necessities, according to the determination of the Rule. Also, suitable places shall be assigned in those monasteries themselves both for the sick and for guests arriving for a time; toward them care and kindness shall be shown according to the provision of the same Rule. Let them lead guests to prayer; let a sacred reading be read to them for edification; and let them wash their feet.

*That monasteries not be burdened with debts.*

Let it be ensured with the greatest foresight that monasteries not be burdened with unbearable loads of debts; that their goods not be dispersed or alienated uselessly and without cause; and that, if any have been alienated, they be diligently recovered.

*Concerning the preservation of property and privileges.*

Abbots presiding, or rectors, shall diligently defend and guard the rights of their monasteries. They shall turn the fruits, revenues, and proceeds of the same monasteries to common uses and benefits. Books, vestments, crosses, chalices, and other vessels and ornaments, treasures, privileges, rights, letters, and the common seals of the convent shall be kept under locks and in safe places.

The rectors of monasteries are also ordered to render an account to their visitors every year. Prelates or rectors shall fully inform the visitors by whom they have been visited concerning the state of their temporal affairs. And the prelates themselves, and each of their officials, shall each year make a lawful account to the communities concerning their administrations.

Abbots shall entirely abstain from excessive mounts, dogs, and birds, and from unnecessary and unbecoming hunting dogs and birds and other animals and beasts, by which the inner man is defiled; so too shall the religious and their household.

Monks may not have weapons within the precincts of the monastery. Let none of the religious presume in any way to have weapons within the precincts of the monastery, or to contract spiritual co-fatherhood or co-motherhood.

*Concerning nuns and their enclosure.*

Nevertheless, insofar as the foregoing can concern abbesses and nuns, we wish it to be extended to their monasteries situated within the limits of your visitation, with perpetual enclosure to be confirmed by you by apostolic authority, according to the decretal *Periculoso*, Book 6.
