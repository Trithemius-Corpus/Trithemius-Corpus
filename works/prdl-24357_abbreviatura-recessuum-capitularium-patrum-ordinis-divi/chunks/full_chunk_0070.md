*That monks not be given license to wander.*

We have also learned that some abbots of monasteries [and?] prelates do not hesitate to receive into obedience, without due proof, fugitive monks and others fleeing the restraints of discipline, and moreover sometimes wandering about after license has been extorted from their superiors; and that they give occasion for wandering to those who wish it, to the peril of their souls and the scandal of very many. We therefore, wishing to cure a deadly disease, strictly forbid such things to be done henceforth, under penalty of infamy and incapacity; after prior warning, the visitors are to impose this penalty on prelates who grant license to wander, and on monks who receive it, with heavier penalties nevertheless to be added if the fault requires it.

*Concerning proctors of absent persons.*

Let one not be proctor for more than three abbots, under penalty of suspension from divine services, to be imposed by the presiding officers for the time being, unless an evident cause stands in the way. Whether that cause is sufficient must be set forth before the presiding officers.

*Concerning the old statutes here repeated anew.*

Consequently, in this chapter very many statutes of past chapters are repeated in form, namely: that abbots are henceforth not to withdraw themselves from the chapter, as above, chapter 13; and that all are publicly and everywhere to wear a habit befitting religion, as above, chapter 1; and likewise that those visited are to pay their visitors three florins for travel expenses, as chapter 4; and certain other things, which are contained more fully above in their own places.
