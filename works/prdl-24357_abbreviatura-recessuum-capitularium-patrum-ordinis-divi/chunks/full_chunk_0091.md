*On the manner and form of reforming monasteries, according to the tenor of the bull of lord Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal of the Roman Church of the title of St. Peter ad Vincula and legate de latere throughout Germany. Rubric 14.*

*Rubric 14.*

First, that the prelates of the monasteries to be visited be so instructed that, by suitable habit and tonsure, by uprightness of life, by gravity of morals, by the word of doctrine, and by the example of holiness, they may laudably and fruitfully preside over their subjects. Let them correct and amend the excesses of individuals according to the rule and statutes of the order, without any dissimulation. Let them recall all their fugitive brethren and absent monks, with a term to be appointed for them by the visitors for this purpose, except those who preside over annexed parishes. And let no one be permitted to use the damnable practice of private property; rather, let all things be reduced to common use.

Against the incontinent, both prelates and subjects, let a heavier discipline and censures proceed, so that not only may the vices of public incontinence be punished as they deserve, but also every occasion be removed from their midst from which suspicion of incontinence could arise.

Let the three substantial elements of the rule especially be preserved most fully by all, so that no dispensation or contrary custom may ever avail anyone against them.

Let silence be observed in the oratory, refectory, and cloister, etc. Likewise, let the brethren take refreshment under table reading, which is to continue until the end of the meal. The eating of meat is to be most strictly forbidden to all healthy persons, in every place and time.

Let all likewise sleep in the dormitory, so far as possibility permits, girded with belts or cords, upon mats, mattresses, or blankets. Let them by no means use linen shirts and sheets.

Concerning the enclosure of monasteries, let due custody be applied, so that entry to the precincts of the monastery may not be open to women, even those not suspect; nor may it be lawful for any religious, except officials, to leave the precincts of his monastery without the special permission of his superior.

Let all healthy persons observe the fasts established both by the rule and by the Church.

Let the divine office, both by day and by night, be performed properly and distinctly, as is fitting, at the appropriate hours. When it has been completed, let them devote themselves to readings or to the works of their hands according to the requirement of time and business, and according to the direction of the aforesaid rule.

Let blankets be supplied to the brethren at the determined time. Likewise, let the beds of the brethren be [unclear] because of particular work.

Let the chapter of faults be observed daily; in it, after the readings of the martyrology and the rule have first been made, let them proceed, if need be, to the exposition of the rule and to the examination and correction of faults.

Let him also provide that the brethren do not neglect to confess, communicate, and celebrate at the appointed times.

Let him diligently provide for the monks concerning food and drink according to the disposition of the rule.

Let suitable places also be assigned in those monasteries, both for sick brethren and for guests arriving for a time; to all of them let care and humanity be shown according as the aforesaid rule requires. Let them lead guests to prayer; let reading be read to them for edification, and let him wash their feet.

Let him take the greatest care that monasteries not be burdened with unbearable loads of debts, and that their goods not be dispersed or alienated, according to the sanctions of Benedict XII and other apostolic sanctions, and according to the oaths sworn by abbots at their confirmation and blessing. Let alienated goods, if there are any, be carefully recovered.

Let abbots, provosts, priors, or rectors diligently preserve the rights of those monasteries. Let them render a lawful account of fruits, revenues, and proceeds, and of the condition of temporal goods.

Let them abstain from superfluous and unbecoming riding-horses, dogs, and hunting birds.
