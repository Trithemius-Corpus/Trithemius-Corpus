LXXIII

Item, Johannes in Wiblingen, Ulrich of [unclear], and Johannes of Saint Giles in Nuremberg.

*That the recesses of the provincial chapters from the time of the Council of Constance be kept in all monasteries and publicly read in the convents.*

Lest the statutes and ordinances of the provincial chapters held up to now become unknown to those who are bound to know them, we will and strictly command, under penalty of 20 florins, that all and each of the fathers cause to be written and have all the recesses of chapters of this kind from the time of the Council of Constance up to the present in one volume, which they are to bring with them at the time of holding a chapter; and that in their convents they see to it that the same recesses are publicly read every quarter. But this statute concerning the reading of each of the recesses would seem too harsh, as it appears to me. Concerning an abbreviation, therefore, I think it must be understood as will appear below.

*Concerning fugitives.*

And lest an occasion for wandering here and there should arise for monks if they were everywhere received with hospitality, we command all our abbots, under penalty of 30 Rhenish florins, that wherever they find a wandering monk of our province, immediately, if opportunity is present, they send him, at the expense of that same monastery, to the monastery from which he had gone out, to his own abbot; and that abbot is bound to receive him so sent, after paying the expenses to the sender, without contradiction.

And under the same penalty we forbid all our prelates to admit in any way, or lodge, any monks or conversi coming aside to their monasteries who have not first shown commendatory or credential letters, as they are commonly called, unless perhaps with the intention that, having detained them, they send them to their abbots.

Furthermore, because no small force of regular observance consists in visitations, *Concerning visitations. To the abbots.* therefore we forbid all those subject to us to presume in any way to impede the visitors in their offices; but let them receive and treat them honorably and reverently, and provide for safe-conduct and traveling expenses of three florins according to the statutes of the preceding chapters, under penalty of 20 florins.

*Concerning the form of visitation.*

But so that the visitors may be able to observe due order in their visitations, we will and strictly command, under penalty of 10 florins, that they take care to fulfill their office according to the tenor of the form of visitation contained in the bull of the lord Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal legate, which he gave to the visitors of our province at that time. And note that this form, contained in the constitutions of the same cardinal concerning visitation, was inserted here word for word in the recess; but since I have placed it separately with the others on folio 42, I judged it superfluous to repeat the same again in one present volume.

*The twenty-fifth provincial chapter, held at Augsburg for the second time, in the year of the Lord 1485.*

In the year of the Lord 1485, in the third indiction, on the Sunday Jubilate, the twenty-fifth provincial chapter was held at Saints Afra and Ulrich in Augsburg for the second time. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Johannes of Saint Giles in Nuremberg, Johannes of Saint Martin in Sponheim, Heinrich of Saints Peter and Paul in Weissenburg, and Paulus of Blessed Mary in Elchingen. There the following statutes were made.

*That criminals and the incorrigible are not to be expelled but imprisoned.*

Although our blessed father Benedict in the Rule orders incorrigible monks to be expelled from the monastery, nevertheless we, considering the manifest danger for many from this if it were put into practice, have judged that the statutes should be varied according to the change of times. Therefore we strictly forbid all our abbots and prelates to presume to expel or exclude from the monastery their monks, conversi, or donati, guilty of whatever crime, since such expulsion of the incorrigible offers them the opportunity to wander and to commit graver offenses. Let them thrust such persons, offending so enormously, into prisons, to be punished for a longer time according to the character of the crime committed.

And two offices are to be performed in the convents after chapters have been held.
