We have decreed and ordain that all should wear large and broad tonsures, so that the circle of them does not exceed a space or thickness of two transverse fingers; and that they should comport themselves decently in clothing, habits, and footwear, according to the Clementine *Ne in agro* and the constitution of Benedict XII, *De forma et honestate vitae*: namely, that they should wear tunics and garments closest to their outer habit, of black, brown, or white color, according to the custom usually observed among them, round and reaching to the ankles, not slit and not marked by any other deformity, having wide sleeves extending to the fist, in no way buttoned or tied, yet not so wide that their width above the fist can be stretched out with the thumb raised. Nor should it extend around the mouth of the sleeve at the elbow, as the abuse of certain persons has it, beyond what would allow the arm to be turned and introduced easily.

They shall be content with almuces of fur in place of hoods. Yet in proper places where they are accustomed to wear them, they may, with the abbot’s permission, use decent hoods slit over the shoulders to a length of at least four transverse fingers.

For linings in their garments they shall not wear sendal, or miniver, or other costly materials, but for the said linings they shall use only skins of lambs or young lambs, or at least other cloths that are not costly. No linings shall appear in any way above the neck or at the edges of garments or sleeves. *On the Habit of Those Travelling.*

Furthermore, when it happens that they travel outside the monastery, they shall wear a frock, cowl, or cloak, and under the cloak, if they have worn it, a cowl or scapular.

Novices, however, shall use a cowl inside the monastery and outside, fitted on both sides. *On the Habit of Novices.* And lay brothers shall use a scapular with a hood attached to it, closed, inside and outside the monastery. *On the Length of Garments, and on the Use of the Hood of Frocks.* We ordain that its width shall not reach the mouth of the elbow by the thickness of two transverse fingers, nor may it be narrower or so short that it does not extend as far as the middle of the elbow and of the upper bone near the shoulder. The said hood, however, shall extend at the back at least as far as the belt, and may not extend beyond it by four fingers. The length of the sleeves shall extend one cubit beyond the hand and no more. And their width shall not exceed another cubit and a half. Yet below it shall be so wide that steps can easily be taken within it. And what has been said of the frock we wish to be understood of the cowl, setting aside sleeves, since it has none.

Within the monastery, however, in places where there are twelve, all shall use the frock, except only the presiding officer in the monastery and the first after him, whom we wish always to wear the frock. Yet in work and in the divine office beneath the alb, and when riding beneath the cloak, they may wear only the scapular, which we wish to comprise a space of at least half a cubit in width.

Likewise they shall not use linen or hemp shirts, but shall wear a woolen undergarment or woolen garment in place of the said shirts. And they shall wear boots or high laced shoes, so that the width comprises a space of four fingers on the shin above the [unclear].

*Monks Living in Common Shall Abstain from Eating Meat When Healthy.*

The brothers shall live in common and eat in the refectory, in which no one at all shall eat meat. And the visitors shall be diligent to cause the constitution of Pope Benedict XII concerning the eating of and abstinence from meats to be observed inviolably. *At the Tables of the Brothers Eating* sacred reading shall not be lacking; and he who is to read throughout the whole week shall enter on Sunday with a blessing, as is contained in Rule chapter 38.
