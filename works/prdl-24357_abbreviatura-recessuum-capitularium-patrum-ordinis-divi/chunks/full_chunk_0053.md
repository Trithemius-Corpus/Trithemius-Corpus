And all the other statutes above were inserted in this recess word for word. *How prelates are to correct their subjects.*

Finally, we exhort and beseech, in the bowels of Jesus Christ, under the attestation of his strict and final judgement, all and each of the abbots, provosts, deans, conventual and cloister priors of the said order, that each should so correct, amend, and reform his subjects and monasteries, and compose their conduct in regular discipline, that when the visitors come to their monasteries for the purpose of visitation, they may find rather what edifies them than what exists as worthy of correction.

*The third provincial chapter was celebrated at Fulda, in the diocese of Würzburg, in the year 1420.*

In the year of the Lord 1420, in the thirteenth indiction, in the third year of the pontificate of lord Martin, pope the fifth, on the fifth day of the month of May, the third provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Boniface at Fulda. In it the venerable fathers Lambert, abbot of the monastery of Michelsberg near Bamberg, Frederick, abbot of Hirsau, and Heinrich, abbot of the monastery of Saint Gotthard at Hildesheim, presided.

*Concerning its statutes.*

In this chapter also many of the statutes which had been issued in the first chapter celebrated at Constance were repeated and inserted word for word, as they are given above. In it it was ordained that visitors ought not to have more than six mounts, unless necessity should require otherwise; and whatever they have beyond that ought to fall to the benefit of the chapter.

*Concerning the discipline of praying.*

For the reform of the order, first, we ordain and decree that the divine office, by day and by night, be celebrated devoutly, drawn out, with pauses, not run through, not syncopated, nor one anticipating another, and at the proper hours, as is contained in the Rule of our most blessed father Benedict. For this, as soon as the signal has been heard, all are to leave everything that was in their hands and hasten together with the greatest speed, yet with gravity, lest frivolity find occasion. And lest under pretext of ignorance, etc., as is contained above in the first chapter. Therefore let nothing be preferred to the work of God, for cursed is the man who does the work of God negligently. But lest anyone, on account of a lack of persons, be able to excuse himself from celebrating divine offices of this kind according to the institution of monks, we will that in each monastery as many monks be maintained and received as can conveniently be supported from their goods and revenues without want.
