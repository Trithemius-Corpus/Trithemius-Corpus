*XLVIII. From the Council of Constance. When and why the provincial chapter was re-erected, and at what time.*

Ancient authority teaches, and experience shows, that it is of little use to establish laws, however celebrated, unless they are duly entrusted to execution. Formerly, at various times, both a general council and Honorius, and also Benedict XII, Roman pontiffs of happy memory, are read to have issued, with much mature deliberation, salutary statutes concerning provincial chapters to be celebrated every three years in this religion and order, and concerning their general chapters to be held each year. These provincial as well as general chapters, in very many regions, are said rarely to have been put into execution for a long time. From their omission many inconveniences and almost irrecoverable losses in spiritual and temporal matters have come upon this religion.

Therefore, since from the celebration of such chapters there are undoubtedly wont to come about the correction of the depraved, the reformation of morals, the avoidance of many evils, and the promotion of manifold good, we, who bear in the bowels of charity the reformation of this sacred religion and order, whose uprightness and venerable character the testimonies of the fathers show, have resolved to restore what has been omitted in this matter, and especially to ordain new executors of the said constitutions in the regions where the old ones do not govern according to their tenor, before the sacred council is dissolved.
