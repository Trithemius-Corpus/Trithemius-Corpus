But what is permitted concerning the provincial chapter to be celebrated from three years to three years, in order to remove the scruple of any hesitation, we wish to be referred to a sound understanding: namely, that when the provincial chapter has been celebrated in one year, it may be omitted in the next following year, and be held in every third year following. Yet this must be noted: that the lord presidents, if there is need, can anticipate the term, and likewise prolong it.

When everything has been completed, after the notary and witnesses have gone out, let the fathers proclaim themselves before the presidents, six or seven [unclear], beginning with the juniors and continuing to the presidents themselves inclusive, and let them receive the salutary penances imposed on them. Then let them all read in like manner, except one president: “I confess to God, to blessed Mary, to all the saints, and to you, father, that I have sinned exceedingly, through my fault, by pride, thought, speech, deed, and omission; I beseech you, pray for me.” And then that president should read the absolution as follows:

“May almighty God, through the intercession of blessed Mary and of all the saints, have mercy on you, and forgive you all your sins, and lead you to eternal life. Amen.

“By the authority of almighty God and of the ecclesiastical keys granted in this respect to our order, we restore you from the censures of the Church and to participation in the sacraments, and we dispense with you and rehabilitate you for the execution of the things to be done, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.”

And when some psalm has been imposed for satisfaction, let all rise, and the same president, kneeling before the other presidents, should read “I confess to God.” One of the presidents should read over him the absolution as above, with the psalm.

And note that this absolution is one of jurisdiction; nor should there be placed in it that clause which many thought ought to be added: “and from your sins.” For the sacramental absolution, which a particular confession of sins ought to precede, has this clause. Likewise it should be known that this absolution avails only, namely absolving from censures if anyone had incurred them by neglecting the statutes of the order and of the chapter. And as sacramental absolution absolves from guilt, so that of jurisdiction absolves from temporal and spiritual penalty. When these things have been done, let an end be imposed on each matter. Yet it should be known that if some utility or necessity is imminent, the said lord presidents can prolong the chapter further, until everything is determined well and maturely. At the end, one of the presidents, if time permits, should deliver to the fathers a brief exhortation before the blessing, on these or similar matters:

“Venerable fathers, since now, with God’s favor, we have celebrated this provincial chapter of ours, lest, as we depart from one another, we forget because of the multitude of them the things that we have heard, listen patiently to the brief admonition that I make. We exhort you first, that each of you remember continually what the Rule says: ‘Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd falls upon him, whatever less of usefulness the father of the household shall have been able to find in his sheep,’ etc.”

And he should add by way of exhortation, as the Lord shall grant, concerning keeping pure religion, concerning faithfully carrying out profession, concerning peace and charity and goodwill toward all, etc. Finally, that they depart from their lodgings with due honesty, after debts have been paid, with the knowledge of their host, not secretly but openly, etc. And that the abbots publish the statutes of this chapter to each of their subjects, and cause two conventual masses to be sung, one for the living, another for the deceased brethren of our order, and other prayers imposed to be fulfilled; and that the recess be read publicly in their monasteries to the brethren in necessary matters.

When these things have been completed, let the blessing be given by the president, as is customary, with all bowing moderately:

“Save your servants, my God, who hope in you.

“The Lord be with you.

“And with your spirit.

“Be present, Lord, to our supplications, and dispose the way of your servants in the prosperity of your salvation, so that among all [unclear].”
