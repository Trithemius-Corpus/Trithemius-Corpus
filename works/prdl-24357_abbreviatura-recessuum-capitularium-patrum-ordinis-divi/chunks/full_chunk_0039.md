If, however, chambers are found in the dormitory, we command that they be destroyed by the visitors or by their mandate, or suitably barred off.

*On silence in four places. Article 12.* Silence is to be observed in the oratory, the dormitory, the refectory, and the cloister. And after Compline let no one be permitted to say anything, except as is contained in the Rule. And before the said Compline let the Conference of the Fathers be read, or something else edifying.

*On the porter of the monastery. Article 13.* At the gate of each monastery let one prudent and discreet porter or doorkeeper be appointed, so that without the permission of the abbot or of another presiding officer no freedom of leaving it may be granted to anyone. For if anyone shall go out without permission of this kind, let him be subjected to fitting and due penalties, as is provided in the constitutions of Benedict XII concerning the permission ordained for going outside the monastery. Let him also be attentive that he in no way permit women to enter the monastery.

*That no one from the order of mendicants be received into our order. Article 14.* Moreover, since we have heard that certain abbots and other presiding officers in monasteries have received mendicant religious as monks without the permission of their superiors, and afterwards, what is worse, permit them to wander, to the detriment of their souls and the scandal of religion, we forbid them to presume such things hereafter. And those whom they have already received, unless by authority and mandate of the Apostolic See or with sufficient permission, they shall recall to the monastery as soon as they can, and others wandering outside the monastery, or at least within a year; otherwise let them be punished by the visitors of the provincial chapter with grave penalties.

*On nuns. Article 15.* But the things that have been said concerning monasteries of men we wish similarly to be observed in monasteries of nuns with respect to the articles suited to them. Nevertheless wishing and ordaining that they be kept under due and perpetual enclosure, as is contained in the constitution of Boniface which begins *Periculoso*, concerning the status of nuns, and is commanded to be observed by this sacred Council of Basel, we enjoin the visitors of the chapter, in virtue of holy obedience, to apply diligence in this matter.

*On the master of novices. Article 16.* Likewise we ordain that abbots and other presiding officers have in their monasteries suitable and sufficient masters, who may introduce their novices to the elementary studies, and then, when they have been instructed, send them suitably, according to the form of the statutes of the aforesaid Benedict XII, to the general studies, assigning them a pension.

*On the number of monks. Article 17.* We will and ordain that in every monastery where resources and revenues suffice, the number of monks instituted from the foundation be maintained; or, if they do not suffice, that as many be maintained as can conveniently be sustained. And so no one is to be excused on the pretext of any privilege, pact, transaction, or custom, which is called a corruption, especially since we reprove that corruption by which some say that they can receive no one unless he is noble. For even if nobles, other things being equal, are preferred in reception, nevertheless others are not excluded on account of this, nor ought they to be excluded.
