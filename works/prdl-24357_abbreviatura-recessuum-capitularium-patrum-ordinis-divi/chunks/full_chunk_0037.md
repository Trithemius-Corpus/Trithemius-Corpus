Yet on this account we do not intend, just as we cannot, to derogate in any way from the other things contained in the aforesaid Rule, even if they are not now explained by us, or from the praiseworthy constitutions of the fathers and the stricter observances, so as to lessen the obligation by which the religious of the said order are bound to them as before.

*First, On the Divine Office. Article 1.*

Since the said Rule of Saint Benedict took very solicitous care concerning the divine service, we, following it, ordain that the divine office, both by day and by night, be celebrated devoutly, deliberately, with pauses, not hurried through, not cut short, at the proper hours, as is contained in the Rule of our most blessed father. And that, as soon as the signal has been heard, leaving everything that they have in their hands, they shall hasten there with the greatest speed, yet with gravity, lest frivolity find fuel; so that nothing be placed before the work of God.

And lest they be able to excuse themselves on the pretext of ignorance, saying that the signal was not heard, we will that the ringing for Matins, which is to begin at midnight or thereabouts, and for Prime and Vespers, continue for at least more than a quarter of an hour.

But anyone who does not come to the aforesaid office shall be punished without remission, as is contained in the Rule.

*On Confession and the Celebration of Masses. Article 2.*

We also will and ordain that the abbots and the others of the aforesaid order confess, celebrate, and receive communion, as is contained in the constitutions of Pope Benedict XII concerning the celebration of Masses.

And that all those who, according to the sanctions of law and of Benedict, ought to be established in sacred orders or in the order of priesthood, shall within a year be promoted to them; and against those who are negligent the visitors shall proceed as they can by law.

*On the Daily Chapter of Faults. Article 3.*

We further will and ordain that every day, in places where there are six monks, and in other places where it is customary, after Prime if only one Mass is said, but if two are said, after the first-mentioned Mass or at another customary hour, the daily chapter shall be held, in which, after the martyrology and the reading of the Rule, which shall be explained in the vernacular by the one presiding over that chapter, or by another whom he has ordered, before proceeding to other matters, the faults and excesses of the negligent or delinquent shall be punished.

*On the Books and Ornaments of the Church. Article 4.*

We will that each abbot or president, when he has come to his monastery, immediately provide within a month for clean and good vessels and a place where the holy Eucharist may be kept; and also that the books, with the other ornaments concerning the divine office and the monastery, be kept under diligent custody.

*On the Form and Decency of Clothing and Tonsure. Article 5.*

Since, through the propriety of outward habit, the professed and novices of the said order ought to show decency of morals and of religion, we decree and ordain that all wear large and broad tonsures, so that the circle of them does not exceed the space or width of two transverse fingers.
