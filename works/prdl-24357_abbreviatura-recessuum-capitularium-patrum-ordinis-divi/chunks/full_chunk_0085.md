*Concerning visitors. Rubric 6.*

Visitors of monasteries, and visitors of the visitors, must be chosen by the presidents in the provincial chapter, as in chapter 16; and only from among the reformed, as in chapter 13. They are obliged to know each single thing that has been transacted and ordained in each chapter, from the first to the last, so that they themselves, first observing most strictly in their own persons the law which they wish and are bound to impose on others, may at length more effectively require it of others and bind them to the same; and also may be able and have power to punish transgressors, as they ought, with due vengeance, under the strictest accounting to be rendered in the dreadful future judgment.

These visitors, after they have come from the chapter, must begin their visitations within a year, under penalty of thirty florins, as in chapter 4, or of three florins for each monastery to be visited, as in chapter 20, indeed also under penalty of excommunication with sentence to be pronounced, as in chapter 8, unless they are excused by reason of hostility, illness, or some other lawful cause, as in chapter 13; and they must faithfully complete them under the virtue of holy obedience, having God before their eyes, to whom they are to render a strict account. Thus, before they leave the monasteries, they are to reform effectively in them, so far as lies in them, according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the canonical sanctions, and the statutes of the provincial chapter and of the order, and especially in the three substantial matters, which are obedience, continence, and poverty. And where they do not find disciplines and ceremonies [unclear]

valid, they must institute those disciplines and ceremonies which they have received as given to them by the chapter, as in chapter 6; and thereafter they must make a report of the whole matter as quickly as possible to the nearest president.

Likewise, in whatever diocese they have been deputed to visit monasteries of men, they must also visit in it the monasteries of nuns, just as do the others to whom the care of them has been committed. Nevertheless, let them see to it that they never visit them from a lust of seeing them, since the sight of them is contagious; but when they have finished, they must depart from there as quickly as possible, as in chapter 16. Similarly, they must visit provostships, priories, and deaneries which are subject to certain monasteries, while nevertheless admitting the abbots to whom they are subject, if they wish to be present. And those visited in such places shall pay half the levy.

They must complete the very act of visitation according to the form prescribed to them by the provincial chapter and the presidents, without demanding an oath, after first setting forth the word of God, as in chapter 6. Nor must they bind anyone by oath, or otherwise indiscreetly, to disclose and make public hidden crimes, unless the order of fraternal correction has been observed. They may also visit again a monastery previously [unclear] by them, and require purgation. Likewise they can and must impose penalties according to the requirement of the faults, and exact those previously imposed either by the provincial chapter or otherwise by anyone, both corporal and monetary. The money, however, which they receive, whether contributory or penal, they must transmit to the keepers of the chest, so that it may be placed in the chest by them.

Likewise, for contumacy or rebellion and disobedience, they can pronounce a sentence of excommunication, and absolve from one previously imposed, with fitting satisfaction first having been made. Likewise, rebels and resisters, if they find any whom they cannot correct, they must denounce to the nearest presidency, so that he may proceed against such persons by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure, according to the measure of their faults.
