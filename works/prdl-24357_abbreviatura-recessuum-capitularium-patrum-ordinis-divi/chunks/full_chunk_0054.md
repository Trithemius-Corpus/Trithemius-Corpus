We most strictly forbid that anyone wishing henceforth to transfer to that religious order should promise any money for his reception, either by himself or through others, or otherwise that he will give anything, except those things which his parents, of their free will and without any agreement with the boy, offer at the altar. And those received in this way, when the year of probation has passed, shall promise to live according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, and to fulfill and observe it according as the vow of profession and promise sounds.

*Concerning the daily chapter of faults.*

Moreover we will that, in accordance with the constitution of Pope Benedict XII, the daily chapter be observed, in which regular discipline and correction shall be treated; and there also all things shall be carried out just as the aforesaid Pope Benedict established.

*That conventual places which have collapsed are to be restored.*

We also will, and most strictly command in virtue of holy obedience, that in monasteries in which perhaps refectories, infirmaries, or dormitories have collapsed, or have otherwise been destroyed, they be restored; and the negligent persons to whom such restoration belongs shall be gravely punished by the visitors.

*That entrance to the monasteries of monks is not open to women.*

We wholly forbid the female sex entrance into the monasteries, choirs, and cloisters, commanding that all doors of the same monasteries of the said order be closed as the visitors shall have arranged.

*Likewise concerning the same.*

We decree that in monasteries, or within their enclosures, in houses in which monks dwell, no one shall presume to keep women, even if they are mothers or sisters. Those doing the contrary shall be duly punished according to the fault.

*That beggars are not to be received into our order, and that those already received who wander are to be recalled to their monasteries.*

We forbid that beggars henceforth be received into our order; but those already received shall be recalled to their cloisters, and shall be instructed and reformed according to the Rule and the statutes of our order.

*Concerning the claustral prior.*

We will, and in virtue of holy obedience strictly command, that in each monastery there be appointed a provident and discreet prior, as the more suitable one can be found in the congregation of the monastery; and let him be such a one as is powerful in work and useful in speech, who is able to exercise care of the cloister and governance in spiritual matters; who shall diligently observe all the aforesaid articles insofar as they concern him, and shall cause them to be observed by all; and who, concerning the discipline of the choir, dormitory, refectory, and other things as they have previously been described, shall have watchful and faithful care in spiritual matters. And if any difficulty arises in any matter, he shall refer it to the abbot, who shall discuss and judge these things.

*Concerning baths.*

Because indeed the use of baths is sometimes necessary for monks, we will that every abbot, or the person to whom it belongs by office, shall make and build a bathing-room in his monastery, so that the way of wandering about may be closed to the monks.

*Concerning conspiracies, games of dice, and betrayals of the secrets of the chapter.*

Moreover we forbid all conspiracies against abbots, as well as private defamations of persons and of brothers, and every betrayal of the secret of the chapter or council; and let every game of dice, knucklebones, chess, and other games and disorders, by whatever name they are reckoned, cease.

*That monks are not to become co-fathers.*

Moreover we decree, prohibiting according to the ancient decrees of the holy fathers, that monks are not to become co-fathers, as in De consecratione, distinction 4, Non licet abbati, and Causa 16, question 1, Placuit communi.

*That the ornaments and books of divine worship are to be kept clean.*

Furthermore it has been decreed and established, with this holy chapter approving, that churches, oratories, altars, vessels, corporals, vestments, and other ecclesiastical ornaments be kept clean and neat. And that full provision be made for breviaries, missals, and other books, vestments, chalices, and any ornaments necessary for divine worship.
