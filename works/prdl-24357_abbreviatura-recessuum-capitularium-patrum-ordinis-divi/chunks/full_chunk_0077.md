LXXIII

In the year of the Lord 1479, in the twelfth indiction, on the Sunday *Misericordia Domini*, the twenty-fourth provincial chapter was held at St. Giles in Nuremberg for the fifth time. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Martin in Schwarzach, Leonhard of St. Peter in Castell, Bernhard in Hirsau, and Hermann of St. James in Mainz.

*Which statutes of the preceding chapters were here repeated again.*

In this sacred provincial [chapter?] several statutes were repeated as though anew; yet these are known to have been ordained earlier in other chapters of preceding times, with few exceptions, in almost the same words. Among them are these. First, concerning not using surreptitious apostolic indults, as above, chapter 20 at the end, with this clause, however, added which follows: those matters which must be expedited or obtained in the Roman curia for the order or for the monasteries, jointly or separately, are to be expedited by all the prelates and monks of our province and order through the procurator of the order then present in the curia, or with his express consent.

Likewise the statute that the visitors are to be received and treated kindly, and that three Rhenish florins are to be paid to them for travel expenses, with the penalty of triple payment added, and that safe-conduct is to be provided for them, was similarly repeated. And certain other matters also; yet because of the penalties and certain particular points it has pleased us to set them out again. Of these, this is the first.

*That no monk be received without letters commendatory from his abbot, under penalty of thirty florins.*

We also very strictly forbid, under penalty of thirty Rhenish florins, all prelates and superiors of our order and province henceforth to receive any brother or monk in their monasteries without letters commendatory from his own abbot; and those already received otherwise they are to dismiss as quickly as possible, under the same penalty, to be paid to the chapter without contradiction as often as they do the contrary.

*That no general licence be granted to anyone, under penalty of fifty florins.*

We also forbid all the same prelates henceforth to grant to their monks and professed members a general letter of licence without the consent of those presiding over the provincial chapter, under penalty of fifty florins, to be paid to the same chapter as often as they transgress this our constitution.

*Concerning visitors.*

We also strictly forbid all those subject by law or custom to our chapter to arrange in any way, either by themselves or through others, with diocesan bishops or princes or nobles, that the visitors should be prevented from freely fulfilling their office. Nor are they to object that visitors are not to be admitted without the consent of the ordinaries, since they are sent by apostolic authority and visit, in place of the lord pope, the monasteries assigned to them, as in the Extravagants, *De statu monachorum*, chapter *In singulis*, section *Ordinentur*. Nor is anyone to impose conditions on the visitors with which they will admit them, or without which they will refuse to admit them, under penalty of thirty florins.

*Concerning the records of visitations.*

Under the same penalty we command all the aforesaid abbots, provosts, priors, deans, monks, and communities diligently to keep, read, preserve, and put into practice the records, ordinances, and statutes made or to be made for them by the visitors.

*That an abbot not allow private property to a monk.*

Since private property is always destructive to monks and the overthrow of all religion, we most strictly forbid all prelates to allow any of their monks private property; for what the supreme pontiff declares that he does not dare to do is judged very rash for an inferior to presume in any way, as we have clearly in the Extravagants, *De statu monachorum*, *Cum ad monasterium*, at the end.
