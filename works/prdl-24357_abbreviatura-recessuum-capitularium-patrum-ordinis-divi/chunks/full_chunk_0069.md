*Concerning the household of the visitors.* The visitors, moreover, shall have with them an honorable household and retinue, and each of them shall be content with four mounts, unless a reasonable cause requires more.

*Those impeding visitation incur the sentence of excommunication.* Lest anyone impede so salutary an office of visitation by any contrived device or pretext, we will that all and singular abbots, priors, provosts, and their monks who refuse to receive their visitors, or who forbid them, and who are rebellious and disobedient to them, and who impede the office of visitation by nod, deed, or writing, or who subordinate it to be impeded by their friends, noble or ignoble, or by any persons whatsoever, or who order or procure by any device, by themselves or through another, or who directly or indirectly do or attempt anything whereby the visitors cannot freely and quietly exercise this office of theirs, shall incur by that very fact the sentence of excommunication. The same visitors are also bound to publish that sentence of excommunication against them and to commit it to due execution. Likewise we will that all and singular abbots, provosts, priors, convents, and persons who have rejected from themselves this reform of the lord cardinal legate and of the chapter, previously received and continued up to the time, shall incur the same sentence of excommunication, unless they effectively resume it at least within the time to be fixed for them by their visitors and continue it.

*The practice of begging is forbidden.* It has come to our ears by reliable report that persons of both sexes of our order, driven not so much by poverty as by the sake of gain, go about procuring that such begging of theirs be announced from pulpits, and even begging door to door, to the disparagement of the order.
