Folio II

*An abridgment of the capitular recesses of the fathers of the Order of Saint Benedict throughout the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg, celebrated from the time of the Council of Constance down to the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1493; and also the method for visiting monasteries of both sexes, together with a summary of the privileges of the aforesaid province, diocese, and order, happily begin.*

*On the visitations of monks, the preface begins. Chapter 1.*

“You shall visit your brothers, to see whether they are acting rightly.” 1 Kings 17. In these words it is briefly indicated what sort of visitation of monasteries there ought to be. For the visitation itself ought to be charitable, not hateful: 10, q. 3, *Cavendum*. And this is marked in that phrase, “your brothers.” For the visitor, in order to produce a worthy fruit of his visitation, should show himself brotherly and charitable to those who are to be visited, and should know that he is the brother of those to be visited, not their lord; he should hate vices, but love the brothers: dist. 86, *Odio*; 23, q. 4, *Duo*. And in the Rule of the holy father Benedict, chapter 2, *What sort of man the abbot ought to be*. For if visitation is to be fruitful, it must be charitable; for charity is the proper gift of Catholic unity and peace: 1, q. 1, *Multae autem*. And where charity is not, justice cannot exist: 24, q. 1, *Ubi*. Therefore visitation must be carried out from charity, without hatred and cruelty: 10, q. 3, *Cavendum*.

Secondly, visitation ought to be examining, that is, investigative concerning the life and morals of those to be visited: 10, q. 1, *Episcopum*; Extra, *De statu monachorum*, *Ea quae*. And this is indicated in the word “you shall visit,” that is, “you shall examine.” Thirdly, visitation should be discreetly deliberative; and this is indicated when it says, “whether they are acting rightly.” But these matters will be spoken of at the appropriate places.
