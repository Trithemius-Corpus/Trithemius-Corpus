*Concerning also the proctors of absentees*

Furthermore, we moderate and mitigate the statute of the fifth chapter, in which it is provided that no one may be proctor for more than three, decreeing that some religious man otherwise suitable may be proctor for four or even five abbots in great necessities.

*Let the visitors first reform themselves and their own, and only then visit others.*

Those appointed by the provincial chapter shall, first and before all else, visit and reform themselves and their own monasteries and those subject to them, lest, when they direct their eyes to the reform of others, the weak should murmur and say, “Physician, heal thyself.” Therefore let them begin first with themselves, and so confidently visit and reform the monasteries committed to them, of monks as well as of nuns.

*Concerning the report of the visitors to the presidents*

Moreover it has been decreed that, when the visitors have completed the duty of their visitation, they shall make a faithful report in chapter to the lord presidents concerning the state of all the monasteries visited by them, both in spiritual and in temporal matters.

*An oath concerning the telling of the truth is not to be extorted from those to be visited.*

It also pleased us that, without the exaction of an oath, after first setting forth the word of God, the visitors themselves shall begin and complete their action according to the form prescribed for them in the sacred canons.

*Likewise concerning the form of visitation.*

We also will and ordain that the visitors shall effectively reform, as far as they are able, all and several monasteries of either sex that are to be visited by them before they depart from the place to be visited, according to the Rule of our most blessed father Benedict, especially in the three essentials, which are obedience, continence, and poverty, without which no religious life subsists.
