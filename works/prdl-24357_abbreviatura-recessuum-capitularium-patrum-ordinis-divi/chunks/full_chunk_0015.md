Likewise, whether he presides in person over the chapter of faults, and how often.

Likewise, whether he corrects offenders without respect of persons.

Likewise, whether he shows himself to the brothers as exemplary and imitable in all good things.

Likewise, whether he frequently and without reasonable cause wanders outside the monastery.

Likewise, whether he takes superfluous mounts with him.

Likewise, whether he has suspect familiars, or ones harmful to the monastery.

Likewise, whether he has any suspect intimacies with women.

Likewise, whether he has in any way been marked by incontinence.

Likewise, whether he admits women into the monastery, and what sort.

Likewise, whether he diligently preserves temporal goods.

Likewise, whether he is excessively intent upon cares for temporal goods.

Likewise, whether he provides necessities for the brothers according to the Rule.

Likewise, whether he burdens the monastery with debts.

Likewise, whether he alienates the immovable goods of the monastery.

Likewise, whether in matters to be done he acts according to the Rule with the counsel of the convent, or not.

Likewise, whether he has subject monasteries; whether he visits them.

Likewise, whether he has had the recesses of the chapter published in the convent.

Likewise, whether he has observed the charter of the last visitation.

Likewise, whether he duly observes the Rule in all things, as is fitting.

And many things similar to these may be inquired into, according as there shall be need; it would be tedious to set them all out here.

*Likewise, questions concerning the other officials and brothers.*

Concerning the other officials, however, questions are to be framed according to the need, namely:

Whether there is a prior; who he is, of what sort, and how great.

Likewise, whether he is useful to the convent in procuring necessities.

Likewise, whether he is zealous for discipline according to the statutes of the fathers.

Likewise, whether he gives the brothers an example of good conduct.

Likewise, whether he corrects offenders according to the Rule.

Likewise, whether he diligently sees that the other regular statutes are observed.

Likewise, whether he is diligent in the office committed to him.

Concerning the cellarer: who he is and of what sort. Of what sort.

Whether he diligently fulfills his office.

Whether he duly attends the divine offices and conventual services when he is otherwise free.

Whether he diligently provides necessities for the brothers according to the Rule.

Whether he is humble, good, and exemplary before outsiders.

How often he renders account to the abbots concerning receipts and expenditures.

Likewise, whether he reverently obeys the abbot’s commands in all things.

Likewise, whether he is wasteful and a squanderer of the monastery’s property.

Whether he is too anxious about temporal affairs.

Likewise, whether he diligently observes the Rule in fasts, vigils, etc.

Concerning the other brothers, however, the visitors shall inquire, according as seems opportune to them, about mutual charity, peace, obedience, continence, private property, etc.

But where the abbot is diligent in correcting, there will be less inquiry concerning the simple brothers.

Finally, after all have been examined, let the abbot himself also be examined, in case perhaps he wishes to say anything. And let the greater articles concerning him, which the brothers have deposed, be set before him; let him be advised seriously, that he may amend. But if there is anything of such a kind that it ought not to pass without correction, and he denies it, the testimonies of those deposing must be examined more carefully, until the truth becomes more clearly evident. Nevertheless, tumults and scandals are to be guarded against.

Concerning the last proclamation of each one. After the examination has been begun according to custom in the chapter. Chapter 24.

When it seems opportune to the visitors, let all be summoned to the chapter-place to continue the act of visitation. When they are seated, let one of the visitors say: Bless ye. And when the response has been made: The Lord. Let him add:
