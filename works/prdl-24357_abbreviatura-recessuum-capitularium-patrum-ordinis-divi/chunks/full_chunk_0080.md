*Concerning those who do not admit the visitors*

It has come to our ears by a certain and serious report that some abbots and monks not only refused to admit their visitors, but, what is more serious, even drove them shamefully from their places with reproach and indignation, to the manifest contempt of the chapter of the order and of the canons. Therefore we exhort all and each of the prelates and professed members established anywhere throughout our province in the order, that henceforth they not presume to attempt such things, under the penalty more fully expressed in the preceding statutes; and we wish that penalty to be extended without remission against rebels of this kind by the visitors themselves or by those presiding. But all who are to be visited should meet their visitors cheerfully and reverently, receive them humbly and gladly, and treat them humanely; and, if need be, they should also provide them, at their own expense, with safe conduct as far as another monastery to be visited by them.

*Concerning the documents of visitations*

It pleased the fathers that, unless contempt has intervened, in the documents of visitations henceforth words of command or threat under censures should not be inserted, but rather exhortatory or entreating words, by which the minds of subjects may be drawn to virtues from love; unless, as we have said, contempt has intervened, or where the matter concerns transgression of the three substantial vows, or otherwise an evident necessity somewhere compels it.

*The twenty-eighth provincial chapter was celebrated in the year of the Lord 1493*

In the year of our Lord 1493, on Jubilate Sunday, the twenty-eighth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul at Hirsau, of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Speyer. In it presided the venerable fathers and lords Johannes in Sponheim, Conrad in Wiblingen, Bartholomew of Holy Cross in Werdea, and Andreas of the Mountain of the Monks near Bamberg, abbots of monasteries of the dioceses of Mainz, Constance, Augsburg, and Bamberg. In this same chapter the statutes of the earlier chapters were confirmed anew, with this one addition, which follows word for word.

*Concerning the confession of monks*

We will and ordain that every monk professed according to the rule of Saint Benedict, in accordance with the Clementine constitution *Ne in agro*, on the status of monks, must and is bound to make confession to his abbot at least once a month concerning matters that occur, and, concerning reserved cases, to the abbot as often as he knows himself to have offended in such matters, under the penalties contained in the constitutions of the supreme pontiffs, general councils, or provincial chapters. It was also decreed that, after this statute of the provincial chapters has been printed and presented to the individual monasteries, it must thereafter be read publicly at least once every three years.
