*On Whether an Oath Is to Be Required or Not Required from Those Being Visited. Chapter 21.*

But it is rightly asked whether those who are to be visited ought to be bound to speak by oath and to answer the truth about the matters asked. To know this: as I found in the ancient statutes, also of other provinces of the order, where it seemed necessary an oath was required from those being visited. This also now seems to me sufficiently reasonable, especially if the monks have conspired, or made a pact, or maliciously concealed the truth. And I think that this statute, which I approve, should be strengthened.

Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal legate, writes as follows, among other things, to the visitors of the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg: But because among very many people words of exhortation profit little unless a coercive penal censure is also added, therefore in the monasteries or places which you shall visit for the time being, after the act of salutary exhortation, when a bodily oath has been received from all concerning speaking the truth in their examination, you shall proceed according to the tenor of the commission delivered to you by our other letters.

Likewise, in the statutes of the chapter I found the contrary. For in the sixth chapter, held at Erfurt, the statute is read thus: It also pleased the fathers that, without demanding an oath, after first setting forth the word of God, the visitors themselves should complete their act. This same thing was established in the following chapter, held at Bamberg, [unclear]. Similarly also in certain others. What then? If he requires an oath, does he commit an offense against obedience, contrary to the statutes of the fathers? If it is not to be required, why did the cardinal legate of the apostolic see and magnificent doctor order it to be required? Can it never be required, and can it not be required? Plainly, I think it is so. Where it is necessary and useful, it can be required; but it ought not lightly to be required. And he took care to insert here such a form of the oath at hand, just as he also found it in the more ancient institutions of the fathers, so that if at any time it should be necessary, there should be no need of long inquiry.

*The Form of the Oath and the Manner of Swearing, with the Hand Placed upon the Text of the Gospel, [unclear]. Chapter 22.*

O brother M., I swear by these holy Gospels of God that concerning all matters about which I shall be questioned by you or by any one of you, generally or specially, I will speak the truth; and also all things that I know to require your reformation, and that are necessary for the reforming of the monastery entrusted to you to know, I will faithfully disclose to you. So may God help me, and these holy Gospels of God. Saying this, let him place his hands upon the Gospel according to Luke.

At that time, as Jesus was speaking to the crowds, a certain woman, lifting up her voice, said to him: Blessed is the womb that bore you, and the breasts that you sucked. But he said: Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and keep it. Amen.

*On Examination. Chapter 23.*

Concerning examination and inquiry: when the visitors have been prepared and are seated at the assigned place, let them have great diligence in inquiries of this kind, in asking all necessary matters one by one. And if anything worthy of correction has been deposed, let one of the visitors mark it on a sheet, which he should also guard diligently, lest it ever come into the hands of anyone.
