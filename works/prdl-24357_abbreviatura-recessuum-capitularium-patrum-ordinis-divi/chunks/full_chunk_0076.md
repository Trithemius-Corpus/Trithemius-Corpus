There were also some of the same statutes inserted anew in this recess, which are contained above word for word, namely: that the presiding visitors and the preacher are to arrive on Wednesday before the appointed day of the chapter. Likewise, that if one of the presidents is absent at this preliminary meeting, the others, having joined to themselves one of the priors if they wish, shall do everything with full power until he comes. Also the decree concerning apostolic indults not to be sought or used, as above in the preceding chapter. Similarly, that no one is to be procurator for more than three; and that the procurators of those visited are to be fully informed about each matter, under the penalty as above, and that they transmit the recess to them within two months. And this: that abbots and monks are to wear their habit decently in every place. Also that which forbids any abbot, in excuse of himself, to dare to obtain letters of princes for the chapter. Likewise, that prelates are to seek out their fugitives, and henceforth grant no general licence-letter to anyone. And that the visitors are to visit provostships, priories, and convents of nuns, the abbot of those places being able to be present if he wishes, as above. Also, that those visited are to pay their visitors three Rhenish florins for travel expenses. And concerning the penalty of three florins for each monastery not visited, imposed on negligent visitors. Likewise also this: that visitors are to denounce the rebellious and disobedient to the nearer president, to be punished. These and similar things, which are contained above word for word, were here again repeated, with the following, however, newly added, as it seemed.

A novice is not to be admitted to profession before the completion of the year of probation. To meet future dangers, relying as far as we can on regular and legal provision, we strictly forbid any prelate henceforth to receive a novice to profession before the completion of the year of his probation.

Concerning the habit of lay brothers. For pleasing uniformity in all respects, the lay brothers shall wear cowls open in the front, after the manner of the monks.

*The twenty-second provincial chapter was celebrated at Monchsberg for the third time in the year of the Lord 1476.*

In the year of the Lord 1476, in the ninth indiction, on Jubilate Sunday, the twenty-second provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Monchsberg near Bamberg for the third time. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Martin in Schwarzach, Hermann of St. James in Mainz, Heinrich in Blaubeuren, and Johannes of St. Egidius in Nuremberg.

*The statutes of previous chapters are first approved.*

*Concerning surreptitious apostolic indults not to be used, under penalty of excommunication.*

Under penalty of excommunication we command that no one presume to use apostolic indults obtained surreptitiously, tending to danger of souls and to the detriment of regular observance, whether already obtained or perhaps to be obtained in the future without the knowledge of the presidents, as is also contained above.

*Concerning other statutes placed here.*

The statute also concerning procurators, that they transmit to those by whom they were sent the recess of the chapter within two months after the chapter has been celebrated, under penalty of two florins if the procurator is an abbot, and of suspension from divine offices if he is a monk, as chapter 20 contains word for word, was here again renewed.
