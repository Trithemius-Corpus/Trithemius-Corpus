Let the visitors take diligent care not to hand over to the abbot the schedules of the deponents, lest occasion be given for acting maliciously, however zealous he may be; but let them take them with them to disclose them to the lord presidents, or certainly, if they have no need of them, let them burn them in the fire.

*What is to be received from those visited for the expenses of journeys. Chapter 28.*

Every abbot and convent visited is bound, according to the decrees of the fathers, to pay three Rhenish florins without any contradiction, as in chapter 11 and in the three immediately following. Hence in the same place it is said thus: The whole community of the fathers decreed that every abbot visited is bound to pay the visitors three Rhenish florins for their travelling expenses, unless the visitors themselves, for some good consideration, wish to act more moderately. Thus there. But let the visitors see to it that they require necessary things in such a way that they do not hide avarice under the appearance of need. For a procuration of this kind ought not to be burdensome, but moderate: 10, question 3, *Cavendum*, and [unclear].

*If visitors or their servants should dare to accept any gift or any money, offered in any way by those visited. Chapter 29.*

Because gifts make the giver and the receiver turn aside from justice: 11, question 3, *Pauper*, and chapter *Qui rector*. It is strictly forbidden to all visitors to accept any gift from those visited, or any money offered in any way or by anyone, either by themselves or through their servants, as is said in [unclear], chapter 15, concerning visitors, where it has as follows: Visitors and their servants shall receive no money on such an occasion or in any other manner; nor shall they, or any of their people, presume to receive any gift, of whatever kind or quality it may be, offered by anyone, so that they may not seem to seek the things that are their own, but those that are Jesus Christ’s. Therefore we establish and ordain that all and each of the visitors of this kind, or their servants, who, besides the expenses to be furnished to them only in victuals, shall presume to demand or receive money even from one willing to give it, or if they themselves, or any of their household servants, shall presume in any way to receive gifts, they shall be bound within a month to restore double what they have received to the church or monastery or other place of the same order or religion from which, or from whom, it was received; otherwise, from that time forward, let visitors of this kind who delay restoring that double amount to them beyond the aforesaid time know themselves to be suspended from office and benefice until they make full satisfaction for this double amount to the churches or monasteries or places thus burdened, no remission, liberality, or favor of the givers being of any force for them in this matter. And nevertheless, if it is expedient, the visitors thus suspended shall be compelled by the presidents to restore the aforesaid double amount, by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure and through other suitable remedies, with the obstacle of appeal removed.
