Whether a visitation can be repeated? The answer is that it can, if there is a reasonable cause. On this matter, in chapter 17, in the third chapter celebrated at Nuremberg, the fathers decreed as follows: We ordain that after the visitors have once visited some monastery and have received procuration from it, if they judge it expedient for the more abundant observance of regular discipline, they may, by the authority of the chapter, exercise the office of visitation again as often as it seems useful to them; and they are also to be received as true visitors, yet without the exaction of procuration. Francis Zabarella says the same thing in the said chapter *Attendentes*, question 10: that visitation is to be made as often as there is need. From all these things I conclude that the visitation in the first year must be completed from the time of the chapter celebrated, and afterward can be repeated as often as there is need; but procuration ought not to be received except within a three-year period.

*How visitors are to be received and treated in monasteries. Chapter 12.*

When, therefore, visitors come to some monastery for the purpose of visitation, let them be received and led to prayer; if they are not neighbors and frequently received, let their feet be washed, and let them be treated charitably. In conventual places let the abbot of the house give way and recognize them; let him not take it amiss if he has others set over him. 2, question 6, *Quis scit*. Yet the abbot himself, according to his custom, should give the blessing to the people in every place, not the visitors. For this has been decreed in several chapters: that visitors be received with honor and reverence and be treated charitably, as is fitting, in all things.

*With how many horses visitors ought to come. Chapter 13.*

In the first two chapters it was decreed that none of the visitors should dare to bring with him more than twelve mounts, under the penalty contained in the same chapters; therefore at that time it was permitted for visitors, and also for those coming to the chapter, to bring twelve mounts with them. Then in the third chapter, which was celebrated at Fulda, it was decreed that a visitor should not bring with him more than six mounts. But Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal legate, in his form of visitation, which he handed over to the visitors, commands that each visitor should be content with a chaplain and a servant, with three horses. Nevertheless, in the same constitutions of lord Nicholas the cardinal, he commands the visitors themselves to conduct themselves decently and to have honorable servants, so that they may not be burdensome to those visited. In chapter 15, celebrated at Seligenstadt for the second time, there is such a statute: The visitors themselves too shall have an honorable household and company, and each of them shall be content with the number four, unless a reasonable cause demands more.

*How long visitors ought to remain in one monastery.*

Therefore, lest the visitors become burdensome by the expenses of visitation, they shall strive to dispatch themselves as quickly as they can; and, as the fathers decreed, they shall not make a stay beyond three or four days in one place visited by them, without evident necessity. But if such things occur as demand delays of a longer time, they shall strive to dispatch themselves in such a way that they prove in all things that they seek not themselves but God and the peace of the visitors. Let the visitors also advise their servants to conduct themselves honorably and without murmuring in all things.
