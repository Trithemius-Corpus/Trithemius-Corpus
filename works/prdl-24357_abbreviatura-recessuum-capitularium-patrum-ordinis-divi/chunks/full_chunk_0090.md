And first, beginning with the hood of the frock, we ordain that its width shall not reach the bone of the elbow by the thickness of two fingers set crosswise; nor may it be narrower or so short that it does not extend as far as the middle between the elbow and the upper bone near the shoulder-blade. The said hood, moreover, at the back shall extend at least as far as the belt; nor may it extend beyond it by as much as four fingers. The length of the sleeves shall extend one cubit beyond the hand, and their width shall not exceed another cubit and a half. Yet below it shall be so wide that steps may easily be taken within it. And what has been said of the frock we wish to be understood also of the cowl, apart from sleeves, since it has none. Within the monastery, however, in places where there are twelve monks, all shall use the frock. But in other places where there is not so large a congregation, they may use cowls; if they wear them, no one shall use the frock, except only the president of the monastery and the first after him, whom we wish altogether to wear the frock. Yet in works, namely in the divine office under the alb, and when riding under the cloak, they shall wear a scapular, at least half a cubit wide. They shall not use linen and hempen shirts, but shall wear either a woollen undergarment or a woollen garment in place of the aforesaid shirts. And they shall wear high boots or buskins or laced shoes, so that their height takes in a space of four fingers of the leg above the ankle. If they are negligent in any of the foregoing, or within three months after they have come to their monasteries, as stated above, then, in addition to the penalties inflicted by law, we wish them to be punished by their superior in religion, or, if they have no superior, by the aforesaid visitors, in such a way that it may serve as an example to the rest.

*Concerning Divine Worship. Rubric 12.*

The office in divine worship, both by day and by night, must be celebrated at the proper hours according to the Rule, devoutly, deliberately, and with pauses, not by running through it or cutting it short. Nothing must be preferred to this work; but as soon as the signal has been heard, leaving everything that is in their hands, all must hasten to it with the greatest speed, yet with gravity, lest buffoonery find occasion, as above, chapters 1 and 3. For this reason also the signal for matins, which must be made at midnight, as above, chapter 1, and for prime likewise and for vespers, must be prolonged for at least a quarter of an hour, where above and in several following chapters. And lest, because of a shortage of persons, some could excuse themselves from carrying out that worship perfectly, it has been ordained above, chapter 3, that everywhere as many monks must be maintained and received as can conveniently be supported from the goods of the monastery; and those negligent in these matters must be punished very severely. Abbots and the others must also celebrate, confess, and communicate, as is contained in the constitutions of Benedict XII concerning the celebration of masses. And all who, according to the laws and Benedictine sanctions, ought to be established in orders must be promoted to orders within a year, as above, chapter 1, and in the following chapters. This also must be considered by the visitors: whether there are any churches annexed to the monastery; and whether any masses are to be performed inside or outside the monastery; how many there are; how they were founded and accepted; and how they are performed, and by whom. Likewise also concerning anniversaries and other foundations, or votive manual actions, and also meditations, whether they are regularly attended to.

*Concerning Monasteries of Nuns. Rubric 13.*

The things that have been said concerning monasteries of men must also be observed in monasteries of nuns, as to the articles appropriate to them, as above, chapter 1 and following. Nor should secular girls be permitted there who are not staying there for the sake of conversion, as above, chapter 16. They must be under perpetual enclosure, as above, chapter 1, as is contained in the constitution of Boniface that begins *Periculoso*, on the state of monks, book 6.
