*Concerning the habit of those travelling, whether riding or walking.*

... we have decreed that it is to be ordered in this way: namely, that the outer habit shall be a mantle hanging to the middle of the calves, cut on each side with one opening, and beneath it a scapular, which shall exceed the mantle by the length of three transverse fingers, whose appendage shall appear outwardly, because of fear of the excommunication issued against such persons as conceal or alter the habit without reasonable cause.

*Privileges of monasteries. Concerning the custody of privileges.*

And other jewels, relics, and treasures we command to be kept under faithful custody and secure enclosure.

*Concerning conspiracy and defamation.*

We forbid abbots and the other prelates and rectors, as well as private persons, [unclear] and defamation of abbots and brethren, and every betrayal of the secret chapter, commanding that transgressors be corrected by their superiors in such a way that the rest may take warning.

*That mendicants are not henceforth to be received into our order.*

We also forbid, by virtue of holy obedience, all prelates subject to our chapter to receive mendicants into our order hereafter. But those who have already been received and wander about here and there are to be recalled as quickly as possible to their monasteries.

*Mercy alone is companion to all things. Concerning deceased brethren.*

Concerning the state of the deceased, we establish and ordain that individual abbots, or their curators, shall henceforth bring and present in each chapter brief notices of the names of the deceased brethren and of the days of death of those who have died within the chapter celebrated and to be celebrated, so that prayers and suffrages may be made for them. And this is the form of the briefs: that such-and-such a priest or Levite, etc., died on such-and-such a day in such-and-such a place, etc.

*Concerning nuns.*

What has been said about monasteries of men we also wish to be observed inviolably in monasteries of nuns, as to the articles that apply to them.

*Concerning the general chapter and the publication of statutes* [unclear]

The statute also concerning the publication of all the proceedings of the present chapter and of the statutes of this chapter, set down several times above, and concerning the general chapter to be held within the year, is here again set down and renewed throughout, as [unclear] c. i at the end; let it be read there.

*Conclusion.*

Many other matters were here enacted, ordained, and treated, which are contained partly above; partly, as less necessary, I have passed over. All, however, are contained in the book of recesses.

*The fifth provincial chapter was celebrated at Saint Stephen’s in Würzburg, in the year of the Lord 1424.*

In the year of the Lord 1424, in the second indiction, on the fourteenth day of the month of May, which was Jubilate Sunday, in the seventh year of the pontificate of lord Martin, pope, the fifth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Stephen at Würzburg. In it the venerable fathers presided: Baldwin in Lüneburg, Theoderic in Northeim, Appello in Suijsheym, and Helfric of Saint John’s in Rheingau, abbots of monasteries.
