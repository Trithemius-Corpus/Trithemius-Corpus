*Concerning Absolution.*

We wish, moreover, that each of the same presidents and persons whom you shall think fit to absolve from sentences, censures, and penalties of this sort, or from any one of them, must and shall be bound to render, to each person to whom satisfaction may perhaps have to be made on that account, that satisfaction within a competent peremptory term to be fixed by you, once any canonical impediment has ceased. Otherwise, when that term has passed, they shall fall back by that very fact into the former sentences, censures, and penalties. The tenor of the aforesaid articles, as they have preceded, follows and is as follows.

*Articles for Reformation.*

Because regular discipline and the holiness of religion have almost wholly fallen, and have miserably collapsed, to the grave scandal of the whole order of Black Monks, and to the dishonor, reproach, and [contempt?] of all professed members of the same order, and, what is more dreadful, to the deadly harm of the salvation of souls. For alas, every adornment of religion has been taken away from it; and that which lately could glory in the Lord, saying, “I am black” in the lowliness of habit, “and beautiful” in holiness of morals and of conduct, now alas, the once holy monastic simplicity has been perverted into wantonness and insolence, so that no image of former holiness now seems to survive either in fact or even in appearance.
