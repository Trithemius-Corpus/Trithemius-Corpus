*The thirteenth provincial chapter, held for the second time in the monastery of Petershausen at Constance. In the year 1447.*

In the year of the Lord 1447, on the seventh day of the month of May, which was Cantate Sunday, the thirteenth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of Petershausen at Constance. Presiding at it were the venerable fathers, the lords abbots of Petershausen there, of Saint Giles at Nuremberg, of Saint Stephen at Würzburg, and of Saint Gotthard at Hildesheim. Concerning the old statutes here renewed and repeated.

First, it receives the often-mentioned bull of the Council of Basel, and orders it to be kept and read in every monastery, as above, in the same words and form. Several other statutes also are set down here in the recess, which nevertheless had often been previously defined in the preceding chapters: namely, concerning the authority of the lord presidents over rebels; and that the visitors of each diocese should visit the monasteries of nuns; and concerning the negligence of visitors to be reported by the curators to the presidents; with many others which are set out fully above in various places.

*That only reformed men are to be deputed to the offices of the provincial chapter.*

As the Lord says in the Gospel: if a blind man leads a blind man, both fall into the ditch. We decree that, so far as can be done, this regulation be observed in the election of presidents and visitors: that reformed men be deputed to such offices, because he who has neglected first to reform himself can never properly instruct others.

*Concerning the negligence of visitors.*

And because we have learned by experience that some visitors have hitherto shown themselves negligent in the tradition and have not taken care to fulfill the office enjoined upon them, therefore we will and command all visitors to strive to fulfill the office of their visitation with all diligence as quickly as possible; otherwise, according to the tenor of the Benedictine [statute?], we will and declare that they and each of them incur by that very fact the penalties which those incur who did not come to the provincial chapter without having a canonical impediment.

*Who are to be excused from the chapter.*

It pleased the fathers that henceforth no one should put forward any excuse for not coming to the chapter except bodily infirmity, enmities, or evident and plainly established poverty. And concerning these only, the proxies of absent abbots are to be admitted in chapter.

*Concerning wandering monks, not to be received.*

Wandering monks without letters commendatory from their abbot are not to be kept, nor received as conventuals, nor lodged in any monastery beyond three days.

*Concerning a monk who has laid violent hands on an abbot.*

If any monk, at the devil’s prompting, should break out into such audacity that he lays violent hands on an abbot or another brother, such a man is not to be accepted or kept in the province.

*Concerning one who wishes to transfer from an unreformed monastery.*

If anyone with pious intention wishes to transfer from an unreformed monastery to a monastery, let him seek permission from his superior; but if he refuses, let him seek it from one of the presidents, who will give him permission to remain there for a time, or even to establish himself there, but with the consent of those receiving him.

*Concerning the safe-conduct and travelling expenses of the visitors.*

Each abbot or prelate visited, with the help of his convent, shall conduct his visitors as far as another monastery to be visited by them; and, when they wish to depart, he shall not refuse to give them three Rhenish florins for the contribution or collection imposed by the chapter for relieving burdens, unless the visitors themselves, for a pious reason, have decided that less should be received.
