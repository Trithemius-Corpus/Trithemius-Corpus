*The Form of Visitation and the Method of Proceeding.*

With gentleness preserve the censure of discipline, correcting crimes and excesses without any respect of persons; strive to extend to all the offices of charity and kindness. Take care to recall those at discord to concord, diligently exhorting prelates to solicitude and vigilance concerning the souls committed to them, and to reflect how much more perfect than others they ought to be, since they have pledged themselves to teach others the way of perfection; and what grave danger hangs over them, because in the strict examination of the Last Judgment they will be bound to render an exact account for their own deeds and for all those individually committed to them; and that all, both prelates and subjects, [unclear] of their profession, how they may fulfill them; likewise how sweet is the yoke of the Lord and light the burden to all of good will.

But because among very many the conversations of exhortation profit little unless the censure of coercion also be added, therefore in the monasteries or places which you shall visit for the time being, after the customary conference of salutary exhortation has first been given, for the carrying out of the act of visitation, once a bodily oath has been received from all concerning the truth to be spoken in their examination, proceed according to the tenor of the commission delivered to you through our other letters, even by the coercion of the censures and penalties there expressed, establishing, ordering, and executing what shall be necessary there for introducing and continuing regular observance and discipline.

Take especial care that the prelates of the monasteries which you shall visit be so instructed and imbued by the ministry of this visitation of yours that, with decent habit and tonsure, with honorable life, gravity of morals, the word of doctrine, and the example of holiness, they may preside over their subjects laudably and fruitfully, and correct the excesses of their subjects according to canonical and regular institutions. Let them recall all their fugitive and absent monks, except only those absent persons who are bound to the parochial cares of annexed churches, as soon as they can, within a suitable term fixed for them by us for this purpose, to their monastery, to live there under discipline and regular observance.

[Let them obey?] abbots, priors, and provosts, and indeed one another in fraternal charity.

*That Monks Are Not to Have Private Property.*

Let no one be permitted to use the damnable private ownership of things or goods acquired in whatever manner; rather, let all things be reduced to common use.

*Concerning the Vice of Incontinence.*

Against the incontinent, both prelates and subjects, let the stricter severity of censure proceed, so that not only public vices of incontinence be punished or restrained by vengeance, but also every material or occasion be removed from the midst from which suspicion of incontinence could arise.

*Concerning the Three Essentials of Religious Life to Be Observed.*

Let the three principal essentials of religious life be most fully observed by all, so that no dispensation or contrary custom may avail anyone against them.

*That Monks Preserve Silence in These Places.*

Let silence be observed in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and in the cloister, and in the other customary places and times.

*How the Monks Are to Take Refreshment.*

Let the brothers take refreshment together under table reading, to be continued until the end of the meal. The eating of meat shall be most strictly forbidden to all at every time and place, except only in a case permitted by the Rule.

*How the Brothers Are to Sleep.*

Let all sleep together in one dormitory, so far as possibility permits, girded with cords or [girdles?]
