For it is commanded in the aforesaid third chapter, and also in the first, by virtue of holy obedience and under penalty of excommunication to be imposed by the visitors, that every abbot hand over to the visitors in writing, distinctly, the former and present state of his monastery; and that all officials render to them a faithful account of what has been received and distributed. Likewise, that inventories be made; that places not be handed over on lease, except according to the Benedictine constitution, which must also be observed in receiving spoils from benefices and in making loans and sales. And transgressors must be severely punished, even beyond the penalties contained in the aforesaid constitutions. This concerning the oratory. Concerning the remaining places it will appear below, in connection with the discipline of persons.

*Concerning the persons who dwell within monasteries. Rubric 9.*

The persons who dwell within monasteries, if they are not professed, must be not only necessary and useful, but also honorable and without suspicion. Hence all superfluous, dishonorable, and suspicious persons must be removed far from the bounds of monasteries. But those who are already actually professed, or are soon to be professed, must be considered most diligently, both according to the quantity of their number and according to the quality of their condition. For everywhere the number of monks formerly established must, if resources suffice, be preserved complete; or at least there must be no fewer than can conveniently be maintained, as above, chapter 1 and certain following chapters, through whom divine worship can be perfectly and laudably discharged. From these, sufficient and necessary officials are to be appointed according to the disposition of the Rule, namely abbot, prior, deans, cellarer, porter, infirmarian, and so with the rest; and especially some suitable men who may sufficiently instruct the young in the rudimentary sciences, according to the form of the statutes of Benedict XII, as above, chapters 1 and 3. And some from reformed houses are to be brought from elsewhere, if the monastery itself has not previously been well reformed.

Likewise, not only nobles are to be received, nor only [Scots?], but according to the Rule, both the one and the other: both the freeborn and one converted from servitude; both the poor man who has nothing at all and the rich, powerful man who has great abundance, as above in the same chapter and consequently in many others, such as 16 and 17. Nor ought anything to be exacted from them, nor may any one of them promise anything for his reception, either by himself or through others, nor otherwise promise that he will give anything, except those things which, without agreement, he himself or his parents, of free will, wishes or has wished to offer at the altar or otherwise to the monastery or to God. And those so received, when the year of probation has elapsed, are to make profession and promise stability according to the Rule, as above, chapter 2. Without this probation, moreover, no one ought to be received as an obedientiary, neither one newly coming from the world nor one arriving from another monastery, with letters or without letters, as above, chapters 8, 13, and 15, etc.

Nor henceforth should brothers be received without the permission of their superiors, nor allowed to wander; if any have hitherto been received, even by authority of the apostolic see or otherwise with sufficient permission, they are to be recalled as quickly as possible, namely within two months, and instructed according to the statutes of our order, under penalty of excommunication and suspension, as above, chapters 1 and 2; or they are even to be sent back to their own monasteries, if they were received without sufficient permission and authority, under the same penalty, as above, chapter 8. Fugitive or wandering monks must not be received without letters of commendation from their abbots or from the nearer presiding superior. Nor may they even be given hospitality except for three days, as above, chapters 8 and 12, under a similar penalty. Likewise, if any have laid or should lay violent hands upon a monk or upon an abbot, he also must not be received nor permitted to remain in the province, as above, chapter 12, under the same penalty.
