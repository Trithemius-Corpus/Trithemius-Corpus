We further decree and ordain that in every monastery of our province, immediately after the chapter has been celebrated, each prelate shall cause two offices to be celebrated conventually on suitable days: one for the living and the other for the dead, with the appropriate vigils, for the brothers and benefactors of the provincial chapter and of the order.

*Concerning the sentence of excommunication.*

We also will and decree that those excommunicated, and those henceforth to be excommunicated in provincial chapters for whatever cause, even for a contribution imposed by the presidents and not paid, shall be proclaimed in their dioceses and monasteries; and that executors shall be appointed for this by the chapter, so that they may not in any way be able to excuse themselves from such sentences and censures, or plead ignorance, until they learn to obey the commands of the presidents.

Visitors of a monastery shall make charters for the monasteries visited by them, and shall send these also to the presidents at the time of celebrating the chapter.

We further command the visitors that they leave the charters of their visitations in the monasteries visited by them, according to the ancient statutes and ordinances; and that they, or their proctors, send copies of these to the chapter itself at the proper time. In these they shall clearly inform the lord presidents concerning the state of those monasteries, in temporal and spiritual matters and in persons, so that the presidents themselves, once the defects are known, may be able to come as quickly as possible to the aid of such monasteries. But if the state and necessity of the monasteries shall demand it from the visitor, then, immediately after the visitation has been made, the visitors shall send copies of the charters to the presidents with the necessary notification.

*Concerning the expenses of monasteries attempting to sever themselves from the order.*

Further, we will and ordain that the expenses which the chapter has happened to incur for monasteries attempting to sever themselves from the order, or even for recovering those already transferred, shall be paid to the chapter by such restored monasteries, according to their own resources, which are to be taken into account.

*Concerning the debts of dead men.*

We will and ordain that in each future chapter the names of deceased brothers shall be sent by the abbots, or by their proctors, in slips to the chapter itself to be celebrated at the proper time, so that they may be commended to the prayers of the fathers.

*The twenty-sixth provincial chapter was celebrated at Saint James in Mainz, in the year of the Lord 1487.*

In the year of the Lord 1487, in the sixth indiction, on the Sunday Jubilate, the twenty-sixth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint James near Mainz. In it there presided the venerable fathers and lords abbots: Heinrich in Wissembourg, Conrad in Wiblingen, Berthamus of Saint Gotthard in Hildesheim, and Johannes in Seligenstadt.

*Concerning definitors to be chosen by the presidents.*

It pleased all of us, gathered in chapter, that henceforth, in each chapter to be celebrated, definitors shall be chosen from the fathers by the presidents.

*Concerning the age required in the person to be received for profession of the order.*

We further forbid all abbots and prelates of our chapter henceforth to receive anyone to profession of the order unless he is established at the proper age: namely, a male at fourteen complete years, and a female at twelve complete years.

*Concerning the age of brothers to be ordained.*

We also forbid all the same prelates henceforth to promote their subjects to major orders while they are minors, contrary to the disposition of common law and the ordinance of the supreme pontiffs.

*Concerning novices obliged to perform the canonical hours.*

We decree that all professed members and novices of our order, even those established in no sacred order, are obliged to say the canonical hours every day according to the ordinance of the rule.

*The twenty-seventh provincial chapter was celebrated in Nuremberg for the sixth time, in the year of the Lord 1490.*

In the year of the Lord 1490, on the Sunday Misericordia Domini, the twenty-seventh provincial chapter was celebrated at Saint Giles in Nuremberg for the sixth time. In it there presided the venerable fathers and lords abbots: Georgius of Saint Stephen in Würzburg, Johannes of Saint Giles in Nuremberg, [unclear].
