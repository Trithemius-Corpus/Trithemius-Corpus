*That abbots and presidents of monasteries are to celebrate there on solemnities. Article 28.*

We also will that the said abbots, on the principal solemnities and on others according to the manner and custom hitherto laudably observed in the said monasteries, celebrate there.

*That monks are not to live with their parents. Article 29.*

We forbid abbots to permit their religious to make a long stay and dwelling with their parents or with other laypeople, or to be entangled in the services of such persons. Rather, if one prior with at least one religious cannot live honorably and fittingly in his priory, let him be brought back to the monastery, according to the constitution of Pope Benedict XII and of the Council of Vienne, which begins *Ne in agro*.

*Concerning beneficed monks to be sent to general studies.*

But if any priors or beneficed men wish to go to study, let the greatest care be taken lest, under the appearance and shadow of study, they wish to wander about in cities. For this reason, when they are seen to be useless and neglectful of study, let them be sent back to the cloister, or to their benefice if, as has been said, they are able to live there. And the prior general of the monks in the study is to take diligent care over this; we will that such priors be diligently visited and corrected by the provincial chapter within whose bounds such a study shall be.

*Concerning the chapter of faults of monks who are studying. Article 31.*

We will that the prior of the student monks of the said order hold his chapter every fifteen days, and there correct and punish, according to the form of the rule, any student monks whenever they have scandalously gone beyond bounds in life or dress; and furthermore he must inquire diligently and appoint upright investigators from that order in the same study.

According to the form of the constitution of lord Pope Benedict XII, no prior general is to remain in the study except for one year. And we will and decree that the same prior be a formed bachelor in theology, or licensed in decrees, or a man of advanced and approved religious life. This prior is also to have authority to punish the contumacious and rebellious by bodily imprisonment, yet with the assent of four or five notable religious men living in the study, to be chosen by the religious of the said order studying there; and further, with the consent of the aforesaid men, when he sees any to be incorrigible and excessively scandalous, he may send them back to the cloister or to their priory.

Likewise we will and ordain that all religious coming to the study present themselves to the said prior within five days, and that he have them all recorded in one book; nor are they to leave the study unless the cause of their return has first been made known to the said prior. If that prior sees that the cause is unjust on the part of the abbot, then let him take care diligently to notify the visitors of the provincial chapter, so that they may provide for this as above.

*We forbid that monks go on pilgrimage. Article 32.*

And that abbots not permit their monks, under pretext of some devotion to some saint, or of indulgences to be sought in some place, to go out from their places, especially beyond the limits of their diocese, unless perhaps to visit certain regions by whose example they may be edified, or to edify them; but this rarely and with great caution. And no prior or beneficed man is to remain outside his diocese for five days unless permission has been obtained from his abbot; otherwise let the visitors of the provincial chapter punish the guilty most sharply.
