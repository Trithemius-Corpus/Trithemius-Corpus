*That those visited provide safe-conduct for their visitors.* Chapter 30.

If need be, the abbots and convents visited shall provide, at their own expense, safe-conduct for their visitors from one monastery visited to another, to be visited at the appointed visitation, as has been established in many provincial chapters.

*That visitors reveal the secrets of those visited to no one.* Chapter 31.

But let the visitors beware that they presume not to reveal to anyone, especially to secular persons, things entrusted to them or even discovered by them in visitations; nor indeed to their own brethren, nor to any familiars whatever. For what each does not wish to be done to himself, let him not do to another. For he is fraudulent who reveals secrets, 6, question 5, *Qui ambulat*. And in the constitution of Pope Benedict XII, chapter [unclear], it is said: And because it proceeds from the virtue of prudence if the way is barred to scandals, we forbid the aforesaid visitors to presume to reveal to others those persons, or their names, who have revealed any secrets to them in visitations, except only when, on account of such things revealed, it will be necessary to proceed to someone’s punishment or penalty. But otherwise let them punish those revealing, more or less as the character of the deed shall require, according to the judgment of the presidents. Thus there.

This brief form of visitation, drawn from the writings and statutes of the fathers for the direction of myself and those like me, when there shall be need, as best I could, I, brother Johannes, abbot in Sponheim of the diocese of Mainz, have copied out and humbly offer to the lords president of the provincial chapter to be examined and corrected. If they approve it to be observed in visitations, I shall not be deprived of the fruit of my labor; but if they reject it, by humbly submitting myself I shall be endowed with the reward of humility. In the year of the Lord 1490. [unclear]

*From the constitutions of Pope Benedict XII, concerning visitors.* Chapter 3.

Furthermore, the visitors who, according to the aforesaid constitution of that general council, shall have been deputed in provincial chapters of this kind, are to be suitable, diligent, and discreet in carrying out their office of visitation; and they shall observe all things that are known to have been established in the aforesaid constitutions of the same council and in the other constitutions of the said Honorius, our predecessor.

But if the visitors themselves negligently omit to carry this out, we will that they incur by the very fact those penalties which are incurred by those who do not come to the provincial chapter without having a canonical impediment.

And because we desire that the conduct of the aforesaid visitors of this kind proceed with all sincerity, we prohibit any visitor from being able to be visitor in the same year of the visitor deputed to him. And the aforesaid visitors, or their familiars, in a cathedral church, monastic church, monastery, or other place of that order or religion to which they have had to turn aside for the purpose of visitation, shall by no means remain more than two days, unless evident necessity or the great advantage of such a church, monastery, or place required a longer stay there by those visitors or their familiars. But then they shall do this with the counsel of the elders of the aforesaid church, monastery, or place.

Nor shall the visitors or their familiars be burdensome and oppressive to the same churches, monasteries, and places visited; they shall not seek sumptuous banquets, but with thanksgiving shall receive what has been honestly and fittingly served to them.

And the said visitors and their familiars shall receive no money on this occasion or in any other way; nor shall they themselves, or any of their people, presume to ask for any gift whatever it may be, or however it may be offered, so that they may seem to seek not the things that are their own, but the things of Christ.
