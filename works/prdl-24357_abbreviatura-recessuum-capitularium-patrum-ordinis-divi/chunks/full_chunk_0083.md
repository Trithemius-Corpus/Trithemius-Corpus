Then, according to the ordinance of the seventeenth chapter and of certain others, the presidents and the other officials of the future chapter are to be appointed, and also the visitors of the monasteries; likewise the one who is to deliver the sermon, and the other who is to celebrate the Mass, not indeed under their proper names but only under the names of their offices, so that, if anyone should resign or die or indeed be deposed, then the abbot of the same place would carry out the office enjoined on him; for although the person is changed, nevertheless the dignity, which does not die, always continues. Finally, action must be taken concerning those things which are to be corrected in the order, and which perhaps will have to be newly ordained and instituted for the advantage of the whole order; here the beginning must first be made with those who are present, each of whom must declare his fault in the matter and voluntarily offer himself for fitting amendment; their visitors are to accuse and proclaim them. Those, however, who at that time are proctors of the absent must undergo disciplines and other fitting penances not only for themselves but also for those who appointed them, such as the presidents themselves, according to the report of the visitors or any other certain and evident knowledge, have justly and reasonably decreed should be inflicted. Secondly, the absent are to be dealt with. And so, consequently, with the remaining business pressing upon the order. And last, each thing issued and handled is to be reduced into a certain form and inserted in the charter or recess. Nor should anyone from among secular men be present at these discussions or be admitted there, except only a public notary and honorable witnesses. But neither should these be admitted indiscriminately, but only then when the act itself is now to be reduced into public form. In that form the names of all the abbots present and of the proctors of the absent must also be written. All who are present must receive a copy of it; but their proctors must transmit it to the absent within two months, under the penalty expressed above, chapter 20.

Likewise, in the chapter itself, where great and difficult matters are discussed, the votes of each individual must be examined, and each must state his vote according to God and conscience, with humility and purity of intention, and assign the reason moving him. Moreover, how the fathers should conduct themselves there in their manner of life has been so ordained that all, both abbots and proctors of abbots, must strive after conformity of manners, especially in habit and tonsure; nor henceforth should anyone use a bare biretta, but should place the hood of the cowl over the biretta, as above, chapter 16. Likewise, during the three days for which the chapter continues, all must come together at one table, so that they may sit together and be refreshed together; there only two courses of fish, one of eggs or fried dishes, and two good pottages are to be served, with cheese or some fruits added, as above, chapter 7. The treasurers, however, must pay the expenses, which they in turn must collect from the contribution of each abbot, as above, chapter 9. Likewise, briefs must also be offered there by the abbots and the proctors of abbots, in which the names of those who have died since the next preceding chapter are contained, so that they may be absolved in the chapter, namely by way of prayer, and prayers may be made for them. The form of these briefs is as follows: Brother N., priest or Levite, died on such a day in that place, etc., as above, chapter 4. *Concerning the presidents. Third rubric.*

The presidents who are to preside in the future chapter must, according to the ordinance of the sixteenth chapter, which is drawn from the constitutions of Benedict XII, be chosen by the presidents of the preceding chapter; and, according to the ordinance of the thirteenth chapter, only from among the reformed. Furthermore, in that same future chapter over which they will preside, they are to choose, just as their predecessors did, all the officials of the chapter, the vice-presidents, visitors, etc., and to do the other things which are contained in the constitutions of the aforementioned Benedict XII.
