We will that those things that have been written and defined concerning the quality and quantity of habits, garments, and footwear in the chapters held at Constance and Mainz be observed inviolably by all.

*That monks live in common. That they sleep in one place.*

We will that the brothers live in common according to the Rule, and likewise eat in one refectory, in which no one at all is to eat meat. They are to maintain an infirmary according to the Rule and the disposition of the abbot. All private little houses also are to be destroyed at the abbot’s command, or assigned to some offices for the benefit of the monastery. But all are to sleep in one dormitory, in partitioned chambers, each in his own bed, according to the disposition of the abbot. Concerning the other statutes of the preceding chapters, repeated here.

Furthermore, the constitutions and statutes decreed in past chapters were repeated precisely, word for word, in this one, just as they are contained above: namely, concerning the divine office; concerning the ringing for matins, prime, and vespers; likewise, that monasteries and conventual places that have fallen into ruin and been destroyed are to be reformed; and that a prudent old man is to be placed at the gate of each monastery; likewise, that abbots and the other officials of monasteries, after debts and expenses have been set out, are to render to the visitors a full and faithful account; that silence also is to be kept most strictly in its four conventual places; and that, concerning loans, leases, spoils, fiefs, those to be sent to studies, and also concerning the preservation of the Eucharist and relics, the Benedictina is to be observed.

*Concerning nuns.*

Those things that have been said and are contained in the [unclear] chapter concerning monasteries of men, we will to be understood also in monasteries of nuns, as regards the articles relevant to them; and we also command them to remain under perpetual enclosure.

*Concerning the observance of the commands.*

All these our aforesaid commands, however, we will, ordain, and command to be observed inviolably by all subject to our chapter, as each is concerned. Nor by this do we restrict the statutes of our order or of preceding chapters, but we will that those likewise be diligently and firmly fulfilled and observed by all, without any contradiction. Let the visitors therefore provide that all and each of the things mentioned above be diligently observed, as they are bound to render account on the day of their death and of the Last Judgment.

*The fourth provincial chapter was held in Seligenstadt in the year of the Lord 1422.*

In the year of the Lord 1422, in the fifteenth indiction, on the third day of the month of May, in the fifth year of Pope Martin V, the fourth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter, martyrs, in Seligenstadt. In it these venerable fathers presided: Albert of Hersfeld, George of [Kastl?], and Theoderic of Amorbach, abbots of monasteries.

*Concerning the statutes of this chapter.*

Before I recount the statutes of this chapter, and before those that were published and ratified in this sacred chapter, it is pleasing to set forth briefly those things that, having been decreed in past chapters, were inserted word for word here into the record. For the repeated insertion of statutes so often serves this purpose: that the more often something has been decreed in several chapters, the greater authority it may possess.

First, this sacred assembly of fathers repeated the statute of the first chapter concerning deceased presidents: that one succeeding someone in an abbacy also succeeds in the presidency.

Likewise, that visitors are not to bring with them more than six mounts, under the penalty contained above.

Likewise, concerning the ringing for matins, prime, and vespers, to be done for the space of one quarter of an hour, as above, chapter 1.

And that the chapter of faults is to be observed daily, as in the same place.
