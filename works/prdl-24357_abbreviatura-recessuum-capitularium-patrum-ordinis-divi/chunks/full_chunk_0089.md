Eighth, how they conduct themselves in going out of the monastery: whether, namely, with permission, and whose permission; whether with a companion, and of what kind; with how many horses and servants, and of what sort; whether with a blessing, both when going out and when coming in; whether also in suitable habit and with books of hours, and so concerning the other matters of which [there is mention] above, chapter 1, and below in the following rubric. Ninth, concerning individual persons, a separate consideration and inquiry must be made.

First concerning the abbot: whether before his election he was reformed, or at least had remained for a year in the reform; whether afterward he continued the reform; whether he attended the chapter and paid the contribution; whether he renders an account every year, and handed over in writing the state of his monastery to the former visitors; and whether he obeyed them in all their ordinances. Whether he alienated, pawned, or handed over to friends the goods of the monastery. How he confers benefices. How he appoints offices: whether with counsel or according to his own opinion. Whether he has priories, provostships, or churches annexed to the monastery. How he confers them, and on whom. Likewise whether each year he receives an account from them and holds a chapter. Whether he frequents the choir or gives himself to hunting; and so concerning the other matters which are noted above in various places about the abbot, and which will still be touched on below in certain rubrics. And the same is also to be done likewise with the prior, cellarer, custodian, vestiarian, and the remaining officials, with each according to the requirement of his office. Likewise with the porter: whether he is old and mature, going with the others to the refectory, dormitory, etc.; likewise whether they allow women to enter or to live inside; whether the brothers frequently go out; and so concerning the other matters. Concerning all these things there is clearer treatment in the decrees of the first and third chapters, and also still of other chapters, and in the manner of visitation, folio 13, as well as in the bull of lord Nicholas, the legate often mentioned, whose points are given below in the last rubric.

*On the propriety of external habit. From chapter 1. Rubric II.*

We ordain and decree that all should wear large and broad tonsures, so that their circle does not exceed the space of two transverse fingers; and that they should conduct themselves fittingly in clothing, habits, and footwear, according to the Clementine *Ne in agro* and the constitution of the aforesaid Pope Benedict, *On the form and propriety of garments*: namely, that they should wear tunics and garments next beneath the outer habit, of black, brown, or white color, according to the custom usual among them; round, reaching to the ankles, not slit, nor marked by any other deformity, having wide sleeves extended as far as the fist, not buttoned or knotted in any way, yet not so wide that their width above the fist can be stretched out with the thumb raised. Nor should they extend beyond the elbow, as the abuse of certain men has it, more than so far that the arm can easily be turned and drawn inside. Let them be content with almuces of skins in place of cowls. Yet in suitable places where they have been accustomed to wear them, they may, by permission of the abbot, use honorable cowls, slit above the shoulders to the length of at least four transverse fingers. In their garments they should not wear sendal for linings, or miniver, or other costly material, but only skins of lambs or young lambs for the said linings; nor should the linings appear in any way above the neck or at the edges of the garments or sleeves. Furthermore, when it happens that they travel outside the monastery, they should wear a frock, cuculla, or cloak open on either side; and the conversi should use a scapular with a cowl attached to it, inside and outside the monastery.
