*On the Power and Authority of Visitors. Chapter 7.*

They have it from the Apostolic See, as in Extra, De statu monachorum, chapter *In singulis*, where it is said: “Let religious and circumspect persons also be appointed in the same chapter, who, in our stead, shall take care to visit the several abbeys of that kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, according to the form prescribed to them.” “In our stead,” he says, so that, while authority is weighed in the visitor, none of those being visited may dare to rebel. Their authority is more fully noted by the abbot above, in the same chapter, and in the final chapter of De statu monachorum. Nevertheless their power is limited, as in the chapter *In singulis*, because they cannot depose the rector of a place from his office without a special mandate.

For if they see that he must be removed altogether, let them denounce him to his own bishop, so that he may see to removing him; but if he does not do so, let the visitors refer this to the judgment of the Apostolic See. Exempt persons, however, are to be denounced immediately to the lord pope. Visitors should also have monks corrected by the abbot of the place, as in the same place, chapter *Ea quae*. But those whom they find contumacious and rebellious, according to the measure of the fault, let them strike with regular censure in the stead of the lord pope, without respect of persons, not sparing rebels on account of their obstinacy or the power of their friends, but rather casting the diseased sheep out of the sheepfold lest it infect the healthy sheep. Nor is there to be appeal from correction: De officio ordinarii, chapter *Licet*; and De appellatione, *Ad nostram*; and chapter *Reprehensibilis*; and chapter *Cum speciali*. Understand this unless the one correcting should exceed the measure: 23, question 4, *Qui peccat*.

Visitors, therefore, according to the decree of Honorius III in chapter *Ea quae*, if they find monks offending, should command the abbots of the places to correct them, as in the aforesaid constitution it says in these words: “But if abbots, in correcting according to the command of the visitors and the regular institutes, are found negligent in themselves or in their monks, let them be proclaimed and corrected, and let them be punished publicly in the general chapter in such a way that their penalty may be an example to others.” And note according to what is said there: a visitation of this sort ought to be made plainly and without the noise of judgments: De accusationibus, *Olim*. For regulars are easily removed from their administrations: De simonia, chapter *Per tuas*. And therefore the judicial order is not in every respect to be observed in inquiries of this sort. Let them inquire diligently, and let them correct and reform, both in spiritual and in temporal matters, the things which they see must be corrected.

*But it is asked whether the bishop of the place can visit the monasteries of his diocese. It is answered that he can, as in the chapter [unclear], *In singulis*, section *Porro*.*

He can visit them, but he ought not to bring secular persons in with him, as Hostiensis notes there in the gloss. For the visitation which the visitors make does not take away the bishop’s own jurisdiction, as is noted in the same chapter *In singulis*. Custom, however, has otherwise; for bishops also very often commit their own turns to abbots.
