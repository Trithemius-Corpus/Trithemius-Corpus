And he may add general matters reported or observed, for admonition and correction. Then the lay brothers, novices, and sub-priests, that is, those not yet constituted in sacred orders, are to be called to the mat, one after another, or two by two; or certainly any concerning whom such a matter has been reported that they deservedly ought to be accused. When the one called comes to the mat, let him bow moderately; and let the visitor tell him his faults; and he shall at once lie prostrate on the ground until he is ordered to rise.

When those who do not belong to chapter have been dealt with and have gone out, first the abbot is to be called to the mat, then the prior and the others according to order. But while the abbot stands in judgment before the visitors, all the other brothers ought, out of reverence, to stand uncovered, until he is ordered to sit again. And note that here the abbot’s faults are to be told briefly and the lesser ones, lest occasion for contempt be given to the weak. But in private, or before a few of the seniors, all things necessary are to be told him, great along with small, etc.

Penances are to be imposed at the visitors’ discretion, with discernment, according to the manner of daily accusation. But if anyone there comes to be punished more severely, let the visitors have this done through the abbot, as in Extra, De statu monachorum, Ea quae.

*On the final exhortation to be made. Chapter 25.*

After all who are to be accused have been accused, and penance has been enjoined on each, let the visitor make a brief exhortation concerning regular observance, rebuking the general negligences of the brothers; and let him explain how such things are contrary to the Rule and harmful to the soul, etc., and the other things in this manner, as shall seem expedient to them. At this exhortation, moreover, the other professed monks and lay brothers not belonging to chapter, who had gone out, may be recalled and be present, unless something else prevents it.

*On the customary absolution. Chapter 26.*

When these things have been completed according to custom, let all humble themselves for absolution, bending their knees, and say the Confiteor, concluding: Pray for me. *Let the visitor, standing, say:*

May almighty God have mercy on you, through the intercession of blessed Mary and all the saints, and forgive you all your sins, and bring you to eternal life. Amen.

By the authority of almighty God, and of the ecclesiastical keys granted in this matter to our order, we absolve you from ecclesiastical censures, and restore you to the communion of the Church and to participation; and we dispense with you and rehabilitate you for the execution of the things to be done. In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. And let some psalms to be said be enjoined on each; *on the lay brothers, the Lord’s Prayer twice or three times.*

When these things have been completed, the visitors may excuse their insufficiency before those visited, and commend themselves to their prayers, as is often done in these or similar words:

Dearest brothers, we ask that the things we have said or done here to all, or also in private to individuals, you receive charitably, and that you think nothing else of us than that we have done everything in charity. Also, I ask you to forgive me for the things said above by me; and if I have offended anyone by words, or if I did not speak as I ought, or otherwise than I ought, impute it to my lack of skill, not to my will, etc.; such things. *And* let him add, if there is time: Our help is in the name of the Lord. Who made heaven and earth.
