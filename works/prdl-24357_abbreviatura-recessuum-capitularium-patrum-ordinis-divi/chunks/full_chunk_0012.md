And let him add:

We also do not doubt that you know that, in the order of visitation, three things are chiefly to be done. First, the visitors themselves ought to present themselves to those to be visited in the chapter-house, and to make known their authority to visit, and to put first a prayer according to the custom of the order, and an exhortation according to the character of the time and of the persons; on the state of monks, *Attendentes*, in the Clementines, and in the constitutions, chapter [unclear], and in [unclear]. Secondly, there ought to be a diligent examination of those to be visited, and an inquiry into their morals and the state of each; 10, question 1, *Episcopum*, and question 3, *Cavendum*; on the state of monks, chapter *Eaque*. Thirdly, the final conclusion and the due correction of offenders, chapter *In singulis*.

Since, therefore, even in the least matters divine aid must always be implored, and, as blessed Dionysius says in the fourth chapter of *On the Divine Names*, in spiritual matters one must above all begin from prayer, it is fitting that we also observe the commands of such teaching and not pass over the praiseworthy custom of the order. Let us therefore pray.

*How necessary matters are to be deposed. Chapter 20.*

When the exhortation has been finished, let the visitor next admonish those to be visited how and in what manner they ought to conduct themselves in their depositions, and let him say thus in order:

Beloved brothers, although, having often been visited, you are not ignorant of the form of the visitation itself, nevertheless, on account of those who have more rarely been present at visitation, I shall briefly touch upon the manner and form in which you ought to conduct yourselves in depositions. First, we exhort you all and each one of you that, just as visitation was instituted for this purpose, that vices may be rooted out and virtues implanted, so also each one in making proclamations should direct his intention to the proper end. Likewise, each one should make deposition out of charity, if he has seen anything that must necessarily be amended, and not out of hatred, which God forbid, nor out of aversion, because this would be a grave sin.
