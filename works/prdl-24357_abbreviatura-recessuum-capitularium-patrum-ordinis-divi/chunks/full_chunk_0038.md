Yet in respectable places, in which they have been accustomed to wear them, they may, with the abbot's permission, use honest hoods, slit over the shoulders to a length of at least four transverse fingers. In their garments let them not wear silk-stuff for linings, nor minever or other costly furs; but let them use the skins of young lambs or lambkins for the said linings, or at least other non-costly cloths. Nor let the linings appear above the collar, or on the hems of garments or of sleeves, in any way.

*Of the habit of those who travel. Article 6.* Moreover, when it befalls them to set out beyond the monastery, let them wear the floccus or the cowl or the cappa; and beneath the cappa, if they have worn it, let them have a cowl or a scapular.

*Of the habit of novices and lay brothers. Article 7.* Let novices use the cowl, open inside the monastery and outside on either side. But let the lay brothers use the scapular with a hood attached to it, closed, inside and outside the monastery.

*Of the length and width of the garments. Article 8.* Since some use the aforesaid habits more ample and long, and others less than they ought, on account of the length and width of the aforesaid [garments] we have determined that the following be inserted in the present [statutes], which we wish to be commonly observed. And first, beginning with the hood of the floccus: we ordain that its width not reach the elbow-bone, by the thickness of two transverse fingers, nor be allowed to be tighter, or so short, but that it extend to the middle of the elbow and the upper bone near the shoulder; and behind let it extend at least as far as the belt, nor beyond it by four fingers. And let the length of the sleeves extend by one cubit beyond the hand and no more; and let their width not exceed another cubit and a half. Yet let the floccus below be made so wide that the step may easily be made

through it. And what has been said of the floccus we wish to be understood of the cowl with sleeves, when they have none set apart. But within the monastery, in places where there have been twelve monks, let all use the floccus or large cowls. In other places, where there has not been so great a congregation, let them use cowls, not flocci—except the president of the monastery and the second after him, whom we wish to wear the floccus in every case. Yet in [divine] works and in the divine office let them be able to wear, beneath the alb, and when riding, beneath the cappa, a scapular of a width of at least half a cubit. And let them wear high boots or boots or strapped shoes, such that their width comprehends a space of four fingers, [reaching] from the shin over the ankle. For if in any of the aforesaid matters they shall not have worn the habits (as is set forth above), then, beyond the penalties inflicted by law, we wish them to be so punished—by their superiors in their religious house, or by the superior if they have none, [or] by the visitors of the provincial chapter—that it may serve as an example to the rest.

*That the monks live in common in the refectory without the eating of meat. Article 9.* Item, that the brothers live and eat in common in the refectory, in which let no one at all eat meat. And let the visitors be diligent to cause the constitution of Benedict concerning the eating of and abstinence from meats to be observed inviolably.

*Of the table-reading of the brothers. Article 10.* Let the sacred reading not be lacking; and let him who is to read for the whole week enter upon it on Sunday after the blessing, as [is laid down] in the Rule, chapter 38, concerning the weekly reader.

*How the monks are to sleep. Article 11.* Let the monks sleep individually, clothed, in individual beds in the same place if it can be done, or at least in tens or twenties; but not in [separate] chambers, especially if these have not been suitably partitioned—so that the bedding in them may be seen aptly and clearly, according to the manner of their way of life
