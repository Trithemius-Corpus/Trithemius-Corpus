XIII

First, let them inquire whether perhaps the abbot has presumed any such thing against the visitation. And if they find any such things, let them release them, as in Extra, De accusationibus, c. Olim.

*Concerning general matters, the things written below, or at least some of them, may be inquired into.*

First let them ask about general matters as seems good to them.

Whether the abbot extorted an oath from the monks about keeping silent the truth, or otherwise forbade them to report anything.

Likewise, whether all are professed, and how.

Likewise, whether any have been received simoniacally. And if so, what has been done with the simoniacal goods.

Likewise, whether they observe true obedience to the abbot and to his superiors according to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

Likewise, whether any or all are proprietors.

Likewise, whether any are fornicators, and who.

Likewise, whether they are chaste, as befits religious.

Likewise, whether, when healthy, they eat meat; whether all do so or some; where, when, and how often.

Likewise, whether they observe silence in the four forbidden places, and whether they do the same at night-time or not.

Likewise, whether they observe the fasts of the Church and of the Rule as they are bound to do.

Likewise, whether there is always reading at table for those eating.

Likewise, whether all eat together in one refectory.

Likewise, whether all sleep together in one place, and whether they are clothed and girded according to the Rule.

Likewise, whether they use linen garments or linen sheets.

Likewise, whether they sleep on feathers or mats.

Likewise, inquiry can be made and questions asked about their habit and tonsure.

Likewise, whether they continually wear the monastic habit.

Likewise, whether they run about outside the enclosures of the monastery without permission of their superiors.

Likewise, let inquiry be made about the state of temporal goods, what it is like, and how each item is spent.

These and similar matters may be inquired into in general, as shall seem expedient to discreet visitors.

*Inquiries about the divine office should also be made thus or similarly.*

Likewise, first, at what time they rise for matins.

Likewise, whether they sing matins and all the other hours, or some.

Likewise, at what time they rise for prime.

Likewise, at what time they perform the other hours.

Likewise, whether they discharge the hours slowly and religiously, as is fitting.

Likewise, how often the chapter of faults is held in the week.

Likewise, how faults are examined there.

Likewise, whether all diligently come together for the work of God.

Likewise, whether consecrated vestments and ecclesiastical ornaments, books, chalices, and other things are handled cleanly.

Likewise, how many masses are celebrated daily.

Likewise, whether they sing high mass every day.

Likewise, how often the brothers celebrate in the week.

Likewise, whether they always confess before mass.

Likewise, whether the Rule is read to them and expounded according to the statutes of the fathers.

And many other things in this manner.

*Questions concerning abbots, provosts, or priors*

if they are the supreme rulers of monasteries. Greater diligence must be applied in inquiring about abbots and their manner of life, since the state of monasteries is often led in either direction by their merit. And many things indeed occur that should be inquired into; but, with a few and the greater matters marked out, let the visitors themselves investigate the rest.

First, therefore, let inquiry be made whether he also has zeal for God and for souls, as befits a shepherd.

Likewise, whether he diligently visits the choir and the divine and conventual places.

Likewise, let him ask about the state of temporal goods, what it is like, and how each item is spent. These and similar matters may be inquired into in general, as shall seem expedient to discreet visitors.

*Inquiries about the divine office also [unclear] to be made for him.*

Likewise, first, at what time they rise for matins. Likewise, whether they sing matins and all the other hours, or some. Likewise, at what time they rise for prime. Likewise, at what time they perform the other hours. Likewise, whether they discharge the hours slowly and religiously, as is fitting. Likewise, how often the chapter of faults is held in the week. Likewise, how faults are examined there. Likewise, whether all diligently come together for the work of God. if they are the supreme rulers of monasteries. Likewise, first, therefore, let inquiry be made whether he also has zeal for God and for souls, as befits a shepherd. Likewise, whether he diligently visits the choir and the divine and conventual places.
