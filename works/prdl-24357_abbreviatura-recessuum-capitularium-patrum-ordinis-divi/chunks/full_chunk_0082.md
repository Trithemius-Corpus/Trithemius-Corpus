*A brief and summary recapitulation, or epilogue, of all the foregoing. In what order and manner individuals must come together for the provincial chapter.*

*First rubric.*

When some chapter is to be celebrated, those who are officials for the time being, namely the presidents, visitors, treasurers, and the one making the sermon there, according to the ordinance of the seventeenth chapter, and certain others, must arrive four days before the chapter itself, so that, namely, they may be present there on Wednesday and may more conveniently inform themselves, both concerning things done and concerning things to be done in their offices, and finally may be able to dispatch matters more quickly. This must be understood concerning the presidents of either chapter, namely [unclear], as appears above, chapter 20. As for the one making the sermon, this has been ordained: namely, that his sermon first be examined, so that, if there were need, it might be corrected and amended. The rest, whether abbots or proctors, must present themselves there, so that when the chapter is to be begun no one may be found to be absent. Nor is the excuse of anyone to be admitted, except either of one who is ill, or has mortal enmities, or certainly is so poor that he cannot at all conveniently present himself to the chapter without danger. Hence no proctor for any absent person without such true and manifest impediments should ever be admitted.

Likewise, no one ought to procure letters from princes or nobles concerning his absence, under penalty of three florins. If, however, any of the officials is absent, the rest will nevertheless be able to carry on the proceedings according to the ordinance of chapter 20. But in the case that none of the presidents is present, the others assembled for the celebration may, on that occasion, depute other suitable presidents by the indult of Pope Martin V, whose bull is found among the acts of the first chapter. By the same indult those presidents may also change the place of the chapter, with prorogation of the term, for causes. Likewise, no one there, whether he is an official or not, ought to depart from there before the end of the chapter without the permission of the presidents, under penalty, as above, chapters 3 and 4, and of excommunication to be imposed, as above, chapter 20. Also, no one coming together for the chapter ought to have more than six mounts; and if anyone does the contrary, he must put back into the chest, for the common use of the chapter, within a month, as much as he is proved to have spent on the rest, as above, chapter 2. And if he has not observed even this, at the next future chapter he must be fined double by the presidents, without any remission. Likewise, each must contribute there three Rhenish florins, which they must pay either then or within the three days during which the visitors happen to be with them, without further delay, under penalty of excommunication; the visitors are most strictly ordered to impose this on them immediately once the term has elapsed, according to [unclear], as above, chapter 1.

*How the chapter is to be celebrated and how the [fathers?] ought to conduct themselves in it. Second rubric.*

If, therefore, the time is at hand for the chapter to be celebrated, which has been established always to be celebrated every three years unless, for a certain and reasonable cause, it is prorogued longer by the presidents, first, after the mass and sermon have been held, and after the [fathers?] have been gathered together in one place in the chapter-room, and after the Benedicite has been said, following the reading of the martyrology and a passage from the text of the Rule recited by the reader, a declaration must be made concerning the non-admission of excommunicated persons, as above, chapter 20.
