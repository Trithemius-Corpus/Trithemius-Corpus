*After dinner on Monday.*

If the matters written above can be dispatched before dinner, well. If not, let them complete them in the vacant time, as shall seem opportune to all the presidents. Likewise let the legate and the last chapter of the recess be read; and if any commissions have been made to anyone, let their executions be examined. Then, if nothing remains, let the fathers be dismissed to their lodgings, to return tomorrow at the sixth hour at the ringing of the bell.

*The Third Day.*

On the third day in the morning let the bell be rung, and let the Mass of the Blessed Virgin Mary be sung solemnly by one of the fathers appointed to it on the preceding evening, at which all the others shall be present. When it is finished, let them go to the chapter, and let the customary prayer be made, *Veni sancte spiritus*, etc. And let one of the presidents say, *Benedicite*. And when the response is made, *Dominus*, let him continue if anything was omitted yesterday. Likewise afterward let attention be turned to the order; and if anything new is to be established, let it be inserted this day in the recess. Likewise, if any replies are to be given or written to princes or to others, let them be made and dispatched by the counsel of the fathers. If the treasurers are not present, let some be appointed who shall collect the expenses, namely three Rhenish florins from each abbot, this day, recording what has been collected on a schedule, lest perhaps anyone, which God forbid, pretend that he has given what he has not given. Likewise let some be deputed as assistance to the notary, who know how to arrange the recess, so that by evening after dinner it may be completed. Likewise, if the necessity of the order requires it, let a proportional contribution be imposed on all the monasteries, and let the cause and form be inserted in the recess, which all shall be bound to pay under penalties to be expressed in the same, according to the constitutions of Pope Benedict XII. And it is to be noted that the lord presidents, with the definitors, can impose a contribution on others according to the Benedictine constitution without their consent; yet they themselves are not free from this sort of contribution. For what they impose on others, they themselves ought first to bear. That it is theirs to impose a contribution is sufficiently shown in the same constitutions, in these words in chapter five:

“We establish and ordain that in the same chapters, notwithstanding any contradiction or absence whatsoever, for liberties, necessities, utilities, common expenses, and other customary things, since a burden divided into parts is borne more lightly, collections, subsidies, or contributions are to be made, imposed, and proclaimed by apostolic authority, according to the judgment of the presidents or definitors, as regards a provincial chapter of this kind.” Meanwhile also, if it shall seem good to the lord presidents, let the common expenses of the chapter be assessed with the father of the house, as is usually done.

*After dinner.*

When dinner has been completed, at the signal of the bell let them at once assemble as above; and when the president has said *Benedicite*, and the response *Dominus* has been made, let the chapter be continued. Then, at the command of the presidents, let the recess be read, which should already be completed. At its end there should be contained the place and day of the future chapter, and the other things which customarily ought to be done.
