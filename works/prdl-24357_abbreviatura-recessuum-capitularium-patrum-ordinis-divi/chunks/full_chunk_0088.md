*Concerning the Discipline of Persons within Monasteries*

*The discipline of monks, both those residing there. Rubric 10.*

According to the Rule, and also according to the statutes of the fathers, it must be weighed and considered in many respects. First, however, concerning the outward aspects of a man’s life, namely tonsure and clothing. And by whom garments are supplied to the brothers: whether by a common keeper of clothing, or whether they have them through friends or from some private source. And whether provision is made for each equally, or differently without infirmity or another reasonable cause. Likewise, the garments should be necessary according to the stature prescribed by the fathers only, namely, and religious both in material and in form.

Second, concerning food and fasting. Whether all, except the sick, take refreshment in the refectory without eating meat. Whether there is a reader at table and ministers, who, according to the Rule, enter on Sunday with a blessing. For it ought not to be allowed that each person, or any from among the individuals, have special youths or servants as ministers, as it is known to be done in certain monasteries. Nor ought anyone to arrange anything for himself by himself, by his labors, through friends, or through any others whatsoever; or to receive anything, even not so arranged but offered freely, without permission, but to hope for all things from the father of the monastery. And if the eating of meat is at times granted to some for a reason, this ought to be done only on fixed days and in the infirmary alone, where no layperson ought to be admitted. But on the road, or outside the monastery, no healthy person ought to eat meat, just as not within the monastery. Likewise, whether alms are given from the surplus. And whether the ministers perform their washings on Saturday at the mandatum. Likewise concerning fasting: whether he duly observes the fast both ecclesiastical and regular; when and how and with whom dispensation is made concerning a later meal; when they use oil, and when eggs or butter; how in Advent, how in Lent.

Third, concerning nocturnal rest and silence. Namely, whether all sleep together in one dormitory, girded and clothed. Whether each one is in a separate bed, or two in one, which ought not to happen. Whether seniors are mixed with juniors. Whether the dormitory is locked, and when. Whether the prior has the keys to all the cells. Whether they sleep in woolen garments. Whether the cells are sufficiently barred, as they ought to be under penalty of a sentence of excommunication already incurred. Whether the beds are frequently inspected because of private property. Whether silence is observed in the dormitory, oratory, cloister, refectory after compline, and before chapter, and at other times and places, in the manner and form just as the Rule has concerning this.

Fourth, concerning subjects: whether they are obedient. Whether there are proprietaries, possessing anything at all from offerings, masses, labors, offices, or any other sources whatsoever. Whether there is a report concerning anyone in the matter of continence.

Fifth, concerning shaving and the bath: when and how, and with what discipline, they have them. And concerning the sick, how they are provided for. Whether they have an infirmarian and cellarer who are solicitous and fear God. Likewise also the rest in the community, how, namely, and whether they themselves are provided for. Whether they have suitable comforts at their proper time. And whether the young are instructed by sufficient masters in morals and ceremonies, and likewise in the first elements of knowledge; and by whom, whether by monks or seculars; and whether any are in a place of study.

Sixth, how they conduct themselves toward one another: whether humbly and charitably. And whether, after a disturbance has arisen, they seek reconciliation according to the Rule.

Seventh, how they conduct themselves toward others and outsiders: whether perhaps they are too familiar with them, or whether on account of them, or even on account of relatives, friends, or parents, it happens that they frequently leave the monastery.
