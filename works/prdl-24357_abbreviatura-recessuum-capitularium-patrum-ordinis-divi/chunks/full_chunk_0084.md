They also have and ought personally to visit, with the visitors, even exempt and royal monasteries. Likewise, from Martin V they have power, for certain reasons, to change the place appointed for holding the chapter, even with prorogation of the term. The same pope aforesaid also grants that if at any time someone elected as president is unwilling to accept the office, or cannot conveniently be present, then the other presidents, with the consent of the greater part of those making up the chapter, or, if none of the presidents should be present, the others assembling for the celebration of the chapter, may elect and appoint other suitable persons as presidents for those same occasions.

*Concerning the chest and the guardians of the chest. Fourth rubric.*

The common chest is always known to be necessary because of the usefulness of the chapter, so that in it may be received and placed both monetary contributions and penalties for contumacy or any other fault whatsoever of abbots, and also the other things which, either through the visitors or through the treasurers, pertain to the necessity or usefulness of the chapter. But it is also necessary that this be kept in a safe and suitable place under trustworthy guardians, who are called treasurers, having full power to exact penalties from those who are liable to penalties, under like censures, and to absolve if it should perhaps happen that any have been excommunicated on account of the aforesaid matters. Therefore in the fifth chapter the monastery of Saint Giles in Nuremberg was appointed as the place where today the chest is stored; and thence treasurers were designated, having the authority already expressed above. They must also provide for the meal during the three days in the provincial chapter, as above, chapter 9. And they must assemble at the chapter itself according to the ordinance expressed above in the first rubric.

*Concerning proctors. Fifth rubric.*

There are two kinds of proctors. Some are general, who belong to the whole chapter; and it falls to them to urge the visitors to carry out the office enjoined upon them vigorously and faithfully; indeed, also to compel them by censures, if there is need, and to report their negligence to the presidents, as above, chapter 13 and in certain following chapters. Others are special, who are substituted for certain abbots who cannot come to the chapter. Concerning these, and concerning the abbots substituting them, enough has been said above in chapter 20 and in very many other foregoing chapters, namely 4, 6, 11, 13, 17, and 18: how they are not to receive the causes of more than three abbots, excepting the causes expressed there; and that they are not to be admitted without a bodily oath effectively taken, and so concerning many other things which can be seen there. The form of the proxy, too, for the sake of the uninstructed, has been inserted in the recess of the nineteenth chapter. And it is as follows. To the reverend fathers in Christ, the lord presidents of the sacred provincial chapter of the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg of the black monks of the Order of Saint Benedict, to be celebrated in the monastery N. of the diocese N. on the coming Sunday Jubilate, N., humble abbot of the monastery N. of the Order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz: whatever reverence and honor he can.
