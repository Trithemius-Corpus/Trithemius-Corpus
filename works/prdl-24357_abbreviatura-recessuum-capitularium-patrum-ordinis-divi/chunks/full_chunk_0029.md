And you, lord scribe, I require with due urgency that you note this act and the following ones diligently, and enter them among the acts of the chapter, with trustworthy witnesses summoned for this. Immediately the notary shall require witnesses, of whom two or three are to be present at all acts, except at the final proclamation of the fathers. Likewise the promoter of the order shall say, in the hearing of all:

By the authority of the lord presidents it is commanded to all and each, both those already present here and those arriving at any hour, in virtue of holy obedience, that no one in any way reveal the secrets of this provincial chapter to any secular person, or to anyone not subject to our aforesaid chapter, to the detriment, disgrace, or burden of the order and its professed members. By the same authority likewise it is commanded to all and each that no one presume to speak elsewhere in the capitular places and proceedings to the lord presidents or to the officials of the chapter deputed and to be deputed, unless leave has been sought and obtained, and this while standing and with head uncovered, with humility, reverence, and subjection; or, if he wishes, let him present it in writing. Likewise, by the same authority it is enjoined on all and each who are bound to be present at this chapter that no one depart from the place of the chapter without leave, but remain until its end, under the penalty of contumacy and of those not coming to chapter.

Then the register of monasteries shall be read by the promoter or the notary, and the absentees shall be marked with a pen by one of them, as is customary. When these have been marked, the promoter shall accuse the contumacy of the absentees in these or similar words, saying:

Reverend fathers, lord presidents, I accuse the contumacy of the absent prelates who by law are bound to be present at this sacred provincial chapter, and I ask that a monition be decreed, to be executed at the doors of this monastery against the same contumacious men and against those rebellious toward the visitors, so that they may appear to show that they have obeyed the mandates of the chapter and have actually presented themselves to it; otherwise, to see themselves declared to have incurred the penalties of [unclear]. Let one of the presidents see to this.

We presidents, wishing to act more graciously and not to condemn anyone quickly, shall wait for the absentees until the following day, and, if necessary, longer, so that meanwhile the proxies of the absentees may also be examined: whether their absence ought deservedly to be excused, and whether the proctors themselves ought to be considered present. In addition, we decree a monition against the rebellious.

After this, if there is time, let them choose definitors from the abbots, who can give counsel and aid to the presidents in the business of the chapter. Likewise let them depute three or four from the same abbots to examine the proxies of the absentees after dinner, or at a fixed time, in an appointed place to which the proctors are to come, with this provision: that if any proxies are considered doubtful, they shall refer the examination to the lord presidents. The form of a sufficient proxy is expressly contained in the acts of the chapter.

Then the president may give advance warning to the visitors that, at a fixed time or hour which they shall determine, they be ready to present to the lord presidents the accounts and summaries of their visitations, according to the constitutions of the preceding fathers. And if the hour is late and nothing difficult now remains, they shall give leave to go to table, with one of the presidents saying in these or similar words:

Venerable fathers, since the hour has now become rather late and it is time for dinner, lest we delay you, weary as you are, any longer from refreshment, we now wish to put an end to these proceedings. Therefore let each man take his place at table according to the promoter’s ordering, with discipline; in silence and with thanksgiving let him eat what has been set before him.

The promoter of the order shall place the fathers in the refectory according to the date of each one’s prelacy, so far as can be done. And first the abbots shall sit, then the priors and provosts, thereafter the proctors of the absentees, and afterward the other simple brothers.
