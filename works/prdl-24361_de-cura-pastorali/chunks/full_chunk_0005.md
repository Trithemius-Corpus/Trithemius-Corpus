What can the rude and unlearned do in these matters, where even the most learned confess themselves insufficient? The experienced physician trembles when he sees the disease: what will the inexperienced man do? But O times, O morals: today the rude and inexperienced come to the care of souls; the sick are appointed physicians of the sick; and while they desire to cure others without knowledge, they kill both themselves and them alike. O shepherds, O abbots of this time, who understand nothing of the holy Scriptures, who do not know the art of curing souls, who scorn to be taught by others. They present themselves as lords of their brothers, not fathers; as killers of souls, not shepherds; and, since through ignorance they do not know how to discern the character of the disease, they apply not remedies but deadly poisons. They do not know how to bestow on their subjects the word of holy exhortation; they blush to console those who are tempted; they have always learned to command, never to ask. They preach nothing about the formation of morals to the sheep entrusted to them; they announce nothing of the future joys of the good; they care absolutely nothing for the salvation of souls. The faults of offenders they either perniciously conceal, or amend indiscreetly and without charity. Others command with harshness toward their subjects and drive the faint-hearted to impatience and rebellion; since they do not know how to apply the moderation of discretion, they permit those whom they allow to go through depraved morals into destruction. Others, having zeal for discipline, are so wrapped in the shadows of ignorance that they do not at all know how they ought to exercise that very zeal for the salvation of souls; and while they desire to amend the morals of offenders, through indiscreet correction they corrupt the observance of discipline. Others, not knowing the differences of temperaments, determine to approach all with one kind of exhortation; and since they neglect to adapt their speech to persons, they bring either no usefulness or little. Others, while they are eager to bring back to peace the souls of their subjects, moved by any displeasures whatsoever, through imprudence render them more disturbed.
