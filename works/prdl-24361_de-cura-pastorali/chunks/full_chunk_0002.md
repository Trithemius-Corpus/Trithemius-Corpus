For of all tasks the most difficult and the most dangerous is the keeping of souls, which is to be entrusted not to boys, not to the untrained, not to the carnal, but to prudent and spiritual men. For it is no small matter to govern souls: to preserve in monastic unity monks who are, moreover, diverse in affection and will; also to bear patiently the infirmities of all and to be supported by no one; to come to the aid of the prudent and the imprudent alike; and, what is most difficult, to conform oneself wholly to the habits of all without giving offense. Again, to transfer, so to speak, the temptations of all one’s subjects into one’s own person with pastoral solicitude, and to apply remedies suitably to each; to know how to rebuke offenders at the proper time; also to know when sometimes prudently to pass over matters in silence: this is not the work of the untrained, not of boys, but of the prudent, the devout, and men experienced in many things.

But if the government of souls is entrusted to one who is untrained, carnal, and inexperienced, monastic discipline is quickly dissipated; for when a vicious man rebukes vices, he does not correct the weak, but rather kills them by his bad example. Therefore pastors of souls must be admonished that, considering their own danger, they should take careful heed; they should feed the sheep committed to them with a threefold nourishment, so that those whom they build up by the word of holy preaching they may also precede by good conduct, and that for those over whom they preside by the authority of governance they may also supply the necessities of life. But before we extend the discourse begun to pastors of souls, let us renew the praiseworthy and altogether necessary custom of antiquity; and, since we are not sufficient of ourselves to say anything good, let us take care to obtain from the Most High God, by prayers, the power of speaking well.

If any of you, fathers, wonders that, contrary to the custom of those who have spoken before me in general chapters, I am going to deliver a plain, clear speech, free of allegorical citations, let him attend to this practice of the ancients and to my intention, and he will not only not wonder, but will also understand, for a most just reason, that the thing at which he wondered before was done rightly. I adhere to the footsteps of antiquity not without cause, since I am not unaware that the holy fathers and all the most learned men used this manner of speaking and writing. For what profit is there in bringing forward testimonies of Scripture in manifest matters, and in tearing a speech apart with superfluous allegorical citations? And so the procedure of the ancients pleases me: they produced sound, whole, and continuous speeches; they spoke by their own spirit rather than by another’s. While I adhere to their examples, no detractions of the unskilled concern me. This manner of speaking of the present time belongs to the schools, not to an assembly of prelates; but this manner of speaking is more suited to persuasion, which curiosity has not corrupted. For citation interrupts speech, confuses the sense and the sentences, and takes away the fruit of the sermon heard. And indeed to cite the Scriptures before learned men is superfluous; before the unlearned, faulty. For the former do not desire it, and the latter do not understand it.

Therefore, since I am about to speak of the care of souls, I shall observe, to the extent of my powers, the tradition of antiquity, so that a speech which, because of the poverty of my talent, is not in itself sufficiently ringing may by no means be made rough by allegorical citations. For I have not come to teach pastors, but to admonish them, since those who are far more learned than I do not require teaching from an unskilled man, but rather exhortation. And so I admonish you, pastors of souls, that if you love almighty God, you direct his sheep committed to you toward the pastures of life. The rule of this holy solicitude is entrusted to every pastor in the sacred Gospel, where the government of the whole Church is committed to the prince of the apostles by a threefold questioning. A simple confession of love is not sufficient in a pastor of souls, from whom the supreme pastor requires a threefold feeding. If, when asked a third time whether you love the Lord, you have confessed true love, you are also a third time ordered to undertake the care of the sheep. As if the Lord were saying to you: My abbot, do you love me? Receive my sheep to be fed with a threefold nourishment.
