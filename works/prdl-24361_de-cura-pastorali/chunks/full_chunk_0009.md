Do not plead to me weakness of spirit; although it may sometimes be tolerable in a monk, nevertheless in an abbot it is judged utterly useless. Therefore either overcome weakness of spirit, or, lest you perish for the faults of others, humbly lay down the burden that you are not able to bear. Do you not know that the kingdom of God suffers violence? If you neglect to do violence to yourself against the vicious motions of nature, how will you obtain the kingdom of God? Weak is the zeal that you have for the salvation of souls, and therefore your care is weaker. Yet I will say what I think must rightly be said. Why do you conceal the faults of your subjects? Certainly you fear being disturbed; you desire to live in a tranquillity that is not good, and for that reason you are afraid to correct offenders. But this is not the rule of holy governance; this is not the fitting duty of a shepherd. For it is fitting that the ruler of weak cloistered men should often be disturbed, not unprofitably, if he wishes to bring back the habits of the wicked to the path of rectitude. Why do you seek peace among vices? “There is no peace for the wicked,” says the Lord. It is better for sinners to be disturbed to repentance than, agreeing with vices, to be sent to Gehenna. But you fear that, provoked by harsh rebuke, they may flee secretly and depart from the monastery with scandal. I do not disapprove discretion, but I accuse negligence. For you do not discreetly conceal sins, but timidly. Indeed, if a monk is wholly rebellious and incorrigible, if he has tried to corrupt others by bad example, he deserves not compassion but prison, not indulgence but punishment. But if he flees, what is that to you? You have the holy apostle on your side: “If the wicked man departs, let him depart.” For it is more tolerable if one perishes than unity. For since Saint Benedict ordered even an incorrigible man to be expelled from the monastery against his will, you have nothing to fear if he departs of his own accord without fault on your part. Yet one must beware lest you have given a just occasion for fleeing; otherwise you are bound to restore the lost man. Observe the manner of correction handed down in the Rule, and you will contract no sin from those who flee.

For even the most holy fathers, rulers of cloistered men, once had very many fugitives and apostates, whose malice neither harmed the good nor was their scandalous flight harmful to the innocent. Hence Saint John the Apostle says of certain such men: “They went out from us, but they were not of us.” There must be fugitives and apostates in religious communities, so that the perseverance of the good may be proved. But you, O shepherd, consider your duty diligently; in all things observe the tradition of the Rule, and if any of your men departs from you without fault on your part, the highest judge will impute it to him, not to you. For what if a bad and wicked man departs? For if a monk is good, he will not flee from the monastery contrary to his vow; but if he has departed, he was not truly good. It is better for one perverse and incorrigible man to flee of his own accord than for a multitude to perish by his wicked example. But you, who ruinously conceal the faults of your subjects, who are afraid to disturb the assembly of evildoers, even if you have lived innocently according to the Rule, will without doubt perish for the sins of those entrusted to you who are perishing. And what does it matter whether you perish by your own desert or by another’s? Therefore either direct the care of souls according to the norm prescribed to you by the holy fathers, or, if you cannot preside usefully, give up the governance. For he requires of you not only that you yourself live well, but also that by word and example you instruct those entrusted to you to live well. Now therefore, shepherds, fear your danger; govern the sheep entrusted to you according to the Rule. Feed them with the word; feed them with example; feed them with nourishment, so that, when you have ministered well, you may come, to be rewarded, to the joys of heaven.

I have spoken.
