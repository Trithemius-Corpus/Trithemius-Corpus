For the monk who entangles himself in carnal vices, who neglects to live according to the rule which he has professed, willingly incurs the greatest vice of ingratitude.

*How the monks of this lowest region are disordered toward their prelate. Article II.*

Toward their prelate carnal monks are disordered in many ways: they tear apart his life with continual detractions; with a stubborn heart they disparage his pastoral office; they refuse, according to the rule, to show the affection of filial love to their pastor. For, blinded by various pleasures of the flesh, the monks of this lowest region disdain to consider whose place the abbot holds in the monastery; and him whom they ought humbly to have venerated as Christ's vicar, they regard, as though like themselves by reason of weakness, as worthy of no honor. Under the snare of this disorderly conduct, the ancient enemy deceives carnal monks, whom in various ways he brings into displeasure with their pastor. For the greatest power of raging against the monastic flock is granted to the devil when he who presides is hated by his subjects. For just as a sick man cannot be healed who, detesting the physician, does not allow him to approach, so a monk cannot be saved who, because of aversion, does not wish to disclose his temptations to his pastor. For how will a sick sheep be able to escape the teeth of the ravening wolf, if it cannot endure the presence of the shepherd?

First, therefore, disorderly monks disparage the life of their pastor, and stubbornly accuse his deeds, because when they consider that their ruler walks according to the traditions of the holy fathers, they fear to be corrected in their depravity. For among reprobate minds the life of good men is always drawn into suspicion; envy is always tormented by the virtues of others; and the monk who voluntarily subjects himself to vices and sins.

A disorderly monk is one who attempts [unclear] against the rules of the monastery of a venerable order. And there are three ways of this: first, when a monk has had something disorderly, he ought to be excommunicated. Second, when a disorderly monk has had excommunication, they ought to be excommunicated by the same. Third, when a monk has had something disorderly, they ought to give excommunication to him.
