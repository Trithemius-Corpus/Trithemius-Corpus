What a putrid corpse is wont to do in the sight of men, sin, in its own way, does incomparably in the sight of the angels. But carnal monks despise the honor of God, and therefore they do not notice the insult of sin. They neglect to amend venial sins, and so commit mortal sins as if with license. For Scripture says: He who neglects the smallest things falls little by little. Hence the saying of Bernard admonishes us, saying: Let no one, brothers, say in his heart, “These things are light; I do not care to correct them. It is no great matter if I remain in these venial and very small sins.” This, brothers, is impenitence; this is unforgivable blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. For no one can escape greater sins if he has despised amending the lesser. Nothing is worse than carelessness of mind.

Secondly, carnal monks, lovers of the lowest region, are exceedingly disordered with respect to penitence, since they do not notice the contagion of sin. For just as a healthy man is very often infected by a contagious disease, so from one sin a man is inclined into another. For one sin is always said to dispose toward another, unless it is amended as quickly as possible through contrition. For the prophet says: Jerusalem has sinned a sin; therefore she has become unstable; let her become filthier still. We have sufficiently shown the contagion of this disease in the degrees of pride: for just as we said that one always arises from another, so we perceive that through carelessness a man becomes slippery from one sin into another.

Thirdly, carnal monks disordered with respect to penitence contract the worst sickness through the habit of sin, while they fall into a pernicious blindness of mind. Every vice often committed is held cheap, and through habit is judged lighter. Just as grace illumines the mind, so the frequency of sin blinds reason. For it is written: Blind the heart of this people and make its ears heavy, so that seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand, lest they be converted and I heal them, says the Lord. For when the wicked man has come into the depth of evils, he feels contempt. No sin is so great that through habit it does not seem light and small.

Fourthly, disorderly cloisterers, blinded through the habit of evil, do not come to the grace of true compunction; they approach confession superficially from habit alone; they do not abandon their depraved will; they do not truly take up the purpose of amendment. Woe to those who, buried in the habit of sin, do not fear the censure of the strict examination; who do not withdraw from blindness of mind through the remedy of contrition, since just as now they despise the long-suffering of God’s goodness, so in that terrible hour they will undergo the sentence of eternal death. What will confession of the mouth be able to profit where there is no true contrition of the heart? Without contrition there is no saving confession, nor can there be contrition without the purpose of amendment. The judgment of contrition is to be unwilling to sin any more.

Fifthly, the disorderly citizens of the lowest region, made despisers of almighty God through the habit of sinning, always fall into graver dangers of the soul, while they always approach wrongly the most holy sacred mysteries of the body and blood of the Lord. They make a superficial confession from habit; they allow themselves, thinking they are well ordered, to proceed great in their consciences. But now, as if allied with hell, they no longer fear God; they contemn the paths of an amended life; they consider themselves well ordered; they do not care with how many vices they are inwardly filled. At times, sensibly affected by a fantastic devotion, they already think that the Holy Spirit is present; they affirm that they are beloved by God and holy. They also shamelessly thrust themselves into the divine mysteries; not discerning the body and blood of the Lord, they receive it for judgment and condemnation upon themselves. Finally, such people, communicating unworthily, are more grievously hardened, because, while they think themselves very secure and to be living well, they do not duly care to cleanse their conscience from vices. Thus Judas the apostate, the evil of betrayal which before, as a certain [unclear] says, he feared to commit against the Savior, after the morsel had been taken and the devil had entered into him, did not fear to carry out boldly and without shame. O how many are the carnal monks of this lowest region, proud, arrogant, envious, murmuring, and bound fast by various sins, whose conscience is rarely or never made clean through true contrition, who nevertheless are not afraid to approach the most holy altar almost daily.
