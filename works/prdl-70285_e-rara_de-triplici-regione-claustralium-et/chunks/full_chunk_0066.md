*At the second signal.*

But when the second signal sounds, the cenobite, now sufficiently recollected toward inward things, should leave his cell with due composure; he should always bless the Lord in his heart; with the wise virgins he should hasten to meet the bridegroom; he should consider that he hears the signal of the great king. *On leaving the cell, let him say:*

Protect, O Lord, your people by the sign of the holy cross from all snares of all enemies, that we may render you pleasing service, and that our sacrifice may become acceptable to you. Direct us, O Lord, in the way of peace, to the everlasting glory of your name.

*On entering the church, let him say:*

Blessed are those who dwell in your house, O Lord; they will praise you forever and ever. Cleanse me, O Lord, from every defilement of mind and body, that I may deserve to praise and bless you in your house forever. *Passing before the crucifix, let him say:*

I beseech you, most loving Lord Jesus, by all the bitterness of your most holy passion which you endured for me, and especially when your most noble soul departed from your body: have mercy on my soul at its departure. Amen.

*At the holy water.*

Remove from me, O Lord, all my iniquities, that with a pure mind I may deserve to enter the holy of holies. You shall sprinkle me, O Lord, with hyssop, etc. *Before the image of the Blessed Virgin Mary.*

O blooming rose, beautiful Mother of the Lord. O gentle Virgin. O most fruitful vine: brighter than the dawn, pray continually for us. *Passing before the altar, let him say:*

Glory to you, O Lord, who were born of a Virgin, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto everlasting ages. *Or:*

You, the unbegotten Father; you, the only-begotten Son; you, the Holy Spirit; the holy and undivided Trinity, I praise, bless, and adore without end. O supreme [unclear] of unity, principle of all things: have mercy on me for your own sake.

*Before the venerable sacrament.*

Hail, living host, truth and life, in whom the ancient sacrifices have been brought to an end. Through you infinite glory is given to the Father. Through you the Church stands continually defended. Grant me rewards in eternal life. Amen. Or: O saving host.

*Standing in his place in choir.*

But when he has come to his accustomed place of standing in choir, if there is time, let him consider what our holy father Benedict said in the Rule about reverence in prayer and psalmody. Then, considering the matter of the psalms to be sung, let him strive to subject his intention to them, and carefully foresee to what he wishes to refer each one. If the office of matins is sung for some saint, let the psalmody be said before the Lord in memory of that saint. But if the matter of the psalms accords with the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, it must be referred to its memory. Yet if the spirit of compunction is present to the singer, it will teach all things through love. For a mind fervent in the love of God knows best how to direct its intention to the praise of the Creator, and to vary it at once in each verse according to the character of the psalmody. For according to the matter of the things that are sung, whenever it wishes it exercises itself, and now embraces the humanity of Christ, now suddenly, with another verse, is raised up into his divinity. Nevertheless, if it pleases him, our faithful cenobite can, while the same signal continues, bring back to mind the fervor of the holy angels praising God without interruption, and conform himself to them by desire according to his powers.

*Before all the hours.*

Since the power of the Lord’s incarnation and passion surpasses incomparably the merits of all the saints, therefore the love of God should say this prayer to God the Father before each hour, with attention of mind, in heart or with the mouth. *Prayer*
