*At the Sanctus.*

With your mind lifted upward when the Sanctus is sung, glorify the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God and Lord; humbly adore his majesty with the holy angels at all times.

*During the Canon.*

After the Sanctus, during the Canon, you may take up meditation on the Lord’s Passion, and offer it, through each of its greater principal articles, to God the Father, for yourself, or for others, or for any necessity. Say, therefore, in this or a similar manner:

Holy Father, I offer to you the bloody sweat and tears of our Lord Jesus Christ, your most beloved Son, in that charity and union by which he voluntarily offered himself to you unto death for us: for my sins, or for that brother, or for this or that necessity; and I beseech you, through the pain of the wounds of the same your sweetest Son, and through the pouring out of his most holy blood and the power of his most innocent death: have mercy on me, or on him, or grant us this or that, etc. Through the same our Lord.

You may also act in like manner concerning the other articles of the Lord’s Passion, and enlarge them in offering according to the intention of your affection.

*At the Elevation.*

If, at the elevation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your mind has not been occupied with higher things, you may pray to the Lord, who is truly present, in this way.

O Christ, king of glory. You are the everlasting Son of the Father. You, to deliver, etc., to the end. For Thomas Aquinas is read to have prayed this part of the hymn to the Lord at the elevation.

*At the Agnus Dei.*

When the Agnus Dei is sung, raise your mind to the Lord Jesus with singular trust, and humbly pray that he receive you into perpetual peace. Direct your affection toward him for love of whom you have left all things; ask from him a stable progress in your holy purpose through reception of sacred communion.

*At the Ite, missa est.*

When at the end the Ite, missa est is sung, see that your mind is lifted up with the holy angels returning into heaven, so that through desire you may merit to behold the Lord Jesus presiding in majesty, whom, having been sent to the Father in the sacrament, you can no longer see on earth. Therefore kindle your desire through the image of desolation, in the manner of those who are separated unwillingly from their friends.

Say, then, in your heart:

*Meditation.*

Behold, my Lord has returned to the heavens; he has left me desolate on earth. What am I doing here among mortals, who have an immortal Lord? Why do I not follow him? Woe is me, for my sojourning has been prolonged; I have dwelt with the inhabitants of darkness; my soul has long been a sojourner. I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ; but who will deliver me, unhappy man, from the body of this death? O most beloved Lord Jesus, O my hope, O God and savior of my soul, draw me after you; I shall run in the fragrance of your ointments. O my sweet love, do not forget me at the end, but deign frequently to lift up my mind to you, so that, since I, placed in this mortality, am unable to see you bodily, at least uplifted by affection and desire in your most holy love I may merit often to behold you in spirit in glory. O my salvation, O heart of my heart, most beloved Jesus, do not abandon me or despise me; be a gracious helper to me, and direct me always in your most holy will, so that I may truly be able to please you.
