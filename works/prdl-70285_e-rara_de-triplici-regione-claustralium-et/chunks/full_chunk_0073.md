*For private Masses.*

Since human nature by its own strength cannot of itself attain entrance into the kingdom of heaven, it pleased the most merciful Father, God, through the death of our Lord Jesus Christ, his only-begotten Son, mercifully to come to our aid, so that we who, because of our weakness, cannot approach the invisible majesty might have a mediator, who is God and man, approaching on our behalf. And the merit of the most holy passion of our mediator opened for us access to the kingdom of heaven, gave the nourishment of spiritual life; and just as a branch draws nourishment from the vine, so in Christ our mind truly lives. But we receive this spiritual nourishment with special sweetness in the sacrament of the body and blood of that same mediator of ours, whenever we devoutly offer it to God the Father as an odor of sweetness. And this most sacred offering of the body and blood of the Lord is profitable not only to us for the forgiveness of sins, but also wonderfully assists the souls of the dead and those placed in purgatory. Therefore this noble sacrifice must be offered with great and continual devotion, since all our good works, which in themselves are exceedingly worthless, are through this most holy offering most pleasing to God the Father. Therefore, so that our devout cenobite may be able to attend more fervently to the offering of this precious sacrifice, let him carefully note four things in order.

*Of the minister at Mass.*

The devout brother who is about to minister at Mass ought to hasten to it with the greatest cheerfulness and fervor, and prepare himself most diligently to minister to the priest. For Saint Hugh says: Minister gladly at Masses, because this is the office of angels; and when you minister at that sacrifice, you make familiar to yourself those whose co-worker you stand as. If, according to the Savior's promise, one who receives a righteous man in the name of a righteous man receives the reward of a righteous man, who will deny the reward of the celebrant to one who devoutly ministers at Mass? Whoever has been accustomed devoutly to stand by at the proper time, with the fervor of divine love, to minister to those celebrating, divine goodness is accustomed to reward him even in this life with some special grace; and when he is dying the holy angels, whom they believe to be known to him with such familiarity, come to meet him and present the soul of the minister of Jesus before his sight with joy. For thus the Son prays to the Father in the holy Gospel: Father, I will that where I am, there also may be my minister. For great indeed in this venerable sacrament is the condescension of divine love, which, as the same Hugh says, God seems to do nothing less in it than daily deign to descend from heaven upon the altar for our salvation, as when, descending from heaven, he assumed human nature and was made man. And just as our Lord Jesus Christ, in memory of his most bitter passion, bestowed on us this venerable sacrament as a sign of the greatest love, so he loves more abundantly and singularly those who dispose themselves with humble devotion and love to receive it, or to minister to those receiving it; and through it he succors those faithfully petitioning him in every necessity.

Nor will Jesus be able to be hard to us when we pray with full faith, he who is seated in heaven, who was so merciful and kind to those asking mercy when he walked on earth. Who once came to his feet and was rejected? What sick man asked to be healed whom, walking among men, he did not cure? Therefore now will the Lord Jesus deny mercy to us asking in faith, he who, while still mortal, was so merciful to those praying to him? For he says: All things are possible to one who believes.

Therefore the one about to minister at Mass ought before all things to purify himself from every evil temptation and disordered affection, and as far as he can draw back into himself the desire of the love of Jesus, so that, purified, he may be able to stand by the Lord, and nevertheless [unclear] for the whole Church, the living and the dead, pray effectively.
