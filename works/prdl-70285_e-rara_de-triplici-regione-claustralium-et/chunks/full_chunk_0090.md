The devout and faithful cenobite, who loves God in truth, does not spare himself in labor, but is accustomed to apply himself with such great fervor, cheerfulness, and fidelity to the works of his hands laid upon him, as though by his labor alone he were compelled to feed all those established in the monastery. For the most prudent soul knows that he spends himself wholly for God, whatever bodily necessity of his servants may happen to make him labor. For just as spiritual things cannot long subsist in religion without the cooperation of temporal things, so I do not hesitate to call the one who, for God's sake, supplies the bodily necessities to those serving God a servant of God. And therefore whatever a good cenobite can acquire for the monastery by his labors with right intention, he should know that he offers all that to the Lord. Nevertheless the monk should always understand that he must labor in such a way that he in no wise omits the monastic prayers, masses, and the other services of holy mother Church.

But manual work that is elaborate and undertaken out of self-complacency is deprived of the merit of obedience before the eternal Judge; and, as idle and presumed without the superior's permission, it is held liable to account. But the good cenobite, who desires to please God alone, in every creeping-in of an idle word, deed, or thought, should say to himself that saying of Bernard: If you were now about to die, would you do this thing which you are now doing, thinking, or saying? But if, were you now about to die, you would not do what you are now doing, you are very foolish, since you so boldly permit yourself, at the time of death, while you are soon to die, unto death. But if you desire to die well when it becomes necessary, do at no time what you would not do if death were already present. For whatever you have done now, after death you will find it, whether it is good or evil.

*Within, let the monk be humble and devout.*

Second, at the time of labor the monk ought to be devout and humble within. If he abandons the progress of inward fervor, what he outwardly acquires through labor profits nothing. Therefore, so that the soul may truly make progress while laboring, let him always recall to memory the degrees of good will written in the first [rule?]. Let him set in order meditations on the Lord's passion with the fervor of devotion, in which he may conform his labors, according to his ability, to the labors of Christ. Let him always be humble in heart through self-denial. Let him be devout in affection through the fervor of holy compunction, so that the outward labor of the hand may profit toward the [unclear] and the usefulness of the community, and the mind may not withdraw from the inward sweetness of divine love. If labor wearies the working cenobite, if heat burns him, if untimely thirst oppresses him, let the Lord's labor, which he endured for us, be recalled, and he will bear all things lightly. Therefore let him say this prayer to the Lord.

*Prayer of one laboring.*

Most sweet and most lovable Lord Jesus, king of heaven and earth, who for my redemption were born of the most pure Virgin Mary, and for thirty-three years were among men as God and man; and who had great labors in walking, teaching, and [unclear]; and endured thirst, hunger, heat, and cold willingly and patiently, for all of which I give thanks to you: receive, I beseech you, these very small labors of mine, though useless, which I offer in humility to your most holy [testament?], in memory and honor of all your labor and of your most holy passion; and may they please you in the union by which your most holy labors pleased God the Father. Amen.

Let our cenobite, moreover, during the time of labor, turn over in his mind the principal articles of the Lord's passion, first by recalling them as though with Christ present in the passion; then by giving thanks at his knees; thirdly by asking mercy in himself according to the likeness [of them].

Let him bring back to memory the conversations of any holy fathers or the sufferings of martyrs, and consider how great a reward and glory they now receive for their labors in the fatherland.
