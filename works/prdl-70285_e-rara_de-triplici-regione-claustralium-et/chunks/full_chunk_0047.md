…[it] cannot [become better]: because neither does the best become better, nor the greatest become greater. God created you by his goodness, not because your creation was necessary to him, but advantageous to you alone. He who considers this goodness of God with intent meditation deservedly praises his Creator.

Secondly, lest you be lifted up into pride, O man, consider the vileness of your condition: that you were made from the slime of the earth, and after a little while, consumed by worms, you will return into dust in the earth. Why then are you proud, [you] earth and ashes? Your conception has fault; [your] nativity, misery; [your] life, penalty; the dissolution of death, anguish. For man, born of woman, living a short time, is filled with many miseries; and then he is most miserable, when he does not acknowledge himself miserable in the flesh.

Thirdly, consider the origin of your conception, and you will find your soul stained with the foul turpitude of original sin. For we are born children of wrath; but, obtaining the grace of baptism in the blood of Christ Jesus, we are cleansed from the original stain. Deservedly do we love our Savior through this consideration, by whose mercy we have been cleansed from the original stain.

Fourthly, consider, O faithful soul, how exceedingly vile you have been made, repeating your ways in the act of sin: since you have shown yourself an adulteress to Christ your spouse, when he had joined you to himself—wholly immaculate—by his blood. What do you do, O wretched one? Why do you desert your most loving spouse, or why, foolish, do you withdraw from your beginning? Know that you are placed in the flesh—not that you should consent to its vices and concupiscences, but rather that you should resist [them] by the command of reason. For as often as you shall have surrendered your consent (so to speak, by dissembling) to the inordinate motions of the flesh, contrary to divine rectitude, so often, turned away from the supreme good and from your spouse, you show yourself an adulteress. Beware, beware, O soul redeemed by the blood of Christ, lest the love of the flesh deceive you; for, just as the first man, deceived by love of his consort woman, fell into death, so any soul whatever, consenting to the pleasures of its flesh, basely withdraws from its beginning, which is God. But, alas, wretched [soul], how have you fallen? You have dwelt in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been counted with those descending into hell.
