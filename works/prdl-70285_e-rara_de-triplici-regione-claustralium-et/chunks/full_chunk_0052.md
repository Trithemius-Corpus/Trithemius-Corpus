Each one thinks his own companion better than the rest; and each, deceived, loves the other not spiritually but carnally, since they do not raise their affections to God: they love nothing but the consolations of the flesh. But the monk whom you see seeking the friendship of weak brethren you must regard not as a servant of God but as a lover of the flesh. For he who truly occupies his whole heart in the love of God cares nothing for human friendship. The love of Jesus suffices him for all things; apart from him he seeks nothing further. But he who has sought consolation of mind in outward things shows by his deed that he does not love God with his whole heart. The love of God loves solitude, hates the tumults of men, and does not seek the conversations of friends. O good Jesus, how sweet is your love, since it penetrates the heart of him who loves you; seeing you, the soul longs [to see] you; possessing you, it desires to embrace you. For truly he who loves you forgets his very self; he meditates on nothing but the sweetest presence of you. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves you; nowhere is there found anything that the soul desires besides you. O with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is distressed; with what most abundant tears the face is watered in secret, because as often as it thinks of the face of the beloved presiding in heaven, so often it laments itself desolate on earth. Meanwhile the languishing mind often tries to say something of its most beloved Jesus; it strives also to provoke others to love of him; but as soon as it has begun to utter the sweetest name of Jesus, unwillingly, because of the greatness of love, it falls silent. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, but is felt with sweetness in the secrecy of the mind. I know one who said: My love has been crucified. Who is your love, O servant of God? Why do you weep? Why do you groan? Ignatius: My love has been crucified, and shall I not mourn? My Lord has died, and shall I be able to restrain my tears? Do not weep, O faithful soul; for although your love died in order to redeem you from death, yet now he has risen from the dead, that in heaven [he may give] the crown of life. Be faithful unto death, and he will give you the crown of life.

*On the manner and degrees of divine love, comprising the conclusion of this second region.*

Since no one suddenly becomes the highest, nor can anyone by his own powers suddenly ascend to that supreme summit of divine love, I now think it useful briefly to note the fixed manners or degrees themselves of loving God.

Therefore, for moving the human heart to divine love, the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, in my judgment, is more effective than all other things, if it has been meditated upon in a man’s mind purely, sweetly, fervently, perseveringly, and diligently. For truly, he who often recalls to memory, with compassion and thanksgiving, the life, Passion, and death of our Savior according to the chief principal articles, prepares a swift ascent of the heart to divine love. But this most holy occupation of the mind concerning the Passion of the Lord we shall treat opportunely in the second principal part of the work, where we shall set forth more broadly the manner of spiritual exercise. But so far as the account of what has been said pertains in this place, I find three manners of loving God, which I shall briefly explain in order.

The first manner commands that you love him who loves you, that you join your whole self to him sweetly, so that your heart loves nothing outside God and seeks nothing from elsewhere. For since God is wholly simple, he alone desires to possess the whole heart of the lover; he drives it far from dissipated thoughts. Therefore if you wish to love sweetly, love wholly. For the love of God makes either everything perfect or nothing. Let meditation on the Passion of Jesus Christ our Savior possess you wholly, until love sweetly grows strong in you. You must despise everything in the flesh that flatters, if you desire to arrive at the love of God. For no one can sweetly love God in this life so long as he strives to love anything in the world. Indeed, love of the world is utterly contrary to divine love, since where one is admitted to the inner chambers of the heart, the other is forbidden to be present.
