The ruler of souls also, having been appointed over the cenobites, should take care to conduct himself and to show himself a true shepherd to the sheep, not a hireling; let him not neglect the salvation of the souls committed to him; let him not put temporal things before spiritual things. Let him hate vices, love the brethren, and show himself exemplary in all good things.

Next, let the brethren cleave to one another as members of one head; let them love one another in the true charity of God, and not withdraw from good zeal. For all of us who, through faith in Christ, trust to come to eternal life by applying ourselves to good works are members of Christ, the supreme head. But whoever persecutes his brother with evil zeal or hatred and other evil affections first separates himself from the head, and then does injury to Christ.

Consequently, let each one show himself ordered with respect to himself: for the love of Christ let him humbly submit himself to all; through humility let him prefer others to himself; and let him believe himself more unworthy than all monks.

In the use of the monastery’s goods let them always beware of exceeding the instruction of the rule; rather, let them strive everywhere to show themselves ordered and faithful in all things. Let them desire to have nothing contrary to the abbot’s will; let them admit no pleasure of the spirit in the use of things.

Then faithful cenobites will take care to order themselves, according to the rule’s instruction, toward contempt of the world, so that they love nothing in this world, but truly despise all things for the love of God.

Let them not neglect to order themselves most diligently to penitence; let them often have recourse to humble confession of their transgressions, since, just as no one is believed to live in this world without sin, so penitence is judged necessary for all, even for monks. Therefore the good cenobite, through the pursuit of compunction, should always learn to have recourse to penitence, humbly to accuse himself of his transgressions in chapter, willingly to perform the imposed penance, and faithfully to amend himself in all things.

*On the Ordered Instruction of True Monastic Progress and the Degrees of Humility. Article Five.*

The greatest virtue of a monk is humility, and the gravest crime in a cenobite is pride. For a monk who is proud with a swollen mind becomes an imitator and servant of the devil, and is stripped of all virtues, if he had any, since no virtue can subsist in a mind in which pride has happened to dwell. Therefore the cenobite who desires to make progress in this middle region should learn to order himself, through humility, toward the true progress of monastic instruction, because no one truly attains humility except one who dwells upon spiritual pursuits. True progress in regular instruction, excluding all adulterine muses, arises from the degrees of humility, which, through their roots, are set down in the rule as twelve. The roots of this progress come from the pursuit of sacred reading, which gradually leads a person to the fear of the Lord. From reading arises spiritual meditation, from which holy compunction is generated. The more useful mode of cenobitic meditation consists in the memory of the Lord’s passion and in the consideration of God’s benefits. Further, those who, exceeding this middle region, come to tranquility of spirit render their meditations more sublime through contemplation of the divine majesty and excellence. And indeed we shall speak of these in their own place. But these two roots receive growth from the watering of continual prayer, and strengthen into the vigor of a very firm plant. For no meditation makes progress without prayer, and study without it will not save.

From these three roots of spiritual progress arises the fear of the Lord, which is the first degree of humility; from it the other degrees are generated, succeeding in order. But monks who neglect the salutary pursuit of the Scriptures and holy meditations together with continual prayers in no way reach this first degree of humility.
