*To Saint Mary.*

Holy and immaculate Mother of God, Virgin Mary: I ask you, through the bitter passion and death of your most sweet Son, do not reject me, a wretched sinner, on account of my unclean conscience, but remember that you are the mother of mercy, and pray for me to God, whom you bore, that he may mercifully forgive me my sins, make me a fervent lover of himself, guard me this night from all snares and evils, and after the death of the flesh lead my soul to eternal life. To your protection, most blessed Virgin Mary, I commit myself this night and at all times, in the virtue and union of that commending by which your beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, when now about to die on the cross, commended you to John, his disciple.

*On the remembrance of the Lord’s Passion.*

Since the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is the gate of our salvation, through which the kingdom of heaven is opened to us, we ought, with whatever devotion we can, to exercise ourselves in it very often, because we shall readily obtain the effect of our prayers if, through the love of compassion, in the virtue of his wounds, we adore the Lord. Therefore, faithful cenobite, with all the diligence I can I admonish, exhort, and ask you that, mindful of God’s benefits, you utterly neglect no day on which you do not give thanks to your Savior for the bitterness of his Passion and death. And if for any reason you cannot manage this at greater length, do not refuse to show yourself grateful to your Redeemer with at least one word. If the Lord Jesus himself revealed to those lovers of his to whom he wished that nothing is more pleasing to him, and nothing more useful to us, than to recall with thanksgiving the memory of his Passion and death, and to admonish others to do the same. Receive, then, an abbreviated word, lest a longer prayer make you weary.

With knees bent to the ground and hands clasped together, say:

I give thanks to you, most sweet Lord Jesus, for all the wounds, pains, and injuries of your most innocent Passion and death; I ask you, through that death of yours, have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death.

Or thus:

To your most bitter Passion and most innocent death, Lord Jesus Christ, with a compassionate soul I give thanks; I ask you, have mercy on me.

Or thus:

I praise, bless, and adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, King of heaven and earth, and I give thanks to you for your holy Passion, by which you delivered wretched me from everlasting death. I ask you, through the virtue of that Passion of yours, make me forever a partaker of your most innocent death.

*Conclusion of this compendium, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.*

Lest I be more prolix than is fitting, here I put an end to the compendium. For that venerable assembly of abbots of our annual chapter instructed me to avoid prolixity in this place, lest lukewarm and worldly cloisterers call weariness as a cause. If the Lord Jesus gives me a life free from cares, as I have always desired, I shall endeavor to satisfy both my own intention and another’s request. For, when certain virgins dedicated to Christ were asking, I resolved to hand down a rule by which any devout cenobite may be able to exercise himself in the Lord’s Passion against all temptations; if he has continued, as he ought, in exercising himself in it for a few months, little by little he will sweetly fly from the humanity of Christ to his divinity by the ray of love. For to those desiring to come to the love of God, continued meditation on the Lord’s Passion opens an easy approach, because while the mind, through compassion, is pierced from the memory of the benefits of so great a lover, burning with tears and desire it is lifted up to love. Therefore you cloisterers dedicated to God, for whom there is no leisure to attend to many things, receive these few things; in them, although there may not be a continuous fire of love, it can nevertheless be renewed for those who persevere.

Here ends the compendium of daily spiritual exercise, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot.
