*The third degree of humility is to subject oneself in all obedience to one’s superior for the love of God, and to show oneself an imitator of Christ by works, not by words only; concerning him the holy Apostle says: “He became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.”* This degree takes its beginning from the second and raises its ascent as far as the third. For just as the second mortifies one’s own will by denying it, so this one, through humble subjection, lays the foundation of holy work. The former subjects itself wholly to the divine will; the latter, for the love of God, also obeys men.

The keeping of one degree without the observance of the other does not suffice in a monk; nor is the will of a cenobite subjected to the divine ordering if it disdains to obey the command of a superior.

Therefore the true cenobite places all his joy in the virtue of holy obedience; he considers it the greatest gain to fulfill in all things the will of his shepherd; he believes it a grave loss to the soul if he should find that he has committed even a little outside obedience. For he always considers with faithful devotion what the Lord says in the Gospel: “He who hears you hears me, and he who despises you despises me.” Hence that blessed Bernard says to the monks: “Whatever a man commands in God’s stead must be received no otherwise than if God himself commanded it in person.” Therefore the true monk should prefer this subjection of obedience to every sacrifice, should do absolutely nothing against its integrity, and should continually say to himself what is written: “Obedience is better.” The rule says: therefore all things are to be done with the abbot’s will.

But this degree, as we have said, arises from the second, because he who, through fear of God, perfectly abandons his own will now seeks nothing else in this world than to follow that will in all things. But because God speaks once and does not repeat the same thing, weak man cannot know the will of the Most High; therefore he has committed himself [unclear] into the hands of his prelate. Let the cenobite therefore obey the vicar of Christ according to the Rule, and without doubt he will fulfill the will of God.

This obedience, however, is owed strictly to prelates according to the Gospel and the monastic rule; for although mutual obedience among the brothers is good, still it must never be preferred to the command of a superior. They command monks to render discreet obedience to one another, and, with the precepts of superiors preserved, to anticipate one another in mutual services. And, to bring an example from our custom: everyone must be obeyed according to order. If a brother calls upon you, obey willingly; if a senior commands something else, set aside the command of the first and obey him. Again, if the prior of the convent interposes his command, all mutual obedience of the brothers will cease. But if the abbot’s command intervenes, no obedience to any other will have force to the contrary. For he is believed to act first in the monastery in Christ’s stead, and therefore he is preferred to all.

There are seven gradual circumstances of the virtue of holy obedience, which blessed Bernard described thus. The first degree of obedience is to obey willingly, namely that you have no will of your own at all, but believe your shepherd’s good pleasure to be entirely your own. The second is to obey simply, so that you do neither more nor otherwise than what has been commanded you, nor, by doing less, omit anything. The third is to obey cheerfully, so that you display serenity in your face and show sweetness in your voice. The fourth is to obey swiftly, so that the hand is not slower in the work than the command is in the voice. The fifth is to obey manfully, lest one withdraw his strength from the command, and pretend that he is weak and unequal to the precepts. The sixth is to obey humbly, not proudly, not arrogantly, not contentiously; since unless obedience is humble, it will bring back not reward but punishment. The seventh is to obey perseveringly, because obedience that is interrupted before completion is not true obedience; and only he who perseveres unto the end is rewarded.
