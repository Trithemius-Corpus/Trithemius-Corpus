Almighty, everlasting God, who created us out of nothing when we did not exist, and, when we had perished, mercifully redeemed us: I beseech you, by your unchangeable majesty, receive these praises and prayers which I am about to say to you now and at all times, in that union of charity and love in which you sent your only-begotten Son into the womb of the most blessed Virgin Mary, and in which you received him suffering and dying for us on the cross. Amen.

*Note concerning the foregoing prayer.*

Do not think this manner of praying frivolous, nor devised by human curiosity; for if you believe that God has been accustomed to teach his friends many things by revelation, you will in no way contradict this most holy form. Rather, read the wondrous works of the Lord which he has shown to those who love him, and you will find what he revealed to Mechthild and to others concerning this manner and form of praying. Know, moreover, that I am wholly persuaded that the prayer which a devout mind resolves to offer to God the Father in union with the Lord's Passion is most pleasing to almighty God. And this offering can be made beforehand, in mind or by mouth, not only in the canonical hours, but also in other prayers, masses, and any good exercises or works. But if through forgetfulness it has not been made beforehand, it can be inserted during the prayer, or even added after its completion.

*How a monk ought to conduct himself in the hours.*

The devout cenobite should employ great diligence, lest the mind admit images or thoughts about passing things; for unless vain thoughts are repelled at once in the beginning, they will disturb the intention of the heart throughout the whole office. Therefore the faithful one at prayer should gather himself wholly within himself, and, according to the Rule, put nothing before the service of God. While chanting the psalms, he should refer the very subject matter of the psalms to the fitting focus of his intention; he should prudently consider the meaning of the prayer; he should pronounce the words distinctly and in order; he should direct his mind upward. After each psalm or lesson, however, he should examine himself by carefully considering himself, and should test whether the compunction of devotion is still fervent in his mind. If he finds that fervor is still present, he should strive to kindle it more fully with holy desires, and to summon himself to higher things. But if he senses that his affection has grown lukewarm or has been distracted by thoughts, immediately reproving himself he should say these or similar words: Woe to me, wretched man, who praise my God so negligently. Behold, my Lord, suffering most bitterly for me the whole night, had no rest, and I could not watch with him one hour? Help me, Lord my God, that I may be able to sing psalms to you wisely.

Finally, under each psalm he should, as occasion offers, stir himself to the compunction of fervor by some ardent words, so that through mental exhortation he may inflame his affection still more. Saying, for example: Would that, Lord God, I could love you with my whole heart above all things, and cling continually to you alone. O good God, kindle my heart in your most holy love. My love has been crucified; and what am I doing upon the earth? O Jesus, my sweet love, possess my heart, for you are the treasure of my heart, my king and my God.

*After Matins.*

When the office of Matins has been completed, and confessions have been made or heard, he should not neglect at once to read the hour of the Holy Cross; when it has been completed, he should add this little prayer:

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, by the pain of your wounds, and by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death.

*Prayer.*

To the holy and undivided Trinity, to the crucified humanity of Jesus Christ our Lord, to the most blessed Mary ever Virgin, and to Saint Anne, his venerable mother, with all the saints, be everlasting praise and glory for infinite ages. Amen.

Then let him say.
