And this lowest region is compared to the airy heaven; for just as hailstorms, snows, lightning, and various tempests are generated in the air, so also in the minds of carnal men inhabiting this dark region various tempests of vices rage. Woe to carnal monks who inhabit this dark region, in which there is frequent motion of crimes, a continual tempest of pleasures. These are monks given over to animal passions, who subject reason to sensuality, who live according to the flesh in bestial desires. Now the works of the flesh dwelling in this lowest region are manifest: fornication, uncleanness, shamelessness, lust, service of idols, sorceries, enmities, rivalries, angers, quarrels, dissensions, sects, envies, murders, drunkenness, revelries, and things like these; concerning which the holy Apostle proclaims that those who do such things shall not obtain the kingdom of God.

These are the signs of the works of the lowest region; these are the deadly tempests of the carnal minds of monks, who live irreligiously in religion, who despise the traditions of the holy fathers, who with ruinous presumption contemn the precepts of their elders. These are the unclean cultivators of the lowest region, given over to vices and carnal desires, who do not care to have God in their knowledge, who enervate themselves with the pleasures of the flesh, who stubbornly break the vow of holy religion which they professed, who, despising the salvation of their souls, disdain to follow the grace of God calling them.

Therefore he handed them over to a reprobate sense and to false judgment, so that they consider evil good, and those things which in truth are very bad they falsely judge to be very good. These are those of whom the holy Apostle prophesied: carnal monks, whisperers, detractors, hateful to God, insulting, proud, exalted, inventors of evils, disobedient to their superiors, foolish, disorderly, without affection, without covenant, without mercy, filled with all iniquity, malice, wickedness, contention, deceit, and malignity. For those who do such things are worthy of everlasting death, and not only those who do them, but also those who consent to those who do them, and those who, from their office, in no way oppose the pursuits of the wicked.

For indeed the ruler of souls who does not correct delinquent subjects entangles himself in the crimes of others for his damnation; and by keeping silent he is made the companion of those whose faults damnation will follow. For whoever, being set as a pastor of souls, neglects to amend sinners according to canonical rule, even if he is without fault of his own, nevertheless before the strict Judge he will be condemned for another’s desert. For the supreme shepherd says to the prophet: Son of man, I have given you as a watchman to the house of Israel; and you shall hear the word from my mouth and announce it to them from me. If, therefore, when I say to the wicked man, You shall die by death, you do not announce it to him, nor speak so that he may turn from his impious way and live, that wicked man indeed shall die in his iniquity, but I shall require his blood from your hand.

And our blessed father says in the Rule: Let the abbot know, he says, that the fault of the shepherd will lie upon him if the householder can find any lack of profit in his sheep. O sentence to be feared above all by all shepherds, by which not only the depraved conduct of subjects is ascribed to the rector, but he is also held guilty before the inward Judge for their lesser progress. O what shall we miserable shepherds say in that strict examination, we who neglect to amend according to the Rule the monks committed to us when they sin in many ways; we who do not call those lying in the lowest region to higher things, but, carnal with the carnal, grow sluggish in the shadow of death through destructive idleness.

Woe to all who remain in this dark region; for unless they do penance, they must be assigned with those who inhabit the outer darkness, where no order but perpetual horror dwells. For just as monks walking according to the rule of the spirit are to be happily crowned without end in heaven with the holy angels, so those who, contrary to the norm of monastic institution, persevere in this lowest region of the flesh until death, worse than pagans, are to be eternally tormented with demons in hell. For that blessed man truly said: Just as I have never seen men better than those who have advanced in the cenobium, so I have not experienced any worse than those who have failed in the monastery. For monastic profession requires the perfection of the spiritual life, and tends toward the summit of virtues as toward its proper due end.
