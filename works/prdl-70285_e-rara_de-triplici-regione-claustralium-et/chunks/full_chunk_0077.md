I would indeed call meditation on the Lord’s passion savory: when a man, purified through true mortification, as soon as he has begun to turn over the Lord’s passion in thought, is dissolved into tears without violence, from love alone, and from the excessive desire of fervor is prepared as though for a certain transport of mind. He who has received this gift from the Lord will never be able to prepare himself better for celebrating than if, before approaching the altar, he always runs through in meditation, with tears, at least one article of the Lord’s passion. For indeed, since the celebration of masses was instituted by our redeemer himself in memory of the Lord’s passion, a man never prepares himself for it better and more fruitfully than if he burns more fervently through meditation on that most bitter passion itself.

*The elevation of the priest’s mind to God during celebration.*

With what great reverence, devotion, and purity of mind we ought to stand before almighty God at the altar, what discourse could express, or what understanding penetrate by comprehending? For there the true body and true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are confected in an ineffable manner; there the Lord of majesty, truly present, is adored; there the army of angels, attending upon its invisible king, rejoices together; there the whole heavenly court adores Jesus, God and man. Therefore the greatest purity of mind, the greatest reverence and humility, are needed when we must stand before so great a majesty, lest the Lord Jesus, who searches the reins and the heart, be able to find in us anything that offends him. Let all worldly cares withdraw far from here; let all thoughts depart. Let nothing be thought here except what pertains to the consummation of so great a mystery; let nothing turn in the mind except what befits the presence of so great a majesty and glory. For here no office of human power is performed, but the miracle of divine majesty; the Son of God is sacrificed to the Father; the body of our redeemer is truly received; the mystery of our reparation is celebrated. Therefore the priest standing at the altar should remember that he stands before an invisible majesty, and should beware of thinking anything from any other source.

*After celebration.*
*Let careful guard be kept over the mind.*

After the celebration, or after communion of the Lord’s body and blood, let the devout cenobite offer thanksgivings to almighty God; and let him keep himself throughout the whole day in the purity and devotion of mind he has begun, through spiritual exercise; let him direct his affections toward God with fiery rays, and not grow lukewarm from his holy purpose. Before all things let him strive to love nothing in this world, but to raise his heart continually to the fire of divine love, wholly stripped of all earthly affections. Let him always think how henceforth he may order his life as though anew, in the holy fervor of his way of life, and humbly dispose himself for the frequently repeated reception of so great a guest. Finally, through the day, in the labors enjoined upon him, let him often offer some prayer to God the Father in the power and union of that sacrament, so that through the exercises of holy communion and regular obedience he may inflame himself more greatly in divine love. And we attain this divine love especially through the power of this venerable sacrament, if, by receiving it more often in memory of the Lord’s passion, we are renewed through the grace of compunction. For indeed the devout mind of man, through communion of this most sacred sacrament, is kindled by the effective operation of fervent divine love; and, having wholly denied itself, is transformed into Christ through grace united [unclear]. Therefore, the more each person, denied to himself through true resignation, has offered himself as a living sacrifice in memory of the Lord’s passion, the more disposed he will be to celebrate worthily.
