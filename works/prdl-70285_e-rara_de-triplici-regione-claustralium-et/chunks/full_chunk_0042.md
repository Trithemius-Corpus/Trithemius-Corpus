O what shall I, a little worm, indeed nothing in comparison with my maker, render to the supreme and incomprehensible majesty, who, when I did not exist, created me by his power, and, when I had perished, became man for me and, dying on the cross, redeemed me? What shall I repay to you, mediator of God and men, Jesus, savior of the world, who, though you are God exalted and inestimable, became man for me, the vilest, and were willing to undergo things so vile and unworthy? Behold, my heart speaks to you; all my inward parts bow toward you, because no voice can express what, concerning your most innocent passion, you grant your faithful inwardly to feel in their hearts.

Fourth, in the work of our justification, the kindly patience of God is worthy of praise, because he not only mercifully bears with sinners, but also calls those who are to be justified to repentance by the promises of eternal joys. Who could suffice to praise this clemency of the most indulgent father, who so mercifully justifies sinners returning to him, gives after a fall the grace of meriting, ordains manifold occasions for living well, and supplies his sacraments to the penitent as certain vessels of grace? Finally, for compunction, instruction, consolation, and thanksgiving, he pours the Holy Spirit himself into his faithful, making friends and sons out of enemies. He does not consider what sort of sinner a man was before; he seeks only that thereafter he remain faithful to him. Great is the clemency of divine compassion, which so mercifully receives the penitent to pardon, and even promises them great rewards after guilt.

Fifth, the deep justice of God is worthy of praise in the damnation of the reprobate, because he permits no evil to pass unpunished, depriving the sinner of the good which, by sinning, the fool does not fear to lose. Finally, to speak in our fashion, the supreme purity of God is shown worthy of praise in this, that it abominates every sin and vice as a putrid corpse. We have also recognized that his wisdom is worthy of praise in the damnation of the impious, which, drawing good things out of evils, compels those to serve him in punishment who, sinning against justice, were unwilling to be subject in guilt.

Sixth, God’s abundant munificence is worthy of praise in the glorification of the blessed, since for a small merit he rewards his elect with so ineffable a recompense, as though he gave them an immortal gift with the holy angels.

Seventh, the incomprehensible excellence of God is worthy of praise in itself, which orders all things according to his providence and, in [unclear], always without motion or time, remains immovable. The contemplation of this seventh consideration pertains to the sabbath of eternal felicity, in which there is no longer the operation of laborious activity, but the blessedness of a happy, eternal dwelling and rest. Indeed, just as God, creator of all things, is read to have completed all his work on the seventh day and to have rested, so not labor but eternal rest will be imposed upon the just in the heavenly sabbath. Therefore, if we desire happily to come to the heavenly mansions, let us rest in God alone, in whom the rational creature is perfected by resting; perfected through the adherence of love, it rejoices with the Lord in ineffable union and joy. Henceforth, in order to praise God, let pure devotion seek nothing outside God, since almighty God himself, perfect and most wise, suffices for all as the matter of his praise, and exceeds all praise by his infinite perfections. For Scripture says: Praise the Lord as much as you can; he will still surpass. For he is greater than all praise; and therefore he is to be praised by us most humbly in himself and for himself rather than from creatures.

From the aforesaid considerations about God there arises a manifold and varied affection of devotion, through which also a more worthy effect of prayer is generated according to the desire of the affection. For when meditation of the heart is taken up concerning the majesty or power of God, reverent wonder is produced.
