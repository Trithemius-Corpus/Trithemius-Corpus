Therefore those who wish to make progress in this region of labor have need of the greatest courage, because no one can resist his temptations to the end unless he strives daily to do violence to himself. But whoever thinks that he has come to this region should not presume on his own powers, for just as the grace of God, in order to work the good in us, requires the choice of our will, so the will necessarily requires grace. For no one is holy by his own powers. Therefore let no one dare to presume on himself, but let him who stands take heed lest he fall. The life of man upon earth is warfare, for through many tribulations one comes to the kingdom, and heaven is acquired by the arduous labors of the virtues. “Going,” says the prophet, “they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves.” Indeed, those who sow in tears shall reap in joy. But if anyone fails in the sowing, certainly he will not rejoice in the harvest. Since, therefore, through the continual labors of spiritual exercise the mind must be cleansed from the dust of the first region, do not seek leisure in this middle region; do not love the repose of negligence, because no one is able to stand here except the one who courageously girds himself for continual labor. Whoever in this middle region does not always advance toward better things soon falls into the negligences of his former life, and there incurs the danger of failing where he has lost the desire of making progress. This is the place of continual combat; whoever neglects to fight in it falls back to be conquered. *The preface ends.*

*Begins the second region of the soul, which is called rational, in which men are occupied amid many labors.* Therefore the second region of the soul is called rational, in which the mind is exercised by continual labor for the inquiry into the highest truth; the farther it separates itself from the lowest, the more it approaches the highest. And this region is proposed as being likened in certain ways to the ethereal heaven, in which the position of the stars and continual motion are found.

For just as that motion itself, by the order of the first mover as cause, is always arranged in heaven, so continual labor is at work in the middle region, so that each intellectual power may perfect its own object and in no way depart from the order naturally implanted in it. Now such powers of the soul are three: memory, understanding, and will.

From these, memory gathers the likenesses and phantasms of things, and extends itself concerning what is arduous. The understanding, by discerning those phantasms, considers them, and here investigates the true. But the will embraces what has been approved by the understanding and ordered, and this is concerned with the known good. When these three powers of the soul rightly preserve their order, they lead the spirit in faith toward heavenly things.

This second region is compared to the ethereal heaven, in which, just as there is continual motion and the position of the stars, so in this middle region there is continual motion and labor against the battles of vices. And just as the motion of the stars fulfills the order of office implanted in it by nature, so each power of the soul, when it is subjected to its own object, is perfected toward the end ordained for it, so far as can happen to wayfarers. But none of these powers can be perfected simply to fullness in this region, since no one in this foreign [unclear] is so perfect that he cannot be more perfect in his own order. For even if memory, in its own object, which is the arduous, could behold the gathered likenesses and phantasms of all creatures, it would not be satisfied unless it could apprehend their principle, which is God; but this is altogether impossible in this region, in which we can form no phantasms or likenesses of God. The understanding also, the counselor, as I might say, of the blind will, extends itself concerning the true; but however much it considers its own object, it is not satisfied until it can apprehend every truth and arrive at the knowledge of the eternal, highest, and infallible truth without the opinion of fantastic speculation.
