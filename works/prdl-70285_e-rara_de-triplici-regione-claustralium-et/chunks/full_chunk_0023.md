For the lowest region always sends its citizens to the lower world, unless, led by penitence, while living they have taken care to renounce dead works. The second abode is called purgatory, and this is compared to the second, rational region. For just as purgatory purges and perfects souls, and sends them, once purged, to the kingdom of heaven, so this second region washes its citizens who persevere in it, through continual exercise, from the filth of sins, and disposes them for the ascent to the third region, which is called spirit. In this abode all are good, yet still to be purged so that they may become better; and in the second region nowhere are there evil persons, yet they are always to be exercised so that they may be made better. This region sends its citizens who die in it to purgatory, since, although all are good, nevertheless they do not yet seem fit for the kingdom without a temporary purgation. Indeed, those who inhabit this middle region, although they seem to be very near the highest, nevertheless have not yet been wholly removed from the dust of the lower one. But the third abode of separated souls is called the kingdom of heaven, and this is compared to the highest region, that of spirit. For just as in that heavenly fatherland there is highest rest and eternal peace, so in the region of spirit there is continual tranquility of mind. *Here ends the first region.*

*Preface to the second treatise of the first part, which deals with the middle region, namely the rational region of cloistered persons.*

Let us now at last leave this dark region of carnal persons, in which the whirlwind of crimes remains, and the continual tempest of punishments. We have granted it enough, and we have lingered over it more than had been our purpose. Let us therefore leave the dead to bury their dead; let us yield to the darkness; let us hasten toward the light of the spiritual life. We must go out from the Egypt of the shadowy region; we must hasten diligently, through the middle region of reason, to the highest summit of spirit. Let us now, then, at last approach the middle region of the soul, through which one comes successively to the highest. This region of the soul is quite suitably called rational, in which there is continual labor for seeking out the highest truth. The fervor of mind strives always to raise the citizens of this middle region to higher things; but the weakness of the flesh, by the motion of a raging whirlwind, struggles to draw them back to the lowest things. From this arises continual labor; from this arise sighs and groans, because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling depresses the sense that thinks many things. This is the narrow way that leads to life, and few walk through it. In this region violence is imposed upon the concupiscences of the flesh until the will is subjected to the spirit at its nod. For the Lord says in the Gospel: The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent seize it. In this region the war of combat is declared to the servant of God; so the wise man testifies: Son, he says, when you approach the service of God, stand in fear, and prepare your soul for temptation. How great the temptations are in this middle region we learn daily through the difficulty of spiritual exercises, while, driven in various ways, we are not able to remain long in the fervor of spirit.
