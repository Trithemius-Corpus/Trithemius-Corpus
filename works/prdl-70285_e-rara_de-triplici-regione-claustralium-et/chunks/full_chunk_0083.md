*Fourth, you ought often to pray in the cell.*

The holy apostle admonishes us to persist in prayer without interruption, because, just as breath is always necessary for us for the life of the flesh, so continuous prayer is altogether necessary for the health of the mind. For just as by readings we are instructed toward good, so by prayers we are freed from evil. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: Pray, that you may not enter into temptation. For prayer refreshes the hungry mind, extinguishes the fire of carnal desire, instructs morals, and drives away demons. If you wish always to be with God, always pray. Let your prayer be pure, simple, and clean; let it be poured out to God through the fervor of love, and it will not be able to be unfruitful.

*Fifth, you ought to rest properly in the cell.*

He rests best who commits no evil. He rests well who despises all the occupations of the world. Be quiet in the cell, so that you do not wander this way and that with any thought; be pure and tranquil in mind, so that you may deserve to attain savory knowledge. For Saint Prosper says in a poem: Wisdom imbues a tranquil mind, empty of cares, and will be the guest of a peaceful breast. And again he says: Those who are quiet can scrutinize the law in some measure, if hearts free from the noise of the world are at leisure. Therefore, so that the mind intent upon pious studies may be aided, let divine love overcome carnal quarrels. Therefore remain quiet in the cell, and by loving you will be able to know the truth. For the mind will not be able to be tranquil and receptive of divine wisdom when the body is restless. Hence Hugh says: In the dormitory be quiet, lest another be disturbed through you. But waking or wishing to sleep, let the memory of God’s goodness always occur to you with thanksgiving, because he himself always watches over us, so that he may guard us, humbly serving him, from every evil.
