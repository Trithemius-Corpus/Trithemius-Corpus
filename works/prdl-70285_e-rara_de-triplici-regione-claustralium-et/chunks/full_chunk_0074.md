Grant me, sweetest Jesus, constancy in adversities, patience in tribulation, discipline in conduct, and true humility always and everywhere in my mind. Let him also pray for his pastor; let him pray for the whole congregation, that the Lord may mercifully direct the former in his will, and, guarding the latter under regular observance, may defend it from every adversity.

Moreover let him pray for the universal Church; let him pray for the faithful and for unbelievers, so that almighty God may preserve believers in the faith and convert those who do not believe to the Catholic faith. Let him also pray for the conversion of useful persons to the order, through whom both his monastery may advance in each state and the whole observance of the order may grow for the better. Let him not forget the faithful souls in the pains of purgatory, awaiting the mercy of the Creator; but, praying for them, let him know that after a little while he will pass over to them, where the prayer now poured forth will return into the bosom of the one who prays.

But let the devout minister of Christ take care that he not neglect, on any occasion, any of the things that belong to his office, nor render the celebrant distracted by his prayers or disturbances. When there is leisure he will be able to meditate or pray in silence; when he must minister, he ought to interrupt at least vocal prayers, and, ministering diligently to the priest in all things, respond.

At the elevation of the most sacred body of Christ, he will be able, after the example of Saint Thomas Aquinas, to say attentively to Christ: “You are the King of glory, O Christ; you are the everlasting Son of the Father,” as far as the end of that hymn.

After the elevation, let the devout monk turn himself to the Lord Jesus, desiring from his inmost heart to have fellowship in so great a mystery, and let him prepare his affection as though he were going to communicate; for as often as a devout lover of God should desire from the fervor of his heart to receive the body of Christ, he is believed to have received the power of the sacrament. For the Lord Jesus said to a certain soul friendly to him: If a man fervent in my love, from desire, should wish to receive my body a thousand times in a day, if that were possible, so many times in my sight, through the power of that same body of mine, he would truly obtain his desire in his soul. And therefore, once this desire has been conceived in the mind of one who loves Jesus, let the monk pray to the Lord in this or a similar manner.

*Prayer of the One Ministering.*

Would that, most loving Lord Jesus, I were so well and fervently disposed that it were permitted me now to receive your most holy body in this venerable sacrament; would that it were permitted me to repeat this holy communion several times in a day. O my sweetness, O sole delight of my heart, O my hope, Lord Jesus: you know the ardor of my mind toward you, and before you is all my desire. I ask you, by all your goodness, grant me to receive the power and grace of this most holy sacrament now and at all times in my soul, so that I may be able to remain continually in you.
