[He] cannot ascend the [first?] step of humility; when will he be able to reach the others? For the second is never ascended if the first is neglected. Therefore he who neglects one ascends none.

The pursuit of sacred reading, however, must not be undertaken for the vanity of the world, nor for the pleasure of a seduced mind, because reading never profits toward compunction when the delight of the flesh or of vanity corrupts it. A monk, moreover, cannot attain devotion through the pursuit of sacred reading unless he strives to change all curiosity into a simple spirit. Therefore whoever desires to advance from the reading of the Scriptures toward compunction, whoever [desires] to ascend to humility, must choose [unclear], but only that which can more fully stir the mind to the love of God. For this purpose the reading of [unclear] seems to me sufficiently suitable, because by memory it is often recalled to love of him. We also willingly admit miracles and the repeated torments of punishments or joys of separated souls, in conformity with the Catholic faith, by which we have found that sluggish hearts are often stirred to compunction. Finally, the passions of the holy martyrs greatly kindle the fervor of devotion; in them, while we bring back to mind the unconquerable charity of Christ, moved to compunction by the example of such great constancy, touched as though by a certain fire of love, we sigh over our imperfection. From reading the material of meditation is taken, and as the former has been, so the latter will be. Not the shameful songs of poets, not the curious doctrines of philosophers, not the swelling writings of rhetoricians: these rather choke the fervor that must be kindled. If, therefore, you wish to persist in good meditations, do not occupy your mind with readings other than holy and devoutly known ones. For indeed not even one [unclear] is generated from study. Let him who studies for the knowledge of this world read those things that suit him for erudition; but let him who seeks compunction think upon those things that kindle the soul to the love of God through fear.

Frequent reading, therefore, should take its beginning from the last things that await us; then let the deeds of the holy fathers be read, so that the memory of future things may shake the mind to the fear of God, and the exemplary life of the saints may kindle the desire of imitation. For the wise man says: Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever. The life of the holy fathers is set before us as an example, and the destruction of the wicked is recounted to us for our instruction, so that virtue rewarded may move desire, and iniquity punished may forbid the license of sinning. Therefore the devout cenobite should always recall to memory the examples and virtues of the saints; he should consider the brief pleasure of the wicked, but their eternal torment, so that by fearing unhappiness he may deserve to escape it, and by insisting on their virtues obtain their perpetual joy. The distinct condition of the good and the wicked is no light matter, since each is described as destined to remain forever and is bounded by no end. O eternity, always to be feared by all mortals, in which after this life there is no fear of consummation for the righteous, and no hope of deliverance for the perverse. Let him who can consider what eternity is; let him who dares imagine an end, and when in eternity he has found nothing other than eternity, let him understand how nothing is all the time in which he lives in this world. For indeed, once the spirit has been stripped of the flesh, it is necessarily compelled to enter the abyss of eternity; and the condition that it has once obtained according to its merits [unclear] without end. Whoever shall have departed once from this life without the recompilation [unclear] of penitence will remain to be tormented without end with the demons in hell. From these meditations fear [enters] the mind, and little by little one comes to love. But so that we may be able to lead the cenobite successively through these roots to charity, it pleases us to explain briefly, in order, the steps of humility described in the Rule.
