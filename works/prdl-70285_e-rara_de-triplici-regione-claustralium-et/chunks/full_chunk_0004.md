But whoever has neglected the exercise of daily instruction falls into a deep torpor, as the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: If you do not hold yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown; and this is understood as a good conscience. The Rule of our father Benedict seems sufficient for a monk, since it is agreed to contain all the material of exercises, both spiritual and bodily, necessary for monks for the preservation of regular discipline. And therefore all the exercises of monks must be humbly subjected to the constitutions of the Rule, because whatever is prescribed either by us or by others for the instruction of monks is all assigned to the observance of the monastic norm. For the whole sum of our religion, which is acquired by the continuation of spiritual exercise, is contained in compendium, as in its root, in the Rule of monastic tradition. Therefore every exercise of monks, whether spiritual or bodily, by which the mind is led from love to a humble lowering of itself, is conformed to the ordinances of the holy Rule; and the more it is known to present a monk as good and humble, close to God, the more it opposes the studies of contemptuous pride. The holy mysteries of this exercise direct the mind of its worshippers, by an invisible teaching, toward the love of God as toward its due end along a secure path; they wondrously acquire the grace of inward devotion; and they cause the monk to despise all earthly things as nothing and to burn sweetly at all times with the lower spirit. The animal man does not receive this wholesome exercise of devotion; the vain and curious monk does not understand this study; the proud man does not admit this knowledge, to be preferred to all things. Happy is he who learns to penetrate the secrets of this exercise; happy is he who has deserved to receive this gift of holy compunction from the Lord, for whoever has once perfectly obtained it will fear no adversities of this world. But that exercise which, lacking its due end, does not direct the minds of cloistered men to the observance of the commandments of the Rule does not infuse the holy spirit of discipline, because where the obedience of subjection is despised, no gift is accepted by almighty God.

For it is written: Obedience is better than sacrifices, and to hearken is better than to offer the fat. And although many little works of spiritual exercise, composed by various persons, are found, nevertheless, because not all things suit everyone, it has seemed good to us also to publish something in conformity with the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, by which we may be able to stir ourselves up more greatly and more nearly to the purity of regular observance. Therefore we divide the present little work, collected from the exercises of the excellent father Theoderic, once abbot of the monastery of Bursfeld, into two principal parts. The first of these is set in the consideration of monastic institution; the second is occupied with the exercise of the spiritual life. In the first you are ordered to know yourself; in the second, to raise the mind from earthly things to heavenly things. Therefore, approaching according to our powers the purpose of the consideration, we divide the first treatise into three parts, according to the threefold state of cloistered men, of whom we may call the first sensual, the second rational, the third spiritual. From this threefold state of men we take a fitting title for this work, which we call *On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Men*. In these three regions the whole crowd of monks is included, as will be said in what follows.

*On the first region of the soul, in which carnal monks dwell with beasts: disordered by many passions, living sensually in the lowest region.*

The first region of the soul, therefore, is called sensual, lowest, and animal; in it are contained the roots of all evils. Men set in this dark region share with beasts according to their kind; indeed they observe, so to speak, beastly manners, while they yield either nothing or little to reason, but concede all things to sensuality like beasts. For in that lowest region three sensual powers of the soul preside.
