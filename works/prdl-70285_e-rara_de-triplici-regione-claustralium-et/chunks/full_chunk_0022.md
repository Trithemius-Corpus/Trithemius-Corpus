This is the far-off region, bestial and of unlikeness, into which that prodigal son went, who squandered the portion of his inheritance luxuriously with harlots. In this region dwell carnal citizens; concerning such people the holy Apostle declares: The animal man does not know the things that are of God.

In this dark region, covered with the gloom of death, no spiritual good inhabits it; in it not peace but bestial discord reigns, as the prophet says: There is no peace for the wicked, says my Lord.

*Concerning those who, being near to this lowest region, more often relapse into it through inconstancy of mind.*

Although between the first region of the evil and the second of the good there is, properly speaking, no middle, nevertheless there are some who often move between the two: while through weakness they are now lukewarm, now through contrition of mind they seem to be devout. These are weak human beings. Although they are not submerged in the lowest region, nevertheless through negligence they are very near it, and indeed frequently fall into it through bad habit, since they do not withdraw as far as they can from the affections of the flesh. Concerning them Bernard says in a certain sermon: Pernicious lukewarmness, because it is dangerous, because it is most troublesome and full of misery and pain, and plainly nearest to hell, is rightly reckoned the shadow of death. Indeed one may find faint-hearted and slack people, failing beneath the burden, needing rod and spurs; whose joy is brief, whose sadness is faint-hearted; whose compunction is brief and rare, since their thought is with animals, their way of life lukewarm; whose obedience is without devotion, speech without circumspection, prayer without intention of heart, reading without edification. Finally, as we see, fear of Gehenna scarcely restrains them; shame restrains them; reason scarcely bridles them; discipline scarcely holds them in check. If people of this kind remain long beneath the burden, either they are crushed and succumb, or in a certain way they are in hell, so that they never fully breathe the sweet yoke and the light burden.

And so such pernicious lukewarmness flows from this, that their will has not yet been purified, nor do they will the good as they have known it, being drawn away and enticed by their own concupiscence. For they love in their flesh little earthly consolations, whether in word or in sign or in deed or in some other thing; and if they sometimes interrupt these things, they nevertheless do not break them off completely. Hence it is that they rarely direct their affections toward God, and their compunction is not continual, but occasional. But the soul that is subject to these distractions cannot be filled with the visitations of the Lord; and the more it is emptied by those things, the more fully it will be filled with these. If much, much; if little, little. Or perhaps, if you approve this more, those things will never be able to be mixed with these for eternity. For spirit and flesh, fire and lukewarmness, do not dwell together in one house, especially since lukewarmness itself is accustomed to provoke the Lord to vomiting. Whoever, therefore, desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the region of the spirit, should take care always to repel from himself the torpor of threatening care, and in holy purpose to burn continually through the fire of divine love. Nothing harms those who wish to serve God so much as lukewarmness of mind, which always arises at the beginning from some small negligence; but unless it is immediately cured, it increases successively and wonderfully. But one placed in the neighborhood of the lowest region often relapses into the same, because the dregs of his former carnal way of life still subtly occupy his will, and easily, on some occasion, disturb the weak fervor of devotion. But no one can rise from the depth of hell with a lukewarm will; no one can overcome a habit without a contrary habit, because unless a certain violence intervenes, habit will always strengthen the vice of sinning. This lowest region is rightly called hell, because by a certain likeness of disturbance and aversion it is compared to hell. After this life there are three dwellings of souls, which receive citizens emigrating from this world out of the three regions. For the first dwelling is called hell, and this is compared to the lowest region.
