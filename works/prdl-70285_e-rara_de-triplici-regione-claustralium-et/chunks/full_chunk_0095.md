*Here begins the compendium of spiritual exercise. Johannes Trithemius.*

If the formula of spiritual exercise, published by us for the edification of cloistered persons, could seem very brief to all the devout who have leisure, nevertheless we fear that to those occupied with household business it may seem rather prolix. For while their thought is stretched out with continual solicitude in arranging the necessities of their subjects, the mind is easily wearied even by very brief spiritual exercises. Therefore, lest our monks who are either given over to exterior matters, or less devout in spiritual conversation, might be able, on the occasion of prolixity, to flee to the argument of their customary excuse, we have been ordered to abridge the same instruction, so that the fervent may keep the greater one in their devotion, and the lukewarm may not omit the lesser.

*There follows the practice of spiritual exercise.*

Before all and each of the Hours in general, let the devout cenobite strive to direct his effort to this end: that in his heart, by the root of His benefits, he may desire with all his strength to magnify God. Let there be in him so great a most fervent ardor of devotion, so great a compunction, and finally so great an overflowing of tears, that whoever sees him chanting psalms may be moved by his example to magnify the Lord. Secondly, let him remember that he can be stirred to charity toward his neighbor, because love is directed toward God in vain if the neighbor is hated. Therefore, when all the thorns of displeasure have been driven out, let the most devout cenobite, turned toward the Lord, pray.

¶ *Prayer*

Almighty, everlasting God, who, instructing us for the exercise of Your love by the manifold creation of things, commanded us to love You above all things with all our strength, and our neighbor as ourselves for Your sake: graciously grant me the grace of Your most holy love and of love of neighbor, so that, all motions of displeasure, aversion, and anger being repressed, I may always love You above all things with a pure heart, and may in no way depart from sincere love of my neighbor; so that, just as a member adhering to a wounded member suffers with it, so my mind, sincerely loving my neighbor, may deserve to be a member of our true head, Jesus Christ. Who lives and reigns with You.

¶ *Another Prayer.*

I beseech You, holy Father, almighty and eternal God, through the virtue of the most holy passion of Your only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ: enlighten the eyes of my heart, so that, pierced in spirit, I may see how often I have offended against Your commandments; and, cleansed by Your mercy from every stain of sin, may never offend You hereafter.

*After Matins.*

When Matins have been finished, while still remaining in the church, or even when placed in your cell, take up for yourself the disposition of true resignation, praying thus.

*Prayer*

O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who at the morning hour, placed in the greatest anguish of Your most holy heart, after threefold prayer most fully resigned Yourself into the will of God Your Father: grant me, in the virtue of that charity of Yours, perfectly to contemn my own will in all things for love of You, and truly to resign myself at all times into the disposition of Your will and of those whom You have set over me. Who lives and reigns.

*After Prime.*

After Prime, when, leaving the choir, you enter the chapter-room, in memory of your most innocent Savior accused before Pilate, you will form your disposition toward humble confession, praying thus to the Lord.

¶ *Prayer.*
