Third, disorderly monks or abbots misuse the goods of the monastery when they indulge them in unlawful uses. Theft and sacrilege are committed together when the goods of the monastery are converted to illicit uses. For the substance of the monastery, dedicated to God, ought to be guarded like the vessels of the holy altar, lest it be dissipated in any way through negligence. But he who forbids the useless expenditure of cloistered goods would by no means, without sin, permit them to be lavished on base uses. What will the monks and rulers of the lowest region answer to the lawgiver in the strict examination, who do not fear to dissipate the goods of the monastery on parents, kinsmen, mimes, actors, and base persons, as an occasion and reward of sin?

Fourth, carnal abbots and monks sin more gravely in the goods of the monastery when they gather them too greedily, to the detriment of divine charity and of salvation. For made subject to the law of avarice, they think of nothing but profits and money; they violate feast days and do not observe them; they always desire to increase temporal goods; they do not notice the losses of charity. They grieve more over a dying beast than over a perishing sinner. So truly have all disorderly cloisterers been blinded that they consider avarice prudence and think riches to be reform. They join field to field; they call profit piety; and where they see temporal goods abound, there they say the best discipline is observed. They refuse to spend even a few things for the common good of the order, because they have truly lost the order of charity. They raise useless, curious, and damnable structures on high; they keep costly garments and ornaments scarcely necessary for divine service; they clothe sacred things with gold; they leave Christ’s poor naked. This greedy curiosity does not befit monks, since we are commanded to imitate the holy men of old and are ordered to sell these things for the support of the poor. But a covetous monk and an avaricious abbot are as far from the purpose of holy religion as the devil is from heaven.

Fifth, carnal monks sin in the goods of the monastery when they do not repay them with prayers. They all cheerfully receive the alms of the faithful, but few render the suffrages of prayers for the givers. From these things many citizens of the lowest region, when they have come to the order, serve not God but the belly; they desire that more food, drink, and clothing be given to them than necessity requires; and those who in the world could scarcely have bread and vegetables in [unclear] now, having become monks, refuse eggs and little fish. They importunately demand that great, varied, diverse, and delicate fish be given to them; murmuring through desire of the flesh with the children of Israel in the desert, they cry out for [unclear]; they say that they cannot eat eggs and peas; they always desire more delicate things.

*In what way the citizens of the lowest region are badly ordered toward contempt of the world.*

*Article VII.*

The kingdom of God suffers violence, and the violent seize it. Therefore whoever desires to enter the heavenly kingdom by the arms of holy obedience, whoever chooses to live for God under regular conversion, must do violence to the pleasures of the flesh and strongly contemn the world with all its vanities. But the carnal monks, citizens of the lowest region, who are badly ordered not only toward adverse things but also toward spiritual things, are also badly ordered toward contempt of the world.

And first, indeed, they are infected by concupiscence of the flesh, for they always burn with the fire of pleasure. In food and drink they are found excessive; in beds and baths, contrary to the rule, delicate; they prefer the desires of the flesh to the salvation of the soul; they do not seek the sweetness of inward conversation. They nourish their flesh too delicately, through which they are continually set aflame in various desires. For no one can truly contemn the world who neglects to restrain the appetite of the flesh even in the smallest things. If you eat to satiety, you do not truly contemn the world. For the first commandment imposed on those who contemn the world is grounded in abstinence alone. But if you fill your belly with food to satiety, you immediately fall either into lust or into severe sloth.
