Yet all that time during which something of the mountain remained, however very long it was, would nevertheless seem, relatively, to be nothing in comparison with eternity. For eternity admits neither the imagining of an end, nor the hope of completion, nor a limit.

O therefore how foolish, how senseless, how mad is the man who casts aside this consideration of eternity, and spends uselessly the acceptable time granted to him for meriting eternal goods. The hour will come, and already hastens near, in which the wretched man will learn what eternity is, when he will no longer be able to do better. But you, my monk, who desire to be faithful, cast away from yourself all anxiety for the world. Set all your care toward the love of God. Do nothing, think nothing, except what can lead you to the love of your Creator, or what you know pertains to the edification of others or to the pure necessity of the body. Separate yourself, I pray, from the lowest things, so that, being good, you may be delighted by eternal things. Let no anxiety of the flesh remove you from the inward tranquillity of mind; let no pleasure corrupt you. Strive always, as much as you are able, to persist in prayers with devotion, and again, through the affection of love, to raise yourself more frequently.

*On Always Having Zeal for God.*

*The Seventh Root of Fear. Root VII.* The monk is commanded to exercise himself in this no little.

For no one who truly loves his lord tolerates with equanimity injuries done to him. You therefore, monk, who desire to be a faithful servant of Jesus Christ, do not neglect zeal for him within yourself. When you see an injury to God, do not conceal it, because whoever does not at once resist those who disparage him is proved to love his lord less. Let men know you as such a lover of God and enemy of vices that they do not dare to speak evil before you, nor presume to do anything evil while you look on.

Be conspicuous in uprightness of soul, renowned for the merit of your life, venerable in zeal. Let the righteous love you as a servant of God; let the reprobate dread your face as a judge. Let no one presume to do anything in your sight that is alien to good morals, since indeed all know that you cannot tolerate an injury to God. Thus Phinehas merited the everlasting priesthood, when by zeal for the law he powerfully appeased the wrath of the Lord. If you see order perishing in the monastery, and if your brothers have begun in any way to corrupt regular observance, do not, if you are a servant of God, keep silence about their vices. Set yourself firmly against them, with strength, for the house of the Lord. Exercise zeal for God as much as you can; destroy the counsels of the malicious; resist the rashness of the proud. If you are a true lover of God, by your face alone you will scatter the gatherings of the depraved. For no one will lay bare his counsel before you if he suspects your zeal to be opposed to him. But if the evil and reprobate, while doing evil, do not fear your presence, assuredly they recognize no zeal for God in you. Therefore, that you may show yourself a true servant of Christ, you ought willingly, for love of God, to forgive your brothers the injuries inflicted on you, and to beseech God for those very persons who injure you. But it is not yours to forgive injuries done to your Lord without his will, but rather to avenge them out of zeal for uprightness. In these matters let love of blood cease; let the bond of friendship give way far off. Zeal for God is rightly to be preferred to all things. For friendship with man is to be observed in such a way that it is never preferred to zeal for God.

Woe to monks and abbots who so boldly cast aside zeal for God; who put familiarities with men before the divine command; who conceal the vices of their subjects through foolish compassion; who observe the traditions of the holy rule neither in themselves nor in others. And what shall I say? We see among cloistered monks that obedience to commands is despised, zeal for uprightness scorned, observance of the rule contemned.
