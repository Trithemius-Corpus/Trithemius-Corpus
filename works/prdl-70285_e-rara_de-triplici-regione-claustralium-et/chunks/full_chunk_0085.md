Have compassion, therefore, as much as you can, on the desolation of the faithful souls groaning in the prison of purgatory, and pour out diligent prayers for them to the Lord. For even if, by his justice, the almighty and merciful Father delays them from the kingdom, nevertheless he loves them with inestimable charity. Therefore whatever good you now do for them by praying, you offer as a pleasing sacrifice to almighty God. And when you come there, you will receive multiplied whatever you have done here for them in charity. For Scripture says: It is holy and wholesome to pray for the dead, that they may be loosed from sins.

Pray for the dead.

When, however, passing through a cemetery, a precinct, or another place of burial of the dead, do not forget your brothers and all the departed, but, moved by charity, have compassion on them, and strive to aid them before the Lord in this or a similar manner.

¶ Prayer

Hail, faithful souls; rest in holy peace. May he who redeemed you with his precious blood deign to free you from punishments and lead you to eternal joys, where you may rejoice and exult forever, blessed ones.

¶ Another

May God the Father, who created you, bless you. May Jesus Christ, who redeemed you with his most precious blood, bless you. May the Holy Spirit, who by his mercy and gift preserved you from eternal damnation, bless you. Amen.

*Read the following prayer often*

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, Son of the living God, by the pain of your wounds, and by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on the souls of all the faithful departed who are in the punishments of purgatory, and give them a dwelling-place of eternal light with all your saints.

After vespers until compline.

Consideration VIII.

After vespers or the vigils of the dead, return to your cell with the customary prayers and inclinations, and there, on bended knees, give thanks to your most merciful preserver, because he has not only mercifully snatched you from the vanities of the world, but has also guarded you in the order from many evils and dangers. Then think over how many bodily and spiritual goods the loving Father has bestowed on you, though you deserve nothing; and, what is greatest, that to you, faithfully persevering in his love, he has promised himself as a reward. Be grateful, and always watchful in prayer, so that you may sweetly burn in the love of God.

But when the signal has been given for supper, prepare yourself humbly to go to it, and observe what we noted above concerning dinner. After supper you may return to your cell and devote yourself to prayer or meditation, until the signal is struck for the collation.

At supper, when you have heard the signal for the spiritual collation, strive at once to go to it with gravity, and to hear the final reading of the day with fervor of spirit. For the word of holy exhortation which has been heard last will cling more tenaciously to the mind throughout the whole night. But if it is on fast-days, the collation is customarily observed in the refectory while drinking. Therefore be attentive to sacred reading in every place, because where the word of holy exhortation is gladly received with attention, it cannot happen otherwise than that the mind is often lifted up.

At compline.

Going to compline, beseech the Lord that he may always make you despise all worldly and carnal things, and grant you to remain constantly in his most holy love, so that, when the course of this present life has been completed, you may be worthy to enter the temple of God not made by hands, and to rejoice forever with all the saints in the Lord.
