Thirdly, many cloistered persons of this lowest region are disordered toward God through neglect of the service owed to him: they serve their own affections and carnal desires in various ways, and always come to the service of God with a distracted mind. Of these, some come lukewarm to choir without any devotion; others come unwillingly, compelled by necessity; certain men, scattered by outward thoughts, are present bodily at the divine offices, but in mind wander outside through various workshops. Others do not fear to seek out false excuses by which they may, as it were lawfully, absent themselves from choir. Now they feign severe pain in the head; now they mendaciously allege this, now that; and while they deceitfully withdraw the service owed to almighty God, they incur the reproach of eternal malediction owed to the negligent.

Fourthly, there are very many disordered toward God through the working of habit: even if they sometimes escape the malediction of the negligent, nevertheless they never approach the fruit of eternal devotion. For they come to the service of God from habit alone, nor are they moved to any good work by devotion. Others, placed under grave necessity, obey the commands of superiors quite unwillingly, and fear temporal penalties rather than the judgment of God. These carry the cross of the Lord with Simon under compulsion; like the heifer of Ephraim trained for threshing, they persist in good works by habit alone. But those do not do good well who come to the service of God with an intention not duly ordered. They also perform the deed of the just without the reward of the just, who in good and just works do not take up a good will.

Fifthly, the citizens of the lowest region are disordered toward God through the vice of ingratitude, which is the greatest crime of a disordered will. And indeed, he who is found ungrateful to the Lord for benefits received is often, by his own deserving, exposed to being scourged by various and most grievous temptations.
