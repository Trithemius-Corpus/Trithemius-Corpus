Then “Glory to God in the highest” is said in the singular number, in which we confess the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one sole true God in the Trinity of persons and the unity of substance. Consequently, during this angelic hymn the monk ought to be very devout and, so to speak, individually attentive to each word; in it the gift of peace is promised to men of good will, and God is humbly adored by us in various expressions.

*At the Collects.*

But when the collects or prayers are uttered to God by the priest, the monk, standing bowed, should think of nothing else, but, listening to the words of the priest, should present his own desire to God in agreement with them.

*At the Epistle.*

When the epistle is read, you ought diligently to attend to everything, and with the greatest fervor of spirit give thanks to God, because, leaving the Jewish people behind, he mercifully willed to call you and your forebears from Gentile error to his faith.

*At the Gradual. Alleluia. Sequence.*

*At the Gradual.*

Or at the tract, consider how long a desire the holy fathers had, who before Christ’s incarnation longed for his coming. Afterwards reflect with how many labors and injuries your Savior was wearied for thirty-three years in this world for your sake, he who voluntarily gave himself to death for your salvation. Then call to mind the long and perilous exile of your soul in the flesh, so that through this meditation on present misery you may be able to inflame your affection more fully toward divine love.

*During the Gospel.*

When the holy Gospel is read, with mind lifted up, exulting in the Lord, give thanks, because the merciful Father willed you to be born of Christian parents, and because he made you a sharer in his holy and most sweet teaching.

*During the Creed.*

When the symbol of the Catholic faith is sung, consider all the words and mysteries with humble fervor of spirit. Hasten at once to recollect yourself wholly within yourself, so that you may be able to give thanks to him who called you to the faith without any merit of your own, and [unclear] to raise yourself through love of the Spirit. Think, if you can, or rather as much as you can, how great, exalted, and immeasurable is the Son of God, who willed to be incarnate for you; how hard and bitter were the things he endured for you.

*At the Offertory.*

At the offertory apply your mind, together with the host to be consecrated, in offering; weigh what is done there mystically. For the host is round and white. In its whiteness are set forth the purity, innocence, and suffering humanity of our Savior, to be offered to God the Father on our behalf against all our impurities; in its roundness his immeasurable divinity, set before us as a reward, is offered. Therefore say to the Lord:

*Prayer.*

I have no worthy gift to offer you, almighty God; as a sacrifice I hand myself over to you. Therefore, holy Father, receive me, in union with the most holy offering of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, as an eternal gift to you and as a perpetual servant; and give me the continual fire of your holy love, that I may be able always to render you service pleasing to you. Through the same.

*Prayer.*

I offer to you, almighty God, the life, passion, and death of the same our Lord Jesus Christ: for my sins; for the holy Roman and universal Church; for the supreme pontiff; for our bishop; for our abbot; for all my brethren; for the unity of our observance; also for our whole order; for the salvation of all the faithful; for the conversion of unbelievers; and for the redemption of the souls of the faithful who are in the pains of purgatory. I beseech you, holy Father, receive this offering of mine in union with that charity with which you received the same our Lord Jesus Christ as he died for us on the cross, and may it be pleasing to you.
