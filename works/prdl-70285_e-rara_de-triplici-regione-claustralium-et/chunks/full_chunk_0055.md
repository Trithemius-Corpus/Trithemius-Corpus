For they are carried toward God with such fervor of love that, lifted above their very selves, they very often abandon all intellectual operations and, borne into the abyss of divine contemplation, admit absolutely nothing except God; and, existing there insensibly through the grace of love, they become one with God by the gift of participation, while the property of the natures remains preserved. For the holy apostle of Jesus Christ says: “He who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit.” And again he says: “Those who are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.” The same apostle also shows himself to be a citizen of this most holy region, when he cries to the Philippians: “Our conversation is in heaven.” For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord; but, as if through a dream, we are sweetly led to our homeland when we dwell in heavenly things by mind and spirit. I would say “through a dream” not properly, but by a remote comparison. For in dreams we go forth by images of things intelligible to us, the forms being preserved in the imagination; in contemplation, beyond every imagination, we are raised pure to God in mind and spirit. And therefore this supreme region of the soul is called spiritual; from it all religious obtain the name of spirituality. Woe to us. From this most blessed region we are called spiritual; we are named religious lovers of God, we who nevertheless desire to attain the summit of this mountain through contemplation. In this region charity holds empire, wisdom the consulship; in it nothing is loved except God, nothing is done inordinately. This most holy region belongs to the empyrean heaven, because those whom it spiritually receives as citizens it will send on to the joys of heaven without intermediary. For just as the lowest region of the flesh will send its citizens, when they die, to hell, and the middle region of reason to purgatory, so this one will send its perfect worshippers, when they depart, to heaven. The dignity of this third and supreme region is expressed by many prerogatives and is especially commended in seven articles, as will be said in order.

*That This Supreme Region Is the Cloistral Spirit. Article 1.*

And first, indeed, this third region of the soul is described by a pleasing comparison, because it is compared to the highest empyrean heaven. For just as all things are tranquil in the empyrean heaven, so on this summit of the supreme region the mind of a man [contemplating?] God is agitated by no disturbances. Here is the highest rest of the contemplating soul; here no crowd of vices dwells; no bitterness of mind comes near. In the empyrean heaven, indeed, the majesty of God is beheld by the holy spirits by an ineffable gift; here, in darkness, through the desire of love, it is seen above understanding. There God is all in all; here nothing among all things pleases except God. There it is granted to the saints to rest from their labors without end; here the mind is permitted, by resting, to foretaste the unspeakable sweetness of inward delight. There is no felicity greater than that in heaven, and there is no tranquillity in the world so sweet as that in this region. Blessed indeed are those who have deserved to ascend this mountain of divine contemplation: they love absolutely nothing in this world; they desire nothing other than always to cleave to God. O how secure is the conscience of such people, how joyful their manner of life, where no evil is committed, where nothing other than that supreme, immutable good is desired. For the mind purified by divine love, which has truly and [fully?] ascended this region, desires nothing carnal, nothing inordinate; it raises itself wholly into contemplation of the highest majesty through desire; it rests sweetly in God alone.
