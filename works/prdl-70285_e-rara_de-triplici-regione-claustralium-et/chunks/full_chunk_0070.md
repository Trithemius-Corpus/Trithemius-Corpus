*At the Second Signal.*

When the second signal sounds, both for Prime and for the remaining hours of the day, let the manner set down above for Matins be observed, insofar as the time allows each one to pray. But when our cenobite enters or leaves the church, let him not neglect to show reverence to God before the altars and the crucifix, but, humbly bowing, let him say the fitting prayers according to custom. For the modest bowing of the body is held to be a sign of the inner man well ordered toward God.

*In the Chapter-Room after Prime.*

As the devout cenobite goes after Prime into the chapter-room, let him remember to give thanks to God for the victory of the holy martyrs, whom he knows to have served as soldiers for the Lord, in the greatest fervor of spirit, despising the torment of their own [bodies?], through the witness of death. Therefore, when you hear the brave deeds of the saints read, pray to the Lord that by their merits he may deign to forgive you your sins, and mercifully strengthen you to bear the yoke of monastic discipline, so that you may attain fellowship with them. And this desire is set before you to take up when, after their passions have been read, divine help is invoked three times by all. We do these things also at the beginning of the day, so that, strengthened to patience by the examples of the saints, we may fear no adversities throughout the whole day. But when you hear that some martyr stood bravely in the struggle of the contest, you will pray to God in these or similar words.

*Prayer.*

Holy soldier of the eternal King, I beseech you by the love of our Lord Jesus Christ, who made you overcome the torments of the flesh and the treacheries of this world: pray for me, that I may deserve to come as quickly as possible to fellowship with you.

If the name of a confessor, or of a virgin, or of a saint of another condition, has been recited, the devout lover of God will easily vary the prayer ex tempore to any saint according to the ardor of his affection. For affective prayers are more effective than all others.

*At the Reading of the Rule.*

When the reader recites, according to custom, one passage from the Rule, let the devout cenobite say in his heart: Help me, most sweet Lord Jesus Christ, that I may be able to live according to this norm of your religious servant Benedict, and to persevere faithfully at all times in your most holy service.

Then let him consider that just as the Rule is read daily for the advancement of the good and devout, so for the lukewarm, the wicked, or the negligent it is recited to the increase of damnation, so that they may be without excuse. Finally, let him know that he is bound to faithful effort in all the things that the Rule contains, just as the tenor of his profession states. For thus he said in his profession: I promise obedience and conversion of my morals according to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

*When the Names of the Dead Are Recited.*

But when, after the reading of the Rule, the names of the dead have been recited, the monk will pray thus for these and for all other faithful departed, entrusted and not entrusted to him, no less out of charity than out of duty.

*Prayer.*

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, by the pain of your wounds and by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on these souls, and on all those entrusted to me in prayer, and on all the faithful departed, and grant them everlasting rest. For it is a great work of mercy to intercede before the merciful Judge for souls placed in the prison of purgatory, and to come to their aid out of charity through the remembrance of the Lord's Passion. For I firmly believe and with pious presumption trust that the prayer which is poured out for the dead from charity alone returns doubled into the bosom of the one who prays.
