Moreover, even by one mortal sin you have deserved to be the devil’s companion without end, to be deprived of all the joys of heaven, to be burned forever in the fires of hell. These are the rewards which sin has brought you; these the gifts which it promised.

Now therefore from these seven considerations make a plaster for the wounded soul; take up for yourself the goad of compunction, so that you may come to the root of fear. But hasten, and do not put it off from day to day, since he who receives the humble confession of the penitent has not promised tomorrow to the one who delays. For where haste finds pardon, delay often cuts off life. Hope of a longer life draws many to destruction, because, while they trust that they can live longer in this world, they make the time of penitence pass without penitence. But you, man, if you accept my counsel, do not defer penitence, since you do not know what tomorrow may bring you. For he deserves to lose the time of penitence who so often does not fear to defer his pursuit.

*On present vanity and eternity, the sixth root.*

The sixth root of fear is raised up in the mind of the one meditating, if the vanity of visible things and the eternity of invisible things are considered with zealous intention. For all things which we behold in this world are wholly subject to vanity and corruption, and indeed will last for a very short time. All mortal things hang upon uncertain chance, and by their own changeableness promise flight. Whatever you have today will perhaps leave you tomorrow; or even now, while you are speaking, it will cease to be yours. For we perceive nothing in this world long-lasting and stable, but see all things change with time.

Ascend now, therefore, the watchtower of your mind, O faithful monk, and consider how all things which time contains are nothing, since whatever visible thing is now perceived will soon be corrupted. Take from things past a consideration of things present, and see that what has passed away is not, and what now seems to be will not be after a little while. Where are the kings of the nations, where the princes, where the leaders, where the rich, where those once powerful in the world? Where are all the lovers of the world; where are the men who despised the arduous way of heaven? Finally, where are all those once known to you in the world, whom death meanwhile snatched away while you were in the cloister? Behold, from the origin of the world, both those who lived for many times and those who lived for few, all died in their own generation; and according to the diversity of their merits, with justice dictating, they also received diverse rewards. The lovers of God receive everlasting bright dwellings, filled with inestimable joys, associated with the creator of all; and the worshippers of the world, despising God’s commandments, once swollen here with honors, puffed up with knowledge, immersed in riches, given over to pleasures and vices, have descended to hell to be tormented without end. What harm, I ask, does that once laborious contempt of the world, contrary to the flesh, now do to the saints in heaven, for which they already enjoy forever the glad vision of God? Or what profit now to the impious buried in hell is the consolation of the flesh once possessed, for which they now receive the torments of eternal damnation? Think on these things while it is profitable; meditate on these things while the opportunity of taking care is present.

Again, ascending higher, try with the gaze of the mind, as far as you can, to fix your eyes on the abyss of infinite depth, and to consider how long that is which is eternal. Eternal is a time which is enclosed by no end, bounded by no space. The saints of God, blessed, will exult forever in glory; sinners, wretched, will burn forever in hell. O eternal name, terrible without end, who will be able to think on you or speak you without fear? Truly, whoever thinks on you never sins forever. The eternal has no end. Hear a comparison, and, since it will not be sufficient for eternity, receive fear.
