*In what way carnal monks, citizens of this lowest region, are disordered in many articles.*

Although the description of this lowest region is generally fitting for any carnal human beings who dwell in sins and crimes with beasts, nevertheless, so that we may attend to our own, we intend to speak of lukewarm and carnal cloistered men, doing no injury at all to the good and devout. For always and in all things we wish the conduct of good cloistered men to remain untouched and unharmed whenever we discuss the evil pursuits of the depraved. Therefore we rebuke evil cloistered men and depraved manners; honoring the good everywhere, as is fitting, we praise them. And what else than evil can we say of the evil, in whom we find no good things to say? In a holy order they live disorderly; they despise the traditions of the elders; they do not admit the words of salvation.

So great, indeed, is the disorder of the monks of the lowest region that, in my judgment, it would be easier to convert a publican than to recall a disorderly monk to penitence. This disorder of carnal monks we comprise in eight articles, although we are not unaware that they are disordered in many things. First, indeed, the citizens of this lowest region, beastly monks, are disordered toward God; next, toward their prelate; third, the prelate toward his subjects; fourth, the members toward one another; again, fifth, they are disordered toward themselves; then toward the goods of the monastery; seventh, toward contempt of the world; consequently, toward penitence for sin. These are the principal disorders of the citizens of the lowest region; these are the beastly affections of carnal monks, who dwell in the lowest things in mind and body, who do not hasten to advance toward the highest things. These disordered conditions of the lowest region, therefore, I shall take care to set forth briefly in order, and to demonstrate, as far as I can, how far carnal monks are from God. For, as the prophet says, salvation is far from sinners.

*In what way the citizens of this lowest region are disordered toward God. Article I.*

Therefore the carnal monks of this lowest region, mad inhabitants, are disordered toward God in many ways: they never feel the sweetness of his love; they admit no compunction of divine fear; in their affections they are beastly and swollen with pride; in their carnal pleasures they are unclean at all times.

And first, indeed, their disorder toward God takes its origin from ignorance, since, given over to animal passions, they do not care to have God in their knowledge, nor do they consent to think on those things that lead the soul to fear of him; but, blinded by evil desires, they vanish away in their thoughts. But what does Scripture say? He who is ignorant will be ignored. They do not wish to understand so that they may do good; they do not tremble before the face of the strict judge; without penitence they continually persevere in evil. Indeed, in the manner of beasts of burden, they do not fear to rot in their pleasures; they do not heed the patience of God calling them to penitence; they seek nothing other than the advantages of their flesh. At all times they walk in the greatest sins without any fear; they stubbornly refuse to know the ways of the Lord.

They think little of the future judgment, or not at all; they do not fear the punishments prepared in hell for sinners; the grace of compunction never pierces their hearts, for the love of God does not inflame their minds. They have no care for God; no dread of death. They obey the belly rather than God; at all times they desire to live according to the flesh; and [unclear] shamefully neglect their own salvation; they descend to hell with demons. Indeed, those who, forgetful of God, keep badly the vow of holy monastic life do not attain the rewards of heavenly life that are to be given to the just.

Second, carnal monks are disordered toward God by foolish profession, because they promise to live according to the Rule, yet do not truly care to fulfill this. And what is more disorderly than to promise holiness of life to the Lord God and to remain in the foulness of one’s former life? Whoever conducts himself in a monastery otherwise than the monastic Rule teaches is a mocker of almighty God, not a monk.
