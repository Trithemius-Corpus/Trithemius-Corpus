Moreover, in this second root we have arranged meditations on the passion of the Lord for each of the hours of the seven days of the week; we have placed them after the end of the third region for this reason: lest in this place we disturb the order of the things to be said by their excessive length. For what we have now set out, we perceive to pertain rather to reading than to meditation, and therefore we postpone those meditations.

*On Meditation on the Benefits of God. Root III.*

The third root of divine fear and love is fixed in meditation on the benefits of God, through which one comes to the grace of devotion. For the beginning of devotion is placed in meditation on the benefits of God, from the remembrance of which love is generated. The middle, as progress within that meditation itself, desires nothing other than to please God above all things, from whom it knows itself to have been anticipated, without merit, by many benefits. But the end loves God above all things with burning desire and pure fervor of mind, and longs to cling there continually to him alone, without intermediary. And although merciful God has conferred many things specially on each one of us, nevertheless, because we are writing this our [synthema] not for one person but for many, we must set forth not particular benefits assigned to individuals, but general benefits allotted to all, repeatedly and at length. Therefore we comprise the more important ones under the number seven, so that through the seven days of the week, or even in the canonical hours themselves, we may have ready at hand material for meditating daily. But if anyone cannot ascend to that summit of theory, let him strive to exercise himself more fervently in these meditations on the benefits of God, so that, while he diligently continues in the lower things, being purified he may ascend stronger to the higher. Therefore let the cenobite devoutly [remember?] what he has received, so that he may be able to show himself grateful. Let him render to God what he has received, if he longs to be good.

For first he gave us body and soul with their adornments; and those whom he created intellectual, in his own image, he also mercifully called to the heavenly kingdoms. What more could he have given us, who, after creating us when we did not exist, gave himself? But what can we render to our Creator, who by our own merit are sinners and weak? Let us render what we are; let us render what we owe: namely, faithful service for the body that we have received from the Lord, and sincere love for the soul by which we were created in the image of God. For thus the prophet admonishes us in the psalm: Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice in him with trembling. Serve faithfully; love reverently. On the part of the body, offer faithful service to your Creator; and on the part of the soul, love him with all your strength. Serve him because the Lord is powerful and great; love him because he is good. His power is shown in your creation; his goodness is declared in your restoration. Since, when you did not exist, he created you by his power, he deserves faithful service; he restored the fallen by his goodness, he deserves the sincere affection of love. For truly he is our Father through love; he is Lord through incomprehensible power.

Second, he gave us faith with the sacraments; for just as he created us without our will, so he called us to the faith without our merit. For Christ says to his disciples in the holy Gospel: You did not choose me, but I chose you. Through faith the Christian soul is led back to its beginning; the sacraments that he gave, we are bound to maintain with perpetual adherence and to remain continually in his holy covenant. For in baptism we renounced the devil and all his pomps; we promised that we would adhere forever to Christ, those who lifted us from the sacred font standing as sureties for us before the Lord in the faith of our parents. Therefore let us always say from the heart with the prophet: It is good for me to adhere to God, to place my hope in the Lord God.

Third, almighty God gave us the art of rectitude and the teachings of truth. For these we owe our Creator faithful execution and perpetual service without fraud.
