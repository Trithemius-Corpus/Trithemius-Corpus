Therefore, if you desire to fly up to the mountain of contemplation, you must, by loving God most fervently, pass wholly beyond yourself. But if you truly love God, you despise both yourself and all things except him alone. For he is truly the treasure of the soul; if anyone has obtained him through love, he will always think of Jesus. This treasure is acquired by loving; no one understands its usefulness who has not received it through love. Whoever desires to have this inestimable treasure should continually fix his heart in love of sweetest Jesus. Truly, he who loves has; he who has loves. The mind that has deserved to have this treasure of the love of Jesus abides continually with him, in affection, in this highest region.

*That this third and highest region of the soul enjoys the greatest freedom of spirit. Article VII.*

Seventh, this most holy region of the soul abounds in great freedom of spirit; in it the spirit, free from the passions of the flesh and united to God through love, reigns. For the holy Apostle says: Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom. And again: Whoever are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God. Truly, the spirit abiding in this region is made free from every defilement of flesh and blood; stripped also of every phantasm of thought, naked and alone, it is lifted into the darkness of divine contemplation. This is the true freedom of the soul: to rest in the fervor of divine love, and to love no consolations of this world. Therefore let a man consider himself and carefully examine his love; and if he finds himself devoutly affected, frequently raised up to divine love by fiery desires even without preceding thought, let him trust that he can laudably be enrolled among the citizens of this region. For the Spirit of God bears witness to our spirit, and the mind that he raises into the ray of divine love he also causes to know the condition of its state. This freedom of soul is acquired through the fervor of divine love, because the Holy Spirit inhabits the heart of one who loves God, and grants true freedom to those who love.

In this most holy freedom divine wisdom is learned; the sweetness of divine goodness is fore-tasted with inestimable sweetness; the mind, made most free, is carried away above itself; resting in the silence of the divine darkness, it is lulled to sleep. And this is called mystical wisdom, consisting in the mind raised up and purified into God, which is hindered by no obstacle of any thing whatever from its effective progress. Ascending into this region, the mind, kindled by love, wholly abandons thoughts and images; it becomes alone, naked, and most free, because thoughts, on account of the office of memory, are compelled to remain in the middle region, nor can they in any way approach the highest region of the spirit, which is above every understanding of man. For one ascends here through love by affection and desire alone; the phantasms of thoughts are driven far from here. For as soon as the mind begins to ascend through thoughts, it falls back, thrust down into the middle region. Nevertheless, the ascent itself is begun through the operation of the intellect; when that soon fails, it is consummated through affection. For the intellect stirs up desire in the love of the highest good; as far as it can, it commends his beauty. But when the affection, stirred up through contemplation of love, has seized upon such beauty, the intellect, failing from amazement, falls silent. But the mind, unable to endure the company of the intellect, alone and naked, and most free from every image of thought, enters the dark mountain shining above; it contemplates the beloved, not indeed face to face, but in an enigma; it fails because of the greatness of love; caught up, it rests in silence.

Once Moses, leader of the Israelite people, called by God, is read to have ascended the dark mountain alone; he is recorded to have forbidden the people access to the mountain under threat of death. In the darkness the Lord appeared; through Moses himself he gave the law to the people. For thus it is written in Exodus: But the whole of Mount Sinai was smoking, because the Lord had descended upon it in fire, and smoke rose from it as from a furnace. And the whole mountain was terrible, and the sound of the trumpet gradually grew greater.
