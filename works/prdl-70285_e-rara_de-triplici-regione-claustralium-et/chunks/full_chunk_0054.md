*Preface to the third treatise of the first part, which describes the highest region of the spirit of cloistered persons.*

At last, as I am about to write, with the Lord’s help, of that highest region of the spirit, I am shaken by fear and shame alike, because, being small, I fear to speak of the greatest things, and I am much ashamed to teach what I have not sufficiently experienced. For what intellect, however practiced, of any man could apprehend the state of a mind sweetly resting in God through love? Or what speech, even of the most eloquent orator, could explain the secret sweetness of inward contemplation? Indeed, the exercise of this third region surpasses human understanding for the greater part; in it the mind of a purified man, through the fervor of divine love, is caught up above itself, and, while its natural essence remains, is absorbed, lulled by wondrous sweetness, in the abyss of divine contemplation. For it is overwhelmed by such sweetness of inward majesty that it seems unable either to understand or to express its own state. For, having abandoned sensory operation, it wholly passes beyond even the intellectual; and, what is more, abandoning itself, without imagination, reason, or understanding, it is lifted by affection above itself to that highest summit of the divine darkness.

But when it has utterly failed away from itself, it does not act but rests; nor, when returned to itself, can it understand what it has undergone, nor reveal to anyone the manner of its elevation. For this elevation of the mind into God is so profound and so sublime that not only can one who has not experienced it not understand it, but not even one who has experienced it can fully explain it in words as it is. But what shall I be able to say about the mountain of divine darkness, which I have not yet deserved to penetrate? For the well of inward contemplation is deep, and I am weak, since I have brought neither a vessel suited to these waters, nor prepared steps [of ascent?] suited to steep places. Well-arranged words do not befit a fool; nor should an unskilled and cold man speak of the secrets of divine contemplation, one who still stands with the weak in the valleys. For only he speaks fruitfully of deep things who is illumined by the double ray of the spirit. Yet since I am a man placed under obedience to the annual chapter, I am compelled to take up the mandate of that synod and to finish, according to my strength, the work set before me. Therefore I enter upon the third region of cloistered persons under the guidance of the venerable father, lord John of Bursfelde, the devout abbot, so that, by bringing back as far as possible to my own manner of speaking what he described in scholastic and truthful discourse, I may seem to have obeyed the chapter.

*Here begins the third region of the soul, which is called spiritual, in which the highest tranquillity of mind is possessed.*

The third, or highest, region of the soul is called spiritual; in it the highest rest and tranquillity of mind is found. And this region is compared to the empyrean heaven, in which there is the true rest of all and the true blessedness of souls. The spirit established in this region leaves the rational beneath itself, and, lifted up through love, wondrously ascends into God. For whatever is done in this most holy region is judged wholly divine. Here the human spirit, caught up above itself, receives the divine inflowing; and, lulled in the darkness of divine contemplation, withdrawn from all creatures, it sweetly comes to rest. In this region almighty God directs the spirit of the contemplative to himself, and perfects the simplified intellectual powers of the soul, recalled from every phantasm into the inner chamber, according to their tendencies and objects; and, leading the mind stripped of all multiplicity to himself, the one principle, he also makes it one in himself, not by nature but by gracious participation.
