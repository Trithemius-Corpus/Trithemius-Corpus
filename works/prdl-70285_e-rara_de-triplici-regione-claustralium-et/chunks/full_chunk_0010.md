But tell me, proud inhabitants of the lowest region, who always establish your own will, who usurp another’s right for yourselves: is this the virtue of obedience, to refuse to yield to the command of a superior, but to cling stubbornly to your own judgment? The holy father did not prescribe in the Rule that you should live thus; he commanded you in all things to follow the will of the abbot. For obedience is the greatest virtue of a monk, which our divine father wished to commend so highly that he said a good work done without the abbot’s will lacks merit. Hence, giving instruction, he concludes thus: therefore let all things be done with the abbot’s will, and let no one in the monastery follow the will of his own heart. But if the ruler’s prudence does not seem sufficient for the ordering of the monastery, that must be imputed to the carnal monks, who voluntarily set over themselves one who was unsuitable. Folly always hates prudence, and wicked monks detest a wise abbot. Those, therefore, who knowingly set a fool over themselves will necessarily be subject to his ordering and will. Finally, no insufficiency of the shepherd ought to extinguish the obedience of subjects, since the Rule commands us to obey even one who is useless. For it says: the abbot’s commands must be obeyed in all things, even if he himself, which God forbid, acts otherwise. But when we rebuke disobedience, carnal monks object: “We do these things rightly and with good intention, since we see the substance of the monastery perishing through bad governance.” But the Apostle contradicts you, because evils must not be done so that good things may come; nor, on account of loss in a temporal matter, is the subjection of humility to be abandoned. For when a choice arises between undergoing some loss of temporal substance and danger to regular obedience, it is better for the substance to perish than for the purity of obedience to be harmed. For God will not require monies from our hands, but souls. Indeed, whoever prefers [unclear] to obedience [unclear]. But the citizens of this lowest region, carnal monks, tear apart the virtue of obedience in various ways and attack the weapons of holy humility, so that it seems easier to me to train a new monk from the world than to be able to amend the sloth of these men. But from their fruits we plainly know what sort they are: namely, those who violate peace and charity in the monastery, disturb the concord of unity, sow discord between the brothers and the abbot, and stubbornly refuse to obey the commands of the Rule. These are the works of bad cloisterers; these are the signs of the lowest citizens, who, as most wicked defenders of their own will, cast away the weapons of holy obedience, live by the flesh and not by the spirit, defer either not at all or only slightly to the statutes of the fathers, and do not acquiesce in sound counsels; their condemnation is just. For just as it is impossible for a blazing fire not to rise upward, so it is impossible for a monk without obedience to penetrate the secrets of heaven.

*How the shepherds of this lowest region are disordered toward their subjects. Article III.*

Since we have shown that many monks of the lowest region are disordered toward their shepherds, shall we, as though sparing ourselves, not show the disorders of prelates toward their subjects? Neither does the name of abbot sanctify anyone, nor does the eminence of pastoral care save an undeserving man. Therefore, since many among the prelates inhabit the mountain of the lowest region with the beasts of the earth, they are by no means to be passed over by us in silence. For many rulers of cloistered men show themselves disordered toward their subjects: they not only neglect to feed according to the Rule the souls entrusted to them, but tear and kill them in various ways. But whenever anyone from the lowest region is taken up as a shepherd, the pursuits of the wicked are strengthened for the worse. Therefore a good man who loves God must be promoted as abbot: one who has long since overcome this lowest region, who has learned how to tame the passions of the flesh, who both wills and is able to ascend to that highest region of the spirit.
