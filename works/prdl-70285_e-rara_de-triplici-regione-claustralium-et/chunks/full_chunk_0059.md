For so long as the affection has not been purged of the passions of its sensuality, the beast still lives in him. But if the mind of man presumes to approach the mountain of God with its bestiality, it is overwhelmed by as many stones as it is wounded by movements of passions. For the wise man says: “The sluggard has been stoned with a miry stone, and all will speak in contempt of him.” By the miry stone carnal affection is expressed, because man is compared to mire. For in Job it is written: “Remember, I beseech thee, that thou hast made me as clay, and wilt reduce me to dust.” And again he says: “I am compared to mire, and I am likened to ashes and cinders.” Thus the sluggard is overwhelmed with a miry stone when the mind, still unclean with carnal and sensual passions, attempts to ascend the mountain of contemplation; there, as unworthy, by the hard stones set against it, it is forced to undergo repulse and ruin. For no one will be able to reach this most tranquil region of the spirit who has not first, through long exercise, been purified in the furnace of the middle region. Whoever, still given over to carnal passions, tries to approach this highest region of the spirit is as one striving to draw water in a sieve. Indeed, this region does not admit carnal minds into itself, since it is prepared for hearts purified from every affection.

*That this highest region of the spirit is made magnificent by a return.*

This highest region of the spirit is said to be magnificent by a return, and therefore it is to be sought by all with the highest desires. For that heavenly father of ours inhabits it as his own proper house, to which he diligently calls us, exiles and pilgrims, as to a paternal dwelling. Into this he mercifully receives us when we return with the prodigal son; through love he rushes into our embraces; he clothes us with the first robe; he grants the garment of charity to those who love. Why, then, do we neglect to return as quickly as possible to the most indulgent father, whom we see to be ready for our embraces even after contempt? We wretches departed through the rashness of our own will; we squandered the portion of our inheritance with the harlots of carnal affections; we have been shamefully stripped of every beauty and paternal adornment. Why do we not return to the father, whom we know to be ineffably merciful? Let us return at least, I beseech you, after fault, we who scorned to avoid ruin. Return, return, wretched Shulamite; return, grievously captive in affections; return, mortified in sorrows. Why do you delay? Why do you tarry? Return, O faithful soul, to the guide of your youth, Jesus; return to your former husband; go back to the most loving father who awaits you with the desire of love. For behold, he awaits you with patience and cries to you through the prophet Isaiah: “Return to me, because I have redeemed you. I have blotted out your iniquities like a cloud, and your sins like a mist.” What is this, O soul, that you are in the land of enemies? You have grown old in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been reckoned with those descending into hell. Remember that almighty God created you out of nothing; consider that he formed you in his image and likeness; be mindful whence you have fallen; see where, cast among swine, you are trampled under the feet of the unclean. Your creator placed you from the beginning in this highest region of the spirit; he entrusted to you the natural exercise of his love; he also bestowed upon you the hope of life that would always endure. But you abandoned the fountain of wisdom; you went out into the lowest region of unlikeness; you squandered the goods of the promised inheritance.
