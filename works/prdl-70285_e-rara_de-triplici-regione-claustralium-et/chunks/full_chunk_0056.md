Here it directs its gaze and its love toward that immutable good of lights; it utterly rejects from itself every created and creatable multiplicity; the soul gathers itself into one, so that, being one in itself, it may be able to rise without the impediment of multiplicity to the principle of light, which is one. Only unity can clearly attain to the one principle of things, because unity flees multiplicity; the one requires unity. For the more you have yielded to multiplicity, the more you withdraw from unity. Therefore God, for those dwelling in this highest region of the spirit, so that love may be able to rise in contemplation of its principle, is accustomed to renounce from himself every multiplicity clinging in imagination or understanding, so that, gathered in its own unity, it may be able to strive ardently toward that simple [unclear] with the affection of love, which it knows to be above every position.

But the spirit wounded by sins, which cannot raise itself to this region by a pure affection of love, is still said to stand farther away from the empyrean heaven. Those, however, who dwell in that most quiet region are nearest to the empyrean heaven, because, weighed down by no stains of sins, they despise all earthly and carnal things and always desire to dwell in heavenly things in mind and spirit.

Indeed those who inhabit the empyrean heaven and those who dwell in this third region have one and the same delight. But there is this difference, that for both there is sweetness in contemplating God: here it is great, there it is greatest; here in the desire of fervent love, there in the possession of most blessed vision.

In this highest region of divine contemplation the rational spirit recalls its powers and faculties into its chamber, in which it too, free from every phantasm, is at rest; it raises itself above itself into the dense darkness of the divine, in which, put to sleep by ecstatic love, it rests sweetly. No troublesome confusion of thoughts is stirred here, no imagining of phantasms; rather the naked mind is raised upward and contemplates without any thought or imagination. For truly, when the mind itself opens an entrance to thoughts, it again shows that it is descending to the middle region, which belongs to reason.

But since the human mind cannot always remain unmoved on the summit of divine contemplation, it is necessary that it often return to the rational exercise of the second region. If, however, it has slipped down into sensual passions, then again it descends to the lowest region. But the soul of the true lover of God, although on account of the frailty of the flesh it cannot always remain in the pleasantness of contemplation, nevertheless strives more often, through burning affection, to lift itself to that region, so that it may fulfill the exercise of reason in such a way that it does not wholly omit the sweetness of inward delight. O how good it is to cling to the Lord; how sweet it is to attend to God alone, so that the mind may thirst for the Lord Jesus before all things and always long to embrace him with burning desires. Indeed, one who stands in this region is nearest to the empyrean heaven, and therefore is inflamed toward it with the greatest desires of love.

O how many groans and tears the mind fervent with love is accustomed to send forth; how ardently it sighs for heavenly things; since the nearer it is known to be to the empyrean heaven while standing in this highest region through the secret of contemplation, the more ardent is its affection for leaving the prison of the body.
