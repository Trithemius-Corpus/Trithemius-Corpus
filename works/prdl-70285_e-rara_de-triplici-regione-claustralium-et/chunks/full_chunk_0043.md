*On meditation upon the aid of our restoration in the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

*Root II.*

The second root of the fear and love of God is generated in meditation on our redemption [unclear], which is produced through diligent exercise in the life, conduct, teaching, and Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. Among all meditations we believe this one to be more useful for us, in which we know our salvation and the sum of eternal happiness to be contained. And [unclear] that the Passion of the Lord, as to its procedure, was unjust; as to its manner, ignominious; as to its pain, bitter; and as to its effect, fruitful, and therefore it must always be kept in memory.

First, as to its procedure, it was unjust, because he who was born of a virgin neither contracted sin nor committed it, nor was deceit found in his mouth. For death is inflicted on us for sin; but he could not justly die, who without any sin was always just and innocent. Indeed, contrary to human law a man is killed if a sentence of death is passed upon one who has neither confessed nor been convicted of a crime. For the Savior was accused before Pilate on three charges: that, calling himself king, he forbade tribute to Caesar; third, that he made himself the Son of God. Of the first two the Gospel speaks, and the accusation is shown to be false. For regarding the first, the contrary was manifestly clear, since Christ said that his kingdom was not earthly. The falsity of the second also did not escape Pilate, who was not ignorant that Jesus had commanded that the things which were Caesar’s be given to Caesar. When asked by the governor about the third, he was silent, because Pilate was unworthy to hear of the divine generation of Jesus, or even of his human birth.

His Passion also stood contrary to custom, because it received no delay of time. He is not placed in prison according to custom; nothing is deliberated in his case; but once captured he is immediately dragged to the judge to be subjected to death; scourged, spat upon, and afflicted with many injuries, blows, and mockeries, he is nailed fast on the cross. Third, it was contrary to the law of nature, and procured from obstinate hatred of heart. For nature forbids raging against the dead, and shudders at lacerating with new blows a body already slain. For after the Redeemer [unclear] had died on the cross, he is pierced with a lance; and contrary to the law of nature, after death he is wounded by the impious.

Second, the Passion of Christ is said to have been ignominious as to its manner, for the six reasons that follow. First, because of the paltry price, since blood of incomparable price is sold by a disciple for thirty pieces of silver, for which no one would deign to sell his little ass. Second, because of the ignominious service, since, full of wounds, he is compelled to carry his cross while half-dead, [unclear]. Thieves and criminal murderers are punished for their own deserts. Fourth, because of the ignominious punishment, since he is hung naked on the cross, a penalty owed only to thieves and criminal men. Indeed, as Scripture says, the Redeemer of the world was condemned by a most shameful death. Fifth, because of the ignominious mockery, since he is hung naked on the cross before the whole multitude and is blasphemed by all as though foolish and insane. Of this the psalmist says: “All day long my shame is before me.” Sixth, because of the ignominious company, since, reckoned with the wicked, he is hung in the midst of thieves. What are you doing now, O Christian soul? Behold, your Savior hangs in the midst among thieves, and you do not fear to sit in the first place in council?
