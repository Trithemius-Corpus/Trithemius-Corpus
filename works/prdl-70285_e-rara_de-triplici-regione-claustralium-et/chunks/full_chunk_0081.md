*On Regular Refection.*

When the signal is sounded for refection, feel heartfelt compassion for the sick, the needy, and the poor who suffer want; and when you can do nothing else for them, beseech the Lord on their behalf. Consider that among Christians there are many far better than you whom harsh poverty oppresses in this life, to whom not even the food necessary for sustenance is offered at this hour, nor even the thinnest garments supplied to clothe the body. But you, though you are a monk and a despiser of the world, clothed in the best garments, sit at table like a prince; food is set before you to satiety by brothers better than yourself; all necessities are ministered to you, an unworthy sinner, by God’s servants, even to the point of nausea. Consider, therefore, what outcome this abundance of things will have in a monk, since you, who by entering religion professed poverty, now in religion feast more splendidly than the rich of the world. Take care, therefore, not to be ungrateful to God for such great benefits; live according to the rule which you have professed; pray for those who contribute temporal substance to your monastery, that almighty God may mercifully grant them eternal life.

*On Table Reading.*

When you are sitting at table, be composed while eating; do not look about here and there, but be intent on eating the food set before you only for nature’s necessity, with humility and thanksgiving. Consider diligently what is read at table, so that just as the body is refreshed by its food, so the soul may by no means be deprived of its own refection. Learn, while eating, often to raise your mind to heavenly things, and by the word of sacred reading, which is never lacking to us as we eat, sweetly to inflame yourself with divine compunction. For the cleric and inward worshipper of God, whenever he hears anything read, even at meals, about the bitterness of the Lord’s passion, about the fervor of divine love, or about the kingdom of the heavenly homeland, at once groans with excessive desire; his affection is kindled in the ardor of love; compelled by exceeding sweetness, the man sheds tears. For truly the mind that genuinely tastes God as sweet, as soon as it hears his name or looks upon the image of the crucified, is dissolved in compunction and is lifted by affection to his feet or his embrace.

*At the Thanksgiving.*

When refection is completed, give thanks to the Lord; and when, under the psalm, you go to the church, direct your mind upward; intercede for benefactors, living and dead. If after the thanksgiving you add some special prayer for the dead, you do well.

*On Regular Reading.*

Leaving the refectory for your cell, if it is not a fast day, fulfill the accustomed prayer; then read something good for edification; meditate, or do the work that has been assigned to you, or write. But if it is a fast day, take up the volume of your study and enter the place of regular reading; sitting with the others, read in silence what you wish. But if you are asked by the president to say something good, you will answer humbly with a few useful words and without curious citations. Use plain speech, and do not bring forward the vain curiosities of secular people; do not entangle the brothers sitting around you or your superiors in your discourses, but express the conception of your mind simply in few words, unless perhaps the president, for a definite reason, orders you to make a longer discourse.

*The Monk’s Exercise after Dinner until Vespers. Consideration VI.*

After the reading, when you have returned to your cell, you should always put some prayer before your works and seek God’s help in everything you do. But when you are in your cell, do not be idle; rather, do something good, especially what the obedience of the abbot has enjoined. Now write; read something good or meditate, because in his cell an idle monk makes either a beast or a demon. But of these things we shall speak in their proper place in what follows. Take care to spend the time usefully, because concerning all these things you are shortly to render an account to God.
