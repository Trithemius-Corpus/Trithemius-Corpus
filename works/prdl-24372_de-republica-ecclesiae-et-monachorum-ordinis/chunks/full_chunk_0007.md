Insofar as, with time, it has departed from its first institution, by so many steps, as it were, it has descended from Jerusalem to Jericho.

Just as once the order of our commonwealth was deservedly exalted above all other orders, when it nourished men mature, zealous, and most learned; firm in faith, strong in hope, radiant with twofold charity; pure in mind, distinguished in devotion, and adorned with the honorableness of all the virtues; in whom there was no care for earthly things, no enticement of pleasures, but, turned with their whole mind toward God, they despised the world with its vanities. From these men, on account of their outstanding learning and inestimable holiness, almost countless men are read to have been raised at different times to the summit of episcopal dignity; and the higher the place in which they had been set, the more brightly they shone forth in virtues and holiness. What church would not seek a priest from the order of venerable Benedict? What people devoted to the Christian name would not desire a teacher from the order of monks?

I have said these things not for the empty glory of our present commonwealth, but rather for useful shame. For just as the life of the saints is set before imitators for glory, so it is recited to the sluggish and idle for judgment. Hence I greatly fear that, while I have praised the most holy former state of our order, I shall be compelled, though with truth, to censure the present one, more than the patience of many will bear. But let the petty know-it-alls be angry, if they please, within themselves; let those puffed up by false righteousness be driven to fury: I would by no means dare to compare the order of our own age with the holiness of the ancients.

For our order flourished, as I had begun to say, of old with most holy men, who, living according to the purity of the Rule, became renowned for countless miracles. For, though placed in the flesh, they overcame the vices of the flesh, since, despising the pleasures of the world, they loved God with their whole heart. They were always burning in the love of God, continual in prayer and devotions, deep in meditation, lofty in contemplation, uniform in holiness of morals. In their way of life holiness shone forth; in action, purity; and in thought, integrity of mind. They were devout and most holy men: poor in spirit through contempt for the world, continent through frugality, humble through humility
