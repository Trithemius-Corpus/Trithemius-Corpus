Of them that saying of the apostle Paul is most fittingly understood: “They went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, needy, distressed, afflicted, of whom the world was not worthy; wandering in deserts, on mountains and in caves and in caverns of the earth.” The integrity of the monks’ life then deserved to be exalted, when by word and example it converted many from iniquity to righteousness. But even now the world is unworthy of such monks, since it spurns the love of God, rejects truth, and despises knowledge of the Scriptures. The Church too flourished in learning and holiness together with our commonwealth, so long as it received none but learned men into the government of souls, and admitted none but the devout. For devotion is not enough for the government of the Lord’s flock, if knowledge of the Scriptures is lacking. But now our monks and abbots are neither devout nor learned; for that reason they are judged unworthy for the governance of churches. What if I should say that the commonwealth of the Church at this time is unworthy of learned and holy rulers? For I think there is no one who could justly be angry with me on that account. For Scripture says of God, the supreme ruler: “Who makes a hypocrite reign because of the sins of the people.” Learned and at the same time devout prelates of churches are rare in our times. Why? Because they are chosen not by the Holy Spirit, but by men. And so it is. But enough of this; shall I proceed to what remains of our subject? I remember often having said formerly, [unclear] revered fathers, that two things chiefly made our order glorious, namely knowledge of the Scriptures and holiness of conduct. Of holiness we have made brief mention; it remains now that we say a few things about learning in the Scriptures. And, to begin from the origin of our commonwealth: from the beginning of our order down to the times of King Henry IV, as I have found from the catalogue of the ancients, many most learned men shone in it. For while Saint Benedict was still living in the flesh, many very noble and very learned young men submitted their necks to his rule, who were considered highly skilled not only in the divine Scriptures but also in secular letters.
