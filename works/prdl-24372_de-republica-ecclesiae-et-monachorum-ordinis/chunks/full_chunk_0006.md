Because, he says, iniquity will abound, the charity of many will grow cold.

Iniquity abounds no less among the people than among the clergy: while anger rages, lust runs wild, pride is mad, discord breaks the covenants of peace. Everything is full of crimes, and crimes are not kept out even by monasteries. Where now is the one heart and one soul of the multitude of believers? And where is the fervor of the Christian faith, where integrity of morals, where purity of deeds? And where is zeal for discipline, where observance of God’s commandments, where love of the heavenly mansions? O Christians, Christians, where now among you is the concord of brotherly love, where faith, where piety, where sincerity of hearts?

O holy commonwealth, where now is the appearance of your beauty, with which once adorned, like a very bright star, you shone above all the kingdoms of the earth? Where is your honor, where fear, where reverence for authority? It has fallen away; it has abandoned its former adornment; it has grown cold from the love of heavenly charity; it has withdrawn from discipline; it has spurned virtues; it has despised observance of the law. Behold, you have fallen among the robbers of crimes, upon the swords of wicked deeds, upon the arrows of vices. Behold, stripped and almost naked and left half-alive, you are despoiled inwardly of your former virtues and outwardly of temporal goods.

O you priests, you Levites, you prelates of the churches and guardians of the Lord’s flock, who see the desolation and ruin of your commonwealth and pretend not to see it: how will you stand before the tribunal of the just judge Jesus Christ, who will require from you a most exact account of the souls of all those entrusted to you? The arbiter of the work entrusted to you the care of his sheep; he will, do not doubt it, seek their blood from your hands. Woe to you. Placed to rebuke vices, you commit vices, and by your wicked morals you kill the souls of your subjects. O what will you answer the supreme shepherd at the last judgment, when he begins to seek his sheep from your hands? Behold, because of your sins the people has fallen, the commonwealth is being destroyed, the treasury of the Church is being scattered, preaching is despised, the granting of indulgences is contemned. But why should I use many words? From the sole of the foot to the crown of the head there is no soundness in our commonwealth. All seek the things that are their own, not the things of Jesus Christ.

For this reason the people of the Lord has been led into captivity: the empire of Constantinople and [unclear] captured by infidels; cities destroyed, churches burned, monasteries thrown down; Christians, from the little child to the old man, either destroyed by a hard death or sold into slavery with harder cruelty. Almost all Asia has deserted the Christian faith; Africa has withdrawn from Christianity; in Europe alone the Catholic faith, though weak, has remained. Alas, how narrow is the estate of the Church, which once with the greatest honor had been spread through the whole earth. Take up this lamentation over it, O pontiffs, priests, prelates, and rulers of the Christian commonwealth; and while you mourn the danger of others, fear for your own. For I am afraid, and I fear greatly, that iniquity, long dissembled, may at last come to be punished with a heavier chastisement. If indeed God did not spare the sanctified land in which, clothed in human flesh, he dwelt, how will he spare ours? Our desolation is nearer than we believe; destruction is closer than we fear. We foolishly presume upon security, because we see that so many Christian lands and kingdoms of ours have been captured by infidels.

But now I shall have said enough about the commonwealth in general, lest the shorter hour compel us to leave our own matter behind. There is also another commonwealth of laymen, which is called temporal; and although it too is now seen to be quite miserable in many respects, nevertheless, since it does not pertain to us, let it also be set aside from our discourse on this occasion. Now at last, reverend fathers, after a long discourse on the common commonwealth, which, persuaded by my elders, I have delivered as far as I could, let us approach our own commonwealth, which concerns us more. Therefore the commonwealth about which we intend to speak is our most sacred order, which follows the ruin of the ecclesiastical commonwealth and is also compared to it by a sufficiently close likeness. For just as that one in almost our own times has lost empire, kingdoms, and provinces, so too ours in recent memory has lost many and very abundant monasteries in these regions. But before we begin to describe its present state, it is pleasing to set forth a little what it once was like. And although I have done this several times in previous conferences, nevertheless I do not think it will be superfluous if we repeat more often what had been well said.
