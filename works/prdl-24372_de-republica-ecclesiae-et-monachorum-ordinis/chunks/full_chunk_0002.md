As I was setting about, reverend fathers in Christ, by reason of obedience to deliver a sermon before your paternities according to laudable custom—after I had long and much deliberated in silence with myself what matter I should bring forward that might more effectively rouse your minds to the piety of the order—I was at length persuaded by certain venerable men of your senate that I should treat, by the gift of the Most High, of our commonwealth, for the love, defense, and adornment of which you have gathered into this assembly from remote places of the earth. It is holy and wholesome, I confess, to speak (o fathers) of your commonwealth, both holy and religious; but [this befits] a learned, an erudite, a holy man, and one suited to persuading—such as I know myself not to be, who indeed am poor in talent, naked in merit, uncultivated in speech. How, then, should I dare presume to speak of so excellent a commonwealth, who am slow to invention, unpolished in speaking, cold in persuading? Another orator, therefore, fathers, another ought to have been sought out for you: one to whom natural talent would supply strength for speaking, erudition understanding for teaching, and for persuading no less learning than merit would befit. But why do I strive in vain, why am I compelled to obey? Although, therefore, the burden imposed far exceeds the strength of my talent, yet, trusting in the compassion of the Almighty and the support of obedience, I shall undertake the enjoined work not so much gladly as cheerfully. Nor will Plato be necessary to me here, nor Aristotle in his eight [books], nor Cicero in his six volumes on the commonwealth: for our Lord Jesus has, in today's Gospel, furnished abundant matter on this subject for our purpose to discuss, when he said: "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, who fell among robbers," and so on. Although this parable is principally to be understood of the perdition of the whole human race, yet nothing prevents us from interpreting it also (saving the faith) of our own commonwealth. For the state of the commonwealth—not only of ours, but of all religious, indeed of the whole Church militant—has fallen away from Jerusalem to Jericho.
