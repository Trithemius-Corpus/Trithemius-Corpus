Jericho, indeed, is interpreted as “the moon,” or its eclipse. Therefore he descends from Jerusalem into Jericho who, falling away from justice, approaches the source of vices. But before I proceed further to the exposition of our theme, since in all actions, especially theological ones, one must begin with prayer, lest we strive in vain we must implore divine aid for ourselves; without it the speech of the speaker avails nothing, and the intention of the hearer bears no fruit. Therefore, to obtain the grace of speaking and to secure the fruit of hearing, let us, with devout mind and sincere intention, bending our knees to the ground, reverently greet the most holy Mother of God, the undefiled Virgin Mary, saying: Hail Mary.

I would very much wish, reverend fathers, for the glory of almighty God, that such power of speaking had been granted me from above that, for your edification, compunction, and piety, I might most aptly be able to develop the theme taken from the Gospel of the present Sunday in accordance with the condition of our commonwealth. For just as that man descending from Jerusalem into Jericho was stripped by robbers, wounds having been inflicted on him, and was left half alive, so the commonwealth of the universal Church, so the condition of nearly all religious houses and orders, so also your commonwealth, fathers, lies stripped and half destroyed, with wounds of crimes, injuries of sins, and punctures of vices inflicted everywhere. And, to begin with the general commonwealth, which concerns all faithful Christians, who are reckoned as one through the Catholic faith: where is the condition of its former beauty? Where is faith? Where is love of God? Where is brotherly affection? See, we behold avarice, lust, pride, discord, anger, envy, and the other plagues of the soul abounding everywhere in the Christian commonwealth; with savage cruelty, in the manner of robbers, they tear apart the condition of the Church with most grievous blows, strip it, lay it bare, and kill it, so that, left half alive, it has no hope or beauty of its ancient loveliness. What will you do, O holy mother Church? Whom will you call upon as physician for your wounds?

A priest? But he passes by after seeing you; he scorns your anguish, despises your pains, and disregards your wounds. A Levite? But the minister of a bad priest is bad; he does not love your honor, he casts off care for you, he hates the healing of your wounds. A Samaritan?
