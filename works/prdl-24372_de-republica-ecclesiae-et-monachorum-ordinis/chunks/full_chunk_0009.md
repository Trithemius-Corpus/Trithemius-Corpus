He advanced so greatly in regular discipline that, being regarded as especially familiar with him beyond many others, he described the life of that man, now dead, in clear and elegant metre. Thus that most illustrious senator Cassiodorus, a man most learned in every respect, having despised senatorial rank for the love of God, became a monk in one of the monasteries of the holy father, and at last abbot.

But what shall I do? The day would fail me if I wished to recount by their own names all who, while our lawgiver was still alive, adorned his order with wonderful learning and holiness. In the succeeding time, however, there was among monks the highest zeal for the Scriptures, and an inestimable love of learning in every science, even secular. For those who came learned to conversion supplied the younger brothers with precepts for study. Hence it came about that they became most learned in every Scripture, since they persisted pure and devout in morals and conduct. For the study of secular letters is dangerous and seems deadly to the carnal monk who has not yet been mortified. Therefore a monk who wishes to learn the Scriptures profitably must begin from humility and amendment of life.

So our monks did in ancient times: to study in letters they applied an indefatigable cultivation of virtues. Such was Bede, priest and monk in England; such Alcuin, his disciple, a deacon; such Boniface the martyr and Rabanus, archbishops of Mainz; such were all monks truly learned.

What shall I relate about the other pillars of the Church? Gregory the Great, pope; Leander of Seville; Gregory of Tours; John of [Gerona?]; Caesarius of Arles; Hincmar of Reims; Haymo of Halberstadt; Aldhelm of the West Saxons; Radbod of Utrecht; Amalarius of Trier; Ansegisus of Sens; Remigius of Auxerre; Rather of Liège; Adelbold of Utrecht; Notger of Liège; Burckard of Worms; Guinomund of Aversa; Alphanus of Salerno; Peter Damian of Ostia; Lanfranc and Anselm of Canterbury, bishops and archbishops, who published almost innumerable books, shone in our order at different times no less by holiness than by erudition in the Scriptures.

I pass over the abbots and monks of our order, once most learned: Eugippius, Columbanus, Eutropius, Paul the Lombard, Regino of Prüm, Paschasius of Corbie, Smaragdus, Hermann Contractus, Rupert of Limburg, William of Hirschau, Rupert of Deutz, abbots, all of whom, learned and most holy, after the publication of many books, were also renowned for miracles in life. It would be long if I wished to run through even their names alone, who in our order shone forth wonderfully both in learning and in holiness.

Great devotion burned in them, an inestimable zeal for regular discipline, which never allowed them to be idle. Always in their studies divine love flourished, which, conceived from holy reading, called the watchful to prayer. For those who had exercised themselves more fully in the divine Scriptures, in the hours free from the service of God, gave attention to expositions of the same; they composed books and various treatises, by which they stirred the studies of their brothers toward the love of God. The other brothers were not free for idleness, but after prayers devotedly applied themselves to the work of their hands according to the rule. Those fit for it copied books for the adornment of the library, and from sheets brought to order works published by others. Some skillfully bound the written codices; others corrected them; others marked them off with red minium. All rejoiced to belong to holy labor. They were instructed in scholastic disciplines: some in grammar, others in dialectic, and others in other disciplines, each according to the force of his nature and talent. But once founded in the first sciences, they were called to the theoretical Scriptures. No place was given to idleness in the monasteries, while individuals applied themselves to their labors and studies with the greatest fervor.

They sent whatever little works they had composed to emperors, kings, princes, and prelates of cathedral churches, by whose favor and authority they came forth into the light more securely for our edification. Thus Alcuin dedicated innumerable little works to Charlemagne; thus Rabanus to Louis; thus Berno of Reichenau to Aribo of Mainz, and to Pilgrim, archbishops of this holy church of Cologne.
