For as all good things are from the first Good, so every love, toward whatever good it is borne, is naturally kindled by that same Good. For since our love cannot rest in any goods as in its end, it has as its end, where it is to rest, the same Good that it had also as its beginning. Therefore, as often as sin is committed against the beauty of God’s justice, the soul is separated from its own beginning. For since God is charity, he requires love in the soul; being peace, he requires peace; he loves what is like himself and hates what is unlike.

Second, consider the enormity of sin, and carefully weigh how greatly its foulness displeases God. Behold, because of pride the just God cast the most noble angel Lucifer out of heaven, and condemned him with his accomplices to the burning of eternal fire. He compelled our first parents, Adam and Eve, to leave paradise because of disobedience, and by a just judgment condemned the whole human race, in their crime, to the sentence of a twofold death. Finally, let us review the whole history of the Old and New Testament, and we shall find that what was rashly committed against God’s justice was often severely punished by that justice. Therefore the gravity of the crime is easily known if the severity of the vengeance that followed is considered.

Third, consider that grave scandal arises from sin, especially when one sins before others. For whoever by his own evil example provokes someone to sin shows himself to be an adversary of God. For God, in his mercy, has willed to save the souls redeemed by the blood of his only-begotten Son; but this man, through evil example, desires to destroy them out of aversion from God. Sin that gives scandal is truly said, with respect to guilt, to be most grave for this reason: because, as we have said, it is opposed to God. But if the one who gives occasion for harm is to be thought to have done harm, the sinner subjects himself to as many sentences of death as there are people whom he has caused by his evil example to fall into mortal crime. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Woe to the man through whom scandal comes.” And again he says: “Whoever scandalizes one of these little ones who believe in me, it is expedient for him that a millstone be hung about his neck and that he be drowned in the depth of the sea.” True repentance is rare in one who scandalizes the little ones, because he neglects to restore another’s loss and by no means reaches the fruits of saving repentance.

Fourth, consider the negligence of your ungrateful heart, because, created in the image of God, you ought to have loved your maker above all things, yet you dishonor him in many ways by your sins. To Christ, the Son of God, by whose blood you were redeemed, you ought to offer continual service of love; but you do not fear to love your flesh more than Jesus your Savior. You ought daily, with a compassionate soul, to revolve his most holy passion in your mind; and you are accustomed to think of nothing less than that. Adopted among the children of God by his merciful calling, you ought to have shown yourself an imitator worthy of the heavenly vocation; and still you do not fear to lie in the lowest things. And why do I say much? Behold: you, marked with the Holy Spirit, counted among the angels, are called to the inheritance of the children of God, are invited by all the saints to the feasts of paradise, and daily partake of the body and blood of our Savior Jesus Christ; and yet you do not keep the commandments of God and the rule that you promised, so that you might be blessed. Examine your conscience diligently, therefore, and you will find that there are many good things you ought to do and omit, and many evil things you ought to omit and instead commit against God’s good pleasure.

Fifth, consider whom you offend by sinning: not a man, but God; not a creature, but the Creator. Great is he against whom you have sinned, and immeasurable, terrible, and glorious; at his majesty the angels tremble, and at his power all the hosts of heaven marvel. To the supreme and unchangeable Good, God, the beginning and end of every creature, the rational spirit ought to offer love and the desire of cleaving beyond human understanding; it ought truly to raise itself with its whole being to touch that one Good as its own center, all phantasms despised, more highly; and it can lift itself up to him so much the more easily [unclear].
