At the elevation of the most holy body of Christ, following the example of blessed Thomas Aquinas, he may say attentively to Christ: “You are the King of glory, O Christ; you are the everlasting Son of the Father,” and so on to the end of that hymn.

After the elevation, let the devout cenobite turn himself to the Lord Jesus, desiring from his inmost heart to have converse with so great a mystery; and let him prepare his affection as if he were about to communicate. For as often as a devout lover of God, from fervent remembrance, desires to receive the body of Christ, he is believed to have received the power of the sacrament. The Lord Jesus said to a soul dear to him: If a man fervent in my love desired, if it were possible, to receive my body a thousand times in one day, so often, before me, through the power of the same my body, he would truly obtain his desire in his soul. Therefore, with this desire conceived in the mind of one who loves Jesus, let the cenobite pray to the Lord in this or a similar manner.

Prayer of the One Ministering

Would that, most loving Lord Jesus, I were so well and fervently disposed that it might be permitted me now to receive your most holy body in this venerable sacrament; would that it might be permitted me to repeat this sacred communion many times in the day. O my sweetness, O only delight of my heart, O my hope, Lord Jesus, you know the ardor of my mind toward you, and before you is all my desire. Therefore I beseech you: by all your goodness, grant me to receive the virtue and grace of this most holy sacrament now and at every time in my soul, so that I may be able to remain continually in you.

The Careful Preparation of the Priest About to Celebrate

When the priest is about to approach to offer this most sacred sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord, let him first examine himself diligently and most carefully consider of which region he is a citizen. If he still finds himself to be a cultivator of the first region, he ought not to approach the altar unless, by contrition and confession, with a firm resolve set upon amending himself, he first strives to cleanse himself. But if he finds himself a citizen of the second region, if he perceives that his powers have been reformed and ordered toward the cenobitic end, he can readily dispose himself to celebrate, since by daily increasing his former affection he has learned more frequently to lift himself upward through desire. For divine goodness quickly enters the affection of a person that is stained by no private desire.

Therefore, wishing to perform this most holy priestly office with devotion and fervor of divine love, let him first set forth some ardent prayers by which he may be able to stir himself to greater compunction. For devotion is rarely granted at the altar if the mind has not first been prepared for fervor. For this reason our fathers, desiring each one of us to advance in his own free devotion, did not wish to burden us, in the manner of certain others, with many psalms before approaching the altar: not because anyone ought therefore to approach without recollection, but so that each one might more freely devote himself in secret to exercises or prayers more suited to his own devotion.

Therefore, wishing to commend elevation of mind to the Lord Jesus for those about to celebrate before approaching the altar, we have placed below a prayer from that which is said to be of Saint Ambrose of Milan, which we believe to be sufficiently effective in view of the merit of the Lord’s Passion. Accordingly, the devout cenobite who is about to approach the altar, if he has leisure and freedom, will be able to use the following, or to set before himself a similar prayer, or to prepare himself in whatever ways he wishes.

Prayer Before Approaching

Highest priest and true pontiff, Jesus Christ, who offered yourself to God the Father as a pure, holy, and spotless victim on the altar of the cross for us miserable sinners; who gave us your flesh to eat and your blood to drink; and who established that mystery by the power of the Holy Spirit, saying, “As often as you do these things, you shall do them in memory of me”: I beseech you, through the pain of your wounds and through your most precious [unclear].
