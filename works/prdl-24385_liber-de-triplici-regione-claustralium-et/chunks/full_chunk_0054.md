On the mode and degrees of divine love: a collected conclusion of this second region.

Since no one suddenly becomes perfect, nor can anyone by his own strength suddenly ascend to that supreme summit of divine love, I now think it useful briefly to note certain modes or degrees of loving God. Therefore, for moving the human heart to divine love, the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is, in my judgment, more effective than all other things, if it is considered in a person’s mind purely, sweetly, fervently, perseveringly, and diligently. For truly, whoever often recalls to memory the life, Passion, and death of our Savior, with compassion and thanksgiving, according to their chief and principal articles, prepares a swift ascent of the heart to divine love. But we shall deal fittingly with this most holy occupation of the mind concerning the Lord’s Passion in the second principal part of this work, where we shall set out more fully the manner of spiritual exercise. As far as the meaning of what has been said in this place is concerned, I find three modes of loving God, which I shall briefly explain in order.

The first mode commands that you love sweetly him who loves you, and that you join your whole self to him sweetly, so that your heart loves nothing outside God and seeks nothing from elsewhere. For since God is the whole good, he alone desires to possess the whole heart of the one who loves him; he drives it far from displeasing thoughts. Therefore, if you wish to love sweetly, love totally. For either the whole of divine love perfects, or nothing does. Let meditation on the Passion of Jesus Christ our Savior possess you wholly, until love grows sweetly strong in you. You must despise everything that flatters in the flesh, if you desire to come to the love of God. For no one can sweetly love God in this life so long as he is convicted of loving anything in the world. Indeed, love of the world is utterly contrary to divine love, because where one is admitted to the inner chambers of the heart, the other is forbidden to be present.

But how much sweetness, how much delight, how much joy of mind the love of God produces in the heart of the lover, no one fully understands except one who, having despised all the things and events of the world, has loved God alone for a long time. The heart of one who loves God loves nothing that is in the world; it despises riches, honors, and the favor of the age; it does not fear to undergo any adversities for the love of its Lord. The love of God is never idle; it always desires to come to its own source; it refuses no labors to be endured in this world. Therefore let him who does not wish to become slothful love.

One’s neighbor also must be loved sweetly for God’s sake, and must in no way be offended against God or justice. Each person should always show himself to his brother, in words and deeds, such as he expects others to be toward himself. For he truly loves his neighbor who exposes his goods, his body, and his soul for him. This Christ taught; this the Son of God did for us. Indeed, whoever says that he truly believes in Christ ought himself to walk just as Christ walked. He does not truly love Christ who does not keep his commandments. For he says: “This is my commandment, that you love one another, as I have loved you.” We are members of one head, Jesus Christ; and then we believe in him when we observe his commandment concerning love toward God and toward neighbor. Therefore let us love our neighbor, because he is a member in Christ together with us, so that just as one member in the body suffers with another, so a brother may humbly be helped by a brother for the love of Christ.

The second mode commands you to love wisely, and to seek nothing in divine love except God. Stretch out your affection toward that Word which was in the beginning with God; lift up your mind above everything that is created, because unless you leave behind yourself and all things that are in the world, you will not be able to come to him whom you love. Finally, if you desire to love God wisely, do not think yourself to be anything else; for where a person thinks himself to be something, he already loves foolishly.

The third mode commands that you love strongly, and that you never fear to expose body and soul for the love of God. He is not a strong lover of God who is afraid to undergo death for him. For love is strong as death. One’s neighbor is loved wisely if it is not the person but the vice that is hated. But he is loved strongly if every injury is forgiven from the heart, by doing good, on the same day.

The second region ends.
