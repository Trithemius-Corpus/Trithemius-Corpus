For by the power of that one itself union is made; nor are things more alike more easily united to one another for any other reason than because in quality they are one. Therefore the soul is then most rightly said to be united with the one principle of things itself, when, having itself become most fully one within itself, it is separated from all multiplicity and, above all imagination, reason, and understanding, its own unity alone comes forth, enjoying through loving affection the unity of God, joining its center, so to speak, with the center of the universe. But when the human soul itself, suffering badly through the contagion, as it were, of the flesh, is turned toward any animal passions, then that very unity is badly changed into a multiplicity contrary to the one alone; it becomes as far removed from its principle as it has been divided from itself. The soul itself contracts great difficulty in returning to that one ineffable divine thing whenever, confused in itself, it falls through sin into apostasy. Think now how great and ineffable he is against whose honor sin is committed; and since you are utterly unable to understand the greatness of his majesty, learn humbly to fear his power.

Sixth, consider what you have lost by sinning, so that the recognized greatness of the loss may hasten penitence and restore through tears what was lost. What then, wretched man, have you lost by sinning? Hear what you have lost. Made like to God by grace, higher than the angels, a member of Christ, and an heir of eternal happiness, you had been chosen; you ought to have lived and rejoiced without end in the good things of the Lord with all the saints. But what happened when you sinned? You were compared to senseless beasts, and made like them. Since you were in honor and did not wish to understand, you abandoned the fountain of life; by sinning you became a companion and member of the devil, a whore of Antichrist, an heir of hell. You abandoned life; you chose everlasting death. If you fear the sentence, change your will, and you will find forgiveness.

Seventh, consider what you have deserved by sinning; for if eternal death is inflicted for one sin, think what a man deserves who has committed many, not to say innumerable, sins. For the soul that has sinned shall itself die. By one mortal sin you have deserved to be a companion of the devil without end, to be deprived of all the joys of heaven, and to be burned forever in the fires of hell. These are the rewards that sin has brought you; these are the gifts it has promised.

Therefore from these seven considerations make a plaster for the wounded soul; take up for yourself the spur of compunction, so that you may come to the root of fear. But on that account do not put it off from day to day, since he who receives the humble confession of the penitent has not promised tomorrow to the one who delays. For where speed finds pardon, delay often intercepts life. The sweet hope of a longer life draws one to destruction, because while a man trusts that he can live longer in this world, he makes the time of penitence pass without penitence. But you, man, if you accept my counsel, will not defer penitence, because you are utterly ignorant what tomorrow will bring you. For he deserves to lose the time of penitence who has so often deserved to defer the zeal for repenting.

On the vanity of the present and on the eternal future.

The root of fear is raised in the mind of one who meditates, if the vanity of visible things and the invisible eternity are considered with studious attention. For all things that we discern in this world are wholly subject to vanity and corruption, and are to endure for only a very little time. All mortal things hang under uncertain chance and, by their own changeableness, promise flight. Whatever you have today will perhaps leave you tomorrow, or even now, while you are speaking, will cease to be yours. For we perceive nothing lasting and stable in this world, but see that all things change with time.
