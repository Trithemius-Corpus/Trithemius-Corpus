In this life he is accustomed to reward with some special grace those at whose death the holy angels, as though meeting one familiarly known to them, are believed to come, and to present the soul of Jesus’ servant before his sight with joy. For thus the Son prays to the Father in the holy Gospel: “Father, I will that where I am, there also my servant may be.”

Great indeed in this venerable sacrament is the condescension of divine goodness. For, as the same Hugh says, God seems to do nothing less in it, when daily for our salvation he deigns to descend from heaven upon the altar, than when he descended from heaven and, having taken human nature, was made man. And just as our Lord Jesus Christ established this venerable sacrament for us in memory of his most bitter passion, as a sign of the greatest love, so he loves more fully and especially those who dispose themselves with humble devotion and love to receive it, or to minister it to those receiving it; and through it he helps them in every necessity when they ask faithfully.

For Jesus, reigning in heaven, will not be able to be harsh to us when we pray with full faith, since he was so gentle and kind to those who asked mercy while he walked on earth. Who ever came to his feet and was rejected? What sick person asked to be healed whom he, walking among men, did not cure? Will the Lord Jesus therefore be thought to deny mercy to us when we ask in faith, he who, while still mortal, was so merciful to those who prayed to him? For he says: “All things are possible to one who believes.”

Therefore, one who is about to serve at Mass should before all things take care to purify himself from every evil temptation and disordered affection, and should bring himself, as much as he can, to the desire of Jesus’ love, so that, purified, he may be able to stand before the Lord and pray effectively both for himself and for the whole Church, the living and the dead. Standing, then, during Mass behind the priest, at a suitable time let him pray in these or similar words.

Prayer of the server.

Most sweet and most beloved Lord Jesus, I beseech you by every anguish of your most holy heart which you endured in your passion; I beseech you by the shedding of your most holy blood and by the power of your most innocent death; I beseech you by the sacred mystery of your holy body and blood, at whose offering to you I, a useless servant, assist: have mercy on me and purify my heart from every unlawful affection, so that I may be able to minister worthily to you. Give me true contrition for every sin and forgiveness of my sins, the grace of holy compunction, continual remembrance of your passion, pure devotion of heart, a fountain of tears, purity of serene conscience, and the perpetual fervor of your most holy love. Give me, I beg you, most merciful lover of mankind, by the pain of your wounds, steadfastness in holy fervor [unclear], true contempt for all worldly things, continual desire for the heavenly homeland, and sure progress in holy religion. Grant me, most sweet Jesus, constancy in adversities, patience in tribulation, discipline in conduct, and always and everywhere true humility in mind. Make me, I pray, a partaker of this most holy sacrifice of your body and blood, so that my mind may feel the sweetness of your suffering, may burn sweetly in your love, and may not inwardly depart from you. Amen.

Let him also pray for his pastor; let him pray for the whole congregation, that the Lord may mercifully direct him according to his will, and may guard this congregation under regular observance and defend it from every adversity.

Furthermore let him pray for the universal Church; let him pray for the faithful and the unbelieving, that almighty God may keep believers in the faith and convert unbelievers to the Catholic faith. Let him also pray for the conversion of useful persons to the order, through whom both his monastery may make progress in each state and the whole observance of the order may grow for the better. Let him not forget the souls of the faithful who, in the pains of purgatory, await the mercy of the Creator; but as he prays for them, let him know that after a little while he will pass to them, where the prayer now poured out will return to the benefit of the one who prays.

But let the devout servant of Christ take care not to neglect, under any pretext, any of the things that belong to his office.
