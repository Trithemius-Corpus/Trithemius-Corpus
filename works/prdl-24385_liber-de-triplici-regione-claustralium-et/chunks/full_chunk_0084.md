On thinking or meditating about God you should allow yourself to fall asleep, so that you may sweetly be lulled to rest amid holy thoughts. For as your occupations have been while awake, so also will be your repose while asleep, and such also will your dreams be. For a mind burning with the single love of God, neither sleeping nor waking, will be able to forget its beloved, as the bride says in the Canticle: “I sleep, and my heart watches.”

At Vespers. Seventh consideration.

When the first bell for Vespers has been heard, and after the customary prayer, “Blessed be the name of the Lord,” has been said, while the others are permitted to busy themselves with secular affairs, give thanks to the almighty Lord, who has willed you to be free from them in order to serve him. Then gather yourself back within, or you may meditate on something concerning the Lord’s Passion, until the second bell sounds and you go to choir. For in my judgment there is no better meditation for beginners and for those making progress, toward compunction and devotion of mind, than that which takes its beginning from devout affections concerning the Passion of the Lord Jesus Christ.

At the Vigils of the Dead.

When the vigil has been reckoned after Vespers, you may recite the hour of the Holy Cross, and afterward think somewhat on the immense charity of God with which he loves faithful souls. Then, as much as you can, consider in how great desolation those holy souls are placed in purgatory, since it is a great pain for a spirit separated from the body and longing to return to the center of its own beginning: not to be able yet to behold the face of its Creator with full enjoyment, but to be forced to wait in pains and torments most grievous to itself. For so great is the desolation, sadness, and grief of a soul separated, placed in pains, and longing to come to God, that no kind of torments known to us in this world could be compared with its sorrow. For there is no comparison of bodily sadness and desolation with the state of a separated spirit, established in the delay of its return to its own beginning.

Therefore, as much as you can, have compassion on the desolation of the faithful souls groaning in the prison of purgatory, and pour out diligent prayers for them to the Lord. For although the almighty and merciful Father, according to his justice, delays them from the kingdom, nevertheless he loves them with inestimable charity. Therefore whatever good you now do for them by praying, you offer as a pleasing sacrifice to almighty God. And when you come there, you will receive multiplied whatever you have done here in charity for them. For Scripture says: “It is a holy and wholesome thing to pray for the dead, that they may be freed from sins.”

When passing through a cemetery or cloister, pray for the dead.

When passing through a cemetery, cloister, or any other place of burial of the dead, do not forget your brothers and all the faithful departed; but, moved by charity, have compassion on them, and strive to help them before the Lord in this or a similar way.

Prayer.

You faithful souls, rest in holy peace. May he who redeemed you by his precious blood deign to free you from pains and lead you to eternal joys, where you may rejoice and exult forever, blessed ones.

Another.

May God the Father, who created you, bless you. May Jesus Christ, who redeemed you by his most precious blood, bless you. May the Holy Spirit, who by his goodness and gift preserved you from eternal damnation, bless you. Amen.

Read the following prayer often.

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, Son of the living God, by the pain of your wounds, by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on the souls of all the faithful departed who exist in the pains of purgatory, and give them a dwelling-place of eternal light with all your saints. Amen.
