Another, through the practice of bending in prayer, used to stir his mind to the compunction of devotion; another, through meditation on the Lord’s Passion, kindled himself; another, by continual attention to his holy purpose, set before his inner eyes each day the image of death close at hand; another, by a fixed manner in his prayers, daily offered himself to the Lord with tears. None among them is believed to have been without a daily spiritual exercise of the mind; none is found to have been given to idleness, because the kingdom of God is promised to those who labor in the vineyard of the Lord, but denied to the idle.

Considering this, the devout fathers of the annual chapter unanimously resolved to adopt the present exercise and to offer it for practice to their subjects who have nothing better. And although so great a multitude of monks subject to the aforesaid chapter neither can nor ought to be restricted to the pattern of a single exercise, still it is proposed to all in common, so that those who do not have a better exercise may use this one. It is especially fitting that one newly converted to the order should exercise himself in the manner set down below, until, grounded in humility, he grows through compunction to better things. But whoever thinks that for a long time now he has been dwelling, by accustomed devotion, in higher things, let him at least permit these things to the little ones without envy. For I do not doubt that there are many living in the order who are devoted to better modes of spiritual exercise, and who have learned by long practice to remain in the highest region of the spirit through the ray of divine love. We in no way force our childish things upon them, for we know that they are higher than the steps of the present exercise. For we, imperfect men, write for beginners who are starting to make progress, not to teach the perfect. Rather, we are humbly ready to listen to them as teachers.

Here begins the second principal part of the little work on the threefold region of cloistered persons, which contains the manner and form of the daily exercise of monks.

Therefore, one who is to serve as a soldier for the king, our Lord Jesus Christ, according to the monastic rule, should first of all strive humbly and perseveringly to take up the strongest weapons of holy obedience; then he should accustom himself to be continually exercised in holy works, to persist in prayers without ceasing and with devotion, and to love God above all things. Whatever he does, let him set before himself the honor of God alone; whatever he begins, let him always send prayer before him to the Lord. For he truly speaks with the Lord God who is always occupied in devout prayers.

So that a good cenobite may always have ready at hand a manner of praying with elevation of mind, let him not neglect to read the present little work frequently and attentively. For in it we have arranged, for the individual hours of the seven offices, certain brief little prayers, by which the mind is more often raised to the love of heavenly things and the fervor of holy purpose is frequently renewed.

At Matins.

Awakened, the devout cenobite rises at once for Matins and shakes off from himself all sluggishness of sleepiness. He should believe that he has been called to fight for his Lord, to whom he has been professed. Therefore let him strive to meet his king with cheerfulness, and to prepare himself humbly, with fervor of spirit, for his immortal service. And so at once let him rise with the greatest dignity, and, sitting on his bed and fortifying himself with the sign of the cross, let him say:

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Blessed be the name of the Lord, from this time forth and forever.

Then, getting down from his bed, with knees bent let him say:

I give thanks to you, almighty God, who have deigned to guard me, an unworthy sinner, this night. I ask you, by your mercy, to direct me through this day and at all times in your praise, to keep me clean from sin, and sweetly to kindle me in your most holy love.
