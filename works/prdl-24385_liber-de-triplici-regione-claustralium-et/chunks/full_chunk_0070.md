Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love; you who, through the diversity of all tongues, gathered the nations together in the unity of faith. Send forth your Spirit and they shall be created. And you shall renew the face of the earth.

O God, to whom every heart lies open, every will speaks, and from whom no secret is hidden, purify the thoughts of our heart by the outpouring of the Holy Spirit, that we may deserve to love you perfectly and praise you worthily. Through Christ our Lord. Let him add “O flowering rose,” and, if he wishes, anything else out of devotion.

At the first signal.

Then, at the first signal of any hour of the day, or even at the striking of the clock, if he has not been occupied with higher meditations, let him say: Blessed be the name of the Lord, from this time forth and forever. Let him add some brief little prayer, formed from the fervor of his mind, in this or a similar way.

A little prayer.

Receive me, sweetest Lord Jesus, as your perpetual servant, that I may always have you as my only good.

Another.

I beseech you, most merciful God: grant me to live so innocently in holy religion that I may be able to come to you, the beginning and end of all good things.

Another.

O that my ways may be directed to keeping your statutes at all times.

Another.

Most loving Lord Jesus, I ask you, by the merits of your most holy Passion, teach me to despise all things, that I may continually deserve to find you, the highest good of my soul.

Then let the general preparation for fervor in prayer, set down above before Matins, be observed, and let the conscience be cleansed if anything has been committed against its purity. Yet if the mind has been lifted up in contemplation of divine love and continual desire, it will have less need of this little exercise.

At the second signal.

At the second signal, both for Prime and for the other hours of the day, when it sounds, let the method set down above for Matins be observed, as much as the time allowed for prayer permits each one. But when a monk enters or leaves our church, let him not neglect to show reverence to God before the altars and the crucifix; rather, bowing humbly, let him say the customary fitting prayers. For the modest bowing of the body is regarded as a sign of the inner good disposition of a soul well ordered toward God.

In the chapter house after Prime.

When the devout monk enters the chapter house after Prime, let him remember to give thanks to God for the victory of the saints and martyrs, whom he knows to have fought for the Lord in the greatest fervor of spirit, despising the torture of their bodies through the witness of death. Therefore, when you have heard the holy and mighty deeds of the saints read aloud, pray the Lord that through their merits he may deign to forgive you your sins, and mercifully strengthen you to bear the yoke of monastic discipline, so that you may attain fellowship with them. And this desire is to be taken up by you when, after the reading of their passions, divine help is invoked three times by all. At the beginning also of the day we pray that, strengthened to patience by the examples of the saints, we may fear no adversities throughout the whole day. But when you hear that some martyr stood bravely in the contest of battle, you may pray to him in these or similar words:

Prayer.

Holy soldier of the eternal King, I ask you, by the love of our Lord Jesus Christ, who made you overcome the torments of the flesh and the snares of this world: pray for me, that I may deserve to come to your fellowship.
