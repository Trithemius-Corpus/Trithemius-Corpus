Whatever visible thing is now seen will shortly decay. Take full consideration of things past, and see that what has passed away does not exist, and that what now seems to exist will, after a little while, no longer be.

Where are the kings of the nations, where the princes, where the leaders, where the rich, where those once powerful in the world? Where are all the lovers of the world, where the men who despised the steep path of heaven? Finally, where are all those once known to you in the world, whom death has meanwhile carried off while you have been in the cloister? Behold, from the beginning of the world, both those who lived for many years and those who lived for few have all died in their own generation; and according to the diversity of their merits, at the dictation of justice, they have also received different rewards.

The lovers of God, joined to the Creator of all, receive everlasting bright dwellings filled with inestimable joys; and the worshipers of the world, despising the commandments of God, once swollen here with honors, puffed up with knowledge, immersed in riches, given over to pleasures and vices, have gone down to hell to be tormented without end. What harm, I ask, does the once laborious contempt of the world, contrary to the flesh, now do to the saints in heaven, for which they now enjoy the glad vision of God forever? What profit now to the impious, buried in hell, is the consolation of the flesh once possessed, for which they now receive the torments of eternal damnation? Think on these things while it is profitable; meditate on these things while the opportunity for taking care is present.

Again, climbing higher, try with all your strength to fix the gaze of your mind in the abyss of infinite depth, and to consider how long that is which is eternal. Eternal is time that is enclosed by no end, bounded by no space. The saints of God will exult forever in blessed glory; sinners will burn forever in hell in misery. O eternity, name terrible without end: who will be able to think of you or speak of you without fear? Truly, he who thinks of you never sins eternally. Eternity has no end.

Hear a comparison, and though it will not be sufficient for eternity, receive fear from it. If a heap of sand were set up in the size of Mount Olympus, stretching to the sky, and from it after ten thousand years one little bird carried away only one grain, that mountain would by no means be said to last forever, but would be subject to an end; and although after innumerable years, nevertheless it would sometime cease to be. Yet all that time in which anything of the mountain remained, however very long it might be, would still seem as nothing in relation to eternity. For what is eternal receives no end even in imagination, nor does it admit hope of completion or limit.

Therefore the man is foolish, senseless, and mad who casts away this consideration of eternity, and uselessly spends the acceptable time granted him for deserving eternal goods. The hour will come, and is already hastening near, in which, no longer able to do better, he will learn what eternity is. But you, my monk, who desire to be faithful, cast from yourself every anxiety of the world; set all your care upon the love of God. Do nothing, say nothing, think nothing except what you know can lead you to the love of the Creator, or what pertains to the edification of others or to the pure necessity of the body.

Separate yourself, I pray, from low things, so that you may be able to delight in eternal goods. Let no anxiety of the flesh move you away from the inner tranquility of the mind; let no pleasure corrupt you. Strive to be always intent on prayers, as much as you can, with devotion, and again to lift yourself up more often through the affection of love.

On always having zeal for God.

The seventh root of the fear of God is called zeal, in which the monk is commanded to exercise himself no little. For no one who truly loves his lord tolerates calmly the injuries done to him. Therefore you, monk, who desire to be a faithful servant of Jesus Christ, do not neglect his zeal within yourself. Do not pretend not to see an injury to God when you see one, because whoever does not at once resist those who disparage his Lord is proved to love him too little. Let men know you as such a lover of God and enemy of vices that they do not dare to speak evil in your presence, nor to do any evil when they suspect you are watching.
