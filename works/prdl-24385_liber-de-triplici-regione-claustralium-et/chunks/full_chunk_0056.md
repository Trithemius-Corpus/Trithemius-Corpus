For they are driven in God by so great a fervor of love that he raises them above themselves; they generally leave behind all intellectual operations, and, carried into the abyss of divine contemplation, admit nothing at all except God. There, existing insensibly through the grace of love, they become one with God, while the property of their natures remains intact, by the gift of participation. For the holy apostle of Jesus Christ says: “He who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit.” And again he says: “Those who are led by the Spirit of God are the sons of God.” The same apostle also shows himself to be a citizen of this most holy region when he cries out to the Philippians: “Our conversation is in heaven.”

For as long as we are in the body we are exiled from the Lord; but as though through sleep we are sweetly led to our homeland when in mind and spirit we have our conversation in heavenly things. I say “through sleep” not properly, but by a remote comparison. For in sleep we go forth among images of things intelligible to us, the forms being preserved in the imagination; but in contemplation, beyond every imagination, we are raised by pure mind and spirit to God. And therefore this supreme region of the soul is called spiritual, from which all religious receive the name of spirituality.

Woe to us. We are called spiritual from this most blessed region; we are named religious, lovers of God; and yet we never climb the summit of this mountain by the height of contemplation, but, weak with the weak, always lie sick upon the bed of carnal desires. Why do we bear a holy name, who are not holy? Let us strive, then, with all our strength, through the continual exercise of the middle region, to ascend to the height of this supreme summit, if we truly desire to obtain the name of a spiritual way of life. In this region charity holds command, wisdom the consulship; in it nothing is loved except God, nothing disorderly is committed.

This most holy region belongs to the empyrean heaven, because those whom it spiritually receives as citizens it sends straight to the joys of heaven. For just as the lowest region of the flesh sends its dying clients to hell, and the middle region of reason sends its own to purgatory, so this region sends its perfect worshipers, when they depart, to heaven. The dignity of this third and supreme region is expressed by many prerogatives and is especially commended in seven articles, as will be said in order.

That this supreme region of the spirit of the cloistered is shown by a pleasing comparison.

First, then, this third region of the soul is described by a pleasing comparison, because it is compared to the highest, empyrean heaven. For just as all things are tranquil in the empyrean heaven, so on this summit of the supreme region the mind of the person contemplating God is disturbed by no agitations. Here is the highest rest of the contemplating soul; here no crowd of vices dwells; no bitterness of mind approaches at all. In the empyrean heaven, indeed, the majesty of God is seen by the holy spirits with an ineffable gift; here, in darkness, through the desire of love, he is seen above understanding. There God is all things in all; here nothing among all things is pleasing except God. There it is granted to the saints to rest endlessly from their labors; here the mind is permitted, in resting, to taste beforehand the unspeakable sweetness of inward delight.

There is no greater happiness in heaven, and there is not in the world so sweet a tranquility as in this region. Blessed indeed are those who have merited to ascend this mountain of divine contemplation, who love nothing at all in this world and always desire nothing other than to cleave to God. How secure is the conscience of these citizens, how joyful their conversation, where no evil is committed and nothing is desired except that supreme, unchangeable good. For the mind purified by divine love, which has truly and perseveringly ascended this region, desires nothing carnal, nothing disorderly, but, wholly raised by desire into contemplation of the highest majesty, rests sweetly in God alone.
