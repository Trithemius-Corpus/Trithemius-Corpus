And this lowest region is compared to the airy heaven; for just as hail, snow, lightning, and various storms are generated in the air, so also various storms of vices follow in the minds of carnal men who inhabit this dark region. Woe to carnal monks who inhabit this dark region, in which there is frequent movement of crimes and a continual storm of pleasures. These are monks given over to animal passions, who subject reason to sensuality, who live according to the flesh in beastly desires.

Now the works of the flesh dwelling in this lowest region are manifest: fornication, uncleanness, immodesty, lust, idolatry, sorcery, enmities, rivalries, anger, quarrels, dissensions, factions, envy, murders, drunkenness, feasting, and things like these. Concerning them the holy apostle proclaims that those who do such things shall not obtain the kingdom of God. These are the signs of the works of the lowest region; these are the deadly storms of the minds of carnal monks, who live irreligiously in religion, who despise the traditions of the holy fathers, who with polluted presumption scorn the commands of the elders. These are the unclean worshipers of the lowest region, given over to vices and carnal desires, who do not care to have God in their knowledge, who serve the pleasures of the flesh, who stubbornly break the vow of holy religion which they have professed, who, despising the salvation of their souls, disdain to follow the grace of God calling them.

Therefore he handed them over to a reprobate sense and to false judgment, so that they think evil good, and falsely suppose that the things which are in truth the worst are themselves the best. These are the carnal monks of whom the holy apostle spoke: whisperers, detractors, hateful to God, insulting, proud, puffed up, inventors of evils, disobedient to superiors, foolish, disorderly, without affection, without covenant, without mercy, filled with all iniquity, malice, wickedness, contention, deceit, and malignity. Though they know the justice of God, they refuse to understand that those who do such things are worthy of eternal death; and not only those who do them, but also those who consent to those who do them, and those who, by duty of office, in no way oppose the pursuits of evil men.

For indeed, when the ruler of souls does not correct delinquent subjects, he entangles himself in the crimes of others and becomes liable to condemnation; by keeping silence he becomes the companion of those whose faults condemnation will pursue. For any pastor of souls who, though placed in office, neglects to amend sinners according to the canonical rule, even if he is free from the offense itself, will nevertheless be condemned before the strict judge for the fault of another. For the supreme shepherd says to the prophet: “Son of man, I have given you as a watchman to the house of Israel; and you shall hear the word from my mouth, and shall announce it to them from me. If, therefore, when I say to the wicked man, ‘You shall surely die,’ you do not announce it to him, nor speak so that he may turn from his wicked way and live, that wicked man indeed shall die in his iniquity, but I shall require his blood from your hand.”

And our blessed father says in the Rule: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd will fall upon him, if the father of the household is able to find any lack of profit in his sheep.” O sentence to be feared by all spiritual pastors, by which not only the depraved conduct of subjects is ascribed to the ruler, but he is also held guilty before the inward judge for their lesser progress. O what shall we wretched pastors say in that strict examination, who neglect to amend according to the Rule the monks entrusted to us when they fall in many ways? We do not call those lying in the lowest region to higher things, but, carnal with the carnal, we grow sluggish in the shadow of death with destructive idleness.

Woe to all who remain in this dark region; for unless they do penance, they must be assigned with those who inhabit the outer darkness, where no order but everlasting horror dwells. For just as monks who walk according to the Rule in the spirit are to be happily crowned without end in heaven with the holy angels, so those who, against the norm of monastic institution, persevere in this lowest region of the flesh all the way to death, worse than pagans, are to be eternally tormented in hell with demons. For that blessed man truly says: “As I have never seen better men than those who have made progress in the cenobium, so I have never experienced worse than those who have failed in the monastery.” For the monastic profession demands the perfection of spiritual life, and tends toward the summit of virtues as toward its proper and due end.
