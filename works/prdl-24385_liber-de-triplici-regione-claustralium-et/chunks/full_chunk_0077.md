Heal my heart, Lord, of every weakness, so that it may feel no sweetness apart from you, seek no love apart from you, and love no consolation apart from you. O most holy Jesus, bread of life and understanding, having in yourself every delight and every sweetness of fragrance, you who always refresh us with yourself and are never lacking in yourself: let my heart, I beseech you, eat you, and let my soul be filled with the sweetness of your delight. O holy bread, O living bread, beautiful bread, pure bread, you who came down from heaven and give life to the world: come into my heart, and cleanse me from every defilement of mind and spirit. Enter into my soul; heal and sanctify me at every time. Be the protection and continual health of my body and soul. Drive away from me the enemies who lie in wait for me, so that, fortified by the sacrament of your body and blood, I may advance through you on the right path under regular discipline, and after this life may come joyfully to your kingdom.

An excuse for the length.

If this prayer seems too long to anyone, let him read whatever part he wishes, since for this reason we have divided it into short paragraphs, so that we might remove weariness. But the one who wishes to read neither the whole nor a part complains of its length in vain, since he will be shown to be unwilling to pray either a long prayer or a short one.

Meditation before approaching the altar.

By a devout meditation on the Lord’s passion, or at least on one of its sacred articles, or on several if the devotion and opportunity of the time allow, the mind of a devout person is wondrously pierced with compunction, if the practice is held as [unclear]. I would call meditation on the Lord’s passion truly savory when a person, purified through true mortification, begins to revolve in thought the death and passion of the Lord, and is dissolved into tears without violence, from love alone, and through the excessive fervor of desire is prepared as it were for a certain rapture of mind. Whoever has received this gift from the Lord will never be able to prepare himself better for celebrating than if, before approaching the altar, he always passes through at least one article of the Lord’s passion in meditation, with tears. For since the celebration of Mass was instituted by our Redeemer himself in memory of the Lord’s passion, a person never prepares himself for it better or more fruitfully than when he is more fervently kindled by meditation on that same most bitter passion.

The elevation of the priest’s mind to God during the celebration.

With how great reverence, devotion, and purity of mind we ought to stand before the almighty Lord at the altar, what speech could express, or what understanding grasp and penetrate? For there the true body and true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are consecrated in an ineffable manner; there the Lord of majesty is truly present and is adored; there the army of invisible angels, standing by its king, rejoices together; there the whole heavenly court adores the Lord and man. Therefore the greatest purity of mind, the greatest reverence and humility are needed when we must stand before so great a majesty, lest the Lord Jesus, who searches the kidneys and hearts, be able to find in us anything that offends him. Let all worldly anxieties depart far from here; let all thoughts withdraw. Let nothing be thought here except what belongs to the consummation of so great a mystery; let nothing be turned over in the mind except what befits the presence of so great a majesty and glory. For here no office of human strength is performed, but the miracle of divine majesty is enacted: the Son of God is offered to the Father; the body of our Redeemer is truly received; the mystery of our reconciliation is celebrated. Therefore, standing at the altar, let the priest remember that he stands before invisible majesty, and let him beware of thinking of anything else.
