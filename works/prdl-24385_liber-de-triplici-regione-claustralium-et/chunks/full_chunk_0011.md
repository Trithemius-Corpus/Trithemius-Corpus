But tell me, proud inhabitants of the lowest region, you who always make decrees according to your own will, you who usurp another’s right: is this the virtue of obedience, to refuse to yield to the command of a superior, but stubbornly to cling to your own judgment? Did the holy father establish for you in the Rule such a way of living, when he commanded you to follow the abbot’s will in all things? For the greatest virtue of a monk is obedience, which our holy father wished to commend so highly that he says a good work done without the abbot’s will lacks merit. Therefore, giving instruction, he concludes thus: let all things be done with the abbot’s will, and let no one in the monastery follow the will of his own heart.

But if the rector’s prudence seems insufficient for ordering the monastery, this must be imputed to the carnal monks, who willingly set over themselves one who was not fit. Folly always hates prudence, and foolish monks detest a wise abbot. Therefore those who knowingly set a fool over themselves must necessarily be subject to his ordering and will. Indeed, no insufficiency of the pastor ought to extinguish obedience in the subject, since the Rule commands us to obey even an unprofitable one. For it says: obedience is to be given to the abbot’s commands in all things, even if he himself, which God forbid, acts otherwise.

But when we rebuke disobedience, carnal monks object: “We do these things with right and good intention, because we see the substance of the monastery perishing through bad governance.” But the Apostle contradicts you: evils must not be done so that good may come; nor, on account of damage to temporal property, must the subjection of humility be abandoned. For where one must undergo either the loss of temporal substance or danger to regular obedience, it is better for the property to perish than for the purity of obedience to be harmed. For God will not require money from our hands, but souls. Indeed, one who is a true monk prefers the virtue of obedience to all the things of the world.

But the citizens of this lowest region, carnal monks, tear apart the virtue of obedience in various ways; they attack the weapons of holy humility, so that it seems to me nothing is easier than to train monks newly come from the world, [unclear] than to be able to amend that sloth of theirs. But from their fruits we plainly know what sort they are: they violate peace and charity in the monastery; they disturb the concord of unity; they sow discord between the brethren and the abbot; they stubbornly refuse obedience to the commands of the Rule. These are the works of evil cloisterers; these are the marks of the lowest citizens, who, as most wicked defenders of their own wills, cast away the weapons of holy obedience, live according to the flesh and not the spirit, give either no deference or very little to the statutes of the fathers, do not acquiesce in sound counsels, and their condemnation is just. For just as it is impossible for a blazing fire not to rise upward, so it is impossible for a monk without obedience to penetrate the secrets of heaven.

In What Way the Pastors of This Lowest Region Are Disordered

Article Three

Since, then, we have shown that many monks of the lowest region are disordered toward their pastors, why should we, as though sparing prelates, not show the disorders of prelates toward their subjects? For the name of abbot sanctifies no one, nor does the dignity of pastoral care save the undeserving. Therefore, since many among the prelates dwell on the mountain of the lowest region with the beasts of the earth, they must by no means be passed over by us in silence. For many rectors of cloistered men show themselves disordered toward their subjects: they not only neglect to feed according to the Rule the souls committed to them, but tear them apart and kill them in various ways.

And whenever anyone from the lowest region is taken up as pastor, the pursuits of the wicked are strengthened for the worse. Therefore a good man and a diligent lover of God must be promoted to the office of abbot: one who has long since overcome this lowest region; one who has known how to tame the passions of the flesh; one who both wills and is able to ascend to that highest region of the spirit. For the abbot of monks ought to be of higher merit than the monks, since whoever is established as ruler of souls must necessarily be adorned with many virtues. For if the pastor, still weak, dwells in the lowest region with the sheep, by neglecting the weak he kills them; he does not strengthen them. For if a blind man offers guidance to the blind, he must necessarily fall into the ditch together with him. But a pastor who does not show himself ordered toward his subjects according to the Rule must be called not a pastor but a hireling, and he will not remain forever in the house of the Lord. Now the disorders of carnal prelates are many, and they harm the sheep all the more because they are more rarely amended.
