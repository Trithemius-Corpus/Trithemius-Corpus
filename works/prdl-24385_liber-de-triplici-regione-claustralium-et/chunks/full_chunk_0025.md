Preface to the second tract of the first part, concerning the middle region, namely the rational region of cloistered persons.

But now let us at last leave the dark region of the carnal, in which the whirlwind of wickedness abides and the continual storm of vices. We have granted enough to it, and have lingered over it longer than our purpose had set. Let us therefore leave the dead to bury their dead; let us yield to the darkness; let us seek the light of the spiritual life. We must go out from the Egypt of the dark region; we must hasten diligently, through the middle way of reason, to the highest summit of the spirit. Let us therefore now at last approach the middle region of the soul, through which one comes gradually to the highest. This region of the soul is quite fittingly called rational, for in it there is continual labor in seeking the highest truth. The fervor of the mind in this middle region always strives to raise itself to higher things; but the weakness of the flesh, through the motion of the storm that follows, runs together with it to draw it back to lower things. From this comes continual labor; from this arise sighs and groans, because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling depresses the mind that thinks on many things. This is the narrow way that leads to life, and few walk by it. In this region force is applied against the desires of the flesh until the will is subjected to the spirit at its nod. For the Lord says in the Gospel: “The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent seize it.” In this region the war of contest is declared for those who are to serve God; so the wise man bears witness: “Son,” he says, “when you come to the service of God, stand in fear and prepare your soul for temptation.” How great the temptations are in this middle region we learn daily through the difficulty of spiritual exercise, while, driven in various ways, we cannot remain long in fervor of spirit. Now the weakness of the flesh calls us back from the pursuit of progress; now the temptation of the ancient enemy troubles us; now the company of our neighbor hinders us; now the deceitfulness of the world seduces us. Therefore those who wish to make progress in this region of labor need the greatest fortitude, because no one can withstand his temptations to the end unless he studies daily to do violence to himself. But whoever thinks that he has come to this region must not presume on his own strength; for although the grace of God, in order to work good in us, requires the free choice of our will, so too the will necessarily requires grace. For no one is holy by his own strength. Therefore let no one dare to presume on himself, but let him who stands see that he does not fall. The life of the good upon the earth is warfare, for through many tribulations they come to the kingdom, and by the arduous labors of the virtues heaven is acquired. “Going,” says the prophet, “they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming, they shall come with rejoicing, carrying their sheaves.” For whoever sows in tears shall reap in joy. But if anyone has failed in sowing, certainly he will not rejoice in the harvest. Since, therefore, through the continual labors of spiritual exercises the mind must be cleansed from the dust of the first region, let us not seek leisure in this middle region; let us not love the rest of negligence, because no one here is able to stand unless he girds himself strongly for continual labor. Whoever in this middle region does not always press on to better things soon falls back through negligence into the former habits of life; there he incurs the danger of failing where he has lost the appetite for making progress. This is the place of continual conflict; whoever neglects to fight in it retreats in order to be conquered.

The preface ends.

The second region of the soul begins, which is called rational, in which human beings are occupied amid many labors.

The second region of the soul, then, is called rational; in it the mind is exercised with continual labor in the search for the highest truth, and the farther it separates itself from the lowest region, the nearer it approaches the highest. And this region is set forth as resembling in certain ways the ethereal heaven, in which there is the position of the stars and continual motion.
