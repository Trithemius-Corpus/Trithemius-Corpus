For just as motion itself is always disposed in heaven by the order of the first mover as cause, so continual labor is exercised in the middle region, so that each intellectual power may be perfected in its own object and in no way depart from the order naturally implanted in it. There are three powers of this sort: memory, intellect, and will.

Of these, memory gathers the likenesses of things and their images, and extends itself toward what is arduous. The intellect, by discerning these images themselves, considers them, and here investigates the true. The will embraces what has been approved and ordered by the intellect, and it is occupied with the known good. When these three powers of the soul rightly keep their order, they lead the spirit by faith toward heavenly things.

The second region of life is compared to the ethereal heaven: for as in that heaven there is continual motion and the fixed position of the stars, so in this middle region there is motion and continual labor against the contests of vices. And as the motion of the stars fulfills the office appointed to it by nature in due order, so also in this region the equity of uprightness is required, so that each power of the soul, while subjected to its own object, may be perfected toward the end appointed for it, so far as this can happen to wayfarers.

But no power itself can be brought simply to fullness in this region, since no one in this pilgrimage is so perfect that he cannot become more perfect in his own order. For even if memory, in its own object, which is the arduous, could behold the gathered likenesses and images of all creatures, it would not be satisfied unless it could apprehend their principle, which is God. But this is altogether impossible in this region, in which we can form no images or likenesses of God. The intellect also, the counselor, so to speak, of the blind will, is extended toward the true; but however much it considers its own object, it is not satisfied until it can apprehend all truth and arrive, without the opinion of imaginative speculation, at knowledge of the eternal, highest, and unfailing truth. But that perfection is possible to none of the wayfarers so long as he remains in the body of mortality. The will too, although it is directed to its own object, which is the good, will not be able to be satisfied and truly gladdened unless it comes to that unchangeable and highest good, which no instability of change overshadows.

The highest truth and unchangeable good is God, who says to Moses: “Man shall not see me and live.” And the disciple of the highest truth says: “No one has ever seen God.” And the prophet, addressing the Lord, says: “Truly you are a hidden God.” But although the aforesaid three powers of the soul cannot perfectly comprehend their objects in this life, none of them ought to cease from its office, lest it incur the danger of failing in the very place where the task of striving has been appointed.

Therefore the citizens of this middle region mortify evil affections in themselves, adorn themselves with continual virtues, direct all their works by right intention, and in no way withdraw from the office of reason.

The citizens of this region are scholastic, contemplative, and active men, [unclear], devoted to honorable arts: they subject the sensual or carnal powers to reason; they keep these three powers of the soul clean from all unlawful objects; they always raise their intention toward the end of honorable goodness; and they reform the will, in act or in habit, toward its due end. Therefore the cenobite who loves this cleansing and desires to ascend to the third region of the spirit ought to consider and keep the following principal articles in the middle region.

The summary of the truth of this middle region is expressed in seven principal articles. Whoever desires to make progress in this middle region according to the vow of his profession should diligently take care to observe the following articles. If he neglects to keep them, he shows that he has not yet become a citizen of the middle region. For, as we said above, no one can remain in this middle region without a continual effort to advance; here not to make progress is to fall away, and not to have ascended is to descend.
