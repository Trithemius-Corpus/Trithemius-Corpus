But whoever neglects the exercise of daily discipline falls into the depth of the body, as the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “If you do not keep yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, the Lord will quickly overturn your house,” which he understands as a good conscience. For the provision of this exercise, so useful and so necessary, the Rule of our most holy father Benedict for monks seems to suffice, since it is clear that it contains all the material of exercises, both spiritual and bodily, necessary for monks to preserve regular discipline. And therefore all monastic exercises must be humbly submitted to the ordinances of the Rule, since whatever is prescribed either by us or by others for the instruction of monks is wholly directed to observing the monastic norm. For the whole sum of our religious life, which is acquired by the continual practice of spiritual exercise, is contained in the Rule of monastic tradition as in one root, in brief compass.

Therefore every spiritual or bodily exercise of monks, by which the mind is led by love to humble obedience and self-denial, is conformed to the institutions of the holy Rule, which is known to present the monk first to God as good and humble, and so much the more opposes the effort of proud contempt. The holy mysteries of this exercise, by an invisible teaching in the mind of their cultivator, direct him by a safe path to the love of God as to his due end; they wonderfully acquire the grace of inward devotion, and make the monk count all earthly things as nothing and sweetly burn at all times in fervor of spirit. The animal man does not grasp this saving exercise of devotion; the vain and curious monk does not understand this pursuit; the proud man does not admit this knowledge, which is to be preferred to all things. Happy is he who has learned to penetrate the secrets of this exercise; happy is he who has deserved to receive this gift of holy compunction from the Lord, for whoever has once attained it perfectly will fear no adversity of this world. But that exercise which, bereft of its due end, does not direct the minds of cloistered men to the observance of the commandments of the Rule, does not pour in the Holy Spirit of discipline; for where the obedience of subjection is despised, no gift is accepted by almighty God.

For it is written: “Obedience is better than victims, and to listen is better than to offer the fat.” And although many little works of spiritual exercise are found composed by different men, yet not all things suit everyone. Therefore it has seemed good also to us to publish something in accordance with the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, by which we may be able to stir ourselves more closely and more nearly toward the purity of regular observance. And so we have divided the present little work, collected from the exercises of the excellent father Theoderic, once abbot of the monastery of Bursfelde, into two principal parts. The first is placed in the consideration of monastic institution; the second is occupied with the exercise of the spiritual life. In the first it commands you to know yourself; in the second, to raise the mind from earthly things to heavenly things.

Therefore, approaching the proposed consideration according to our strength, we have divided the first treatise into three parts, according to the threefold state of cloistered men, whom we may call first sensual, second rational, and third spiritual. From this threefold state of men we take a fitting title for this work, which we call On the Three Regions of Cloistered Men. In these three regions the whole crowd of monks is included, as will be said in what follows.

On the first region of the soul, in which carnal monks live with beasts, disordered by many passions, dwelling sensually in the lowest region.

The first region of the soul, then, is called sensual, lowest, and animal; in it the roots of all evils are contained. Men established in this dark region share in their own kind with beasts, since they observe, so to speak, beastly habits, while they give either nothing or little to reason, but grant everything to sensuality like beasts. For in that lowest region three sensual powers of the soul preside. The first, the sensitive power, is spread about the objects of the five exterior senses; the second, the concupiscible power, delights in carnal things; the third is the irascible power.
