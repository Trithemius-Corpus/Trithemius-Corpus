A possession acquired with difficulty is always held dearer, and a good that is acquired laboriously is guarded with greater diligence. Thus the loving Father, God, permits us, when conquered by the temptations of the adversary, to fall for a time into the pit of sin through alternating reversals, until, conscious of our weakness, we learn to hope in nothing in our own strength, but only in his grace. But you, man, who often interrupt your progress by sinning, never delay to rise again; for even if you cannot perfectly subject the enemy to yourself, it will nevertheless be good for you if the last day can find you standing and fighting, even after a frequently repeated fall. But this counsel does not release those to whom the mercy of God has granted strength and grace for defeating the adversary. Nevertheless, let him who stands take heed lest he fall.

On the attentive reform of the works of the first region.

Article Two.

Since, as we taught at the end of the first treatise, this middle region is nearest to the lowest part of the first region, in which, if the cenobite has been even a little negligent and remiss, he immediately falls back again without interval into the former region. And therefore whoever desires to persevere in this region by making progress should always strive, with attentive vigilance, to reform the works of the lower region. And, lest we be lengthy, whoever desires to exercise this reform in himself should take up, in this region, works contrary to the eight articles of the first region. For, as is taught by the tradition of physicians, contraries are cured by contraries.

Therefore faithful monks, citizens of this middle region, are first commanded to look to this: that each strive before all things to order himself toward God. This ordering is said to be done well when God, according to the law, is loved with the whole heart above all things.

Next, the devout cenobite should keep himself ordered toward his prelate, offer him humility and reverence, strive to love him with sincere charity, and observe according to the Rule all that he commands.

The rector also, [unclear] of souls, should take care to walk in ordered fashion toward the cenobites, to show himself a shepherd to the sheep and not a hireling, not to neglect the salvation of the souls entrusted to him, and not to set temporal things before spiritual things. Let him hate vices, love the brothers, and show himself an example to all in every good thing.

Next, let brothers cling to one another as members of one head; let them love one another mutually in the true charity of God, and not withdraw from good zeal. For all of us who trust, through the faith of Christ, to come to eternal life by persevering in good works are members of Christ, the supreme head. But whoever pursues his brother with evil zeal or hatred and other evil affections first separates himself from the head, and then does injury to Christ.

Consequently, let each one show himself ordered toward himself: let him humbly subject himself to all for the love of Christ, prefer others to himself through humility, and believe himself more unworthy than all monks.

In the use of the things of the monastery, let them always take care not to exceed the institution of the Rule, but strive everywhere to show themselves ordered and faithful in all things. Let no one have or seek anything contrary to the will of the abbot; let him admit no pleasure into his soul in the use of things.

Then faithful cenobites should take care to order themselves, according to the institution of the Rule, to contempt of the world, so that they love nothing in this world, but truly despise all things for the love of God.

Let them never neglect to order themselves most diligently to penance; let them often have recourse to humble confession of their faults. For just as no one is believed to live in this world without sin, so penance is judged necessary for all, even monks. Therefore the good cenobite, through the practice of compunction, will always have recourse to penance, humbly accuse himself of his faults in chapter, willingly perform the penance imposed, and faithfully amend himself in all things.
