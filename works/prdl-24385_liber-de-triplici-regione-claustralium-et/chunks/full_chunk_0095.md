Here is the end of the *Spiritual Exercise of Cloistered Persons*, by Peter of Friedberg in the noble city of [Hagenau?], on the eighth day before the Ides of August, in the year of salvation 1498.

*Here begins the compendium of spiritual exercise. Johannes Trithemius.*

This form of spiritual exercise, issued by us for the edification of cloistered persons, might appear very brief to all devout persons who have leisure; yet we fear that to those sufficiently occupied in household affairs it may seem rather lengthy. For when thought is stretched by continual solicitude in arranging necessary matters [unclear], the mind is easily wearied in spiritual exercises, even the very briefest. Therefore, lest our monks who are either devoted to external things or less devout in spiritual conversation should be able, on the occasion of prolixity, to flee loosely to an argument of excuse, we have been ordered to abbreviate the same instruction, so that both the more fervent in their devotion may retain the greater one, and the lukewarm may not omit the lesser.

*There follows the practice of spiritual exercise.*

At all and each of the hours generally, let the devout cenobite strive to direct his effort to this end: that with all his strength he may desire to magnify God in his heart as the root of His benefits. Let there be in him so very fervent an ardor of devotion, so much compunction, so great an inundation of tears coming on, that whoever sees him chanting the psalms may be moved by his example to magnify the Lord. Secondly, let him remember that he can be stirred up to the charity of the first [unclear]; for love is directed toward God in vain if the neighbor is hated. Therefore, when all the thorns of displeasure have been expelled, let the most devout cenobite, turned toward God, pray.

O almighty, everlasting God, who, instructing us for the exercise of Your love by the manifold creation of things, have commanded us to love You above all things with all our strength and our neighbor as ourselves for Your sake: graciously grant me the grace of Your most holy love and of love of neighbor, so that, all motions of displeasure, aversion, and anger being restrained, I may love You always with a pure heart above all things, and may in no way withdraw from sincere love of neighbor; so that, just as a member joined to a member suffers with one that is injured, so may my mind, sincerely loving its neighbor, deserve to be a member of our true head, Jesus Christ. Who lives and reigns with You.

*Another Prayer.*

I beseech You, holy Father, almighty eternal God, by the virtue of the most holy passion of Your only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ: enlighten the eyes of my heart, that, pierced by the spirit, I may see how often I have offended in Your commandments; and, cleansed by Your mercy from every filth of sin, may I never offend You hereafter.

*After Matins.*

When Matins has ended, while still remaining in the church, or also when placed in your cell, take up for yourself the disposition of true resignation, praying thus.

*Prayer.*

Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who at the morning hour, set in the greatest anguish of Your most holy heart, after a threefold prayer fully resigned Yourself into the will of God the Father: grant me, by the virtue of that charity of Yours, perfectly to despise my own will in all things for love of You, and truly to resign myself at all times into the disposition of Your will and of those whom You have placed over me. Who live and reign.

*After Prime.*

After Prime, when, leaving the choir, you enter the chapter place, in memory of your most innocent Savior accused before Pilate, you will form your disposition toward humble confession, praying thus to the Lord.
