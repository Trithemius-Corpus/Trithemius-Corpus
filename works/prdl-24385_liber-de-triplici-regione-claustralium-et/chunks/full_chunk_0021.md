When things are offered, all receive them cheerfully; but few repay the givers with the support of prayer. Among these are many citizens of the lowest region who, when they have come to the order, serve not God but their belly. They desire to have more food, drink, and clothing given to them than necessity requires; and those who in the world could scarcely have had bread and vegetables for food, now, having become monks, refuse eggs and little fish. They importunately demand that large, varied, different, and delicate fish be given them; murmuring through the concupiscence of the flesh with the children of Israel in the desert, they cry out against their leader. They say that they cannot eat eggs and peas; they always desire more delicate things.

How the citizens of the lowest region are ill-ordered toward contempt of the world.

Article Seven.

The kingdom of God suffers violence, and the violent seize it. Therefore whoever desires to enter the heavenly kingdom through the weapons of holy obedience, whoever chooses to live for God under regular discipline, must do violence to the pleasures of the flesh and strongly contemn the world with all its vanities. But carnal monks, citizens of the lowest region, who are so badly ordered toward God and toward themselves, are also badly ordered toward contempt of the world.

First, they are infected by the concupiscence of the flesh, for they are always burning with the fire of pleasure. In food and drink they are found excessive; in beds and baths they are delicate against the rules; they prefer the desires of the flesh to the salvation of the soul; they do not seek the sweetness of inward conversation. They nourish their flesh too delicately, and because of this they are continually inflamed with various desires. For no one can truly contemn the world who neglects to restrain the appetite of the flesh even in the smallest things. If you eat to satiety, you do not truly contemn the world. The first great commandment imposed on those who contemn the world is founded on abstinence. But if you fill your belly even once to satiety with food, you immediately fall either into lust or into heavy sloth. Therefore the concupiscence of the flesh draws back the mind of the disordered cenobite, so that he cannot contemn the world.

Second, they are disordered with respect to contemning the world because of the vain concupiscence of the eyes. For the monk who forgets the true honors of faithful souls takes delight in worldly displays and utterly curious ornaments. And truly, what each person is inwardly, hidden within himself, his outward apparel makes manifest. To carry rings on the fingers, corals on the rosaries, scent-vessels in the hands, and pleats in the cowls does not show a monk but a soldier; it displays not a humble cenobite but a proud worldling. Such adornment in monks can please none but the reprobate, and it is often found to have displeased even the proud. Woe to you, disordered cloisterers, who descend to hell through the double concupiscence of the eyes: while you desire to see in others what is not fitting, and while you present yourselves to be seen by others otherwise than is proper. What have the trinkets of the world to do with you, or the curiosities of the world? Beauty of clothing, as Bernard testifies, quickly makes a masculine soul effeminate; the honorable fashions of outward attire quickly weaken a manly disposition. These men are like children in little rags who, because of one red thread fastened to a torn sleeve, think themselves very beautiful and handsome, and insolently mock others standing before them in the best garments as if they were naked. These pompous cloisterers see others walking in humble clothing, contemning the world in the spirit of poverty, and for that reason pleasing both God and men; they see themselves despised by the wise and by princes, and sometimes not even admitted to conversation. Seeing these things, the proud are deeply grieved, as if by an injury; yet they utterly refuse to lay aside their arrogance.

Third, the citizen of the lowest region is badly ordered toward contempt of the world because of the pride of life: for they are not only infected with the vain pleasures of the flesh, but are also proud through the swelling of the mind. For all who follow the desires of the flesh vainly raise themselves above themselves; they contemn others, however much better they may be; they do not know how to endure equals; they do not accept superiors. They take delight in having a great retinue of servants stand by them in [unclear].
