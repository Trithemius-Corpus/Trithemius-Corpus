Consider, if you can, or rather as much as you can, how great, exalted, and immeasurable is the Son of God, who for your sake willed to become incarnate; how hard and bitter were the things he endured for you.

At the Offertory.

When the offertory is taking place, apply your mind, together with the host to be consecrated, to the offering, and ponder what is being done there mystically. For the host is round and white. In its whiteness are set forth the purity, innocence, and suffering humanity of our Savior, to be offered to God the Father for us against all our impurities; in its roundness his immeasurable divinity is offered to us as the appointed reward. Say therefore to the Lord:

Prayer.

I have no worthy gift to offer you, almighty God; I hand myself over to you as a sacrifice. Receive, therefore, holy Father, in union with the most holy offering of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, me as an eternal gift to you and as a perpetual servant; and give me the continual fire of your holy love, that I may always be able to render you pleasing service. Through the same.

Prayer.

I offer you, almighty God, the life, passion, and death of the same our Lord Jesus Christ, for my sins, for the holy Roman and universal Church, for the supreme pontiff, for our bishop, for our abbot, for all my brothers, for the union of our observance, for our whole order wherever it may be, for the salvation of all the faithful, for the conversion of unbelievers, and for the redemption of the souls of the faithful existing in the pains of purgatory. Receive, I beseech you, holy Father, this offering of mine in union with that charity with which you received the same our Lord Jesus Christ dying for us on the cross, and may it be pleasing to you.

During the Preface.

When the preface is being said, take care to raise your mind from all earthly things to heavenly things, and to prepare it to praise the Lord with the holy angels. Remember that through the merit of his holy passion you have been called to the fellowship of the angels, and take care that through observance of the commandments you may come to it.

At the Sanctus.

With your mind lifted upward, glorify the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God and Lord, to whom the Sanctus is sung, and humbly adore his majesty with the holy angels at all times.

During the Canon.

After the Sanctus, during the canon, you may take up meditation on the Lord’s passion, and offer it, article by article, especially the greater and principal ones, to God the Father for yourself, or for others, or for any necessity. Say, therefore, in this or a similar manner:

O holy Father, I offer you the bloody sweat and tears of our Lord Jesus Christ, your most beloved Son, in that charity and union in which he voluntarily offered himself to you in death for us: for my sins, or for that brother, or for that servant-girl, or for that necessity. And I ask you, through the pain of the wounds of that same sweetest Son of yours, and through the pouring out of his most holy blood and the power of his most innocent death: have mercy on me, or on that person, or grant us this or that, and so forth. Through the same our Lord.

With the other articles of the Lord’s passion you will be able to proceed in the same way, and to enlarge upon them in offering according to the intention of your affection.

At the Elevation.

If, at the elevation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your mind is not occupied with higher things, you may pray to the Lord, who is truly present, in this way:

You are the King of glory, O Christ. You are the everlasting Son of the Father. You, to deliver man, did not shrink from the Virgin’s womb, and so on to the end. For this part of the hymn, Saint Thomas Aquinas is read to have prayed to the Lord during the elevation.
