How the carnal monks of this lowest region are disordered in many respects.

Although the description of this lowest region generally applies to any carnal people at all who live in sins and crimes with the habits of beasts, nevertheless, so that we may attend to our own, we intend to speak of lukewarm and carnal cloistered monks, doing absolutely no injury to the good and devout. For always and in all things we wish the conduct of good cloistered monks to remain untouched and unharmed whenever we discuss the evil pursuits of the wicked. We reproach the bad cloistered monks and their corrupt ways; the good we honor and praise everywhere, as is fitting. And what else but evil can we say of the evil, in whom we find nothing good to say? They live disorderly in a holy order; they despise the traditions of the elders; they do not receive words of salvation.

So great, indeed, is the disorder of the monks of the lowest region that, in my judgment, it would be easier to convert a publican than to call a disorderly monk back to repentance. We include this disorder of carnal monks under eight headings, although we know that they are disorderly in many other ways as well. First, the citizens of this lowest region, beastlike monks, are disordered toward God; then toward their own prelate; third, prelates toward their subjects; fourth, members toward one another; again, fifth, they are disordered toward themselves; then toward the goods of the monastery; seventh, toward contempt of the world; and finally toward repentance for sin. These are the principal disorders of the citizens of the lowest region; these are the beastlike affections of carnal monks, who dwell in the depths in mind and heart, because they do not hasten to press on to the summit. I shall take care to set out briefly, in order, these disordered conditions of the lowest region, and to show, as far as I can, how far carnal monks are from God. For thus, as the prophet says, salvation is far from sinners.

How the citizens of this lowest region are disordered toward God. Article One.

Therefore the carnal monks of this lowest region, its mad inhabitants, are disordered toward God in many ways: they do not now taste the sweetness of his love; they admit no compunction of divine fear; swollen and beastlike in their affections, they are at all times unclean in their carnal pleasures.

First, their disorder toward God takes its origin from ignorance, since, given over to animal passions, they do not care to have God in their knowledge, nor do they consent to think upon those things that lead the soul to fear him; but, blinded by evil desires, they become vain in their thoughts. But what does Scripture say? “He who is ignorant shall be ignored.” They do not wish to understand so that they may do good; they do not tremble before the face of the strict judge; without repentance they continually persevere in evils. For the man swollen in his own manner of life does not fear to rot away in his pleasures; they do not heed the patience of God calling them to repentance; they seek nothing except the comforts of their flesh. At all times they walk in the greatest sins without any fear; they stubbornly refuse to know the ways of the Lord. Of the future judgment they think either nothing or very little; they do not fear the punishments prepared for sinners in hell; no grace of compunction now pierces their hearts, because the love of God does not kindle their minds. They have no care for God; no dread of death. They obey the belly more than God; at all times they desire to live according to the flesh; and while they shamefully neglect their own salvation, they descend to hell with the demons. For those who, having forgotten God, keep badly the vow of holy monastic profession do not attain the rewards of heavenly life that are to be given to the just.

Second, carnal monks are disordered toward God by a foolish profession, because they promise to live according to the Rule, yet do not truly care to fulfill it. And what is more disorderly than to promise to the one God a consecrated life, and yet remain in the foulness of one’s former life? It is a mockery of almighty God, is it not, for anyone in the monastery to live otherwise than the monastic Rule teaches?
