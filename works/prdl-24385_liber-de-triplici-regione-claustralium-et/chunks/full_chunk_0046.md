He suffered in his whole body, because from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no soundness in him. He suffers in his friends, because he is betrayed by a disciple and, deserted by all, is even publicly denied by Peter. He is afflicted also in compassion for his mother, whose most holy soul is remembered as having been pierced by the sword of grief. He suffered much from ingratitude, because he foresaw that the death which he underwent in compassion for all would be lost in the case of many reprobates. Finally, he suffered in all his senses, as we are taught from the Gospel. He suffers in his eyes, because, with many tears poured out, they are covered with a cloth. He suffers in his hearing, when he is mocked, reviled, and blasphemed with many insults and abuses. He suffers in his smell, because he is defiled with the most foul spittle of the Jews. He suffers in his touch, because he is tortured in every member of his body. He suffers in his taste, because, thirsting, he is given gall and vinegar to drink.

Sixth, because of the cause of the Passion, which was solely the envy of the Jews, since he suffered wholly without guilt. For indeed, whoever suffers unjustly is tormented all the more in his soul. But the innocent Son of God dies for our sins; he is tormented for our crimes, as is read in Isaiah: “Truly he himself bore our infirmities.”

Seventh, because of the lack of every consolation, since Christ found no one to console him in his Passion. For he says in the Psalm: “You have put far from me friend and neighbor, and my acquaintances because of misery.” Indeed, his acquaintances stood far off.

Fourth, the Passion of Christ was most fruitful in its effect, which is to be considered principally in three things: first, the remission of sins; second, the bestowal of grace; third, the showing forth of glory. And these three things are indicated by the title fastened to the cross, which, as the Evangelist says, contained the cause of Jesus’ Passion: “Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews.” For “Jesus,” saving sinners, refers to the first; “of Nazareth,” that is, conferring grace, refers to the second; “King of the Jews,” showing the heavenly kingdom to those who confess him, refers to the third.

Moreover, in this second root we have arranged meditations on the Passion of the Lord for the several hours of the seven days of the week; we have placed them after the end of the third section for this reason, lest, by setting them down in order here, we should disturb this place with their excessive abundance. For the things we have now set out seem to pertain rather to readings than to meditation; therefore we postpone them.

On Meditation on the Benefits of God

The third root of divine fear and love lies in meditation on the benefits of God, through which one comes to the grace of devotion. Indeed, the beginning of devotion is placed in meditation on the benefits of God, from which love is generated by memory. Its middle is progress in that meditation itself, when one desires nothing above all things except to please God, from whom he knows that he has been anticipated by many benefits without any merit of his own. Its end, however, is to love God above all things with burning desire and the pure fervor of the mind, and to long to cling continually to him alone without any intermediary.

And since the merciful God has bestowed many things specially on each one of us, yet because we are writing these our symbols not for one person but for many, we must briefly place before them not the particular benefits given to individuals, but the general benefits common to all. Therefore we comprehend the chief ones under the number seven, so that through the seven days of the week, or even in the canonical hours themselves, we may have material ready each day for meditation. But if anyone, through the elevation of his mind, has experience in higher things concerning God, let him not seek the spoils of one still a novice. But whoever, because of the weakness of his mind, cannot ascend to that supreme summit of contemplation should strive to exercise himself more fervently in these meditations on the benefits of God, so that, while he diligently continues in lower things, purified and stronger, he may ascend to higher things.
