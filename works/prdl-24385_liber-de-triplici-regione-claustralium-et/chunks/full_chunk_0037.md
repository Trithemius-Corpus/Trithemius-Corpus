The patience of a prudent man who loves God preserves the purpose of humility: in the removal of goods and temporal advantages it receives increase, and in bodily injury the silent consciousness of virtue preserves its undertaking. Consider now, faithful soul, how your most loving Savior Jesus endured these three things most patiently for your redemption; and if you believe that you love him, do not think it burdensome, for love of him, to endure similar things with patience.

From the three grades immediately preceding we establish one sum of the cenobitic end, which, that it may seem reasonable to us, we call the threefold grade. For by three grades, namely the second, third, and fourth, the end of the cenobitic institution is fulfilled, and the earlier renunciation of pleasure is brought to completion. For where patience, the guardian of the virtues, is joined to the earlier renunciation of pleasure and to obedience, every pleasure and care of the body is mortified. Whoever has deserved to ascend this threefold grade has fulfilled the Lord’s command, by which he says in the Gospel: “If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself,” through the second grade of humility; “and take up his cross,” through the third; “and follow me,” through the fourth.

The fifth grade of humility is true confession of sin. A faithful cenobite is said to ascend to this if he confesses to his abbot, with humble trust, all evil thoughts that come into his heart, or any evil deeds committed in secret. For if anyone, through impatience and at the adversary’s prompting, sins in the preceding threefold grade, in this one he is restored through contrition and humble confession. For no one is found so perfect that he never contracts the stain of earthly conduct, as Scripture says: “In many things we all offend.” For no one is clean from sin, not even an infant whose life upon earth is one day. Therefore this grade necessarily follows immediately after the fourth, and in it all the negligences of the preceding grades are amended. True confession lifts up the soldier who falls in battle, heals the one wounded by the enemy, and strengthens the weak and faint-hearted. Therefore whoever desires to stand in battle and bravely conquer the enemy should more often have recourse to the defenses of true confession. For it is impossible for one wounded by the enemy to recover health if he neglects to reveal the wound itself to the physician. Therefore you, faithful cenobite, who desire to save your soul and to be a good soldier of Jesus Christ, do not omit humble confession, because you will never be able to conquer the devil better than if you are zealous to disclose faithfully to your abbot all the thoughts he suggests to you and your sins. It is certain to me, and proved by much experience, that a cenobite hands himself over to be greatly conquered by temptations when he refuses to keep this grade.

The sixth grade of humility arises from true confession: namely, that the monk judge himself vile and unworthy for all things. For according to the Rule we believe him to be a true monk whom we see content with every vileness and extremity in food, clothing, and humble patience. Vileness is referred to common useful things, but extremity is placed in abject and heavier hardships. For he gladly embraces with patience those things that are viler in clothing, food, and all necessities of the body; he delights in all things: to serve the sick, the foul, lepers, and the rejected; to be confounded, degraded, and despised. Therefore the monk who is truly dead to the flesh and the world should be content with every vileness and extremity, should show himself a faithful worker in all things, and should also believe himself vile and unworthy because of the corruption of nature, by which he offends in many things. For indeed, whatever good we do in this world, however holy and religiously we live in this age, it cannot suffice to merit eternal life unless the great mercy of God deigns to come to our aid. We receive the greatest things for the least: we expect the kingdom of God not as owed to us from our merit, but as promised from grace. For the holy Apostle says: “The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the future glory that shall be revealed in us.” But if we are judged without mercy by the justice of God, we are worthy of hell.
