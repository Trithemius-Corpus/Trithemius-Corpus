After confession.

It is our custom that those who are to celebrate on a given day should prepare themselves by confession. Therefore the devout monk, after making his confession, at whatever time has been appointed, should at once devoutly carry out the penance enjoined upon him; and he should not approach the altar unless sacramental confession has always first been made, so far as this can be done.

On leaving the church.

When each Hour has been completed in the divine office, or also whenever he begins, he may repeat the order and manner of praying at entering the church described above, and observe the same rite in each of his places. But if, for greater devotion, he wishes to vary this manner, he shall pray the following little prayers, or pray silently.

Before the high altar, bowing:

Blessed be the holy Trinity and the undivided Unity. We shall confess him, because he has shown his mercy to us. O highest majesty, beginning of all things, have mercy on me. Amen.

Then, after returning to the cell.

At each Hour of the day, when the devout lover of Christ has returned to his cell, let him fall on his knees before his bed and humbly give thanks to almighty God, because he has deigned to admit him, an insufficient servant, to his service, and there to number him among his sons. Therefore let him say this or a similar prayer.

Prayer.

I give thanks to you, almighty and eternal God, because you have deigned to call me, an unworthy sinner, to your service, not by any merits of mine, but by the sole condescension of your mercy. I beseech you, by the pain of the wounds of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, and by the shedding of his most holy blood, and by the power of his most innocent death: receive this sacrifice of my service for the glory of your majesty, for my sins, for your holy Church, and for the souls of all the faithful departed who are placed in the pains of purgatory, in that union of charity in which you received our same Lord Jesus Christ dying for us upon the cross; and grant me, through the merit of his passion, to love you in truth and to remain continually faithful in your service. Amen.

Morning recollection.

Then enter for a little while into the examination of your conscience, and see whether you still have in yourself the marks of a good cloistered monk described in the seven articles of the second region. If you know that anything is lacking, do not neglect to reform it as quickly as possible, lest little by little you vanish away through negligence. For it is written: “He who neglects the least things gradually falls.” And Augustine, the holy lover of God, says: “No sin is so small that it does not grow when neglected.” Therefore, when your conscience has been examined and reformed in holy fervor, turn to the Lord.

Prayer for perseverance.

Grant, we beseech you, almighty God, through the merit of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, and through the intercession of blessed Mary ever virgin, of all the holy heavenly powers, and of the blessed patriarchs, prophets, apostles, martyrs, confessors, virgins, and all your elect, perseverance for us in your will, so that we may be able continually to offer you service pleasing to you. Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

Approaching the bed, let him fortify himself with the sign of the holy cross; and while going up to his bed, with attentive spirit, let him say:

In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ I intend to lay myself down, so that, restored by his divine majesty, I may be able to praise him more fully through the day.

Sitting on the bed:

May the soul of our Lord Jesus Christ, the integrity of blessed Mary ever virgin, the sign of the holy cross, and the virtue of the Lord’s passion, and the intercession of all the saints, guard my mind and spirit. In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Signing himself, let him lie down.

The exercise of the monk at Prime.

Consideration I.

When our devout monk has been awakened for Prime, as usual, let him observe the order and manner of recollecting himself and of the prayers set out above before Matins.
