Let them not presume against you. Be conspicuous for uprightness of soul, renowned for the merit of your life, worthy of reverence for your zeal. Let the upright love you as a servant of God; let the wicked dread your face as that of a judge. Let no one in your sight presume to do anything alien to good morals, since all should know that you cannot tolerate injury done to God.

Thus Phinehas the priest merited an everlasting covenant, when, by zealously defending the Lord’s law, he bravely appeased his anger. If you see order perishing in the monastery, if your brothers have begun in any way to corrupt regular observance, do not, if you are God’s servant, keep silence about their vices. Now set yourself bravely on behalf of the house of the Lord; exercise zeal for God as much as you can; destroy the counsels of the malicious; withstand the rashness of the proud. If you are a true lover of God, by your face alone you will scatter wicked gatherings. For no one will lay bare his plan before you if he suspects your zeal to be opposed to it. But if the wicked do not fear your presence when they are doing evil, they certainly recognize no zeal for God in you.

Therefore, to show yourself a true servant of Christ, you ought, for love of God, gladly to forgive injuries inflicted on yourself, and to pray to God for those who injure you. But injuries against the Lord, which it is not yours to forgive without his will, you should rather avenge out of zeal for righteousness. Let love of blood-kin cease in these matters; let the bond of friendship withdraw far away; zeal for God must rightly be preferred to all things. For friendship with a man must be maintained in such a way that it is never preferred to zeal for God.

Woe to monks and abbots who so boldly cast away the zeal of God, who place human familiarities before the divine command, who through foolish compassion conceal the vices of their subjects, who observe the traditions of the holy Rule neither in themselves nor in others. And what shall I say? We discern among cloistered monks that obedience to commands is despised, zeal for righteousness is scorned, observance of the Rule is contemned. Two or more contract among themselves a covenant of carnal friendship under the name of charity; the weak entrust the sicknesses of their souls to those weaker than themselves, though the friendship of God especially requires purity of mind.

Each one thinks his own companion better than the rest; one loves another, deceived not spiritually but carnally, because, since they do not raise their affections to God, they love nothing but the consolations of the flesh. But when you see a monk seeking friendship with a weak brother, you ought to judge him not a servant of God but a lover of the flesh. For he who truly occupies his heart in the love of God cares nothing for human friendship. The love of Jesus is enough for him in all things; outside it he seeks nothing further. But he who seeks the consolations of his mind in outward things shows by his deeds that he does not love God with his whole heart.

The love of God loves solitude, hates the tumult of men, and does not require the conversations of friends. O good Jesus, sweet is your love, because it pierces the heart of the one who loves you; seeing you, it desires to see you; having you, the soul longs to embrace you. Truly, he who loves you forgets himself; he meditates on nothing but the sweetest pleasure of you. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves you; nothing is found anywhere that the soul desires except for your sake.

O with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is distressed; with what abundant tears the face is drenched in secret, because as often as it thinks of the beloved’s face enthroned in heaven, so often it weeps that it is desolate on earth. Meanwhile the mind, languishing with love, often attempts to say something about its most beloved Jesus; it tries also to rouse others to his love. But as soon as it begins to utter the sweetest name of Jesus, unwillingly, from the greatness of love, it falls silent. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, but is felt with sweetness in the secret of the mind.

I know who said, “My love is crucified.” Who is your love, O servant of God? Why do you weep? Why do you groan? Ignatius: “My love is crucified, and shall I not mourn? My Lord is dead, and shall I be able to hold back my tears?” Do not weep, O faithful soul, for although your love died to redeem you from death, yet now he has risen from the dead to crown you in heaven. Be faithful unto death, and he will give you the crown of life.
