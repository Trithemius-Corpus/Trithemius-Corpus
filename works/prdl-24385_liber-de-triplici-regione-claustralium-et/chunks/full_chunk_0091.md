A monk should not speak without permission, if he truly desires to be good. But when permission to speak has been granted, let him say only few and useful things; and let him know that idle and jesting words are condemned by the Rule in every place. Let him establish for himself such a manner of speaking, with circumspection in every place and time, that by speaking he offend no one. Let him be more willing to listen than pleased to speak; nor let him say those things that [unclear] suggests, if he fears the reproach of fools. For thus Hugh says to the monk: Do not pour out everything that I know, like a vessel without a lid, which, as soon as it is tilted, pours out whatever it contains, and lies open to every dust and filth. Carefully beware lest you seem, directly or indirectly, to seek your own glory, or imprudently reveal to anyone, from a desire for boasting, something praiseworthy that lies hidden in you, or [unclear]. If you keep silent about yourself and conceal it, you will be more pleasing; if you bring yourself forward and commend yourself, you will become worthless before all and will be mocked. Beware of idle words, because while we do not attend to such things, we slip little by little into harmful ones, from which are generated either [unclear] or dissolutions or grievous corrodings of patience. Be such in your inner way of life that, through love of spiritual meditation, silence delights you much more than talkativeness.
