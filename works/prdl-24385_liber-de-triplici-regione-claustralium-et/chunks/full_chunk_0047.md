What more could he have given us, he who, when we had been created and were not, gave himself? But what can we render to our Creator, since by our deserts we are sinners and weak? Let us render what we are; let us render what we owe: faithful service, for the body which we have received from the Lord, and sincere love for the soul, by which we were created in the image of God. Thus the prophet admonishes us in the psalm: “Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice before him with trembling.” Serve faithfully; love reverently. On the part of the body, offer faithful service to your Creator; on the part of the soul, love him with all your strength. Serve him, because he is powerful and great among lords; love him, because he is good. His power is shown in your creation; his goodness is made manifest in your restoration. For since he created you, when you were not, by his power, he deserves faithful service; since he restored the fallen by his goodness, he deserves the affection of sincere love. Truly he is our father through love; he is Lord through incomprehensible power.

Secondly, he gave us faith with the sacraments; for just as he created us without our will, so he called us to faith without our merit. For Christ says to his disciples in the holy Gospel: “You did not choose me, but I chose you.” Through faith the Christian soul is led back to its beginning; the sacraments renew it. Therefore, incorporated into Christ through faith and the sacraments which he gave, we are bound to cleave to him forever, and to remain continually in his holy covenant. For in baptism we renounced the devil and all his pomps; we promised that we would forever adhere to Christ, when, lifting us from the sacred font, our sponsors set us before the Lord in the faith of our parents. Let us therefore always say from the heart with the prophet: “It is good for me to cling to God, to put my hope in the Lord God.”

Thirdly, almighty God gave us the art of uprightness and the teachings of truth. For these we are bound to our Creator in faithful performance and in perpetual service without deceit.

Therefore we are commanded first of all to do everything for the praise of God, and in all that we do to raise our intention principally toward that good. For only a good intention in God distinguishes works; only love makes them fruitful. Behold, nature has bestowed on us the art of praising God; moreover, he has given us the teachings of salvation through the law; he requires from us praise and love above all things. Let us therefore exercise, for the honor of God, the skills entrusted to us, and let the pure eye of intention be formed through the teaching of the law.

Fourthly, the merciful Father bestowed on us the world with its necessary elements; for these he requires from us due contemplation of himself in sincerity. Whatever we behold in this world ought to lead us to the remembrance of God, and continually summon us to love its Creator above all things. Hence Bernard, trained in sacred things, says: The whole and true lover of God, wherever he turns, has a reminder of his familiar Creator; he uses things as a mirror, and from creatures is lifted up to the Creator by thinking thus about each one: If these things that are created in time, changed, and passing away seem good, beautiful, and sweet, what goodness, beauty, and sweetness does their author, God himself, possess, who remains eternal and has reserved incomparably better things for his own? By this consideration the devout person looks upon all people with the simple eye of the mind, as though in the likeness of God’s image, and does not curiously attend to whether the form of the body is pleasing or displeasing. In this he considers not what he sees outwardly, but the creature made in God’s image, whom he knows to be a sharer in eternal happiness.

Fifthly, the Son of God offered himself in torments for us, and redeemed us, lost as we were, from the destruction of perpetual death by his own blood. We are commanded to offer him a loving imitation of our Savior’s charity, if we desire to reach his kingdom. For the prince of the apostles says to the faithful: “Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow in his footsteps.” What shall I render to you, O good Jesus, for your benefits to me, so many, so great, so immeasurable?
