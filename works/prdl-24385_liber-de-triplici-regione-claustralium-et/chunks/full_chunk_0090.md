Let him order meditations on the Lord’s passion with the fervor of devotion, in which, according to the possibility of his labor, he may conform his labors to Christ’s labor. Let there always be in his heart humble self-denial. Let him be devout in affection with the fervor of holy union, so that the labor of the hand outwardly may advance toward the perfection and usefulness of the community, and the mind may not withdraw from the eternal sweetness of divine love. If labor wearies the monk at work, if heat burns him, if untimely thirst or hunger afflicts him, let him call to mind how many labors the Lord Jesus sustained for us, and he will bear all things lightly. Therefore, praying, let him say to the Lord:

Most sweet and most loving *Prayer of one who labors.*

Lord Jesus, king of heaven and earth, who for my redemption were born of the most pure Virgin Mary, and for thirty-three years lived among men as God and man; who had great labors in walking, teaching, and preaching; who for me willingly and patiently endured weariness, hunger, heat, and cold: for all these things I give you thanks. Receive, I beseech you, my very small labors, however useless, which I humbly offer to your most holy majesty in memory and honor of all your labors and of your most holy passion; and may they be pleasing to you in that union in which your labors were most pleasing to God the Father. Amen.

But under labor, if time permits, let our monk turn over the principal articles of the Lord’s passion: first those which were present to Christ in the passion by commemoration; then at his knees by giving thanks; third, by asking mercy in them according to [their?] common likeness.

Let him also recall to memory the conversations of the holy fathers or the passions of the martyrs, and consider how great a reward and glory they now receive in the fatherland for their labors.

*In speech let the monk be sparing and edifying*
