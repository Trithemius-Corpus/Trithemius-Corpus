Vicious lukewarmness flows from the fact that the will has not yet been purified; they do not will the good as they know it, being drawn away and enticed by evil desire. For they love earthly little consolations in their flesh, whether in word, or in signs, or in deeds, or in something else; and even if this sometimes interrupts these things, it does not entirely break them off. Hence it is that they rarely direct their affections toward God, and their compunction is not continuous, but only for an hour.

But a soul that lies under these distractions cannot be filled with divine visitations; and the more it is emptied of the former, the more fully it will be filled with the latter. If much, much; if little, little. Or perhaps, if you prefer it put more strongly: these things will never be able to be mixed with those for eternity. For spirit and flesh do not dwell together, nor fire and lukewarmness in one dwelling, especially since lukewarmness itself is accustomed to provoke vomiting in the Lord.

Therefore whoever desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the region of the spirit, should always take care to repel from himself the sluggishness of mind, and in holy purpose to burn continually with the fire of divine love. Nothing harms those who wish to serve God so much as lukewarmness of mind. It always arises at first from some small negligence, but unless it is cured at once, it grows wonderfully by degrees.

One who is placed near the lowest region always slips down into it, because the dregs of his former carnal way of life still subtly occupy his will, and on an easy occasion disturb his weak fervor of devotion. But no one can rise from the depth of hell with a lukewarm will; no one can overcome habit without a contrary habit, because unless some violence intervenes, the habit of sinning will always strengthen vice.

Rightly, then, this lowest region is called hell, since by a certain likeness of disturbance and aversion it is compared to hell. Now after this life there are three dwelling places of souls, which receive their citizens departing from the three regions and from this world.

The first dwelling place is called hell, and this is compared to the lowest region. For just as in hell no order, but perpetual horror, dwells, so in this lowest region all things are disturbed by the fury of a mind blinded by carnal affections. In hell all are evil, and in the lowest region nowhere are the good found. All who fall into hell have belonged to the lowest region. The lowest region always sends its citizens to the lower world, unless, led by penitence, while still living they have taken care to renounce dead works.

The second dwelling place is called purgatory, and this is compared to the second, rational region. For just as purgatory cleanses and perfects souls, and sends them, once purified, to the kingdom of heaven, so this second region washes its citizens, persevering within it through continual exercise, from the stains of sins, and disposes them for ascent to the third region, which is called spirit. In this dwelling place all are good, but still they must be purified so that they may become better; and in the second region nowhere are there evil people, but still they must always be exercised so that they may become better. This region sends its citizens who die within it to purgatory, because, even though all are good, they do not yet seem fit for the kingdom without temporal purification. For those who inhabit this middle region, however near they may seem to be to the highest, are still not yet entirely removed from the dust of the lower one.

The third dwelling place of the separated soul is called the kingdom of heaven, and this is compared to the highest region of the spirit. For just as in that heavenly homeland there is supreme rest and eternal peace, so in the region of the spirit there is continual tranquility of mind.

Here ends the first region.
