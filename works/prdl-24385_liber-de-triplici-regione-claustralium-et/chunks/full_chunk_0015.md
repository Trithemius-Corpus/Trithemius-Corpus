Carnal monks and carnal men of the lowest region are disordered among themselves in many different ways. Since, stirred up by animal passions in the manner of beasts, they do not direct themselves by the right judgment of reason, it is no wonder if they overturn the order of brotherly life. For a troubled conscience is always presumptuous and fierce, and an evil mind never has the joys of peace, but is continually shaken by the grief of vices and inwardly tormented by the everlasting fires of crimes. Therefore the monks, citizens of the dark region, blinded by evil and disordered affections, do not consider the end of the monastic profession; they love nothing but the advantage of this present life; they neglect their own salvation; they do not rise up to the love of eternity.

First, then, they are greatly disordered toward one another, when they cast aside the intellectual regard that should be had for brothers; when they do not perceive the image of God in man; when they do not think of the communion of grace and glory infused into human beings and to be bestowed in the future; nor do they take care to show one another the love of brotherly fellowship. They live with one another like horse and mule, concordant in bodily society: whatever is pleasing to sensuality they love, but whatever accords with reason they do not admit. One is loved by another not because he is a man, but because he is like his lover in the affection of sensuality. For the man of flesh loves in a man not virtues, not religion, not even nature itself, but flesh, blood, and bodily quality. Since, therefore, the love of flesh is carnal, it is easily changeable when occasion arises. For the citizens of this lowest region venerate manners like their own, and carnal monks always embrace carnal monks with affection. The man whom God has loved from eternity the disordered cenobite persecutes; the man whom God despises for his demerits, this monk venerates as holy.

Second, cloistered men are disordered toward one another by reason of dissimilarity, when either the seniors, stirred up by harshness and pride, despise their juniors, or the juniors stubbornly oppose their seniors. And this disorder is proved to be very contrary to the holy Rule, which commands seniors to love juniors with fatherly affection, and instructs juniors to obey their seniors humbly and for God’s sake. But since the seniors, citizens of this lowest region, withhold fatherly affection from the juniors, the lesser men burn with a heavy hatred; and because the young men consider that they are unjustly despised by them, they daily accuse their corrupt morals. Thus the weak young men learn the crooked morals of the seniors, and in his proverb he says, considering those greater than himself to sing: as the greater sing, so the lesser dance.

Third, monks are badly ordered toward one another when, casting away zeal for the good and mutual obedience, they take up an evil kind of zeal forbidden by the Rule. In words and deeds they commit various injuries against one another; one strives, out of rivalry, to watch another for evil; one is provoked by another to inflict insult. They reproach one another with their weaknesses; they are always panting for vengeance. Zeal for regular discipline is despised; the passion of indignation and bitterness holds sway. They meet one another with faces turned away, and by open signs they reveal how much hatred lies hidden in the breast. Not so, impious men, not so: for whoever persecutes his brother with hatred is proved to be a murderer and unworthy of the kingdom of God. O wretched condition always of carnal monks, which is nearer to hell the more it has been estranged from the fear of God. For they imitate not angels but demons, who by evil zeal delight to be discordant in the school of peace. Indeed, just as a brute animal does not abandon the savagery of its nature, so a beastly monk does not withdraw from the worst zeal.

In what way the monks, citizens of the lowest region, are disordered toward themselves.

Article Five.

The carnal monks, citizens of the lowest region, are disordered toward nothing more than toward themselves. For, having cast aside every consideration of themselves, they do not attend to the purpose for which they were created without end; and while each one pollutes himself with the filth of vice, the wicked mind recedes from the original uprightness of its condition. Nor is it surprising if the inhabitant of the lowest region becomes outwardly disordered toward others, since through various passions [unclear].
