Therefore, if you desire to fly to the mountain of contemplation, you must, by loving God most fervently, wholly exceed yourself. But if you truly love God, you will despise both yourself and all things for the sake of him alone. For he is truly the treasure of the soul; if anyone has obtained him through love, he will always think upon that treasure. This treasure is acquired by loving; no one understands its usefulness who has not received it through love. Whoever desires to have this inestimable treasure should continually fix his heart in the love of most sweet Jesus. For truly, he who loves has; he who has, loves. The mind that has deserved to possess this treasure of the love of Jesus remains continually with him in affection, in this highest region.

That this third and highest region of the soul rejoices in the greatest freedom of spirit.

Seventh, this most holy region of the soul overflows with great freedom of spirit; in it the mind, free from the passions of the flesh, reigns with God alone through love. For the holy Apostle says: “Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.” And again: “Whoever is led by the Spirit of God, they are the children of God.” For truly, the spirit abiding in this region is made free from every defilement of flesh and blood; stripped of every phantom of thought, naked and alone, it is lifted up into the darkness of divine contemplation. This is the true freedom of the soul: to rest in the fervor of divine love and to love none of the consolations of this world.

Therefore let a person consider himself, and diligently examine his love; and if he finds himself devoutly affected, often raised up to divine love by fiery desires even without prior thought, let him trust that he can rightly be counted among the citizens of this region. For the Spirit of God bears witness to our spirit, and when he raises the mind into the ray of divine love, he also makes it know the condition of its own state. This freedom of soul is acquired through the fervor of divine love, because the supreme Holy Spirit inhabits the heart of one who loves God, and grants true freedom to those who love.

In this most holy freedom divine wisdom is learned; the sweetness of divine goodness is tasted with inestimable delight; the mind, made most free, is carried above itself; resting in the silence of divine darkness, it is lulled to sleep. And this mystical wisdom is said to consist in a mind raised up and purified in God, which is hindered by no obstacle whatever from its effective advance. Ascending in this region, the mind kindled by love wholly abandons thoughts and images; it escapes alone, naked, and most free, because thoughts, on account of the office of memory, are compelled to remain in the middle region, and can in no way appropriate to themselves the highest region of the spirit, which is above all understanding. For one ascends here by love through affection and desire alone; far from here the phantasms of thoughts are driven away. For as soon as the mind begins to ascend by thoughts, it is thrust back and falls into the middle region.

Nevertheless, the ascent itself is begun through the operation of the intellect; when that soon fails, it is completed through affection. For the intellect stirs up desire into love of the highest good and, as far as it can, commends its beauty. But when affection, stirred by love, has begun to ascend to the contemplation of so great a beauty, the intellect, failing in amazement, falls silent. But the mind, not enduring the commotion of the intellect, alone and naked, most free from every image of thought, enters the dark mountain that shines above; contemplating the beloved, not face to face indeed but in a riddle, it fails because of the greatness of love, and, carried away, rests in silence.

We read that Moses, once the leader of the children of Israel, called by God, ascended alone into the dark mountain; and it is recorded that he kept the people back from approaching the mountain under threat of death. The Lord appeared in the darkness and gave the law to the people through Moses himself. For so it is written in Exodus: “The whole of Mount Sinai was smoking, because the Lord had descended upon it in fire; and its smoke went up as from a furnace. And the mountain was very terrible, and the sound of the trumpet gradually grew louder.”
