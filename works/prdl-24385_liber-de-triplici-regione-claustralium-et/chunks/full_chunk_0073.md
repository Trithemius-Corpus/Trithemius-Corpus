The fourth chapter is that of discussions and counsels.

We call the fourth chapter deliberative: when we summon the brothers to counsel about some difficult matter. How this chapter ought to be held, Saint Benedict makes clear in the Rule, in the passage about admitting brothers to counsel. Therefore the humble and true cenobite, when he has been called to counsel, approaches humbly, with reverence and modesty; when asked what he thinks, he says little; he is in no way offended if his own counsel is disregarded and another’s is approved. What he thinks he states humbly, but always preferring the counsels of others to his own, and with true submission he entrusts both himself and his judgment to be examined by the one presiding. For in deep humility he so annihilates himself that he always believes others more than himself, and trusts that good counsel is divinely inspired in those better than he; and in his mind he is accustomed to set his prelate before all, as Christ’s vicar. Indeed, the monk makes very great progress toward the mortification of his own will when, in giving counsel, he submits his own opinion to the abbot at all times with cheerful humility. But when the devout cenobite perceives that his counsel has been disregarded or rejected, let him give thanks to almighty God out of true humility, and recognize that an occasion for pride has been taken away from him.

At private Masses.

Since human nature, by its own strength, cannot gain entrance into the kingdom of heaven, it pleased God, the most merciful Father, mercifully to help us through the death of our Lord Jesus Christ, his only-begotten Son, so that we, who because of our weakness cannot approach the invisible majesty, might have a mediator approaching on our behalf, who is both God and man. Truly, the merit of the most holy passion of our mediator has opened to us the entrance of the kingdom of heaven, has given us the food of spiritual life, and, as the branch draws nourishment from the vine, so our mind truly lives in Christ. We receive this spiritual nourishment with special sweetness in the sacrament of the body and blood of this same mediator of ours, whenever we devoutly offer it to God the Father as an odor of sweetness. And this most sacred oblation of the body and blood of the Lord avails not only for the forgiveness of our sins, but also wonderfully assists the souls of the departed who are placed in purgatory. Therefore so noble a sacrifice must be offered with great and continual devotion, since all our good works, which of themselves are too worthless, become most pleasing to God the Father through this most holy oblation. Therefore, so that the devout cenobite may be able fervently to apply himself to the offering of this precious sacrifice, let him be willing to note four things in order.

Concerning the one serving at Mass.

The devout brother who is to serve at Mass ought to come with the greatest cheerfulness and fervor, and prepare himself most diligently to serve the priest. For Saint Hugh says: Serve willingly at Masses, for that office belongs to angels; and when you serve at that sacrifice, you make familiar to yourself those whose fellow-worker you stand as. If, according to the Savior’s promise, one who receives a just man in the name of a just man receives the reward of a just man, who will deny the reward of the celebrant to one who devoutly serves at Mass?
