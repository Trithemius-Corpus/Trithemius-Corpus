**The third article: this supreme region of the soul is called divine instruction.**

This supreme region of the soul is called divine instruction, in which the mind, burning with divine love, is taught not by man but by the Holy Spirit. For Christ said to his lovers in the holy Gospel: “When that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth.” And again, that word of the prophet: “And they shall all be taught by God.” The Holy Spirit of eternity dwells in the heart of the one who loves [unclear], and illumines it by his invisible teaching.

In this region the spirit chooses true wisdom, the best part of Mary, in silent tranquillity; mystical theology is taught in secret, in the ineffable sweetness of the divine darkness; the mind, disposed toward its beloved by the fervor of most holy love, speaks in secret with him and continually raises itself to him through desire. This wisdom, or mystical theology, can indeed be commended by man, but it cannot be taught except in divine instruction. For it is the work of the Holy Spirit when the mind is illumined in true contemplation.

But we must consider that mystical theology is acquired through the exercise of a purified affection and is taught through the fire of love. Speculative theology, which we call scholastic, is learned through the exercise of the intellect, while the mind devotes itself continually to study. For the acquisition of mystical theology, the purification of the affection from every entanglement is required, so that the will, depraved through aversion, may be reformed toward its beginning through the quest of inward love. For unless the affection has been purified from every disordered concupiscence, it does not ascend to the height of this most sacred wisdom. But if it has cast off sensual passions, if the human will has been conformed to the divine will, the mind is raised upward through fiery love and is lifted by anagogical movements into the immensity of the divine darkness.

No one understands the certainty of this theology unless he receives it; no one receives it unless he seeks it alone above all things through the sighs of love. The psalmist sought this when he prayed to the Lord: “O that my ways may be directed to keep your statutes at all times.” For by such movements of desire the holy man was borne upward to obedience to the divine will, so that in that same Psalm 118 he said almost no verse in which he did not place some word equivalent to the divine judgments. The exercise of this purification is begun in the second region and is completed in this one. For no small labor is undertaken, that the affection may be purified from the concupiscences of the world. But once the exercise of spiritual reformation has been taken up, the mind gradually advances in love, desires to ascend to this region by sure steps, hastens through desire to the invisible school of this theology, and, sighing with continual groans, knocks at the door of divine compassion.

But the mind is not admitted into the secret of this mystical wisdom unless, after complete purification of the affection, it is freed from every creature and unites itself to itself, and withdraws far from every reason or imagination of thinkable things. For unless it withdraws from every creature, it does not approach the Creator, who is above all things. Therefore in this school of divine instruction the Holy Spirit is believed to preside over those who learn: he teaches a person to love nothing in this world and to conform himself in all things to the will of God. Happy is he who has deserved to be the disciple of this master; happy is he who has entered this school through the desire of love, for in mystical silence he receives wonderful things that God has hidden from those who do not love him.
