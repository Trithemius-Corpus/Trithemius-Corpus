Sometimes contemplation raises the elevation of the mind toward God: when the mind has been divinely irradiated above human effort, it draws it, as though carried beyond itself, into the contemplation of divine things. This mode also lifts it up into the height of contemplation by three degrees. For sometimes understanding, divinely inspired for the good, receives something that wholly surpasses the mode of foreknowledge. The second degree raises the mind above effort, when, divinely enlightened, it receives something to which no human effort attains. In the third degree the mind is raised on high, when it is divinely illumined in contemplation above the limits of human nature and effort.

Sometimes, too, contemplation directs itself by alienation of mind toward the mountain of divine darkness, when, the memory of present things being lulled to sleep, the soul is drawn into an unknown state beyond human knowledge by the illumination of divine operation. And this mode belongs especially to this highest region of the spirit, in which the mind of the true lover of God, kindled with so great a fervor of love, perceives that it is often proved to be estranged from itself. For sometimes, by the greatness of devotion, it is rapt outside itself; sometimes, by exceeding wonder, it is lifted above itself. Sometimes, because of immense exultation, it leaves itself behind, and through the ray of love passes into the highest elevation of contemplation. But mortal man does not understand the loveliness of this most high and supreme region if he has not ascended to it, purified through experience of love.

Here ends the third region of cloistered persons.

Preface to the second part of the principal exercise of cloistered persons. It happily begins.

Since, with the help of our Lord Jesus Christ, we have recently completed the little work on the threefold region of cloistered persons, set forth for us by chapters, it remains now that, undertaking according to our strength the second principal part of the work, we describe the mode and form of daily spiritual exercise.

For there are many among cloistered persons who are weak and feeble; and of these, some perhaps would gladly ascend to the contemplation of divine love if they had a mode suited to beginners. For all who are established in holy religion must take the greatest care that the mind not grow sluggish in idleness, since whoever has failed in the continual labor of spiritual exercise gradually falls into the depth of the body. For just as this visible fire of ours cannot continue in its operation without fuel suited to it, so the love of inward compunction cannot remain in a person’s mind without the continual exercise of devotion. For when the labor of spiritual study has died through inward negligence, all compunction of devotion gradually vanishes. No one placed in a holy state can remain steadfast and firm if his mind does not strive each day to be renewed by some exercise. For when the fuel of divine love is withdrawn, devotion is changed into torpor. Scripture says: “In desires is every idle man.” Therefore, lest the mind gradually flow away through sloth from the holy fervor of the spirit, it must learn to renew itself daily by some spiritual exercise, so that, while the fuel of singular love is supplied day by day, the desire for greater progress may be more fully kindled.
