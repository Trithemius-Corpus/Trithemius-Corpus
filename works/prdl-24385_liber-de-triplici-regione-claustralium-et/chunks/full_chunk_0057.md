To the one principle of all things, clearly, unity alone can attain, because unity flees multiplicity and seeks the one. For the more you yield to multiplicity, the more you withdraw from unity. Therefore the mind, in this supreme region of the spirit in which man dwells, when it seeks to ascend by contemplation to its principle through love, is accustomed to cast away from itself every multiplicity clinging to imagination or understanding, so that, gathered in its own unity, it may be able to direct itself ardently toward that one with the simple affection of love, whom it knows to be above every position.

That this supreme region of the spirit is sweet and lovable by reason of its nearness.

Article two.

Secondly, this supreme region of the spirit is sweet and most pleasant by likeness and singular nearness. For the citizen of this most holy region is separated from the citizens above by only one clay wall, so to speak, since as long as the spirit inhabits the flesh, it cries out to heaven as though through a wall. But the clay wall of flesh is the prison from which the spirit, when set free and about to migrate to heaven, goes forth when it is separated from the body by death. But the spirit wounded by sins, which cannot lift itself to this region through the pure affection of love, is still said to stand farther away from the empyrean heaven. Those, however, who dwell in this most quiet region are nearest to the empyrean heaven, because, weighed down by no stains of sins, they despise all earthly and carnal things, and always long to live in mind and spirit among heavenly things.

Indeed, those who inhabit the empyrean heaven and those who dwell in the third region have one and the same delight. But this is the difference: these contemplate God in a mirror and enigma, while those contemplate him in clear vision. The sweetness of those contemplating is on both sides; here it is great, there it is greatest; here it is in the fierce desire of love, there in possession of the most blessed vision.

In this supreme region of divine contemplation the rational spirit recalls its powers and capacities into its own chamber; and there, free from every phantasm, it is empty of them, raises itself above itself into the dense darkness of the divine, and, lulled to sleep by ecstatic love, rests sweetly. Here no troublesome confusion of thoughts moves about, no imagining of phantasms, but the bare mind is lifted upward and contemplates without any thought or any imagination. For whenever the mind itself opens access to thoughts, it shows that it is descending again to the middle region, which belongs to reason. But since the mind of man cannot always remain unmoved on the summit of divine contemplation, it must often return to the rational exercise of the second region. If, however, it has slipped down into sensual passions, then it has again descended to the lowest region. But the spirit of the true lover of God, although because of the frailty of the flesh it cannot always remain in the pleasantness of contemplation, nevertheless strives more often, through the affections of fire, to lift itself to that region, so that it may fulfill the exercise of reason in such a way that it does not entirely lose the sweetness of inward delight. O how good it is to cling to the Lord; how sweet it is to attend to God alone, so that the mind may thirst for him above all things and always long to embrace him with burning desires. Indeed, the one who stands in this region is nearest to the empyrean heaven, and therefore is inflamed toward it with the greatest desires of love. O how many groans and tears the mind fervent with love is accustomed to send forth; how ardently it sighs for heavenly things, since the nearer it is to the empyrean heaven through the secret of contemplation while standing in this supreme region, the more ardent its desire to go forth from the prison of the body is known to be.
