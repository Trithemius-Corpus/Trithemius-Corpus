How greatly idle and useless words are against God, a sufficiently clear story in the *Lives of the Holy Fathers* warns us, concerning the infernal swine that appeared among brothers who were speaking useless things.

How greatly evil and pernicious words offend almighty God became plain in the five-year-old boy and blasphemer, whom the devil, snatching him from his father’s lap, carried alive to hell, as is believed.

How much [unclear] deeds and negligences concerning duty displease [God] is plain in the sons of Eli, who were negligent and took too little care over the rite of sacrifice.

How much even good works, customary ones, done [with obedience set aside?], displease God, Saint [Astion?] taught us by his own deed: although not against a [superior’s?] command, he did not carry the customary waters with impunity. And we read of many other similar cases.

But when our devout cenobite, in his recollections, finds himself burdened with things committed, or with any other negligences or sins, however grave, let him assume such contrition as is possible for him over what he has committed, a firm purpose of amendment to beware in the future, and penance for what has been committed. Therefore let him take to himself [unclear], and let him say with compunction:

Truth I. Lord my God, I confess [unclear] to your most holy goodness that in this or that way I have sinned against your will. For this I grieve deeply; and because I have offended you, who are wholly good, I humbly do penance.

Truth II. Lord my God, who know all things, you know that, by your generosity, I have a good intention, and I intend, with your help, to amend myself in the future as far as I can. He should beware of sins by prayers, calling upon God for help; by inflicting penitential pains on the body; by flight, avoiding the occasion of sin. Therefore, for the third truth, let him say:

Truth III. Lord my God, I have a good will to make satisfaction for my sins according to your command and that of holy mother Church, [unclear], wholly and without pretense.

Whoever has uttered these three truths to the Lord from sincerity of heart, without falsity in mouth or mind, however much he has sinned before, is now secure, if he dies in such a state even without confession in the absence of a priest; yet in such a way that in purgatory he makes satisfaction for what he has not completed by doing penance. But whoever, because of the pleasure of sinning in which he actually delights, or because of a wicked intention by which he scorns to avoid sins and their occasions, cannot truly say these same truths from a pure mind, is in a state of damnation; not even the pope can absolve him.

*II. The Declaration of Future Caution*

The second thing which he prescribes as to be observed by the cenobite in his recollection is called the declaration of future caution. Let him weigh what have been the occasions of his past transgression, and he will more easily guard himself from future danger. For the pit of sin is never better stopped up than if the occasion of sinning is avoided. And Saint Bernard says: To the approaching night, indeed, an announcement must be made, by distributing one’s exercises according to the rule of the common institution. In these things he duly pays every debt, if the spirit is subjected to God and the flesh to the spirit, and if anything has been omitted through negligence or imperfection, it does not remain unrecompensed in manner, time, and place.

*III. Request for Suffrages*

The third thing in the recollection of a good cenobite is the devout request for suffrages; for he, understanding his own weakness very well, asks help from those stronger than himself. Therefore, when he has completed his recognition [of himself?], he turns to the holy and undivided Trinity to seek mercy; then he calls upon the most blessed Mother of God; afterward he implores the prayers and aids of all the saints. And here he remembers not only himself, but pours out prayers for the whole order and for the Church.
