At the second signal.

When the second signal sounds, the monk, now sufficiently recollected within, should leave his cell with composure, always blessing the Lord in his heart. With the prudent virgins let him hasten to meet the bridegroom; let him consider that he hears the signal of the great king. On leaving his cell, let him say:

Protect, Lord, your people by the sign of the holy cross from all snares of every enemy, that we may render you pleasing service, and that our sacrifice may become acceptable to you. Direct us, Lord, in the way of peace, to the everlasting glory of your name.

On entering the church, let him say:

Blessed are they who dwell in your house, Lord; they shall praise you forever and ever. Cleanse me, Lord, from every defilement of mind and body, that I may be worthy to praise and bless you in your house forever.

Passing before the crucifix, let him say:

I beseech you, most loving Lord Jesus, by all the bitterness of your most holy Passion which you endured for me, and especially when your most noble soul went forth from your body: have mercy on my soul at its departure. Amen.

At the holy water:

Take away from me, Lord, all my iniquities, that with a pure mind I may be worthy to enter into the holy of holies. You shall sprinkle me, Lord, with hyssop, and so forth.

Before the image of the blessed Virgin Mary:

O blooming rose, beautiful mother of the Lord. O gentle virgin. O most fruitful vine, brighter than the dawn, pray for us continually.

Passing before the altar, let him say:

Glory to you, Lord, who were born of the Virgin, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto everlasting ages.

Or:

You, the unbegotten Father, you, the only-begotten Son, you, the Holy Spirit, the holy and undivided Trinity, I praise, bless, and adore without end. O highest unity, beginning of all things, have mercy on me for your own sake.

Before the venerable sacrament:

Hail, living host, truth and life, in whom the ancient sacrifices are ended. Through you the glory of the homeland is given without end. Through you the Church stands ever fortified. Grant me rewards in eternal life. Amen. Hail, saving host.

Praying in his place in choir.

When he has come to his customary place of standing in choir, if there is time, let him consider what our holy father Benedict said in the Rule about reverence in prayer and psalmody. Looking carefully at the matter of the psalms to be sung, let him strive to subject his intention to them, and diligently foresee to what he wishes to refer each thing. If the morning office is being sung for some saint, let the psalmody be said before the Lord in memory of that saint. But if the subject matter of the psalms fits the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, it should be referred to his memory. Yet if the spirit of compunction is present to the singer, it will teach all things through love. For a mind fervent in the love of God knows very well how to direct its intention to the praise of the Creator, and how, according to the nature of the psalmody, to vary it suddenly in individual verses. For according to the subject of what is being sung, it exercises itself on whatever it wishes: now it embraces the humanity of Christ, now suddenly in another verse it is raised up to his divinity. Nevertheless, if it pleases him, our faithful monk may, while the same signal lasts, bring back to mind the fervor of the saints and angels praising God without ceasing, and conform himself to them by desire, according to his powers.
