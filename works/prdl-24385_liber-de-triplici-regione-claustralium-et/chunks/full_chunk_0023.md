The blinded do not come to the grace of true compunction. They approach confession superficially, from habit alone. They have not abandoned their depraved will. They do not truly take up a resolution of amendment. Such people, buried in the habit of sin, do not fear the censure of strict examination; they do not withdraw from blindness of mind through the remedy of contrition. As they now despise the long-suffering goodness of God, so in that terrible hour they undergo the sentence of eternal death. What can confession of the mouth avail where there is no true contrition of heart? Without contrition, confession is not saving; nor can contrition exist without a resolution of amendment. The sign of contrition is the will to sin no more.

Fifth, the disordered citizens of the lowest region, made despisers of almighty God through the habit of sinning, continually fall into graver dangers of soul, because they always approach the most holy sacred mysteries of the Lord’s body and blood in an evil manner. They make a superficial confession from habit; they allow the great beasts of vices to run through their senses. And thereafter, being in league with hell, they do not fear God; they despise the paths of amendment of life; they think themselves well ordered; they do not care with how many vices they are inwardly filled. Sometimes, sensibly moved by a fanciful devotion, they suppose that the Holy Spirit is now present; they affirm that they are beloved by God and holy. They impudently thrust themselves into the divine mysteries; not discerning the Lord’s body and blood, they receive them to judgment and condemnation.

Such people, communicating unworthily, are gravely hardened; they think themselves very secure and living well, and they do not duly care to cleanse their conscience from vices. Thus Judas the apostate did not fear boldly and shamelessly to complete the evil of betrayal which at first, as someone says, he feared to carry out against the Savior, after the morsel entered and the devil entered into him. O how many citizens of this lowest region there are: carnal monks, puffed up, proud, envious, murmurers, and entangled in various sins, whose conscience is rarely or never made clean by true contrition, and who nevertheless do not fear to approach the most holy altars almost every day.

Behold the evil company of all who dwell in this lowest region, whose end is destruction, for whom, after bodily death, a descent into hell is prepared. This is the far country of beastliness and unlikeness, into which that prodigal son went away, and squandered the portion of his inheritance luxuriously with harlots. In this region carnal citizens dwell, of whom the holy Apostle says: “The animal man does not perceive the things that are of God.” In this dark region, covered with the gloom of the dead, no spiritual good dwells; in it there is not peace, but beastly discord reigns, the prophet saying: “There is no peace for the wicked, says the Lord.”

Concerning Those Who, Being Near This Lowest Region, Often Fall Back [unclear] Through Inconstancy of Mind

Although between the first region, which is evil, and the second, which is good, there is properly no middle, nevertheless there are some who often move between the two: at one time, from weakness, they are lukewarm; at another, from contrition of mind, they seem to be devout. These are weak people. Though they are not submerged in the lowest region, nevertheless through negligence they are very near it, and frequently indeed they fall into that evil habit, because they do not withdraw, as far as they can, from the affections of the flesh. Of these Bernard says in a certain sermon: “Pernicious lukewarmness, how dangerous it is, how troublesome and full of misery and grief, and plainly nearest to hell! It is rightly judged the shadow of death. Indeed, one finds people faint-hearted and slack, failing beneath the burden, needing rod and spurs; their joy is slack, their sadness faint-hearted, their compunction brief and rare; their thought is animal, their conduct lukewarm, their obedience without devotion, their speech without circumspection, their prayer without intention of heart, their reading without edification. Finally, as we see, fear of Gehenna scarcely restrains them, shame scarcely holds them back, reason scarcely bridles them, discipline scarcely keeps them in check.
