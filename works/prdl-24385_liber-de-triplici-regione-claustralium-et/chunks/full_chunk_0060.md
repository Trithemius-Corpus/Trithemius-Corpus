The Scripture says concerning the summit: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” A beast is said to touch the mountain when a man, not yet purified by the exercise of the second region, tries to ascend to the third. For as long as his affection has not been cleansed from the passions of his sensuality, the beast still lives in him. But if the mind of man permits itself, with its beastliness, to approach the mountain of God, it will be overwhelmed by as many stones as there are movements of the passions by which it is wounded.

For the wise man says: “The sluggard is stoned with a muddy stone, and all will speak in contempt of him.” By the muddy stone carnal affection is meant, because man is compared to mud. For it is written in Job: “Remember, I pray, that you made me like clay, and you will bring me back to dust.” And again he says: “I am compared to mud, and I am likened to ashes and dust.” Thus the sluggard is overwhelmed with a muddy stone when the mind, still unclean with carnal and sensual passions, attempts to ascend to the mountain of contemplation; there, as unworthy, it is forced to suffer repulse and ruin by the hard stones set against it. For no one will be able to reach this most tranquil region of the spirit who has not first cleansed himself by long exercise in the furnace of the middle region. Whoever, still given over to carnal passions, tries to approach this highest region of the spirit is striving, as it were, to draw water in a sieve. For this region does not admit carnal minds into itself, since it is prepared for hearts purified from every affection.

That this supreme region of the spirit is magnificent in restoration.

Article V.

It diligently calls strangers and pilgrims as if to their father’s dwelling. In this region, returning like the prodigal son, he receives us mercifully; he rushes with love into our embrace; he clothes us with the first robe; he grants the garment of charity to those who love. Why, then, do we neglect to return as quickly as possible to the most indulgent Father, whom we see ready for our embrace even after our contempt? We wretched ones have withdrawn by our own will and by our deserts; we squander the portion of our inheritance with the prostitutes of carnal affection; we have been shamefully stripped of all beauty and paternal adornment. Why do we not return to the Father, whom we know to be ineffably merciful? Let us return at least, I beg, after our fault, since we have scorned to avoid ruin.

Return, return, wretched soul; return, you who are grievously captive in your affections; return, you who are mortified in sorrows. Why do you delay? Why do you tarry? Return, faithful soul, to the leader of your youth; return to your former husband; go back to your most loving Father, who awaits you with love and desire. For behold, with patience he awaits you, and through Isaiah the prophet he cries to you: “Return to me, for I have redeemed you. I have blotted out your iniquities like a cloud, and your sins like a mist.”

Why, soul, are you in the land of the enemy? You have grown old in a foreign land; you are defiled with the dead; you are counted among those who descend into hell. Remember that almighty God created you from nothing; consider that he formed you to his image and likeness; remember from where you have fallen; see where you have gone, among swine, and are trampled under the feet of filthy affections. Your Creator placed you from the beginning in this supreme region of the spirit; he entrusted to you the natural exercise of love for him; he bestowed on you the hope of life that will endure forever. But you have abandoned the fountain of wisdom; you have gone out into the lowest region of unlikeness; you have squandered the promised goods of your inheritance. But return, even late at last, to the exercise and repentance of the middle region, until, purified from all your iniquities, you deserve to be brought back to the pleasantness of the region [unclear].
