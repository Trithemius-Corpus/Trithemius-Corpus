Thus the Lord Jesus himself revealed to those lovers of his to whom he wished: that nothing is more pleasing to him, and nothing more useful to us, than to recall the memory of his passion and death with thanksgiving, and to admonish others to do the same. Receive, therefore, the abbreviated word, lest a longer prayer make you weary.

*With knees bent on the ground and hands clasped together, say:*

I give thanks to you, most sweet Lord Jesus, for all the wounds, pains, and injuries of your most innocent passion and death; I pray you, through that same death of yours, have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death.

To your most bitter passion and your most innocent death, Lord Jesus Christ, compassionating in spirit, I give thanks; I pray you, have mercy on me.

I praise, bless, and adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, king of heaven and earth, and I give thanks to you for your holy passion, by which you delivered me, a wretch, from perpetual death. I pray you, through the power of that same passion of yours: make me a participant for ever in your most innocent death.

*Conclusion of this compendium, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.*

And, lest I be more prolix than is fitting, here I set an end to the compendium. For the venerable whole assembly of the abbots of our annual chapter commanded me in this place to avoid prolixity, lest lukewarm and worldly cloistered men make weariness their excuse. If the Lord Jesus, as I have always wished, gives us a life free from cares, I shall try to satisfy both my own intention and another’s request. For I have resolved, at the request of certain virgins dedicated to Christ, to hand down a rule by which any devout cenobite may exercise himself in the Lord’s passion against all temptations; and if he continues in it for a few months, exercising himself as he should, little by little he will fly sweetly from the humanity of Christ to his divinity by the ray of love. For to those who desire to arrive at the love of God, continual meditation on the Lord’s passion affords an easy approach, because while the mind, through compassion arising from the memory of the benefit of so great a lover, is pierced with compunction, it is lifted up, burning with tears and desire, to love. You, therefore, cloistered men dedicated to God, who have no leisure to attend to many things, receive these few things; in them, although there may not be a continuous blaze of love, it can nevertheless be renewed for those who weep.

*Here ends the compendium of daily spiritual exercise, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot.*
