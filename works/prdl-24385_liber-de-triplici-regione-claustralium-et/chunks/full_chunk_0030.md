Then let our faithful cenobite not neglect to strip his memory of every occupation with an unlawful thing. Let him admit no forms of thought that are shameful or less useful; let him strive to repel the phantasms of irrational passions and [unclear] meditations, and, so far as can be done, to think nothing foolish.

The will also must be purified from every disordered affection, so that it wills nothing except what right reason suggests should be willed, and rejects nothing except what reason, according to the judgment of truth, forbids it.

But this restraint of virtue and cleansing of the powers, without which we say that no one can advance at all, is granted only through diligence great, discerning, and constant in a strong battle. We know that the kingdom of God suffers violence, and no one can subject the enemy to himself unless he has resolved to fight against him fiercely and perseveringly. For we have learned from the testimony of the blessed pontiff Augustine that a man does not die well unless he has first died to vices and desires. But no one truly dies to these among men unless he resists his own wills through continual labor, sowing in tears. Therefore the soul must be fortified with saving doctrines; the strength of patience must be taken up in all things. For just as a naked man, unarmed and impatient of labor, cannot overthrow an armed enemy, so a monk without spiritual arms and without a certain continual force of fighting cannot cleanse his intellectual powers in any childish way. Now although the arms of spiritual warfare are many in its progress, three especially must be taken up by the monk.

First, indeed, vigor is necessary for the cenobite in all that he does, so that he may bravely dare things contrary to the flesh, and in no way grow lukewarm in his holy purpose. For spiritual exercises are hard and altogether contrary to the flesh, and he who scorns to put on most strongly the arms of vigor is not enough to persevere constantly in them. Therefore, if you desire to subject all your affections to the spirit, if you wish to fight successfully against the vices of the flesh, take care to do prudent violence to yourself and to arm yourself bravely against all things.

Next, a certain holy severity must be taken up by you, through which all contrary and adverse things of this world are borne as though they were nothing. For truly, whoever for love of God and desire for heavenly things has once genuinely taken up this kind disposition of the soul, no longer fears any adversities of the flesh; rather, he is so deeply filled within by contemplation of the divine reward that he seems to delight more in adversities that occur than in prosperity.

But severity is by no means pleasing to the weak unless it has been somewhat tempered by the sweetness of kindness. Therefore kindness itself must next be taken up by the soldier, so that when, tempted in battle by the weakness of the flesh, or even wounded, he sometimes wavers, he may soon rise again through repentance to fight once more with greater strength. For the soldier of Christ, if he falls in battle, must rise again through contrition and the other remedies of penance as often as necessary, to fight again, until at last, at some time, by God’s mercy, he is able to overcome the adversary. If the just man falls seven times in a day and rises again, how should the sinner, now penitent, not hope for pardon? Rise, soldiers, to fight again as often as you have fallen when the adversary has conquered. Always renew the warfare more strongly from the beginning, so that the enemy whom you cannot yet destroy by strength, you may at last overcome by perseverance. Do not despair of victory, faithful cenobite, because you have often been conquered by temptation and vice and have fallen into the snare. Do not lie prostrate any longer because you feel yourself weak, but rise again as often as you have fallen, and trust that you have a merciful Lord, by whose help you will at last be victorious against the adversary. For no one suddenly becomes perfect, nor does God grant immediate victory over the enemy to incautious soldiers, lest those who are about to perish fall into grave pride.
