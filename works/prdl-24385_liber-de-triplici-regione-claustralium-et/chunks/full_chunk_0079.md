Let our devout cenobite keep in his heart the merit of the Lord’s passion, upon which the whole office of the Mass may sweetly return, as upon a foundation stone. For every Mass, of whatever kind it may be, which is said in the holy Church of God, is at all times referred to the merit of the Lord’s passion.

On the Introit of the Mass.

We do not intend to explain the office of the Mass point by point, since each of us can find many expositions by others; rather, we wish to give the devout brothers an occasion for meditation, whom we know to be inflamed with inward desires. You may, however, in meditation refer the first part of the introit and the verse to the incarnation of the Lord, and refer the repetition with the Gloria to his nativity. Nevertheless, you must diligently consider what the introit contains according to the literal sense, so that you may be able to find a spiritual understanding suitable for yourself. For nothing is done in vain in the divine office; rather, all things are filled with mysteries and sacred actions.

On the Kyrie eleison.

Kyrie eleison is Greek, composed of a noun and a verb, and among them they are two words. The first is from kyrios, which means “Lord”; kyrie is said in the vocative. The second is from eleeo, which means “I have mercy”; eleeson, “have mercy,” is pronounced in the imperative. From this comes Kyrie eleison, that is, “Lord, have mercy,” by elision for Kyrie eleison, since a vowel before a vowel can be elided for a better sound.

Three times, therefore, he says Kyrie eleison to the Father, so that he who, out of exceeding charity, willed his Son to be incarnate of the Virgin and to die for us, may also be willing to come to our aid by his mercy in our necessities.

To the Son also we say Christe eleison three times, in which we pray him to come to our aid.

Three times also we utter Kyrie eleison to the Holy Spirit, in which we humbly call upon him for our help, according to his ineffable goodness.

On the Gloria in excelsis.

Then Gloria in excelsis Deo is said in the singular number, in which we confess the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit to be one sole true God in a Trinity of persons and a unity of substance.

Accordingly, during that angelic hymn the cenobite ought to be very devout, and, so to speak, especially attentive to each word; in it the gift of peace is promised to men of good will, and God is humbly adored by us in various expressions.

At the Collects.

When the collects or prayers are offered by the priest to God, the devout cenobite should think of nothing else, but, listening to the priest’s words, should present his own desire to God in agreement with them.

At the Epistle.

When the epistle is read, you must attend diligently to everything, and give spiritual thanks to God with the greatest fervor, because, leaving behind the Jewish people, he so mercifully willed to call you and your forebears from Gentile error to his faith.

At the Gradual.

At the gradual, alleluia, sequence, and tract, think how long a desire the holy fathers had, who before the incarnation of Christ longed for his coming. Then consider with how many labors and injuries your Savior was wearied for you in this world for thirty-three years, who voluntarily gave himself up to death for your salvation. Then call to mind the long exile of your soul in the flesh and amid dangers, so that by this abundant meditation, full of misery, you may inflame your affection toward divine love.

During the Gospel.

At the holy Gospel, with your mind lifted up to the Lord, give thanks with rejoicing, because the merciful Father willed you to be born of Christian parents, and because he made you a sharer in his holy and most sweet teaching.

During the Creed.

When the symbol of the catholic faith is sung, consider all its words and mysteries with humble fervor of spirit, [unclear].
