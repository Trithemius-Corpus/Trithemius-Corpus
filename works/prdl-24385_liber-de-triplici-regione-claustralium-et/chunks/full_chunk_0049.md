God created you by his goodness, not because your creation was necessary to him, but because it was useful to you alone. Whoever considers this goodness of God with intent meditation rightly praises his creator.

Second, lest you be lifted up into pride, O man, consider the vileness of your condition: you were made from the mud of the earth, and after a little while, consumed by worms, you will return to the dust of the earth. Why then are you proud, earth and ashes? Your conception has guilt, your birth misery, your life punishment, your dissolution the anguish of death. For man, born of woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries; and he is then most miserable when he does not recognize himself as miserable in the flesh.

Third, consider the origin of your conception, and you will find your soul stained with the foulness of original sin. For we are born children of God’s wrath; but by the grace of baptism, in the blood of Christ Jesus, we are cleansed from the original stain. Through this consideration we rightly love our Savior, by whose mercy we have been cleansed from the original stain.

Fourth, faithful soul, consider how very foolish and [unclear] you are when you repeat your ways in the act of sin: you, who have presented yourself as an adulteress to Christ your bridegroom, though he joined you to himself by his own blood, wholly unstained. What are you doing, O wretched one? Why do you desert your most loving bridegroom? Or why, foolish one, do you withdraw from your beginning? Know that you have been placed in the flesh, not so that you should yield to vicious and concupiscent impulses, but rather so that you should resist their prompt command. For as often as you hand yourself over to the disordered motions of the flesh, contrary to divine rectitude, by dissembling your [unclear], so often, turned away from the good, you show yourself an adulteress to your bridegroom. Beware, beware, O soul redeemed by the blood of Christ, lest love of the flesh deceive you; for just as the first man, deceived by love of his companion, the woman, fell into death, so every soul that consents to the pleasures of its flesh shamefully withdraws from its beginning, which is God. But alas, wretched soul, where have you fallen? You have dwelt in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been counted with those who go down into hell. You have forsaken the fountain of wisdom; you have sought a backward path, adultery.

But hear my counsel, and return as quickly as possible to the guide of your youth, Jesus. Submit yourself wholly to him in humility. He is merciful; he will gladly receive you when you return to him, forgive your sins, and, if you ask, bestow the grace of his love. But you must withdraw from the affections of the flesh if you desire to come into the sight of your Savior. Therefore, if you turn to Jesus, turn in truth, because supreme Truth can love only what is true.

There is also another mode of self-knowledge, which is taken up with respect to God whenever one thinks upon the incomparable comparison between God and man. Concerning this mode Bernard, renowned in contemplation, says: “The mode of human imperfection is never better perceived than in the light of God’s countenance and the mirror of divine vision. For there one who is more in the day, and sees more clearly what is lacking in himself, amends day by day through likeness whatever he has lost through unlikeness, drawing near to him by likeness from whom he has been made far off by unlikeness.” Let us therefore raise the gaze of the mind to the most bright mirror of divine perfection; let us submit our affection to that unchangeable good, so that in God’s wisdom we may be able to perceive our foolishness, and in his power consider our weakness.

On Keeping the Memory of One’s Sins

Root Six

The fifth root of the fear of God is the memory of sins, and the foulness of sin itself often pondered. For anyone who truly understands the ugliness of mortal sin always dreads the pit of this evil. But so that you may in some measure recognize the gravity of sin, you must consider the seven things that follow.

First, then, consider the beauty of God’s justice, to which sin is wholly contrary, and for that reason must be fled above all. For through sin the soul turns utterly away from the highest good, and the love that is naturally in it toward its beginning is changed into hatred.
