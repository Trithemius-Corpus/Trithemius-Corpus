For when our mind, confirmed in the faith of Jesus Christ, has become purer through love, then, having been made more like the holy angels, it obtains greater intimacy with them. And whoever has obtained complete intimacy with the holy angels will, whenever he wishes, shine forth in miracles, God granting it. For the holy angels reveal to pure men, fervent in the love of God, secrets hidden from others, and by command of the Creator they make many things manifest to them. For the Lord is near to those who call upon him in truth. The Lord will do the will of those who fear him, and he will hear their prayers and save them. For whoever by grace is one with Christ is refreshed by frequent visitation of the angels, and often, when necessity demands it, is made illustrious by signs and powers through their ministry. Hence it is that even those weighed down by various illnesses betake themselves to the intercessions of the saints, and, turning over in their mind the promises of the Lord, obtain through the merits of others what they cannot obtain by their own. Their holy devotion in the faith of Christ receives the Lord’s salvation, because, aided by the purity of their own conscience and supported by the merit of another’s holiness, it rises, not rashly, into certain hope.

Miracles are done in the Church of God by divine power with a threefold respect or means. Sometimes almighty God works a miracle through the ministry of angels, solely by the merits of the one who performs it, as Saint Martin healed the blind man and the lame man against their will. Sometimes miracles are done solely by the merit of the sufferer, as we read that many were cured through bad Christians whom the sick believed to be very good in the faith. Third, there is no doubt that healings of this kind are done by the merit of both, that is, of the sufferer and the one performing it together, as Christ says to a certain person in the holy Gospel: If you can believe, all things are possible to one who believes. For faith is required for a miracle to such a degree that our Lord Jesus Christ, coming into his own country, marveled that he could do no mighty work there because of their unbelief. But none of these things is done among unbelievers, where neither holiness in the working nor orthodox faith in the sufferer is found.

Third, then, a man performs miracles through the mediation of the cooperation of diabolical wickedness, and this can happen in three ways: namely by manifest invocation of demons, by involvement, and by supposition.

By manifest invocation, necromancers and the little women whom we commonly call witches do wondrous things, who, corrupted by a likeness of will, have passed into fellowship with demons. For just as in the Church of God the sacraments are signs of the invisible grace of the Holy Spirit, so in the school of demons certain characters and modes of invocation are marks of the pact between man and the malignant spirit. Hence, when witches display the signs of demons, the malignant spirits invoked are accustomed gladly to appear, and to produce both horrible acts of witchcraft and certain wondrous things.

But witches and necromancers produce wondrous effects by the invocation of demons in different ways. Witches subject themselves to demons by a certain kind of profession; by their ministry they disturb the air, raise storms, lay waste crops, make men and beasts sick, carry on foul commerce of most filthy lust with demons, and by destructive spells call back from the underworld into sight whomever they wish. Their deeds are not to be called miracles, but rather acts of witchcraft, and they deservedly are to be punished with the utmost penalty.

Necromancers, however, subject themselves to demons not by manifest profession but by an implicit condition, when they claim that they can compel them visibly to the circle not only by conjurations and the display of characters, but also by sacrifices, fumigations, and vows. Their crimes are countless, since it is plain that all of them conspire in fellowship with demons, either implicitly or explicitly. Although necromancers are not all equal either in art or in practice, nevertheless all are agreed, to their own destruction, in damnable curiosity, by which they desire to obtain by demonic aid what they cannot discover naturally. All their works and books are full of superstitions and impiety, and for that reason they must be driven far from the borders of believers.

There is a great multitude of enchanters and necromancers in the East, who by their illusions miserably deceive the human race, and have turned almost all Greater and Lesser Asia away from the faith of Christ. Illusions can be produced in three ways: by the ministry of demons, by the operation of nature, and by the deception of human art.
