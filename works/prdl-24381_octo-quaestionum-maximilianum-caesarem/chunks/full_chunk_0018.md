Why Scripture, containing the mysteries of the Old Testament and of our faith, was not given open, manifest, everywhere uniform, and plainly sufficient for all things, but rather wrapped up and in many places full of enigmas. For many things necessary for the perfection of faith are not found sufficiently expressed in the divine Scriptures. To this most sacred question of your Majesty it seems one must reply.

All Scripture, inspired by God, is the perfect work of the Godhead, foreordained before all time, written in time, not otherwise than God willed, who inspired men that it should be written. But all God’s works are perfect; and therefore, since the Scripture of which we speak is God’s work, it is necessary to confess that it is perfect in all things. For God, the supreme judge, all whose works are perfect, gave to his Scriptures the order that he willed and as he willed, and without doubt infused into those who received them the manner in which they should write. For almighty God bestowed such authority and majesty upon the sacred Scriptures that, in comparison with them, all the words of this world fail, since divine things cannot properly be comprehended in human speech.

Therefore divine Scripture, although on the surface of its words it may appear confused to the ignorant, seems manifest to those who know it, since unless it is read in that Spirit by which it is believed to have been infused into men, one by no means arrives at an understanding of it. The learned man who burns with vain desires under the shelter of the flesh reads it in one way, and the understanding man whom the love of God truly inflames, stripped of pleasures, reads it in another. Hence it is no wonder if men who are wise according to the flesh do not understand the divine Scriptures, since, blinded by the pleasures of the flesh, they have never perceived the sweetness of God’s Spirit.

Just as, if you present to a boy learning the first elements of letters any written volume whose character is unlike that in his little book, when he cannot read the writing he will at once cry out that it is bad and not properly formed; in the same way, men who approach the reading of the divine Scriptures with only fleshly wisdom, who do not grasp the things that are of the divine Spirit, weighed down by vices and rashness, when they ought to have blamed their own ignorance and unworthiness, reproach the holy Scriptures which they do not understand. For it is not permissible for all indiscriminately to interpret the divine Scriptures, lest even a beast touching the mountain be overwhelmed with stones.

Now, in a perverse order, many arise who have spent the whole time of their life in humane letters and, having become old men, wrongly interpreting the divine Scriptures which they never learned, do not explain them but confuse them, and examine them according to their own sense. Hence whoever does not understand the sacred Scriptures by that Spirit by which they were infused into those who wrote them has a veil of ignorance and rashness spread over his heart in the manner of the Jews; and from the very place from which the humble, approaching in the Spirit of God, have received the cup of salvation, they very often drink the poison of pernicious error.

For heavenly doctrine is not contained in the words of the Scriptures but in the sense, not on the surface of the sayings but in the understanding, not in the leaves of ornate speech but in the hidden depth of mystical inspiration. Hence the holy Pope Gregory says: Sacred Scripture far surpasses all the sciences of this world in the manner of its speech, because in one and the same discourse, while it narrates the text, it discloses the hidden mystery; and just as it refreshes the simple on the surface, so it exercises the wise with mystery. For it has openly that from which it may nourish little ones.
