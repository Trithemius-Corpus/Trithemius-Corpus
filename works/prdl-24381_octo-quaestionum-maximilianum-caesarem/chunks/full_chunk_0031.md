Thereafter their power was diminished, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of this world will be cast out.” For the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints have greatly restrained the boldness of demons, so that they can now rightly be called inciters of evils, but by no means compellers. Nor would the devil grow strong against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” And again: “Do not give place to the devil.”

Therefore, in concluding the discussion for understanding the proposed question, why God permits so many acts of witchcraft to be done, I believe the reasons have been made sufficiently clear. For although the will of demons is always wicked, their power can never be unlimited, because what a demon wills wickedly he has from his own free choice, but what he is able to do wickedly he has only from God. And these things have been said as far as concerns those who do evil.

But why does almighty God permit demons, through the agency of works of witchcraft, to harm people who have been baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity and who faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ? For if they could harm only the wicked, the perverse, and unbelievers in body and possessions, but were restrained from access to and injury of the good and faithful, the judgment of the permission would rightly seem just. But now, since more often the good and innocent are weakened by demonic works of witchcraft, and sometimes are even deprived of life, to pass over in silence the loss of temporal goods which those bewitched often suffer, it comes as a scruple to many why the Almighty permits these things, he who permits nothing unjustly.

I shall set out in order, as briefly as I can, the necessary reasons for this proposed question, which I have received from none other than the ancient and Catholic doctors. The first reason is the testing of Christians’ faith and the proving of their integrity, so that, when scourged by the works of demons, they may learn in the sufferings of the flesh what faith and love toward God they possess in the hidden place of the mind. To be patient in prosperity is not difficult; but in adversity to await with humble patience help from the Savior of the faithful is not the mark of the soft, but of the strong and truly faithful Christians. In his tribulation most holy Job learned how great a constancy of faith toward God he had, he who previously in tranquillity had preserved piety without the dart of temptation.

Hence it must also be noted that those whom works of witchcraft by demons or by men have in any way made ill, and who, in order to recover health, either approach or consult witches and any enchanters, whether by themselves or through others, act against the commandments of God and of the Church, and have neither true faith in God nor love. For they prefer the health of their mortal flesh to God, and seek salvation not from Christ but from the devil and his minister. For they prefer to be healthy in the name of the devil and live unhappily, rather than to suffer a little here for the love of Christ and afterward rejoice with him forever. But whoever does not approach witches because God has forbidden it, preferring to live in poverty and sickness while observing the commandments of God rather than against God in riches and bodily health, truly loves God and rightly preserves the integrity of the Christian faith.

Among Christians, most sacred Caesar, men corrupted in mind and reprobate concerning the faith must not be tolerated: those who cure the bewitched by works of witchcraft, who reveal thefts and adulteries by the ministry of demons, who by inspection of stones or mirrors [unclear].
