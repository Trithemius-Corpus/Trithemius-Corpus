Great is the authority of the Catholic Church, which alone, in those matters that concern the faith, has the right in doubtful cases to interpret the Scriptures; to it the necks of all powers must be subjected, and if anyone departs from its definition with rash daring, he has denied the Gospel of Christ. There is one congregation of the faithful, the mother of Christians, the holy Church; outside its unity no one will be saved. Though in morals it may sometimes seem weak, nevertheless in faith it has always remained constant and has never erred, by Christ’s saving mercy.

On the reprobate and maleficent. The Fifth Question.

The fifth question of your most serene Highness was this: Why are impious, evil, and reprobate people in matters of faith, such as those women whom we commonly call witches, able to command malignant spirits, when good men and the most zealous observers of the Christian faith can command neither good spirits nor always malignant ones?

I remember that I once answered this question as follows. In all the works of the divine majesty there is a sequence and order of beauty, of inviolable equity, by which individual things, fittingly disposed, persevere in the degrees they have received. For the freedom of will which the Creator of all things gave to human beings is not driven by any violence of power outside its proper order, so as to fail always to remain in the order which the first man received at the beginning of his creation. Hence no mortal is good except by the counsel of his own will, aided by the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil except of his own accord, deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin.

By rectitude of will we are made like the good spirits, if we prove by holy and necessary works the piety of the faith which we have in Christ. And just as the consummation of a good will makes good men equal to the angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious renders them like demons. Hence evil and perverse men, the more they have been depraved by the malice of their own will, become that much more like demons in disposition. By likeness, friendship and familiarity are introduced, and from this mutual confidence is gradually generated. From the confidence of the lesser toward the greater there arises command, whether it is true or simulated by cunning. Therefore, if wicked men seem to have any command over demons, it arises from the greatness of their depravity, namely when their disposition is depraved to such a degree that by their reprobate will they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons who consort with them.

For demons are not all equal in malice or intelligence, but one is much more cunning than another in quickness of understanding and far worse in depraved affection. For those who, having fallen from the high seats, are more closely joined to the earth and inhabit caves and caverns are certainly duller than the rest, to such a degree that they even seem to fear swords, rods, and threats from terrifying men, as may be observed in those whom we call possessed. But the demons who dwell farther removed from the earth are of a subtler nature and more rarely associate with human beings. Men full of malice, however, the more they have advanced in perversity of will, become that much more like demons; and once admitted into familiarity with them, they thereafter become bold and receive command over the demons whose familiarity they enjoy, not by the violence of power but by feigned simulation.

For demons, in order to keep as willing participants in their wickedness those men whom they have once received into surrender, gladly subject themselves to them under a simulated dominion, and what they do of their own accord they pretend to do as though compelled to obedience. For no one obtains true command over demons except one whom faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.”

Therefore evil men command demons because malice has made them like them, likeness familiar with them, and familiarity their own and subject to them. It is as if some servant of low birth, joined by long familiarity to a great prince, as often happens, were said to command him, since he obtains from him without contradiction everything he has asked, so that he seems rather to rule his lord than to be ruled by him.
