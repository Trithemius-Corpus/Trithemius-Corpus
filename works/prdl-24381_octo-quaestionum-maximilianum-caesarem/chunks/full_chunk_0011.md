The cause of their perishing was sin; but that they were created had no other cause than the uncircumscribed goodness of God. For few are saved while many perish. The figure preceded the mystery: Noah’s ark was the sacrament of the Christian faith. For just as all who were outside Noah’s ark perished in the flood, so now all who are outside the unity of the Catholic Church of Christians will without doubt perish eternally in their sins, Jews as well as pagans, heretics together with schismatics. The justice of God condemns those whom his goodness created. But mercy saves those whom grace has strengthened, because, as the holy Apostle says, it is not of him who wills nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy, since he has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens. Let us rightly weigh the freedom of human choice, and we shall know the just will of God.

On the Miracles of Unbelievers. The Third Question.

Your Most Serene Majesty’s third question was this: if no one is saved outside the faith, whence come miracles among unbelievers, since we know from the report of many that astonishing things are also done among unbelievers? As it seems to me, there will be no difficult solution to this question if we apply the necessary consideration to the faith of the Scriptures and to the natural powers of the soul.

A miracle is a new and unusual occurrence, which happens beyond custom and draws men into wonder. Hence good Christians, wicked Christians, and unbelievers perform miracles in different ways. Good Christians perform miracles through public righteousness; the wicked, through the signs of public righteousness; but unbelievers through private contracts with demons, whether implicit or explicit. Of the good the Lord said in the holy Gospel: “These signs shall follow those who have believed. In my name they shall cast out demons; they shall speak with new tongues; they shall take up serpents; and if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not harm them. They shall lay their hands upon the sick, and they shall be well.” But concerning wicked Christians who perform miracles in the name of Jesus Christ, we read thus written in the Gospel: “Many will say to me on that day: Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and in your name cast out demons, and in your name do many mighty works? And then I will declare to them: I never knew you; depart from me, you who work iniquity.”

But outside the Church of God, where the name of our Lord Jesus Christ is not invoked, if any wonders are done beyond the custom of nature or of human art, no good Christian doubts that they are done by the cooperation of demons. And Your Most Serene Caesarean Majesty, by discerning their power, judges that works of this sort should be called wonders rather than miracles. Nor does this judgment of truth depart from reason, so that a necessary distinction may be maintained between the prodigies of light and of darkness.

I find four effectors of wonders; but I acknowledge one alone, God, who truly performs miracles principally as the cause of all honors. Therefore the first and greatest effector of all wonderful things is God, who filled and illumined the whole world with signs, miracles, and powers. None of the others is to be compared with him, who created heaven and earth, the sea, and all things that are in them; who alone is almighty, just, holy and upright, good, incomprehensible, greatest, glorious, immeasurable, and everlasting. All other things are beneath him, since whatever can happen wonderfully in this world is certainly less than the whole world itself, which he was able to create out of nothing.

Every miracle that can be done through man is less than man himself, whom God, the creator of all things, formed from the beginning in his image and likeness. And although the miracles of God in visible natures have now, through the frequency with which mortals see them, almost become cheapened, nevertheless, if we look upon them wisely, they will appear greater and rarer than all things that are unusual.

Now the manner in which God performs miracles is the immutable will of God, which, since it is omnipotent, does all things whatsoever it has willed in heaven and on earth. For he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created. The will of God is ordered and eternal; therefore his miracles are incomparably greater than all others. For greater than the miracles of this whole world is what we perceive: the entire universe, and whatever is contained in it, created by the will of God. The immutable divine will works all things, abiding forever, since it lacks beginning and end; but whatever God wills, he always wills, because, since in him to will and to be able are the same, there is no labor for the immutable one.
