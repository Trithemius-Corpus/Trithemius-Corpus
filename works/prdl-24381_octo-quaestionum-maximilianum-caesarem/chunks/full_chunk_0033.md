Therefore, out of mercy, he changes eternal punishment for the penitent into temporal punishment, which they must necessarily suffer either in this world or after death in purgatory. But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and very grievous, merciful God is accustomed to purge in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the discomfort of diverse tribulations in body and in possessions. For the harmful works of the impious profit good and righteous people as a remedy, but for those who inflict them they lead to damnation.

The sixth reason is so that a person harmed by the assaults of demons may be called back to repentance and may resolve in his heart upon the necessary amendment of life. For almighty God does not immediately send vengeance when we sin, but waits for our amendment until the appointed time. But if we have neglected to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not expect it; we sinners, who in tranquility scorned to recognize ourselves by repenting, will at last, though late, recognize ourselves in tribulation. Hence the holy prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: “If his children forsake my law, and do not walk in my judgments, and do not keep my commandments, I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their sins with stripes; but my mercy I will not scatter from them, nor will I harm them in my truth.”

The seventh reason why almighty God permits the harmful works of demons to prevail against the good and innocent is so that an excessively unchecked opportunity for sinning may be cut away from them. For there are many poor and sick people who are pleasing to God, good, devout, and humble, whom poverty and bodily illness preserve in the love of God; if they were rich and raised to honors, they would quickly flow away from their purpose of holiness into the depth of vices. It is not expedient for the salvation of all to have honors and riches together with health, nor does a longer life always make a person better. Therefore almighty God, wishing to show his mercy toward those whom he has chosen, permits the good, righteous, and innocent to be subject to various calamities. And sometimes through demons, sometimes through evil people, sometimes through various other ways and means, he takes away from such people riches and honors, which he foresees they would use badly, so that they may preserve in poverty and abasement the humility which, if placed in wealth and exaltation, they would by no means have preserved. He also very often permits many great illnesses to befall them, when cheerful bodily health would have brought the greatest sickness upon the mind. Finally, he permits certain people to be driven to death by harmful magic, so that there may be cut away from them the freedom to sin which they would have abused if they had been allowed to live longer. Hence Scripture says of the righteous man: “He was taken away lest malice should change his understanding, and lest deceit should deceive his soul.”

The eighth reason for divine permission is so that human beings may venerate the sacraments of the Church, which have great power against all the harmful works of malign spirits. For one who venerates with due honor the sacraments and rites of the Catholic Church is rarely harmed by the harmful works of demons. And by the confession of witches themselves we have often heard that hearing Masses and using things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons. For they are not blessed in the Church of God in vain, but so that they may be, as it were, little signs of divine protection, showing demons defenses to be feared. Concerning children and infants, however, who, reborn in Christ, are sometimes killed by the harmful works of demons or of human beings, we do not doubt that divine providence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things for the better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish by harmful magic because of the sins of their parents.
