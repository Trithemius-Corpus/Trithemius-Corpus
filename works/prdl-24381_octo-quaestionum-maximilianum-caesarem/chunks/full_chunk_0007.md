But because a cause of just consideration has moved your most serene Highness to ask these things, not because you do not know what must be held as Catholic in this position, but so that you may have something true to set against those who attack it, I shall answer the questions as briefly as I can.

If anyone could be saved outside the Christian faith, Christ, the Son of almighty God, would seem to have become man superfluously and without cause. For if there is salvation outside Christ, then Christ is not the Savior of all. But because it was necessary, according to the Scriptures, that the only-begotten Son of God should, for the salvation of the human race, be born as man in the world, suffer, die, and rise again on the third day, he did not come into this world in vain, nor superfluously, nor without cause. Since this is so, whoever asserts that anyone can be saved outside the faith of Christ shows himself not a Christian, but an adversary of the faith and a deserter of Christ. For whoever does not believe in Christ, the Son of God, promises himself eternal salvation in vain. For Jesus Christ himself, who is the Truth, says in the holy Gospel: “He who does not believe will be condemned.”

And again: “He who does not believe is already judged, because he does not believe in the name of the only-begotten Son of God.” And again, a little later, he adds, saying: “He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him.” Saint Christophylus also says: “Without faith it is impossible to please God.” The solution of the proposed question is now clear to all who truly believe in Christ: through him, the Savior of the world, there is salvation, and whoever does not believe in him cannot be saved. Nor is it permitted for a Christian in any way to pass beyond the limits of his religion, so as to presume to explain this question otherwise than Christ taught. Whoever, by asserting the contrary, departs from this rule of faith is plainly shown not to think rightly concerning Christ, the Savior of the faithful. For whoever promises salvation to anyone outside Christ wishes to declare Christ a liar. But we, confirmed by the most sacred decrees of the Gospels, firmly believe and pronounce without doubt that no one can be saved outside the faith of Christ, however much he may trust in his own paternal or another religion.

Christ is salvation, and outside the Christian faith no one will be saved. For why did Christ, the Son of God, will to die for the sins of the world, if each person could have been saved outside his faith in his own paternal tradition? From the beginning of the world, after the fall of the human race, it has been impossible for anyone to be saved unto eternal life otherwise than in the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ. What of the fact that the greater part of mankind today remains outside the faith of Jesus? Did not the Savior of the world himself long ago foretell in the holy Gospel that many are called, but few are chosen? And again he says: “Enter through the narrow gate, because wide is the gate and broad is the way that leads to destruction, and there are many who enter through it.” What we may say can contribute nothing to God, our salvation, who has no need of our goods. For whether many are saved or many are damned, God himself is the immutable Lord of all. Nothing is added to him if all are saved; nothing is lacking if the whole world perishes. There is an infallible order of divine providence and majesty, which embraces those who are to be damned no less than those who are to be saved, each and all alike.

Certainly no one of those who are to be damned suffers injustice, because just as in the first man we all fell, so no one is saved except in the blood of Jesus Christ through mercy, and no one is damned except in his own unbelief and according to his deserts through justice.

From what has been said there arises another question of no small difficulty, which human curiosity is accustomed to raise against those who think rightly concerning the faith of Christ. Why does almighty God, since he is just and merciful, not cease to create so many thousands of human beings whom he most certainly foreknew would be damned? Why does he not spare the wretched, and why does he bring forth in such a multitude to destruction those whom he foreknew would turn aside to evil? For it would seem better not to exist than to exist badly.

Let that man answer this question, if anyone thinks himself God’s counselor. Who am I, that I should explain the cause of the divine will? For whatever the Lord willed he has done in heaven and on earth, and there is no one who can resist his will. Hence a certain wise man says to God: “Who will charge you with blame if the nations you made perish?” The Almighty established an order of change in things, which no one ever [unclear].
