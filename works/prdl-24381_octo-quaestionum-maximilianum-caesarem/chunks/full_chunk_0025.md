But why can good and righteous men obtain nothing from God’s holy angels, into whose likeness they have passed by goodness of will? I think that, from what has been said above, the solution of this question would easily be given if the difference among intellects were recalled to mind. For although there are many men established in the Church of God whose righteousness profits many unto salvation, nevertheless in our times very few advance so far that, by the merit of holiness, they are either feared by demons or received by angels into familiar fellowship. Hence it comes about that the men of our age neither command malignant spirits by merit of holiness nor deserve to be familiar with the holy angels. But if any advance to that degree of holiness in which, by working miracles in the Church, certain saints once advanced, then they can both powerfully command demons and have familiar fellowship with the blessed spirits. For it is certain that holy men, beloved by God, in the primitive Church of Christians once bestowed greater benefits upon mortals through the working of good spirits than the impiety of wicked men could ever have inflicted as harms through the ministry of demons. For any true worshiper of God who has become like the holy angels can accomplish many miracles by prayers.

On the Power of Witches. The Sixth Question.

The sixth question of your unconquered Majesty was this: whence comes this great power to witches, by which they can perpetrate so many, so various, and so astonishing things, even in a single hour, such and so great as no good man could do in his whole life?

If we wish to answer this question briefly, we must necessarily say that all power is from the Lord God, without whom no creature can do anything. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Without me you can do nothing.” Hence it is manifest that every power of doing anything is granted to human beings by the Lord God and from no other source, whether men do good or evil. Thus the most holy bishop Augustine says: “The will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily forms and motions, and nothing happens visibly in this most vast and immeasurable commonwealth of all creation unless it is ordered or permitted from the inner, invisible, and intelligible court of the supreme Emperor, according to the ineffable justice of rewards and punishments.” Therefore, Caesar, it must not be doubted that that power of witches, by which they produce astonishing and horrible effects with the cooperation of demons, is granted by the Lord God, whose will no one can resist and without whose influence not even a man can move his hand.

For the holy doctors conclude that neither demons nor witches can do anything, whether to harm a man or a beast, unless almighty God wills, consents, and grants them the power to do what they have already resolved in their perverse will. Unless God consents and wills, no creature can do anything; otherwise, if something could be done without his willing and consenting, it would necessarily follow that God is weak, since he could not prevent what he would be forced, unwilling, to permit to happen. But since every man, insofar as he is a creature, depends on God, he can do nothing without the will of him from whom he has both willing and power. Therefore whatever witches do is done by God’s permission, without whose will they can do nothing; for unless God granted that they could do what they wish, they would obtain it neither from themselves nor from demons.

Thus all power is from the Lord God, in whom all things both exist and are moved, as in the first cause of all things, since from him from whom we have received freedom of will, from him also we have the ability to do what we will. But since the understanding of witches is very confused, they do not perpetrate the evils they do by human art, but by the cooperation of demons, whom both subtle nature and long experience have made swift in producing marvels. For the astonishing effects of witches are not caused by the sorts of means they use for witchcraft, such as heads of the dead, toads, serpents, characters, images, and things like these, but by the devil who instituted these signs; for since he is most proud, he desires in all things to seem comparable to God.
