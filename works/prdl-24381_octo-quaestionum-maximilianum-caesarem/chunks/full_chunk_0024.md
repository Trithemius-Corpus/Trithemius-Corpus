Men of this sort have grown far too strong, and although they are despised by demons, they nevertheless persevere in their pernicious doctrines and deceive many. Believe me, Caesar: this kind of men is very harmful to your sacred empire, and therefore must be utterly exterminated no less than a harmful source [unclear]. They promise great things, but they lie in everything. Indeed, all the things promised in their books are empty and false, and they provide those who believe them with nothing other than tricks, frauds, and most subtle deceptions. But if at any time they seem to produce what they have promised, it is known to all that this happens only through a demon’s cooperation, whose company must be fled no less than the most savage torments of hell. For demons generally come to these necromantic operations, and, to keep their invokers stubborn in the treachery they have begun, they grant them what is asked, if they can. Sometimes, however, when invoked by conjurations, they disdain to appear; and nevertheless they have led astray the minds of those who invoke them.

There is another error, no less foolish than superstitious: that of those who wish to have no dealings at all with demons, and nevertheless in their operations use things vainly assigned to demons, and for that reason wholly unlawful. For they think that a great and hidden power resides in characters, and therefore they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But since it is not for the present occasion to recount their madness, let us pursue our purpose.

Why good and just men do not obtain dominion over demons must be stated briefly. Just as likeness of will makes evil and impious men like demons, so unlikeness of will renders good and just men altogether opposed to them. Between those who are opposed and unlike there can be no familiarity; rather, continual repugnance and hatred prevail. But where no familiarity contracted through likeness of will is found, there can be neither confidence of one toward the other, nor mutual cooperation in the working of marvels. Nor do you, glorious Caesar, since you are a great ruler and prince of men, admit as familiar to yourself anyone wholly contrary and unlike you in morals, and continually resisting your will.

But good and upright men, because they always resist the utterly depraved will of demons, can by no means pass into fellowship with those with whom they neither wish nor consent to be familiar; rather, with all their strength they flee and detest their approach. And therefore they do not obtain that feigned dominion over them of which I have spoken. For there is no safe familiarity where no likeness of wills has preceded it; nor can there be concord between opposites, nor established friendship between those unlike in will. Yet demons do not obey all evil and reprobate men indiscriminately, but only those with whom they are allied by pacts and services; and when invoked, they willingly come to those whom they cannot otherwise keep faithful, although they lie that they are compelled.

It is therefore no wonder, excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to obey wicked and perverse men, whom, as we have said, both malevolence like their own and destructive hostility toward the human race have made concordant. But let it be far from every Christian to wish to rule demons by a wicked likeness of will, because if anyone in this life wishes to have [unclear] over malignant spirits.
