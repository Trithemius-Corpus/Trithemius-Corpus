And Saint Augustine says on Genesis: Divine Scripture speaks in such a way that by its height it mocks the proud, by its depth terrifies the attentive, by its power feeds the great, and by its affability nourishes the little ones. For the authority of sacred Scripture is greater than all the keenness of human intellect.

Therefore, in order to arrive at its true understanding, the soul must first be cleansed of every carnal affection, and the help of divine goodness must be diligently implored with humility, so that by his Spirit he may enlighten the understanding of the learner. For the necks of all authorities must be bowed to the authority of the divine Scriptures; and, as Saint Jerome says, no one must ever be flattered in such a way that the truth of the holy Scriptures is neglected, which in both Testaments is wholly true in its narration of deeds, right in its admonitions, certain in its promises, and terribly to be feared in its threats. Therefore it is not given to all to be able to penetrate the mysteries of the divine Scriptures, but only to those, in whatever measure it is permitted, in whom love sustains continual study, and to whose purified minds the Spirit of God grants true understanding.

But whoever is filthy with the darkness of vices, whoever walks proudly with a swollen mind, whoever doubts the truth of the Scriptures of God, whether in part or in whole, let him never trust that he can attain to their understanding. For the authority of Scripture itself says: Unless you believe, you will not understand. It is our slowness of intellect, and also our impurity of desire, whenever we do not understand the divine Scriptures rightly, namely when the veil of harmful occupation remains spread over our hearts, which does not allow us to come to true understanding. Hence it happens that we do not understand the meaning which the Holy Spirit has poured into the divine Scriptures, but, grasping the bare words of the writers, twist them to another meaning.

Indeed, if our minds were purified from carnal vices, if the intellect were exercised in the Scriptures through continual zeal for sobriety, if that belief and reverence were applied to the holy Scriptures which their dignity requires, every studious person would understand more clearly than light that there is nothing confused in the sacred readings, nothing discordant, nothing unsuitable, nothing absurd, or inserted without the most just reason. All passages of divine Scripture are indeed full of mysteries, and whenever anything in them seems to be set down obscurely, it has been done by divine providence, so that the studious mind may have the necessary exercise of piety, and the faith of the simple may not lose the place of due reverence.

Therefore it was not by chance, but by eternal providence, that he willed the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that they might abound in so many mysteries, which, by the inspiration of the Spirit of God, are expounded in a catholic sense by teachers. For, as holy Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture is useful and very profitable, because it gives birth to many meanings of truth and brings them forth into the light for human beings; since, when one person draws out this meaning and another a different one by expounding it in catholic fashion, it becomes known how high and deep it is.
