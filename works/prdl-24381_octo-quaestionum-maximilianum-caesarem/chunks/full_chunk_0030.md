The second reason is that the inscrutable wisdom of God may become known to mortals, which knows how to draw good from our evils, when it makes the sin of one person become the merit of another for the increase of virtue. For if he did not permit evil to be done, which of us would understand the good? For God makes and orders good things; he does not make evils, but when they have been done by the impious, he himself disposes them so that they may not come about in a disorderly way. Nor does the malice of men hinder the will of God, nor can man do anything that God does not permit.

The third cause is that the Almighty permits evil deeds to be done so that he may manifest his most merciful goodness toward those who have sinned and have done penance, which he could in no way show if he did not permit the creature to sin. For if there were no sinner, God could not be one who shows mercy. But since we all offend in many things, everywhere God finds something to forgive; everywhere a guilty person presents himself, in whom the kind Creator may show his mercy. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: If the disease were slight, the physician would be despised; he would not be sought; if the physician were not sought, the disease would not be ended. Therefore, where sin abounded, grace also abounded, which alone distinguishes the redeemed from the lost, whom the mass of perdition, drawn from the origin of creation, heaps together into one. Therefore the mercy of the Lord is over all his works.

The fourth reason for divine permission is that the justice of the Creator may be declared against those who have sinned and have not done penance, because if all did good and no one sinned, the justice of God would have nothing to punish. Therefore, so that justice may have a place, the creature sins by divine permission. For the Lord is just, and he will render to each according to the works of his will.

The fifth reason is the dignity of the human condition, because if God did not permit man to sin, man would seem worse than the beasts, which, with no violence driving them, freely use the motions of nature. For although brute beasts lack reason, nevertheless they have a free motion of nature which they follow; in comparison with them man would seem to be of a lower condition if he did not have the will of free choice. But God permitted man a free will, over which neither kings nor princes command.

The sixth reason is the praise of the merit of constancy in human virtue, when we praise a just man because, although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, with the grace of God helping him, remained steadfast in a good will.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits demons to do such great evil deeds through the agency of witches is that those who, having despised the truth, delight in falsehood may, seduced by the devil, the inventor of falsehood, be utterly deceived. For it is fitting that enchanters and evildoers who scorn to follow the saving teachings of Christ should all, deceived by demons, be plunged down to hell to be punished forever. They seek help from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have adored. For it is just that one who, deserting the truth by his own voluntary will, clings to falsehood should be hurled by the devil into destruction.

The eighth cause is the manifestation of divine power, whose might and strength are over every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits great things to the devil and denies him certain small things, he shows how weak the enemy of the human race is, and how subject in all things to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing of himself, but all that he does, he does with God permitting it. Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God permits evil deeds to be done, is justice, by which he preserves in an inviolable order the freedom which he once bestowed on the creature from the beginning. But because every power of the creature, dependent on its Creator, is restricted and limited, it is clear that the devil cannot do everything he wishes, nor can man, however depraved in will. The will is indeed free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wish, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would shake all things to ruin. The history of holy Job shows by a clear example the bound power of demons, for Satan did not dare to tempt him beyond what had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if, at their own discretion, they could always use a free power of harming.
