By likeness, evil and reprobate men who are admitted by demons into familiarity with them, and through lengthened confidence become very bold, take on a counterfeit, not to say familiar, command over them, which they have deserved through the likeness of a most depraved will. Hence whatever they have demanded from demons, with the demons cooperating with them by an implicit or explicit pact, they receive, except when, being forbidden by divine ordinance, the demons cannot provide what is sought. Yet they do not thus come to the aid of all the reprobate and wicked indiscriminately, but only of those with whom they have entered into some covenant through the intervention of a pact, such as sorcerous women, who have rendered homage to the devil, and those invokers of demons whom the common people call by the customary name necromancers. There are two kinds of men who can lawfully command demons.

The first is of those men who, beloved by God for the merit of holiness, have received from him the power of trampling the head of the ancient serpent, such as in the primitive Church were the apostles and their followers and disciples, of whom the Lord speaks in the holy Gospel: “In my name they shall cast out demons,” and “they shall take up serpents.”

The second kind, however, is of those men to whom, by reason of their order in the Church of God, it is granted that, with the power of the ministry they have received, they drive demons out of men’s bodies. And this ministry is entrusted to exorcists in the Church of God, namely when they are ordained by the bishop.

Again, there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons; how unlawfully they presume to do this is shown by their most wicked conditions.

The first and most pernicious kind is that of witches, who enter into pacts with demons and, after first making a solemn profession, hand themselves over to some demon as his property and for perpetual service. How many evils this most destructive kind of people does in your empire, wisest Caesar, no one can express. For first they deny God, the faith of Christ, and holy baptism; they offer themselves, mind and body, to demons; they afflict, harm, and kill men and beasts by their sorceries. There is no disease which, God permitting, they cannot bring on, because, when they show themselves servants to demons in keeping the covenant they have made, through their cooperation they dare whatever they wish. Therefore they are nowhere to be tolerated, but rather are to be utterly exterminated in every place, as God the creator of all commands: “You shall not suffer witches to live.” Some witches call demons with charms; others entice them with prayers and sacrifices; the rest, provoking them with unspeakable things and ceremonies, draw them into the service of their impiety. It is therefore no wonder that witches seem to command demons, since through the impiety of their perverse will they have become like them, and by mutual association are in agreement for evil against the human race.

The second kind of wicked men who wish to command demons is that of those whom people commonly call necromancers, who profess arts full of execrations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly visible, enclosed in crystal or in some other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of lies and superstitions, commit such great evils as no good man could devise. For, to entice demons into their service, they very often use foul sacrifices and enter into an explicit pact with them. They carry around many different little books, full of the filthiest impiety and lies, falsely ascribed to the names of old philosophers and sages; by displaying these they deceive many of the unwary and curious, and draw them with themselves into the demons’ pit. They promise great and utterly incredible things, at the hearing of which they falsely hold men in wonder. By rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations, they say that they can compel malignant demons to appear visibly in a circle, or in crystal, or in another receptacle, and to answer distinctly all questions put to them. All of them indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found whom the malignant spirits obey even in this way, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it, lest the curiosity of men, prone to novelties, after despising the religion of Christ, should in necessity seek help from demons. But all those whom the malignant spirits obey when summoned by the conjurations of necromancers have without doubt passed into fellowship with them by an explicit or implicit pact. As for the rest, their number is greater: the malignant spirits disdain to obey them, although they do not at all times truly [unclear] in the profane arts of necromancers.
