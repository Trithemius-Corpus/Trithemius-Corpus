God’s illumination dwells very far from the filthy, the wanton, murderers, the impious, the proud, and those alien to piety, leaving them to the judgment of the avenging demon, who, striking in the sharp point of fierce fire, fastens upon their senses and mind, and arms and drives the impious all the more to commit crimes, so that, guilty of a more shameful fault, they may also become liable to a harsher punishment. Hence those entangled in vices and worldly desires not only cannot penetrate the divine Scriptures with understanding, but, neglecting themselves, do not even know themselves.

Since, therefore, sacred Scripture is the perfect work of God, we ought, O Caesar, so to strengthen our mind that we believe it to be true, right, whole, solid, divinely inspired, and most abundantly sufficient for all who understand it unto the confirmation of the Christian faith. For indeed, unless divine Scripture is believed to be true, holy, perfect, and whole in all things, the Christian faith would be deprived of its foundation. Therefore every Christian must take the greatest care that, by examining the divine Scriptures more than is fitting, he not pass beyond the bounds of piety, because man is not the author of the Scriptures, but the Holy Spirit; and whoever resists the integrity of God’s Scriptures is proved to resist his ordering.

Scripture, however, by God’s ordaining, is usefully woven with parables, riddles, likenesses, comparisons, and figures, so that readers may be exercised toward understanding, and, once its inward sweetness has been tasted, the mind may be strengthened unto piety. Therefore, if with purified minds we attend to the sense of the Scriptures, not the words, we shall understand how rightly and properly all things have been ordered. For it was not fitting that the sacred Scriptures, in which knowledge of the divine majesty is handed down, should be so plain and manifest as to be understood everywhere by all, since it is set forth to be understood only by the good and holy, not also by evil, filthy, and polluted minds.

Hence also it is written: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” What is signified here by the beast, if not men who, in imitation of brute animals, live without the love of God in the pleasures of the flesh? And what is expressed by the mountain, if not the height of the divine Scriptures? When the beast approaches this holy mountain, it is overwhelmed with stones, because a man impure in mind and weak in faith, who attempts to search out the height of the divine Scriptures without a teacher handing it down, is wounded by various and destructive errors as though by blows of stones. For just as all things are clean to the clean, so all things seem profane to the profane. God therefore placed his hiding place in the height of the Scriptures, which is so inaccessible and closed to the reprobate that nevertheless it always lies open and manifest to the saints.

But why almighty God did not give holy Scripture as sufficient and manifest for all things that concern the Christian faith, we answer briefly. There are two things which the Most High provided for believers for the confirmation of the Christian faith: namely, the Church and Scripture, so that whatever is not expressed clearly enough in the one may be declared more fully by the other. Hence, if in the holy Scriptures we find anything concerning the rules of faith expressed less sufficiently, we at once have recourse to the Church; and where the Church has need of saving instruction for piety, it immediately turns to Scripture. From this it follows that neither would have it without Scripture, so that the judgment of the two may become one. For Augustine says: “I would not believe the Gospel unless the authority of the Church compelled me.” John Gerson says: “I would not believe the Church unless the Gospel compelled me.”

The Church confirms holy Scripture, and in like manner she herself is confirmed by Scripture. The Church abounds whenever Scripture is lacking, and if at any time the Church is in doubt, she has recourse to the counsel of Scripture. For there is one Holy Spirit of God who founded the Church in the faith of Jesus Christ, the same almighty Spirit who alone inspired Scripture. And this is the threefold cord which is not easily broken, where divine Scripture is joined by the Holy Spirit to the authority of the Church, to such a degree that the Church commends Scripture, and Scripture in turn commends the Church.

Therefore, as it seems to me, O Caesar of unconquered majesty, we have assigned a sufficient cause for the proposed question: because if all things that concern the piety of faith were manifestly expressed in the holy Scriptures, holy mother Church would not seem to have such great authority, and the merit of saving obedience would in large part be emptied out.
