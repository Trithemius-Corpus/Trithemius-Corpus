Do not listen, I beg you, to those men who, ignorant of the divine laws, think ministers of demons of this kind useful to your rule because they know how to undo sorceries by sorcery; for that pernicious healing of bodies brings death upon souls, and makes men withdraw from God, the creator of all, and cling to impious apostate demons, with whom after death they will be damned forever.

The second reason why Almighty God permits the innocent to be harmed by sorcery is the increase of merits, so that their patience, proved in tribulation, may deserve a worthier crown of glory in heaven. For, as holy Augustine says, a man is unknown to himself, what he can bear and what he cannot bear; sometimes he fears that he cannot do what nevertheless he can. When temptation comes, as a kind of questioner, a man is discovered by himself, who before, being hidden, did not know himself. The blessed arch-apostle Peter, before temptation came upon him, was hidden both from himself and from others as to how rash and weak he was; when it came, the weakness that had lain hidden appeared. Therefore temptation is the greatest testing of a man, as sacred Scripture bears witness: the furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men. Patience is easy in prosperity, because no adversity strikes the mind. But many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from them all our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the preservation of humility, so that the faithful and righteous man may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or righteousness. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: They are not in the toil of men, and they will not be scourged with men. Therefore pride has held them; they are covered with their own iniquity and impiety; their iniquity has come forth as if from fatness. It belongs to the mercy of God that he sometimes permits good and righteous men to be shaken by adversities, whom he knows cannot be humbled in prosperity. For tribulation has made many humble whom favorable circumstances would have rendered proud. Finally, the fearful thought of sorceries makes a good man fear his God and diligently invoke his help, fortified by which he can trust that he is safe against the assaults of demons. But let no proud man, presuming upon his own strength, think himself safe from the sorceries of the demon, because he whom, puffed up by pride, the protection of God abandons, without doubt lies open to the sorceries of demons.

The fourth reason is so that there may be a distinction between good and bad people. For Scripture says of good men beloved by God, in the Acts of the Apostles, that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. And Saint Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution. The most wise King Solomon also says in Proverbs: The Lord corrects whom he loves, and, like a father, he takes pleasure in his son. It is a very glorious thing for a Christian to endure adversities patiently for the love of God, whether they are inflicted by evil men or by demons. For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and righteous men, not for their destruction, as [unclear] think, but rather for the greatest and sure consolation of the soul. For indeed, when a righteous man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeping his commandments with all solicitude, and nevertheless is troubled by sorcerers, whether in body, or in possessions, or in both, that man is truly blessed, because when he has been proved he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the Catholic Church of Christians, and patiently endures the evils he receives in this world for the love of God, has a certain sign that he perseveres in the grace of God and will be saved. Therefore we read that it is written thus of the evil and reprobate: Why do the impious live, have they been raised up and strengthened with riches? Their houses are secure and peaceful, and the rod of God is not upon them; they spend their days in good things, and in a moment they go down to hell. For it is a manifest sign of damnation if here you live wickedly in sins and vices, opposed to God, and prosper in the goods of fortune and in bodily health.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and righteous men to be troubled by the evil and reprobate is because of the complete purification of the mind.
