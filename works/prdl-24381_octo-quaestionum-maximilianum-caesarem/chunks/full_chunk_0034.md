For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us prove this opinion first by the Scriptures, and afterward confirm it by reasons, as briefly as we may.

In the Book of Judith we read: “All your ways are peaceful, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the wisest king, Solomon, speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth occasion to make your soul sin, nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest perhaps the Lord, angered by your words, destroy all the works of your hands.” The clearest testimonies of providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to his disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? And not one of them falls upon the earth before God?” Great is this commendation of divine providence, which has care not only for men but also for tiny birds. Indeed, if the Almighty cares for such small things, who would not believe that he has care for great ones? “Look,” he says, “at the birds of the sky, which neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them.” And a little later he adds, saying: “If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is today and tomorrow is cast into the oven, how much more you, O you of little faith?”

Because almighty God created all things and brought them into being when they did not exist, he rules and governs the universe, lest it slip and fall away; and this could by no means happen if he did not have providence and care for all things. For if providence is removed, it must necessarily follow that the ordered continuity of the whole universe is thrown into confusion. But the providence of the Creator cares for all things, since he who feeds the birds, gives beasts their food, fills every animal with blessing, and clothes the grass of the field, adorning violets and lilies with beautiful grace, cannot forget man, whom he created from the beginning in his own image. He who governs all things cares for all things, and he exercises providence not only over man but also over all beasts; for all things are placed under his rule, and there is no one who can resist his will in anything.

Let us now therefore consider this vast machine of the whole universe: how it always persists in its original disposition and motion in the most orderly fashion. Unless we shamefully depart from reason, we shall understand by the clearest reasoning that that divine and unchangeable providence preserves, directs, and governs these things. Next let us contemplate all earthly things, how each and every one is born and proceeds in its natural disposition in the most orderly way. This cannot happen by chance; rather, they must be moved by the will of him by whose power they were made and created. Consequently, let us also contemplate the earth, placed in the middle of the world like a point or center, around which the whole universe unfolds at an equal distance; nothing more beautiful, nothing more adorned, nothing finally more delightful could offer itself to the senses for contemplation. In this point, enclosed by the immense mass itself, we see all things wonderfully ordered; and we see that nothing can be left unordered, so that all things which at times seem to us somehow to drift about we may know for certain not to lack, within the order of the universe, their own particular order. For by the simple and wholly unchangeable command of God, the whole machine of the entire universe, assembled out of so many different and movable things, works together and is ruled in the most orderly fashion.

We certainly see, in those things that often present themselves to our senses, that the greater the intelligence and power that arises over time in any governor, the less there [unclear] can anything disordered occur by random chance. Therefore, since that highest intellect, the creator, preserver, and ruler of all things, almighty God, rules and governs the world by his unchangeable providence, holding all things under his command, surely no place anywhere is left for rash chance, which would obstruct the will of so great a majesty.

Therefore the whole universe, whatever exists anywhere or can exist, is contained under the command of the one divine mind, whose infallible judgment nothing can escape. Unexpected accidents happen to us because we are changeable and are changed almost daily, having no determinate knowledge of future things. But that best maker of all things, God, since he is altogether unchangeable, knows all things, beholds all things, and understands all things together and at once. His providence is not deceived; his will is never changed; his power is not broken; his ordering is not violated; his counsel is never intercepted, even if his sentence sometimes seems to be changed.
