They exempted [man] from corruptible things, saying that God has care and providence for man, but despises all other earthly things. Against their false opinion I think enough has been said, and to bring forward many arguments would be wholly superfluous. For Scripture says: “You will save men and beasts, O Lord.” And in another place a certain wise man said: “The Lord made the small and the great, and he has equal care for all.”

Therefore the providence of the divine majesty orders, rules, disposes, and cares for all and each of the lower things, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible; his most powerful will produced all things from nothing, as sacred Scripture again bears witness: “But your providence, Father, governs all things from the beginning.”

Let men corrupt in mind, impious and reprobate concerning the Christian faith, therefore be ashamed: those who, puffed up with mere talk, have dared to deny the providence of the all-knowing one, and do not consider how great an injury they do to God. For what, to use the judgment of Firmianus, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he provides for nothing, if he neglects lower things, if he does not care for mortal things, he has now lost all dignity. But it is impossible, as holy Anselm bears witness, for God to lose his honor, since, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, no corruption, and no sin. Therefore whoever removes providence removes the whole substance of God; and what does he say, except that God does not exist?

But let those who deny that God’s providence cares for lower things now tell me this. If God neglects lower things, let them consider what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is henceforth no reason why we should honor him with worship or venerate him with religion, since we obtain no benefit from him; rather, being neglected and despised, let us ourselves neglect him too.

But if he unknowingly despises and neglects us, there is now utterly no reason why we should worship one who does not know us, and for that reason cannot confer any good, or invoke him with prayers. For if God does not care for human affairs, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every reason for piety ceases, since there is no hope [unclear] of salvation.

What regard for piety can there be among mortals, what hope for the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human affairs? Indeed, if providence is removed, whom do we worship, whom do we adore? If there is no providence for mortals with God, certainly he neither bestows benefits on men, nor imposes punishments on apostates, nor can help mortals, nor wishes to, nor cares at all, nor notices what we do, and he hears neither the prayers nor the petitions of anyone. Why then do we offer him any worship, why honor, why prayers, or why do we so greatly fear or dread a God who does not know us, who, having neglected and despised us, can do us neither good nor harm?

For when providence has been removed, neither holiness nor religion will remain; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disturbance of life and dreadful confusion will follow. With piety toward God removed, what will the commonwealth now be except everlasting confusion? Public trust among men will necessarily perish, and the society of the human race and all justice together with it will vanish and perish. What is to be thought of the Christian faith if God does not care for human affairs? Finally, what is to be thought of the Savior of all the living, our Lord Jesus Christ, unless the whole thing will be regarded as an empty tale and a fiction, if there is no providence of God caring for mortal things? Therefore whatever good we do in this life, whatever evil we endure for the love of God, is wholly vain and useless to us if he has no care for us at all.

Let those who deny the providence of God now see and tremble, when they behold the end of their opinion: the worst despair of all. Therefore let no one, led on by the empty talk of worldly philosophers, instruct himself in contempt of God; let no one remove the providence of the highest and unchangeable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of and ungrateful for the benefits of the most merciful Savior.
