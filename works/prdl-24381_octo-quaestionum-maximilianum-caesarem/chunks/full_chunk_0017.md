There are also those who add a fourth mode to the three already described, saying that the mind or spirit of the human being himself can naturally do marvelous things, provided it knows how to recall itself, away from everything adventitious, above sense, into unity within itself. For those who maintain such things promise that they can do marvels of nature, predict the future, disclose people’s secrets, remove diseases, and suddenly impress counsels upon human beings. But this mode of miracles cannot be simply natural, certain, common, and infallible; for if it is true, which I have not experienced, some external power must already be involved, and either a good angel or an evil spirit must occupy part of the one who is thinking. But if no power from outside mixes itself in, then it is necessary that the one prophesying form the beginning of his speech either from conjectures or from a bold presumption, and pronounce a judgment sometimes true and sometimes false.

For as long as the spirit dwells in the body, it naturally understands nothing without the service of sense. Hence I would not wholly resist if they grant that our confused and crude understanding is aided by a like service. For we see in those who, often in the desire of inward love for Jesus, through contemplation exceed the measure of the flesh, that they are lifted up from the earth into the air; and we think that they overcome bodily weight not only by their own keenness of mind, but also by divine power. Therefore the three principles of the opinion just mentioned, unknown to many, are enough, in my view, for a few to hold that the mind, placed in the body, works marvelous things outside itself by its own power.

What then? Let anyone ignorant of nature tell me whether bodily weight can be lightened by the operation of the mind. With two men bearing witness with me, I speak of a thing I experienced. When, as a young man, I was pursuing the study of letters, four of us were sleeping one night in a single bed. A companion of the same age rose from my side and, sleeping as he was accustomed to do, went about the house with his eyes closed, while the moon was shining in, on about the fifteenth day, as if awake. He climbed walls, and overcame the window by his agility. Still asleep, he crossed the bed a second and third time; he trod on us all with his feet, and we felt no greater weight than if a little mouse had touched us. Wherever his sleeping body moved, all the fastenings of the doors opened of their own accord. He entered the higher parts of the house very swiftly, and clung to the roofs like a sparrow. I speak of things I saw, not things heard by vague report.

These seem to be acts of the soul, not of the body; and the soul, so to speak, uses its own natural power almost freely when, the senses of the flesh being bound, it wanders outside the dwelling of the body. But since this material does not in every respect fit the question proposed, let us postpone the treatment of the causes of these marvels to another time. For we do not believe these things marvelous to the wise, who have understood very well the power and nobility of the human mind, since it is held back only by the interposition of the body, and in the rest is considered equal to the angels.

Finally, we pass over the various modes and kinds of fascinations, by which a greatly frenzied man produces effects outwardly very marvelous. A most strongly confident imagination can also do much to produce marvelous effects, whether it is borne toward a true object or toward one merely supposed. Hence, since there are so many kinds and modes of working marvels, it is not to be wondered at if Jews, Saracens, pagans, gentiles, and all the rest placed outside the faith of Christ, unbelievers who cannot work miracles through public righteousness, accomplish marvels either by the aid of demons or by artful industry. Nor does it escape me that by the same reasoning the miracles of the saints are attacked by the impious, as though they were not by the power of the Creator but rather by the involvement or substitution of the devil. Whoever truly believes that the Christian faith alone is true does not doubt that all the miracles of unbelievers are performed by the cooperation of demons. But if anyone rashly and stubbornly thinks the contrary, he shows himself to be an unbeliever, not a Christian.
