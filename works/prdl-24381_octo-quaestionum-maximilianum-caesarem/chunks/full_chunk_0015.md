There are many among Christians, devoted to the works of demons: enchanters, sorcerers, and those reprobate concerning the faith, who deceive many by excessive curiosity and by their arts promise impossible things.

By the involvement of demons, however, marvels sometimes occur, namely whenever a person not sufficiently and rightly established in the faith does or desires something otherwise than befits a Christian, even if he does not believe it to be any sin; and although it ought not to happen, nevertheless the devil, inserting himself, often supplies the desired effect. Such, indeed, is what sufferers from spasms commonly use. On the holy day of Good Friday they fashion a ring of whatever metal during the office, which afterward they always wear on the ring finger. These things are utterly vain and have no root of truth at all. Yet I cannot deny that I have seen certain people cured by this same vanity and freed from spasm.

Likewise, I know that certain people have opened very strong locks with words and herbs, which I do not think was done either by the power of God or by the force of the things applied. I could introduce countless similar things that I have seen and heard; what are their manifest effects other than certain miracles of demons?

Finally, there are some excessively curious people who stubbornly put faith and confidence in characters, slips of paper, rings, figures, necklaces, and unknown words, and often obtain marvelous effects; we in no way doubt that the devil has supplied these, not by merit of holiness nor by God’s merciful concession, but rather through faithless credulity. For demons, having the greatest envy of the human race, in order to destroy the souls of mortals, gladly come to meet the vain operations of the impious. What else are characters and interpretations of hidden names but certain foods and drinks of demons? Whoever puts faith in characters promising marvelous effects has implicitly denied the faith of Christ and the Church of the saints. Alas, how many Christians today, how many clerics, how many priests, to say nothing of greater persons, use many and various superstitions in their daily actions, by which they follow pacts with demons entangled in them, to the disgrace of the Christian faith and their own condemnation. And because these evils have now multiplied far too much among Christians, the Almighty has withdrawn his hand and delivered them into the dominion of the faithless, so that they may feel as exactors of punishments those whose example they followed when they stubbornly served not the living and true God, but vain superstitions.

Moreover, if we inquire more diligently into the truth, we plainly recognize that marvels are done by the substitution of demons. For if Jews, Saracens, pagans, or gentiles, who do not believe in our God, Jesus Christ, perform any miracles, we believe they are done not by the power of God, not by the ministry of angels, not by the merit of holiness, but through the substitution of demons. For as often as they invoke almighty God, in whose only-begotten Son they do not believe, for the mercy of some marvelous effect, the devil, knowing that there is no salvation for sinners and that the Most High does not hear unbelieving sinners, secretly applies himself in place of the God invoked, in order to preserve them stubborn in their errors; and if he can, with the Lord permitting it, he grants to those praying what is requested. The devil uses this substitution all the more freely, the less he is hindered by faith in Christ and the sacraments of the Church. For the wrath of God remains upon all unbelievers who do not believe in his only-begotten Son, and they are in the power of the devil; and therefore he thrusts himself upon those who invoke him, in place of God.

Thus, blinded in their errors, they invoke God, in whose Son they do not believe; and since he despises hearing them while they remain under wrath, the devil, the false worker of miracles, approaches. Therefore those who scorn believing the true miracles of God believe the mockeries of the devil. Those who refused by believing to follow the truth, that is, Christ, receive from demons false miracles suited to their errors.

For there are many among unbelievers who worship one God, creator of all things, do not adore idols, and firmly believe that they will be saved in their own faith. But because they scorn believing the mystery of the Lord’s incarnation, the wrath of God remains upon them, and they vanish away in their foolishness. Thinking God to be near to them, though they do not believe rightly in him, they are subjected to demons; and by putting faith in their false miracles, they are confirmed in their errors by a lie. It is not irksome, O Caesar, to introduce a likeness of this matter.
