To the most serene and most unconquered lord of the world, Emperor Maximilian: the book of eight questions by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of [unclear], begins in the name of Christ.

I have obeyed your commands, most unconquered Emperor Maximilian, and the eight questions which you proposed to me some time ago, when I had been summoned to your highness at the castle of Boppard, I have answered as briefly as I could and set down in the form of this little book. You wished, as I rightly remember, that in solving them I should, as far as possible, follow the path of nature, on account of those who yield less to belief. Yet this did not seem possible to me in every case, because in many places the matter of the questions rested on theological rather than natural principles.

For although the Christian religion, among all the sects of mortals, is held to be nearer, more conformable, and closest to the laws of nature, nevertheless not all the chief articles of our faith are provable according to the common way of nature. We believe that this was necessarily so, in order that faith too might have its place, and firm belief might not lack its own merit. Therefore, undertaking by your command a middle course of disputation as best I could, I have used partly the testimony of nature and partly that of the divine Scriptures. For the things which it is fitting for us to believe must not be rashly examined by human arguments, since, as holy Gregory says, faith has no merit when human reason supplies proof. Therefore, with fear of God and reverence for our undefiled Christian religion, I have resolved to answer the questions proposed in such a way that I depart neither in jest nor in earnest from the definitions of the Roman Church. Therefore whatever I have ever written, or shall write, I wish to be wholly subject to its determination and judgment.

On Faith and Understanding: the first question.

The first question of your most serene majesty was this: Why did almighty God wish to be believed by mortals rather than to be known and understood, namely in that mode of knowledge and understanding by which he is known and understood by the holy angels?

I remember that I answered this question in the presence of your most sacred majesty as follows. Almighty God, who is without beginning, pure intellect, everlasting and unchangeable, existing above every position knowable to us, far more perfect than infinite perfections, by his most wise will created from nothing two intellects from the beginning, namely the angelic and the human. The angelic intellect, at the beginning of its creation, was made in act with respect to all knowable things, and knows all things. The human intellect, however, first exists in potency with respect to all things, but is ignorant of all things. Yet in the end, through very many and continuous actions directed upward, it will become like the angelic intellect, knowing both God and all things that pertain to the promised knowledge and understanding.

The two intellects, angelic and human, differ as any two opposites do: namely as act and potency, light and darkness, knowledge and ignorance, possession and privation, beginning and end, immobile and mobile, substance and accident, truth and likeness, nature and art, health and sickness, whole and part. For the angelic intellect is as act, light, knowledge, possession, beginning, the immobile, the immediate, simple substance, truth, nature, and whole. The human intellect, however, is as potency, darkness, ignorance, privation, end, the mobile, the mediate, the composite, accident, likeness, art, and part.

Hence whatever belongs to the angelic intellect from the beginning, these same things belong to the human intellect at the end, after it has laid aside the burden of the flesh; for just as beginning and nature perfect the angelic intellect, so the end and art, after many exercises at last, raise the human intellect upward. Therefore between these two intellects there is great inequality from the beginning, yet in the end they are at last made equal, when the human intellect will be as great through art as the angelic intellect was before through nature.
