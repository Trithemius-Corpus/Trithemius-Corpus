Some of the terrestrial spirits also, as has been said, serve sorceresses; yet, being ranked in a lower degree, they can by no means bring about those greater acts of sorcery. It is therefore manifestly clear that the whole power of sorceresses, by which they do such astonishing things, is granted to demons in limited measure by the Lord God, and when he does not permit it, they accomplish nothing.

On divine permission. The seventh question.

This was the seventh question of your most serene majesty: Why does God, since he is just and the avenger of evils, permit such great acts of sorcery to be done for the destruction of the human race, by which not only sinners but also many innocent people perish, grievously tormented?

I remember that, when I stood before your majesty, I answered this question in this way. The best God, just and upright, kind and merciful, whatever he permits, permits justly, because he is just, and there is no iniquity with him; rather, all things that happen he either does or permits in true judgment. Therefore, when it is asked why God permits evils to be done, we answer: he permits them because he is just. Nor, Caesar, should the answer we have given seem absurd to your majesty, since it in no way prejudices the divine goodness that he permits evils to be done for a most just reason; rather, it would prejudice it if he did not permit evils to be done in the present state.

For truly, if he did not permit evils to be done, man could no longer sin; he would certainly be impeccable, which does not befit his nature, since from the beginning the Creator of all things gave him free choice. For man, since he is a creature and not the Creator, is necessarily able to be turned both to good and to evil, since his being depends on the supreme maker of all creatures, just as every caused thing depends on the cause of its being. But to create has been left to God alone, who is incorruptible and everlasting; every corruptible thing necessarily fails, yet it is preserved by the goodness of the Creator so long as it does not lose the influence of its cause. Hence it neither could nor ought to have happened that man, by the condition of his nature, should be created impeccable, since God alone, the maker of all things, who is unchangeable and everlasting, naturally possesses this. But whenever it is granted to man that he seems unable to sin, the freedom of choice is not removed, nor is possibility simply taken away, but the will is confirmed in good. And whenever this gift is conferred on a creature, it is a gift of grace, not of nature.

For Scripture says: “God created man from the beginning and left him in the hand of his own counsel.” For, properly speaking, it cannot be that mortal man, conceived in original displeasure, should both have free choice and be unable to commit sin. For the most holy bishop Augustine says: “The first freedom of the will was to be able to sin; the last, in glory, will be much greater: to be unable to sin.” And so, if man could not sin, he would not have mastery over his own will. Therefore, although almighty God does not will sin, he nevertheless justly permits human beings to sin and to do evil, so that, while freedom of choice remains, both the virtue of a good work may receive its reward and voluntary wickedness may receive what evil it has done. For no one either does good or sins unwillingly. Therefore almighty God justly permits human beings to sin and to do evil, since from the beginning he granted them freedom of choice.

Many reasons have long since been assigned by the holy doctors why the Almighty permits such great acts of sorcery to be done through the cooperation of demons; we shall necessarily mention them briefly in order.

The first is so that the excellence of divine power may be made manifest, for he alone is unchangeable and far above every position [unclear]. Every creature, however, is changeable and has no comparison with the unchangeable Creator. Hence it is read in Numbers: God is not like a man, that he should lie, nor like a son of man, that he should change. In Isaiah also it is said: God is faithful, without iniquity, just and upright. In the book of Tobit too it is written: You are just, Lord, and all your judgments are true, and all your ways are mercy, truth, and judgment. And in the book of holy Job we read: Far be impiety from God, and iniquity from the Almighty. For he will repay a man’s work to him, and according to the ways of each he will restore to them. Therefore every man who rightly understands his own ignorance and weakness plainly recognizes what a nothing he is compared with his Creator. For God justly permits man to sin, both so that freedom of choice may continue and so that the excellence of the great God may become known to human beings.
