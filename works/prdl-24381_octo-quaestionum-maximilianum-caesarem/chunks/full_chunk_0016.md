It happened that someone passing by saw, while the swineherd was absent, that a demon had charge of the herd, and said to him, “What are you doing here, wretch?” He answered, “I am guarding those pigs.” The other said, “Who entrusted the care of the pigs to you?” Again he answered, “The foolish confidence of the swineherd. For he has sinned against his own law and follows our vanities, believing that a certain slip of paper enclosed in a staff is of great holy power, by which, through the merits of a certain Blasius, wolves are kept away from the pigs. But because Blasius, though invoked, does not appear, I guard the man’s pigs so that he may not lose his confidence, and I willingly put myself in place of Blasius. In this way,” he said, “mortals trouble us with vain conjurations, so that, unless we are willing to lose all esteem and honor among them, we must very often answer their foolish invocations.”

By the likeness of this deed, demons in seeming mercy deceive unbelievers, and the miracles which those who pray ask from God very often follow through the substitution of malignant spirits. For in matters that concern either the worship or the honor of God, demons gladly thrust themselves in; and those who cannot obtain open honors are, of course, delighted by hidden ones.

At the same time it must be noted that, from the beginning of the world, no religion or sect of outstanding principal rank has ever lacked miracles, although they have not all been done from one source. For whatever miracles have been done in the true religion have been done from no other source than God. The law of nature had its miracles; the written law had them; and now grace too has them. The almighty God who gave religion also performs miracles. But in false religions, where truth is not worshiped, if any miracles are done, it is not God but the devil who works them.

For God, since he is truth, did not establish false religions, and therefore he never adorned a lie with his miracles. Muhammad, who, filled with an evil demon, established a false religion, adorned it with false miracles not from God but by the cooperation of demons. For the demon who gave the cause of error could also, once an occasion had been taken, display a false miracle to those deceived. Hence, if miracles had been entirely lacking to Muhammad when he was persuading an untaught people to accept a new religion, perhaps no one would have believed him. For he too, both by subtlety of intellect and by the cooperation of the devil, showed certain marvels to the peoples, so that he might seem to have confirmed a new doctrine also with new miracles. Pagan idolaters too did not lack miracles, if we do not deny credit to the histories.

The Jews, however, before the incarnation of the Son of God, receiving his law through Moses, were frequently illumined by divine miracles. But after the Lord’s resurrection, when the Mosaic law had been made void, whoever do not believe in the only-begotten Son of God remain under the wrath of his indignation, and all miracles of truth have ceased among them. Hence, if they do any marvels today, they are works of demons and manifest judgments of error. For demons keep watch with the greatest zeal so that those men whom they have once made wander from the way of truth, which is Christ, they may, by the continuance of fraud, make obstinate in error until death. Indeed, when God for a just reason withdraws his grace from unbelievers, their deceiver, the malignant spirit, approaches unknown; and since he sees that salvation is far away, by false miracles he leads ignorant minds astray as if the Lord were near.

Therefore whatever miracles occur among unbelievers are works of demons, since just as there is no salvation for mortals outside the unity of the Christian faith, so without Christ the most holy power of wonders is not granted. For now demons, lurking in their snares, long ago conquered and confounded by the trophy of Christ, fear to appear openly as they did before the incarnation of the Lord; and therefore among unbelievers they falsely present themselves as good spirits.
