From this a new question, shut off before, struck my mind: what should be thought concerning the infants and simple-minded persons of unbelieving pagans and Jews? To this a brief answer must be given. Just as the infants of Christians, baptized in the faith of their parents, are saved, so the children of all unbelievers, not baptized, will be damned in the unbelief of their parents. The judgment is similar concerning the simple-minded of either religion: just as we believe without doubt that baptized simple-minded Christians are saved, so we say without doubt that an unbelieving simple-minded person is to be damned.

Again, here it is asked what is to be thought in a Catholic manner about those people who inhabit islands newly discovered in the great sea, of which no mention is made among the ancient cosmographers, and who, as we are informed, have never heard anything at all about the Christian religion. A brief answer is given, conforming in all respects to what has been set forth: since Truth has utterly excepted no unbeliever from damnation, neither islander, nor one contiguous to our world, nor anyone else, whether dwelling nearby or far away, but has included them without distinction and without defining persons, saying: “Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved; but whoever does not believe will be condemned,” and the wrath of God remains upon him. Who removes the wrath of God from man except our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the mediator between God and men? By what means, except through faith formed in him who, though innocent, tasted death?

Since, therefore, no one is saved without faith, we cannot promise salvation to those ignorant of Christ, whom it is evident do not have the piety of faith in him. Necessarily, then, we shall say that all who do not believe in the Son of God are to be damned, whether they have heard of him or are utterly ignorant of him.

But this one thing we grant: if any among those who are ignorant of Christ have lived well according to the law of nature, invoking one God, even if they have sometimes sinned, provided they had done penance before death, they have been, or are to be, damned less gravely than those who either, having heard of Christ, despise him, or among both groups have lived wickedly and foully. For God is just, doing injury to no one, but rendering to each according to his works. Yet the wrath of God remains upon everyone who does not believe in his only-begotten Son, because of the original sin of the first man, from which no one is justified except through faith and the baptism of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

Original sin, however, if it is in a human being alone, as in little children, unless it is removed through Christ, damns the one who has it to the punishment of loss only, by which, namely, he is barred from the vision of God and from the enjoyment of the happiness of the saints forever. But an adult who sins mortally will be punished after death with a double punishment, namely that of loss and that of sensible pain, so that he does not see God and, feeling the punishments of the fire of Gehenna without end, never escapes. Jews, moreover, and Saracens, and all the rest who, having heard the faith of Christ, despise it, are without doubt damned to both punishments, that of loss and that of sensible pain, whether they otherwise live innocently according to the law of nature or the contrary. But among those who have never had any hearing of Christ, if any were to live innocently according to the law of nature until death, I do not think they have supplied a cause by which they may be said or believed to be damned to the punishment of sensible pain, although they are deprived forever of the vision of God, which theologians call the punishment of loss.

For no one will behold the face of God the Father almighty unless he has been reborn through faith in Christ. Now let us conclude the judgment against those to whom foolish reflection objects, when we assert these things in Catholic fashion according to Christ’s teaching: “Why did almighty God, since he is supremely good, will to create so many thousands of human beings whom from eternity he foreknew would be damned? For if a comparison of Christians is made with unbelievers, scarcely a twentieth part of the people of the whole world today believes in our Lord Jesus Christ.”

The boundless curiosity of this question has often struck many; if their faith in Christ were firm and devotion fervent in them with love, they would not ask things so unusual and difficult. For who is found among mortals to be a man of such learning or holiness that, apart from God himself, he can disclose to us the cause of the divine will? For the cause of God’s will is incomprehensible, since his own most upright will is itself the cause. It is the everlasting, inviolable order; and, as I said above, God does injury to no one. When God brought the universal flood upon the earth, were not only eight human beings saved in the ark?
