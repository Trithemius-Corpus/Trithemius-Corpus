Some demons of these, more fastened to the earthly elements and, because they go about among men, exceedingly restless, whom the ancients called bacucci, terrify men by night, and seek nothing more from mortals than to be thought divine. Hence, in the presence of those with whom they are permitted to be by reason of likeness, they sometimes perform various mockeries, and present themselves in men’s minds as terrifying, as occasion allows.

Whenever they can, they gladly put on the likeness of a human body, but they do not always find a suitable material of air with which to clothe themselves. Therefore they more often appear monstrous and horrible, because they have not had suitable fluids or vapors for the perfection of the desired appearance. And the more they are fastened to earthly dwellings, the duller they have become through their fall in regard to matter. Hence, because of the ignorance which they contracted through disobedience, the predictions of demons are frequently false, although by divine permission they are sometimes also verified. For, as that most holy John Chrysostom says, the devil is sometimes allowed to speak the truth, so that he may commend his lie by a rare truth; for if he never spoke truth, he could deceive no one, and his seduction would by no means suffice for temptation.

It is therefore no wonder if demons sometimes seem to foretell true things to unbelieving men, since they were once able to furnish prophecies among the idols of the Gentiles. Nor is it any wonder if they sometimes show care for the sick, since they know the natures of all herbs, can most easily apply themselves to the diseased, and can either remove illnesses which they had previously inflicted, or for a time withdraw from the senses those which accident has brought on. For almighty God, by the right of imperial majesty over all things, uses even the ministry of demons, both for the testing of the faithful and for the punishment of the impious.

Since they are perverse in affection, obstinate in their rebellion, and proud, they very greatly desire to be regarded and honored as divine; and therefore, when unbelievers pour out invocations to their gods, as they are accustomed to do, the demons gladly come to them as though they themselves were gods. Among unbelievers they sometimes seem to restore the dead to life, and they show marvelous things to curious men, so that, as though confirmed by miracles in their former error, they may become utterly obstinate. Yet they do not truly restore the dead, but deceive men’s senses in various ways: either they themselves enter the corpses of the dead and carry them about for a time as though alive, or, after the bodies have been removed to a more distant place, they present to the beholders some image in the likeness of the deceased person’s life, as the experience of certain men has taught us.

We believe it manifest that demons can show, in a certain false likeness, all the things that the saints perform as miracles in truth. All demonic miracles are either phantasms or works produced by a certain natural skill. For if men experienced in the more hidden mysteries of nature, by applying matter to form in ways obscure to others, produce unusual and marvelous effects, such as forming natural eels from the dew of heaven, bees from calves’ flesh, serpents from certain roots of herbs, and many things like these, not to mention greater ones, as trial has made us experienced in very many cases, who will deny that demons, being of subtler nature and greater experience, can naturally perform many marvelous things which are manifest to no men?

The fourth worker of miracles is man, who works marvels in four ways, which to those who know neither their cause nor their manner do not seem human. First, by invocation of the divine name, when, in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the merit of accepted holiness concurs. In this way the holy apostles and their imitators performed miracles by the power of God, together with the merit of holiness, in the unity of faith. But unbelievers cannot perform miracles in this way, since they do not walk the necessary path of faith toward salvation. For although many believe in one God, yet because they refuse to believe in the mediator between God and men, they have gone far away from God’s mercy. But, by the promise of the holy Gospel, all things are possible to those who believe in God, our Lord Jesus Christ, namely to those who adorn right faith with holy works. For just as without faith it is impossible to please God, so also faith without works is dead. For almighty God would grant us all things that we ask in the faith of Christ, if we did all things according to the faith as we ought. Believe firmly and work faithfully, and you will always obtain all things.
