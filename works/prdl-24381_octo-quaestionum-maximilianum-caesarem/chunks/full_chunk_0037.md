Whether on earth or in the sea, they are ruled and governed by divine providence, and nothing at all in the whole universe is outside his care. For divine providence provides for the human race the crops and all the other things the earth brings forth, and also storms, the varieties of the seasons, and the changes of the sky, by which all things that the earth produces ripen and mature. It takes care of all things and each thing, not only the great but also the very smallest, to such a degree that not even the thinnest leaf of a tree falls to the earth without the will of almighty God the Father, who created it and rules and cares for it. But if anyone thinks that this whole vastness of the universe cannot be ruled and governed by one God, who alone is God, he is greatly mistaken, and is shown not to know how great the power of divine might is. Indeed, he who was able to bring the whole world from nothing into being can also most wisely govern forever what he made. For he knows all things, understands all things, can do all things, who alone, being omnipotent, created all things.

Therefore we, believing the holy Gospel, let us above all venerate divine providence, and let us also raise our minds upward, with what strength we can, away from these things that are perishable; and, brought back to ourselves, let us be united, so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God present everywhere. Since it is wholly uncircumscribable, it holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, surpasses all things, and sustains all things. It does not sustain from one part and surpass from another, nor fill from one part and encompass from another; but by encompassing it fills, and by filling it encompasses; by sustaining it surpasses, and by surpassing it sustains all things. By bodily mass he is nowhere, and by his uncircumscribable substance he is nowhere absent; he is not perceived by the eyes of flesh, but is seen through faith in purity of heart.

O inexplicable power. O incomprehensible loving-kindness. O ineffable wisdom. O uncircumscribable divinity, which is not composed of parts, as a body is; is not enriched by affections, as a soul is; does not underlie forms, as do all the other things that have been made through you; which is in all things and above all things, most wisely ruling and governing the universe.

By these most sacred testimonies, therefore, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, I think it has been sufficiently proved that this whole world is governed by God’s providence. Since he is pure understanding, simple, uncreated, and eternal, he knows all things, understands all things, and fully comprehends all things; and since he is omnipotent by nature, being able to do all things, he can do all things. For him nothing is difficult, nothing impossible. He created such wondrous things by his power, established them in truth, perfected them by reason, sustains them by might, and illuminates them by goodness. To him be praise, to him honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, God glorious and exalted, forever and ever. Amen.

The author’s protestation to Caesar.

These, most invincible Caesar Maximilian, are the things that seemed to me should be said in reply to the eight questions proposed by your majesty. For you wished to have my judgment also on the same matters in writing, making an estimate of me beyond the truth. I am not to be compared in talent with those whose opinion I heard before my own. Let pardon make me worthy if I have said anything less suitable, since at the command of your highness, being a traveler set upon the road, I dictated as I could, at intervals, whatever occurred to me as needing answer.

I say, however, from my heart and openly protest that I am a sincere confessor of the Christian faith, and that I wish to remain until death in the union of the most holy Roman Church. Therefore if anything, either in this little work of mine or in others, should be found said or written otherwise than that Church holds, thinks, believes, and teaches, I, as a true son of obedience, by the attestation of these presents declare and wish it simply to be, and to be held forever, revoked.

For this reason, most wise Caesar, I humbly beg and earnestly admonish your majesty that, before you cause my responses to come to the knowledge of those for whom they are being kept, you have them approved by the judgment of many. For the greatest care must be taken that nothing be proposed to those over whose salvation your serenity keeps watch unless it is solid, approved, manifest, and lawfully confirmed in the Catholic faith by the opinion of very many wise men.

May almighty God direct and preserve you always by his providence, to the praise of his name, Maximilian, greatest of emperors. From the city of Neometum, on the tenth day of the month of July, in the year of Christians one thousand five hundred and eight.

Johannes Trithemius, abbot.
