The second worker of miracles is a good angel, who very often works wonders for mortals both by natural power and by divine authority. For those most blessed spirits are sent, by divine ordination, into ministry for the faithful; and they bestow upon both good and evil men those things that are benefits of nature, but only to those who believe in God and in our Lord Jesus Christ do they administer the gifts of saving grace. Hence there is no doubt that those miracles which are done in the Church of the saints by invocation of the divine name are displayed through their ministry and cooperation. But the wonders of unbelievers, if any are done today or are read to have been done in former times, are either fabricated or falsely invented, or are performed by the mockery of demons. For it cannot be that any unbelievers work miracles through the ministry of good angels, since where Christ Jesus, the Son of God, is not believed in, no signs of grace are displayed by good spirits to impious and reprobate men. Indeed, since they are obstinate in the error of false religion, if they have attained anything miraculous in their own estimation, it must in no way be doubted that it is done through the ministry of demons. For since they are altogether unlike the good angels, they attain no familiarity with them.

No despiser of the Christian faith can please God; but whoever is proved to displease almighty God is without doubt deprived of the fellowship of the holy angels. Yet by this we do not retract the point that every man is committed to some good spirit, by whose ministry, even if he is an unbeliever in the Son of God, he may receive the benefits of nature, although he does not attain to the gifts of grace. But to those who believe in our Lord Jesus Christ they not only confer the benefits of nature, but also minister the gifts of grace. It is therefore clear that if any miracles are done among unbelievers, they cannot be done by the gift of God or by the ministry of the holy angels, since all such people are placed outside the unity of the Church, and signs cannot be worked by the impious through public righteousness. For God, as has been said, does not remove his original wrath from man except through faith in Jesus Christ; but everyone who is unbelieving toward the Son of God will not see eternal life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. Therefore everyone upon whom the wrath of God remains cannot please God; and for that reason, if any wonders are done at his prayers, they should be believed to have been accomplished not by the power of God nor by the ministry of angels, but rather by the substitution of demons.

The third worker of wonders is the devil, who, like an ape, longs to imitate whatever he has seen, and deceives the minds of men, especially unbelievers, with various illusions, and seduces those who believe him by a certain likeness of miracles. For I do not judge it difficult for demons to be able to mock the senses of men, since it is agreed that by nature they were created more subtle than all mortals. But they cannot know the thoughts of mortals except by a certain conjecture, namely when we make our inner thoughts manifest by certain signs. Demons foretell future things, and sometimes they speak truth, while sometimes they are deceived in their conjectures and deceive others. From the beginning of the world they have known certain signs that precede events; by experience and practice with these, they sometimes foretell the truth about what is going to happen, because when they have seen signs known to them that precede an event, they at once conjecture what is in a man’s thought.

Yet more often they deceive, because they themselves are deceived. For when they are asked about some matter that they do not know, for example whether a messenger sent far away will return soon, or on what day, they immediately arrive in the blink of an eye at the place where they know him to be; and when they see that he has already begun his journey back, they carefully count the days’ stages, and, returning at once to the one who asked, confidently affirm the day on which the absent man will come. But if he has either delayed on the road or turned aside to other places, then certainly the demons, deceived in their judgment, must now be mistaken. They gladly involve themselves in prophecies, and are quite ready to predict future things to men for the sake of honor and esteem, because they especially desire to be regarded as worthy of admiration, they seek divine honors, and they greatly desire to be feared by mortals. Just as boys hiding in secret places, or running about masked, suddenly burst out from their hiding places and shake their companions, who suspect nothing of the sort, with unexpected terror, and then hide themselves again so that they may not be recognized; when they see the ignorant ones terrified, they seem to themselves to have gained from this some great honor.
