John Chrysostom, in the third book On Providence, addressed to the monk Stagirius, says: “Stronger for doing harm than every diabolical action is the greatness of grief, because whomever a demon overcomes, he overcomes through grief. If you take this away, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon.”

This kind of demons, however, is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy of its emotions and the fickleness by which they delight in terrifying many rather than being subject to the command of one little woman. Yet sometimes, as they promise to madwomen, they remain in glass, or in crystal, or in a mirror, and, stirred up by charms, give answers to women; and if anyone coming to them has spoken ill of them on the way, they announce it beforehand. And this kind of witches sometimes differs greatly from the rest, because what the others make sick by witchcraft, these promise to cure. But because they for the most part repel witchcraft by witchcraft, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, because, immersing itself in waters, it dwells around lakes and rivers: full of anger, disturbed, restless, and deceitful. It stirs up storms at sea, and by sinking ships into the deep takes away the lives of many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, they appear more often in the female sex, and are more rarely seen in the male, because they live in moist places and follow a softer manner of life, nor can they easily assume a male shape according to the custom of the others. Hence antiquity long ago named the Naiads and Nereids, nymphs of the waters, not in the male sex but in the female. Among our people, however, they are commonly called aquatic women, that is, Wasserfrauen. But those who dwell in drier and somewhat arid places, whenever they appear visibly and bodily to human beings, transform themselves into men. They also assume the forms of various beasts, according as they are touched by various emotions. But the holy angels, because they are never varied by emotion, always appear uniformly in male form. For nowhere do we read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast whatever, but always in the appearance of a man. Demons, however, have quite often been seen around rivers and springs in the appearance of women, sometimes dancing in choruses, sometimes letting down their hair in the manner of women, and sometimes speaking with humans and practicing various mockeries. And this kind of demons is not accustomed to have commerce with witches.

The fifth kind is called subterranean, because it dwells in caves and caverns and in the remote hollows of mountains. And these demons are very wicked in disposition, and they especially attack those who dig wells and mines, and those who seek treasures hidden in the earth, being most ready for the destruction of the human race. They make openings in the earth, stir up flame-vomiting winds, and shake the foundations of buildings. Sometimes at night they go out from the mountains in troops and lead marvelous and astonishing dances in the fields; and, as though fearing the command of a single leader, they suddenly vanish at a signal and return to their hiding places. Sometimes the sound of bells is heard among them, and they sometimes lie that they are the spirits of people who have died. They seek nothing more than the fear and amazement of human beings. Hence we have found that they have sometimes led some of the simpler sort of people into their hiding places in the mountains, showing astonishing spectacles to those who marveled, and they lie that they are friends of the living, as though the dwellings of the blessed were there. Among these demons also some are guardians of treasures, which the greed of mortals has hidden in the earth; and, lest they again come into human use, they carry them off, steal them, guard them, and sometimes move them from place to place. And this kind of demons is joined in no commerce with witches.

The sixth kind of demons is called light-fleeing, because they especially dread and detest light: for they never appear by day, nor can they assume a body in any other way than by night. This is the business that walks about in darkness, a kind of demons unsearchable and utterly dark, driven by cold passions, malicious, restless, and disturbed. It violently assaults people it meets in the hours of night, and often, God permitting, kills some either by its breath or by its touch. This kind of demons does not associate with witches, nor can it be summoned by charms, because it flees light, human voices, and likewise every disturbance.
