Trithemius
Johannes Trithemius (c. 1480–1555) was a German humanist, canon lawyer, antiquarian, bibliophile, scholar, and author who became Bishop of Utrecht. He served as Master of the School of St. Stephen's Cathedral Church in Vienna between 1519 and his death in 1555. His works include theological treatises on biblical interpretation, commentaries on the Psalms by King David, and extensive writings concerning church history, liturgy, canon law, and patristic studies. He also compiled an early edition of the Greek New Testament, known as the "Vulgate" (Latin: Vulgata), which is based upon Jerome's Latin translation from the Hebrew Bible. This work remains one of the most widely used editions for many years after its publication. In addition to these scholarly pursuits, he was a prominent figure in the Reformation debates of his time. Trithemius' library was particularly notable; it contained over 10,000 books at the time of his death.
The Bibliotheca Regia Monacensis refers to the Royal Library of Monaco. It houses a significant collection of historical documents including manuscripts, printed books, maps, coins, and other artifacts related to the history of Monaco and surrounding regions. The library serves not only as a repository but also as a research facility where scholars can access its resources for their studies. Its collections reflect Monaco’s rich cultural heritage spanning various periods starting from medieval times up until modern era publications. With such diverse holdings covering multiple disciplines like archaeology, genealogy, cartography etc., this institution plays an important role both locally within Monaco itself et internationally among academic communities interested in local or regional histories tied closely with maritime activities along Mediterranean coastlines historically associated with Kingdoms like Genoa, Venice, Spain, France during different epochs when they contested control over territories around Mediterranean Sea shores including present day Monaco territory.
