That rich man who, clothed in purple, feasted splendidly every day, unmindful of future death, refused to hear the cry of the poor man; now he cries out among the dead, and no one comes to his aid. Where now are the cellars once filled with various liquors? Where are the granaries heaped with grain? Where is the splendid table? Where are fine linen and purple? All things have passed away like a shadow, and the possession of temporal things has perished together with its possessor. What profit could the riches lost in the world bring to the rich man among the dead? What good are honors, what a beautiful wife, what most beloved offspring? Purple will not defend anyone among the dead from cold, nor fine linen from heat, nor gold from want. There the affection of those once beloved in the world will cease; there the kinship of the flesh perishes, because each person will be judged in his own crime.

Now, therefore, man, remember the time that has passed, and from the death of others know that you too are mortal. Where are your ancestors? Where is your father, where your mother, where your brother or sister? Where are the young men with whom you once played the games of childhood? Where are the men, where the women, whose times and deaths you once knew? Where is your wife, where your children? Behold, they are dead, and the memory of them has perished from among men. What good now are riches to them, what honors, what pleasures of the flesh? What, I ask, do the delights of the world profit the dead, for which they now receive the torments of hell? Or what harm after death could poverty and tribulation endured in the world now do to the just, for which they receive the joys of eternal happiness? When a man dies, what profit is it to him that he lived long? What if you should live on earth for a thousand years, rich and healthy? By no multitude of years, even if you delay it, can you escape death.

Indeed, when the hour of death has come, it takes away every pleasure of life. In that hour you will reckon your having lived as nothing; you will count as nothing all the gladness of the life you have had, since you fear that you are about to pass to punishment. Then you will wish that you had despised the world, and you will say: Alas, wretched me, who have spent my whole life so shamefully in sins; who, loving the world, have walked after vanities. Why, when I was healthy, did I not fear this hour full of dangers? Would that I had spent my days in the service of God; would that I had lived as a monk in a monastery; now I would pass securely from the world. What good are riches to me now? What help are honors? What power, what nobility, what the vain knowledge of the world? I was rich and powerful; I possessed gold and silver, and there was no end to my substance. But what good are all these things to me now? Behold, now I die, and for the merits of my life I am about to receive a sorrowful sentence. What does the gold in which I trusted profit me, when it cannot free me from the dominion of death? Woe to wretched me. While healthy I put off repentance; now I lie on the little bed of death, and I am wholly unable to think anything for the salvation of my soul, now departing. Now I wish to repent, but I cannot. O accursed cares of the world, O vain and perishing riches, O pleasures of the flesh, always harmful, how you have deceived wretched me. I had believed my life to be good and pleasing to God, if I persevered in the Christian faith. But faith without works of piety is vain. Now countless crimes leap forth from their hiding places; now many wicked deeds present themselves to memory, of which, while healthy, I had no consciousness at all. Now, then, what shall I do? To whom shall I flee? O how securely I would depart from this life if I had walked religiously and piously in the way of the Lord. Meditate on these things, O man, while you live; think on these things while you are healthy; and do not fail at all times to live as you desire to be found in the hour of death.
