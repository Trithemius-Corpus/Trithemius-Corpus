Hence the wise man admonishes, saying: “Do not fear the judgment of death.” As if he were saying openly: Do not fear what you cannot escape; nor increase the danger by fearing, lest you fall into despair. What then? Is death therefore not to be feared? It is to be feared by the wicked and the reprobate, for whom it makes an entrance to hell. But by the good and the faithful it is to be feared in such a way that it restrains them from sin, not that it breaks their constancy. Yet for all, discreet meditation on death is good and useful; the memory of it preserves the good and the just in their holy purpose, and calls back the impious and sinners to repentance. For to the holy and the just, death is the entrance to eternal life, as the blessed Apostle says: “For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.” Why gain? Because by death a passage is prepared for me to joy; through it I go from labor to rest, from abasement to glory, from poverty to incomparable riches, from fear to security, from the world to heaven, from death to eternal life. But this confidence belongs only to the perfect, to whom this world has been crucified, and they to the world. Unhappy I, Trithemius: who will deliver me from the body of this death? I endure the weariness of the present life, and I greatly fear to die. I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, but I fear to appear before the inward judge, whom I recognize that I have offended by many sins. I bear my sojourn with distress, in which I see that I have made no progress; and therefore with the holy Apostle I mix lamentation. For he says: “I know that good does not dwell in me, that is, in my flesh. For to will is present with me, but to accomplish I do not find. For the good that I will, I do not do; but the evil that I do not will.” If the holy Apostle could say this of himself, what shall we wretches do? Woe to us, to whom at times neither to will is present nor to accomplish. O if the strict examination of the inward judge never fled from the eyes of our mind; O if the memory of the last things never withdrew from our thoughts, the world would easily become worthless to us, and the bitterness of repentance would please us. How strictly God judges the sins of men after the deed is shown by many examples. For no sin is so small that a punishment would not have to be assigned for it, if it has not been purged in this life through repentance.

Let us carefully consider what is read concerning the saints of God, Severinus, archbishop of Cologne, and Paschasius, deacon of the Roman Church: that both, shining with miracles, were seen after death in the punishments of purgatory; the one because, occupied with the affairs of the commonwealth, he had at times performed the canonical hours too negligently; the other because in the election of Pope Symmachus he had dissented from the will of others, although with good zeal. What shall we wretches do, if men most holy and renowned for miracles could not escape the punishments of purgatory? What, I say, shall we unjust men do, if saints are tortured after death in the punishments of purgatory? How will the impious man be able to escape punishments, if the saint could not? Therefore let us fear, brothers; by fearing let us take care, lest through negligence we fall. Let us do penance at all times, because in this life alone is penance fruitful, when we can not only escape punishments, but also merit glory.

*That, by consideration of death and judgment, the world, the flesh, and all the pleasures of this life are to be despised.*

*Chapter 10.*

Divine Scripture admonishes us, saying: “In all things remember your last things.” And to the sinful nation Moses says: “It is a nation without counsel and without prudence: would that they were wise and understood, and would provide for their last things.” What last things? Those, namely, that follow the present life. What are they? Judgment and death. Indeed, how useful the memory of death is to a man, the wise man declares, saying: “Son, remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.” Behold, death hastens; the strict judgment of God approaches. How long will you sleep? How long will you put off repentance? You will not be able to live long in this world; you will quickly be snatched to the judgment of God, where you are to receive a sorrowful sentence for the negligence of your life.
