*Book on the vanity and misery of human life, by lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, [unclear].*

*It begins.*

Since the present life is nothing when compared with the felicity above, for which we sigh earnestly, I cannot sufficiently marvel at your madness, my soul: for you bear your exile, which, prolonged as it is, you ought to have wept over, delightfully, as though it were your fatherland. A thing indeed most worthy of wonder, but to your folly, when, neglecting the banquets of eternal life, you embrace the filth of this world.

O rude and miserable one, who, forgetful of your Father’s goodness, pursue things perishable and empty, unmindful of eternal things. You seek vain solace in pilgrimage, though you are called to the heavenly kingdom. But you err, O my soul: in this life there is no fatherland, but a way by which one goes to joys. For the holy apostle says: We do not have here on earth an abiding city, but we seek the future one in heaven. But the heavenly city admits no one unless he loves. If you love, why do you not hasten? The fire of charity knows no delay.

In this life we have been placed to trade, through holy exercises, our expenses for eternal life; there, whoever fails in seedtime will not rejoice in the harvest. Where love is, there is possession. Love, therefore, what you cannot lose; seek what you may possess without end. Behold, the world passes away, and its concupiscence, and no further space for returning is left. Now, therefore, you must take the greatest care not to perish with what flows away; for if you love the world as it passes, you are preparing to run with it to destruction.

Turn now to your rest, miserable soul; return to the guide of your youth, desolate daughter; flee to your creator, wandering sheep. What is this, that you are in the land of enemies? You have grown old in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been counted with those who go down to hell. Why is this? Because you have abandoned the fountain of wisdom; you have withdrawn from your God through the byways of disobedience; you have made void the first faith of baptism. For if you had walked in the way of God, if you had resisted the passions of the flesh, you would surely have dwelt in peace upon the earth.

But learn where prudence is, where virtue is, so that you may know at the same time where length of life and sustenance are, where the light of the eyes and peace are. But let him who has fallen add to his effort that he may rise again. Why then do you delay? Why do you put off the necessary amendment of life? For Scripture says: Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day; for he who today is not disposed to take up the ordinances of a more amended life will be less fit tomorrow.

Why then does today’s salvation await a doubtful tomorrow? Do good, and do not be reluctant; watch over saving gains; death will soon be your end. Now, therefore, miserable soul, what else is appointed for you to do, except to give yourself to tears, to bewail your life already passed, and henceforth to correct the remainder of your life?

O miserable condition of human life, which does not grieve as much as it knows itself, but rather, as though secure, rejoices in what it does not know. O security of the soul, always harmful, which neglects virtues, nourishes vices, and shamefully consumes the saving time of penitence. How long, then, do you sleep, soul? How long do you dissemble? You have sown wholly in sins, and dare to trust in the security of the saints?

What are your fruits, fruitless tree? What good works? What progress, stock worthy of burning? You are barren; your fruits are the piercing thorns of sins. Would that they pierced in such a way that they brought compunction; would that they became so bitter that they vanished. O dry wood, empty of good fruits, why do you still occupy the earth? You are a fruitless tree, O my soul, which the supreme, fatherly God has long endured as unfruitful in his field; he has watered it with the dung of heavenly fatness and marked it with many gracious gifts.

Take care, therefore, lest at the end you appear barren; lest you put off the possibility of penitence; lest you lose the acceptable time. For truly the judge of all, who now patiently tolerates you for penitence, condemns more severely if you neglect it. Consider how you may meet him who comes to demand an account of your whole life. What will you answer on that day, O soul, now polluted with so many sins? What, I say, will you answer? You will not be able to hide, when you are called into sight
