You spend the whole, very brief time of your illness in arranging temporal affairs, and you think little about the salvation of the soul. You make a testament for temporal things, and you do not care for the wounds of the soul. You arrange for the pomp of the funeral to be elaborately disposed, and you do not provide for where the soul, soon to depart, is going to go. You solemnly compose a testament, and you do not know whether it will come to execution. For often the avaricious testator takes on unfaithful executors. Nor undeservedly. For he who has proved unfaithful to himself, what other faithful man will he expect? Behold the rich and avaricious man, who while alive contributed little in alms, when dying makes a testament and commands many gifts to be bestowed on the poor, monasteries, and churches, and trusts others more than himself. But when the testator is now dead, what very often follows is not unknown. Now the family of his kin intercepts the testamentary writing; now earthly power opposes itself with violence; now the hands of the executors carry off all the best things, and they scorn to do what the testator commands. If the unfaithful executors find with him an old and rare gold or silver coin, if they see a beautiful codex or anything else at all that is pleasing, they either carry it off secretly, or, if they cannot steal it, buy it from their associates fraudulently and under deceit. Good executors do not do this, but unfaithful ones do. It happens, however, that fraud is done to the avaricious after death, so that those who, while living, had been unwilling to give alms are, even after death, neglected by their own people through the just judgment of God. The rich and avaricious man thinks he will live long in the world, and while he fears that his expenses on the way will be diminished, he loses both life and expenses alike. He thinks himself a [unclear] of the world, and makes no haste at all toward the heavenly homeland. But in a moment he is snatched away, and is appointed to the lower regions to be tormented forever. What then will the vain riches of the world profit him, which he now has as impediments to the heavenly way? “Their gold and silver,” says the prophet, “will not be able to free them on the day of the wrath of the Lord.” O unclean world, how do you reward your own, whom you send across to be tormented in eternal fires? You very often exalt those who love you, and while they trust in temporal goods, suddenly they descend to the lower regions without repentance.

Every dominion is of brief life, and the possession of riches is not stable in the world. For mortal life is brief, earthly power briefer, and prosperity in both very brief. Therefore know your condition, O man; know life; know destruction. Your condition is mortal, life brief, destruction certain. Live in such a way that you do not fear to die. A good conscience does not fear death, through which it believes that it will come to the heavenly homeland. If you are rich and powerful, think that you are soon going to be poor and weak, because he who is a great prince today can tomorrow be a putrid corpse. Let not the vain prosperity of the world deceive you; let not greed for gold seduce you; let not the power of brief honor raise you up. Do good while you live, lest you be sorry; honor the Lord from your substance, because after death it will no longer be in your power. Do not trust others more than yourself; and do not desire that the good which should be done after death be postponed, nor commit now, while alive, to others for distribution at your death what is necessary for your provision on the way. Work good toward all while you can; labor as a good soldier of Jesus Christ, because whatever you have worked as a soldier, you will receive at the end. Whatever you have done on the way of this pilgrimage, at the end you will receive. Therefore whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly, because after death there is no working, but the repayment of good and evil works. Flee the desires of the flesh, which war against the soul; flee sloth; flee vanity. [unclear] now and for a very brief time you will labor, and as your reward you will receive eternal life. Behold, death hastens; you will not be able to live long; you will die soon. But all the things that with great labor over many years you have gathered, you are going to lose in one hour. You will carry nothing with you from all of them: not gold, not silver, not honors, not powers. For all things that are earthly will leave you in a moment; only conscience, with its adornments, will follow you. Riches will profit the dying man nothing, the ambition of the world nothing, the authority of power nothing; but good works alone do not leave the soul desolate.
