Since, therefore, all things are vain, surely whoever loves vanity is vain and desires to vanish away. Hence blessed John admonishes us, saying: “Little children, do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world; for if anyone loves the world, the charity of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world is the concupiscence of the flesh and the concupiscence of the eyes and the pride of life.” The world, therefore, must be fled, because it repays its lovers badly: it damns them forever.

*That this life is a kind of pilgrimage away from our heavenly homeland, in which all must conduct their business. Chapter 3.*

The holy apostle says: “As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord.” And blessed Job says: “The life of man upon earth is warfare.” If, then, we are pilgrims, why do we not hasten to our homeland? If soldiers, why do we not serve? Behold, placed in this life, we know that we are pilgrims, and yet we do not pray that our exile may be ended; but, forgetful of the heavenly homeland, we love the prison of pilgrimage. He who serves badly fears the end of the war. Thus every man who overflows with the riches and honors of the world is very afraid to die. For if he knew that he had completed his service well, he would hasten to the reward. But because he remembers that he has committed many evils, for that reason he is very afraid to depart from the body and appear before the supreme judge. Why do you not do penance while you are healthy? He dies securely who lives well. Recognize that you are a pilgrim [unclear]; the hope of a longer life has deceived [unclear], because while they desire to live longer in the world, they lose the time for repentance. For often a man, by the just judgment of God, while he postpones the will to conversion, is overtaken by death and loses both the will and the power alike. For almighty God, who grants pardon to the penitent, has promised tomorrow to no one who delays.

Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: “Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.” It always cries “today”; today God always admonishes us, and we wait for tomorrow! The day of our last death will come, when we shall pray that the time for penance be prolonged, and we shall not obtain it. Now, healthy and strong, we neglect to repent; when the hour of death arrives, with great force we promise amendment of our life, and afterward we are no longer able to render anything. Why do we not now do what we shall then wish that we had done? At the end the rewards of labor are to be expected, not battles to be undertaken anew. For indeed, as we have said, the present life is nothing other than a pilgrimage and a certain passage to the homeland, in which we cannot stand long, but must always necessarily go forward. Every hour of time diminishes part of our life, and at every single moment cuts away something from our pilgrimage. But he who desires to go unencumbered to the homeland above should lay aside from himself every care of the world.

For the road of the heavenly homeland is narrow: it does not receive one burdened with riches; it despises one swollen with honors; it does not admit one slippery with vices. “It is easier,” says the Lord, “for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven.” Therefore, if riches abound, do not set your heart on them; do not possess them greedily; do not guard them anxiously. “Better is a little for the just man than many riches of sinners.” Hence the holy apostle admonishes us, saying: “Having food and covering, let us be content with these. For those who wish to become rich in this world often fall into the temptations of the devil.” What use are gold and silver to you, O rich men of the world? How long will you thirst for and seek gold? What is money doing in little chests? For whom are you storing up treasure? For yourselves, or for your children? Not for yourselves, who possess nothing for long in this world. Therefore you seek money for your children, whom you have made followers of your avarice. But what of you who have no children? For whom are you storing up treasure? Not for yourselves, but for others; not for your own, but for strangers. Placed in the pilgrimage of this life, burdened by the weight of riches out of love for others, you go along; and while you seek to enrich your children, you wander far from the way of truth.
