For from the highest prince of mortals down to the farmer's son, no man on earth is without misery. For the whole world is full of misery and bitterness. If you enter religious life, you will find misery; if you remain in the world, you will encounter misery. If you have become learned in every variety of writings, you will find misery in all things, since he who adds knowledge adds also sorrow. If you shine forth as a great, rich, and powerful man, you will not lack much misery. Nor can misery be absent from the humble and the lowly, who are often the more unhappy in that they do not recognize themselves as miserable. The rustic life also, necessary to the human race, is full of many temptations and miseries. O vanity of vanities, why do you think yourself happy? Each man is displeased with his own estate, and another's pleases him. Few are content with their own bounds. The man who has taken a wife in marriage groans that he is bound; he longs to recover the liberty he has lost; he praises the life of monks. He loathes what he has and desires what he does not have. Another, bound to the monastic profession, shudders at the order and the habit; he turns his intention back to the world; he censures the purpose of holy living; he loudly praises marriage. But why do I use many words? Each man judges another's fortune better. And this is vanity and affliction of spirit. Believe me, every estate in the course of human life has its own miseries; and where happiness is thought to be greater, there misery overflows more copiously. Those who are set over others think the fortune of their subjects better; but those who are compelled to be subject to prelates believe themselves miserable and the others happy. But we are all deceived when we think that any estate in the world lacks misery. Everywhere in this world there is misery; everywhere there is temptation. For no one should truly be called happy before death.

*On the brevity of the present life and the necessity of death. Chapter VIII. C.III.*

Man, living for a brief time, is shaken in this life by many and almost continual miseries, so that through the temptations which he endures in the world he may hasten to come to eternal joys. For Scripture says: through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. The time of our life on earth is very brief and subject to many calamities. He who today lives healthy and glad may perhaps tomorrow be counted among the dead. Why then are you proud, earth and ashes? Why do you not recognize that you are mortal? Or if you recognize it, why do you not fear? You will not be able to remain long in this life, since at every hour you are running toward death. Why do you swell with empty honors, soon to be food for worms? Why do you think yourself rich, when you know that very shortly you will be utterly poor? Why are you puffed up over worldly knowledge, when after a little while you are going to lose all your senses? Why do you glory in beauty, when in a little while you will be a most vile corpse and rotting dung? O ever blind folly of the human condition. Why do you not recognize yourself as miserable, O mortal man? You think yourself rich and happy, and you give scarcely any thought to how near your death is. It is at the doors; it is already hastening swiftly to your destruction. Why do you presume upon earthly power? For every dominion is brief. You are a bishop: you will soon die, and another will rise up in your place. Where are your predecessors? Where is the one immediately before you? They are dead, and you have risen up in their place. You will not be able to remain long in the chair of mortals, but you will soon follow those whom you have sent on before. You are abbot and prior of monks: you will not remain long. Death will come, and is already hastening; it will give your priory to another, but will present you to the supreme judge to be examined. What then will that brief power profit you, when by then it has already been lost? What will episcopal dignity, what will pastoral reverence, be able to help? Indeed, for the sheep entrusted to you, you must then be presented to God the supreme shepherd, and you will have to render account to him for all of them. On that day you will know that it falls upon the pastor as guilt if God, the inward shepherd, has been able to find any less usefulness in his sheep.
