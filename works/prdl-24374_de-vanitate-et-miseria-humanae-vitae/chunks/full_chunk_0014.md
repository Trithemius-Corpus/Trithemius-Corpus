But he who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly. As long as we live in this life, we are wretched and are shaken in the world by various tribulations. No one is free from miseries, no one is untouched by tribulations, no one lives without temptations. And just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so tribulation does not earn merit, but patience does. The just have temptations in the world; sinners have them too. But for the just, adversity through patience is turned into the merit of eternal happiness; for sinners and the impious, however, because of their impatience and reprobate life, it is reckoned as sin. Tribulation makes no one blessed; only the love of God and patience cause merit. For sinners too are vexed by many miseries in this world, yet they are driven away stripped of merit. Why is this? The fear of God is not before their eyes; they endure evils not for love of the Lord nor with regard to the heavenly fatherland, but by the necessity of human life. For many are the scourges of the sinner, but mercy will encompass only those who hope in the Lord. Since, then, no one can live in the world without tribulations, let us make a virtue of necessity, so that the evils we necessarily bear we may turn toward the love of God. Let the misery of our own condition never be hidden from us; let it never depart from our memory, so that, while we bring it to mind in the prosperity of the world, we may beware of all pride and vain security. Finally, from the very consideration of our own vileness we perceive what we ought to think about ourselves. We are conceived and born in sins; with labor and sorrow we are nourished, and by many continual tribulations we are beaten. Meanwhile, what does mortal man hope for? Death alone will give an end to miseries. What, then, do you hope for, O man? The end of misery. Why, then, do you fear death? For no one will be happy before he dies. Do you desire the misery of this life to be ended, and do you dread the judgment of death? O misery and most grievous affliction of spirit. To live is misery for us, and to die is danger. We shudder at the prison, and we fear to go out. What, then, shall we do? Wherever we turn, we find misery. Behold, those who serve the world are not without misery, but are vexed daily by many tribulations and anxieties. To what end? For what reward? That they may obtain in the world what they desire. For the devil too has his martyrs; the world has them; pleasures have them. Indeed, those who serve the devil in carnal sins endure many miseries in this world, so that they may know that from temporal punishments they will pass to eternal ones. The lovers of this world also, who seek empty honors, who seek perishable riches, who fulfill the desires of the flesh, daily subject themselves to endless miseries and anxieties. Envy follows the happiness of this life, and each person exposes himself to greater danger in proportion as he rises higher in power. For it is necessary that the one whom many fear should himself fear many. Finally, who will be able to set forth the anxieties and continual miseries of the rich? For the wise man says: The satiety of the rich man does not let him sleep. Where there are riches, there is anxiety. These are the martyrs of the world, for whom death will not be the departure from miseries, but the entrance into eternal tribulation. They pass from small anxieties to the greatest, from temporal punishments to eternal ones, from the lightest tribulations to dreadful ones. For death does not bring the impious and sinners the end of misery, but the beginning of eternal calamity. But the just, through the approach of death, come to the end of human tribulation. For at the very point where in this world they end life and misery by dying, there they begin to live happily without end. Therefore, whoever desires to come to the joys of eternity, whoever longs for those choirs of the citizens above, let him present himself as a martyr of God, and patiently and humbly endure all the evils of this world for the love of God. It is better to enter the kingdoms of heaven through the miseries of this life with patience than through impatience to be sent to hell. What profit is the brief consolation of the world, which perishes like an instant? What are riches, what are honors, what is the vain presumption of life? Woe to the lovers of the world, woe to the rich, avaricious and impious, who, having despised the love of God, feed upon the pleasures of the flesh. Why do you deceive yourself, O rich lover of the world? Why do you daily slay your immortal soul through the desires of the flesh? To what end do you trust in the multitude of your riches?
