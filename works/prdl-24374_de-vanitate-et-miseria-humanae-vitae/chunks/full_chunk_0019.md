Hence it is said of Pharaoh in Exodus: The heart of Pharaoh has been hardened; he does not wish to let my people go except by a strong hand and an outstretched arm. Who is understood by Pharaoh except the devil, who is said to hold hardened sinners in his servitude? For he is the head of all the wicked. Whoever he has once subjected to his rule in the mire of depraved habit, he does not permit to flee to the remedies of penitence except by a strong hand.

What is the strong hand? What is the outstretched arm? Listen. The strong hand signifies the grace of almighty God, without whose aid no one rises again to penitence. For truly, one who has once fallen of his own accord into the pit of sin will not be able to rise without the special grace of God. No one can stand before a fall unless the grace of the Almighty, by preserving him, goes before him; no one can rise after a fall unless the clemency of the merciful God, by helping him, raises him up.

What then is free will? What is the outstretched arm? Listen. Freedom of will is necessary for every good work; through it the grace of God works in us both to will and to accomplish, according to good will. As an artisan works and completes his work through a suitable instrument, so the grace of God through free will disposes and does a good work in human beings. And as free will without the grace of God accomplishes nothing, so the grace of God, for working good in us, requires our will. Hence the holy Apostle, writing to the Corinthians, says: We exhort you not to receive grace in vain.

Is the grace of God, then, sometimes received in vain? Plainly in vain. How is this? Listen. Whoever, while in health, neglects to do penance; whoever scorns doing good while he can, has received the grace of God in vain. Therefore it is said: He will not let my people go except by a strong hand and an outstretched arm; that is, the grace of God is at hand. Stretch [it] out, lest you lose both grace and [at the same time] the time for penitence. Why do you delay? Why do you tarry? What is suffering violence? How long will you dissemble? How long will you receive the grace of God in vain? Why do you not take account of the strong hand for your liberation? Why do you not stretch out the arm of your will toward the grace of God, which offers itself freely? Why do you not run to meet God as he calls? Why are you negligent?

There is no virtue without labor, no struggle against evil habit without very great sweat. One does not come to the heavenly kingdom by the desires of the flesh, but of the spirit. No one, however, can extinguish the desires of the flesh unless, aided by the grace of God, he has learned to do violence to himself. What violence? Hear it from the Lord. Whoever wishes, he says, to come after me, let him deny himself to himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. Here violence lies hidden; here consists the sinew of victory. If, with the flesh conquered, you desire to come to the kingdom of heaven, you must despise your own will for the love of God. Walk according to the spirit, and you will overcome the desires of the flesh. Nevertheless, in all these things do not neglect to bear your cross daily, if you desire to come after the Lord to your salvation. What cross? That of daily temptation. In what way must it be borne? Through patience. For the Lord himself says to his followers. And the holy Apostle says: Patience is necessary for you.

Now therefore stretch out the arm of your will, if you wish to escape dark Egypt. Why are you afraid? Why do you hesitate? Why do you doubt? Why do you not begin penitence today, since you are not unaware that you will soon die? Behold, death, the cutter-off of penitence, is already hastening; it will extinguish alike your life and your long-deferred purpose of repenting.

*On the Bitterness of Death and the Severity of Judgment. Chapter IX.*

Of all terrible things death is the more terrible: with the greatest pains it snatches the soul from the body; it [turns] the flesh into dust and sends the spirit, to be judged according to its merits, to the tribunal of God. For the just man, by whatever death he has been overtaken, will be in refreshment. Not so the impious, not so: for them death gives an end to the present life and a beginning to eternal calamity.
