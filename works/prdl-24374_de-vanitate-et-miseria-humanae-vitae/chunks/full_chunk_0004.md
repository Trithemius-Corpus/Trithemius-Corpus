But to appear will be fearsome, except for the good: those who in this life patiently undergo evils for the love of God; who love the joys of heavenly happiness; who manfully despise this world. There all things are open; there the thoughts of hearts lie exposed; there no crimes of evil men can be hidden. Many sins will there suddenly rush forth, as if from ambush, which now are reckoned either nothing or slight. Happy is the soul that anticipates this day with careful meditation; that, while now remaining in the flesh, turns itself to holy exercises and carefully keeps the house of its conscience clean, as far as it can. No one can confidently appear before the sight of the inward judge if he has not kept himself clean from this wicked age. For everyone who remembers that he has offended his lord and judge fears to come to his judgment. For the more he knows himself guilty in crime, the more he refuses to appear. Yet in vain does he try to flee from the sight of the judge, whose presence fills all things. And truly at that time it will be intolerable for the wicked to appear before the supreme judge, but possible for none to hide. For the holy Apostle says: We shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ, so that each may receive the reward of his work.

There the just man will stand in great confidence before the Lord, receiving the recompense of his proven faith, since in this life he showed himself a pilgrim to the world, bravely subdued the vices of the flesh while placed in the flesh, and with the whole desire of his mind sighed for heavenly things. But those who, now placed in the flesh, love this life as their homeland, who seek the empty honors of the world, who thirst for perishable riches [unclear], who plunge themselves into the mire of sins, will stand there shamefully, broken from their confidence. O who is wise and will understand these things? who is prudent and will fear them, so that, understanding future dangers, he may fear, and fearing may escape through care? But as long as we live in this mortal life, we are on the way to the heavenly homeland; on this way it is never granted us to stand still through sloth, but we are commanded to journey from virtue to virtue, as though panting toward our homeland. On this way, he who does not advance falls back; he who does not run retreats. For our standing still is to advance in virtues; to retreat is to grow sluggish in good work. But then we properly tend toward our heavenly homeland, when with sincere devotion we obey the commandments of God and of the Catholic Church. Therefore whoever desires to come to the heavenly homeland should seek no delays on the way, nor comforts of distraction. For whoever delights in the beauty of exile is proved to love his homeland less. And so he who does not despise the pleasures of this world will not be able to come to the heavenly homeland. Therefore, for those who wish to advance on the way of God, before all things contempt for pleasures is enjoined; without it the charity of Christ is never acquired. For he himself said: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself to himself, and take up his cross and follow me. Christ our Lord, the way, the truth, and the life, commanded these things, taught these things, did these things.

Christ is the way to eternal life, as he himself says: I am the way, the truth, and the life; I am the door, since no one comes to the Father except through me. What else do you seek, wretched soul; what more do you require? Christ Jesus, the Son of God and man, handed down to you the rule of living: he taught contempt of the world by word and showed it by example. Behold, God, the supreme arbiter, calls you to contempt of the world, and do you still dissemble? Why do you not willingly forsake the world for the love of God, which you are soon to leave through the death of the flesh? Nothing under the sun remains in the power of man, but all things vanish like smoke.

*That all worldly things are subject to vanity.*

*Vanity of vanities. Chapter 2.* That preacher, once the wisest of men, says: Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity. What does “all” mean? What is “vanity of vanities”? All things that totter and perish by time and change. The world passes away, and its desire; and we all die, and like waters poured out on the earth, we do not return again. Therefore whatever is corrupted is rightly named vanity of vanities. But all things that are generated in the world are subject to corruption; and therefore all things are vain, and vanity of vanities.
