Yesterday, when he passed by, the knees of all were bent; today he is carried dead to the pit, and no one deigns to touch the covering of his head. Yesterday men were tortured at his command; today, dead, he is tortured in hell, and there is no one to free him. Yesterday, when he spoke, the ears of all listened in silence; today he cries out with a loud voice in torments, and no one feels compassion. Yesterday, as though he would not die, he overflowed with royal banquets; today, because of the heat of the fires, he burns with thirst, and there is no one to give drink to the thirsty man. Yesterday, sitting on the tribunal, gleaming with gold and little gems, he drew the peoples into admiration of himself; today, wrapped about with the vilest linen cloth, he swarms with horrible worms. Yesterday he delighted in the sweet melodies of singers; today, placed in hell, he has become the mockery of demons. Yesterday his fat and shining body was cherished with very temperate baths; today in hell he is tortured with pitch and sulphur, as it is written: “Give to him in torments as much as he was in delights.” And again: “The powerful,” he says, “will suffer powerful torments.” What then will the honors of time past profit the wretched soul? What riches? What powers? There the impious have ceased from tumult; there the honor of earthly power is despised; there the greatness of riches is looked down upon. Why then are you proud, puffed-up power? Why do you swell, insatiable greed for gold? Whatever honor or riches you possess in the world, you will soon, and perhaps today, lose. And when you have crossed to hell by way of Acheron, you will carry absolutely nothing with you from this world, except this alone: what good or evil you have done in the body. The world passes away, and its desire; every earthly power passes away briefly, nor does anything lasting remain under the sun. What if a man is great upon the earth? What if he is powerful, learned, brave, handsome, rich? The life of all is very brief, nor is there any distinction of persons or dignities: whatever is mortal dies. For the wise man says: No one lives forever, or has experience of this thing. Established
