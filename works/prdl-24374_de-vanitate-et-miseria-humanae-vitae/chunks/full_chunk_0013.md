For you say, weighed down by age and vices: “Ah, wretched me, why did I spend the days of my youth so uselessly? Why did I put off the paths of a more amended life? Why, when young and strong, did I not put on the habit of holy conduct? Unhappy man, I thought old age more suited to penitence; and what seemed difficult to me in youth, I hoped would be easy in old age. But my folly deceived me, and I have been shamefully deceived. Now I have learned by experience that old age does not receive habits, but has them. Whatever the youthful mind has once drunk in, the old man will keep even to the grave. Now, as an old man, I wish to do penance, but I cannot, because, pressed down under evil habit, I groan. Whatever good I begin contrary to habit I cannot bring to completion, because it is hard for an old mind to think new things. The practice of virtues, which in youth, if I had wished, seemed easy enough to me, now in old age appears impossible. Woe to me, because in me that saying of the wise man has been verified: ‘A youth according to his way: even when he has grown old, he will not depart from it.’ For old age is entangled in many inconveniences, from which it is not freed except by death. Therefore, O young men, whose age is fit for bearing labors, learn to devote yourselves to virtues and holy works; grow accustomed to overcoming the desires of the flesh, because he who as a young man neglects the pursuit of a more amended life will abhor it as an old man. As each person desires to be found in old age, so let him live in youth. For he who in his youth keeps watch over good morals easily perseveres good even to old age. For whatever is grasped in adolescence or youth is held more firmly. But at whatever age a man betakes himself to penitence, in all things patience will be necessary. For he will experience many temptations and adversities while walking in the law of the Lord, which he did not know before, when he was proceeding in the path of the world. And so, approaching the service of God, a man of whatever condition will endure [unclear], against which he will fight in the fear of the Lord with patience. For truly the kingdom of God suffers violence, and the violent seize it. He who neglects to bring force against the temptations of the flesh will depart not a victor but conquered, and will not be fit for the kingdom of God. But why do I proceed with many words? There is no righteous man on earth who is free from temptations. For Scripture says: ‘Many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these the Lord will deliver them.’ And another Scripture: ‘Because through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.’ But that all adversities of the present life are to be borne by us with equanimity, the holy apostle indicates, saying: ‘The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that will be revealed in us.’ For everything that we can endure on earth is of the briefest time; but the reward due to our sufferings and patience remains forever. Therefore, if anything adverse befalls us, if any temptation of worldly depravity comes upon us, mindful always of future recompense, let us embrace patience, knowing that all these things will not last long.

*On the manifold misery of the human condition. Chapter six.*

We know the miseries of the human condition to be manifold, so that, if the present life were eternal, it would not undeservedly seem contemptible to the wise. Hence holy Job says: ‘Man born of woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries, and never remains in the same state.’ Indeed, the whole life of man upon earth is misery: to live is labor for him, to die is danger. How long, then, O man, will you love your misery? How long will you love your exile in this world? See with how many evils, with how many miseries, with how many tribulations the world pursues you, and still you do not consent to be separated from its love? Daily it drags you toward destruction, and you pretend not to flee from what is collapsing. Man is a pilgrim upon earth; at every hour something is snatched away from the road of one passing by, and daily he is pressed on toward the end of the journey. Like the days of a hireling, man at every moment tends toward evening, where he will receive wages for his labor. Whoever has labored well and bravely in the vineyard of the Lord will receive the eternal reward of labor. Why then do we delay? Why do we lie idle all day in the marketplace?
