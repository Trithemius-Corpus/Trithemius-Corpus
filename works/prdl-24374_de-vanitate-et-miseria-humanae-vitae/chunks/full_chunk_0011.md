For no one suddenly becomes highest; rather, one ascends to the heights of perfection by fixed steps. The greatest difficulty of the spiritual life is set before beginners; it is gradually overcome by those making progress, and wholly driven away by the perfect. No one, moreover, has ever been able to make progress who did not first begin in due order. And many indeed begin the amendment of a wicked way of life, who nevertheless do not attain progress, because the sins which today they appear to lament, tomorrow they again do not fear to commit. But weak is the purpose of repenting which is so often broken by a new sin. The sluggard wishes to rise and does not wish it; and often, while he does not fear to postpone his own willing, he loses both time and ability alike. He [unclear] himself in the bed of wicked habit; and while he neglects to break it, he creates for himself a certain necessity of sinning. For everyone who commits sin is made the servant of sin. Indeed, he who, through the grace of God, could have stood if he had wished before the fall, is no longer easily permitted to rise after his ruin. For the habit of sin weighs down the fallen man, whom it does not permit to rise without the special grace of God. O how many sinners are found today: the avaricious, adulterers, fornicators, the unclean, and those like them; when wicked and long-lasting habit is not resisted, a certain necessity is produced. But one must take up the purpose of amending one’s life with great fortitude, because if continual patience is lacking, a man’s will quickly wavers. Therefore let each person who desires to make progress in his own state clothe his mind as with the hardest rock: not so that he may kick against sounder counsels, but so that he may resist evil suggestions with a strong spirit. For security is close to danger.

*That Many and Various Things Draw Us Back from a Holy Purpose and Impede Us from the Fruit of Penance. Chapter 5.*

Finally, no one is able fully to consider how many snares, how many pits, and how many impediments the devil is accustomed to set before penitents. He drives them on with a thousand arts and deceits, so that he may be able to recall from his purpose the man whom he has perceived to be seeking the remedies of penance. And indeed, according to the variety of the state, sex, and condition of human beings, he uses different ways of doing harm; and what he cannot accomplish by himself, he strives to fulfill through evil people by various associations. Often, moreover, he pursues the penitent even to death; and the one whom, as a sinner, he permits to live calmly in evils, once converted he tries to overwhelm with infinite tribulations. Now he stirs up an improper wife against a man of holy purpose; now he inflames friends and relatives to anger, so that they do not fear to persecute and blaspheme as delirious and insane the man whom, as a penitent, they ought to have imitated. But if he has perceived that he cannot make progress in this way either, he turns to more serious things. He turns into hatred those once dear to the man, and beloved companions and friends, so that those who once pleasingly clung to him while he was sinning withdraw far from him as a penitent. Then the enemy puts the hand of his cruelty upon the temporal substance of the penitent; he arranges fires in the houses of the penitent man; he takes away honors; he tears away riches in various ways; he inflames the minds of judges against him; he sets up lawsuits; he seeks occasions. Now earthly power oppresses the innocent man; it draws temporal possessions into the treasury as though they belonged to a criminal; it does not allow him to live in peace. Amid these things the spirit of a man withdrawing himself from the world is very often violently shaken; he suspects that he has been abandoned by God; and unless he has put on fortitude and patience against all things that can oppose him, he soon falls away from the holy purpose of one who amends his life. But if, in all these things, the champion and lover of penance has fought bravely, a more serious contest very often approaches. For when the devil considers himself defeated in the removal of temporal things, he lets loose his force against the flesh of the penitent. With God’s permission he applies various calamities to the man, so that he may turn him away from a good purpose through impatience.
