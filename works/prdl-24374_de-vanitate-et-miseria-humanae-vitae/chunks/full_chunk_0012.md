I had hoped to have you as a husband, a manager of my affairs; and you not only neglect domestic care, but also despise wretched me.”

From this, quarrels rise up against the penitent; from this, the woman’s hatreds arise; here the sum of inward calamity is woven together. No one believes, unless he has experienced it, how great a misery is found among married persons of unlike purpose. For always the man who desires to live according to the spirit carries his own most intimate adversaries with him.

And if every outward tribulation also should be lacking, even so the devil does not rest, but turns himself to inward battles. He suggests many and varied things to the penitent; he sets before him the difficulty of perseverance; he subtly shows the frailty of the human condition. Consequently the inward thought will say: “How long will you keep up this rigor of penitence?” If he is young, it will say: “Why do you torture your youth before its time? Will you obtain the kingdom of God by affliction more than the rest? You are young; you will not be able to endure this labor of penitence long. Rather, young man, rejoice in your youth; and when you have come to old age, then you will take up the remedies of penitence.”

But to old men it says: “Why do you destroy yourself, an old man advanced in days? Why do you not spare the age of old age, and why do you kill your weak little body by indiscreetly mortifying it? God does not require plunder as a burnt offering. But act more discreetly; let your service be reasonable, lest it be reckoned to you as sin. God is merciful; he will accept your good will in place of the work of penitence.”

In short, in this manner the devil suggests many things to the penitent man; the flesh sets many similar things against him; the world also shows many things that ought to break his holy purpose. But the strong and wise man despises all these things, scorns all the suggestions of the devil, and by perseverance bursts apart all the bonds of the world. Those doing penance in the flesh are never without struggles; those fleeing the world are never without hindrances, because the man who is not touched by one is struck more severely by another. Each man feels and carries his own cross within himself; each bears the weight of his own temptations, since for as long as one lives in this world, no one is permitted to be free from temptations.

And in all these things patience is necessary, without which no one can possess his own soul. Do not pay heed to the suggestions of the enemy, O man; do not yield to the deceitful speeches of the flesh, if you desire to come to the joys of light. While you are young and strong, do not neglect the remedies of penitence, since you have no certain knowledge at all whether you can become old. Death threatens old men openly; for the young it lies hidden in ambush. For an old man cannot live long in the world, and a young man can die quickly.

But if you are old, by no means abhor penitence. Run as much as you can, because you will not be able to remain long in this life. The more years of age you understand yourself to have lived in the world, the more you recognize that you are entangled in greater faults of crimes. Why, therefore, do you put off doing penance, when you know that you are now near death? For a long time the mercy of God has borne with you unto penitence; for a long time the supreme Judge has awaited your amendment; he has granted abundant time for making satisfaction for sins. But you, according to your hardness and impenitent heart, despise the long-suffering of God’s patience; you do not recognize that you have been preserved so that you may repent; you do not dismiss the offenses of your youth. In the time of youth you lived without the fruit of spiritual work; you continued the vice once embedded within you; and, having become older, you do not labor to begin. You do not abandon the vices of your adolescence; you do not fear avarice, detraction, rash judgment concerning your neighbor; and while you do not fear to do evil things, you go down to hell without penitence. Behold, death the cutter-off is now hastening penitence; behold, the end of your life is approaching; and still you put off doing penance, until you lose both life and time together.
