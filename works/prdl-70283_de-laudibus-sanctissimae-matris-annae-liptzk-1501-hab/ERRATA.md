# Errata — prdl-70283 (De Laudibus Sanctissimae Matris Annae, Leipzig witness)

## 2026-06-09 — full re-translation of all five chunks (Fable 5 revision pass)

All five chunks were re-translated from scratch against the source page-scans,
superseding the earlier machine rendering, and independently re-graded against
the Latin; the superseded text is retained in version control. Claude (Fable 5)
worked from the Herzog August Bibliothek page scans rather than from the badly
damaged OCR alone.

This pass supersedes the earlier machine translation that the GPT-5.5 audit
graded (tier C for this work). **The audit grades in `chunks/grades.csv`
describe the superseded text, not the present text.**

What this witness actually is — recovered structure the OCR had buried:

- An anthology of humanist verse for St Anne around Trithemius's treatise:
  tetrastichs by **Trithemius, Conrad Celtis, Theodoric Gresemund the Younger,
  Rudolf von Langen, Josse Bade (Jodocus Badius) of Ghent**, an excerpt of
  **Rudolf Agricola**, and image-epigrams by **Rutger of Venray** (chunk 1 —
  the OCR had mangled nearly every author's name);
- **Jodocus Beissel's *Rosarius* of St Anne** (Aachen, 11 April 1493): elegiac
  couplets in anaphora on "Anna," with "Ave" after each couplet and "Pater
  noster" dividing the decades — the rosary structure is now reproduced
  (chunks 2–3);
- dated, tune-rubricated hymns: **Adam Werner of Themar** (Heidelberg Castle,
  30 January 1493), a hymn to the melody "Gaude visceribus" (Heidelberg,
  5 February 1493), **Trithemius's own sapphic** to the melody of *Ut queant
  laxis* (Sponheim 1494) and his elegiac poem (Sponheim, 17 July 1494), and
  **Rutgerus Sycamber's** hymn cycle (chunks 3–4);
- **Trithemius's letter to the reader** defending the Immaculate-Conception
  chapter, with the printer's note that the letter "is missing from certain
  copies through negligence," and the colophon **"Printed at Leipzig by
  Melchior Lotter"** (chunk 5).

Representative corrections: "in aedibus... puer Laurenti" → *in aduersis
sanctam pulsauerit annam*; "thumb devoutly bent" → *poplite flexo* ("knee");
"nania fluit" → *nauita fluctu* (the sailor on the flood); the letter's
broken-apart paragraphs restored to continuous argument with the Sixtus IV
appeal and the university list legible.

Print cruxes that survive are flagged in a bracketed *Translator's note* at
the end of each chunk; conjectures are identified as conjectures.

## 2026-06-09 — independent codex (GPT-5.5) check of the revision pass

The new chunks were graded twice: once against the damaged OCR (the v3 audit's
frame of reference) and once against the page images themselves. The
image-referenced pass confirmed the recovered structure and caught real errors
that were then applied: von Langen's *audis* ("you hear"), Beissel's "garlands
and roses," the Revelation 12:1 sun/moon image, *sistere delubris* ("stand in
the shrines"), the hymn author read as "Johannes Herbst of Lauterburg(?)," the
rubric dates 3 February 1493 and 7 July 1494, *maculas peccata* ("stains and
sins"), and a more cautious university list in the letter.

## 2026-07-10 — corrected chunk 1/2 order (swapped vs working lane)

Chunks 1 and 2 were swapped relative to the working translation lane: the published order had the tetrastich (chunk 1) before the blank-page placeholder (chunk 2), but the source page order is blank-page then tetrastich. Swapped them back to match the lane and the Latin segment order (placeholder / segment 1 first, tetrastich / segment 2 second). The reading text is unaffected (the placeholder is skipped); the parallel viewer now shows the correct English opposite each Latin segment.
