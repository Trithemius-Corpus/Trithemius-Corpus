By the merits of the exalted Mother, richly endowed,
who brought forth for the world
her who was the parent of the Son of God the Father.

Her life, ever to be venerated by us,
is deservedly praised as blessed;
with [unclear] endowment she increases [unclear]
for wretched minds.

She heals the sick, cleanses all evils,
brightens the hearts of the sorrowful with joy,
sets in order what is fitting, rules and governs,
smooths what is rough.

Mighty, dwelling in the court
of the highest King, she will wipe away stains and sins,
bestows grace, and leads faithful souls
to the stars.

Here, as the brothers throng your feasts,
uttering vows and praying good things of you,
unite, warm, refresh, and govern them,
glorious Mother.

Let honor and power be to the Thunderer above,
who, God simple and likewise threefold,
bestowed upon Anne the crown of great glory
forever. Amen.

*Elegiac poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on Saint Mother Anne.*

How great the rewards that Anne gives back to her worshippers,
no one grasps in mind or can speak with the mouth.
She drives away harsh things, obtains pardon, increases
ornaments of honor for her own, and causes them to be in heaven.
On the other hand, many very bitter things attend
the despisers of Mother Anne; look to yourself, wicked one.
She tramples the proud, casts the powerful down from honor,
and brings it about that they often suffer great scandals.
This is clear and remains fixed: Anne offers joyful things
to those who honor her, but brings back more sorrowful things to her enemies.

Therefore, men, I admonish you: serve so great a patroness;
and let the choir of women also be present together in love.
It is clearly established by examples, what we write;
let open ears receive what helps pious hearts.

*From Sponheim, on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August, 1493.*

*Sapphic poem of Rutger Siccamber, as a hymn in praise of Saint Anne.*

We sing your praises, venerable
Anne; favor those who praise you with melodies,
obtaining pardon for all, granting
rewards of salvation.

You, Anne, parent of the Mother of Christ,
shining in the highest citadel of heaven,
obtain by prayer whatever [you seek] from the Son
of the Almighty.

You heal the sick, wipe away stains,
make faithful servants obtain their wish;
at last you grant them to climb
the heavenly kingdom.

By [unclear] morals, Anne, you bent
the highest Lord with many prayers,
so that he would grant you to become in the world
the parent of so great a child.

Let honor and power be to the highest Father,
to the heavenly Son, and to the Holy Spirit;
brightness, virtue, piety, and strength
through every age.

*Another by the same.*

Let us all resound the diligent praises
of Anne, mother of the Virgin Mother,
with all our strength, from the marrow of the soul,
with ringing voice.

She brought forth for the wretched race,
foul with the stain of mother Eve,
the noble offspring and radiant brightness
which she gave to the world.

This light of the darkening world
drove out the darkness, shining upon all
with heavenly light, as Saint John proclaims
in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine maiden,
whose Son you call your grandson
only according to the flesh, do not despise
the praying people.

Virgin, favor the prayers of your mother Anne,
who entreats earnestly for us;
you, grandson Jesus, readily fulfill
her petition.

To the Son of the daughter of mother Anne,
together with the highest God, let honor and power be;
let equal honor be to the blessed Spirit
for all time.

*Another poem by the same, iambic dimeter, which is sung to the melody of the hymns of Ambrose.*

Bent low, let us honor
the feast of blessed Anne,
singing due thanks with our voices
and with our inmost hearts.

This is the mother of the gracious Virgin,
who brought forth the radiance of the world;
this is she who by her virtues
made herself most pleasing to God.

She drives away delays, confers grace
by her prayers,
relieves the most dreadful diseases,
of body as well as of spirit.

And in praise of her, O King of heaven,
to you with sonorous songs,
and at the same time to the Son and the Spirit,
we utter pious praises. Amen.

*[Unclear] poem by the same, which is sung to the melody “Crux fidelis” or “Pange lingua.”*

This company, with voice, vows,
and pious hearts,
desires to proclaim the feasts
of Mother Anne, so illustrious.
Kindly be present, we pray,
to those who praise you together,
you parent of Mary’s mother,
you most pleasing to God.
