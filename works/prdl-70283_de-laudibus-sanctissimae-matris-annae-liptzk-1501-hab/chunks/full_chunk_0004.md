Hail, mother Anne: the sailor calls upon you amid the fearful wave, if winds and billow toss his torn boat. Hail.
Hail, mother Anne: you are always to be feared by the black tyrant. Hail.
As ice flees fire, so that guilty one flees you. Hail.
Hail, Anne: mortal hearts cannot ask of you in vain; whatever you will, your daughter and God too will will. Hail.
Therefore, Anne, blessed by your merits and by so great an offspring, great mother Anne, look upon our prayers. Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary*

Almighty and most loving God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as the mother of the mother of your Son, graciously grant that we, who honor her memory with faithful devotion, may obtain, by her merits and prayers, the aids of eternal life. Through [the same].

Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beissel, professor of both laws, patrician of Aachen, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1494.

*Poem of Adam Werner of Themar on most holy Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot*

We compose praises of the renowned grandmother; dear Jesus, grandson, move the plectra of the heart; and you, daughter, chaste mother, do not be absent from the singing choir.

Anne, offspring of kings, long remains barren to her husband; she laments that she is unfruitful, and with weeping prays for offspring; nor do her tears flow in vain.

Heavenly messages promise a child; pregnant, joined to her husband, then as a mother she is proved by the child born, through whom our salvation came.

Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green; a fruitful rod comes forth with fertile shoot, by whose flower, restored, we live again from death to life.

By this birth Joachim becomes worthy to present himself in the shrines, removing the reproaches of the high priest. Thus, Anne, prepare us to carry pure prayers to your grandson.

We ask in prayer that you commend us to the boy whom pious chastity, as mother, brought forth. O blessed matron, Christ denies you nothing.

To Christ, who reigns in fellowship with the Father and the Spirit, be glory through all ages; your prayers, O Anne, make him reconciled to us. Amen.

This hymn is sung to the melody of that hymn which, on the Nativity of Saint Mary, is commonly used in many churches, and begins, “Rejoice in your innermost womb, mother,” etc. From the castle of Heidelberg, on the third day before the Kalends of February, 1493.

*Poem or hymn of Johannes Herbsluterburgensis on Saint Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot*

Rejoice, Anne, refuge for the wretched. We pray, we sing, we bring forth songs. On the feast, be gracious with your offspring; now we speak praises to you.

Rejoice, shining earth, fertile with grass and wet with divine dew rained down: a virgin unknowing of crime.

Rejoice, you who were of royal seed; already given to a husband, you had led a renowned marriage, long lacking offspring, made a mother by your vow.

Rejoice, you who, glad when Joachim met you, were overcome; you call upon God with tears. Heard, you receive your husband as he meets you beneath the gate called Golden. She is conceived in the womb.

Rejoice, simplicity wise by angelic [unclear], you who, praised by the choir and divided threefold, distribute your goods to the temple and the poor, and a part to your servants.

Rejoice: the people of the faithful celebrate the feasts, that you, Anne, as patroness for them, may by your prayers render the Spirit with the Father and Christ the begotten propitious. Amen.

This hymn takes the melody of that which is sung for the Ascension of the Lord, namely “Now the celebrated feast,” etc., and of that hymn on the martyrs, “The glorious merits of the saints,” etc. From Heidelberg, on the third day before the Nones of February, in the year 1493.

*Elegiac poem of Rutger of Venray, regular canon in Heyna, of the diocese of Worms, on Saint Anne*

Hail, splendid mother of the Virgin Mother of the Father’s splendor, of the brightness of undefiled light.

You, holy one, bore for the Lord the divine maiden, from whom Jesus, the kindly light of the world, came forth.

You, steadfastly address [your daughter’s?] child, your grandson, although he himself made the heavens and the earth.

Your grandson himself, Jesus, in the flesh joined highest deity to flesh, for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always existed, nevertheless he desires and deigns to be called your grandson.

Do not hesitate to strike such a grandson with prayers, Anne; we ask it, for you will bring all things that are asked.

If he does not bend ready ears to the first requests, Anne, hasten back to your daughter.

Let your daughter show her breasts to her son Christ; at once the kindly mother will bring what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to obtain by prayer, let the daughter seek aid together with her mother.

My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that the Lord will at once hear both as they pray, with loving ear.

Wherefore, brothers, one ought continually to seek aid by the prayers of Anne and Mary together.

It is certain: if you honor the mother of the Lord’s mother, [unclear] you yourself cherish the titles of the Virgin Mother.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it becomes clear that you prepare praises for the Lord in his mother.

May praise, power, and honor be to the Son of Anne’s daughter, and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
