Hail, Anne, [unclear], whom the world does not contain; may God himself willingly enter the chaste womb. Hail.

Anne, at last from your blood there will arise the blood which, when poured out, will bring hope continually to wretched guilty men. Hail.

Anne, so great an expectation befitted your childbirth. Hail. It is no easy thing to be accounted the mother of the mother of God. Hail.

Anne, the praises of your offspring will fly above the stars. Hail. All ages will call you [unclear] blessed. Hail.

Our Father. [unclear] of God.

Hail, Anne, you are believed to have conceived Mary pure from evil, and soon to have held her holy for God. Hail.

Anne, you bear another than the figures signified. Hail. The one to come, whom the holy fathers sang of. Hail.

Anne, you bear enclosed paradise, the tree of life. Hail. Blessed, and sacred with every honor of the field. Hail.

Anne, heavily you bear the birth which the flaming sword guards. Hail. Burning day and night, [of the cherub?]. Hail.

Anne, you bear the flower that surpasses white lilies. Hail. And lowly violets and the scarlet rose. Hail.

Anne, in you the Mosaic ark is discerned. Hail. And the golden urn of heavenly bread lies there. Hail.

Anne, you bear the bride of the true Solomon, and the house adorned within and without with every jewel. Hail.

Anne, you bear the temple whose inner chambers sweet sounds have filled, and every rejoicing music. Hail.

Anne, you bear the veil under a beautiful image, holding in itself the deeds of the fathers, which may cover the high things of God. Hail.

Anne, you bear the offspring whom golden flame of Phoebus clothes. Hail. The bright moon clothes her snow-white feet. Hail.

Our Father. [unclear] of God.

Hail. Anne, now made possessor of the longed-for Mary, Hail. You scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have arisen. Hail.

Anne, all the stars applauded Mary newly born. Hail. And an unaccustomed radiance flowed from the pole. Hail.

Anne, it is right to believe that at the birthday of your child, Hail, angelic choirs celebrated around you. Hail.

Anne, the depths below felt your childbirth. Hail. Then the new light itself terrified the Stygian pools. Hail.

Anne, as a mother you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands. Hail. She is more ruddy than the sun and the lights of the heaven. Hail.

Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl? Hail. She smiles upon her mother; she causes no sad things. The gods.

Anne, take courage; see, your daughter stretches to your milky breasts, Hail, and seeks your tender hands and your neck. Hail.

Anne, you break off delays, and baring your breasts and chest, Hail, you receive her tender limbs into your tender bosom. Hail.

Anne, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast she draws sweet juices. Hail. From her rosy mouth she makes pleasing murmurs as she sounds them. Hail.

Anne, if you knew how many sorrows your daughter will bear, Hail, and how many deaths she will bear, you would now be no one. Hail.

Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, it is dear to your heart to adorn your daughter with approved morals, Hail, and to compose her for the highest God. Hail.

Anne, you prepare to bring your daughter into the lofty shrine, Hail, but of her own will the gracious one reached the step of the heavens. Hail.

Anne, with slow step and labor you follow the swift one. Hail. The ready little virgin consoles her weary mother. Hail.

Anne, why do you marvel at strength in a tender virgin? Hail. You see not human things, but pious signs of God. Hail.

Anne, you are accustomed to give gifts to the needy and to sacred temples. Hail. Now you give that than which no humble woman has given more. Hail.

Anne, when you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell, Hail, she gladly entered the cloisters and the virginal house. Hail.

Anne, about to send away the pious one, you wear out your cheeks and eyes with weeping. Hail. A deep love holds you unmoved. Hail.

Anne, return home; leave your daughter to the Thunderer. Hail. Let her think of nothing except the Jesus who is to come. Hail.

Anne, may your daughter fly through the ethereal retreats. Hail. May love sent from the highest summit occupy her. Hail.

Anne, it is enough for you to serve two Marys. Hail. These also will honor you with no small devotion. Hail.

Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, by your own weeping you have learned to succor one who weeps. Hail. Hence you too readily favor the wretched with their prayers. Hail.

Anne, you are accustomed to overturn the fixed laws of nature. Hail. And so you will wish it: let the barren woman now be a joyful mother. Hail.

Anne, you also stay the gifts of flowing fortune. Hail. Lest they flee from our [unclear], having suddenly slipped away. Hail.

Anne, you drive away disgrace; you give the gifts of fair fame. Hail. You [unclear] mourning, and love yields to you. Hail.

Anne, you help, whether hostile force terrifies someone, or whether dark prisons hold [unclear] bound. Hail.

Anne, be present as relief from the stars for sick mortals. Hail. With you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing. Hail.
