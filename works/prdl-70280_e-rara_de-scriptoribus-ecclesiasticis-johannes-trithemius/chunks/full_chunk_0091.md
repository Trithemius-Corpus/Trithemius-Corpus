*On the day of the Nativity of the Lord, the eighth day before the Kalends of January.*

Chrodegangus, bishop of Metz, nephew of King Pippin through his sister, ennobling the nobility of his birth by holy conduct and zeal for the Scriptures, an outstanding venerator of the bodies of the holy martyrs, wishing to instruct the clergy and people committed to him in justice by word and example, wrote for the clergy of the church of Metz a sufficiently instructive and useful *Rule of Canons*, 1 book, and certain other things.

He transferred the bodies of the holy martyrs Gorgonius, Nabor, and Nazarius from Rome into Francia; and the body of Saint Nazarius was laid up in the monastery of Lorsch, then of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Worms.

He flourished under King Pippin, father of the emperor Charles, in the year of the Lord 760.

Leo III, Roman by nation, sat as pope after the pontiff Adrian for twenty years: a man very learned in both divine and secular writings, and also most renowned for the holiness of his character and life. He anointed Charles, great in name and in deed, to the empire of the Romans, and transferred this dignity from the Greeks to the Franks. Among other things he wrote letters to various persons, 1 book.

He died under Charles on the Ides of June, in the year of the Lord 816, indiction 9.

Aimo, the third bishop of the church of Halberstadt in Saxony, formerly abbot of the monastery of Hersfeld, of the order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, once a hearer of Alcuin the monk, for whom Rabanus, then abbot of Fulda, composed twenty-two books *On the Universe*, was certainly a very learned man from every point of view, a most acute interpreter of the divine Scriptures, and of excellent talent in delivering homilies to the people.

He was first a monk of the monastery of Fulda with Rabanus; afterward he was made the third abbot of Hersfeld; finally he was consecrated third bishop of Halberstadt. He governed the same church vigorously by word and example for thirteen years. He wrote almost countless little works; of these, the following which we have placed below are reported:

On Ruth, 1 book.

On Ezra and Nehemiah, 1 book.

On Job, 1 book.

On the Song of Songs, 1 book.

On Isaiah the prophet, 4 books. Incipit: “The prophet Isaiah, of noble [unclear].”

On Jeremiah, 1 book.

On Daniel, 1 book.

On the twelve minor prophets, 14 books. Incipit: “The word which was made unto.”

Also on the Psalms, 1 book.

On the Gospels, homilies, 2 books. Incipit: “The Lord, consulting human [unclear].”

On the variety of books, 4 books. Incipit: “Since there is one and most holy [unclear].”

On the Holy Trinity, 1 book.
