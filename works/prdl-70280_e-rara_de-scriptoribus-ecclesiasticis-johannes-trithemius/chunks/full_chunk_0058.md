He flourished under the rulers Theodosius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Orosius the priest, a Spaniard by nation, formerly a hearer of blessed Augustine, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most skilled in secular letters, and very distinguished in life and conduct. Blessed Augustine, when he came to him from Spain to learn the question concerning the nature of the soul, sent him to Saint Jerome in Bethlehem of Judea; after staying there a little while, he learned clearly the question for which he had come, and, returning to Hippo, brought with him relics of the protomartyr Stephen. To this most studious man there are extant several little works of blessed Augustine and solutions of various questions of the Scriptures. He himself also wrote the following volumes:

An Apologetic work.

Against the Pelagians: one book. “I have obeyed your commands, blessed...”

On the honorable chronicle of the world: seven books. “Since among you, heavenly...”

On the nature of the soul: one book.

Several letters to Augustine: one book.

Also letters to various persons: one book.

On the Song of Songs: one book.

He flourished, illustrious in life and doctrine, together with Saint Augustine, in the year of the Lord 420.

John, bishop and patriarch of Jerusalem, studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, but too much devoted to the doctrines of Origen, stirred up writings of the fathers against himself, namely of the most holy priest Jerome and of Bishop Epiphanius, as is found in the letters sent by them to him. But John himself, giving an account of his study, says that he imitated not the faith of Origen but his genius. For on this account especially he wrote a work worthy of note:

Against the detractors of his study: one book.

And certain letters to various persons.

And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 400.

Maximus, bishop of Turin, most learned in the holy Scriptures and second to none in his time in delivering homilies to the people, was not only distinguished in doctrine, but also shone most brightly in holiness of life. He wrote many excellent little works, which holy mother Church has accepted with great authority to be read. Of these the following are reported:
