[unclear]: a very celebrated theologian, philosopher, orator, and most outstanding poet of all; first at Erfurt and then in the school at Heidelberg, publicly lecturing on philosophy and sacred Scripture, he acquired great renown for his learning. A man learned in every respect, he is subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, excelling in both verse and prose. He wrote in both kinds some distinguished little works, by which he spread his name far and wide. Of these the following are extant. To Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, he composed a very celebrated work, most elegantly in hexameter and pentameter verse, which he entitled *On the Threefold Brightness*:

*In Praise of the Divine Mary*, one book.
*Precepts of Rhetoric*, one book.
*Praises of the Church of Speyer*, one book.
*On the Holy Spirit*, an oration, one book.
*To the King of France*, an oration, one book.
*On the Art of Poetry*, one book.
*The Greatest Hope of Our [unclear]*.
*I Do Not Know If Your [unclear], Theoderic*.
*How [unclear] Expect*.
*Now Eight Lustra to Be Enjoyed*.
Many poems of various kinds.

He composed many elegant letters, both to me and to others, the number of which is uncertain. At the request of Ludwig, bishop of Speyer, he composed the office on the compassion of the blessed Mary ever virgin, which is solemnly performed every year throughout that same diocese. To this Jacobus we have already long since dedicated the book *On the Luminaries, or Illustrious Men, of Germany*. He lives, in manly age, being about forty-three years old, in the renowned city of Speyer; he writes various works under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1493.

Baptista de Salis, of the Order of Friars Minor of the province of Genoa, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed both in each law and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse, devout in life and conduct, outstanding in observance of regular discipline: he published certain little works necessary for the instruction of simple priests, by which he spread his name far and wide. Of these there is extant a very useful summa on cases in the forum of conscience, which, after the author’s name, is called:

*The Baptistinian Summa*, one book.
And certain other works.

He completed that same summa under Frederick, the third emperor, and he is still living while his son Maximilian holds the kingdom, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Engelhard Funck of Schwabach, an East Franconian, German by nation, a learned man skilled in every respect, a philosopher, distinguished orator and poet, not ignorant of divine Scripture and pontifical law, outstanding in intellect, eloquent in speech, excelling in both verse and prose: he is said to have fashioned certain little works in both kinds of style, by which he made his name known. In elegiac verse:

*On the Praise of His Fatherland*, one book.
*On a Ring Given to Him*, one book.
Several letters to various people.
Various epigrams.
And many other works.

He is flourishing at Rome, as an agent or advocate of cases; he composes various works under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1493.
