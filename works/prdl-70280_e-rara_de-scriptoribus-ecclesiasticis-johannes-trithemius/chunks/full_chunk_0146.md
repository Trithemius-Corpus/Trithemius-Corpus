Upertus of Rusia, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, since indeed he taught theology excellently for many years and left after him many distinguished disciples. He wrote some excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Among these there is extant, addressed to Haymo the Englishman, minister general of his order, likewise a most learned man, a declaration
On the rule of Saint Francis: one book.
Also on the Sentences: one book.
Various sermons: one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Ulricus of Strasbourg, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, once a hearer and disciple of Albert the Great, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, composed little works of reading not to be despised, by which he became known both to present and to future generations. Among these there are extant:
On the Sentences: one book.
A Summa of theology: one book.
Also On the Soul: one book.
And many other things.
He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

Haymo, an Englishman by nation, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, a man trained by long study in the holy Scriptures, since, having obtained the magisterial chair of the theological faculty, he instructed many disciples in it; at the command of Pope Alexander IV he corrected and rubricated the Roman Breviary. He is said, among other things, to have written concisely:
On the Sentences: one book.
And certain sermons: one book.
And certain other things.

Otto, bishop of Freising in Bavaria in Germany, and, as some write, nephew of the emperor Frederick I, a German by nation, whom Pope Pius II, citing him in a certain oration, commends; a man learned in the divine Scriptures and by no means poorly taught in secular letters, subtle in talent and clear in eloquence. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is extant an outstanding work, to be honored with every praise:
Of Histories: one book.

Petrus Hispanus, a Portuguese, bishop of Tusculum and cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, and finally supreme pontiff after Hadrian V, a man very learned in medicines and magnificently taught in secular philosophy, sat in the pontificate only eight months. He wrote little works in medicine not to be despised; among them the following are reported:
The Treasury of the Poor: one book.
On Problems: one book.
Canons of Medicine: one book.
Letters also to various persons: one book.
And certain other things.
