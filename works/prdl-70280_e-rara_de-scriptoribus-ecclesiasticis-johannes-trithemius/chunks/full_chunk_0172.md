He flourished around the times of Louis the Fourth, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Johannes Andreas, an Italian by nation, a native of Bologna, a jurist most celebrated to the admiration of all, a fountain of canons and a most cautious interpreter of doubts, distinguished in talent, profound in knowledge, orderly in speech, who, contrary to the custom of men of our time, although he was bound by the ties of a wife, nevertheless devoted incredible zeal to letters, so that nothing could have been sweeter to him than to write something for the edification of posterity, as his distinguished volumes bear witness; by them he won eternal memory for his name. Of these I have been able to see a few:

On the Decretals: five books.
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals: one book.
On the Novellae, in two forms: three books.
Additions to the Speculum: one book.
The prefatory Mercuriale: one book.
A Summa on betrothal and marriage: one book.
On the Clementines: one book.
On the praises of Saint Jerome: three books.
On the tree of consanguinity: one book.
Of the rest I have seen nothing.

At length he died of the plague and was buried at Bologna, under Charles the Fourth, emperor, and Pope Clement the Sixth, in the year of the Lord 1348, in the first indiction.

Cinus of Pistoia, born of the noble family of the Symbaldi, formerly hearer and disciple of the most distinguished man Dino of Mugello, and a close friend of Johannes Andreas, certainly the most learned of all in civil law in his time, subtle in talent, ready and very abundant in knowledge, published many little works in that faculty, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code: nine books.
On the first part of the Old Digest: one book.
On the second part: one book.
Additions on the remaining parts: one book.
And many other things.

He flourished under Louis the Fourth, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Monaldus, of the Order of Friars Minor, a theologian and most learned jurist, subtle in talent, cautious in knowledge, scholastic in speech, a man of outstanding industry in delivering sermons to the people, published, for the benefit of the simple, little works not to be scorned even by a doctor; through them he usefully provided both for those then present and for those who would come long afterward. Of these the following are reported, necessary and very fine:

A Summa of canon law: one book. “Since one who is ignorant [unclear].”
Questions on the Sentences: three books.
Various sermons: one book.
And very many other things, which have by no means come to our knowledge.

Oldradus de Laude, an Italian by nation, formerly a hearer of the very learned man Dino of Mugello, a very close friend of Johannes Andreas, a man highly skilled in both laws and also excellently imbued with the disciplines of philosophy, who for many years was a consistorial advocate, acquired great praise and glory for his learning. He wrote several distinguished little works in both laws, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

A volume of counsels: one book. “In the name of the Lord, amen. In the case.”
Of the rest I have seen nothing.
