Serapion, formerly an anchorite, eighth bishop of the church of Antioch, a man of keen intelligence, is said to have been very zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, venerable no less in life than in knowledge. He is reported to have composed several little works for the benefit of believers, in which he shows himself a learned man and a very vigorous defender of the Christian faith. Among these are counted:

*To Domno, Judaizing*, one book.

*To Carinus, on Montanus*, one book.

*On the Gospel under the Name of Peter*, one book.

*Short Letters, but Many*, one book.

He was ordained bishop of the aforesaid city under Commodus, emperor of the Romans, in the 193rd year of the Lord, in the first indiction.

Apollonius, senator of the city of Rome, a man most learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, venerable no less for holiness than for knowledge, when he had been denounced before the Senate because he was a Christian and would not deny Christ, received sentence of death; the old law among them prevailing, that Christians who had once been brought before their judgment should not be released without denial.

He wrote, among other things:

*An Apology for Our Faith*, one book.

After he had recited this in order before the Senate, he was beheaded.

He flourished at Rome under the emperors Commodus and Severus, in the year of the Lord 190.

Tertullian, priest, by homeland a Carthaginian of the province of Africa, the son of a centurion, a proconsular man, a man of sharp and vehement intelligence, most learned both in divine and in secular writings, inasmuch as he taught rhetoric gloriously for many years at Carthage, wrote in the Latin language almost innumerable little works, in which he powerfully crushed the errors of the heretics. The most blessed martyr Cyprian held this man’s writings in such veneration that he let no day pass without reading them. For he was accustomed to say to his secretary, “Give me the master,” meaning Tertullian. At last he is said to have slipped into the error of Montanus, on account of which his little works are counted among the apocrypha, Distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*.
