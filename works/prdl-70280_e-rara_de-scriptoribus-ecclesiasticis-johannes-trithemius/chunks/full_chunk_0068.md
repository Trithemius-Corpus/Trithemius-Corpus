*lector*

[Unclear] an excellent declaimer of sermons, and a notable persecutor of heretics, is said to have compiled many volumes, in which he instructed the devout people and strongly refuted those erring concerning the faith. Among these the following are reported:

Against the Arians: one book.
Against the Donatists: one book.
A consolatory work to Basilius: one book.
On penance also: one book.
Homilies to the people: two books.
He also published not a few other works, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished under Marcian Augustus, in the year of the Lord 550.

*ectorinus*

Victorinus, an Aquitanian by nation, a man very devoted to the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, is said to have left certain little works of his own talent, which have as yet by no means come to my knowledge. At the command of Pope Hilarius he wrote, for finding the term of Easter:

The great cycle of 532 years: one book.

This is called the great cycle of 532 years; when these have passed, all computations of the luminaries return to the same point. Dionysius the abbot afterward corrected it, and down to this our age, although it has failed in some respects, nevertheless it has endured. This Victorinus also wrote certain letters. He flourished under Emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460, and wrote his cycle under Pope Hilarius, in the third year of his pontificate.

*p*

Petrus, presbyter of the church of Edessa, practiced in the holy Scriptures, and endowed with a most excellent natural ability for declaiming homilies to the people, so that in his own times he had no equal. He wrote in the Syriac language many distinguished volumes, of which only very few titles have come to my knowledge:

Commentaries on the Psalms: one book.
He also played with the Psalms in meter: one book.

He dictated and delivered homilies to the people, almost infinitely many, which are rarely found among us. But neither is the number of his treatises certain to us.
He flourished under Emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

*Abatius*

Abatius, bishop of the Gallican province, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, subtle and keen in intellect, often fought against the madness of heretics, both by writing and by disputing. Hence, having been asked by a certain Secunda, a virgin dedicated to Christ, he wrote a distinguished and memorable volume:

Against Marcion and Valentinus: one book.
Against Eunomius and Aetius: one book.
He also published some other works, but they have not yet come to my knowledge.
