Abbo, abbot of the monastery of Fleury, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man exercised in the divine Scriptures by continual reading and study, and excellently learned in secular disciplines, notable also in life and conduct, and proved worthy even of the crown of martyrdom: while he was preaching the word of God in Gascony, he became a witness of the Christian faith by the shedding of his blood. He wrote:

On the calculation of Victorius, one book.

Additions to the same, one book.

And certain other things. He flourished in the times of Otto I, in the year of the Lord 970.

Robert, king of France, noble, brave, just, learned, and religious, deservedly receives a place among ecclesiastical writers, since he composed chants and responsories with sweet and regular melody for the honor of the catholic Church. He was most learned in humane letters, especially in music; and he was of such religion that he sang the canonical hours in church with the clerics, and outside, at home, read them with his own people. He was of such piety and compassion toward the poor that, sometimes stripping himself of his own garments, he covered their nakedness many times. A book never left his hands, so that, sitting on the tribunal, amid the noise of judgments and lawsuits, he had an open psalter in his lap. Hence he is also read to have shone with miracles after death.

His is that well-known sequence which is sung on the days of Pentecost:

On the Holy Spirit, namely, “Come, Holy Spirit, and send forth.”

A responsory on Saint Peter, namely, “Cornelius the centurion, a man,” one.

On the nativity of the Lord, namely, “Judea and Jerusalem, do not fear.”

And that Alleluia, namely, “Deliver me from my enemies.”

And many other things which I do not know.

The responsory “Cornelius the Centurion”: at Rome, while the supreme pontiff was celebrating Mass, he offered it with great devotion to Saint Peter at the altar at the time of the offertory. But the ministers of the altar, running up, thought that the wealthy king had offered gold of great weight; but, finding a written and noted sheet, they marveled, praising the talent and devotion of the prince. Hence the pope decreed, at the request of the clerics, in memory of the most holy king, that this responsory should thereafter be sung in honor of Saint Peter.

He flourished under Otto II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 980.

Aimoinus, monk of the monastery of Fleury, of the Order of Saint Benedict, French by nation, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, is reported to have composed some little works both in meter and in prose, but they have by no means come into our hands. After the monk Adrevald he wrote one volume:

On the miracles of Saint Benedict, one book.

He flourished under Otto III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 990.

Heriger, abbot of the monastery of Lobbes, of the Order of Saint Benedict, most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly learned in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, whom Abbot Berno of Reichenau mentions in a certain book to Aribo, archbishop of Mainz, wrote a distinguished dialogue under the person of himself and of Adelbold, then a cleric and monk living at Rome, afterward bishop of Utrecht, concerning whom we must speak immediately, entitled:

On the discordance of the Church, one book.

The Life of Saint Ursmar in meter, one book.

To Hugh, On Questions, one book.

On the Divine Offices, one book.
