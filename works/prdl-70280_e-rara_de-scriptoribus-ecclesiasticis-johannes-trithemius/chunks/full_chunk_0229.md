Johannes Ernesti, German by nation, once rector and ornament of the University of Heidelberg, learned in the holy Scriptures, rich in his reading of the ancients, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech with singular charm, an outstanding disputant of questions, wrote, as they say, certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Disputed Questions, one book.
Many Sermons, one book.
On the Nativity of the Lord, an oration, one book: “And if of speaking concerning the greatest [unclear].”
On Fasting, to the University, one book: “Most sacred [unclear] desires.”
Many Various Orations, one book.
On the Authority of the Council, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, at the beginning of the reign of Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Bartholomaeus, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Bethlehem in the town of Roermond in Guelders, German by nation, acute in sacred letters, a devout and celebrated interpreter, wrote for the edification of his brothers certain distinguished little works, by which he magnificently exercised his intellect and left to posterity the memory of his name. Of these the following are reported:

Exercise of Devotion, one book.
Summary of Vices, one book.
On Abstinence from Meat, one book.
On the Proclamations of the Brothers, one book.
On Rash Judgments, one book.
On the Oath, one book.
On the Vow also, one book.
And certain other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1046, in the ninth indiction, on the third day before the Ides of July.

Heinricus de Werlis, German by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, minister of the province of Cologne, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, excellent in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, an acute disputant, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, composed certain lucid volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name. Among these there is an outstanding work:

On the Sentences, four books.
On Ecclesiastical Power, one book.
Various Sermons also, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1490.
