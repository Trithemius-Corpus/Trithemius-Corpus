Anatolius of Alexandria, bishop of Laodicea in Syria, a man skilled in the divine Scriptures and, in secular letters, second to none in his time, an outstanding rhetorician, astronomer, and geometer, left many distinguished little works of his genius; yet very few of them have come to our knowledge. There is extant his notable volume entitled:

On Easter: ten books.
On instruction in arithmetic: one book.
He flourished under the emperors Probus and Carus, in the year of the Lord 285.

Archelaus, bishop of Mesopotamia in Syria, a learned man and acute disputant, wrote some little works in the Syriac tongue, which are rarely found among the Latins. He disputed vigorously against Mani, the heresiarch, coming out of Persia, and confuted all his arguments, although he could not recall him from error. He wrote that same disputation:

Against Mani: one book.
The other works are unknown to us.
He flourished under the emperors Aurelian and Probus, in the year of the Lord 280.

Lucian, priest of the church of Antioch, afterward bishop of Nicomedia, a learned and eloquent man, was of excellent talent in delivering homilies to the people, and venerable no less for his life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures. Under the persecution of Maximian he was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ, and was buried at Helenopolis in Bithynia. He wrote, among other things:

On Our Faith: three books.
And various other treatises, sermons, homilies, and letters.
He flourished under the emperor Maximian, in the year of the Lord 240.
