Thilmannus, of the order of regular canons of Saint Augustine, provost of the monastery of the divine martyr Christopher in Ravengiersburg, at the edge of the diocese of Mainz, beside the Hercynian Forest, which extends its tail from the river Rhine toward the Mediomatrici and the Treveri, in a place that from antiquity is called the Retreat of the Huns; a German by nation, zealous in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct. He wrote, in a simple and plain style, for the edification of the brothers, certain little works full of devotion and charity, in which he exercised his talent. Of these the following are extant:

On the Spiritual Vineyard, one book. “I should wonder, dearest...”

On the Instruction of Novices, one book. “Having entered, dearest, upon the warfare...”

And certain small exercises.

He died an old man and full of days, under Frederick, the third emperor, and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1485, indiction 3.

[Henricus?] Arnoldi, a Saxon by nation, by profession a Carthusian monk of the house of Basel; a man devout in religion, distinguished in talent, sincere in affection, grave in manners, sweet in speech, affable in conduct, pious, gentle, shining by example, very much practiced in sacred letters, and not ignorant of pontifical law. When he was performing the office of notary in the holy Council of Basel for many years, he was approved by the testimony of all as faithful, just, and prudent. At length, seeing that all things are vanity, he despised everything for the love of Christ, choosing and entering the enclosure of the Carthusians; there, appointed prior of the same house, he ruled laudably in peace and tranquility for many years the monks subject to him, the lay brothers, and the others, pursuing them with paternal love. He wrote many little works, very devout and salutary. Of these, those that have come to our knowledge are set down below:

Many devout prayers concerning various saints, one book. “The following prayers...”

Several sermons, one book.

Histories concerning various saints, one book.

A Concordance of the Evangelists on the Passion of the Lord, one book.

Meditations on the Life of Christ, one treatise.

On the Instruction of Novices, one treatise.

A Dialogue between Jesus and Mary, one treatise.

A Dialogue on Exercises, that is, between a saint and his little servant, one treatise.

Another dialogue between the same persons on the cross, one treatise.

On the Commendation of Charity, one treatise. “The excellence of charity...”

On Holy Humility, in the form of a prayer, one treatise. “Of perfect humility...”

Again on humility, one treatise. “Dearest brothers, humble...”

A Chronicle of the Carthusian House in Basel, one treatise.

He happily departed from this life under Frederick III, emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1487.

Jodocus of Calw, a German by nation, a Swabian by homeland; a man practiced by long study in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of scholastic philosophy; prompt and vigorous in talent. In the Heidelberg school he was held in esteem for many years by reading, teaching, and preaching; there also, according to custom, he for some time carried out the office of preacher in the church of the Holy Spirit. He wrote, as they say, certain treatises not to be rejected, but they have not come to the knowledge of our reading. There are extant by him, on various matters:

Various sermons, one book.

A Vocabulary for Preachers, one book.

And various treatises.
