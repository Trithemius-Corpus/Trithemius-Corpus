Joachim, abbot of the monastery of Fiore, of the Order of Saint Benedict, originally from Calabria: a man studious and practiced in the divine Scriptures, who, since he was regarded in his time as a prophet, also attempted to predict future events. But it would seem to me, speaking without prejudice to his own opinion or that of his followers, that he spoke more from conjectures drawn from Scripture than prophetically. For, to pass over the rest for the moment, he dreamed that the emperor Frederick III would be a future enemy of the Church; yet we all know that, right up to his death, he remained peaceful, subject to the Roman pontiff and faithful. He wrote a number of little works in clear language, in almost all of which he was accustomed to mingle, as if future and foreclosed, [unclear].

On Jeremiah the prophet: 1 book.
On Daniel the prophet: 1 book.
On the Gospel of John: 1 book.
On the Apocalypse of John: 8 books.
To Henry VI: 1 book. “You piously ask that something be explained.”
On the Seven Seals: 1 book. “The first synagogical time.”
The Psalter of Ten Strings: 2 books.
The Book of Concord: [unclear] book(s).
On the Fifteen Pontiffs: [unclear] book(s).
On Future Times: [unclear] book(s).
Letters to various persons: [unclear] book(s).

He also published many other works, especially against the Jews and other adversaries of the Catholic faith, which have not come into our hands. But the treatise which he wrote against Peter Lombard, bishop of Paris, was condemned in a general council, as is clear at the beginning of the Decretals, “We condemn.” He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Rui, of the fathers of Benedict, a Frenchman by nationality, a man practiced by constant study in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular disciplines, distinguished in talent and clear in eloquence, wrote several little works of his own genius, by which his name became known to posterity. Of these I have seen only the notable History of the deeds done in the Holy Land by Christians in the time of Urban II against the infidels: [unclear] book(s). “Among all the historiographers.”

I have seen nothing of the others. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Osuida, a noble woman born in Saxony, a nun in the monastery of Gandersheim, which is not far from Hildesheim, was distinguished by wondrous talent and learning. For the sacred virgins, exhorting them to chastity and continence, she wrote in Latin:

Six comedies. “The one hope of the world,” etc.
A panegyric on the deeds of the Ottos, in hexameter verse.
Also the Passion of Saint Dionysius in elegiac verse.
Also, on Saint Gangolf and other saints, several works.

She also had knowledge of the Greek language. She was a contemporary of John the Englishman, who by her learning deserved the papacy.

Dudechinus, priest of the church in Langenstein, a German by nationality, a man learned in the holy Scriptures through long practice and sufficiently instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, for the hope of eternal reward set out with many others under the emperor Conrad III for Jerusalem. In frequent naval battles with the Saracens, he shone forth with great devotion and constancy for the faith of Christ. After he returned to his homeland, at the request of Cuno, abbot of Mount Saint Disibod, then of our order, he wrote, in sufficiently elegant discourse and in the form of an epistle or little book, the History of that same pilgrimage: [unclear] book(s).
Letters to various persons: [unclear] book(s).
And certain other works. He flourished under the emperor Conrad III, in the year of the Lord 1140.

Rupert, bishop of Lincoln, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and, in the disciplines of secular letters, the most skilled of all the learned men of his time; a philosopher, astronomer, and notable calculator; easily the foremost theologian of his time; subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, transmitted his name with glory to posterity by writing many and various works. Of his little works few have come into our hands. The following, however, are reported:
