Jacobus Balduini, formerly a pupil of Odofredus Beneventanus, a most distinguished man, a professor of civil law and an outstanding interpreter, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, eminent in counsel and action, wrote in that faculty certain excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code: twelve books.
On the Old Digest: twenty-four books.
On the New Digest: twelve books.
On the Infortiatum: thirteen books.
And certain other works. He flourished under Frederick, the second emperor of that name, in the year of the Lord 1240.

David, of the Order, as they say, of the Minors, German by nation, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in life and conduct, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain little works in plain style, whose reading is not to be despised by devout brothers. Of these the following are extant. To novices:

On the ordering of the outer man: one book. Religious progress, [seven?] [unclear].
On the ordering of the inner man: one book. My conferences which [unclear].
Sermons on the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
And certain other works. He flourished not long after blessed Francis. He is buried at Augsburg.

Humbertus, of the Order of Preachers the fifth master general, from Vienne, a learned and excellent declaimer, evangelical in life, pure in conscience, eminent in action, sweet in eloquence and fit for persuasion, drew Hugo, once his teacher in sacred theology, to the Order. He wrote certain devout little works, by which his name came to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Rule of Saint Augustine: one book. When I was thinking about the sermon.
Summa for preachers: three books. Every man is to be exhorted.
Deeds of the brothers of the Order of Preachers: five books.
On the three substantial elements of religious life: one book. To the sons of grace, co-heirs.
Letters to various persons: one book.
And certain other works.
He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1250.

Vincentius, a Gaul, Burgundian by nation, of the Order of Preachers, called Belvacensis, a man studious and practiced in the divine Scriptures and rich in the reading of the ancients, subtle in intellect, composed in speech, applied himself so much to the study of letters that he could never be called away from their cultivation by any labors, vigils, or occupations, without always either reading, writing, or preaching. He was therefore so great that after him he has not yet had an equal, if we weigh with an even balance the arduous little works which he wrote, gathered together with the others. For, at the request and with the expense supplied by the king of the Franks, he brought together with very great labors, in many very large volumes, the great and admirable work of the Mirrors concerning every, so to speak, knowable thing; and he entitled and divided it as follows:

The Historical Mirror: thirty-two books. God is an incorporeal substance.
The Natural Mirror: thirty-four books. Since the multitude of books.
The Moral Mirror: three books. In all your affairs remember.
The Doctrinal Mirror: thirteen books. Since the multitude of books.
Also On the grace of God: eight books. The book of the generation [unclear].
On the praises of Saint Mary: one book. If with prophetic mouth God commands.
