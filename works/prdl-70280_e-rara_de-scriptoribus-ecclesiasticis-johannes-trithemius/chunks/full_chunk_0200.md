Jacobus, who is called the Great, by country a Toledan, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished for his knowledge of natural philosophy, and rich in reading of the ancients. He is said to have issued various treatises and commentaries on the divine books, which have not yet come into our hands. I found one excellent, notable volume of his, entitled On the discourse of the inquiry of wisdom:

Sophilogium: book.

Of the others I have seen nothing.

Johannes Campscen[us?], of the order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, an Englishman by nation, long exercised in the holy Scriptures by study, rich in talent through continual reading of the ancients, an excellent preacher of sermons. He is said to have forged certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these, the following are reported to be extant and very notable:

Sermons on the Temporale: book.
Sermons on the Saints: book.

But I could not find the titles of the other works which he composed.

Thomas Gualensis, of the order of the Friars Preachers, an Englishman by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, very studious, and also nobly learned in secular letters, especially in scholastic philosophy; subtle in talent, clear in eloquence; likewise an excellent preacher of sermons, an acute disputant in scholastic lectures. He wrote many distinguished little works on the holy Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Yet of these few have come into our hands. For I found only the following:

On the whole Psalter: book.
On the Proverbs of Solomon: book.
On Augustine’s City of God: book.
On the Song of Songs: book.

He flourished in the University of Oxford, where he is said to have composed many volumes, especially various lectures on several books of the Bible.

Philippus, abbot of the monastery of Otterburg, in the diocese of Worms, of the Cistercian order, a German by nation, a man studious in the divine Scriptures, learned by long exercise, not ignorant of secular literature. For the edification of readers he issued volumes not to be despised; by them, exercising his talent properly, he also left to posterity the memory of his name. Among these the following are reported. A distinguished, notable work:

Sermons on the Temporale: book.
Sermons on the Saints: book.
Book of letters to various persons.

If he issued anything further, it has as yet by no means come to my knowledge.
