He wrote many distinguished volumes, both in verse and in prose, by which he commended his name to posterity: On the metrical contest of Robert Gaguin concerning the most pure conception of the holy Virgin Mary, Mother of God, against Vincent de Castronovo of the Order of Preachers, a most elegant work, Commentaries, one book, “We do not doubt from the Apostle.” Praises of the Order of Carmelites, one book, “While the Parthian turns his weapons.” Odes in praise of Christ, three books, “How much you surpass created things.” On the Conception against Vincent, one book. An iambic poem on the same, one book. An oration on the Conception to [unclear], one book. Many letters, two books. Almost innumerable poems, one book. Elegies on contempt of the world, one book. And many other things. He is living today, blind but very learned, composing many and varied works in the aforesaid monastery, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, indiction 12.

Johannes Fernandus, brother of the aforesaid Carolus, himself a German by birth, a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher, a very celebrated orator and poet, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech; the king of the French, granting him a perpetual stipend, took him into his service as a musician. He wrote, both in verse and in prose, many useful little works suited to devotion, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of many. Of these the following are extant: On Saint John the Baptist, one book, “With what fitting plectrum.” Many various orations, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. Several poems and epigrams. And many other things. He also wrote the Hours of the Holy Cross and of the Compassion of holy Mary the Virgin in elegant verse, which, recently printed at Paris, are in circulation. He is living to this day with the king of France in the highest esteem, and composes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495.

Hieronymus Donatus, a Venetian patrician, a very distinguished man, devoted to the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a very celebrated translator in both languages, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech. Although care for his wife and children now distracts him, nevertheless with marvelous diligence and incredible zeal he burns for letters, writing many things day by day and translating many things from Greek; if someone else, free even from every care, were to compose them, it could be a matter for wonder. Of these there are extant: An oration to the king of Portugal, one book. Various orations also, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. And several other things. He translated into Latin John Damascene on the immaculate mysteries; Dionysius also On the Divine Names; certain letters of the same author; also a volume On the Soul by Alexander of Aphrodisias the philosopher; and many other things. He is living to this day at Venice, thirty-seven years old, writing and translating various works, under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1495.
