*Book*

To the bishops of Egypt and Libya: one book. “All things whatsoever our Lord ...”

On the Holy Spirit: one book. “The letters of your holy charity ...”

Against the Gentiles: one book. “Of divine worship to the whole ...”

Against the Arians: one book. “Heresies, however many he himself ...”

Against the same: one book. “The raging Arians ...”

On the homoousion of the Father and the Son: one book. “Those who wickedly divine ...”

Dialogue with an Arian: one book. “It has been done by the providence of God ...”

Exhortation to the monks: one book. “And if anything to glory in ...”

Life of Saint Antony: one book. “Best in these things, brothers, [unclear] ...”

Symbol of his faith: one book. “Whoever wishes to be saved ...”

On all the epistles of Paul: fourteen books. “Paul. Nor Moses himself ...”

On the persecution of the Arians: two books.

On his flight and exile: one book. He published several letters to various persons, and also various sermons and treatises, which either have not yet been translated into Latin or have not come to my notice. He died in the time of the emperors Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 379, the forty-sixth of his priesthood, on the sixth day before the Nones of May, in the seventh indiction.

Theodorus, bishop of Heraclea of the Thracians, diligent and learned in the divine Scriptures, and an outstanding declaimer, of elegant and well-composed speech, produced commentaries on the sacred volumes, not to be despised for reading. Of these the following are reported:

On the Psalter: one book. On the Gospel of Matthew: one book. On the Gospel of John: one book. On the epistles of Paul: fourteen books. He is also said to have written some other works, but they have not come to my notice. He flourished in the times of Constantine II, in the year of the Lord 340.

Eusebius, bishop of Emesa, a man most learned in the divine writings, second to none in his time in delivering homilies to the people, a rhetorician-theologian most celebrated in everyone’s opinion, is said to have written many, almost innumerable, little works; but we have been able to find only the titles and numbers of a few, namely:

Against the Gentiles: one book. Against the Jews: one book. To the Novatians: one book. On the Epistle to the Galatians: ten books. Of many homilies: one book. Sermons to the people. He flourished when Constantine and his brothers were reigning, in the year of the Lord 350.

Oriphilius, Cypriot, bishop of [Litensis?] or Leucontheon, a man most learned both in divine and in secular writings, and easily the most eloquent of all prelates in his age, is said to have written not a few volumes and to have expounded the divine Scriptures in many ways; but I do not recall that anything of his works has come into our hands. His exposition is reported:

On the Song of Songs: one book. He flourished in the times of the emperor Constantius, in the year of the Lord 301.

Serapion, formerly bishop of [Monuseos?] and a very close friend of the blessed abbot Antony, was a man of such learning and eloquence that, because of the excellence of his talent, he received the surname Scholasticus. He is said to have written many little works. Of these the following are extant:

Against the Manichaean: one book. On the titles of the Psalms: one book.
