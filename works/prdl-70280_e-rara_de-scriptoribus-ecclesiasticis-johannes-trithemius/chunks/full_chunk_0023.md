He also wrote many other things and various treatises, which as yet have by no means come into our hands; and he lived on into old age, zealous in his declining years.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Severus. In the year of the Lord two hundred.

Origen, who is Adamantius, a priest, an Alexandrian by nation, son of the martyr Leonides, once a disciple of Clement of Alexandria, was a man of great and excellent talent, so that in zeal and learning he never had his equal. In the tenth year of Severus Pertinax, when a persecution was set in motion against the Christians, and when his father Leonides was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ, he was left poor with his widowed mother and six brothers, being about seventeen years old. But the treasury had seized all their substance because of the confession of Christ, so that they no longer had anything from which they might live. And although the number of his years was reckoned as smaller in the young man, nevertheless his learning was already marvelous, and the constancy of his Christian faith admirable. For he had resolved to die bravely with his father Leonides for the faith of Christ, had he not, by his mother’s stratagem, since he was to become a great teacher for the usefulness of the Church, happened to be preserved. For when he had determined, on the morning before, to run to his father at the place of contest, his garments were taken away by his mother during the night, the doors of the chamber were bolted, and he was compelled to remain at home.

But when he had grown into a man, he resolved to live an apostolic life, proclaiming the word of God in his circuit; for many years he went about with bare feet. Indeed he never slept on a bed stuffed with feathers; from wine and meats, except because of illness, he abstained entirely; he was intent on serving God devoutly with fasts, vigils, continual prayers, studies and expositions of the divine Scriptures. By Christ’s counsel he also accustomed himself to wear only one tunic; having become a despiser of all worldly things, he gave no thought for the morrow. From love of chastity, so that he might dare to preach safely to women, he caused himself to be castrated, although in this deed his zeal remained without knowledge.

Because of his doctrine, eloquence, and apostolic life, Mammaea, the most Christian mother of the emperor Alexander, seized by a desire to hear him, came from Alexandria to Antioch, and heard his honey-sweet teaching with great admiration. He was, moreover, so intent on the study of the divine Scriptures that he never took either food or sleep unless one of the brothers had first recited a reading of the Scriptures while all listened. Like another Ezra he knew the New and Old Testament by memory; already an old man, contrary to his age, he learned the Hebrew language; with wonderful zeal he gathered into one all the editions of the interpreters besides the Seventy.

In dictating editions of the Scriptures he was of such admirable memory and subtlety that he wore out seven notaries and just as many girls extremely swift in writing, besides other young men, all writing different things at the same time from his mouth, by the incredible agility of his utterance. And although he was held so great and was an outstanding teacher among all ecclesiastical men, nevertheless he is marked by great errors, in which either he himself was foolish, or, through others inserting false things, was corrupted in his writings. For there has always been varied opinion among the learned concerning Origen, some asserting that he was a heretic, others affirming that fraud was imposed upon him. It is not surprising if the first man, without precedent, erred, who explained so many volumes with no teacher preceding him. Yet it is our part, not only concerning Origen but concerning any writers whatsoever, to agree with the holy Roman Church and to despise superfluous opinions. He published almost infinite volumes.
