Aietanus, a native of Vicenza, a canon of Padua, a man devoted and learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and physician, a very celebrated theologian, who publicly taught sacred scholastic letters in the Paduan gymnasium and acquired great praise for his learning. He wrote, as they report, several little works in both literatures, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these they report the following:

On the books of the Physics, eight books.
On the books On the Soul, three books.
On the books On the Heavens and the World, three books.
And very many other works. He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Heinricus Gulpen, abbot of the monastery of St Giles at Nuremberg, of the order of the holy father Benedict, German by nation, a distinguished jurist, excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular philosophy, and no less venerable for religious conduct than for knowledge of the Scriptures. Johannes Nider, in his little work On Ants, commends from experience the readiness of his intellect and the foresight of his counsel. He wrote elegantly on the seven distinctions:

On Penance, one book.
On Consecration, one book.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book.
And certain other works. He flourished at the Council of Constance in person, when he was still a secular master of arts studying law, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Jacobus Forliviensis, a philosopher and physician of very celebrated reputation, whom many declare to have far surpassed all professors of philosophy and medicine of his time, distinguished not only for learning but also for experience in curing human illnesses. He wrote, in his own faculty, very useful little works for those laboring in study, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these they report as the chief:

On the Extension of the Degrees of Forms, one book.
On the First Canon of Avicenna, one book.
On the Aphorisms of the same, one book.
Also on the Tegni, one book.
And certain other works. He flourished in the times of Sigismund, most Christian emperor, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1430.

Rudolphus de Buxella, from a village of Swabia, German by nation, of the diocese of Speyer, a man devoted and learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in the secular philosophy which they call Aristotelian, a distinguished professor of sacred theology, who acquired great praise for his learning in the Heidelberg gymnasium. He wrote in that faculty little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity, namely: Questions on the Sentences, one book; very useful sermons, one book; various questions, one book; many orations to the fathers, one book; And if holy mother Church. And many other works. He flourished in the Heidelberg gymnasium at the time of the Council of Basel and thereafter, in the year of the Lord 1420.
