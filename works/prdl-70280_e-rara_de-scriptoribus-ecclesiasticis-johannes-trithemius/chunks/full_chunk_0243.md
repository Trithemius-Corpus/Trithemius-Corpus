*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 120

On the Sentences: four books.
Various Questions: one book.
And very many other works.

His hearer and disciple once was a very learned man and easily the foremost of the theologians of our age: Master Stephanus, called Bruleffer, a Gaul by nation, of the same order. He first taught theology in the convent at Paris; afterwards, betaking himself to the observance of the order at Mainz in Germany, he began there to lecture on the Sentences, which he is continuing in the convent at Metz with no small praise. This William flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Albertus de Eybe, a German by nation, canon of the church of Bamberg and chamberlain of Pope Pius II, was a man zealous and learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, an outstanding rhetorician and poet. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading, of which the following are extant. To Lord Johannes, duke of Bavaria, bishop of Münster and afterwards primate of Magdeburg, an outstanding work entitled:

The Poetic Pearl: one book. You have very often wished, reverend father.
Also, On Matrimony: one book.
Letters to Various Persons: one book.
And very many other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Rodericus, bishop of Zamora, was a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote little works of no contemptible authority, products of his intellect, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have so far seen only one volume, which he entitled: Mirror of Human Life: two books. Above all human things.

The rest he has not seen.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Ambrosius Choriolanus, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, prior general, a Roman by country, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and distinguished in knowledge of secular letters, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech, learned and eloquent. He wrote certain splendid little works, by which he commended his name to immortality. Of these, however, only a few came to my knowledge through reading; I have set them down below:

On the Rule of Saint Augustine: one book. From the parent of all doctors.
A Commendation of the Same Rule: one book. After blessed Augustine.
On the Second Rule: one book. Keen in intellect, splendid in life.
On the Life of Saint Augustine: one book. Very often, when in great.
On the Praises of the Same: one book. They have been accustomed, most blessed father.
Various Orations: one book.
He also wrote many other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Benedictus Stendel of Halle, a town of Saxony, a German by nation, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect and clear in speech. In the academy of Erfurt he gloriously taught theology for many years, and shone forth distinguished both in life and in doctrine. He wrote certain little works not to be despised in reading, from which in the said university he lectured ordinarily, the following:

On Genesis: one book.
On Exodus: one book.
On Leviticus: one book.
