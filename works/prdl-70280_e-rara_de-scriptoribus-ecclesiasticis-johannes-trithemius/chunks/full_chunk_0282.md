Johannes de Dusseldorf, German by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Strasbourg: a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, a very celebrated interpreter of the theological faculty, outstanding in intellect, excellent in life. He is said to write many things but to conceal them. I have seen certain treatises of his, whose titles I could not now recall to memory. He lives to this day at Strasbourg, under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494, the twelfth indiction.

Geodericus Visenius, a Frisian man of singular skill in learning, in poetry and oration. Among these there are extant by him:

Polished elegies;
and other epigrams;
and many other things that will come down to posterity.

He is still living under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Hartmann Schedel of Nuremberg, German by nation, a distinguished doctor of arts and medicine, [unclear] of Roman eloquence, not ignorant also of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in intellect; and adding to Jacobus Bergomensis and other historiographers certain things, especially concerning the affairs of the Germans, he produced a great and notable work, which contains:

Histories of the times, one book. Of the rest he has seen nothing.

He lives to this day at Nuremberg, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord [unclear].
