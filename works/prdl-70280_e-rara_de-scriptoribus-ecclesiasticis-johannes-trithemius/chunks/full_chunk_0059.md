Julian, bishop of Campania, a man of sharp and vehement talent, learned in the holy Scriptures, schooled in Greek and Latin discourse, before he openly revealed in himself the impiety of Pelagius, was distinguished among the doctors of the Church. But afterwards, wishing to defend his heresy, he wrote against the holy doctors little works agreeing with his own opinion, of which there are extant:

Against Saint Augustine: one book.
Other books against the same: [unclear].
A dialogue of an altercation of the parties: one book.

And several others, all of which, because of the author’s errors, have deservedly been rejected from the ecclesiastical library. In a time of famine’s distress he distributed all his possessions in alms to the poor; he associated with his heresy many noble religious men, deceived under the appearance of piety. He flourished under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Innocent, the first pope, a very learned and holy man, Alban by nation, the forty-second after blessed Peter, presided over the universal Church for fifteen years; he gloriously ordained many things for its benefit. To him, when he was still a priest, blessed Jerome composed the history of the woman struck seven times, as the prologue of that same work shows. Since he was most learned in the divine Scriptures, he wrote certain excellent little works for the edification of the faithful. Of these I have found only the following:

A decree against the Pelagians: one book. “We have long wondered.”
Against the Bonosiacs: one book. “In inquiring about these things.”
Many letters to various persons: one book. “To the most reverend Dama.”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He died in the year of the Lord 417, in the fifteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Kalends of August.

Eusebius of Cremona, monk and disciple of blessed Jerome the priest, a studious and learned man, venerable in life and conduct, continually intent upon holy prayers and divine readings after the example of his most reverend master, achieved a great reputation for holiness. He wrote to Damasus, bishop of Portus, and Theodosius, senator of the city of Rome, a sufficiently elegant and lengthy volume:

On the passing of Saint Jerome: one book. “Against the pagans, concerning.”

Possidius the African, bishop of Calama, formerly a hearer and disciple of blessed Augustine, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, having lived familiarly for many years with Saint Augustine, shone brightly among ecclesiastical prelates in life and doctrine. Among other things, moreover, he wrote for the edification of the faithful:

The Life of Saint Augustine: one book. “By the inspiration of the maker of all things.”
A short catalogue of his books: one book.
