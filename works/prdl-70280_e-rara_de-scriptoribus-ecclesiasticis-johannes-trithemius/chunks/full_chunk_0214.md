Leonardus Aretinus, an outstanding philosopher, orator, and historian, and a most learned man [unclear], skilled in Greek and Latin speech, was first secretary of the Roman pontiffs Innocent VII, Gregory XII, Alexander V, and John XXIII, up to the Council of Constance; and afterward, having been summoned by the Florentine senate, he was appointed general chancellor. In this office he remained until the end of his life with great fidelity and authority. Among other things he wrote:

A notable work of Dialogues, 1 book
A History of His Own Time, 1 book
On the Deeds Well Performed by the Florentines, 12 books
On the Praises of Florence, 1 book
A History of the Goths, 3 books
A History of the First Punic War, 2 books
The Life of Aristotle, 1 book
On the Life of Cicero, 1 book
A volume of Letters, 1 book

In addition he composed some other works with great elegance, which have not come to my knowledge. Besides this he translated many works from Greek into Latin, namely the treatise of the blessed priest Basil on the instruction of pupils; he also translated from Plutarch the lives of Tiberius and Gracchus [unclear], of Paulus Aemilius, of the younger Cato, and of Sertorius the Romans. He also translated the little book of Xenophon On the Tyrant, Plato's Gorgias and Phaedo, and certain letters; Aristotle's Economics and the books of the Ethics and Politics he translated [unclear]. And other things which have not come to me. He flourished personally at the Council of Constance under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420. He died, however, under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1443, indiction 7, on the seventh Ides of March, at about the sixty-fourth year of his age.

Pogius Florentinus, a philosopher and eloquent rhetor, perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin speech, for many years a most eloquent writer within the apostolic [unclear]. He is said to have composed some little works of excellent talent, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Laurentius Valla was a very sharp rival of this Pogius, and wrote various things against him. He himself published:

On the Situation of the Region of India, 1 book
Against Laurentius Valla, 2 books
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book
Various Orations, 1 book
And many other things.

He made Dionysius Siculus and many other Greek authors Latin. Besides this, at the Council of Constance he delivered very many funeral orations. The work of filth which he entitled Facetiae we have judged rightly to be rejected from this catalogue of illustrious men, since its reading offends the devout, harms the unwary, and infects the carnal. He flourished personally at the Council of Constance under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Laurentius Valla, a Roman patrician, by far the foremost of the grammarians of this age in precepts, a philosopher, rhetorician, and most excellent theologian, subtle in intellect, sharp and eloquent, preferring Latin eloquence to Greek, composed many things for its adornment, and was very skilled in Greek and Latin speech. He published little works not to be scorned in reading, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following are reported:

Elegantiae, 6 books
On the Reciprocation of suum and suus, 1 book
Many Letters, 1 book
Also Against Quintilian, 12 books
Dialectic, 1 book
