Hieronymus Balbus, a man of very celebrated reputation, excellently learned in all the disciplines of the good arts, a distinguished philosopher, an eloquent rhetorician, excellent in metrical composition, outstanding in talent, fluent in speech, who by teaching, lecturing publicly, and writing earned great glory among the French at Paris, wrote certain excellent little works of his talent, by which he commended his name to posterity. Of these I have so far seen only a volume seasoned with great wit of eloquence, in which he answers the slanders of a certain Guilhelmus Tardivus, a rhetorician, in the manner of a dialogue, under the names of himself, Carolus Phernandus, Petrus Cohardus, and his own enemy; he gave it the title:

The Glorious Rhetorician: one book. “Among those duties by which.”
I have seen nothing of the others.

He is still living in France, writing and composing various works under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Egidius Nettelet, dean of the church of Cambrai, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, distinguished in life and conduct, conspicuous for singular devotion, published certain excellent volumes by which he made his name known. Of these there exists a very celebrated work, necessary for every estate, which with marvelous industry and very fine sequence he gathered into one from the letters of:

Saint Jerome the priest: three books. The rest is unknown to me.

He lives to this day in the city of Cambrai, very celebrated for his life and learning, and composes various works under Maximilian, most renowned king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1495.

Philippus Callimachus, Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, secretary of the king of Poland, a man most learned on every side, a distinguished philosopher, an eloquent orator, an outstanding poet, and not ignorant of the divine scriptures, outstanding in talent, fluent in speech, is said to have fashioned many excellent little works, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of their number the following are reported. A notable work on the deeds of Attila, the impious king, which he entitled:

Attila: one book. A Polish history against the Turk: one book. On the customs of the Tartars: one book. Loves in diverse meter: one book. And many other works.
