Hilarius, pope, by nation a Sardinian, after Leo the Great sat in the pontificate for six years and as many months; a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished by the adornment of many virtues, who, constantly intent upon alms and holy works, shone with no small reputation for sanctity. He, after holding a synod of fifty bishops at Rome, established certain things for ecclesiastical honor. He wrote certain necessary little works; of these I have found only:

A synodal decree, one book: “Since the religious [unclear].”
To the bishops of the province of Tarragona, a letter: “After your letters.”
To Ascanius, bishop of Tarragona, a letter: “Of the divine grace toward us.”
On the Council of Nicaea, one letter.
On the Council of Ephesus, one letter.
On the Council of Chalcedon, one letter.
Letters to various persons, one book.

He died under Leo Augustus, in the year of the Lord 468, in the sixth indiction, on the ninth day before the Kalends of March; Simplicius succeeded him.

Eusebius, bishop of Milan, learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, beloved and intimate with the most blessed pontiff Leo, fought bravely for the holy catholic faith against the heretics by writing. He is said to have published some little works for the edification of the faithful, but they have not come to my knowledge.

For I have seen only one letter of his, which he is said to have written in matters of faith:

To Saint Pope Leo, a letter: “The brethren having returned, with the Lord granting it.”

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 401.

Asclepius the African, bishop of the territory of Vaga, a studious and learned man, an outstanding declaimer of homilies to the people, a most vigorous assailant of heretics, wrote many volumes for the edification of the people; by the publication of these he was named glorious in every land, since he showed himself a useful teacher to the common people not only in words but also in works. For in his age many very learned men made it renowned. Of his writings the following are reported:

Against the Donatists, one book.
Against the Arians, one book.
Countless homilies to the people, one book.
And many other things unknown to us.

He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Prosper, bishop of Reggio, an Aquitanian after Faventius, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, eloquent in speech, profound in understanding, subtle in talent, vigorous in assertion, appeared most holy in life and conduct. He was once notary of blessed Pope Leo the Great and dictated many letters in his person. He also wrote from his own vein many excellent little works. Of these I have found only the items set down below. From the Sentences of blessed Augustine, bishop, in hexameter and pentameter verses, mixed with prose, a very beautiful work which he wished to preface:

Epigrams, one book: “While it pleases the mind with sacred things.”
