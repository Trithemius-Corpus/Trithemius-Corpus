*Book*

Rabanus Maurus, sixth archbishop of Mainz, formerly abbot of the monastery of Fulda of the Order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, from the city of Fulda in Buchonia, once a pupil of Albinus the Englishman, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, astronomer, and most subtle poet; to whom, to speak without envy, neither Italy bore a like man nor Germany an equal. In the monastery of Fulda, while still a young man, he put on the monastic habit, and advanced wonderfully both in morals and in knowledge; whence also, consecrated fourth abbot of the same community, he presided for twenty-four years over the sheep entrusted to him, by word and example, and composed almost innumerable little works on the Scriptures. At length, offended by the wickedness of his monks, who said that, being too intent on the Scriptures, he neglected temporal affairs, the devil bringing this about in them, they conceived displeasure against him and were scandalized in him by that by which they ought most of all to have been edified. Therefore, giving place to anger, and not consenting to remain any longer with ungrateful sheep, he left the monastery and its inhabitants, and went over to Emperor Louis, son of Charles, with whom he remained for many days. But the monks, led by repentance, sent an embassy to him and asked that he return to the monastery; but they did not obtain it. He therefore remained with the emperor until the death of Otgarius, archbishop of Mainz; having been appointed in his place, he presided over the aforesaid church of Mainz for nine years. Having also held a synod, he established many things for the benefit of the faithful. While still settled in the monastery of Fulda, he wrote many volumes, both in verse and in prose, chiefly on the holy Scriptures, all of which he explained from beginning to end. Concerning his writings the following are reported. To Freculphus, bishop of Lisieux, of whom we made mention above, he wrote a distinguished work:

On Genesis, three books: “In the beginning,” etc.; many things on [unclear].
On Exodus, three books: “Exodus can be called a going out.”
On Numbers, three books: “The book of Numbers [is] manifold.”
On Leviticus, three books: “Of the following book, that is, Leviticus.”
On Deuteronomy, three books: “The children of Israel moved camp.”
On Joshua, two books: “To the most blessed lord and truly [unclear].”
On Judges, one book: “After the death of Joshua.”
On the book of Ruth, three books: “Therefore to those examining the history.”
On Kings, to Hilduin, three books: “There was a man,” etc.; “Let us first say.”
On Chronicles, three books: “Adam,” etc.; “the tenth from Adam.”
On Ezra and Nehemiah, [three?] books: “Chosen lord and deservedly.”
On Tobit, one book: “The book of Esther, which the Hebrews.”
