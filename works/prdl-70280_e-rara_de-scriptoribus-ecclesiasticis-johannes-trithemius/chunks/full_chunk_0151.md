[Thaddaeus?], a Florentine by nation, a most skilled doctor of the medical art, who taught that science excellently at Bologna for certain years, hired at a public salary, and, gathering an almost incredible treasure, acquired it by the great reputation of his experience. For when he was summoned by some prince, he wished to have no less than fifty gold pieces as his daily fee. Once summoned by Pope Honorius IV, he was unwilling to come until one hundred gold pieces a day were assigned to him; when he had been cured of a very serious illness, he gave him ten thousand gold pieces. He wrote:

On the Aphorisms of Hippocrates, one book; On the Prognostics of the same, one book; On his work On Acute Diseases, one book; On Galen’s Tegni, one book; On the Isagoge of Johannitius, one book; On preserving health, one book; Various counsels, one book; and certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280. And at length he died at Bologna, an octogenarian.

John of Parma, from the territory of Bologna, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, a man learned in the divine scriptures, devout in life and conduct, and an outstanding lover of holy poverty; from the Parisian school, where he had lectured on the Sentences, [unclear] he is said to have written many and various treatises, by which his name has come down to our memory. Of these I have found only:

On the Sentences, three books; On the conduct of religious, two books.

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1260.

John Christophorus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, trained in the scriptures by long and continual study, and nobly learned in secular philosophy, ready in intellect and scholastic in speech, is said to have produced many little works on the holy scriptures; their publication, useful to those then present, made him known to those to come. For over a long time he publicly expounded the divine writings by lecturing on them, and explained them with subtle understanding. From his commentaries the following volumes are reported, namely:

On the Gospel of Matthew, one book; On Mark, one book; On Luke, one book; On John, one book; On all the epistles of Paul, fourteen books; and many others.

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1260.
