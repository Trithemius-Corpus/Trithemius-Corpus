He died under Emperor Albert and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1439, the twentieth of his abbacy, in the first indiction, on the first day of the month of December. He was buried in the said monastery of Saint Matthias the Apostle at Trier, before the altar of the protomartyr Stephen, in the right apse of the choir, under a stone inscribed with his name.

Guarinus of Verona, once a hearer of Chrysoloras the Byzantine philosopher, a man not ignorant of Greek letters and most skilled in Greek and Latin speech; through his labors the studies of the liberal arts were aroused and called back into the light, which had seemed weakened and already almost extinct. He taught first at Venice, afterwards at Ferrara; in both places he had very famous pupils. He wrote to Prince Leonello a work entitled On the Difference between a Flatterer and a Friend, one book; also a brief Grammar, one book; A Comparison of Scipio and Gaius, one book; Letters to Various Persons, sixteen books; several Orations, one book; many Poems; and not a few other things. By order of Pope Nicholas V he translated into Latin Strabo’s Geography, a work no less useful than delightful. From Plutarch he made Latin versions of the lives of many illustrious men; but Plutarch himself On the Education of Children, with many other works, he translated most elegantly, which are partly extant. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460, in the eighth indiction, on the day before the Nones of December. He was buried at Ferrara in the convent of the Carmelites, honorably endowed with a marble tomb.

Heinrich Kalteisen, archbishop of the churches of Nidaros and Caesarea, of the Order of Friars Preachers, German by nation, a native of Koblenz, where he entered the order; first lector of the sacred palace, preacher of Holy Cross, inquisitor of heretical depravity, afterwards, as we have said, consecrated chief bishop of two provinces by apostolic authority; through his learning and preaching he became famously known to the whole world. Since he was a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, he wrote, among other notable things: Sermons on the Season, one book; Sermons on the Saints, one book; Sermons through Lent, one book; On the Magnificat, one book; Various Questions, one book; Conferences to the Clergy; and many other things. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1466, in the fourteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Nones of October. He was buried in his own convent at Koblenz.
