*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 138.

He is still living today at the court of the king of Poland, in the highest esteem, and writes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which I wrote these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Aulus Niavis, German by nation, a man excellently learned in humane letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, outstanding in ability, eloquent in speech, has acquired great fame for his name by teaching and writing various things in the gymnasium of Leipzig. For he wrote certain excellent little works, whose reading is not to be despised. Of these the following are reported: A little work for religious novices; *On the instruction of Latinizing*, one book, “Since I have often considered”; *The Vision of a Certain Hermit*, one book, “A vast grove clearly”; *The Colors of the Art of Rhetoric*, one book, “You once wished, chosen”; *Dialogue of the Idiot*, one book, “When I very often repeated”; brief formal letters, one book, “Great is wont to be the force”; letters to the same, of moderate length, one book, “I promised, honorable man”; and also longer letters, one book, “I conceive great…”; and certain other things.

He is still living today in Germany, composing various things under Maximilian, the most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Bohuslaus of Hassenstein, German by nation, a noble man, most learned from every side, a distinguished orator and poet, who, contrary to the custom of the other nobles of this age, handles arms, cultivates letters with marvelous zeal, fosters and celebrates them, so that, like another Pliny the Younger, even while serving as a soldier he composes books and poems. He wrote certain excellent little works, both in meter and in prose, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are extant: an excellent work *On the Germans’ and Italians’ Own Inventions*, one book; also a book of poems, one book; various letters; and many other things.

He is still living today in his castle of Hassenstein, writing various things, under Maximilian, the most excellent king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Heinricus Northeymensis, German by nation, a man studious and learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in ability, eloquent in speech, excelling in meter and prose, wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, by which he commended his name to posterity with glory. Of these I have seen only that excellent work which he wrote in meter and prose to Frederick, margrave of Meissen and duke of Saxony, entitled *Distichomachia*, *On the Conflict of Virtues and Vices*, one book, “You, my laurel-bearing…”

Concerning the rest that he composed, nothing has come to my knowledge. He is still living today, writing various things, under Maximilian, the most illustrious king of the Romans, with Alexander VI as supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Quirinus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, deriving his origin of life from Sweden, a man studious in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, a celebrated philosopher and excellent mathematician, outstanding in ability, polished in speech, imitating Boethius, wrote a most commendable work *On the Proportions of Numbers and Sounds*, one book; certain letters; and certain other things.

He is still living today in esteem with Otto, duke of Bavaria, and writes various things, under Maximilian, the most invincible king, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.
