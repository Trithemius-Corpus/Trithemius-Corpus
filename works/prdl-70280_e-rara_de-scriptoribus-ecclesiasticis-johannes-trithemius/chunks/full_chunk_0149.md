Erardus, monk of the monastery of Saint Quentin in Insula, a man devoted to the holy Scriptures, skilled through constant exercise, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, quick in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain little works of no contemptible reading, by which he made his name known to present and future generations. Among these is said to be a history of the translation of the crown and of a portion of the Lord’s cross, relics which Louis, king of France, obtained from the emperor of Constantinople and brought to Paris. He also wrote, concerning blessed Elizabeth the landgravine:

*Miracles Performed after Her Death*, one book.

*Chanted Responsories concerning the Same*, one book.

And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270, and composed various little works in both kinds of letters.

Richard, archbishop of Reims in Gaul, of the order of the Friars Minor, a man excellently learned in the holy Scriptures, inferior to none of the doctors of his time in delivering sermons to the people, subtle in talent, is said to have written many distinguished volumes, by which he made himself useful to those then present and known to those to come; but I do not recall that any of them has come into our hands. They report his:

*Sermons on the Temporal Cycle*, one book.

And also *Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.
