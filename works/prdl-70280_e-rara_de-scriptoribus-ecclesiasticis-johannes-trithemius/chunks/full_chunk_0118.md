He also composed many sermons and other different and varied treatises.
He flourished under Emperor Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1130.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Deutz near Cologne Agrippina, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, with the Holy Spirit enlightening him through a vision, and not ignorant of secular letters, a monk of Saint Lawrence of Liege, was taken thence on account of his incredible learning by Frederick, archbishop of Cologne, and elevated as abbot of the monastery of Deutz. He composed many excellent little works. For from the time when in his youth he entered the monastery, he had exercised himself so greatly in the Holy Scriptures that not even while sleeping did he seem able to rest from meditating on them, but his tongue and lips moved as though he were reading. Of his little works I have at present found only the following. To Cuno, abbot of Siegburg and afterwards bishop of Regensburg:
