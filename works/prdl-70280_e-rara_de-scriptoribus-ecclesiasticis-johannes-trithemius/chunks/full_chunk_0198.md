Johannes de Bromyard, of the order of Friars Preachers, a holy man, devoted to the divine Scriptures and learned by long practice, also not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent, an outstanding declaimer, wrote certain distinguished volumes by which he established the memory of his name among posterity. Of these, the following are reported: a great and very lucid work, very useful to preachers, which he entitled:
A Summa of Preachers, one book, [incipit:] “The life of preachers, etc.”
A Summa of Moral Law, one book, [incipit:] “In the following little work [unclear].”
Sermons for the Temporal Cycle, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book, [incipit:] “Lay up for yourselves treas[ures].”
He is said besides to have written many other things which have not come to my knowledge.

Johannes Gluel, German by nation, of the order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Cologne, sprung from Aachen, an imperial city, a man outstandingly learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, of excellent and distinguished talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote, among the other little works of his study, a volume not to be rejected by the cultivators of this religion, on the origin and progress of his order, which he entitled:
Mirror of the Carmelites, one book.
Sermons for the Temporal Cycle, one book, [incipit:] “Lay up [unclear].”
Lenten Sermons, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book.
He flourished in his convent at Cologne, writing and composing various little works, where at last, when his strength failed, he ended his day and was buried in peace.

Heinricus Euta, or Oyta, German by nation, the unique ornament and co-founder from the beginning of the University of Vienna in Austria, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote many distinguished volumes, of which very few have so far come into our hands. For I have read only:
On the Sentences, four books, [incipit:] “Concerning the prologue of the first [book] of the Sentences [unclear].”
On Contracts, one book, [incipit:] “Love justice, you who judge.”
On the Conception of Saint Mary, [incipit:] “A virgin shall arise from Israel.”
And certain sermons. Of the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished in the University of Vienna under Emperor Wenceslaus, together with Heinricus de Hassia, in the year of the Lord 1390.
