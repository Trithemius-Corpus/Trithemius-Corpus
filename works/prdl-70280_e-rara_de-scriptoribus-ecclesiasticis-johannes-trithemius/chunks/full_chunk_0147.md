He flourished in the pontificate under the name of John the Twenty-first, in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1275.

William, a citizen of Piacenza, a learned doctor of the art of medicine and not ignorant of other humane letters, who, hired at a public salary at Verona, held a school in that faculty for many years and instructed several excellent pupils. He wrote, for those who profess this science, little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known both to those present and to those to come. Among these there survives a notable volume which he wished to be entitled:

On the Whole of Medicine, four books.
Also On Surgery, one book.
Of the rest I have found nothing.

He flourished under Emperor Arnolph, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William, bishop of Paris, by homeland an Auvergnat, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, clear in speech, and venerable no less in conduct than in learning, composed not a few works of his erudition, by which, showing himself a learned and devout master, he rendered the memory of his name immortal. Of these, the following are reported: a notable volume entitled

On the Holy Trinity, one book.
On the First Principle, one book.
On the Sacraments, one book.
Why God Became Man, one book. I shall set down four more.
Summa of Vices, one book. In the order of divine wisdom.
On Faith and Laws, one book.
On Human Souls, one book.
On Good and Evil, six books.
On the Universe, six books. The second table after [unclear].
On the Profession of Novices, one book. After, by the help of God, thus far.
On the Gift of Knowledge, one book.
On the Works of Virtues, one book.
On the Thirty Remedies for Temptations, one book.
Against Plurality of Benefices, one book.
On the Cloister of the Soul, one book. In sacred and signifying things.
On Divine Rhetoric, one book.
On Penitence, one book.
On the Song of Songs, one book.
On Demons, one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
He also published some other works which have by no means come to my knowledge.

He flourished under Frederick, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 1230.

John Valensis, otherwise called Galensis, English by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, who for a long time taught theology gloriously in the school of Paris, was of such erudition and eloquence that he was called the tree of life. He wrote many distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. But few of them have come into our hands.
