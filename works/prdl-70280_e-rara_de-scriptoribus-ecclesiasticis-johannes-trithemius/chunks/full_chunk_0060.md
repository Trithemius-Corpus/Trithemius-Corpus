*On Ecclesiastical Writers, fol. 26*

He flourished under the emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 430.

Siricius, pope, a Roman by birth, sat in the pontificate after Damasus for fifteen years. A learned and holy man, and a most vigorous defender of the catholic faith, he condemned to exile all the Manichaeans whom he found in the city; but those who were converted he relegated to a monastery, to perform penance until the end of their life. Among other things, he wrote to Himerius, bishop of Tarraco:

A decretal treatise, one book.
Against Jovinian and Auxentius, one book.

He died under the emperors Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 398, in the sixth indiction, on the ninth of March.

Fastidius, bishop of the Britons, a man learned in the holy Scriptures and an excellent preacher of the word of God, also distinguished in life and conduct, renowned in speech and intellect, wrote some devout little works. Among these there is said to be extant a certain volume, written with sound doctrine:

On Christian Doctrine, one book.
On Preserving Widowhood, one book.

He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Pope Celestine celebrated, through Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, the council of two hundred fathers at Ephesus, in which Nestorius and Pelagius were condemned with their wicked doctrines. There too it was decreed that thereafter the most blessed Virgin Mary should be called Mother of God. Blessed Celestine wrote, among other things, a solemn decree:

Against Nestorius, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
And certain other things.

He died under the younger emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 433, in the tenth year of his pontificate, in the first indiction, on the sixth Ides of April.

Theodoric, bishop of the city which from its founder Cyrus, king of the Persians, is called Cyria, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, is said to have composed some little works on the holy volumes, but they have not come into our hands. There is, however, extant his notable work, edited after Jerome:

Of Ecclesiastical History, one book.
On the Incarnation of the Lord against Eutyches, one book.

He flourished in the times of the most sacred emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390. His history is now inserted in the Tripartite History.

Evodius, bishop in Africa, disciple of blessed Augustine, bishop of Hippo, to whom the same Augustine wrote the treatise On the Soul of Christ among the Dead, and many letters; a man learned in the holy Scriptures and most upright in his way of life, who deserves to be placed in the catalogue of the saints. He is said to have written certain devout little works. Of these there is extant a volume on the miracles performed in Africa through the relics brought by the priest Orosius:

Of Saint Stephen the Martyr, one book.
And several letters to various persons, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Saint Augustine, in the year of the Lord 420.

Palladius, of the Britons, or Scots, of the island of Ireland, first ordained bishop by Pope Celestine, a Greek by birth, a learned and holy man, by his very sweet preaching converted to the faith of Christ the people entrusted to him. After him Saint Patrick, a Briton by race, consecrated by Saint Pope Celestine as archbishop of Ireland, shining for forty years with signs and miracles, converted the whole island to the faith. Palladius wrote:

The Life of Saint John Chrysostom, one book.

He was sent to Scotland by Pope Celestine I in the year of the Lord 432, in the fifteenth indiction, according to Marianus Scotus.
