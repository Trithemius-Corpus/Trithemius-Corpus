There are those who write that they have read six thousand treatises of Origen, a great number unknown to the Latins. Nor is there any doubt that many little works of his have recently been translated which have by no means come to the notice of our reading. He died, however, an old man, poor, and was buried at Tyre, under the emperors Valerian and Gallienus, in the year of the Lord 261, in the ninth indiction, in the seventieth year of his age.

Ammonius of Alexandria, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a philosopher and rhetorician the most celebrated of all his time, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote in the Greek language certain excellent volumes, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of their number the following are extant:

On the Harmony of Jesus and Moses: one book, “In the first canon four.”
One out of Four: one book, “Since indeed many have undertaken.”
And certain other things.

The most impious Porphyry, a very bitter enemy of the Christians, a pagan philosopher, falsely accuses him of having denied the faith and become a heathen; but it is agreed that he remained a devout Christian until death.

He flourished under Alexander, son of Mamaea, in the year of the Lord 230.

Ambrose, a hearer of Origen, at first a Marcionite and afterward corrected by Origen himself, an outstanding confessor of the Christian faith, even deserved to be promoted to the office of deacon. He was certainly a most learned man and a devoted lover of books; when he supplied at his own expense notaries and scribes, our Adamantius composed almost infinite volumes. He was so devoted a cultivator of Origen’s labors that day and night he urged him, by spoken and written words, to write something new, so that in a certain familiar letter he is named by Origen as the taskmaster of the work. He was accustomed to say that he never went to bed while Origen was present, nor took food, unless first, from the brothers who were always at hand, he had made the sacred writings resound. He wrote to Origen giving thanks for his conversion.

Of letters: one book.
