The life of Saint Hilary of Poitiers: one book.
To Oporicus on his own life: one book.
The life of a holy bishop: one book.
Letters to various persons: one book.
Poems to friends: one book.
“The king, the summit of the region,” etc.
He also composed many other works, which, since they are short, I have not wished to mark under the name of books. For he wrote many epitaphs and brief verses, which I have seen.
He flourished under Justin the Younger Augustus, in the year of the Lord 570.

Gregory, bishop of Tours, formerly a monk and once a disciple of Saint Avitus, bishop of the Auvergnats, a learned and noble man, and also gentle and humble, was of such great holiness and purity that even in life he shone with miracles. When he visited the thresholds of the apostles Peter and Paul, he was received by the blessed Pope Gregory with the greatest reverence and charity, as a truly holy man and a special friend, uniquely beloved by him. He wrote several volumes in a simple and plain style, of which there survive:

On the praise of the holy martyrs: one book.
On the life of certain confessors: two books.
On the miracles of Saint Julian the martyr: one book.
On the miracles of Saint Martin: four books.
History of the Franks: ten books.
An epitome of the same: one book.
Chronicle of ecclesiastical history: one book.
The other works which he composed have not yet come to our knowledge through reading.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 600.

Martin, bishop, formerly ruler and abbot of the monastery of Dumio, a man learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, coming from the regions of the East into Gaul, converted the people of the Sueves by teaching and miracles from Arian impiety to the right faith; he established rules of orthodox faith, founded monasteries, and reformed churches. He wrote to Mito, king of Galicia, on the four virtues which are called cardinal, an instructive volume which he wished to entitle:

The Form of an Honorable Life: one book.
A book of letters full of the cultivation of virtues: one book.
And certain other works which I have not seen.
He flourished under Theodemir, king of the Sueves, while Justinian Augustus governed the Constantinopolitan empire, in the year of the Lord 540.

Paterius, notary and secundicerius of the Roman Church, a man trained by constant study in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, gathered all the testimonies of divine Scripture by which the blessed Pope Gregory clarified the obscure points of his exposition, and published three books: two, namely, on testimonies of the Old Testament, and one on the Testament; and he entitled the codex itself:

Testimony: four books. “When of the most blessed and apostolic...”
I have seen nothing concerning the rest.
He flourished at Rome at the same time when Pope Gregory presided over the universal Church, and at his urging he compiled the aforesaid volume, while Maurice Augustus held the empire and after him Phocas, in the year of the Lord 600.

Columbanus, first founder and abbot of the monastery of Luxeuil, a Scot by nation, a man of outstanding holiness, merit, and learning, father of many thousands of monks and founder of several monasteries, an excellent sower of the divine word, coming from Ireland into Gaul and Germany, increased divine worship, monasteries, and servants, as once the chief of the Druids had done.
