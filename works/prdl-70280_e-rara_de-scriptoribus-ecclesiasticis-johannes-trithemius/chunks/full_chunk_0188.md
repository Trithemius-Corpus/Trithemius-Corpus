Johannes de Tambacho, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man much practiced and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy either, outstanding in talent, composed in expression with scholastic brilliance, published certain volumes not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these I have found only the following: a great work, not to be rejected, against the weapons of raging adverse [fortune?], no less useful than pleasant, which he wished to be entitled:

*The Consolation of Theology*, 15 books. “Whatever things have been written,” etc.
*On the Sensible Delights of Paradise*, 1 book. “You were in the delights of the paradise of God.”
He published *Sermons*, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Leonardus de Chifano, of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, the twenty-fourth minister general, and thereafter priest-cardinal of the Roman Church, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, very skilled in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence. He is said to have left many monuments of his talent. Of these I have found very few:

*On the Sentences*, 3 books.
*On the Song of Songs*, 1 book.
A notable *Summa*, 1 book.
Various *Sermons*, 1 book.

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370.

Johannes Balistarii, a Catalan by country, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the seventeenth prior general, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, who governed his order vigorously in deed and word for sixteen years, composed distinguished monuments of his talent. Of these there are reported:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*On the War of the Church Militant*, 1 book.
Various *Sermons*, 1 book.
And certain other works.

The aforesaid distinguished work *On the Strong War of the Church Militant*, against Antichrist assailing it, he sent dedicated to Pope Gregory XI. He died in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Gregory XI, in the year of the Lord 1373, in the twelfth indiction, in his convent of Majorca.

Johannes Boccacius of Certaldo, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by country, a man easily the most learned of all in secular letters in his time, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a poet, philosopher, astronomer, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, published many distinguished volumes, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported. To Robert, king of Sicily:

*On the Genealogy of the Gods*, 14 books. “If sufficiently from the things reported, lord.”
*On Illustrious Men*, 1 book. “As I was seeking what.”
*On Famous Women*, [unclear] book. “On the day before, distinguished among women.”
*On the Heresies of the Bohemians*, [unclear] book. “I had indeed risen weary.”
*On the Victories of Sigismund*, [unclear] book.
*On the Wars of the Emperors*, [unclear] book.
*On Mountains*, 1 book. “Afterwards concerning mountains [unclear].”
*On Springs*, 1 book.
