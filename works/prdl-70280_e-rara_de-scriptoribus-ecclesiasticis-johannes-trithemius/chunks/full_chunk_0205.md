Johannes Capreolus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, by homeland a Toulousain, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, excellently taught in the disciplines of Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect, quite capable in disputation, a most vigorous champion and defender of the sayings of Saint Thomas Aquinas. He wrote very distinguished monuments of his talent, by which he made his name known. Of these many volumes are extant:

On the Sentences: 3 books.
And various sermons: 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1415.

Henricus de Coesfeldia, by nation a German, of the Carthusian Order, prior of the house of Saint Mary near the Mount of Saint Gertrude in Holland, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, of great reputation, subtle in intellect, clear in speech, and also a very celebrated preacher of sermons. He wrote many excellent volumes, whose frequent use is judged useful for devout brothers, lovers of their own salvation. Among them the following are reported:

On Exodus, mystically: 1 book.
On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans: 1 book.
On the three substantial vows: 2 books. “Do not love the world.”
Against the vice of ownership: 1 book.
On the three monastic guardianships: 1 book.
On the instruction of novices: 1 book.
The Mystical Circumcisorium: 1 book.
On the sacrament of the altar: 1 book.
On the Lord’s Annunciation: 1 book.
Eulogium of Paul the Hermit: 1 book.
Sermons on the season: 1 book.
Letters to various persons: 1 book.
And very many other things.

The difficult work of William of Paris, bishop, On the Whole Corporeal, Incorporeal, or Spiritual Universe, he traversed with wonderful industry and beautifully enclosed in a brief compendium. He died in the house of the Valley of Grace near Bruges, beneficially occupied in the act of visitation, in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1312, indiction 3, on the sixth day before the Ides of July.

Henricus Hachenburg, of the Order of Friars Preachers, by nation a German, a man magnificently practiced and well instructed in the divine Scriptures, of excellent talent and very celebrated reputation in declaiming sermons to the people. He wrote volumes not to be rejected for the direction of simple priests. Of these there are extant:

Sermons on the season: 1 book. “I will come and heal him.”
Sermons on the saints: 1 book.

He flourished, as his own people report, in the convent at Koblenz, where he composed various works.

Jordanus, of the Order of the Friars of Saint Augustine of the Hermitage, by nation a German, a devout man, an excellent expounder and interpreter of the divine Scriptures, of most excellent talent in preaching sermons. He wrote several elegant treatises useful to devout brothers, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Yet few of these have come into our hands. The following, however, are reported:

On the Apocalypse of John: 1 book.
Apology of his order: 1 book.
On the Passion of the Lord: 1 book. “Look and make according to the example” [unclear].
