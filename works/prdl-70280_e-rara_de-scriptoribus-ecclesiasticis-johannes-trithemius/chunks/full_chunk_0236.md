He died in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1466, in the fourteenth indiction.

Nicolaus Dorbellus, of the Order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, of the province of Tours, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in scholastic philosophy, renowned for his intellect, and sufficiently fitted for disputing and disentangling questions of the Scriptures, wrote certain excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. In the school at Poitiers, both by teaching and by writing, he earned great praise for his learning. Concerning his little works the following are reported: an outstanding and most celebrated work, almost divine, pleasing in its singular usefulness:

And certain other things. He was, moreover, a profound interpreter and most keen defender of the sayings of John Scotus; from his writings, expounding philosophy, he named this very work a compendium, dividing it into three parts: mathematical, theological, and ethical. He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Callixtus III, in the year of the Lord 1455.
