At length he died after many labors, full of days, under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 422, in the fifth indiction, on the last day of the month of September, at the hour of compline, in the ninety-ninth year of his age. He was buried at Bethlehem, shining with many miracles; Eusebius, his disciple, wrote his life.

Audentius, a Spanish bishop, a man whose talent was exercised in the divine Scriptures, wrote against the Manichaeans, Sabellians, and Arians, but with special intention against the Photinians, who afterward were called Bonosiacs, a notable work, which is said to have been entitled:

*On the Faith, Against Heretics*, one book.

He also published some other works, which have by no means come to my knowledge.

Rufinus, monk and priest of the church of Aquileia, an eloquent man and very learned in the divine Scriptures, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, was of no small reputation among the ecclesiastical doctors. Although his writings are many, nevertheless they are counted among the apocrypha, distinction 15, *Sancta romana*, because he wrote invective letters against the most holy man Jerome, as can be understood from reading them. But afterward, when they returned to the harmony of brotherly love, the works of Rufinus were made authentic insofar as they were not said to be contrary to Jerome. Concerning the writings of Rufinus, therefore, the following are reported:

*On the Prophet Hosea*, one book.

*On the Prophet Amos*, one book.

*On the Prophet Joel*, one book.

*On His Faith, to Bishop Laurentius*, one book. “To me indeed, most faithful father.”

*Invectives Against Jerome*, three books. “I have read your writings, [unclear].”

*On His Faith, to Anastasius*, one book. “I have heard that certain men, when among.”

*On the Blessings of the Patriarchs*, one book.

*On the Sacraments*, one book.

*Exhortation to Proba*, one book.

Additions to the *Ecclesiastical History*, three books. He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, and wrote many and various works, in the year of the Lord 400.

John, bishop of Constantinople, [unclear] of the eloquence of Eusebius, bishop of Emesa, is named Chrysostom, that is, “golden mouth.” He was a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, of outstanding talent in declaiming homilies to the people, and no less distinguished for holiness than for learning; he was held by the people in great reverence and love. This most blessed prelate suffered many injuries from Theophilus of Alexandria, and by his fraudulent and deceitful machinations was driven from the episcopate into exile, as is found in his life and in the *Tripartite History*. He wrote many volumes in Greek; of these I, among the Latins, have seen only the following:

“When I behold you, blessed [unclear].”

*That No One Is Harmed Except by Himself*, one book. “I know that to the coarser.”

*On the Restoration of the Fallen*, one book. “Who will give water to my head.”

*On the Gospel of Matthew*, three books. “As they report Matthew.”

*On the Gospel of John*, 87 homilies, one book. “They who [unclear] the contests.”

*On Paul’s Epistle to the Hebrews*, six books. “In many ways and in many.”

*To Stagirius the Monk, Possessed by a Spirit*, three books. “There is certainly nothing.”
