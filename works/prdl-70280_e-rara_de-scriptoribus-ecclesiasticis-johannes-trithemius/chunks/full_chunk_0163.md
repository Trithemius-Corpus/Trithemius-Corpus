He flourished before his pontificate, lecturing on the Sentences at Paris, in the time of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1317.

Gentilis of Foligno, a distinguished professor of medicine and magnificently learned in other disciplines of secular philosophy, had no small skill in that faculty. He wrote many excellent volumes in medicine, as they say, by which his name became known with praise not only to those then present but also to posterity. But I have seen none of them. The following, however, are reported:

Commentaries on Avicenna: 5 books.
Against the Pestilence: 1 book.
Also On Fevers: 1 book.
On the Degrees of Medicine: 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Henry VII, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Dante Alighieri, Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, a man inferior to none in his age in divine scriptures, a philosopher and poet, subtle in intellect and brilliant in eloquence, the keenest disputant of all, wrote many splendid volumes both in meter and in prose, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Driven from his homeland, he lived in exile all his days: for some time in Gaul, and afterward with the king of Aragon; and on his own misfortune he composed various works. Of his little works these are reported:

Of Comedies: 1 book.
On the Monarchy of the World: 1 book.
Several letters.
And certain other things.

At last he died in exile at Ravenna, under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1321, in the fourth indiction, and in the fifty-sixth year of his age.

Michael of Cesena, the seventeenth minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in divine scriptures, ready in intellect and brilliant in eloquence, and no less venerable for religion than for knowledge, published in the holy scriptures little works of exposition not to be despised, by which he became widely known far and wide both to those then present and now to posterity. Of these I have so far been able to find very few, namely:

On the Sentences: 4 books.
On the prophet Ezekiel: 1 book.
Sermons on the Season: 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints: 1 book.

He published lectures and commentaries on many books of the Bible, which I have not yet seen.

He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Bertrand, of the Order of Friars Minor, Milanese by homeland, born of a noble family, and, as some hold, priest cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, was certainly a man outstandingly learned in divine scriptures and not moderately instructed in secular philosophy. In delivering sermons to the people he was of excellent talent. He wrote several little works of great usefulness. Of these I found:

On the Sentences: 4 books.
Sermons on the Epistles: [unclear] books.
Sermons on the Gospels: [unclear] books, “They will see the Son,” etc., “the word.”
