I judge it necessary and useful; most people call it the Common Discourse, because it contains something concerning every state; but he himself entitled it *Summa of Conferences*, seven books: “When the teacher or preacher ...” *On the Sentences*, four books: “Since for every business there is a time ...” *On the Ordering of Life*, one book: “Abstinence is a good thing in food ...” *Handful of Flowers*, one book. *On the Apocalypse of John*, one book. Various sermons, one book. *On Various Subjects*, fifteen books. He flourished in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William Durand, bishop of Mende, once a hearer and disciple of Henry of Susa, whom they call the Speculator, a doctor most skilled in law and very zealous for ecclesiastical observance, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, wrote to Ottobono, cardinal of Saint Adrian, later supreme pontiff Adrian V, an excellent work divided into three volumes, which he entitled *Speculum of Law*, four books: “To the reverend father in Christ ...” *Rationale of Divine Offices*, eight books: “Whatever things in ecclesiastical offices ...” *Repertory of Law*, one book: “By the rust of the first-formed man ...” And certain other works. He flourished under Emperor Rudolf. And he completed his work, the *Rationale of Divine Offices*, as he himself testifies, in the year of the Lord 1286.

Jacobus de Belviso, a most distinguished doctor of both laws and a cautious interpreter, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, profound in counsel and knowledge, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, is said to have written certain excellent little works in law, by which he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity. Of these I have not yet been able to see any. His works are said to include: *On the Authentics*, one book. *On the Use of Fiefs*, one book. *Additions on the Novels*, one book. And various disputations. And many other works. He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William of Lyon, of the Order of Friars Preachers, as they report bishop of the same church, a man much learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, sweet in intellect and subtle in speech, not affected, yet well arranged, was quite outstanding in delivering sermons to the people. He is said to have written some little works, by which he magnificently instructed both those then present and those who would come afterward. Among these are reported: *On the Sentences*, four books: “Since, concerning individual useful studies ...” *Summa of Virtues*, five books: “About to speak of individual vices ...” *Sermons to the People*, one book. He is also said to have written many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.
