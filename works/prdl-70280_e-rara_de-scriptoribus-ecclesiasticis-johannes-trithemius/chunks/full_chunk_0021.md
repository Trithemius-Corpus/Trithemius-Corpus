Theodotion, an Ephesian by birth, formerly a disciple of Tatian the philosopher while he was still a believer, a man certainly most learned in every respect and the third translator of the divine Scriptures: besides the translation of the Scriptures which he made from Hebrew into Latin, he also composed certain other little works, which nevertheless have not yet come into our hands at all. He flourished in the time of the emperor Commodus, in the year of the Lord 180.

Rhodon, Asian by race, once a hearer and disciple of Tatian, a man very learned both in the divine Scriptures and also in secular letters, is said to have written many things in Greek; examples of these are rarely found among our people. Of them, however, the following are reported as the more important, which we have set down below:

*Against Marcion*: three books
*Against the Cataphrygians*: one book
*To Callistion*: one book
*On the Hexaemeron*: six books

He flourished under the princes Commodus and Severus, in the year of the Lord 200.

Clement, priest of the Alexandrian church, once a hearer and disciple of Pantaenus, the Stoic Christian philosopher, who after him held the ecclesiastical school at Alexandria for many years, was a devout, learned, and most eloquent man. He wrote many excellent volumes, among which these are reported:

*Stromata*, that is, *Miscellanies*: eight books
*Against the Gentiles*: one book
*The Pedagogue*: three books
*On Easter*: one book
*On Fasting*: one book
*On Disputation*: one book
*On the Salvation of the Rich Man*: one book
*On Detraction*: one book
*Against the Judaizers*: one book
*On Ecclesiastical Canons*: one book

He also published many letters and various treatises, whose titles we do not know.

He flourished in the times of the princes Commodus and Severus, in the year of the Lord 300.

Melciades, whom Rhodon recalled in his work which he composed against the Cataphrygians, Montanus, Prisca, and Maximilla, insane prophets: a man very learned in secular letters and exercised with constant study in the divine Scriptures, wrote not a few little works for the edification of the faithful. Of these the following are reported: *Apologeticus for our faith*: one book.

He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of Marcus Antoninus Commodus, in the year of the Lord 170.
