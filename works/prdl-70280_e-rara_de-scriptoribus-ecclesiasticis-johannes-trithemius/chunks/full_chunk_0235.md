Henricus Gorrickem, German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and notably learned in secular philosophy, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, and an acute disputant, was regarded in his own time as distinguished in the University of Cologne by lecturing and composing. He wrote little works of no contemptible reading, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory. Of these there are extant:

*Conclusions on the Sentences*, three books: “Desiring something concerning ...”

*On Superstitions*, one book: “In the reading ...”

Against the Hussites also, one book.

*On the Celebration of Feasts*, one book.

A summary of Saint Thomas, one book.

Sermons and conferences, one book.

In addition he published various questions, determinations, lectures, different books, and treatises, which have by no means come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1460.

Johannes Kanneman, of the Order of Friars Minor, German by nation, a man learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence, was excellent in declaiming sermons to the people. Since, thinking wrongly about ecclesiastical power, he was being sought by the minister of Saxony for prison, he slipped away in flight and took refuge with the Observantines; thereafter he amended his error together with his life. Johannes de Hagen, a Carthusian and most learned man, had already long before corrected him and recalled him to the paths of justice. He wrote:

A defense of himself, one book.

*On the Passion of the Lord*, one book.

Various sermons, one book.

Certain questions, one book.

And many other things.

He flourished in the times of Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Jacobus Luterbuck, German by nation, of the Order of Carthusians, vicar of the house of Mount Saint Savior near Erfurt, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in talent, clear in speech, a cautious interpreter of doubtful matters, and devout also in life and conduct, wrote many various little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Concerning these the following are reported:

*On the Jubilee Year*, one book.

*On the Causes of Many Sufferings*, one book.

*On the Threefold State of Men*, one book.

*Consolation for the Evils of This World*, one book.

*On the Manner of Observing an Interdict*, one book.

*Apologeticus of Religious*, one book.

*On the Precautions of Diverse Estates*, one book.

*On the Knowledge of Secrets*, one book: “For cutting away errors ...”
