Rupertus of Licio, bishop of Aquila, a city of the Marsi or of Abruzzo, of the Order of Friars Minor: a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature; excellent in talent, sweet and well-ordered in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, and famous throughout the whole Christian world, who by word and example converted many from iniquity. He wrote, for the edification of readers and especially of preachers of the divine word, certain excellent volumes, of which the following are reported:

Sermons on the Temporale, a book beginning: “Nox praeceps,” etc.; these are the words.
On the Praises of the Saints, a book beginning: “Let not the wise man glory in ...”
On Penitence for Lent, a book beginning: “Many are ...” etc. [unclear].
On Sins, [unclear], a book beginning: “And certain other things.”

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and under the pontiffs Sixtus and Innocent, in the year of the Lord 1486. I do not know whether he is still living.

Johannes Pfeffer of Widenberg, German by nation, by homeland an East Franconian: a man learned by long study in the divine Scriptures, and excellently trained in secular philosophy; outstanding in talent, devout, gentle, and distinguished in life and conduct. Sent from the University of Heidelberg, he adorned with his learning the beginning of the schools at Freiburg, in which he has already taught theology gloriously for very many years and has instructed many of his hearers uprightly. He wrote certain excellent little works, among which there exists a distinguished work on the Epistles of Paul, which he marked out for public reading:

Priestly Directory, fourteen books, beginning: “Concerning the reading of the epistle of blessed ...”
And many other things.

He was still flourishing among men under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1490. He may still be living.

Johannes Britannicus, born at Palazzolo, a town of the Brescian territory: a man most learned in secular studies and not ignorant of divine Scripture; [unclear] famous, an outstanding poet and splendid rhetorician, who among his own Brescians is today held in honor. By invaluable learning, teaching, and writings he makes them daily more renowned. He wrote many and various works, by which he makes his name known to posterity and venerable to those now living. Of these the following are reported:

A very celebrated work of commentary:
On Persius, one book.
On Statius, likewise one book.
On Ovid’s Metamorphoses, one book.
Likewise various letters.
And many other things.

He is distinguished in the city of Brescia and composes many little works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493.

Battista of Ferrara, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel: a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular literature; outstanding in verse and prose, and skilled in both languages; in declaiming sermons to the people very celebrated and of almost divine talent. He wrote many excellent volumes, by which he makes himself useful to those now living and known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

A most lucid work of histories from the times of Christ down to us, which he entitled Florida.
Chronicle of his Order.
On the Fall of the Roman Empire.
On Mount Sinai.
Life of Mechtild.
Chronicle of Ferrara.
