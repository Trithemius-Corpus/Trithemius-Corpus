Petrus Boherius, abbot of Saint Avianus, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a jurist, an eminent cultivator of sacred Scripture, subtle in intellect, distinguished in speech, a most celebrated director of monastic discipline, produced for the edification of his brothers several little works, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these I have found only:

On the Rule of Saint Benedict: one book.
On the Mirror of Monks: one book.
On the signs of utterances: one book.
Of the rest I have seen nothing.

Since he was a most celebrated doctor of decrees, he commented on the Rule of the holy father Benedict in a new and unusual kind of exposition, comparing all its chapters, judgments, doctrines, and also, what requires greater diligence, its words, counsels, and precepts with pontifical law, and with admirable subtlety confirmed them by the canons of the fathers. He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Jacobus de Altavilla, German by nation, of Rinaugia by homeland, of the diocese of Mainz, a most learned professor of sacred letters, nobly instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, produced several notable little works on the divine Scriptures, by which he destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have hitherto been found worthy to see only a notable volume:

On the Sentences: four books.
Also various Questions: one book.

He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Johannes de Ymenhusen, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, ready in intellect, cautious in knowledge, scholastic in speech, an outstanding teacher no less by example than by word, wrote certain excellent volumes, not to be despised in reading; by these, magnificently exercising his intellect, he also made known to posterity the memory of his name. Of these I have found only:

On the Sentences: three books.
Elegant sermons: one book.
Of the others I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Charles IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Petrus Berthorius, French by nation, of Poitiers by homeland, prior of the monastery of Saint Eligius at Paris, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man most studious in the divine writings, very learned, and notably instructed in secular letters, of excellent intellect in declaiming sermons to the people, an outstanding philosopher, historian, and cosmographer, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported. To Johannes de Pratis, bishop of Palestrina, cardinal of the holy Roman Church, he wrote a great and distinguished work, now printed in three volumes, not useless for the simpler preachers, which they call the Dictionary, but he himself wished it to be entitled:

Moral Repertory: three books. "To the reverend father in Christ, lord..."
Also Moral Reductory: four books. "See how not only in the..."
Moral Inductory: one book. "Considering the beginning of history..."
He wrote a Cosmography: one book.
Various sermons: two books. And many other things.

Finally, at the instance of Johannes, king of France, he translated Titus Livius into the French language.
