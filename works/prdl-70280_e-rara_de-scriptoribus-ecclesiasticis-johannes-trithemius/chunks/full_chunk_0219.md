*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 108

The Rule of his order: one book.
Sermons for the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
And certain other works.
Martin V confirmed this rule, newly compiled in thirty-two chapters. He flourished at the time of the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Dominicus de Sancto Geminiano, a most excellent jurisconsult, and nobly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in the other literature of secular disciplines, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain outstanding volumes in canon law, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is an outstanding and very celebrated work divided into two volumes: *Since the first gloss without*.
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals: one book.
And various declarations.
But the other works which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

Paulus de Sancta Maria, a Jew by birth, a Christian by faith, first bishop of Cartagena and afterward prelate of Burgos in Spain, arch-chancellor of the king of Castile and Leon, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures, very learned, master of sacred theology, converted to the Catholic faith in his mature age, advanced so much in life and doctrine that among all the doctors of Spain he had no equal in his time. He wrote to his son Alphonsus, dean of Compostela, doctor of laws, on the postils of Nicholas of Lyra:
Notable Additions: two books. *What do you wish for yourself, that you corrupt*.
The Scrutiny of the Scriptures: one book. *Search the Scriptures*.
And certain other works.
He flourished, and completed this Scrutiny of his, under the emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1433, in the twelfth indiction, in the eighty-first year of his age.

Petrus Paulus Vergerius, by homeland a Justinopolitan, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a philosopher, a most celebrated rhetorician, perfectly trained in Greek and Latin speech, [translated?] certain outstanding little works of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher teaching Greek letters at Venice, by which he left remembrance of himself to posterity. Among these there is an outstanding work containing, on the deeds of the lords of Carrara:
A very fine History: one book.
On Noble Manners: one book.
In Praise of Saint Jerome: one book.
And many other works.
He flourished at the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1421.

Mapheus Vegius, by homeland a man of Lodi, datary of Pope Martin V, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned, and most learned in secular letters, a most celebrated orator and poet, distinguished no less for conduct than for knowledge. Through his care and at his expense, the relics of Saint Monica, mother of Saint Augustine, were transferred from Ostia Tiberina to Rome and placed in a new chapel richly built by him, in which he himself is buried. He wrote, both in meter and in prose, certain outstanding volumes, among which there is a very celebrated work, necessary for all religious, composed with divine and human learning, addressed to certain sisters, which he entitled:
On Perseverance: seven books. *Since serious matters often, as happens*.
On the Penitential Psalms: one book.
In Praise of Saint Monica: one book.
The thirteenth book of the Aeneid, in heroic verse: one book.
Many epigrams: one book.
Many letters.
