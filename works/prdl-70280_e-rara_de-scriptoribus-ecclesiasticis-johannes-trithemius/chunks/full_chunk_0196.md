Philippus de Monte Galerio, of the Order of the Friars of Saint Francis, a man practiced in sacred Scripture, a great and distinguished preacher of the word of God, wrote little works in which, nobly exercising his talent, he also transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only the following:

Sermons for the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
Lenten sermons: one book.

He is further said to have composed various other treatises and to have explained some of the divine books, but none of them has come to me.

Johannes, surnamed Grossus, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, nineteenth prior general; others have twenty-first; by homeland a Toulousain; a studious and learned man. He wrote, in a clear style, volumes not to be despised, by which, having magnificently exercised his practiced talent, he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have seen only a distinguished work, which contains the foundation and progress of the Carmelite Order, entitled:

Viridarium: three books. “By what things [unclear].”

His sermons are reported: one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1400.

Nicolaus de Gorran, of the Order of the Friars Preachers of Saint Dominic, a man most studious in the holy Scriptures, nobly learned, outstanding in talent, published in a scholastic and clear style many volumes, the reading of which will not be to be rejected by those who desire to be instructed in the holy Scriptures. Of these I recall that few have come into our hands. They report, however, the following among many:

On the Sentences: four books.
On the five books of Moses: five books.
On Isaiah the prophet: one book.
On Jeremiah also: one book.
A large work on Ecclesiasticus: one book.
On the Song of Songs: one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew: one book.
On the Gospel of Mark: one book.
On the Gospel of Luke: one book.
On the Gospel of John: one book.
