[Flodoardus?], a monk of Reims, of the Order of Saint Benedict, learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote little works of reading not to be despised; by his study of these he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among them there is said to be an outstanding work from the founding of the city of Reims, which, he records, soldiers of Remus, killed by his brother Romulus, built after they had been put to flight, in memory of their lord, and so named it, down to his own times; in it he intersperses many things about the holy martyrs and other matters and deeds. He wished to give this volume the title:
*The Deeds of the Bishops of Reims*: three books.
Also *Lives of Many Saints*: one book.
He is said besides to have written many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Libertus, a monk of Lobbes and afterwards abbot of the monastery of Gembloux, teacher of Burchard, bishop of Worms, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very well trained in secular letters, while he was still a monk held the public school of the monks at Lobbes, in which he instructed Burchard, together with others, in both kinds of literature. Among other works of his talent he wrote:
*Lives of Several Saints*: one book.
Chants in honor of the saints.
And certain other things. He flourished in great religious devotion and discipline in the times of Emperor Otto II, in the year of the Lord 880.

Aribo, archbishop of Mainz, the nineteenth after the holy martyr Boniface, to whom Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, wrote a treatise on the Advent of the Lord, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, held a synod at Seligenstadt with Burchard, bishop of Worms, and other bishops and abbots of his, in the year of the Lord 1023, in the sixth indiction, and established many useful things, which are contained in authentic writings. He also wrote:
*On the Fifteen Gradual Psalms*: one book.
To Abbot Berno: one letter.
And certain letters to others.
He died under Conrad the Younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1031.

Burchard, formerly a monk of the monastery of Lobbes, of the Order of Saint Benedict, bishop of Worms in Germany, once a hearer and disciple at Lobbes of Albert, abbot of Gembloux, of whom mention is made in the canon, distinction 74, *In nomine domini*, a man most devoted to the divine writings and very learned, wrote from the opinions of the holy fathers, the general councils, and the supreme pontiffs a great and distinguished work after Isidore, namely:
*Decrees*, which he divided into twenty books. “In the New Testament after ...”
Letters to various persons: one book.
He flourished under the holy emperor Henry, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the diocese of Constance, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very nobly trained in secular letters, a musician and poet, second to none in his time, distinguished in life and conduct, wrote certain excellent volumes both in verse and in prose. Of these I have found only the following. To Pilgrim, archbishop of Cologne, he composed an excellent and useful volume:
*On Music or Tones*: one book. “Therefore every regular mon...”
*On the Instruments of Music*: one book. “That music is not to be despised...”
*On the Advent of the Lord, to Aribo*: one book. “At the summit of the highest dignity...”
Also *On the Office of the Mass*: one book.
