*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 44

On the Gospel of John: one book, “Among all the things of divine history …”

On all the epistles of Paul: twenty-four books, “To the venerable brother Lupus …”

On the canonical epistles: seven books.

On the Acts of the Apostles: one book.

On the Apocalypse of John: one book.

On the praise of the holy cross: one book, “The King of kings, the Lord … the world …”

To Bishop Haymo, On the Universe: twenty-two books, “First among the Hebrews of God …”

On universal nature: one book, “The discussion of the universe …”

On the instruction of clerics: three books, “To the lord, most reverend and religious Haistulf … Because you, venerable …”

On the divine offices: one book.

On computus, a dialogue: one book.

To King Louis: one book.

On questions of the canons: one book.

To Bishop Reginald: one book.

On the blessings of the patriarchs: one book.

Letters to various persons: one book.

Of almost innumerable sermons: one book. He also published many other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He died under the princes Louis and Lothar, in the year of the Lord 855, in the third indiction, in the ninth year of his episcopate. He was buried in the monastery of Saint Alban near Mainz, not without a reputation for sanctity; I have read his epitaph in nearly twenty verses.

Bellator, a priest of a certain church, zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, rich in the reading of the ancients, distinguished in talent and eloquence, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, is said to have expounded many volumes on the holy Scriptures, in which he magnificently exercised his talent. There survives, among the rest, his notable volume, which he published at the request of friends:

On the Book of Wisdom: seven books.

I have found nothing about the others. He says that he flourished in the times of Emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 841.

Strabus, a monk of Fulda, German by nation, formerly a hearer and scribe of Abbot Rabanus, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in the studies of secular letters, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote no few small works in exposition of the sacred volumes; in these, showing himself a learned man, he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Therefore, imitating his teacher Abbot Rabanus, he wrote:

On Genesis: one book.

On Exodus: one book.

A very brief treatment of the Book of Leviticus.

On Leviticus: one book.

And many other works. This Strabus, indeed, is remembered as the first to have brought together, from the sayings of the holy fathers, the gloss which is now called ordinary, on the whole Bible; others afterwards enlarged it with many added judgments of the fathers. He flourished under Emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Lupus Servatus, priest, to whom Rabanus, abbot of Fulda, is read to have written eight books on the epistle of Paul the Apostle to the Romans, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and, according to the age of his time, abundantly skilled in secular letters, wrote some small works of his talent, of which very few have been able to come to my knowledge. There survives his volume:

On Three Questions: one book.

And certain letters, as well as other works. He flourished under Lothar, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 850.

John, called Erigena, a monk learned in the divine Scriptures, most learned in the discipline of secular letters, fully instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, subtle in talent, composed in speech, at the command of Charles, son of Louis …
