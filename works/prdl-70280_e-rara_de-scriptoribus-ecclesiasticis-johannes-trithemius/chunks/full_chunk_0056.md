He also published many other works; if anyone desires to know them more fully, let him read the list of his books edited by Possidius, from which we too have taken many items. Jacobus de Voragine, bishop of Genoa, writes in his treatise which he composed on the books of Augustine that the number of his books, treatises, sermons, and letters rises above one thousand and thirty. And he concludes thus: And therefore no one has ever been found who could discover all the books of Augustine. And Possidius says: He lies who professes that he has read you entire, Augustine. But in this work I have deliberately omitted certain treatises which are ascribed by very many to blessed Augustine. Among these is the book *On Spirit and Soul*, in whose thirty-seventh chapter Boethius is cited, who was born a long time after Augustine. Moreover, the style itself does not smell of Augustine. Likewise the book *On True and False Penitence*, which has now been printed under Augustine’s name, is falsely ascribed to him, since in the seventeenth chapter, near the end, Augustine himself is cited. The book *On the Conflict of Virtues and Vices*, which begins “The apostolic voice,” both echoes the style of Pope Gregory and agrees with the older copies. Likewise the book *On the Ladder of Paradise*, whose beginning is “When one day,” is inscribed in ancient copies to blessed Bernard, and seems to display his spirit and manner of speech. Why should I strive after many details? So many are the treatises and sermons falsely inscribed to blessed Augustine that a review of them would require its own volume. Nor is it for me, as I think, to sit in judgment and deprive of their titles works that are another’s. Let the prudent reader carefully consider what he should do or hold in these matters.

The holy doctor Augustine died in the city of Hippo, while it was being besieged by the Vandals, in the year of the Lord 430, in the fourteenth indiction, in the seventy-sixth year of his age and the fortieth of his priesthood, according to Marianus Scotus, on the fifth day before the Kalends of September, in the third month of the city’s siege; amid the assaults of the besieging Vandals, at the very end of his days, he was answering the books of Julian the Manichaean and gloriously persevering in defense of the Christian faith. He made no will, because he had nothing from which to make one, except the church’s library, which even at his death he most diligently commended to the brothers to be preserved. Poor in possessions, rich in merits and knowledge, Augustine the bishop rested in the Lord; Possidius, bishop of Calama, wrote his life.
