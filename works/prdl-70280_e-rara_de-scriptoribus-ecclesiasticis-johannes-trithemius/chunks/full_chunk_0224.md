*Book [unclear].* Johannes Gritsch, German by nation, from Basel by homeland, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed both in canon law and in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, of a most celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, wrote for preachers of the word of God some little works not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among them these are reported: Sermons on the season, one book; Sermons on the saints, one book; Sermons through Lent, one book, “When you fast,” etc., according to Matthew; On the Passion of the Lord, one book; and certain others. He flourished, as his own people say, in the times of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Johannes Nider, of the Order of Friars Preachers, German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently taught in scholastic philosophy, of excellent and distinguished talent in delivering sermons to the people, an inquisitor of heretical depravity, a most keen investigator of the little women whom the common people call witches, wrote in clear speech a number of volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among them these are reported: On Merchants’ Contracts, one book, “Since the office of merchants,” etc.; On Moral Leprosy, one book, “Once, when we read in Leviticus”; On the Precepts of the Decalogue, one book, “The most sacred law of the Decalogue”; On Ants Morally, five books, “Often traversing those which”; On the Consolation of a Fearful Conscience, one book, “Among disciplines we find”; Mirror of Perfection, one book, “Behold your king,” etc.; Three [unclear]; Sermons on the Season, one book, “Come after me,” etc.; Three [unclear]; Sermons on the Saints, one book, “Turn ye to,” etc.; [unclear]; Lenten Sermons, one book; and many other things.

Nicolaus Siculus, abbot of Monacensis, of the Order of Saint Benedict, sprung from the city of Catania, called de Tudeschis, first auditor of cases of the lord pope’s Rota, afterward archbishop of Palermo, and finally cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, the most celebrated jurist of all, subtle and acute in talent, ready and cautious in counsel, clear and orderly in speech, who is deservedly called the lamp of the law, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported: On the Decree, unfinished, one book, “Since every principle of the heavenly”; On the Decretals, five books; For the Council of Basel, one book, “Since the truth of the words”; Judicial Process, one book, “The peace-making king of all”; One Hundred and Four Counsels, one book, “The circumstance of the fact is such”; Various Disputations, one book.
