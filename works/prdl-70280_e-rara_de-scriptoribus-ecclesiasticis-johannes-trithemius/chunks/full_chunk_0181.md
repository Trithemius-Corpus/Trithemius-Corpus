Alphonsus, bishop of Seville, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man very learned both in divine Scripture and in secular letters, and among the ecclesiastical doctors most celebrated in his own time, distinguished in intellect and eloquence. He published some volumes of great authority, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these I have found only the subtle and notable work:

On the Sentences, four books.

Of the rest I have learned nothing.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Johannes de Bachone, an Englishman, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in divine Scripture and excellently instructed both in canon law and in secular philosophy; in the school of Paris he shone with no less distinction in conduct than in learning, and afterwards, promoted to provincial of England, he vigorously governed his order by word and example. He wrote many notable volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Among them the following are reported:

On the Gospel of Matthew, one book.
On the Gospel of Mark, one book.
On the Gospel of Luke, one book.
On the Gospel of John, one book.
On the Epistles of Paul, one book.
On the Acts of the Apostles, one book.
On the Canonical Epistles, one book.
On the Apocalypse, one book.
On the Sentences, one book.
Various Quodlibets, one book.
Mirror of His Order, one book.
Defense of His Order, one book.
Against the Jews, one book.
On the Poverty of Christ, one book.
On the Rule of His Order, one book.
And many others.

He also published explanations on the books of blessed Augustine On the Holy Trinity and The City of God, and of Anselm, Why God Became Man and On the Incarnation of the Word; in all these he exercised his talent magnificently. He flourished in the times of Charles, the fourth emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Johannes Eligerus de Gonderbleuen, a German by nation, once a hearer of Johannes de Saxonia, the most distinguished astronomer; a man most learned in secular letters, a philosopher and astronomer most celebrated among all those of his time, and sufficiently imbued with divine Scripture; subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote volumes in the mathematical science that are not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Among them the following are reported:

On the Construction of the Astrolabe, one book.
On the Usefulness of the Astrolabe, one book.
On the Usefulness of the Quadrant, one book.
Also On the Magnet, one book.
On the Astrogemeter, one book.

Ptolemy, king of the stars.
With principal zeal and understanding.
The uses of the new quadrant.
About to treat of the magnet.
The highest [unclear] for us.
And several others.

He flourished in the times of Charles, the fourth emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.
