Angelus Saxo of Brunswick, a German by nationality, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, keen in intellect, distinguished in life, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, since after obtaining the magisterium of teaching in the school at Erfurt he performed the office of preacher for a long time at Mainz. Among other things he wrote a certain notable work:

On the Canon of the Mass: one book.
Various sermons: one book.
Diverse questions: one book.
And certain other things.

He died at Strasbourg, buried in the monastery of the penitents, under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1481, in the fourteenth indiction.

Franciscus Philelfus, born from the ancient town of Tolentino, a golden knight and poet laureate, a philosopher and very celebrated orator, once in his youth at Venice a pupil of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher, from whom he learned Greek and Latin eloquence most perfectly; a man so studious of the good arts, an untiring lover and cultivator of them, that, like another Pliny, amid the continual occupations of wars he was always either reading something to himself or dictating something to others. For he wrote in both languages, in prose as well as in verse, many excellent volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of a very celebrated intellect. Of these there survive:

Eutyphro, on piety: one book.
On moral discipline: eight books.
Life of Pope Nicholas V: two books.
A consolatory work to a friend: one book.
On the diversity of laws: one book.
The Sforziad, in heroic verse: nine books.
Satires, in the same meter: ten books.
On jests and serious matters, in hexameter: six books.
Lyric poems in every kind of meter: six books.
Various orations, sixty: one book. Incipit: “Since I desire something to...”
Latin letters: twenty-one books. Incipit: “Because of many laws...”
Greek letters: thirty-nine books.
In Greek verses, in odes: three books.
Banquets, in prose: two books.
Also On Exile: three books.
And many other works.

Besides these he translated many things into Latin, among which the following are reported: Aristotle’s Rhetoric to King Alexander: one book. From Plato: two books. Hippocrates, On winds and the sufferings of the body: two books. Of Latin letters: the lives of Theseus, Galba, and Otho. Also Apophthegms: two books. And many other things.
