*On Ecclesiastical Writers, fol. 65.*

Sermons on the saints: 2 books.

He also published many other things which have not come to my knowledge. He wrote the said sermons for the cycle of the year: first, indeed, brief ones, briefly expounding the Gospel and Apostolic readings. Afterwards, however, he published other sermons, both on Sundays and on feast days, so very lengthy, adducing so many testimonies of the Scriptures, that they can scarcely be committed to memory; yet these have now in part lost their value as newer ones have come upon the scene.

Raymundus de Pennaforte, a Catalan by nation, or a provincial, of the Order of Preachers, the third master general, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, ready in talent, distinguished for knowledge of canon law, made his name immortal by writing various works; by his little works he bound the Church to himself as a perpetual debtor, if I may so speak. For at the command of Pope Gregory IX he compiled:

The Decretals of law: 5 books. “The peaceful king, by pious mercy...”

A Summa on cases: 3 books. “Since, as Jerome says, the second...”

The Constitutions of his order: 1 book.

And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1230.

Albertus Galioti, by homeland a Parmesan, professor and famous interpreter of canon laws, subtle in talent, cautious in counsel, scholastic in speech, who on account of his learning and experience in affairs was held among the foremost doctors by the old jurists, wrote certain little works in law not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with immortal praise. Of these there is extant a summa:

The Pearl of Questions: 1 book. “Since I, Albertus Galioti...”

And Declarations of Judgments: 1 book.

And certain other diverse works, unknown to us, are possessed by various people.

Odofredus of Benevento, hearer of Azo of Bologna, afterwards teacher of Jacobus Baldewinus, a man most skilled in both laws, subtle in talent, clear in speech, who left many distinguished pupils after him, on account of his learning and erudition both in law and in other humane letters was held in great authority among the ancients. Many little works of his are reported, by which he made his name known to posterity:

On the Formation of Libelli: 1 book. “After the work of notarial practice...”

On Judicial Procedure: 1 book. “In the manner in which, Christ favoring...”

On the Art of the Notariate: 1 book.

And other things which I have not seen.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1230.

Johannes de Blanosco, a Burgundian by nation, a man most learned in both laws, sufficiently instructed in the precepts of secular philosophers, sharp and ready in talent, wrote in clear language little works not to be spurned by professors of this faculty; by these the fame of his name became known far and wide, both to those then present and to those who would come afterwards. Of these there is extant a volume of great usefulness, which he entitled:

On the Actions of Advocates: 1 book. “I, Johannes de Blano...”

And various questions.

The rest, however, which he composed, have by no means come to my knowledge.

Johannes by name, a German by nation, born from the city of Freiburg, bishop of Bosnia in Hungary, of the Order of Preachers, a man much practiced and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently skilled in secular law and philosophy, who on account of his erudition and the holiness of his life had been taken from the order to the episcopate; afterwards abandoning it, he returned to his order.
