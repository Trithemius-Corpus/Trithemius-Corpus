He included an epitaph of Berengar, once his teacher, in fifty-two verses. He also wrote many other things still unknown to us.
He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1090.

Anselm, archbishop of Canterbury in England, formerly abbot of the monastery of Bec in Normandy, of the Order of Saint Benedict, once a hearer and disciple of Lanfranc, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular [unclear] and skill second to none in his time; upright and most holy in life and conduct; devout in mind; eloquent in speech; vigorous in action; angelic in countenance; mature in bearing; exemplary in his course of life; constant in the study of the Scriptures; and adorned and distinguished by every virtue.
