Incentius Gruner, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, brilliant in intellect, scholastic in speech, published little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there survives a notable lecture, which he publicly wrote and read before the students at the new foundation of the Leipzig academy:
On the office of the Mass, three books, *To the honor of the blessed...*
Disputed questions, one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished at the time of the transfer of the University of Prague to Leipzig, into the province of the Margraviate of Meissen; Alexander V, pope, confirmed it and endowed it with privileges, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Johannes de Aurbach, German by nation, priest of the church of Bamberg, a man zealously learned in the divine Scriptures, brilliant in intellect, scholastic in speech, published little works of authority not to be dismissed, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have seen only a distinguished work:
On the sacraments of the Church, one book, *To the praise of God and the salvation of souls...*
Of the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished in the city of Bamberg and wrote various little works.

Dionysius de Burgo Sancti Sepulcri, of the order of the Hermit Friars of the divine father Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote little works not to be scorned in each literature, by which he left perpetual monuments of his intellect to posterity. Of these the following are reported: very beautiful and elegant commentaries:
On Valerius Maximus, one book.
On the Metamorphoses, [unclear] book.
On the works of Virgil, [unclear] book.
On the tragedies of Seneca, [unclear] book.
On the Politics, [unclear] book.
On the Rhetoric, [unclear] book.
And certain other things.
Moreover he reduced all the fables to a pleasing tropological sense. He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1412.

Petrus de Aliaco, bishop of Cambrai and cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, once chancellor of the Paris academy, teacher of Johannes de Gerson, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, especially in Aristotelian philosophy, excellent in intellect, brilliant in speech, and also an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name and intellect. Of these I have found the following:
On the Sentences, four books, *What doctrine is this...*
On the praise of Holy Scripture, one book, *Upon this rock build...*
On the beginning of Mark, one book, *What doctrine is this...*
On the stability of the Church, two books, *Whether the Church of Peter by law...*
The mirror of consideration, three books, *Whoever from the trouble of the world...*
A compendium of contemplation, three books, *Since many wise men...*
On the spiritual ladder, one book, *There are four spiritual...*
Briefly on the Psalter, one book, *A shortened word he made...*
Meditations on Psalm 70, one book, *O Lord my God, it terrifies me...*
