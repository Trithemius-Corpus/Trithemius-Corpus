John, bishop of Lombariensis, German by nation, Basel by homeland, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and renowned for eloquence, a most celebrated preacher of sermons, published some little works of no contemptible reading, by which he handed down the memory of his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Among these, the principal are reported to be:

On the Sentences: four books.
Various sermons: one book.
And certain other works.

He is said besides to have delivered many orations and conferences to the clergy, and also before the supreme pontiff and the assembly of cardinals, which I have not seen.

John Zacharias, German by nation, Erfurt by homeland, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and very learned, also an excellent interpreter of secular philosophy, subtle in talent and a very acute disputant, very capable in delivering sermons both to the clergy and to the people, wrote certain excellent little works by which he made his name known. Of these are reported:

On the Sentences: four books.
On Genesis: one book.
On Exodus: one book.
On Leviticus: one book.
On the Epistles of Paul: fourteen books.
Various sermons: one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, and at the Council of Constance he was magnificently praised for disputing against heretics, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Antonius de Butrio, Italian by nation, Bologna by homeland, easily the most celebrated jurist of all in his time, and exceptionally learned in other writings, both divine and human, renowned in talent and eloquence, and no less venerable for honorable conduct than for learning, published little works on the laws, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Decretals: five books.
On the Clementines: one book.
A repertory of canon law: one book.
A repertory of civil law: one book.
Most elegant counsels: one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of the general Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord [1427?].

Gabriel of Spoleto, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent and an outstanding preacher of sermons, wrote several commendable little works, by which he showed the knowledge of his lively talent also to posterity. But I have not hitherto deserved to see any of them. Yet the chief works of his are reported to be:

Against the heretics: one book.
Against the Fraticelli: one book.
