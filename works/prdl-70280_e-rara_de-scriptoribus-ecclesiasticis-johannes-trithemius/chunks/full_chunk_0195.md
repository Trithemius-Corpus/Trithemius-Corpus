*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 96

Prevented by death, he did not complete this divine work of the new dictionary, but brought it through only from A as far as C inclusive, in three volumes. He died in the last year of the emperor Wenceslaus, under Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1416, on the Kalends of December, in the seventh indiction. And, honorably buried before the high altar of his convent at Bologna, he rests in the peace of Christ.

Philippus Ferrariensis, of Toulouse, bishop of the church of Pax in Spain, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, an excellent declaimer of sermons, ready in talent, distinguished in eloquence, once preaching in the kingdom of Sicily, converted many from iniquity and brought forth great fruits of his learning for God. He wrote elegant works:

Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
And certain other things.

There are some who write that, because of his exceptional learning and the uprightness of his life, he was raised to the honor of the cardinalate; of this I am not certain.

Alterus Disse, English by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, nuncio and legate of Pope Boniface IX in the kingdoms of England, Spain, Portugal, and Aragon, in the parts of Gascony, and in the other lands and kingdoms, preaching the cross against infidel schismatics; a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, an excellent declaimer of sermons. He wrote not-to-be-despised little works, by which he made his name known. Of these there are extant:

Against the Lollards, one book.
On the schism also, one book.
On some psalms, one book.
Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Gerardus Zutphaniensis, German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant also of secular letters, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence, and no less to be preferred for his manner of life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures. He published certain little works pertaining to the instruction of devout brothers, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

On Interior Reformation, one book, “A certain man went down from...”
On Spiritual Ascent, one book, “Blessed is the man whose help is...”
Various sermons, one book.
Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

From his writings it appears that he was a devout and religious man, of some order, though it is not known by us. There are indeed those who write that he was a canon regular; we pass this over as probable. He flourished in the school of Cologne. He wrote various things.

Johannes de Hisdinio, of the order of the brothers of the Hospital of Saint John of Jerusalem, a man learned in the divine writings, not ignorant of secular philosophy, a distinguished doctor of sacred Scripture, an excellent declaimer of sermons, acute in talent and composed in eloquence. He published many splendid volumes; at the University of Paris he delivered many elegant lectures. Of these the following are extant:

On the Gospel of Mark, one book, “He was made like a lion in...”
On the Gospel of John, one book.
On the Epistle to the Romans, one book.
On the Epistles to the Corinthians, one book.
On the Epistle to the Galatians, one book.
