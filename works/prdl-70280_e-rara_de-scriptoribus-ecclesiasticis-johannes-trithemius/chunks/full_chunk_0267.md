Baptista Guarinus of Verona, son of Guarinus, a most learned man, of whom we have made mention in his proper place: certainly a man not inferior to his father, but most learned in every branch of letters and in the discipline of the liberal arts, a philosopher, orator, and poet most celebrated among all, very skilled in the Greek and Latin languages and very eloquent in both. He wrote and translated many excellent little works, by which he commends to those now living and to posterity the almost divine force and keenness of his talent. Among these the following are reported:

A very beautiful work of dialogues.
On the Epicurean sect, one book.
On the order of teaching, one book.
On administering a kingdom, one book.
On Cicero’s Letters, one book.
On Ovid’s Fasti, one book.
On Lucan’s Pharsalia, one book.
On the poet Catullus, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Several orations also, one book.
And poems of various kinds.

He translated the following into Latin: from Xenophon, the Praise of Agesilaus, which he dedicated to Hermolaus Barbarus the elder, bishop of Verona; also certain orations of Demosthenes and of Dio the historian; and also some orations of Gregory Nazianzen to Christ [and] Mary. He published and translated many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge. He is distinguished to this day at Ferrara, where, succeeding his father, he has now for thirty-three years taught both languages most brilliantly, and he is writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction. He himself has a son learned in Greek and Latin, following the discipline of his forefathers.

Angelus Politianus, a man skilled in every respect and most learned, a celebrated grammarian, a distinguished philosopher, orator, and poet, perfectly equipped in Greek and Latin eloquence, excellent in talent, who for a long time illuminated Italy by his learning and teaching. He wrote various excellent volumes, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

Certain Centuries, one book.
On the errors of the Latin language, one book.
Various letters to various persons, one book.

And he is said to have written certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge. This man dared to present Homer’s Iliad in verse for Latins to read. He is still distinguished in Italy, as I have learned, writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.
