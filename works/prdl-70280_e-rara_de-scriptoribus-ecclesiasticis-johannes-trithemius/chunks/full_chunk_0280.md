Theodericus of Osnabrück, by nation a German, of the Order of Friars Minor, of the province of Cologne, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures, a most famous declaimer of popular sermons, whose life all who knew him at this time proclaim to be most holy: in making sermons to the people he is illumined by divine grace so greatly, and in short moves his hearers with such compunction and sweetness, that among the Germans he has had no one like him. Hence he was also appointed general preacher throughout the whole province. Recently, when he was with me at Cologne, he read to me a distinguished little work, suited to devotion, which he had composed at the urging of Hermann, archbishop of Cologne: On the Passion of the Lord, one book, “To the most reverend in Christ”; Manual for the Simple, one book, “Because, as Augustine”; On Interior Exercise, one book, “He who shall have persevered”; and certain others. He is still living in the province of Cologne, vigorously applying himself to the office he has begun, under King Maximilian and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Bartholomaeus of Cologne, by nation a German, a man excellently learned in secular letters, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, a philosopher and very distinguished poet, whose poems even the most learned poets both admire and extol with praises: he wrote in both styles some little works worthy to be celebrated, by which he made his name known. Of these I have so far seen only a little work composed in meter on the praises of philosophy, which he entitled: Grove of Poems, one book, “Why gold before Pluto”; On the Sect of Diogenes, one book, “Whoever longs in Cynic fashion.” And I have seen nothing of the others. He is living to this day at Deventer, composing various things, under King Maximilian and Alexander VI, the supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Johannes Thurotz, a Pannonian, a man devoted to and practiced in secular letters, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent and renowned in eloquence: he is said to have fashioned some little works, by which he made his name known. Of these there exists a volume not to be rejected, which with great industry he compiled, containing the Chronicle of Hungary, three books, “In the continual leisure of these years for us”; and certain others. He is said still to be living and to be composing various things, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1493.
