Michael Berbrant of Düren, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a German by nation, lecturer in sacred theology and prior of the convent of Kreuznach in the diocese of Mainz, a learned man and so distinguished a preacher that, summoned not only in the neighborhood but also to distant places and cathedral churches, he preached to the admiration of all. He was in great familiarity and authority with John, the last count of Sponheim, and obtained many benefits from him for his convent. Among other things he wrote elegant works:

Sermons on the season: 1 book.
Sermons on the saints: 1 book.
Synodal conferences: 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Petrus of Speyer, of the Order of the Hermit Brothers of Saint Augustine, a German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and splendidly instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and famous for eloquence, very highly regarded in public opinion for preaching sermons to the people. He wrote certain excellent little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among these are reported:

Sermons on the season: 1 book.
Sermons on the saints: 1 book.
On the books of the Ethics: 7 books.
But the other things that he composed have not yet come to our knowledge by reading.

Einardus of Fronthoven, a German by nation, of the Order, as they report, of the Friars Preachers, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and skilled in them by long practice, and not ignorant of secular literature. He wrote works not without usefulness for preachers of the word of God, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these I have seen only:

Sermons on the season: 1 book.
