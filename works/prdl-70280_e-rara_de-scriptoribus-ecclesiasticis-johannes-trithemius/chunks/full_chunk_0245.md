Bernardus Justinianus, a Venetian by homeland, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, who, because of his singular grace in speaking, was held in esteem not only among his own Venetian senate, but also among the Roman pontiffs, cardinals, and the other princes of Italy and senators of cities. Among the other monuments of his talent he wrote, on Laurentius Justinianus, Venetian patriarch, his maternal uncle:

The Life and Most Holy Conduct, one book.
Also Histories, one book.
Several elegant Orations, one book.
Letters and certain other things.

He flourished under Frederick, the third emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1470.

Henricus de Piro, a German by nation, born from the city of Cologne, of the order of the Carthusians, professed of the house of Saint Barbara in the same city; before entering the order a subtle and famous doctor of both laws, and afterward also practiced in the divine Scriptures. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, of which the following are reported:

On the Institutes, three books. “With the lawgiver of all granting me...”
Also, To Novices, one book.
Counsels in Various Cases, one book.
Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Frederick, the third emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Nicolaus Donis, a German by nation, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man studious and learned in secular letters, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished philosopher and mathematician, a most vigilant discoverer and most sagacious restorer of Ptolemy’s Cosmography, excellent in talent and clear in eloquence. He wrote volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Among them there exists a wonderful and very celebrated work, with pictures and new tables most elegantly arranged and most diligently corrected:

On the Cosmography of Ptolemy, to Pope Paul II, seven books. “It does not escape me, most blessed father...”
Also On Marvelous Places, one book. “Since, as Augustine says...”
Various Letters to different persons.
And certain other things.

He flourished, moreover, under Frederick, the third emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Georgius de Burbach, a German by nation, a man very experienced in secular letters and knowledge, and not ignorant of the holy Scriptures, a philosopher and easily the most brilliant astronomer of all men of his time. In the university of Vienna he publicly taught philosophy and the science of the stars for a long time; there, among countless other pupils, he instructed Johannes de Regiomonte, afterward a most learned man. He wrote certain distinguished little works of penetrating talent. Of their number I have seen only:

The New Theorics of the Planets, one book. “The sun has three orbs from itself...”

Of the others I have as yet seen nothing. He flourished under Frederick, the third emperor, and the Roman pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1470.
