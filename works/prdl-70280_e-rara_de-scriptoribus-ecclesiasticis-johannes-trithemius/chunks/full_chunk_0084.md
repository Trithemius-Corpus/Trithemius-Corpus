Maximus, bishop of the city of Saragossa, learned in the divine Scriptures and not moderately erudite in secular [letters], prompt in talent and polished in eloquence, excelling in verse [and] prose, published in either style quite illustrious volumes, which have as yet by no means come to the notice of our reading. But there is reported [as] his a notable volume, a pleasant work:
On the deeds of the Goths in Spain: 1 book.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Phocas, in the year of the Lord 610.

Jonas, a monk, disciple of Saint Columbanus the abbot, companion of his pilgrimage, a Scot by nation, a studious man, erudite, renowned for knowledge, no less venerable for religion than for learning, wrote in composed speech, at the request of the brothers, for the edification of many:
The Life of Saint Columbanus: 1 book.
The Life of Saint Attala the monk: 1 book.
The Life of Saint Eustasius the abbot: 1 book.
He flourished under the emperor Heraclius, in the year of the Lord 630.

Leontius, bishop of the city of Neapolis on the island of Cyprus, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, also most renowned in life and conversation, a prelate worthy of God. He wrote, among the other little works of his talent, a notable volume on the acts [and] admirable conversation of that patriarch of Alexandria who, on account of [his] incredible piety [and] compassion toward the poor, was surnamed
John the Almsgiver: 1 book.
Of the rest I have found nothing.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Phocas, in the year of the Lord 610.

Isidore the Younger, bishop of Seville after Leander, a man most erudite in the divine Scriptures, second to none of his time in secular letters, subtle in talent, clear in sense, composed in eloquence, excelling in verse [and] prose, shone forth no less distinguished for sanctity than for learning. He is called "the Younger" to distinguish him from the elder, bishop of Cordoba, of whom we made mention above, or also from another, bishop of Seville, who preceded him. This Isidore the Younger wrote many illustrious little works, of which I have found only the following. To Braulio the bishop:
