Guarino of Verona, once a pupil of Chrysoloras the Byzantine philosopher, a man not ignorant of Greek letters and most skilled in the Greek and Latin languages, by whose labors the studies of the good arts were stirred up and recalled into the light, studies which seemed weakened and already almost extinct. He taught first at Venice, afterwards at Ferrara; in both places he had very celebrated pupils. He wrote to Prince Leonello a work entitled *On the Difference Between a Flatterer and a Friend*, one book; also a short *Grammar*, one book; a *Comparison of Scipio and Gaius*, one book; *Letters to Various Persons*, sixteen books; several *Orations*, one book; many poems; and not a few other works. By command of Pope Nicholas V he translated into Latin the *Geography* of Strabo, a work no less useful than delightful. From Plutarch he made Latin versions of the lives of many illustrious men; but he translated Plutarch himself *On the Education of Children*, along with many other works, most elegantly; these are in part extant. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460, in the eighth indiction, on the day before the Nones of December. He was buried at Ferrara in the convent of the Carmelites, honorably endowed with a marble tomb.

Heinrich Kalteisen, archbishop of the churches of Nidaros and Caesarea, of the Order of Preachers, German by nation, a native of Koblenz, where he entered the order; first lector of the Sacred Palace, preacher of the Holy Cross, and inquisitor of heretical depravity; afterwards, as we have said, consecrated by apostolic authority as chief bishop of two provinces: by his learning and preaching he became known as famous throughout the whole world. Since he was a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, he wrote, among other notable works: *Sermons on the Season*, one book; *Sermons on the Saints*, one book; *Sermons Through Lent*, one book; *On the Magnificat*, one book; *Various Questions*, one book; *Addresses to the Clergy*; and several other works. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1465, in the fourteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Nones of October. He was buried in his convent at Koblenz.
