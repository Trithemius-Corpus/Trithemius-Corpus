John, monk and priest of Damascus, a learned and holy man, about whom wonderful things are related because of the excellence of his teaching and the sincerity of his life, held in great esteem at Constantinople and appointed prelate of the monks, turned many away from wickedness. He wrote in Greek some treatises of profound meaning, but few of them have come into our hands. I have read only that distinguished work:

On the tradition of the orthodox faith: three books. “No one has ever seen God.”
The History of Barlaam and Josaphat: one book.
Of the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished under Theodosius, the most devout prince, in the year of the Lord 490.

Iconius the African, whom blessed Augustine records in the third book On Christian Doctrine to have belonged to the Donatist party, although in certain matters he disagrees with them, was certainly an acute man and much practiced in the divine Scriptures. He wrote not a few treatises, of which these are reported, namely:

On internal war: three books.
An expository work on various causes: one book.
On the Apocalypse of John: one book.
Rules of Sacred Scripture: one book.
Several letters to various persons,
and many other works.
He flourished when Theodosius and his sons were ruling, in the year of the Lord 390.

Heraclides the monk, a man holy in conduct and distinguished in learning in the divine Scriptures, a most diligent [unclear] of the holy fathers, anchorites and monks, gathering the flowers of virtues into the little garden of his heart, produced the honey of spiritual sweetness. Among other things he wrote, for the edification of readers, a distinguished work on the life and conduct of the holy fathers living in the wilderness or in monasteries under a rule, whose title is:

Paradise: one book.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Gratian, in the year of the Lord 380.

Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, a man very studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, sufficiently imbued also with secular letters, and no less worthy of the priesthood by his manner of life than by his knowledge of the Scriptures, wrote among other things the useful work:

On the Holy Spirit: one book.
Of the rest which he is said to have composed, nothing has come to my knowledge.
He flourished in the times of the devout emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390.

Epiphanius, bishop of Salamis, a city of Cyprus, a man sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures and of such holiness that he deserved to be illumined with the spirit of prophecy and to become a performer of many miracles. He was a very close friend of blessed Jerome and wrote several letters to him. If anyone desires to know his deeds, let him read the Tripartite History. He composed some treatises, of which these are reported:
