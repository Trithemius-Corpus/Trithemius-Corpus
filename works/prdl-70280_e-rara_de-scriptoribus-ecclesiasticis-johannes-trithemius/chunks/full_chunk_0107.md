Guimundus, archbishop of Aversa, formerly a monk of the monastery of the Cross of Saint Leufredus, of the order of the most holy father Benedict, a man devoted to and very skilled in the divine Scriptures, and also not ignorant of secular letters, wrote to Roger the monk against the errors of Berengar of Tours:

*On the Body and Blood of the Lord*, three books. “For the matter necessary in these times.”

And certain other works not seen by us.

He flourished under Emperor Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1060.

Alphanus, archbishop of Salerno, formerly a monk of the monastery of Cassino, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man nobly learned in the divine Scriptures and perfectly instructed in secular letters, a philosopher and theologian of most celebrated reputation, wrote certain little works of profound meaning, in which, exercising his talent, he also made his name known to posterity. Of these there survive:

*On the Union of the Word of God and Man*, one book.

*On the Union of Body and Soul*, one book.

*On the Four Humors of the Body*, one book.

He also composed other volumes in every branch of literature, which have not at all come down to us.

Campanus, a Lombard by nation, a philosopher and astronomer, in everyone’s opinion most celebrated in his time, acute in talent, scholastic in speech, and also not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished calculator and computist, produced not a few little works of his talent, the reading of which will not be contemptible to the rulers of churches. Of these I have read only the following, a great and distinguished work:

*On the Ecclesiastical Computus*, one book. “One of these asked me.”

*On the Composition of the Quadrant*, one book. “You should know that the circle [unclear].”

Also a *Calendar*, one book.

He also composed many other works in astronomy, which have not come into our hands.

He is said to have flourished under King Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1030.

Helpericus, a monk of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a philosopher, poet, and distinguished astronomer, subtle in talent and composed in speech, wrote some little works both in meter and in prose. Of these I have been able to find only the excellent work:

*On the Ecclesiastical Computus*, one book. “When, as an adolescent, with our brothers.”

Also *On Music*, one book.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Emperor Henry III, in the year 1069.

[Berthorius?], abbot of the monastery of Cassino, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the sacred Scriptures and nobly instructed in the discipline of secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and physician. Before his conversion he is said to have written many little works in his faculty. But afterward, changing his study and being made a physician of the soul as well, he wrote for the edification of his brothers:

*Elegant Sermons*, one book.

He also produced other additional and various treatises, knowledge of which has not come to me.

Rogerius, a monk of the order of Saint Benedict, to whom Guimundus of Aversa, bishop, while still established in the monastery, is said to have written three books against Berengar, a learned man and instructed in both divine and secular letters, described for the edification of many, in sufficiently lucid speech:

*The Life of Bruno, Bishop of Cologne*, one book.

And certain other works. He flourished under Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord 1040.
