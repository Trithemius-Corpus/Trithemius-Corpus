As for conferences, both those delivered in general chapters and in observantial chapters, or on other occasions, without a fixed number, some people have them, and some have been withdrawn. These, then, are the things, apart from those which were still unfinished in my hands, which I recall having written at present: in the thirty-fourth year of my age, the tenth of my assumption of the office of abbot, and the twelfth of my conversion, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and the supreme pontiff Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord, in which I have now for the third time corrected, enlarged, and completed this catalogue, having labored on it for almost seven years, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

And now, therefore, after many labors at last, shall I put an end to our work? For although many illustrious writers remain worthy of the catalogue, yet it was impossible for me to find even the names alone of them all. But those of whom I had certain knowledge I have, as far as I could, carefully recorded; nor have I knowingly omitted anyone whose little works, ability, and times I could have known. Nor am I unaware that very many highly learned men survive today, composing various works, of whom I make no mention: not because I judged them unworthy of our catalogue, but because they preferred either to conceal their little works, or these have not been so far multiplied as to be able to come to my knowledge. I have done as much as I could, not as much as I wished, since, had it been permitted, I would have brought together for myself all writers, however many there are or ever have been. I leave that province to those who come after, that they may add from their own vein where I, fatigued, have stopped. Moreover, I ask you, enrolled fathers, who possess the heavenly kingdoms, who adorn this catalogue of ours with your names, abilities, and labors by lamplight: if I have written true, just, worthy, and finally pleasing things about you, pray to the clemency of almighty God for us, that he may wash away our faults, grant pardon, confirm our holy purpose, illumine the understanding, purify the affections, and mercifully make us sharers in your joys. But if in some matters I have been less able than the matter itself required, grant us pardon, O most holy ones, since wherever there has been error, it is the fault of ignorance, not of will. You too, O most renowned men and writers, who still lead an earthly and mortal life, I address; I entreat and ask you: if in this catalogue I have accomplished anything less worthy of your merits, abilities, or labors by lamplight, ascribe it not to my will but to our inexperience; pardon, which...
