Marius Philelphus, son of the aforesaid Francis Philelphus, a knight with golden spurs, such as our people call knights, and a laureate poet, a doctor of philosophy and the arts of very celebrated reputation, and distinguished by knowledge both of the divine Scriptures and of both laws, was engaged for a public salary to lecture in the gymnasium of Mantua; there also he brought to an end his life and his studies. Among other things he wrote:

A distinguished work of precepts of the art of rhetoric, two books.
Elegant orations, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Songs and rhythms. “I had already long ago determined...”

But he also composed many other things in verse and prose, which have not come to me. He died at Mantua under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480, the thirteenth indiction.

Theodericus de Herxen, a German by nation, visitor of the order of the brethren of the clerics living in common, a devout man, and studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, clear in speech, outstanding in life and conduct. For the edification of simple brethren he wrote many devout volumes, in which, usefully exercising his talent, he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the whole Psalter, one book.
On the Lord’s Prayer, one book.
On the Ave Maria, one book.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book.
On the desire of dying, one book.

And many other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1476.

Dominicus de Dominicis, a Venetian by homeland, first bishop of Torcello and afterwards bishop of Brescia, and apostolic referendary, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, excellently adorned with philosophy, dialectic, astronomy, and eloquence. Because of his great virtues he was held in high esteem among the Roman pontiffs Pius, Paul, and Sixtus, and often fulfilled the office of legation. He wrote several excellent little works of excellent talent, but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands. The following elegant works of his are reported:

Orations on various subjects, one book.
Also various sermons, one book.
And many treatises.

He died at Brescia under Emperor Frederick III and Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1478, the eleventh indiction, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of March, in the sixtieth year of his age.

Ludovicus Donatus, a Venetian patrician and bishop of Bergamo, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of scholastic philosophy, distinguished also in piety, religion, clemency, and mildness, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence. He wrote certain lucid little works, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these I have never deserved to see any. His notable work addressed to Paul II is reported:

On the Sentences, four books.
Also various orations, one book.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the second indiction. He was buried in the church of Saint Vincent the Martyr. 1484.
