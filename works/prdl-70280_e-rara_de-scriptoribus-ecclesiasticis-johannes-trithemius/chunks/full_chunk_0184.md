*Philobiblon*, one book, “A desirable treasure.”
Several letters, one book.
And certain other works. He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John [Walßgram?], an Englishman by nation, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in secular philosophy, subtle and acute in intellect, adorned with the master’s cap in the school of Paris, shone forth as most celebrated in lecturing and disputing. He wrote certain excellent volumes, by which he made his name known. Among these there exists a distinguished work:

On the *Sentences*, three books.
Various Questions, one book.
The rest which he is said to have composed have not yet come to my knowledge.

John de Saxo, of the Order of the Friars Minor, learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught both in canon law and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, an outstanding preacher in delivering sermons to the people and very famous in his time. He produced certain small works of reading not to be despised, which are necessary for the instruction of simple priests. Of these I have found only:

A Summary of Law, one book.
Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Thomas of Argentina, a German by nation, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, after Gregory of Rimini prior general, a man most learned in the holy Scriptures and an excellent cultivator of secular philosophy, distinguished for his life and the honesty of his conduct, who wonderfully exalted his order by word and example. He wrote some excellent volumes on the Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there exists a very lucid work:

On the *Sentences*, three books, “The deep gave its voice.”
Constitutions of his order, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John Bramart of Aachen, a German by nation, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior of Lower Germany, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and a most celebrated expounder of secular philosophy according to the custom of the scholastics of this time; distinguished in intellect and eloquence, an outstanding teacher in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, for the usefulness of the faithful, small works not to be despised, by which, exercising his talent, he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among these there are:

On the *Sentences*, four books.
Various sermons, one book.

He is also said to have written many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Henry of Erfurt, a German by nation, [unclear] in the divine Scriptures by long study, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote some volumes for the edification of the Church, by which he made his name known to many by report. Among these an historical work is reported:

On Memorable Deeds, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.
