Various sermons: three books. He published very many epigrams, and various ones, which we hope will soon be made public. He also translated from Greek into Latin several sermons of blessed John Chrysostom; and many other things unknown to us. He is still living at [Ferrara?], and is composing various works under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494.

Peter Brutus, Venetian by nation, bishop of Cattaro, certainly a man most learned in divine Scripture, excellently instructed in secular letters, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin tongues, who, for the confirmation of the Christian faith, undertook many contests of disputations against the perfidious Jews, always as victor. Among other little works of his talent he wrote to the senate of Vicenza a very celebrated and notable work, abundantly furnished with authorities from the holy Scriptures:

Against the Jews: one book. “As I was reading the letters of very many.”

And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1485. And I do not know whether he is still living.

Ubertinus, a cleric of Crescentino, a man most learned in secular letters, not ignorant of divine Scripture, a philosopher, rhetorician, distinguished poet, most skilled in both languages, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, who in the very celebrated university of Pavia lectured publicly for seven years on a stipend, with the greatest attention and profit of his hearers. He wrote many excellent little works, both in verse and in prose, of which the following are extant:

On the Letters of Cicero: sixteen books. “The letters of this volume.”

On the Duties of the same: three books. [unclear]

On the Metamorphoses of Naso: one book.

Also a book of letters: one book.

Many poems: one book.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I do not know whether he is still living.

William of Aachen, German by nation, a man learned in divine Scripture, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in eloquence, distinguished in life and conduct, who formerly held the office of preacher in the church of Basel. He wrote many commendable little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity; from these, in the university of Erfurt, he publicly lectured on certain ones, with everyone’s attention and praise. He wrote:

On the Gospel of John: one book.

On the Canon of the Mass: one book.

On the Passion of the Lord: one book. “He handed Jesus over to their will.”

Disputed Questions: one book.

Itinerary of the Holy Land: one book.

Various sermons: one book.

And many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1485. And I believe that he is still living.

Johannes de Lapide, German by nation, a monk of the Carthusian order of the house of Basel, a man most learned in divine Scripture, not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct. Having formerly obtained the mastership in the faculty of arts in the university of Paris, he governed in the same for several years; and, diligently pursuing the study of sacred letters by teaching, lecturing, and disputing, he deserved to be adorned with the doctoral dignity in the school of theologians.
