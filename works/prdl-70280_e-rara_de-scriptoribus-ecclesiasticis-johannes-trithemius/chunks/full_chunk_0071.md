Simplicius, pope, by birth a Tiburtine, after the death of Hilarius presided over the Roman and universal Church for fifteen years. He was a learned and holy man, who appeared not only simple according to his name, but also upright, turning away from evil and fearing God. He restored many churches that had collapsed from extreme age; he issued statutes necessary for the Christian commonwealth; and he wrote some things for the edification of the faithful. Of these I have read only:

Eruuldeus, bishop of a certain church: I have not been able to find the name of the city. He was a man learned by constant practice in the divine Scriptures, rich in reading of the ancients, subtle in intellect, apt in speech. He is said to have produced some little works. Of these there survives a volume, furnished from the sacred Scriptures, against those who say that Christ, while dwelling in this life, did not see the Father with his fleshly eyes. The heading of this work is:

*That the Son saw the Father*: 1 book.

He also wrote certain other things.

In this work he shows that the Son of God, even from the hour of his conception in the womb of the Virgin Mary by the Holy Spirit, always saw his God and Father with fleshly eyes, both thereafter and now, clothed in human flesh. He is said to have flourished under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 480.

Victor, bishop of Capua, learned in the sacred Scriptures and sufficiently imbued with secular letters, an excellent theologian and computist, keen in intellect and distinguished in speech, is said to have written some little works on the sacred volumes; but very few of them have come to my knowledge. He refuted the error of Victorius of Aquitaine, who, at the command of Pope Hilarius, writing paschal cycles, taught that Easter should be celebrated on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of May in the year of the Lord 455, although it was more correctly to be celebrated in that year on the seventh day before the Kalends of May. Therefore, when this occasion arose, he wrote:

*On Easter*.
*One Gospel from the four*: 1 book.

He flourished under the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

Erealis, an African bishop, a man very skilled in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of the discipline of secular studies. When he was asked by Maximinus, bishop of the Arians, whether he could assign in a few words testimonies of divine Scripture, without disputations, merely for the assertion of the consubstantiality of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, he answered not in a few words, as that man had mockingly requested, but by composing a distinguished work with many testimonies from both Testaments:

*Against the Arians*.

He also wrote other things. He flourished in the time of the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460.
