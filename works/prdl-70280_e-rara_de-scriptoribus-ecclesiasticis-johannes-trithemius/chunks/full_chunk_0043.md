Theophilus, the twenty-first bishop of the church of Alexandria, a man devoted to the Scriptures and a most vigorous assailant of the Origenists, who burned with great discord against Saint John Chrysostom and compelled him, driven from the episcopate by plots, to die in exile, as is contained more fully in the histories of the ancients. He is said to have written certain little works, of which few have come to my knowledge. There is extant by him, to various persons on various matters:
Letters: one book.
To Epiphanius on heresies: one book.
On Easter: one book.
To the most beloved lord, a[unclear].
He also published many other things, as may be gathered from the letter of blessed Jerome addressed to him, in which he asks that they be sent to him to be translated.
He flourished under the emperors Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 390.

Antiochus, bishop of a certain city, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly instructed in secular letters; his character and learning commended him to his people. He wrote for the edification of readers certain excellent volumes, of which these are reported the more important:
Against avarice: two books.
On the illumination of the man born blind: one book.
Several homilies to the people: one book.
And other works unknown to us.
He flourished under the emperors Theodosius and Arcadius, in the year of the Lord 390.

Macarius, an Egyptian monk, formerly a disciple of the blessed father Antony, a devout man of most holy life, of whom many miracles are told, and how he even obtained voices of the dead from their tombs for the confirmation of the faith. He sent a letter useful to all religious persons:
To monks: one book. It begins: Certainly at first, if he shall have begun.
And others to other persons.
He flourished in great holiness under the emperors Constantine and Constantius, in the year of the Lord 450. His deeds are extant.

Faustinus, a priest, very learned in the sacred Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, of the party of Lucifer, and for that reason deservedly blameworthy. He wrote many excellent volumes, which are judged useful for the confutation of heretics. Of these I have found only the titles of a few:
Against the Arians and Macedonians: seven books.
An Apology to Valentinian: one book.
He flourished under Valentinian and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 370.

Jerome, priest and monk, born of his father Eusebius from the town of Stridon, a man very learned in secular letters, and among all the doctors most learned in the divine Scriptures; distinguished by knowledge of many languages; interpreter of the sacred law; a prudent hammer of heretics; bringing forth from his treasury new things and old, and giving to his fellow servants at the proper time the measure of spiritual wheat, he stripped the proud Hebrews and enriched the humble Latins. This is the true evangelical steward, faithful and prudent, who, bringing forth from his treasury new things and old, and giving to his fellow servants at the proper time the measure of spiritual wheat, stripped the proud Hebrews and enriched the humble Latins.
