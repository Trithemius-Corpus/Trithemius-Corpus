He translated into Latin Homer, Thucydides, and several others. He flourished in person at the Council of Constance, under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420. Of him someone says as follows: What did that most biting censor of the Latin language, Lorenzo Valla, beget for himself by his harsh and exacting reproof? What other praise, I ask, did he obtain after death than that, according to his merits, that fine epigram composed about him is read and proclaimed everywhere: “Now, after his death, Valla’s shades seek [unclear]; but he fears to be the censor of his own tongue.” What he thought about the Donation of Constantine, by which Constantine endowed the Roman Church, his pestilential book shows; would that so great a man had never published it.

Johannes Tortellius, an Aretine by nation, very close friend of Lorenzo Valla, to whom he wrote the six books of the Elegances of the Latin Language; subdeacon and chamberlain of the Roman pontiff, and afterward companion of Pope Nicholas, the intimate associate of his studies; perfectly trained in Greek and Latin letters, a very celebrated philosopher, rhetorician, and theologian. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote a great and distinguished volume, which he entitled:

Commentaries on Grammar, one book: “The Greek letters by which...”
Also on dialectic, one book.
He published various letters, one book.
And several other works.

He translated into Latin the Life of Athanasius of Alexandria for Pope Eugenius; he rendered the historian Appian word for word. And other works. He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Hermannus Petra of Studorp, of the Carthusian order, a German by nation; a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct; vicar and rector of the house of nuns of his order, of Saint Anne near Bruges in Flanders, where for ninety years he flourished with much religious devotion and learning. He wrote certain devout little works of his talent, of which the following are reported: a notable work, divided into six volumes for the circle of the year, containing:

Sermons on the temporal cycle, three books.
Sermons on the saints, three books.
On the Lord’s Prayer, 50 sermons, one book.
On the governance of nuns, one book.
And several other works.

He died under the emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1428, on the ninth day before the Kalends of May, in the sixth indiction.

Omnibonus Leonicus, a man most learned in humane letters, perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, the most celebrated philosopher and rhetorician of all in his time; once, at Venice, a hearer of Manuel Chrysoloras of Constantinople, who brought Greek letters back into Italy. He wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On Cicero’s Orator, three books: “How great Cicero’s Orator...”
On the poet Lucan, one book: “In expounding this author...”
On Quintilian, one book: “How much admiration...”
On the praises of eloquence, one book.
Many orations.
