Since he was very skilled in law and saw that new varieties of cases in the contentious court, which could not be determined by the decretals, were emerging daily, he composed, through three men most learned in law, new constitutions which he wished to be called:

*The Sixth of the Decretals.*
One book, *Of the holy Roman Church*.
Several letters to various persons, one book.

He died under the emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1303, in the first indiction. Of him it had previously been prophesied: “He entered like a fox, lived like a lion, died like a dog,” etc.

*William Mandagot*, archdeacon of Nimes and afterward archbishop of Embrun, and finally cardinal of the holy Roman Church, a man very skilled in both laws, one of those who, by order of Pope Boniface VIII, published the code of the *Sixth of the Decretals*, as is clear at the beginning of that volume, wrote in that faculty works not to be despised, by which he consecrated his name to immortality. Of these I have seen only the distinguished work which he wrote, while he still held the office of archdeacon in the church of Nimes, to Berengar, succentor of the church of Beziers and later bishop:

*On the Election of Prelates*, one book, “Because when elections are impending.”
*Exposition of the Same*, one book, “Since I am not of that authority.”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under the emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

*Berengar*, succentor of the church of Beziers and later bishop, and at length cardinal of the holy Church, to whom the aforementioned William Mandagot, while both were still established in lesser offices, composed the book *On the Elections of Prelates*, was another of those who, at the command of Boniface VIII, compiled the sixth book of the decretals. A man most learned in the same legal faculty, he composed for the same William, then archdeacon of Nimes, a distinguished work on the *Summa* of Hostiensis according to alphabetical order; since it is, as it were, an elucidation of the same *Summa*, he entitled it:

*The Eye*, one book.
*On the Sentence of Excommunication*, one book.

He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

*Richard de Senis*, vice-chancellor of the Roman Church and afterward cardinal deacon of the title of Saint Eustachius, doctor of both laws, in his time very celebrated by the praise of all, was the third of those who compiled the *Sixth of the Decretals* by order of Pope Boniface VIII; his name is expressly contained at the beginning of that volume. He is reported to have written some small works in law, but I do not recall that any of them has come into our hands. Let my studious reader seek them out. He flourished under the emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

*Dinus Mugelanus*, professor of both laws, a distinguished elucidator, subtle in intellect, and very ready and acute in disputing and resolving doubtful questions of law, who by teaching and writing acquired great glory for himself among all the doctors of Italy. He published in his faculty many excellent works. Of these the following are reported:

On the decretals.
On the Code, nine books.
On the old Digest, twenty-four books.
