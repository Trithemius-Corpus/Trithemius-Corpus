Nicholas of Clémanges, archdeacon of Bayeux, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, having obtained the mastership of the sacred page in the university of Paris, gave great proof of his learning. He wrote, in both styles, many distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of his most brilliant talent. Of these, the following are reported. Johannes Dominici, cardinal of the title of St. Sixtus, commonly called Ragusan, a Florentine by homeland, of the Order of Friars Preachers, who was sent by Pope Gregory XII to the Council of Constance on behalf of union, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people, wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:
