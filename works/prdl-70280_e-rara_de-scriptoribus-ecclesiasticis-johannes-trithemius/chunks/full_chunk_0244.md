Ilfridus, titular bishop of Cyrene, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a German by nation, vicar in pontificals of Theoderic, archbishop of Mainz, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin languages, subtle in talent, distinguished in speech, an acute disputant, an outstanding preacher of the divine word, ready in counsel, and illustrious in life, wrote certain little works worthy of reading, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these, the following are extant, addressed to the aforesaid Archbishop Theoderic:

On the Usury of the Jews: one book. It asks in the forum of conscience.
On the Redemption of Rents: one book. It first asked whether [unclear].
Sermons and conferences: one book.
Various questions: one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and under Pius and Paul, Roman pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Godescalcus of Meschede, a German by nation, a Westphalian by homeland, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently taught in secular philosophy, keen in talent, illustrious in life, a profound doctor of sacred theology, who for many years taught that science gloriously in the academy of Erfurt, earned from all great praise for his learning. In the same university he wrote and lectured on certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among them are reported:

Questions on the Sentences: three books.
Sermons and conferences: one book.
Various disputed questions: one book.
And many other things.

His brother Theodericus Gresmundt of Meschede, doctor of arts and medicine, a man most learned in every respect, still surviving, resides at Mainz; he himself, among other things, composed an elegant treatise On the Regimen of Health in Time of Pestilence. He has a son called Theoderic after his own name, scarcely completing three lustrums of age; his admirable talent, and his singular learning as well as eloquence, far surpass many of mature age, even doctors. There is extant, addressed to me, his distinguished book On the Seven Liberal Arts. For he wrote, both to me and to others, almost countless letters, most ornate on every sort of subject, and various orations displaying Ciceronian eloquence, so that, like another Adeodatus, once the son of most holy Augustine, by his talent, readiness, and skill in writing and arguing he is an object of admiration and astonishment to many, even learned men. But Godescalcus, his father’s brother, flourished under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1470, and died before this decade.

Stephanus Hoest of Ladenburg, formerly canon of the church of Speyer, a German by nation, a man learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, keen in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in delivering sermons to the people, since for some years he held the office of preacher in the castle of Heidelberg, wrote little works not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among them is extant:

On the Books of the Ethics: ten books.
Sermons and conferences: one book.
