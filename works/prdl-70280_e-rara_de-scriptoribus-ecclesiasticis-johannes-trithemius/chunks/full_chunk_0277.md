He now adorns Lyon by his learning; he reads the poets publicly and privately, instructing the sons of several magnates. He wrote in verse and prose many distinguished short works, of which the following are extant. A notable metrical work against vices, which he entitled:

Various letters.
And many other things.

He is alive to this day, thirty-two years old, composing many various works, under the illustrious King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Elias Regnier, a very celebrated jurist, remarkably learned both in divine Scripture and in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, distinguished in speech, cautious in counsel, who for a long time publicly taught law in the school of Poitiers. He wrote certain distinguished short works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have seen only the following: a celebrated work which contains:

*Cases of the Sixth Book of Decretals*, one book. Boniface VIII, compiler.
*Cases of the Clementines*, one book. Bishop Johannes. “Divide in [unclear].”
I have seen nothing of the others.

He is still alive in the University of Poitiers, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Utgerus Venray, German by nation, of the order of the Canons Regular of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of Saint Peter the Apostle, which is called Heyne, situated not far from Worms; a man zealous in divine Scripture, not ignorant of secular literature, practiced in meter and prose. He wrote in both kinds certain works not to be despised, of which the following are extant. A *Tetralogue*, which he entitled:

*On the Instruction of Boys*, one book. “When I observed the boyish [unclear].”
*On the Pursuit of the Blessed Life*, one book. “In the year in which Ceres [unclear].”
*Hymns on Saint Mary*, one book. “Behold, of Mary, mother of God.”
*On the Various Use of Letters*, one book. “Although, excellent Peter.”
*On a Wondrous Matter*, one book. “For recognizing cunning.”
*On New Matters*, one book. “As I turn over many things in my mind.”
*On the Kinds of Meters*, one book. “Most familiarly in Christ.”
*A Panegyric to Me*, one book. “This tetralogue, which concerning.”
Various epigrams.
And certain other things.

I hear that he is fashioning for me a work on the calamities of these times, which I have not yet seen. He has written very many letters, both to me and to others, in verse and in prose, not inelegant. He is alive to this day and composes various works, under Maximilian, King of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Jodocus Beysselius, patrician of the royal city of Aachen, German by nation; a man zealous in sacred Scripture and most learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, a fluent orator, and a notable poet. Although he is necessarily distracted by care for his wife and household affairs, nevertheless he burns with an incredible love of the Scriptures, daily writing and composing many things, by which his name is everywhere made known with great glory.
