He flourished under the emperors Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 370.

Basil, bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia, a learned and most holy man, was of such authority among the Greeks that no one, however learned, ever dared to contradict him in any matter of doctrine or writings. He wrote many excellent little works in the Greek language, but few of them have come into our hands.

On the Holy Spirit: one book.
Against Eunomius: one book.
On the Hexaemeron: nine books. “In the beginning,” etc. [unclear].
The Rule of Monks: one book. “The human race.”
An Exhortation of Monks: one book. “Hear, my son, the admonition.”
On the Instruction of Boys: one book.
On Faith: one book. “God indeed without inter-.”
On Hidden Speech: one book. “God [gave] the use of speech.”
On Several Psalms: one book. “All Scripture is divinely [inspired].”
To a Fallen Virgin: one book. “It is time to cry out.”
Several homilies.
He also composed other treatises, sermons, and letters to various persons.
He flourished under Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 380.

Ephrem, a monk and distinguished inhabitant of the desert, and deacon of the church of Edessa, was ordained priest by blessed Basil; because of his humility he now approached the divine mysteries to be performed, for he was held worthy before God and men. Nevertheless, so far as pertained to his office, in admonishing the people he delivered countless homilies. As is read in the Life of the Holy Fathers, he was seen to have received from an angel a book written in golden letters; from this, soon on the next day and thereafter, with the Holy Spirit speaking in him, he had such grace in declaiming that in his time he found no one like himself. He wrote, in the Syriac language for the instruction of readers, certain devout little works, of which the following are found among the Latins, namely:

On the Holy Spirit: one book.
On Compunction of Heart: one book. “Grief compels me.”
On Contrition of Heart: one book. “Come, my dearest ones, to me.”
On Penance: one book. “Our Lord Jesus Christ, who.”
On the Struggles of the World: one book. “In the struggles of this.”
On the Day of Judgment: one book. “Come, most beloved brothers.”
On the Resurrection: one book. “Glory to almighty God.”
On the Blessedness of the Soul: one book. “Blessed is he who has hated.”
On Thoughts: one book. “In the holy Scriptures.”
On Hard Preaching: one book. “Whenever, brothers.”
On the Celebration of the Mysteries: one book. “I ask you, brothers.”
Lamentation over the City of Edessa: one book.
He also published not a few other works, especially sermons and homilies to the people, which have not yet come to my knowledge.
He flourished under Valentinian and Valens, a very close friend of blessed Basil, as you will be able to find in his legend, in the year of the Lord 380.

Gregory, bishop of [Nyssa?], brother of blessed Basil, prelate of Caesarea in Cappadocia, a man very studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, stirred by zeal for his brother, himself also wrote some little works in the Greek language. Of these I have found among our people only:

On the Image and Formation of Man: one book. “Man, from the rational soul.”
Against Eunomius: two books.
