Francis Petrarch, of Tuscan nationality, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular letters by far the most learned of all in his time, a philosopher, rhetorician, and very celebrated poet, who, after long silences, called the letters of humanity, dead, so to speak, back from the lower world to the upper, shone forth no less clearly for holy conduct than for knowledge. For from love of Christ and of salutary philosophy he entered upon the solitary life; persevering in it until death by meditating, praying, and writing, his fame was carried through the whole world.

Of his works I have found:

On the Solitary Life, one book, “I have known few men.”
On the Remedies of Fortune, two books, “When things and fortunes...”
On the Seven Penitential Psalms, one book.
On the Conflict of Cares, one book.
On the Present World, one book.
On the Story of Griselda, one book.
Bucolics, in meter, one book, “Eight...” [unclear] “in tranquil...”
Letters to various persons, one book.
Africa, to King Robert, one book.
The Life of Scipio Africanus, one book.
On Memorable Matters, one book.
On the [pomeria?], to [Sagemor?], one book.

He also published many other works, which have by no means come into our hands. He died in his hermitage in the territory of Padua under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Gregory XI, in the year of the Lord 1373, in the twelfth indiction.

Michael de Massa, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, learned, rich in reading of the ancients, and nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent, distinguished in speech, and an excellent declaimer of sermons, also notable for the goodness of his life and conduct. He published many excellent volumes for the edification of the faithful, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among them the following are reported:

On the Sentences, four books.
On Isaiah the Prophet, one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, one book.
On the Gospel of Mark, one book.
On the Gospel of Luke, one book.
On the Gospel of John, one book.
On the Life of Christ, one book.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book.
On the Four Virtues, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
And many other works.

Moreover, he is said to have labored elegantly on commentaries on many books of the Bible. He flourished in the times of Louis IV and likewise Charles IV, emperors, and of Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Richard de Bury, bishop of Durham, chancellor of Edward III, king of the English, to whom there is extant a letter of Francis Petrarch in response to an inquiry about the island of Thule, a man most devoted to and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters; a singular and very celebrated lover of books. With many expenses and labors he founded a distinguished library in the University of Oxford, which he established there with incredible zeal for the public benefit of the students.
