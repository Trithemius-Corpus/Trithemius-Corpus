Thomas of Cantimpre, by nation a Brabantine, of the Order of Friars Preachers, formerly, as they say, a disciple of Albert the Great, a devout man and outstandingly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech, is said to have written certain excellent volumes, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there is extant a work on the mystical bees, which he entitled:

The Universal Good, one book. To the reverend father in Christ, Brother H.

On the Nature of Things, one book.

The Life of Saint Christina, one book.

And other works which I have not seen.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270. And there are those who write that he had knowledge of the Greek language, and that he also translated the books of Aristotle which were then in use in the schools.

Alfonso, king of Castile and Leon, a philosopher and astronomer, most excellent among all men of his time, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, and a distinguished lover and cultivator of letters: after, following the long vacancy of the Roman Empire, he had been elected king of the Romans against Richard, brother of the king of England, and since he was a good and prudent man, when Richard had died in the fifteenth year after his election, with a view to peace, in the presence of Pope Gregory X, he voluntarily withdrew and completely renounced every right which he had had in his election. He wrote very useful and elegant works:

Tables in astronomy, one book.

Letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished during the time when the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Peter, of the Order of Friars Preachers, by nation a Frenchman, and provincial prior of the same religious order in France, a man learned in the divine Scriptures through long study and not ignorant of secular philosophy, acute in intellect and ready in speech, was suited for delivering sermons to the people. Hence, for the benefit of readers, he composed, for the cycle of the year, works not to be despised:

Sermons on the Season, one book.

Sermons also on the Saints, one book.

But he is said to have written a number of other things as well, which have not come to my knowledge.

Consecrated canonically under the name of the fifth [unclear], he sat in the supreme pontificate for only five months; he was certainly a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in Aristotelian philosophy, who taught theology excellently for many years and acquired great praise for his learning. He wrote many excellent little works, by which he made his name known both to those then present and to those who would come afterward. Among them are extant:

On the Sentences, four books. Do you know the order of heaven?

On all the Epistles of Paul, fourteen books. The principles of things must be investigated.

Also on the Psalms, one book.

On the Song of Songs, one book.

He also wrote, as is said, several other lectures on the Bible, which I have not yet seen.

He flourished in the pontificate under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1275.

John of Ascoli, from Ascoli, of the Order of Friars Minor, after Saint Bonaventure minister general, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and an outstanding preacher of sermons, was sent to Constantinople by Pope Gregory X; by his exhortations he brought the Greeks back to obedience to the Roman Church, and persuaded them to come with him to the Council of Lyon. For the edification of the faithful he wrote sermons on the season, not to be despised, one book.
