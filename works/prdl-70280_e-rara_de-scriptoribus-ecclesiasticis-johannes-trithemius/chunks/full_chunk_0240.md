*Bessarion, commonly so called*, patriarch of Constantinople, a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher, an orator, second to none in both languages, wrote certain distinguished volumes of his genius, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with great glory. Among them the following are reported. Against Mark, bishop of Ephesus, who, after the manner of the Greeks, held erroneous views concerning the Holy Spirit at the Council of Florence, he wrote a distinguished and excellent work, which he entitled:

Confutation, 1 book.
On that passage, “Thus I wish him to remain,” 1 book.
In Praise of Plato’s Philosophy, 5 books.
Praises of His Own Country, 3 books.
To the Greek Emperor, of Consolation, 3 books.
Several Orations and Letters.
And certain other things.

He also founded at Venice a notable library, most richly furnished with many books, both Greek and Latin; over it was placed, with no small stipend, Marcus Antonius Sabellicus, a most learned man, of whom we shall speak later. At length he died after many days, while on an embassy to France, under Emperor Frederick and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1473, in the sixth indiction.

*Johannes Hagen*, otherwise “de Indagine,” a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, of the house of Mount Saint Savior near Erfurt, prior in Eisenach, in Stettin, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures, very learned, and excellently taught in canon law, brilliant in talent, ready and prudent in counsel, wrote in clear language many distinguished volumes to princes, bishops, and other prelates of churches, when asked about various and diverse questions; of these I remember that only a few have come into our hands. For, as I learned from the writings of a certain devout father of the same order, he published more than three hundred treatises; of all these I myself have seen and read only those which I have set down below in order. A notable work which he entitled:

On Interior Adornment, 1 book. “All the glory of the king’s daughter is from …”

On Grace and Free Will, 1 book. “Not I alone, but grace …”
On Correction and the Grace of God, 1 book. “The just man shall correct me in …”
