Henricus, bishop of Vercelli, formerly a cleric of the church of Trier, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in the discipline of secular letters, made his name known to posterity by writing, although his little works have not come to the knowledge of our reading. In that discord which existed between Hildebrand, Pope Gregory VII, and Henry, Emperor III or King IV, he wrote, in the person of Theoderic, bishop of Verdun, while he was still a cleric at Trier, to the same Pope Gregory a volume furnished with divine Scriptures: not rebuking him, but entreating him as an elder, as a father, with the friendly affection of one grieving, setting before him all the things which talkative rumor had spread abroad that he had done or said against the right of laws, divine law, and equity. He entitled it:

*On the Discord between the Kingdom and the Priesthood*, one book.

Concerning the rest I have found nothing. He flourished under Henry IV, in the year 1080.

Theodericus, a monk of the order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, is said to have written certain little works of reading not to be despised. Of these, few have come to my knowledge. They are read to Richard, abbot of Fulda:

*On the Life of Saint Benedict*, one book, “When of heavenly sacraments …”

*On the Translation of the Body of the Same*, one book, “When for a long time the Lombard people …”

Letters also to various persons, one book.

He also composed many other works, both in verse and in prose, which have not come down to us. He flourished under Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1040.

Adam, abbot of Perseigne, of the order of the holy father Benedict, learned in the divine Scriptures by constant exercise, rich in the reading of the ancients, and sufficiently instructed in the knowledge of secular letters, of very celebrated reputation in delivering declamations and homilies, transmitted his name with praise to posterity by composing various treatises. There are extant his devout and useful:

*Sermons to the Brothers*, one book.

*On Saints, on Various Subjects*, one book.

He is also said to have expounded certain treatises of Sacred Scripture, in which he showed the abundant vein of his talent; but they have not come to my knowledge.

Anselm, bishop of Lucca, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not meanly instructed in secular letters, the tireless co-worker of Pope Gregory VII, an outstanding defender against Guibert, who had been subordinated to him by Henry, so that by the authority of the law of the Scriptures he defended all his deeds, sayings, or writings against Henry the emperor. He left very famous monuments of a great talent, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant:

*On Jeremiah the Prophet*, one book.

*On the Psalter* also, one book.

*Against Guibert the Antipope*, one book.

And several letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1080.

Willelmus, abbot of Merseburg, of the order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, practiced in verse and prose; after he had spent some time applying himself to letters at the University of Paris, he returned to his homeland and was made scholasticus of the church of Bamberg. Then, considering that the glory of the world passes away with time, having despised the world, he put on the monk in the monastery of Fulda for love of Christ; and at last, as he increased in merits, he was consecrated abbot in the monastery of Saint Peter at Merseburg, and made his name known to posterity by writing. Of his little works I have read only a volume composed in a beautiful variety, in mixed meter and verse with prose, on the nuptials of Christ and the Church:

*On the Song of Songs*, two books, “I behold the studies of the elders …”

Concerning the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished under Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1070.
