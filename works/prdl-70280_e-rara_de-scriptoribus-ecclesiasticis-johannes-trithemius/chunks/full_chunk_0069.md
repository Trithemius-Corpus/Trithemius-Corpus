*Book C*

[Ursinus?] the monk, learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of humane letters, subtle in intellect, cautious in speech, is said to have composed many treatises for the benefit of the faithful; but I do not recall that anything of his has ever come into our hands. There is, however, reported an outstanding work of his against those who rebaptize persons once duly baptized, whose title is:

*On the manner of not rebaptizing heretics*, one book.

He flourished, as I find, under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Julianus, bishop of the church of Toledo in Spain, and by nation a Moor, to whom Saint Prosper is said to have written three books *On Contemplation*, was a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular disciplines, scholastic in speech, apt in understanding. He wrote many distinguished volumes, of which the following are reported:

*The faith of the nascent Church, by testimonies*, [unclear].
*On holy virginity*, one book.
*On the nature of the soul*, eight books.
*On the resurrection, questions*, one book.
*On contempt of the world*, one book.
*Prognosticon of the future age*, two books.
*On vices and virtues*, one book.
*Letters to various persons*, one book.

He is also said to have composed some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 550.

Victorinus Buconius, bishop of the city of [Castellum?] in Mauritania, a man exercised by constant study in the divine Scriptures and not moderately learned in secular letters and disciplines, a very strong hammer against heretics and an outstanding teacher of the faithful people, is said to have written many little works for the defense of the catholic faith. Of these the principal ones are reported to be these:

*Against the Jews*, one book.
*Against the Arians*, one book.
*Against all heretics*, two books.
*On the sacraments also*, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Musaeus, priest of the church of Marseilles, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and polished by very subtle exercise in their meanings, scholastic and refined in language, at the urging of Saint Venerius the bishop, extracted from the sacred Scriptures responsive readings suited to each feast day throughout the whole year, and arranged psalms suited to the several times to be sung in church. He also wrote to Bishop Eustasius an outstanding work:

*Of sacraments*, seven books.
Several homilies to the people.
And certain other things.

He flourished under the princes Marcian and Leo, in the year of the Lord 440.

Vincentius, a priest, by nation a Gaul, much exercised in the divine Scriptures, having a language polished by practice in speaking and by great reading, and also not ignorant of secular letters, wrote, as our Gennadius reports, an outstanding work, and one to be embraced most greatly by devout men:

*Commentaries on the Psalms*, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Cyrus, Alexandrian by birth, a physician by profession, from a philosopher made a monk for the love of Christ, a man most learned in secular letters, distinguished in skill of speaking, using syllogisms in his writings more than the authorities of divine readings: while he inveighs too strongly against Nestorius, he seems to foster the Timothean doctrine, and, uncertain, he suspends his mind from assent to the Council of Chalcedon, nor does he think it should be accepted that the Son of God after the incarnation consists of two natures. Of his writings there is reported:

*Against Nestorius*, one book.
