Ansegisus, abbot of the monastery of Lobbes, of the order of Saint Benedict, afterward archbishop of Sens, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, distinguished in speech and talent, arranged and brought together for the use of the catholic Church the laws of Emperor Charles the Great and of Louis, which I have seen, as follows, namely:
The ecclesiastical statutes of Charles, two books: “While our Lord Jesus reigns.”
The secular laws of the same, two books: “Concerning peace we admonish that.”
The ecclesiastical statutes of Louis, two books: “To all of you either by sight.”
The secular laws of the same, two books: “If slaves through contumacy.”
He also published some other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished under Emperor Lothar, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Amulus, bishop of Lyons in Gaul, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, learned by reading the ancients, and not moderately instructed in secular letters; distinguished in both the Hebrew and the Latin language, keen and forceful in talent, scholastic and composed in eloquence, wrote little works of reading not to be despised. Of these I have read only the distinguished work addressed to Charles:
Against the Jews, one book: “The detestable perfidy of the Jews.”
Of the rest I have as yet seen none.
He flourished under Louis, son of Charles the Great, in the year of the Lord 830.

Bertramus, priest and monk, very skilled in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in the letters of secular disciplines, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence, and no less notable in life than in learning, wrote many excellent little works, of which few have come to my knowledge. To King Charles, brother of Emperor Lothar, he wrote a commendable work:
On Predestination, one book.
On the Body and Blood of the Lord, one book.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 840.

Turpinus, archbishop of Reims, friend and secretary of Emperor Charles the Great, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, having a talent exercised in verse and prose, advocate of the poor, beloved by God in life and conduct, who declares that he, together with Charles, often fought the Saracens with their own hands and weapons, wrote elegantly:
The Deeds of Charles the Great, two books.
Letters to various persons, one book.
He flourished under the same Charles and Louis his son, in the year of the Lord 830.

Anastasius, keeper of the apostolic library, a most sagacious restorer, learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, very skilled in Greek and Latin expression, is said to have composed some little works; but none of them has come into our hands. He translated many volumes of the Greeks into Latin. For, at the command of Pope Nicholas I, he translated:
The Life of John the Almsgiver, one book.
One *Hierarchy* of Dionysius, one book.
The seventh universal synod, one book.
The Lives of many fathers, one book.
And many other things.
He flourished under Louis, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 860.
