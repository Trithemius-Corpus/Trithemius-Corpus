These are the writings of blessed Dionysius that I find among the Latins; if he gave anything more, it remained among the Greeks. But since the aforesaid little works were wrapped in great obscurity, they were made clear by the explanation of the following fathers. Of these, John Scotus, a monk of our order, first translated and polished them; then Hugh of Saint Victor, Robert of Lincoln, Albert the Great, and Dionysius the Carthusian, and many others, whose memory is not now ready to hand. Blessed Dionysius suffered in the Parisian field, together with the companions named above, under the emperor Domitian, in the year of the Lord 96, in the ninth indiction, and in the ninetieth year of his age. He was buried at Paris in the church consecrated in his name, shining with miracles.

Lucius Annaeus Seneca of Corduba, philosopher, disciple of the Stoic Photion, and uncle of the poet Lucan, was a man of most restrained life. By the testimony of blessed Jerome, I have placed him, celebrated in the catalogue of ecclesiastical writers, for this reason: because he was also the very close friend of the apostle Paul, and composed many elegant works for instruction in the moral life. Nero Caesar, ill-starred, killed him two years before Peter and Paul were crowned; Nero had once been his most unworthy pupil. Of his little works I have found the following:
