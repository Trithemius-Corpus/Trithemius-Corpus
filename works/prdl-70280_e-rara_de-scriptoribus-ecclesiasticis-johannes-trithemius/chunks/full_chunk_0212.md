Raphael Fulgosius, a Piacenzan by nation, a man most learned in both laws and well instructed in the precepts of secular philosophy, excellent in talent, cautious and steady in counsel, published certain distinguished volumes in civil law, by which he showed the force and liveliness of his talent also to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books.
On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.
On the New Digest, twelve books.
Many Counsels, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1416.

Johannes de Palomar, archdeacon of the church of Barcelona, an eminent doctor of decrees, nobly learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, published little works not unworthy of reading, through which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there survives an eminent treatise addressed to John, abbot of the monastery of the Scots of the order of Saint Benedict at Vienna:

On Abstinence from Meats, one book. “The sons of Rechab shall rise in judgment.”
Sermons and Questions.

Johannes de Teneramuda, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Sillimacus in Savoy, a man studious in the divine scriptures and very learned, devout in life and conduct, distinguished in talent, composed in speech, published little works very useful for the edification of religious brothers; by these he instructed not only those then present, but also those who would come after him, in the love of God. And they are these:

On the Knowledge of God, one book.
On the Nature and Fall of Man, one book.
On the Restoration of the Fallen, one book.
On the Love of God, one book.
On the Joy of Man, one book.

On the Christian Faith, one book.
On the Honor of God, one book.
On the Conception of Saint Mary, one book.

He also elegantly composed many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Asparinus, a very celebrated grammarian, a native of Bergamo, born from the little village of Barzizza, a man most learned in secular letters, a philosopher and a most outstanding rhetorician, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, who raised the elegance of the Latin tongue, half-dead, from the underworld. He wrote many distinguished little works in that faculty, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survive:

On Orthography, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
Various Orations, one book.
On the Letters of Cicero, one book.
On the same author, On Duties, one book.
On the same author, On the Orator, one book.
On him, On Old Age, one book.
