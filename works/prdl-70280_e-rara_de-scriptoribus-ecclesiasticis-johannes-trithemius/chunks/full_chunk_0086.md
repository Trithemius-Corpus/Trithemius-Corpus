Delmus, bishop of the West Saxons, formerly abbot of the monastery of Maildub in Scotia, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a devout man of outstanding holiness, zealous and learned in the Holy Scriptures, and in secular letters the most learned of all in England in his time, excellent in verse and prose, a most fervent preacher of the word of God. He wrote in both styles certain instructive volumes, of which I have found only the following. While he was still abbot, at the command of the synod of his people, he wrote against the error of the Britons a notable and useful work:

*On the Celebration of Easter*, one book.

*On the Virginity of the Saints*, in verse, one book.

*On the Same Subject*, in prose, one book.

Several letters to various persons, one book.

He is also read to have written many other works, which have not come to the notice of our reading.

He flourished under Justinian the Younger, in the year of the Lord 680.

Ceolfrid, after Benedict the second abbot of the monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul in England, of the Order of Saint Benedict, teacher and nurturer of Bede the priest, a devout and learned man, and a vigorous governor of regular discipline. He wrote to the king of the Picts, who requested it, a fairly lengthy letter in the form of a little book:

*On the Observance of Easter*, one book.

He flourished in England in the times of the emperor Justinian the Younger, namely in the year of the Lord 680. At last, when he was returning from visiting the thresholds of the apostles, he died in the city of Langres, in the seventy-fourth year of his age.

Egidius, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, Greek by nation, learned in the divine Scriptures, very skilled in secular letters, a physician, philosopher, and distinguished poet, venerable no less in his manner of life than in knowledge, wrote certain little works of no contemptible usefulness, of which I have found only the following. A notable work composed in meter:

*On Pulses*, one book.

*On Veins* also, one book.

Of the others I have seen nothing. It begins: “When a vein is made [unclear].” “The powers of talent with modest [unclear].”

He flourished under Emperor Tiberius, in the year of the Lord 700.

Bede, monk and priest of the monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, which is in England, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man most zealous and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and very skilled in secular letters, a philosopher, astronomer, calculator, and distinguished poet, not ignorant of the Greek language, excellent in talent, not elaborate in eloquence, yet sweet and well composed. He wrote many volumes, in which he proves the keenness of his intellect. When he was seven years old, he was given by the care of his relatives to be educated by Abbot Benedict, and then by Ceolfrid of the said monastery of Wearmouth; and from that time he spent the whole course of his life in residence in the same monastery, devoted all his effort to meditating on the Scriptures, and, amid the observance of regular discipline and the daily care of singing in church, always found it sweet either to learn, or to teach, or to write. In the nineteenth year of his age he received the diaconate, and in the thirtieth the rank of priesthood, both through the ministry of Bishop John, at the command of Abbot Ceolfrid. From the time he received the priesthood until the end of his life he composed the following little works:

*On Genesis as far as Isaac*, one book.

*On Exodus*, one book.

*On Numbers*, one book.
