Salvianus, presbyter of the church of Marseille, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, a teacher of the bishops of his time, wrote in a scholastic and clear style many and various little works, of which the following are reported. To Claudius, bishop of Vienne, he wrote several letters seasoned with the salt of wisdom. To Marcellus the presbyter:

On the good of virginity: three books.
Against avarice: four books.
On the present judgment: five books.
To Bishop Salonius: one book.
To Claudius, bishop of Vienne: one book.
On the beginning of Genesis, the Hexaemeron: one book.
On the final part of Ecclesiastes: one book.
Letters to various persons: one book.
Homilies made to bishops: one book.
Also On the Sacraments: one book.
Many homilies to the people: one book.
He also published many other things, which have by no means come to my knowledge.

He flourished under Emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

Emanuel, presbyter of the church of Edessa, learned in the divine Scriptures through continual exercise, an outstanding declaimer of homilies to the people, skilled in Greek and Syriac speech, flourished first at Edessa; afterward, coming to Constantinople, and being held there in great esteem, he ended his last day. He wrote, both in Greek and in Syriac, almost innumerable little works. Of these, very few have come to my knowledge. For I have found only:

Against the Nestorians: one book.
Against the Eutychians: one book.
Against the Timotheans: one book.

He flourished under Emperor Zeno Augustus, in the year of the Lord 480.

Gennadius, bishop of the city of Constantinople, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote in Greek some little works on the sacred volumes, but they have by no means come into our hands. For he was an outstanding declaimer of homilies, and distinguished no less in life than in knowledge of the Scriptures. Among other things, a work of his arranged as commentaries is reported:

On Daniel the prophet: one book.
Many homilies to the people: one book.

He died under Emperor Leo I, who was reigning in the year of the Lord 460.

Claudius, bishop of Vienne, to whom the presbyter Salvianus composed one book, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters; no less distinguished in holiness than in knowledge, he shone forth in his own times. Because of frequent earthquakes, he first instituted the litanies, which the Roman Church thereafter took up and continued. He wrote:

On the state of the soul: three books.
Also Letters: one book.
And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

And let the name Claudius not trouble the uninformed: he should know that the same man is called Mamertus.

Sidonius, bishop of the Arverni, learned in the divine Scriptures and, in the judgment of all, the most learned man of his time in the disciplines of secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech. He wrote, both in meter and in prose, many distinguished little works. Of these I was able to find only those which I have set down below, namely:

A panegyric to Anthemius Augustus: one book.
A panegyric to Julius Valerius: one book.
By auspice and number [unclear].
Conceive the past things.
