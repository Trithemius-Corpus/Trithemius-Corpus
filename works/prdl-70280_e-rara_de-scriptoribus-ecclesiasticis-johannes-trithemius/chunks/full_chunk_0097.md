*ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS. Fol. 45.*

Nicholas, pope, the first, a Roman by country, sat in the pontificate after Benedict III for eight years, a man magnificently learned in the holy Scriptures, most holy in life and conversation, who established many things for the usefulness of the Catholic Church; by whose permission and authority the use of Sequences in the Gallican churches first began, Notker, abbot of Saint Gall, procuring [it]. He wrote, among other things, in the person of his pontificate:
Of letters, to various [persons]: book.
He dies under Lothair, or Louis II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 869, in the second indiction, the 8th [day before] the Ides of December.

John, deacon of the Roman church, a man erudite in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, venerable in life and conversation no less than in learning. He wrote, for the edification of many, [addressed] to Pope John VII, in a quite illustrious style, a great volume which contains:
The Life of Saint Gregory, pope, the first: 3 books.
And certain others. He flourished under Charles the Bald, emperor, in the year of the Lord 870.

Otgerus [Notker], bishop of Liège, from [being] abbot of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the order of the Divine father Benedict, a Teuton by nation, a man most erudite in the divine Scriptures, and, in the disciplines of secular letters, the most learned philosopher of all his time, a distinguished poet and musician, and venerable no less for sanctity than for knowledge. He wrote, both in meter and in prose, many little works, of which few have come into our hands. I have read only the volume which he entitled "Of Sequences," which he had written while still a monk in the monastery of Saint Gall, [addressed] to Liutward, bishop of Vercelli and archchaplain of Charles the Great, emperor. This work begins thus:
The Prologue or preface: a letter. ["By the merit of [his] supreme sanctity."]
The Sequence-book, thus: book. ["Let all now render thanks."]
On music and symphony: 1 book. ["When the prudent [son] and of the prudent."]
On the expositors of sacred Scripture: 1 book.
Of letters, to various [persons]: 1 book.
Of the rest which he composed, nothing has come to the notice of our reading. Of this most learned man's Sequences, Pope Nicholas I approved [them] and commanded [them] to be sung at the masses. He, on account of the renown of [his] knowledge and life, [was] first abbot, then in his old age consecrated bishop, [and] presided, tenth in order, over the governance of the church of Liège, under Lothair the emperor, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 850; under whom also he dies, not without an opinion of sanctity.

Christian, who [is] also [called] Druthmar, monk and priest of Corbie, of the order of holy memory Benedict of Cassino, an Aquitanian by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, imbued with the Greek and Latin tongue, who, coming from Aquitaine into Gaul, made his name known by writing. Of his little works I have hitherto deserved to see only a great and illustrious work, [addressed] to the monks of the monasteries of Stavelot and Malmedy—existing under one abbot in the diocese of the church of Liège—written in open speech:
On the Gospel of Matthew: 1 book. ["He did not make this title…"]
Of others I have seen nothing.
In the prologue of his exposition of the Gospel according to Matthew, he promised to write also on the other evangelists, which he is said also to have done, but [it is] unknown to us.

Wandelbert, deacon and monk of the monastery of Prüm, of the order of Saint Benedict, of the diocese of Trier, a Teuton by nation, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures and magnificently learned in secular letters, prompt in talent and renowned in eloquence, wrote, both in meter and in prose, not a few volumes, of which I have seen only [those addressed] to a certain abbot Marward:
The Life of Saint Goar the priest: 1 book.
The Miracles of the same: 1 book.
A Martyrology, in meter: 1 book.
He flourished under Lothair the emperor, in the year of the Lord 850.
