*Book*

A certain little work of Dionysius the Areopagite, for Arnold Bostius, Carmelite of Ghent. He translated very many other things as well. His work *On Marriage* contains about six hundred verses; in it he argues most elegantly whether it is expedient for a wise man to take a wife, concluding that it is not.

He died, not without the very great loss of scholars, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493, in the thirty-ninth year of his age, in the eleventh indiction.

Junianus Maius, Parthenopaean, a Neapolitan, a man certainly most devoted to secular letters and excellently learned, not ignorant also of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these there survives a large and distinguished volume addressed to Heinrich, archbishop of Acerenza, confessor of Ferdinand, king of Naples, and chiefly to the king himself, which, gathering it from the books of the ancients, he entitled:

*On the Property of Ancient Words*, one book. *Letters to Various Persons*, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I am uncertain whether he is still alive.

Benedictus Capra, of Perugia, a man most celebratedly learned in both laws, well exercised both in the divine Scriptures and in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle in talent, an excellent disputant, and distinguished also by knowledge of many ancient matters, publicly taught law for a long time in the university of Perugia and acquired a great reputation for his learning. Among other things he wrote:

*On the Decretals*, five books. Also very many *Counsels*, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480.

Johannes Andreas, bishop of Aleria, secretary of the apostolic library, formerly a companion of Cardinal Nicholas of Cusa, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters, a jurist, a most eloquent orator, excellent in talent, outstanding in life and conduct, skilled in both Greek and Latin speech, wrote certain little works of no middling authority, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of their number the following are reported:

*On the Fourth Book of the Decretals*, one book. *On the Use of Fiefs*, one book. *On Appeals*, one book. *Letters to Various Persons*, one book. And very many other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1451, in the eleventh indiction.

Johannes Campeius of Bologna, a very celebrated jurist, not moderately learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular philosophy, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence, publicly taught law for a long time in the university of Bologna, where, instructing many very distinguished pupils, he gained great fruit and praise for his knowledge. He wrote in that faculty many excellent commentaries and treatises, as they report, by which he commended his name to posterity. But none of them has come into our hands. There is reported to be his distinguished:

*Treatise on Dowry*, one book. And very beautiful *Counsels*, with many other things.
