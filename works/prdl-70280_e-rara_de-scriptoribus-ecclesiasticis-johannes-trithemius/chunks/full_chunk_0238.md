*On Ecclesiastical Writers, fol. 117* ...had no one equal to him. He applied himself to contemplation and prayer with such fervor that one would never have thought he could have written anything. Conversely, in writing and reading he was so diligent that one would have judged him never able to pray or contemplate. He slept very little; he was admirable in abstinence from food and drink, for, following the example of blessed Jerome, he judged it sweet to meditate day and night on the law of the Lord, always either writing something useful or reading, so that prayer often interrupted reading, and sacred reading frequently followed prayer.
