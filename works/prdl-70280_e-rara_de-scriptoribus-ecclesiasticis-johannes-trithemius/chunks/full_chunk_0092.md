Claudius, a monk of the order of Saint Benedict, formerly, as they report, Bede’s disciple in England, colleague of Albinus the Levite and abbot, a Scot by nation, a man very zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular learning, practiced in verse and prose, composed many small works on the sacred volumes:

On the five books of Moses: five books, of which they report [unclear].
Also on Judges: one book.
On the book of Ruth: one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew: one book.
And many other things.

He flourished in Gaul under Charlemagne, in the year of the Lord 800.

Freculphus, bishop of Lisieux in the province of Rouen, a monk of the order of Saint Benedict, to whom Rabanus, while still an abbot, devoted his talent in many volumes, a man most zealous and very skilled in the divine scriptures, and quite fit for teaching the people, no less distinguished in manner of life than in knowledge, is reported to have written certain excellent small works. Of these I have read only a large and distinguished volume, containing from the beginning of the world down to the birth of our Lord Jesus Christ:

A history of deeds accomplished: seven books, through a certain sequence of time.

Of the others he has as yet seen nothing.

He wrote the aforesaid work to Helisachar, in which he also unraveled the more difficult questions of the scriptures; he set forth in a lucid style the deeds of both the Jews and also the gentiles from the beginning of the world down to the Virgin’s giving birth.

He flourished under Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Theodulfus, bishop of Orléans, formerly abbot of the monastery of Fleury of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine scriptures, instructed from youth in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, is reported to have composed some small works in both styles, but they have not yet come into our hands. When this man was innocently sent to prison by the emperor Louis, I do not know on what suspicion, he composed the hymn “Gloria laus”; and when on Palm Sunday, as the emperor was passing by the prison, he sang it in a loud voice, it so pleased the emperor that he immediately allowed him to go free. Among other things he wrote, in various meters:

Hymns: one book.
Letters: one book.

He flourished under the empire of Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Einhard, chancellor of the emperor Charlemagne, a German by nation, afterwards a monk of the order of the holy father Benedict, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine scriptures, wrote small works of reading not to be despised. Of these they report the following. Imitating Bede, who abbreviated the Hebrew psalter by extracting from it all the verses having the words of a prayer, he himself abbreviated the Gallican psalter, extracting from it all the verses having the words of a prayer. He also wrote, both in his own person and in that of the emperor:

Letters to various persons: one book.
The Life of the emperor Charles: one book.
A history of the times: one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Charles and under Louis his son, in the year of the Lord 820.
