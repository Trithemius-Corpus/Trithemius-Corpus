He arranged Graduals, Alleluias, and Tracts, with prefaces suitable for the Mass; he separated the authentic books of the holy fathers from the apocryphal, as is found in distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*. He died while Anastasius Augustus was emperor, in the year of the Lord 496.

Faustus, from being abbot of the monastery of Lerins, became bishop of Riez in Gaul: a learned and eloquent man, indeed, but stained by the Pelagian error. For this reason his little works are counted among the apocrypha, as is clear in distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*. At length, corrected and amended by blessed Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe, he abandoned the error and returned to the truth of the catholic faith; as proof of this he published an excellent work against his former errors, which he entitled *On the Grace of God*. He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year 490.

Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe in Africa, was a man most learned in the divine scriptures and nobly trained in secular letters; subtle in ability, profound in understanding, sweet in speech, polished in expression, easy in teaching, sharp in argument, fit for persuasion, and very gentle. He shone forth no less clearly for holiness of life than for knowledge of the scriptures. He wrote many outstanding volumes, of which the following are reported:
