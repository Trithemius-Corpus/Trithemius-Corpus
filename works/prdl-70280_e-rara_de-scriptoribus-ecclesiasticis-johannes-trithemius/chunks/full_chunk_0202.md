James of Thessalonica, of the order of Friars Preachers, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, excelling in talent, polished and composed in speech, second to none of his time in declaiming sermons to the people. He published certain illustrious volumes, by which he left to posterity a praiseworthy memory of his name. Of these there is extant a notable work, entitled "On the morals of men and the offices of the noble and of the common people," setting forth [its] subject:
On the game of chess: 3 books. ["Among all kinds of signs…"]
Various sermons: 1 book.
But the others which he is said to have written have by no means come to our knowledge.

Thomas de Haselbach, a Teuton by nation, erudite in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, scholastic in speech, but too diffuse [and] prolix, and on that account liable to the reproach of certain [persons]. For when he was the ordinary lector of sacred theology in the gymnasium of Vienna, reading for twenty-one years on the prophet Isaiah, he did not reach the end of the first chapter. He wrote some little works on the divine Scriptures, of which the following are reported—many volumes:
On Isaiah the prophet: 1 book. ["After [that], by God's gift, in such wise as…"]
On the Sentences: 3 books.
On the Ten Precepts: 1 book.
On the five senses of man: 1 book.
Sermons on the [seasons of the] year: 1 book.
Sermons on the saints: 1 book.
But the others which he is said to have written have by no means come to our knowledge.

Thomasinus, of Ferrara by country, of the order of Friars Preachers of Saint Dominic, a man studious [and] learned in the holy Scriptures, a distinguished declaimer in correcting the sins of peoples. He is reported to have forged certain illustrious little works for the instruction of simple priests, by which he transmitted to posterity the notice of his name with praise. Of whose number these are foremost reported:
Sermons on the [seasons of the] year: 1 book.
Sermons on the saints: 1 book.
Sermons for Lent: 1 book.
But the rest which he composed have not come to our knowledge hitherto.
He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and of Gregory XII, pope, in the year of the Lord 1410.
