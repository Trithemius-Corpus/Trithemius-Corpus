Pius II, pope, Tuscan by nation, Sienese by native city, formerly called Aeneas Silvius, sat in the supreme pontificate after Calixtus III for six years: a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher, orator, and distinguished poet; he left to posterity many monuments of his talent. Once, when poor, he served a certain prelate at the Council of Basel. Then, after being taken on as a scribe in the chancery of Frederick III, emperor, and after receiving the poet’s laurel, he was made first bishop of Trieste, afterward of Siena, then cardinal, and most recently was elected pope by the vote of all. Many epigrams and certain other very elegant little works, which I have not seen, he is said to have written; by these he made his name known to posterity. He died under Emperor Frederick III, at Ancona, as is said, in the year of the Lord 1464, in the twelfth indiction, on 1 September. He was buried at Rome in the church of Saint Peter, beside the altar of Saint Andrew.
