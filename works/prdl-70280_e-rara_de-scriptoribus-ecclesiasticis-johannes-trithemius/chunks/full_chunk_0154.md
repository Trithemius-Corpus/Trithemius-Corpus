*On Ecclesiastical Writers, fol. 75*

Nicholas the Frenchman, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the seventh prior general, a learned and holy man, after he had governed his order vigorously, as far as he was able, for nearly twenty years, resigned his office for love of Christ and returned to the quiet of the hermitage, where he persevered in holy conduct until death. Among other things he wrote a mournful work lamenting the condition of his order, after the brothers had been expelled from the Holy Land, which he entitled:

*The Fiery Arrow*, 1 book.

He also wrote other works. He flourished in the time of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Matthew of Aquasparta, of the Order of Friars Minor, the twelfth minister general, and afterwards bishop of Porto and cardinal of the holy Roman Church, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and, according to the custom of this time, magnificently learned in secular philosophy, composed certain distinguished little works of excellent talent, by which he made his name known far and wide. Of these the following are extant:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*Inventory of Sentences*, 1 book.
*On the Epistle to the Romans*, 1 book.
Several sermons, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

William Peraldus, French by nation, of the Order of Preachers, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and also in secular letters, especially sufficiently distinguished in Aristotelian philosophy, ready in intellect and scholastic in eloquence, was of excellent industry in delivering sermons to the people. He published several well-ordered volumes in both faculties, by which he made his name known not only to those then present, but also to future generations. Of these the following are extant:

*Summa of Virtues and Vices*, 2 books.
*On the Instruction of Religious*, 1 book.
Sermons for the season, 1 book.
Sermons on the saints, 1 book.
And many other works which have not yet come to my knowledge.

Garcias, Spanish by nation, a man very practiced in both laws, subtle in intellect, ready and cautious in counsel, and acute in resolving questions of cases, was held in esteem in his time among the professors of this faculty; by teaching and writing he spread his name abroad. For he is said to have written several little works both on the laws and on canon law, which have not come into our hands. Of his there are extant:

*On the Decretals*, 5 books.
And several works on the laws.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Pope Nicholas IV, of the Order of Friars Minor, previously called Jerome, Picene by nation, born from the city of Ascoli, sat after Honorius IV in the chair of Peter for four years. He was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not meanly learned in secular letters, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, and no less venerable in life and goodness than in learning. He wrote a sufficiently distinguished work:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
And certain sermons.

He is also said to have published useful commentaries on several books of the Bible. He died at Rome, while the empire was vacant after Rudolf’s death, in the year of the Lord 1291, in the fourth indiction.
