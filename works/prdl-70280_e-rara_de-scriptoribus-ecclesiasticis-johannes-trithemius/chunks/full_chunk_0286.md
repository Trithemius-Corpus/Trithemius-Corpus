Do you then think that such great men, so useful to the Catholic Church, should be rejected? Far be it from us to inflict upon them a rash injury, by which they might think us stirred by zeal for rivalry, not by regard for piety, and might say to us that proverb commonly bandied about: Knowledge has no enemy except the ignorant man. Thus I wished to answer you briefly, so that you might know that what you previously wondered had been done was not done without cause. Farewell; remember me before God in your prayers. From Sponheim, the fourth day before the Nones of April, in the year of the Lord 1402.

*In praise of the catalogue of ecclesiastical writers and of the excellent father Johannes de Trittenheim, abbot of Sponheim: a commendation by Sebastian Brant.*

Whoever you are who wish to know the approved writers
of the Church, and whatever distinction each one has,
read this epitome of the learned abbot Johannes
from Sponheim, whom his eloquent life proves.
As here he renews the ancient teachings of the fathers of his order,
and as he renews the sacred laws of Benedict,
so he takes care to gather together the scattered names
of the ancient writers, and to collect them in one book.
He sets down a catalogue, in which he reckons the times, and what
so venerable a cohort of authors wrote.
He rescues from decay so many worthy names of men
whom devouring time has just now trampled under foot.
And it is a marvel that one man could, in order, either read
so many worthy men, or remember so many great men,
so that I plainly wonder whether perhaps he did not read them all;
yet much of what he saw came to him from all of them.
Hence it comes about that by right I place in the number of the learned
this high-minded and distinguished man too.
