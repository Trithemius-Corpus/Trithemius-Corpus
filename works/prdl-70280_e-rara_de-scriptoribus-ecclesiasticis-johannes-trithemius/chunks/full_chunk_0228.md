*Book I*

On the eternal Gospel: a book.
On the Christian religion: a book.
On the conception of Saint Mary: a book.
On the Passion of the Lord: a book.
Sermons for the Temporal cycle: a book.
Sermons for the Sanctoral: a book.
And certain other works.

He died at Aquila, a city of Abruzzo, in the reign of Emperor Frederick III, under Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1443, in the sixth indiction, in the sixty-fourth year of his age. And because of his frequent miracles he was enrolled in the catalogue of the saints by Pope Nicholas V, in the year 1450, the seventh after his death. His feast is kept on the twentieth day of the month of May.

Antonius Gaynerus, of Pavia by origin, a very celebrated doctor of medicine, nobly learned also in the precepts of the philosophers, excellent in talent, and notably distinguished in skill in his profession, wrote in that discipline certain lucid little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On illness of the stomach: one book.
Also on fever: one book.
On illnesses in general: one book.
On pleurisy: one book.
On the womb: one book.
On fluxes: one book.
On arthritis in the joints: one book.
On the afflictions of stone: one book.
On the third of Avicenna: one book.
And many other works.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Gerardus Stredam, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of All Apostles near Liège, a man most zealous in the divine Scriptures and excellently learned by long practice, venerable also for religion, prudence, and authority, wrote for the edification of simple brothers certain excellent little works, of which the following are reported. To the abbot of Saint Hadrian of Giraldus’s Mount:

On pastoral care: one book.
On the Ten Commandments: one book.
On the seven sacraments: one book.
The Mirror of Religious: one book.
A Dialogue on the virtues: one book.
A Dialogue on the vices: one book.
Sermons for the Temporal cycle: one book.
Sermons for the Sanctoral: one book.
And certain other works.

He died at the beginning of the reign of Emperor Frederick III, under Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1443, in the sixth indiction.

Matthaeus Palmerius, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by native city, a historian, philosopher, and most eloquent orator, who in the Council of Florence, which Pope Eugene IV celebrated against the Synod of Basel, was counted among the illustrious men, wrote a book on the angels full of error. Persistently defending it, he was condemned as a heretic and was burned at the city of [unclear]. Carefully examining the Chronicle of Eusebius of Caesarea, he corrected it; and, completing it down to the year of the Lord 1448, he inscribed it to Piero di Cosimo de’ Medici: one book. It begins: “That it is innate in our minds.”

Letters to various persons: one book.
