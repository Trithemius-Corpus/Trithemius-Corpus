He is still living, when we wrote this, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1493.

[Utelwolfus?] de Lapide, a noble man, German by nation and a Swabian, a man of great talent and a vigorous emulator of Roman eloquence, and for that reason having obtained great honor with the Margrave of Brandenburg, wrote to Georgius de Lapide, his uncle, a very great lover of letters, one book:
A very polished letter-collection.
And other things by which he will bring forth great praise for Germany among the cultivators of the Roman tongue. He is still living today, under Maximilian, in the year in which this was written, 1493.

Rodulphus Langius, German by nation, canon of the church of Münster in Westphalia, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a philosopher, orator, and very celebrated poet, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, wrote many distinguished little works both in meter and in prose, by which he has spread his name far and wide with immense glory. Of these the following are extant. To Hermann Langius, dean of the same church:
*On the City of Jerusalem*, two books: Most splendid once was the city.
To the dean of Cologne, a book of poems, one book: Bright virtue and of the heavenly ones.
*On the Siege of the City of Neuss*, one book.
A poem *On the Three Magi*, one book.
Several letters to various persons.
Various poems also,
and a great many other things.
He is still living, a very old man, in the city of Münster, composing many things under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which these things were written, 1493.

Sebastianus Murrho of Colmar, German by nation, priest and canon in Colmar, a man devoted and learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, an outstanding philosopher, orator, poet, and cosmographer, most skilled in the three languages, Hebrew, [Greek?], and Latin, renowned for knowledge of laws and canons, second to none in architecture, music, and the whole quadrivium, wrote, among other things, to Jacobus Wimphelingus of Schlettstadt, whom we mentioned above, a brief but very beautiful
*Epitome on the Praises of the Germans*, one book.
On the works of Baptista Mantuanus, one book.
Several letters to various persons,
and certain other things.
He lives today in Colmar, where he is a canon, and writes various things which we hope will be published day by day, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1493.

Carolus Fernandus, German by nation, born at the town of Bruges in Flanders, deprived of sight from early childhood and for that reason utterly ignorant even of the elements, offers so great a marvel of himself that, like another Homer, he has become a poet, musician, [unclear], and very celebrated orator; also a most penetrating interpreter of the divine Scriptures, excelling in verse and prose, subtle in talent, eloquent in expression, most upright in life and conduct. Having been granted a public and perpetual stipend by the king of the French, he taught humane letters excellently for a long time in the very celebrated school of Paris, and expounded a good many authors most beautifully.
