Commentaries on the whole Psalter, one book. And certain letters to the brothers. He died under the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 598, in the first indiction, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of December.

John, bishop of Constantinople, a Cappadocian by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, of inestimable holiness, so intent upon alms and works of piety that the emperor Maurice, moved against him by a zeal of avarice, ordered all the poor and beggars to be expelled from the city of Constantinople. He wrote in Greek to Saint Leander, bishop of Seville, and certain other things. He flourished under the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 600.

Licinianus, bishop of Cartagena, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, after many exercises of study, was killed at Constantinople by his rivals with a cup of poison under the emperor Maurice. Sudden death did not harm him, since Scripture says: “The just man, by whatever death he may have been overtaken, shall be in a place of refreshment.” For then he truly begins to live, when, adorned with good works, he goes through death to Christ, never to die. He wrote certain little works not to be despised in reading, of which I have not yet deserved to see any. The following are said to be his: On the sacrament of baptism, one book; Letters to various persons, one book; and certain other things. He flourished under the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 590.

Severus, bishop of the city of Malaga, formerly the companion and colleague of the aforesaid bishop Licinianus, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, nobly instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, acute in intellect and distinguished in speech, wrote against Vincentius, bishop of the city of Zaragoza, who had passed from the Catholic faith into Arian depravity: A Correction, one book; On virginity, to his sister, one book; Letters to various persons, one book. He flourished in the year of the Lord 580, under the emperor Maurice, under whom he died.

Eutropius, bishop of the church of Valencia, formerly abbot of the monastery of Servitanum of the order of Saint Benedict, to whom Licinianus, while still living in the aforesaid monastery, wrote many letters, a man magnificently learned both in divine writings and in secular letters, and excellently imbued with monastic disciplines. While he was still abbot he wrote to Bishop Licinianus why, after infants have been baptized, the triple anointing with chrism follows afterwards, one book; On the correction of monks, one book; and several letters to various persons. He flourished in the times of the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 600.

John, bishop of Gerona, formerly a monk of our order, a Goth by nation, from the province of Lusitania, a man studious and learned in the divine writings, also distinguished in morals and conduct, learned and famous in Greek and Latin speech: when, as a young man, he went to Constantinople out of love of learning, after seven years had passed he returned to Spain. When King Leovigild could not bring him over to Arian impiety, he condemned him to a fairly harsh exile for ten years. Recalled from there by the most Christian king [Reccaredus?], he founded a monastery, where by word and example he showed his brothers the path of regular discipline. He wrote: An Exhortation to monks, one book; Additions to the chronicle, one book; [unclear], of which we spoke above: a little work [unclear], one book.
