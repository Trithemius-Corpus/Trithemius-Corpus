And many other works.
He flourished in the time of the Council of Basel and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Henricus of Gauda, a German by nation, magnificently learned in the holy Scriptures, subtle in intellect, a most acute disputant of scholastic questions, in the university of Heidelberg, both by lecturing and by composing, easily held first place in his time. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, of which only a few have so far come to my knowledge:

The Fountain of Gardens, the Well of Water: one book.
On the Celebration of Mass: one book.
Various Prayers.
Disputed Questions.
And certain other works.
He flourished in the time of the Council of Basel and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1425.

Sanctes de Arduinis, born from the city of Pesaro, a man most learned in philosophy and medicine, most experienced in healing human bodies, held in great esteem among the Venetians in his time, published certain distinguished little works of this faculty, by which he destined the memory of his most celebrated name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is extant a notable work on all natural and artificial poisons: one book.
Also On Smelling: one book.
On Prolification: one book.
And certain other works.
He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Nicolaus de Susato, a German by nation, a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, most learned both in secular philosophy and in the divine Scriptures, held in esteem by Ludovicus, duke of Bavaria, count palatine of the Rhine, was joined with the cardinals from the German nation, along with the others, at the Council of Constance for the election of the supreme pontiff, as is found in the acts of that same council. He wrote several distinguished little works of his talent, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these are reported:

Questions on the Sentences: one book.
Many Sermons: one book.
Various Questions.
And many other works.
He flourished at the Council of Constance, under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Johannes de Marliano, a very celebrated philosopher and physician, excellent in intellect, renowned for experience in this art, not least among the physicians of his age, published certain distinguished and lucid little works both in philosophy and in medicine, the reading of which is said to be not a little useful to students of these subjects; namely:

On Reaction, Although [unclear].
On the Heat of Bodies: one book.
On Antiperistasis: one book.
And many other works.
He flourished in the time of Sigismund, most Christian emperor, in the year of the Lord 1430.
