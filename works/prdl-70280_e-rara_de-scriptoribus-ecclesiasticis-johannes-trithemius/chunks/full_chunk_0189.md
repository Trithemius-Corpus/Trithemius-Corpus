In the vernacular language, moreover, many books of his are extant, full of jests and vanities, such as the book of the Hundred Tales; Corbatius; Philostratus. He died under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Gregory XI, in the year of the Lord 1375, in the thirteenth indiction, at the age of sixty-two.

Johannes Hildesheimensis, German by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, learned in the Holy Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, an outstanding philosopher and poet, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He was formerly at Avignon the devoted household servant of blessed Peter Thomas, the General, a most holy man, of whom we made mention above. He wrote many distinguished little works in verse and prose, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these, the following are reported. To Florentius, bishop of Münster, he published a lengthy volume:

On the Three Holy Kings: one book.
A Chronicle of Histories: one book.
A Defence of His Order: one book.
On Monsters in the Church: one book.
On the Fountain of Life: one book.
On Antichrist: one book.
Against the Jews: one book.
Against One Painting Shameful Things: one book.
Eighty Letters: one book.
Various Sermons: one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370.

Johannes Marronis, English by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, outstanding in secular philosophy [unclear]. He composed certain distinguished little works commending his talent, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Among these there is said to be the notable work which he entitled:

A Table of Originals: one book.
Against the Wycliffites: one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the school of Oxford, where by teaching, disputing, and writing he earned great praise from all, at about the time when the errors of John Wyclif were condemned by the archbishop of Canterbury.
