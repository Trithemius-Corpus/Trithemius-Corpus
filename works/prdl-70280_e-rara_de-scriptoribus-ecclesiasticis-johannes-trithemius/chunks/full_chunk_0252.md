*Book*

Conradus of Rodenberg, German by nation, abbot of the monastery of Saint John the Baptist at Rheingau, in the diocese of Mainz, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a devout and learned man, and a particular guardian and lover of regular observance, who previously for many years held the office of prior at Saint Matthias the Apostle in Trier and at Saint Martin in Cologne. Since he was a most ardent lover of the blessed Mary ever virgin, he wrote in her honor, in plain discourse, a very celebrated volume, taking the material of praise from each of her members, under the figure of a cluster of grapes, to which he gave the very apt title:

On the vineyard of the Lord Sabaoth: one book. Its foundations are in the mountains.
The exercise of novices: one book. Consider your calling, brothers.
On preparation before Mass: one book. Our Lord Jesus Christ.
On the ruin of the order, an oration: one book. The crown has fallen from our head.
On the causes of the ruin, an oration: one book. Catch us the little foxes.
On pastoral care, an oration: one book. Blessed is the servant whom he finds.
And various collections in chapters.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1486, in the fourth indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of January. He was buried in the right apse of the choir before the altar of Saint Benedict, toward the ambulatory.

Stephanus of Gaeta, a Neapolitan, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and a most learned professor and interpreter of both laws, acute in intellect and sufficiently capable in debating and resolving questions of the Scriptures, clear and orderly in speech. He wrote to Johannes Baptista de Bentivoliis of Sassoferrato, counselor of Ferdinand, king of Sicily, a very celebrated work:

On the sacraments: seven books. To the thresholds of blessed Peter the Apostle.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Georgius Molitoris of Neuenburg, German by nation, a man trained by long study and excellently learned in the divine Scriptures, and not moderately instructed in scholastic philosophy, which they call Aristotelian; keen in intellect, distinguished in life. In the academy of Erfurt he taught theology publicly for several years and won great praise for his learning. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his intellect, by which he commended his memory to posterity; among them they report the following:

On the Sentences: four books.
On the Passion of the Lord: one book.
Many sermons: one book.
Various questions.
And several other works.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484, in the second indiction.

Bartholomaeus, surnamed Platina, a Cremonese by homeland, formerly an apostolic abbreviator, a man most learned in every respect, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, subtle and forceful in intellect, eloquent and pleasing in speech, steadfast and strong in spirit. He suffered many calamities from Paul II, to such an extent that, stripped of all his goods and dignity, after suspension on the rack he was most cruelly thrust into prison, and was detained until the death of that Paul. When he was soon freed by Sixtus, in imitation of Eutrandus, deacon of Pavia, who suffered similar things from Berengar, king of Italy, he not ineptly marked the character and vices of Paul by a counter-reckoning. For he wrote to Pope Sixtus IV:

On the deeds of the Roman pontiffs: one book.
On the natures of things: one book.
Letters to various persons: one book.
