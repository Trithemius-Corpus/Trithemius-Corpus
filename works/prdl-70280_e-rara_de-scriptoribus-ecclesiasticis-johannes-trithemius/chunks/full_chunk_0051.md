He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, around the year of the Lord 410.

Severus, a presbyter, surnamed Sulpicius, born from the province of Aquitaine, a man noble in birth and letters, and conspicuous for his love of poverty and humility. Dear also to the holy men Martin of Tours and Paulinus of Nola, bishops, in his old age he was deceived by the Pelagians; and, recognizing the fault of loquacity, he kept silence until death, so that the sin which he had contracted by speaking he might healthfully amend by keeping silent. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading, of which the following are reported:

*On the Life of Saint Martin, Bishop*, three books: “Most mortals.”

*The Conference of Postumianus and Gallus*, three books: “When I, into one place.”

*Letters to His Sister*, one book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

*To Paulinus, Bishop of Nola*, two books.

He is also said to have written some other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished with Saint Martin, archbishop of Tours, under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Ausonius, bishop of Bordeaux, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular letters second to none in his time, excelling in verse and prose, shone forth no less illustrious for holiness than for learning. While at Trier, in the council under the emperor Maximus, together with the holy men Martin, Ambrose of Milan, and Jerome the presbyter, he did many splendid things. While staying there he wrote, among other things, in meter, a notable little work entitled:

*Mosella*, one book.

*On the Latin [unclear] of the Same Country*, one book.

But he is also said to have written many other little works, which have by no means come down to us.

He flourished under Maximus, in the year of the Lord 220.

Augustine, bishop of Hippo, by nation a Carthaginian in Africa, a man most learned among all the doctors both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, eloquent in speech, renowned in expression, profound in understanding, so that I should believe no one able either to read or to possess all his works. Hence without doubt he is proved to lie who admits that he has found Augustine entire. For he surpasses all writers both in the number of his books and in usefulness, having composed more than a thousand distinguished treatises. No one expounded sacred Scripture so profoundly as Augustine; no one established Christian morals so usefully; nor did anyone defend the catholic faith more ardently than he. The king brought him into the wine-cellar, and clothed him with such great strength that he powerfully laid low all the adversaries of the faith with the sword of his mouth.

If you have him as your teacher, even if the rest are lacking, he alone is sufficient for you. Now therefore, from the many books which he published, let us record at least a few:

*On the Holy Trinity*, fifteen books: “The reader about to read these things which concerning the Trinity.”

*On the City of God*, twenty-two books: “The most glorious city of God.”

*On the Predestination of the Saints*, two books: “Indeed, to have said, I know the Apostle.”

*On Genesis According to the Letter*, twelve books: “All divine Scripture.”

*On Genesis, Unfinished*, one book: “Concerning the obscure things of natural matters.”

*On Genesis against the Manichaeans*, two books: “If the Manichaeans would choose.”

*On the Academics*, three books: “Would that, Romanianus, men.”

*On the Happy Life*, one book: “If to the harbor of philosophy.”

*Also On Order*, three books: “On the order of things.”
