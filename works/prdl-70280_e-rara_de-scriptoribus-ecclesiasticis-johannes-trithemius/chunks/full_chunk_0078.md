Who would doubt that he also wrote other things, especially letters, both many and elegant, which have not come into our hands? He at last, in his old age, became mindless and foolish, and died in the year of the Lord 565, in the thirteenth indiction. Justin the Younger succeeded him.

Dionysius, a Roman abbot, surnamed, as is reported, Exiguus: a man certainly great and most distinguished in talent and knowledge, learned in the sacred Scriptures, and nobly instructed in the discipline of secular letters; renowned in Greek and Latin eloquence; an outstanding computist and reckoner of times. He wrote some little works in the Latin language, of which I have found only the following: year 532.

A Great Paschal Cycle: 1 book. “The reckoning of the paschal feast [unclear].”
On the Reckoning of the Paschal Feast: 1 book.

He also translated from Greek into Latin the Life of Saint Pachomius the abbot, and also the book of Gregory of Emesa On the Formation of Man, the writings of Proterius, bishop of Alexandria, to Pope Leo, and certain other things. He began his cycle in the year of the Lord 532, which was the last year of the great paschal cycle, which, once completed, always returns to the same point. He therefore flourished under Justinian the Great, in the year of the Lord 540.

Hecundus, bishop of Hermiane, richly learned in the sacred Scriptures by reading the ancients, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, is said to have written some volumes for the edification of readers. Of these, only a great and excellent work has come down to us, composed in an elegant style and furnished with great authorities from the ancients:

In Defense of the Three Chapters: 12 books.

He flourished under the emperor Justinian, in the year of the Lord 540.

Justinian, bishop of Valencia: a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of humane letters. From the same mother he had three brothers who were bishops, namely Justus, Nebridius, and Elpidius, learned and erudite men who composed various little works; these, however, have by no means come into our hands. He himself, among other things, composed for a certain man named Rusticus:

On Five Questions: 1 book. And certain other things.

He flourished in Spain under the emperor Justinian, in the year of the Lord 540.

Prigius, bishop of the church of Pax in Spain: learned in the divine Scriptures, most erudite in secular letters, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech. He expounded the sacred Scriptures so gloriously and profoundly that, in comparison with him, the ancient writers would seem not to have expounded them. Of his little works there survives a notable volume, subtle in meaning and splendid in speech:

On the Apocalypse of John: 1 book.
On the Song of Songs: 1 book.

He also published many other things, which have not come into our hands.
