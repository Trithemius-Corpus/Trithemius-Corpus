He flourished in the times of Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Dinus de Garbo, a Florentine by country, a most skilled doctor of the art of medicine, and also nobly learned in the principles of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, published in his field, for students, little works not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these are reported:

Commentaries on Galen.
On the harmfulness of varying complexion: one book.
On Hippocrates, On the Nature of the Foetus: one book.
A miscellany of questions: one book.
On Avicenna also: one book.
Various works also in philosophy.
And certain other works.

His father Bruno, a most skilled physician in the art of surgery, handed this Dinus over to Thaddaeus, the Florentine physician, to be instructed in that science.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

William of Cuneo, a Gaul by nation, a most learned interpreter of civil law, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, who in that faculty in his time had few more learned than himself, magnificently adorned the school of Orleans by teaching and writing. He published for professors of laws little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity:

On the Old Digest: twenty-four books.
On the Code also: nine books.
And various other works.

He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Jacobus of Viterbo, archbishop of Naples, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, and no less venerable in conduct than in learning, wrote for the edification of readers little works not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have not yet deserved to see any. But there is reported to be by him, addressed to Pope Clement V:

On the Governance of Christians: one book.
On the Sentences: three books.
Quodlibets also: one book.
And some other works.

He flourished in the times of Henry of Luxembourg, the seventh emperor, and Pope Clement V, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Heinricus of Brussels, a monk of the monastery of Affligem, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man studious in divine writings and skilled through continual exercise, an excellent philosopher and computist, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote some commendable little works, by which he became widely known in his time. Of these are reported:

On the method of computus: one book.
A calendar, on [unclear]: one book.
And certain other works, which have not yet by any means come to my knowledge.
