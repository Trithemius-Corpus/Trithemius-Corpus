Albert, bishop of Regensburg, of the Order of Friars Preachers, German by nation, Swabian by homeland, born at Lauingen, a man very studious and learned in divine writings, and second to none in secular philosophy in his own time; subtle in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, devout and most holy in life and conduct. Because of his doctrine and learning he was called “the Great.” He taught sacred letters and Aristotelian philosophy for many years, first at Paris, afterward at Cologne, where, among others, he had Thomas of Aquino as an outstanding disciple. Then, having been raised to the episcopate of the church of Regensburg, after he had governed it for some years he left it; for love of salutary study he returned to his convent at Cologne, where he persevered in teaching and writing until the end of his life.
