*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 66

...sufficiently learned in the disciplines of philosophy; subtle in intellect; scholastic in speech; who among the ancients was of great authority. He wrote many and various little works in both laws, by which the memory of his name came to the knowledge of many. Of these, a few have come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following:

A *Summa of Cavillations*, seven books: “For the honor of the supreme Trinity.”
Another, entitled *Judgment*, four books: “At our beginning [unclear].”
A table of the *Decree*, two books: “The book of the decrees of distinctions.”
A table of the *Decretals*, one book: “For those wishing readily.”
A concordance of the *Decree* and the *Decretals*, one book: “A treatise concerning constitutions.”
Various questions.

He taught, however, in the city of Bologna, where he composed the aforesaid works and others.

Hugo of Barcelona, of the Order of Preachers, cardinal priest of the title of Saint Sabina of the most holy Roman Church: a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, who before entering the order had been a master of sacred theology, and had many distinguished disciples. He wrote several excellent little works, by which he consecrated his name to immortality.
