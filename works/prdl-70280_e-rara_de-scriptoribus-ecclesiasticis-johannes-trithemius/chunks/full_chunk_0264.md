[Marcus Antonius Sabellicus?], the most celebrated poet of all our age, adorning Venice with books by his learning, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, skilled in both languages; to him Hermolaus Barbarus, the Venetian archbishop and patriarch of Aquileia, once composed a book on the precepts for writing history. He wrote, both in verse and in prose, certain distinguished volumes, by which he secured perpetual memory for his name among posterity. Of these I have seen the following: a very celebrated work addressed to the Venetian senate, containing, from the beginning of the city:
History and Deeds of the Venetians, 34 books, beginning: “I, about to write from the city...”
On the Antiquity of Aquileia [unclear], 6 books, beginning: “And if from all the writers...”
On the Sontian Flood, a poem, one book, beginning: “The swift [river] pours forth from Illyrian [unclear]...”
On the Sontian Slaughter, a poem, one book, beginning: “Whither, headlong Fortune, do you rush?”
On the Carnic Fire, one book, beginning: “[unclear], night set forth on spread wheels...”
On the Shipwreck of Peter’s Boat, one book, beginning: “The gracious vessel is tossed.”
On the Inventors of Things and Arts, one book, beginning: “Among the pleasing things of study you are accustomed...”
On the Origin of Udine, a poem, one book, beginning: “These things [unclear] on the ground...”
In Praise of Mary, fourteen elegies, one book, beginning: “The gracious day of the Virgin [unclear].”
I have seen nothing of the rest.
He is renowned today among the Venetians in very high esteem, composing many and various things, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we write this, 1494.

Hubertus Leonardus, bishop of Dariensis, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, famous for eloquence, who once taught theology with distinction in the Paris academy, wrote many highly commendable little works, by which he made his name known with glory. Of these the following are reported:
On the Genealogy of the Noble Franks and Their Deeds, to Louis, bishop of Liege, one book.
On the Gospel of Luke, one book.
On the Governance of Princes, three books.
On Ecclesiastical Immunity, three books.
Against the Heretics of Nivelles, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
A Notable Lenten Collection, one book.
And many other things.
When he was inquisitor of heretical depravity against certain heretics of Nivelles through the diocese of Liege, he confuted and defeated them with the sword of the Holy Spirit, writing the aforesaid very celebrated work, containing twenty-eight principal propositions; in it, vigorously and eloquently attacking just as many erroneous articles of theirs, he rejected them. He flourished in Germany, from which he came, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1490. And I believe that he is still alive.
