Anthony, of the Order of Friars Preachers, archbishop of Florence, an Italian by birth, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent, distinguished in eloquence, famous in delivering sermons to the people, of celebrated reputation, and no less venerable for his life and conduct than for his learning. He wrote many splendid volumes, by which he labored magnificently for the edification of the Church militant. Among these there is a very famous great work, divided into four volumes of no small size, which he entitles:
A Summa of cases of conscience: three books, “They have been magnified.”
A huge and notable Chronicle: three books, “I shall speak propositions from [unclear].”

On ecclesiastical censure: a book, “Turn to the Lord.”
Sermons on the season: a book. And certain other works.
