Acacius, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, a man indeed practiced in the divine Scriptures, but perverted by Arian impiety, was held in such great regard by the emperor Constantius that he obtained the appointment, in the place of Pope Liberius, of Felix, an Arian priest, as bishop. This Acacius, because he was one-eyed, was called Monoculus. He wrote many and various treatises, which truly would have been useful if they had had a Catholic author. Of these, the more important are said to be those which we have set down below:

On Ecclesiastes, 17 books.

[Sethematon], 1 book.

[Simmiton], 1 book.

He was distinguished under the emperor Constantius, in the year of the Lord 350.

Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Aquitaine, a man highly venerable in character, life, and learning, suffered many wrongs from the Arians for the faith of Christ. For, through the faction of Saturninus, bishop of Arles, he was banished from the synod of Beziers to Phrygia; for four years he bore harsh exile with steadfast courage. There, by his prayers, he is read to have called a dead man back to life. Since he was a man most learned in every respect, he composed many little works. Concerning him, the following are reported as to their number:

Against Dioscorus, 1 book.

Against Valens and Ursatius, 1 book.

On the Holy Trinity against the Arians, 12 books. “As I consider.”

Defense of the Catholic Faith, 1 book. “God, who is everywhere, everywhere.”

Against the Arians, 1 book. “To the most beloved brothers.”

On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book. “The step which Matthew.”

On the Synods, to the bishops of Gaul, etc., 1 book. “I had resolved with myself.”

On Certain Psalms, 22 treatises. “The time for speaking has now passed.”

On the Psalm “Blessed are the undefiled,” 42 treatises.

To the emperor Constantius, 3 books.

Hymns, 1 book.

Mysteries, 1 book.

On the Book of Job, 1 book. “To the most blessed, most glorious.”

Against Auxentius, 1 book.

On Heresies, 1 book.

On the Song of Songs, 1 book.

Chronicle, 1 book.

Letters to various persons, 1 book.

He also published some other works which have not come into our hands. At length, after returning from his exile to his own city, he died under the emperor Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 371, in the fourteenth indiction; his feast is celebrated on the Ides of January.

Victorinus Afer, a man of consular rank, the most celebrated rhetorician of all in his time, taught rhetoric in the city of Rome under the emperor Constantine, where, because of the excellence of his learning, he earned a bronze statue in the Forum of Trajan. Converted to the faith of Christ in his old age, he published many treatises on the holy Scriptures, in the dialectical manner, sufficiently obscure and scarcely accessible even to very learned men. Of these the following are extant:

Against the Arians, 1 book.

On the Holy Trinity, 1 book. “In the first discourse of this work.”

Against Heretics, 1 book. “Logos, or,” etc.

On the Homoousion also, 1 book. “Life lives, and life [is] one, not [unclear].”

On the Trinity, hymns, 1 book. “Be present, true light, Father.”
