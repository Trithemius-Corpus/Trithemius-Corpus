Franciscus Mataracius of Perugia, a man learned in secular literature and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, a very celebrated orator and poet, highly skilled in both languages, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these there is extant a very celebrated little work addressed to the young Petrus Paulus Cornelius:

*On Composing Verses*, one book: “The ancients [held] the poetic art.”

*Letters to Various Persons*, [unclear] book[s].

Many epigrams.

And many other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Supreme Pontiff Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1475. And I do not know whether he is still alive.

Dominicus Mancinus, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular literature, outstanding in verse and prose, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain approved little works, by which he made his name known. Of these I have read only that treatise which he composed in elegant verse:

*On the Passion of the Lord*, one book: “Not the praises of men, nor...”

He wrote other works.

He is said still to be alive and to be writing various things, under Maximilian, most noble king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Ludovicus Brunus, Italian by nation, from the marquisate of Montferrat, a man most learned in every respect, a very celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, a laureate poet, an outstanding philosopher and orator, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, is said to have fashioned many distinguished little works in both kinds of writing; but I do not recall that any of them has come into our hands except that treatise which he composed in elegant song:

*On the Coronation of King Maximilian*, one book: “Holy rock through eternity.”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He is still alive and is said to be writing various things under the same King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Jacobus Sprenger, German by nation, provincial of Cologne of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and clear in speech, when he had earlier been appointed by Pope Innocent VIII as inquisitor of heretical depravity, together with Heinrich Institoris, theologian of the same order, compiled and wrote, with the same colleague assisting him, a volume against little women who are witches, instruments of the devil, for the protection of the simple, which he entitled:

*The Hammer of Witches*, three books: “[Whether?] to assert witches.”

Whether he has written anything further I do not know.

He is alive down to the present day in his convent at Cologne, under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.
