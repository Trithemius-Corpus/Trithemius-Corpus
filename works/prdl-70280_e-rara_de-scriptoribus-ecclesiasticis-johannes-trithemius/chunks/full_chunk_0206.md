Petrus, bishop of Nova Civitas, an Italian by birth from the town of Bruniquelli, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in ability and renowned in eloquence. He published several little works of great usefulness, by which, with magnificently exercised talent, he entrusted his memory to posterity. Of these, the following are reported. A certain notable work in which he reduces all the histories of the New and Old Testament to alphabetical order, and arranged them under individual topics:

On the Sentences: four books.
On the Proverbs of Solomon: one book.
Also on Ecclesiastes: one book.
On the Song of Songs: one book.
And many other things.

Moreover, he is said to have adorned almost the whole Bible with excellent commentaries. He flourished under Rupert, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Laurentius Justinianus, a Venetian by birth, of the Celestine Order, and afterwards patriarch of Venice, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in the conduct of his life; although he could have possessed the greatest riches and honors of this world, scorning all things for the love of Christ, he entered the aforesaid order. In it, shining with great religion and abstinence, he published very devout little works for the edification of the simple, whose titles are reported below:

The Tree of Life: one book.
On Monastic Discipline: one book.
On the Marriage of the Word and the Soul: one book.
On the Agony of Christ: one book.
On the Interior Conflict: one book.
On the Lamentation of the Church: one book.
On the Sacrament of the Altar: one book.
On the Solitary Life: one book.
On Contempt of the World: one book.
On the Lord's Discourse at Supper: one book.
On the Pastoral Office: one book.
Also on Obedience: one book.
On the Degrees of Perfection: one book.
Forty sermons: one book.

He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1410. He was buried at Venice in the cathedral church. At his tomb many miracles are divinely performed. His life was clearly described by Bernardus Justinianus, his nephew.

Augustinus of Rome, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, prior general, a man very devoted to and deeply learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly educated in secular philosophy, outstanding in ability and renowned in eloquence, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there survive:

On the Sentences: four books.
On all the epistles of Paul: fourteen books.
On the Apocalypse of John: one book.
On Free Will: one book.
On Original Sin: one book.
