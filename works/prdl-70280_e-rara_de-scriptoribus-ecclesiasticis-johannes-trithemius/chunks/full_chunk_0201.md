Johannes de Duren, of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, German by nation, a man studious in the Holy Scriptures and nobly practiced in them, of great and distinguished reputation in delivering sermons to the people. He is said to have fashioned certain little works for the guidance of simple priest-preachers, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survive: *Sermones de tempore*, one book; *Sermones de sanctis*, one book; *Quadragesimale*, one book, “Sanctificate the fast ...”; “When a suitable confessor ...”; *De septem peccatis mortalibus*, one book, “There is a way which seems right to a man”; *De occultis vitiis*, one book; *De confessione*, one book.

He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Hilmannus de Hachenberg, of the Order of Friars Minor, German by nation, from the diocese of Trier, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and learned by long practice, having a polished tongue through practice in speaking; in delivering sermons to the people he shone forth as a very great and most renowned man. He wrote certain little works, not to be despised by simple persons, by which, fruitfully applying his talent, he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survive: *Sermones de tempore*, one book, “There shall be signs ... he foretold”; *Sermones de sanctis*, one book; *Sermones per quadragesimam*, one book.

And certain others.

He flourished in his convent at Koblenz, where he died and was buried.

Johannes de Lignano, a very celebrated jurist, well instructed in the disciplines of the philosophers, outstanding in talent, renowned for eloquence. He is said to have fashioned certain distinguished little works in canon law; by applying himself to these he spent his time usefully and also left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, as Franciscus de Zabarellis testifies in the prologue on the Clementines, there survive compilations: *Super Clementinis*, one book; and certain others.

He flourished under Rupert the Bavarian, king of the Romans, and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1405.

[Hugo?] de Sletstadt, German by nation, a man studious and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently learned in the disciplines of the philosophers, subtle in talent and an outstanding disputant of scholastic questions. He wrote certain, as they say, distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survives: *Quaestiones sententiarum*, three books, a work [unclear]; and certain others. He was, moreover, in his determinations, a follower of the Order of Friars Minor of the holy father Bonaventure, a clear interpreter, explaining those things which in that man’s singular philosophy could have seemed obscure and rather uncommon to simple persons.
