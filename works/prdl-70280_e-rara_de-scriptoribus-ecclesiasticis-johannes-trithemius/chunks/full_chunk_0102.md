He also wrote a chant on the Holy Trinity from Alcuin, which we use in the octave of Pentecost; on the finding of Saint Stephen; and a nocturnal chant on Saint Lambert, bishop and martyr; all of which, together with certain others, he composed with authentic and sweet melody. He flourished in the times of Louis, son of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 910.

Adulphus, a monk of Flavigny, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and in his own time most learned in all the studies of humane letters, subtle in intellect, profound in understanding, sweet in speech, ornate and eloquent, acute and very distinguished in expounding the sacred books. He wrote two great and splendid volumes: *On Leviticus*, 20 books; *On the Epistles of Paul*, 14 books; *A History of the Franks*, 1 book; also *A Brief Chronicle*, 1 book. Besides these he published many other works, which have not come to my knowledge. There are those who write that he was a monk of the monastery of Fulda in Germany.

Smaragdus, abbot of the monastery of Saint Michael, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and practiced in constant study of them, a guide of regular discipline, by expounding the holy Scriptures made his name known far and wide and acquired a great reputation for his knowledge and way of life. For he wrote many excellent little works. Of these I have found only the following: *On the Rule of Saint Benedict*, 1 book; *The Diadem of Monks*, 1 book; *On the Whole Psalter*, 1 book; *On the Epistles and Gospels for the Year*, 1 book; also several *Sermons*, 1 book. But concerning his remaining little works I have been able to find nothing anywhere. They say, however, that he published many commentaries and treatises on the divine Scriptures. But he also expounded the major Donatus, and explained many authors of secular literature.

Indichinus, a monk of the monastery of Corvey in Saxony, of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, prompt in intellect and clear in speech, excellent in verse and prose. He wrote in another style many very excellent little works. Of these I have so far found the following. From the beginning of the Saxons down to the death of Otto, the first emperor, he wrote to Mechtild, his daughter: *A History of the Saxons*, 3 books; *The Deeds of Otto the First*, 1 book; *The Passion of Thecla the Virgin*, in verse, 1 book; *The Life of Paul the First Hermit*, 1 book. He flourished under Otto the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 950.

Vtrandus, deacon of the church of Pavia, an Italian by nation, sprung from the city of Pavia, once scribe and secretary of Berengar, king of Italy, a man very learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, keen and forceful in intellect, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, and no less excellent in verse and prose. He is reported to have written certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known. Driven from Italy by King Berengar, he came into Germany and stayed at Frankfurt. He wrote a notable historical work containing the history of his time and the tyranny of that same Berengar; this, because of the series of injuries received from him, he entitled: *Antapodosis*, that is, *Retribution*, 7 books.
