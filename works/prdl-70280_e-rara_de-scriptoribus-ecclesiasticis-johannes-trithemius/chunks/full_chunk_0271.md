Moreover he translated many little works from Greek into Latin for our common benefit: Xenophon’s *Apology for Socrates*; *Nestorius the Heretic*; the *Confession of Cyprian*; Proclus *On the Blessed Virgin*; some of Lucian’s *Dialogues of the Dead*; a dialogue of the same author *On the Council of the Gods*; the *Monomachia* from Homer’s *Iliad*, on the duel of Paris and Menelaus, in the German language, in verse; Epiphanius *On the Life and Death of the Prophets*; Maximus of Tyre, the Platonic philosopher, in the oration: *If God Made Good Things, Whence Do Evils Come?* He lives to this day, devoted to letters, under Maximilian the king, and under his own prince Eberhard of Württemberg, whose illustrious name that distinguished Capnion renders still more illustrious by his talent, in the year in which we write these things, 1494.

Conradus Leontorius, commonly called von Leonberg, of German nation, a monk of the monastery of Maulbronn, of the Cistercian order, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently taught in secular matters, a philosopher, an orator, and a poet not to be rejected, skilled, as they say, in the three principal languages, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote some little works in verse and prose, by which he made his name known. Conradus Celtis Protucius, of German nation, born from Franconia in Germany, a man most learned in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, a fluent orator, an outstanding astronomer and cosmographer, a laureate poet, also not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, skilled in both languages, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, wrote certain very fine little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he commended his memory to posterity.
