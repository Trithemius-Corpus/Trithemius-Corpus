Reinerus of Forlì, a jurist most celebrated among all men in his time, teacher of Bartolus of Saxoferrato, a man distinguished in talent and eloquence, is said to have composed various little works in civil law, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. But I have not been able to find any of them anywhere. He died under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1358, the eleventh indiction.

Simon of Cascia, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, and no less venerable for the holiness of his life than for his knowledge, since he is said to have been illumined by the spirit of prophecy in the Florentine convent, produced several volumes of great usefulness, in which he shows himself a learned and devout man. Among these I have found a notable work on the whole Gospel, commonly entitled:

The Deeds of the Savior, fifteen books. [A bono super bono creato?]
On Christian Doctrine, one book.
On Patience, one book.
An Exposition of the Creed, one book.
On the Mirror of the Cross, one book.
On the Discipline of Spiritual Men, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Simon of Speyer, German by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, master of Paris, very learned in the holy Scriptures and skilled in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse, taught theology gloriously for a long time in the school of Cologne, and gained from all men great praise for his learning. He wrote a notable volume:

On the Sentences, four books.
Also Against the Jews, one book.
A Postil read at Cologne, one book.
Besides these he produced some other works, but they have by no means come to our knowledge.

Ertorius, whom some call Fortanerius, of the order of Friars Minor, the nineteenth minister general, English by nation, Welsh by homeland, afterward archbishop of Ravenna and finally patriarch of Grado, cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, produced certain distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Among these there exists a notable work:

On Augustine’s City of God, twenty-two books.

Of the rest I have found nothing. Finally, he is also said to have labored over compilations of the books of the Bible. He flourished; he was elected minister general of his order in the chapter at Marseille, under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1356, the ninth indiction.

Albericus Roxiatus, of Bergamo by homeland, an eminent professor of both laws, a very illustrious interpreter, also excellently learned in the other secular letters, ready in intellect and orderly in eloquence, produced many well-instructed volumes on the laws, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to later generations. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, twelve books.
On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.
On the New Digest, twelve books.
On the Infortiatum, thirteen books.
On the Institutes, four books.
