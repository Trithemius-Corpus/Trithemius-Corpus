Theodolus, a priest, Italian by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, coming from Italy into Coelesyria, is remembered there as having been ordained priest and finally bishop; and by writing and composing many things he transmitted his name to posterity. Of his little works I have found only a metrical work on the miracles of the Old Testament and the fables of the poets, to which he gave the title:

Eclogues: one book.
On the harmony of the Scriptures: one book.
Concerning the rest I have found nothing.
He flourished in the year of the Lord 480 under the emperor Zeno, under whom he also died.

Eugenius, bishop of Carthage in Africa, a learned and holy man, a public confessor of the undivided Trinity, endured many injuries from the Arians under Huneric, king of the Vandals, who after Genseric his father persecuted Christians in Africa. For with many other bishops, after their tongues had been cut out, he was sent into exile. He wrote, moreover, certain little works full of faith and learning, of which the following are reported:

Exposition of the Catholic Faith: one book.
A disputation held with the Arians: one book.
An apologetic for the faith: one book.
To his church, while he was going into exile: a letter.
He is nevertheless said to have written other things, but they have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

Felix, the third pope, Roman by nation, after Saint Simplicius the pontiff sat in the chair of Peter for eight years; a good, learned, and holy man, who, kindled with zeal for the faith, condemned Peter and Acacius, Eutychian bishops, and banished them into exile. For this reason, holding a synod at Rome, he established many things for ecclesiastical utility, and wrote:

A synodal decree: one book.
Several decretal letters: one book.
He died under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 493, in the first indiction.

Gennadius, priest of the church of Marseille, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, perfectly instructed in both Greek and Latin eloquence, composed many little works, and translated not a few volumes of the ancient fathers from Greek into the Latin tongue. Concerning his own writings he himself, at the end of *Illustrious Men*, listed the following:

On his faith, to Pope Gelasius: one book.
Against Nestorius: six books.
Against Eutyches: eleven books.
Against Pelagius: three books.
Against all heresies: eight books.
On illustrious men: one book. Jacobus, surnamed the Wise.
On the heresy of the thousand years: one book.
He wrote these and certain other things, but they have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 490.

Gelasius, the first pope, African by nation, sat after Felix in the pontificate for four years; a holy man and most learned in the divine Scriptures. Soon after entering upon the pontifical office, he sent into exile the Manichaeans whom he found in the city, and publicly burned their books at the basilica of Saint Mary Major.
