Melito the Asian, bishop of Sardis, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and no less venerable for holiness than for doctrine, shone forth renowned among the Catholic preachers. He wrote many excellent volumes for the benefit of the Church of the faithful, of which these are reported:

On Easter, 2 books
On the Life of the Prophets, 1 book
On the Fallen, 1 book
On the Body and the Soul, 1 book
On Baptism, 1 book
On Truth, 1 book
On the Generation of Christ, 1 book
On Prophecy, 1 book
Of Eclogues, 6 books
On the Passing of Saint Mary, 1 book: “Melito, servant of Christ, bishop.”
On the Church, 1 book
On the Lord’s Day, 1 book
On the Senses, 1 book
On Faith, 1 book
Philoxenia, 1 book
That which is entitled Key, 1 book
On the Devil, 1 book
On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book

He also published other little works, which antiquity has not permitted to come down to our memory. Tertullian, commemorating in a certain passage his elegant and declamatory talent, states that he was regarded by our people as a prophet. It should certainly be noted that the book On the Passing of Saint Mary, ascribed to him, is reckoned among the apocrypha, 15, distinction, Sancta Romana. He flourished under the emperor Marcus Antonius Verus, in the year of the Lord 160.

Apollinaris of Hierapolis in Asia, bishop, a learned man and distinguished defender of the Christian faith, renowned in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote little works not of contemptible reading, in which he showed himself a man not so much learned as zealous. Of these, the following are reported:

An Apologeticum for the Christians, 2 books
Against the Gentiles, 1 book
On Truth, 3 books
Against the Cataphrygians, 2 books
