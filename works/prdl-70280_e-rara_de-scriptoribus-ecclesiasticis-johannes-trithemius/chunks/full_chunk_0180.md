Johannes de Saxonia, of the Order of Friars Minor, German by nationality, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, sufficiently instructed both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, and of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people. He published certain distinguished volumes of his talent, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these there is extant:
A Summa on cases, one book.
And other works which I have not seen.
He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Gerardus de Senis, Italian by nationality, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man by no means moderately learned in the holy Scriptures, skilled through long exercise in both laws, subtle and acute in intellect; Johannes Andreas, his contemporary, often commends and cites his learning. He wrote some distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, the following are said to be the more important:
On the first book of the Sentences, one book.
On contracts of usury, one book.
On prescriptions, one book.
I have found nothing concerning the rest.
He flourished with Johannes Andreas, under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Johannes de Rupe Scissa, of the Order of Friars Minor, very zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy; led astray by some spirit, lest I say induced, he began, as though a prophet, to foretell the desolation of the Catholic Church and certain other terrible things. He was cast into prison by the ministers of his order; a lying spirit was found in his mouth. He wrote in chains to a certain cardinal:
His Revelations, one book, beginning: “Most reverend father in Christ.”
Also on the four books of the Sentences.
And certain other things. He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Gerardus, bishop of Savona, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, Bergamasque by homeland, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in canon law, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence, no less distinguished in life than in learning, composed for the use of readers little works not to be despised. By profitably exercising his talent in these, he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these they report:
On the Sentences, three books.
Two quodlibets, one [book].
On the Song of Songs, one book, beginning: “The two wings of the cherubim.”
On the Epistle to the Hebrews, one [book].
On the sixth book of the Decretals, “On relics and the veneration of saints,” [unclear], in the year of the Lord 1340.
He is also said to have written many other little works, which have not come to my knowledge. In the Parisian school he ascended the magisterial chair. He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Thomas de Garbo, Florentine by homeland, son of Dino, once a physician, a man learned and skilled in medicine, nobly instructed in the disciplines of the philosophers, adhering to the footsteps of his most learned father. He published, in his own faculty, little works not to be spurned by physicians, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these there is extant a very notable Summa of medicine, one book.
And certain other works. He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, fourth emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1340.
