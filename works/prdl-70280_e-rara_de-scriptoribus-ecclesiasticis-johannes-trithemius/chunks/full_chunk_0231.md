Henry of Herp, of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a German by nationality, of the province of Cologne, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of pontifical law, outstanding in intellect and clear in speech, of a very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, no less venerable for his religious life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, wrote little works of authority not to be rejected, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of their number the following are reported. A large and notable volume:
On the Precepts of the Decalogue: 1 book, “I find another law in...”
Sermons on the Season: 1 book, “Behold, your king comes.”
Sermons on the Saints: 1 book, “Rabbi, where do you dwell?” according to [unclear].
Sermons for Advent: 1 book, “Who will be able to think...”
On the Three Parts of Penitence: 1 book, “No one can serve two masters.”
And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes de Turrecremata, of the Order of Friars Preachers, bishop of Sabina and cardinal priest of the Holy Roman Church, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and an excellent preacher of sermons, ready in intellect and clear in speech, wrote many little works of his intellect, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are reported. To Arsenius, monk of the monastery of Saint Benedict at Lando, which he himself held in commendam, he composed a large work, not to be rejected:
On the Rule of Saint Benedict: 1 book, “Since, with the help of our Lord...”
On the Whole Psalter: 1 book, “To the most blessed father and merciful...”
Questions on the Sunday Gospels: 1 book, “To the holy and most venerable...”
Questions on the Gospels for Saints: 1 book, “Recently the reverend father enjoined upon me...”
On Blessed Water: 1 book, “Divine Scripture teaches us...”
On the Salvation of the Soul: 1 book.
Sermons on the Season: 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints: 1 book.
And many other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and the supreme pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes of Mechelen, a German by nationality, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures and not moderately learned in scholastic philosophy, excellent in intellect and renowned for knowledge, taught theology publicly for many years in the school of Cologne and acquired great praise for the light of his erudition. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his intellect, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported. A notable work:
On the Sentences: 4 books.
On the Psalter: 1 book.
Various Questions: 1 book.
Sermons and Collations: 1 book.
And many other things. He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.
