The other works that he composed have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1325.

[Gerardus?] of Bologna, of the order of the Brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel, the eleventh prior general, a Parisian doctor, most learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, venerable no less for his way of life than for his knowledge, governed his order energetically for twenty years. He wrote several volumes of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name even to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences: three books.
Quodlibets: one book.
Ordinary Questions: one book.
Sermons on the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
And many others.

He began an excellent Summa of theology, which nevertheless, overtaken by death, he left unfinished. He died under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1317, in the fifteenth indiction. He was buried at Avignon.

John of Colonna, Roman by country, of the order of the Preaching Friars, archbishop of Messina, a man possessing talent in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, a most sagacious investigator of the histories of past time, wrote works of noble talent not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there exists the volume that he entitled:

Sea of Histories: ten books.
Various letters to various persons: one book.
He also produced some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Martinus, of the order of the Preaching Friars, penitentiary of the lord pope in the city of Rome, and afterwards, as they report, bishop of a certain city, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain excellent works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have seen only:

A Table of the Decretum: one book. Among all other things of the faithful.
A concise Chronicle: two books. Since to know the times of the highest ones.
Sermons on the season: one book. Knowing that now the hour is.
Sermons on the saints: one book. Confession is made with the mouth unto salvation.
On various miracles: one book. A certain householder.
Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320. There are some who write that this Martinus was of the order of our father Benedict, but in my judgment they have been deceived by the resemblance of the name of Marianus Scotus, of our order, whom I mentioned above, who himself also wrote a chronicle. There are likewise others who think he should be joined to the college of the Cistercian order; these either ignorance of the facts or spite has clouded.

Gerardus Odonis, of the order of Friars Minor, the eighteenth minister general, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in scholastic philosophy, subtle in intellect, clear in speech, and quite suitable for disputing, is said to have composed certain works in the holy Scriptures. Of these few have become known to me:

On the Sentences: three books.
Also on Logic: one book.
