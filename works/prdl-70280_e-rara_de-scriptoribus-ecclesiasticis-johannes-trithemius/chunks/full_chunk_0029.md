Phileas, bishop of the city of Thebaid in Egypt, a noble and most learned man, distinguished no less in character than in knowledge of the Scriptures, after many labors and various treatises published for the benefit of the faithful, at last under the emperor Maximian became a martyr for the faith of Christ and flew away to the heavenly ones. Of his writings there is extant a disputation held *Against the Unjust Judge*, one book; *On the Praise of Martyrs*, one book. Other things that he wrote are unknown to us, although many are reported by the fathers. He flourished under the prince Maximian, in the year of the Lord 240.

Arnobius the presbyter, an outstanding philosopher and rhetorician: while he was still a pagan and teaching rhetoric, he was called by divine mercy and began to profess himself a Christian. And when, going to the bishops, he sought to be baptized, they did not believe him, fearing that a man swollen with worldly eloquence was trying to mock the sacraments of the faith; and therefore they deferred their judgment. But Arnobius, seeing that no trust was being placed in him, as proof of his sincerity wrote and offered to the bishops an excellent volume, *Against the Nations*, seven books. Also *On the Psalter*, one book; *On Rhetorical Instruction*, one book. He also published many other things which have not come to my knowledge. He taught rhetoric in Africa under the prince Diocletian, and had many excellent and noble pupils who turned out to be most learned men. He therefore flourished around the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 300.

John Climacus, which in Latin means “of the ladder,” abbot of Mount Sinai, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellent in talent, gentle and humble, devout and most holy in life and conduct, most urgently sought and obtained that he be made [unclear]. Then, after nineteen years, when he had learned, according to the rule of monks, to fight and conquer as a solitary, with the permission of his abbot he sought the wilderness, in which he lived alone with God for many years in all holiness. Finally, when his most holy life had become known to everyone, by the unanimous consent of all he was elected abbot, and, though resisting, was recalled to the monastery. He wrote to John, abbot of Raythu, a very famous work, very suitable for the instruction of monks, containing thirty sermons, which is entitled *On the Thirty Steps*, one book, “From good and concerning good and” [unclear]. *To the Shepherd*, one sermon, one book. In the lower book, several letters to monks, one book. And certain other things.
