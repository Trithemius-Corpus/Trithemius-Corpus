Titus, bishop of Bostra, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and notably instructed in secular letters, keen and forceful in intellect, is said to have composed many little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

Against the Manichaeans: two books.
Commentaries on the Gospel: one book.
And many other things.
He flourished in the year of the Lord 340. And he died under the emperor Valens.

Damasus, pope, a Spaniard by nation, sat after Liberius in the chair of Peter for eighteen years; a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and notably instructed in secular letters, a very celebrated rhetorician and poet, and highly skilled in composing verses; subtle in intellect, devout and most holy in life; greatly beloved of the priest Saint Jerome. He wrote various little works in both kinds. Of these the following are extant:

The Deeds of the Supreme Pontiffs: one book. “Blessed Peter, of the apostles...”
On the Faith, against Heretics: one book. “After the Nicene council...”
Letters to Various Persons: one book. “Damasus, bishop of the city...”
Various poems. And other things.
He flourished under Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 370.

Apollinaris of Laodicea, bishop in Syria, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and by far most learned in grammatical instruction, keen and forceful in intellect, distinguished in speech, wrote in defense of the Catholic faith certain very excellent volumes, by which he commended his name to posterity. Of these there is extant a work Against the mad Porphyry: thirty books, very celebrated.
And certain other things.
He flourished under the emperor Gratian, in the year of the Lord 380.

Didymus of Alexandria, blind from early childhood, and for this reason entirely ignorant even of the elements, presented so great a marvel of himself that he learned dialectic and geometry, which especially requires sight, to perfection. In the divine Scriptures he was most learned, the teacher of blessed Jerome and the very close friend of Saint Antony; keen in intellect and distinguished in life. He wrote in the Greek language the following works:

Against the Arians: two books.
On Doctrines also: one book.
On the Canonical Epistles: eight books. “James, etc., just as mortal...”
On the Holy Spirit: one book. “All things indeed which are divine...”
On the book of Saint Job: one book.
On Isaiah the prophet: ten books.
On Hosea, to Jerome: four books.
On Zechariah, to the same: five books.
On the Gospel of Matthew: one book.
On the Gospel of John: one book.
On the Psalter also: one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished as an octogenarian under the emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 395.
