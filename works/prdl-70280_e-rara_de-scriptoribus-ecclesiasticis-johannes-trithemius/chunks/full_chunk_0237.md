Henricus de Herph, of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a German by nation, of the province of Cologne, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of pontifical law, excellent in talent and clear in speech, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, venerable no less for his religious life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, wrote little works of authority not to be rejected, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among them the following are reported. A large and distinguished volume:

On the precepts of the Decalogue: one book. *I find another law in*.
Sermons for the season: one book. *Behold, thy king comes*.
Sermons on the saints: one book. *Rabbi, where dwellest thou*: [unclear].
Sermons for Advent: one book. *Who will be able to think*.
On the three parts of penitence: one book. *No one can serve two lords*.
And certain other works. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes de Turrecremata, of the Order of Friars Preachers, bishop of Sabina and cardinal priest of the holy Roman Church, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and an outstanding preacher of sermons, ready in talent and clear in speech, wrote many little works of his talent, by which he made his name known. Among these the following are reported. To Arsenius, monk of the monastery of Saint Benedict, which he himself held in commendam, he composed a large and not-to-be-rejected work:

On the Rule of Saint Benedict: one book. *With the help of our Lord*.
On the whole Psalter: one book. *To the most blessed father and most merciful*.
Questions on the Sunday Gospels: one book. *To the holy and most reverend*.
Questions on the Gospels for the saints: one book. *The reverend father lately enjoined me*.
On blessed water: one book. *Divine Scripture teaches us*.
On the salvation of the soul: one book.
Sermons for the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
And several other works. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and under Pius and Paul, supreme pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes de Mechilinia, a German by nation, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures and not moderately learned in scholastic philosophy, excellent in talent and distinguished in knowledge, taught theology publicly for many years in the academy of Cologne and acquired the praise of great erudition. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among them the following are reported. A distinguished work:

On the Sentences: four books.
On the Psalter: one book.
Various questions: one book.
Sermons and conferences: one book.
And several other works. He flourished in the times of Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.
