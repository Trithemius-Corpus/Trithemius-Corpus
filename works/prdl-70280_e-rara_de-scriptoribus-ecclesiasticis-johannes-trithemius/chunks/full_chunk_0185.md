John Tacesphale, an Englishman by birth, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a learned doctor of the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent, composed in speech, the most celebrated preacher of all sermons in his time. He published works not to be despised in reading; by these, exercising his talent usefully, he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences: books 4.
On the Apocalypse of John: book 1.
Sermons on the Season: book 1.
Sermons on the Saints: book 1.
And certain others.

He flourished in his convent at Norwich, where, buried, he rests in peace.

Rupert Holkoth, an Englishman by birth, of the order of preachers, a man most learned in divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, and also an excellent preacher of sermons. He wrote many outstanding works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

Whether every wayfarer.
“The Lord is my rock and my strength.”
“This is the way; walk in it.”
Theodosius on the Life [unclear].
On the Sentences: books 4.
On the Book of Wisdom: book 1.
On the Seven Deadly Sins: book 1.
Moralizations: book 1.
On the Immortality of the Soul: book [unclear].
On the Song of Songs: book [unclear].
On Ecclesiasticus, unfinished: book [unclear].
And many others.

He died of the plague, after he had begun his lecture on the Book of Ecclesiasticus, under Charles, fourth emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1349, in the second indiction.

Nicholas Dorbin, an Englishman by birth, of the order of the brothers of most blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in divine Scriptures and excellently taught in secular philosophy, who in the University of Oxford, by lecturing and disputing, earned great praise from all. He wrote works not to be scorned in reading, by which he showed the rich and varied vein of his very lively talent. Of these there are extant:

Whether every wayfarer.
“The Lord is my rock and my strength.”
“This is the way; walk in it.”
Theodosius on the Life [unclear].
On the Sentences: books 4.
Determinations of Questions: book 1.
And certain others.

He flourished in the school at Oxford, and wrote many various volumes.

Paul of Perugia, professor of both laws and a most celebrated interpreter, and excellently learned in other humane letters, perfectly trained in Greek and Latin eloquence, was for a long time keeper and master of the library of Rupert, king of Sicily, a most learned and most studious prince. He wrote certain outstanding works by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there is extant a distinguished volume, in which there are many pleasant and useful things concerning the gods of the gentiles and of others, which he entitled:

Collections: book 1.
And certain others.

He flourished in the times of Charles, fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1350.
