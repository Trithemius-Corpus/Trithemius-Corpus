Thomas Walden, of the order of the friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, English by birth, prior general of the English province, a man most learned in divine scriptures and nobly instructed in Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect, sufficiently fit for disputation, no less distinguished in conduct than in knowledge, also an excellent declaimer of sermons, and formerly confessor and secretary of Henry V, king of the English, wrote, among the other monuments of his intellect, a notable work divided into three great volumes against the mad Wycliffite and Hussite heretics, which Pope Martin V, after it had been examined, confirmed by apostolic authority. This work is entitled:

The Doctrinal of the Church, three books: “Let God arise, let them be scattered.”

Also On the Sentences, four books.

On the books of the Ethics, ten books.

On the books of the Physics, eight books.

On the books On the Heaven and the World, four books.

On Generation and Corruption, two books.

On the books of the Meteors, four books.

On the books On the Soul, four books.

He is said to have written many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge. He died at Rouen and was honorably buried in his own convent, under Emperor Sigismund, in the last year of Pope Martin V, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1430, in the seventh indiction, on the third day of the month of November, that is, the third Nones.

Paulus de Castro, the most celebrated jurist of all his time, a man of great learning and eloquence, who for many years taught civil law gloriously, having been engaged for a public salary at Siena, Florence, and Padua, composed many little works on the laws, known and dear to the professors of this faculty, by which he left to posterity the memory of his most celebrated name. Among these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books.

On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.

On the New Digest, twelve books.

On the Infortiatum of the Digest, fourteen books.

On the Institutes, four books.

Most elegant Counsels, one book.

And several other things.

He died in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1437, in the fifteenth indiction.

Ambrosius Florentinus, general abbot of the Camaldolese order, once a hearer of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher teaching Greek letters at Venice, a man most learned both in divine scriptures and in secular studies, a most brilliant philosopher, rhetorician, and theologian, left to posterity many distinguished monuments of his intellect. He was nuncio and envoy of Pope Eugenius to the Council of Basel, where on his behalf he delivered a solemn oration to the fathers. He wrote a notable volume:

Letters to various persons, one book.

Various orations.

And several other things. He also translated into Latin from Greek the four books of blessed Dionysius the Areopagite, in a more elegant and easier style. Also the original Life of blessed John Chrysostom, for Emperor Sigismund. Likewise Basil On Virginity, for Cardinal Gabriel, afterwards Pope Eugenius IV.
