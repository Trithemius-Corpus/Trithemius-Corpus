*BOOK.*

Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, a Greek by nation, once the ornament and distinguished cultivator of Mount Carmel, a learned and holy man: by the command of Pope Celestine I he presided in the council of two hundred fathers at Ephesus, in which, condemning Nestorius and Pelagius with their errors, he uttered many elegant sentences. Since he was most erudite in the holy Scriptures, he wrote many little works, of which these are reported:
On the faith against heretics: book[s].
Against Nestorius: 1 book.
On the failing of the synagogue: 1 book.
On the birth and praise of holy Mary: 1 book.
On the paschal cycle, of 95 years: 1 book.
Of commentaries on the Gospel: 4 books.
He composed, besides, letters, homilies, and several sermons, and other various treatises, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished, presiding in the Council of Ephesus under Theodosius the Younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 432, in the 15th indiction.

Theodorus of Ancyra, bishop of Galatia, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters, especially strong in dialectic, eloquent in speech and a keen disputant, being illustrious enough in the Council of Ephesus, wrote there, in the dialectical manner, very obscurely:
Against Nestorius: 1 book.
And certain others.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, prince, in the year of the Lord 430.

Sozomen, a Greek by nation, distinguished in secular letters and a cultivator [of them], and erudite enough in the divine Scriptures, is reported to have written some little works for the edification of the Church; of which there is extant a notable work [addressed] to Theodosius the Younger, prince, from the third consulate of Crispus and Constantine, the Caesars, up to the 17th [year] of the same Theodosius:
Of ecclesiastical history: 9 books. ["A thought once [came] to me…"]
The rest which he composed have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished under the aforesaid emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 430.

Socrates, a certain [man], a Greek by nation, a learned and eloquent historiographer, distinguished, and renowned for [his] experience of great matters, wrote, from the time of the first Constantine the Great, emperor, up to the reign of Theodosius the Younger, a volume:
Of ecclesiastical history: 1 book.
He is said to have written also some others, but they have not come into our hands. And it is to be noted that three Greek authors—namely, Theodoret bishop of Cyrrhus, Sozomen, and Socrates—wrote, almost at one time, individual [histories] of ecclesiastical matters; which histories Epiphanius Scholasticus translated into Latin as they stood; out of which Cassiodorus, senator and monk, made one continuous history, which he called the Tripartite.
