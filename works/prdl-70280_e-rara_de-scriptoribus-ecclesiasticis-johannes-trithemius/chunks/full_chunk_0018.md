*On Ecclesiastical Writers*, fol. 4

He suffered at Rome under the emperor Trajan, in the year of the Lord 111.

Papias, hearer and disciple of Saint John, bishop of Hierapolis in Asia, a most steadfast propagator and defender of the Christian faith, and a disciple and zealous imitator of the holy apostles, wrote little works of no contemptible authority. Of these there survives an outstanding work, divided into five volumes, which he entitled thus:

*Explanation of the Sayings of the Lord*, five books.

Other things that he wrote we have not seen.

He first devised the novelty of the thousand years, after which he dreamed that Christ would reign again in the flesh with his elect upon the earth. Others later spread this error, and were called Chiliasts from the thousand.

He flourished in the times of Caesar Trajan, around the year of the Lord 100.

Hegesippus, a Jew by nation, converted to the faith of Christ, shone forth wonderfully in the church of God in learning and holiness; his feast, with those of the other saints, has obtained a place in the martyrology. He wrote several little works of excellent talent. Of them I have found only an outstanding work, from the time of the Lord’s Passion down to his own age:

*A History of Ecclesiastical Acts*, five books, “The Parthian war which between ...”

I have seen no other of his works.

He flourished near the apostolic times, under Antoninus Pius, in the year of the Lord 160.

Justin, converted from a philosopher to the catholic faith, an eminent lover and worshipper of Christ, when in the city of Rome he refuted Crescens the Cynic, who was blaspheming Christians, as gluttonous, afraid of death, and a follower of lust and debaucheries, was accused before the Senate through that man’s plots because he was a Christian, and was crowned with martyrdom for Christ. He wrote many distinguished little works in defense of our faith. Among them these are reported:

*Against the Gentiles, to Antoninus*, two books.

*To Aurelius, Against the Gentiles*, one book.

Likewise *Against the Gentiles*, one book.

*Reproofs Against the Gentiles*, one book.

*On the Monarchy of God*, one book.

*On the Soul*, one book.

[unclear], entitled [unclear], one book.

*Against Trypho the Jew*, one book.

*Against Marcion*, one book.

*Against All Heresies*, one book.

He also composed many other things which have not come to our knowledge.

He flourished under Antoninus Pius, in the year of the Lord 150.

Quadratus, an Athenian, bishop after the martyr Publius, formerly a hearer and disciple of the holy apostles, a steadfast man of incomparable learning, laboring much for the usefulness of the Christian faith, made great progress: he gathered back into the unity of faith the church dispersed by fear of persecution. Since he was an eminent teacher, he wrote, among other things, on behalf of the Christians to the emperor Hadrian, full of instruction:

*An Apology*, one book.

And other things unknown to us.

At last he suffered for the defense of the catholic faith on the seventh day before the Kalends of June.

He flourished under the emperor Hadrian, in the year of the Lord 120.
