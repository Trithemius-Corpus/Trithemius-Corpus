Adelboldus, bishop of Utrecht, formerly a monk, as they say, of Lobbes, of the Order of Saint Benedict, under whose name Abbot Heriger composed the dialogue on the coming of the Lord, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, held in great esteem at the court of Saint Henry the Emperor, overthrew the unbelievers no less by arms than by learning. He wrote:

The Life of Saint Henry the Emperor: one book.
On the Praise of the Holy Cross.
On the Praise of the Blessed Mary. But he also published some other works, both in verse and in prose, which have not come to my knowledge. He died under Conrad the Younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1027, in the tenth indiction, in the eighteenth year of his episcopate, on the fifth day before the Kalends of December.

Sbertus, a monk of Canterbury, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very skilled in the art of music, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, published various treatises in both kinds of learning, by which he transmitted the fame of his name to posterity. Among other things he wrote:

The Life of Saint Dunstan, archbishop: one book.
On music also: one book.

He flourished in the times of Saint Henry the Emperor, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Dilo, the fourth abbot of Cluny, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a holy and learned man, presiding over the monastery for fifty-six years, directed the souls of many to Christ by word and example; his life, full of virtues and miracles, Peter Damian, bishop and cardinal of Ostia, described in an elegant discourse. He composed for elegant editors: one book. [unclear] of his brothers.

He died under Henry, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 1048, in the first indiction, in the eighty-seventh year of his age, on the first day of the month of January.

Evpertus, bishop of Chartres, most learned in the divine Scriptures, and, in secular letters and in all disciplines, the most learned of the learned men of his time, a distinguished poet and a most subtle dialectician, presiding for many years over the public school, trained very many highly learned pupils. Also of a most holy life, he is read to have shone with many miracles; among other things, it is said that he wrote:

Letters to various persons: one book.
In praises of Saint Mary: one book.
Various hymns and prayers,
and some other devout works.

He flourished in the times of Henry, the first king, in the year of the Lord 1010.

Bruno, bishop of Würzburg, cousin of Conrad, the second emperor, German by nation, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, and most holy in life and conduct, who because of the excellence of his merits was enrolled in the catalogue of the saints, is regarded as the special patron of Eastern Francia. From the writings of the ancient fathers he compiled a beautiful exposition:

On the whole Psalter: one book. Blessed is the man who has not gone away.

Whether he wrote anything else I do not know.

He died under Henry, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 1045, in the thirteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Kalends of June.
