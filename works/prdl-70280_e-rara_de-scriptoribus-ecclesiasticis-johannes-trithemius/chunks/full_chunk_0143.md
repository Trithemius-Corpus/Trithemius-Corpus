He commented on all the books of Aristotle. He composed many [unclear], which have not come to my knowledge. He died under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280, in the eighth indiction, in the eightieth year of his age. He was buried in the middle of the choir of his convent at Cologne. And after him there did not arise a man like him, who was so learned, instructed, and experienced in all letters, sciences, and matters. But whoever accuses him of necromancy does injury to a man beloved of God.

Simon of Tournai, priest, learned in the divine Scriptures, subtly taught in secular philosophy, a most acute dialectician and most skilled in all the liberal arts, was made master of the theological school and taught for many years in the school of Paris, where he instructed very many hearers. But while he followed Aristotle too much, he is found to have erred in certain matters. He wrote certain little works of his own talent, of which the following are reported:
His own account of the Sentences.
Various Questions: one book.
On the Symbol of Athanasius: one book.
He also published some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Thomas of Aquino, once a hearer and disciple in the convent at Cologne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in secular letters, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, easily obtained first place among all the doctors of his time.
