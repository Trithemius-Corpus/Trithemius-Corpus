Finally, it is said that he wrote many other things too, and explained the New and Old Testament for the greater part in many books; of these up to the present we have seen nothing except the works which we have marked. He flourished in great holiness, shining with doctrine and miracles, under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1124. And he died under Lothar III.

B[ertus?], abbot of the monastery of Limburg in the diocese of Speyer, of the holy order, magnificently learned, distinguished in life and conduct, so that he deserved to be visited by frequent divine revelations. For many years he devoted himself in the school of Paris to secular philosophy, and became a noble philosopher. But, considering the passing nature of the present life, he abandoned the pursuit of vanity and, returning to his homeland, became a monk in the said monastery and finally was made abbot. He wrote, however, little works not contemptible for reading; of these I have found only the following:

On the Song of Songs: 4 books. “Whoever of himself and of things...”
On contempt of the world: 2 books. “The world is called by an improper name...”
On Saint John the Baptist: 1 book. “The historian of the Gospel relates...”
Of his revelations: 1 book. “In the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1124...”

He wrote certain sermons on the saints whose bodies or patronages were held in the same monastery of Limburg. He flourished under the emperor Henry V, with no small reputation for holiness, in the year of the Lord 1124.

Rudolf, abbot of Saint-Trond in the diocese of Liège, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, a man practiced with constant zeal in the divine Scriptures, and not meanly learned in secular letters; strong in verse and prose, an excellent musician, and no less renowned in work than in knowledge. He wrote in both styles little works not to be despised, of which they report:

Against the simoniacs: 7 books.
Also a Chronicle: 1 book.
Many poems.

He also composed many chants in praise of the saints, in sweet and regular melody. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1125.

Hranco, second abbot of the monastery of Affligem, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, sufficiently instructed in secular doctrine, strong in talent and renowned for eloquence. He wrote certain commendable little works, both in meter and prose, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. While he was still a simple monk in the aforesaid monastery, at the command of Fulgentius his abbot he began a small work, which afterward, when made abbot, he completed, adorned with knowledge and eloquence:

On the grace of God: 1 book.
On the state of future glory, in meter: 1 book.
Letters to various persons: 1 book.
Sermons on the blessed Virgin: 1 book.
And certain other things.
