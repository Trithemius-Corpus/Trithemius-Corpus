He is said besides to have written many other things, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of Pope John XXIII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Baldus Perusinus, once a hearer and disciple of Bartolus of Sassoferrato, the most celebrated jurisconsult of all his time, and also nobly learned in secular philosophy, acute in talent, profound in judgment, cautious and firm in counsel, wrote many distinguished little works in both laws, by which he secured for his name eternal remembrance among posterity. Of these number the following are reported:

On the Code: nine books.
On the Old Digest: twenty-four books.
On the New Digest: twelve books.
On the Infortiatum: thirteen books.
On the Institutes: four books.
On the use of fiefs: one book.
On the Authenticae: one book.
On the second book of the Decretals: one book.
Additions to the Speculum: one book.
Many counsels: one book.
And many other things.

He died in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1423, in the first indiction, and was buried with honor at Pavia in the convent of the friars of Saint Francis, in the month of July.

Angelus Perusinus, brother of the above-mentioned Baldus, and his companion and associate in studies, most learned in civil law, distinguished in talent, and most celebrated in fame and reputation among jurisconsults, also composed many volumes in his faculty; by establishing in them monuments of an excellent intellect, he destined the memory of his name for the knowledge of many. Of these number the most elegant commentaries are reported:

On the Code: nine books.
On the Old Digest: twenty-four books.
On the New Digest: twelve books.
On the Infortiatum: thirteen books.
On the Institutes: four books.

Indeed, on the whole body of civil law; and he is said to have labored over certain other things. He died in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the same year as his brother Baldus, in the year of the Lord 1423, in the first indiction.

Petrus de [Ancharano?], Italian by nation, Bolognese by homeland, once a hearer of Baldus Perusinus, a man most skilled in both laws and not ignorant of Aristotelian philosophy; although he was bound by the bond of a wife and occupied with family affairs, nevertheless, always engaged in the study of letters, he published many little works in both laws, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these number are reported:

On the Decretals: five books.
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals: one book.
On the Clementines: one book.
On the rules of law: fourteen books.
On the Old Digest: thirteen books.
On the New Digest: twelve books.
