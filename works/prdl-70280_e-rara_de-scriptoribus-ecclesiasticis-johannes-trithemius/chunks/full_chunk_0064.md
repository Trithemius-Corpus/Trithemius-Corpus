He delivered several homilies to the people, but they have by no means come into our hands.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Eucherius, bishop of Lyon in Gaul, a learned man, imbued from youth with the discipline of monks, prompt in talent and composed in speech, by life and conversation pleasing to God and men—since he turned many from iniquity both by word and by example—wrote several excellent volumes; but few have as yet come to my knowledge.
For I have found only these:
On contempt of the world: book.
Expositions of the divine names: book.
To the sons of [Salonius?] and Veranus: book.
An Epitome of Cassian: book.
Certain letters to various [persons]: book.
To Hilary the bishop, on the praise of the hermitage: book.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Hilary, bishop of Arles—to whom, as he sought the deserts of the hermitage, Eucherius, prelate of Lyon, issued a book on the praise of the hermitage—a man learned in the divine Scriptures and eloquent enough in speech, whose merits of sanctity the Catholic Church proclaims. He was of such great piety toward the poor that, all his [goods] having been cheerfully distributed for their use, he was thereafter called, and was, the father of the poor. He wrote, among other things, in quite illustrious eloquence, [the life] of his predecessor:
The Life of Saint Honoratus, bishop: book.
Homilies almost infinite,
And certain others.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, prince, in the year of the Lord 440. But he dies under the princes Valentinian and Marcian.

Zosimus, pope, a Greek by nation—[who held] the pontificate after Saint Innocent for one year [and] eight months—a learned man, holy and beloved of God, who governed strenuously by word and example the Church committed to him, and for its edification established many things elegantly, as is contained in his deeds. He wrote, concerning the ecclesiastical state, to various [persons]:
Of letters: book.
On the Pasch, to Hilary: book.
On the priesthood, to [Hesychius?]: book.
From the report of our brother.
He dies under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 419, in the 2nd indiction, the 7th [day before] the Kalends of January.

Boniface, pope, the first, succeeding blessed Zosimus, presided over the Church three years and four months, a man of great learning and suited enough to the rule of souls, who established many things for ecclesiastical usefulness, which are had scattered in the canons of the holy fathers. He wrote for the edification of the faithful, as they report, some little works, of which I have found only:
Of decretal letters: book. ["Of the Roman Church, to which God…"]
He dies under the princes Honorius [and] Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 422, in the fifth indiction, the eighth [day before] the Kalends of November.
