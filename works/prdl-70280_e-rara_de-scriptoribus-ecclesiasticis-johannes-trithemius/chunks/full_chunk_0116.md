After these he also published other works, which he did not mark with the above-written little works.
He flourished in the times of Henry, the third emperor, the fourth king, in the year of the Lord 1110.
He carried his Chronicle down to the year 1111.

Calixtus, the second pope, a Burgundian by nation, brother of the duke of Burgundy, formerly archbishop of Vienne, sat in the pontificate after Gelasius for six years. He was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, a particular lover of Saint James the Apostle, in whose honor he raised the church of Compostela to an archbishopric. He wrote, in a composed style and with the greatest diligence:

On the Miracles of Saint James: one book. “Calixtus, bishop, servant of the servants.”
Letters to various persons: one book.
And certain other things. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Stephanus, also called Herrandus, the fourteenth bishop of the church of Halberstadt, from being abbot of the monastery of Ilsenburg, of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech. He wrote some little works not unworthy of reading, but few have come into our hands. In the person of Count Louis he wrote a very elegant and useful letter to Waltram, bishop of the church of Nienburg, defending Henry, rebel against the Roman Church:

A Defense of the Pope: one book. “As a good man from a good...”
And several letters to various persons: one book.
And certain other things. He died under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1107, in the fifteenth indiction, and was honorably buried in Reinsburg.

Bernard, first abbot of Clairvaux, of the Cistercian order, a Burgundian by nation, was a noble and holy man, who was renowned for many miracles in life and after death; learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters; subtle in intellect, adorned in speech, since indeed he neglected nothing in his writings that could contribute to the adornment of his discourse. He published many excellent little works, of which the following are reported:

On Consideration, to Eugene: five books. “The thought comes to me to dictate something.”
Of His Meditations: one book. “Many people know many things, but...”
On Contempt of the World, to clerics: one book. “To hear, as we believe...”
On Grace and Free Will: one book. “You asked me, brother Gotfrid...”
To the Knights of the Temple: one book. “When I was speaking recently before...”
The Mirror of the Religious Life: one book. “To Hugh, soldier of Christ...”
On the Cloistered Ladder: one book. “If anyone among you to amend...”
To the Carthusians: one book. “When on a certain day bodily...”
