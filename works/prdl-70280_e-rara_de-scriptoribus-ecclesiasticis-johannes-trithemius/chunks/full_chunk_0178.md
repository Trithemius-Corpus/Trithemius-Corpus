Carmel: certainly a man most learned in the Holy Scriptures, and excellently instructed in secular philosophy; of sharp and vigorous intellect, scholastic eloquence, subtle in debating and resolving questions of the Scriptures. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, three books: “One conclusion having been proposed.”
Various Determinations, one book: “It is asked whether [unclear].”
Also Various Sermons, one book.
The rest which he composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

John Estuidi, English by nation, a man easily the most learned of all in secular letters in his time, a philosopher and astronomer second to none, outstanding in talent, sweet in eloquence. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is extant a notable work of judicial astrology, on the accidents of the world, which is commonly called:

The English Summa, two books: “My intention in this book.”
Concerning the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished and completed the same Summa, as he acknowledges at its beginning, under Charles, the fourth emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1347, in the fifteenth indiction, on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of January.

Bernard, abbot of the monastery of Cassino, of the order of the holy father Benedict, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent, distinguished in life, and an eminent cultivator and propagator of regular observance. He wrote certain distinguished little works for the edification of monks, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are extant:

Mirror of Monks, one book: “He reads in Proverbs: teaching.”
On the Rule of Saint Benedict, one book.
On the regular precepts, one book.
Sermons to the Brothers, one book.
He is also said to have composed many other things, which have by no means come down to us.

Simon of Cremona, of the order of the hermit friars of Saint Augustine, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of the liberal arts, outstanding in talent, renowned in eloquence, quite excellent in declaiming sermons to the people; indeed he labored at this office for a long time at Venice, where by word and example he instructed very many. He wrote little works, not to be despised, for the instruction of the aforementioned simple people. Of these the following are extant. To Antonius, his brother, a priest:

Sermons.
On the Gospels, one book: “Lift up your heads, because.”
Also On the Epistles, one book: “Let us cast off the works of darkness.”
The rest which he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading.

Peter Raymundi of Insula Grassa, French by nation, of the order of the friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, fifteenth prior general, who after Peter de Casa of Limoges vigorously ruled his order for ten years; a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy. He wrote elegantly:

On the Sentences, four books.
And certain other things.
He flourished, and was elected general from the convent of Carcassonne in the province of Narbonne
