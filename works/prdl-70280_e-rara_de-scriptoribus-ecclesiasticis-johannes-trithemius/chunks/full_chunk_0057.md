Paulinus, priest, a disciple of Saint Augustine of Hippo and afterward bishop of Nola in Campania, to whom many letters of Augustine and Jerome, and various treatises, are said to be addressed, was very learned in the holy Scriptures and, in secular letters, second to none in his time. He shone forth, no less distinguished for holiness than for learning. Concerning him the blessed Pope Gregory writes in the third book of the Dialogues that he was of such great piety and compassion that, after he had spent all his possessions for the use of the poor and the freeing of captives, he even handed himself over into servitude for the son of a certain widow; from that servitude he was at last released, together with many captives, because of the merit of his life. He wrote many volumes both in meter and in prose, of which these are reported. First, at the request of Saint Augustine, he wrote:

The Life of Blessed Ambrose of Milan, one book.

The Life of Saint Felix, bishop of Nola, one book.

On the Sacraments also, one book.

On the Death of a Baptized Infant, one book.

A Panegyric of Theodosius, one book.

Hymns in Diverse Meter, one book.

On Contempt of the World, one book.

On Penitence, one book.

On the Blessings of the Patriarchs, one book.

On the Praise of the Martyrs, one book.

To Sulpicius Severus, four letters.

Letters to Various Persons, one book.

He also published some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 420.

Nicetas, bishop of the city of Romaciana, was devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, acute in intellect, clear and polished in speech. For the edification of readers he wrote little works not contemptible in their reading. Of these, the following are reported:

To the Competentes, who are being prepared for baptism: A Preparatory Work, one book.

On the Errors of Paganism, one book.

On the Faith of the One Majesty, one book.

Against Genealogy, one book.

On the Creed also, one book.

On the Victim of the Paschal Lamb, one book.

To a Fallen Virgin, one book.

And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of the emperors Theodosius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Simplicianus of Milan, bishop after Saint Ambrose, was devoted to the divine Scriptures and a lover of poverty. By many letters he urged Augustine, still a priest, to exercise his intellect and devote himself to expositions of the Scriptures, so that he might be seen as, as it were, another Ambrose, one who compelled the work of Origen. Hence Augustine also resolved many questions and letters addressed to his person. But he himself too wrote useful letters to Augustine and various others, which are found here and there in part.

He flourished under the emperors Theodosius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Isidore the Elder, bishop of Corduba, was very skilled in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, ready in intellect and sufficiently eloquent in speech, distinguished no less for the conduct of his morals than for knowledge of the Scriptures. His learning was held in great esteem among the ancients. He wrote not a few little works on the divine volumes. Of these, however, I have found an excellent work which he published to Saint Orosius, the Spanish priest.
