*On Ecclesiastical Writers, fol. 133*

On the art of versifying: three books, "The things concerning the making of verses..."
Many very elegant orations: one book.
Also a book of letters: one book.
Countless epigrams.
And many other works.

He is still living at Paris, residing at Saint-Mathurin. And day by day he writes various works to be published, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Darynghus Sifridi Sinnama, born at Hagis in Frisia, who is commonly called Hermannus, German by nation, a very celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, eminently learned both in divine Scripture and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, prompt and firm in counsel, whose authority is held in great esteem among the princes and prelates of the churches: in the university of Cologne, by teaching, writing, and giving counsel, he easily holds the first place. Among other things he wrote a great and very celebrated work, explaining legal titles with the utmost clarity, very useful and necessary for all beginning to study in both laws or in one of them:

On the titles of the Code: twelve books, "And if verbosity without [unclear]..."
On the title of the Old Digest: twenty-four books, "But what new [unclear]..."
On the title of the Infortiatum: fourteen books, "It will have to be known here in..."
On the title of the New Digest: twelve books, "Because there are seven parts..."
On the title of the Authenticae: one book, "Who the constitutions..."
On the usages of fiefs: one book, "Because this book of fiefs..."
On the Peace of Constance: one book, "Because the emperor Frederick..."
On the title of the Institutes: four books, "Because from this book..."
On the title of the Decretals: five books, "All, as the emperor says..."
Certain other works are extant.

He is living in manhood in the city of Cologne, illuminating it by that learning of his, under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Raphael Regius, Italian by nation, a man most learned in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, a celebrated poet, an eminent orator, skilled in both languages, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech, who in the university of Padua acquired the greatest praises for his learning by teaching, disputing, and composing. He is said to have fashioned many excellent little works, by which he made his name and intellect known with great glory. Of these the following are extant:

Corrections on Quintilian: one book, "Great-hearted men are wont kindly..."
On the praises of eloquence: one book, "If anyone ever of an excellent work..."
On one letter of Pliny: one book, "In the inscription of this work..."
Against Johannes Calphurnius: one book, "Although by the very grave..."
A dialogue against the same: one book, "Yesterday a crowd of boys [unclear]..."
Various orations.
Also many letters.
And many other works.

He is still living at Padua, as I have learned, and is composing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Laurentius Bonincontrius, an eminent philosopher and astronomer, and excellently learned in the other disciplines of the liberal arts, outstanding in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, strong in verse and prose. He is said to have fashioned many excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of many; but I have not yet been able to see any of them. There is said, however, to be an eminent work of his for the cycle of the year:

Entitled Fasti: one book.
And certain commentaries.
