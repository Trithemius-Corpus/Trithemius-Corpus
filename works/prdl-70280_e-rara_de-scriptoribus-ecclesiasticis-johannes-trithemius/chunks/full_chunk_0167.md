Augustinus of Ancona, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man devoted to the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech, wrote many volumes not unworthy of reading, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported: To Pope John XXII, a work of no small size, On Ecclesiastical Power: three books. “Most holy and most reverend...” On the Sentences: one book.
Quodlibets also: one book.
On the Holy Spirit against the Greeks: one book.
On the Ten Strings: one book.
On the Entry into the Promised Land: one book.
On the Powers of the Soul: one book.
On the Resurrection: one book.
On Matthew: one book.
On Mark: one book.
On Luke also: one book.
On John: one book.
On Missus est: one book.
On the Magnificat: one book.
On the Epistles of Paul: fourteen books.
On the Canonical Epistles: seven books.
On the Acts of the Apostles: one book.
On the Apocalypse: one book.
And very many others.

This man began a distinguished volume from the writings of the divine father Augustine, which he entitled the Milleloquium; but, overtaken by death, he left it unfinished. Afterwards Bartholomaeus of Urbino, of the same order, completed it. He flourished under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Petrus de Apono, an Italian by nation, a Paduan by homeland, a man devoted to and learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, renowned for knowledge of many languages, is reported to have worked out many little works both in philosophy and in medicine, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. There are, however, some who write that he was a magician, and showed many marvels through that superstitious art; whether this is true I would not dare to affirm, since indeed I know nothing certain about it. For I do not recall that I have read anything from his little works, or ever seen them. Concerning these, however, the following are reported. A distinguished work divided into 110 distinctions, which he entitled On Every Kind of Medicine: one book.
