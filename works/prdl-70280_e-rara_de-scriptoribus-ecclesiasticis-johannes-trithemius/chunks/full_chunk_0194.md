Johannes Ruysbroeck, prior of the regular canons of Groenendaal in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, of German nation, a man, as they say, devout but little learned, wrote in his native tongue certain little works for the edification of the simple. These were afterward turned into Latin by another; in some respects they are excellent, but in certain points they are said by the doctors to be erroneous. And concerning these Johannes de Gerson, chancellor of Paris, composed advisements. Of them these are reported:

On the Adornment of Spiritual Nuptials, three books, “Ecce sponsus venit ex.”
On the Progress of Religious, one book. Exhortatory Letters, one book.
And certain others.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Johannes Schodehouen, of German nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Mechelen, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote for the use of preachers of the word of God volumes not to be despised. Of these the following are extant: a distinguished work on vices and virtues, and on every preach-able subject according to alphabetical order, in two volumes, which he entitled:

Polypodium, three books, “Abscondunt se multi.”
Various Sermons, one book. He also published some other works, which have by no means come into our hands.

Ostemper [unclear] Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular philosophy and canon law, whom Pope Urban VI first appointed vicar of the whole order; finally elected general in the year of the Lord 1381, he governed the order under the obedience of Pope Urban for nine years. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which these are reported. To the Cardinal of Santa Maria in Trastevere, a distinguished and excellent work:

On the Conception of Saint Mary, one book. Questions on the Sentences, one book. On the Whole Psalter, five books. On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. A Table of the Sentences, three books. On the Gospel of Luke, one book. Lenten Sermons, one book. He began a Dictionary, three books. On Valerius Maximus, one book. And certain others.
A Table of the Moralia of Gregory, one book, “Erant ambo [unclear] Isidorus.”
On the Ethics of Aristotle, one book.
