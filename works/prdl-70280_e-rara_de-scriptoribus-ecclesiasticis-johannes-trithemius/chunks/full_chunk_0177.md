William of Kayotho, of the Order of Friars Preachers, provincial prior in France, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, of excellent ability in delivering sermons to the people, wrote certain works not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is said to be:

An abbreviation of the Summa of John the Teuton, which is called that of confessors: one book. “What is simony? [unclear].”
Various sermons: one book.
The rest that he composed have by no means come to our knowledge.

Peter de Casa, of Limoges, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the fourteenth prior general, and afterward bishop of [Basionensis], and finally, as they say, patriarch of Jerusalem, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in talent, distinguished in eloquence, learned and eloquent in delivering sermons to the people, published certain excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are said to be:

On the Sentences: three books.
Sermons for the season: one book.
Sermons on the saints: one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished when he was elected prior general of his order from the convent of Limoges, under Louis IV, emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330, in the thirteenth indiction. He vigorously ruled his order for twelve years.

Paul of Perugia, of the Order of Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount [Sophia], sufficiently instructed, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, published several works of great authority; by exercising his brilliant talent in them, he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity with great glory. Of these there survives a very celebrated, almost divine work, which he recited in the Paris school:

On the Sentences: three books. Concerning the first book of the Sentences.
Quodlibets also: one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished at Paris and completed his lectureship under Louis the Bavarian, fourth emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1354, in the twelfth indiction. And not long after that same lectureship of his he died at Paris and was buried there in the convent.
