Many letters to various persons. He flourished under Philip and Otto, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Helinandus, monk of Froidmont, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures through long practice, and nobly instructed in secular letters, sharp in intellect and eloquent in speech, appeared not least among the ecclesiastical doctors of his time because of the excellence of his learning and the integrity of his life. For he wrote several little works, by which he gave his name to immortality. Of these the following are reported: from the beginning of the world down to his own age, a great and notable volume of histories, forty-eight books; *Lamentations* and [unclear]; *On the Restoration of the Fallen*, one book; many sermons, one book; letters to various persons, one book; and many other things. He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Albertus, a monk of the Cistercian order and afterwards abbot of a certain monastery, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, after the death of the holy abbot Bernard wrote, where he had left off, namely from that place, “On my bed by night,” down to the end, forty-six subtle and most elegant sermons on the Song of Songs, one book. “Varied are the feelings of lovers.” And certain other things. He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Aelred, abbot of a certain monastery of the Cistercian order, a man exercised by constant study in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, gentle in disposition, sweet in speech, outstanding in devotion. He wrote some little works for the benefit of his brothers, by which he both profited them and transmitted knowledge of his name to posterity. Among these there is extant a little work of great usefulness to religious men, filled with spiritual sayings, which he entitled *The Mirror of Charity*, one book, homily one: “When Jesus was twelve years old.” And other things which I have not seen. He flourished in the times of Henry VI, around the year of the Lord 1200.

Mauricius, bishop of Paris, very studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, inasmuch as before his episcopate he taught theology gloriously for many years at Paris; subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech; in delivering sermons to the people he was of excellent study. He is reported to have composed certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these are reported, throughout the cycle of the whole year, both for Sundays and for feast days: *Sermons to the People*, two books; *On the Care of Souls, to Priests*, one book. But the other things which he is said to have written have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1190.
