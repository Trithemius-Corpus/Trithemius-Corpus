[H]lmannus, a monk of the monastery of Altuvillare, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote various small works both in verse and in prose, by which he made his name known also to posterity. And because in his time Francia was being laid waste by the Northmen, after the example of the prophet Jeremiah he lamented the desolation of that land and of his monastery in a fourfold alphabet, composing:

On the lamentation: one book.
The Life of Bishop Vinardus: one book.
The Life of Saint Syndulphus: one book.
The Life of Saint Helena Augusta: one book.
The Translation of the same: one book.

He is said also to have written some other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Charles III, in the year of the Lord 890.

Milo, a monk of the monastery of Saint Amand, of the Order of divine Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned in every respect, a distinguished orator, poet, and musician, and no less venerable for uprightness of morals than for knowledge of the Scriptures. He wrote not a few volumes both in verse and in prose. Of these the following especially are reported:

To Charles the Bald, On Sobriety, in verse: one book.
The Life of Saint Amand, in verse: one book.
Letters to various persons: one book.

He flourished in the times of the second emperor Charles, surnamed the Bald, in the year of the Lord 870, and composed many excellent works.

Hucbaldus, a monk of the monastery of Elnone, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man most highly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters; in music, poetry, philosophy, and the other liberal arts second to none in his time, as the many small works written by him in both styles attest. Of these I have so far found only the following: to the emperor Charles the Bald, a remarkable work of 136 verses, in which all the words begin with one letter, c, as: “Carmina clarisonae calvis cantate Camenae”; it is entitled:

On the Praise of Bald Men: one book.
On the Art of Music: one book.
The Deeds of Saint Lebuinus: one book.
Letters to various persons: one book.

He also described the lives of many saints in adorned eloquence, and composed chants in their honor with sweet and regular melody. He flourished in the times of the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 880.

Remigius, a monk of Auxerre, of the Order of Saint Benedict, and afterwards, as they report, bishop of that same city, a man most highly learned in divine and human writings, a distinguished writer in verse and prose, teacher of Saint Odo, the first abbot of Cluny, renowned in life and doctrine. He published many volumes in both styles, of which the following are reported:

On the Song of Songs: one book.
On the whole Psalter: one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew: one book.
On the Canon of the Mass: one book.
On the Divine Offices: one book.
To the bishop of the Edui: one book.
Also several sermons: one book.

In addition he composed many other works both in sacred letters and also in secular writings, expounding Donatus and the ancient authors, which are possessed by many. He flourished under the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 880.
