Dionysius, bishop of the church of the Corinthians, a most eloquent man, very learned in the divine Scriptures, and a most watchful shepherd of Christ’s flock, was of such painstaking diligence that he instructed not only the people of his own city with salutary teachings, but also summoned the congregations of foreign cities to steadfastness in the faith by letters. These letters are said to be so useful and extensive that they may not improperly be called books; of them the following are listed:

To the Lacedaemonians: one epistle.
To the Athenians: one epistle.
To the Nicomedians: one epistle.
To the Cretans: one epistle.
To the [Cnossians?]: one epistle.
To the Roman pope: one epistle.
To the [Amastrians?]: one epistle.
To Chrysophora, a woman: one epistle.

He flourished under the rulers Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius, in the year of the Lord 170. Not without a reputation for holiness, he wrote various works.

Tatian, a heresiarch, hearer and follower of Justin Martyr, did not for a long time depart from his side. But afterward, when Saint Justin had been crowned, swollen with pride in his eloquence, he founded a new heresy which is called that of the Encratites; later Severus increased it, from whom the heretics of the same faction were called Severians. They abominate meats; they do not drink wine; they do not accept the Old Testament; nor do they believe in the resurrection. Tatian wrote almost innumerable volumes, which nevertheless have not come to my knowledge. For because of the error of the teacher, even his volumes, despised, perished. There survives, as Jerome attests, a distinguished volume of his which he entitled:

Against the Nations: one book.

He flourished under Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 170.

Philip, bishop of Crete, that is, of the city of Gortyna, whom Dionysius, bishop of the Corinthians, placed above, mentions in his epistle which he had written to the church of the same city, was a noble and learned man. He published for the benefit of believers volumes sufficiently furnished, as they say, which have by no means come into our hands. There survives his:

Against Marcion: one book.

He also wrote certain epistles to various persons and various treatises, which have still by no means come into our hands. He flourished in the year of the Lord 170, in the times of the emperor Marcus Antoninus Verus.

Modestus, a man of flourishing talent, though of what dignity or rank he was is unknown to us, wrote, as blessed Jerome attests, little works not to be despised in reading, in which the abundant vein of his talent is seen. Of these there survives a distinguished volume:

Against Marcion: one book.

The rest are unknown to us.

He flourished under the rulers Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 185.
