We next warn and beseech the reader of this catalogue not readily to be moved against us if he finds the names, dignities, native land, or little works of writers set down otherwise than he himself perhaps has read elsewhere, since no one has ever been able to reconcile the disagreement of histories concerning transmitted time. Nor have we written anything that has not been confirmed in many places or by testimonies. But, as we professed at the beginning of this work, we gladly yield to the opinion of any well-disposed corrector, which both solid truth and charity will have rendered sincere.

Lastly, however, our rivals are to be laughed at by us: barking like dogs from the worst habit, they condemn everything, blame everything, tear everything to pieces that either they themselves have not made or have found to be contrary to their own opinions. As soon as they buy it or have begun to read even a little, they will knit their brows in anger, thrust out a slanderous tongue, say that this is false and that is not true, having become rash judges before being examiners of truth. But what concern have we with those who blame nothing good in a man, praise nothing bad in a man? Envious dogs, therefore, are to be despised, not instructed. But although we judge it glorious to look down upon malicious men, nevertheless we think it much more glorious to have pleased good and learned men; by them, if there should be an error anywhere, we are gladly corrected, and, once corrected, are more fruitfully amended. Let love of the Scriptures excuse us, for whose sake we have taken this heavy burden upon an unafraid neck.

*The book on ecclesiastical writers by the most eloquent father lord Johannes of Trittenheim, abbot of Sponheim, has been completed: Basel, in the year of the Lord one thousand four hundred ninety-four.*
