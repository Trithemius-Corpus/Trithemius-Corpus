Conrad of Halberstadt, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man nobly learned both in divine Scriptures and in secular letters, a philosopher and no mean theologian, since, lecturing publicly in that faculty, he taught many and acquired great praise for his learning. He wrote certain treatises in both sciences, not to be scorned, by which his name has been brought down to the memory of posterity. But few of them have become known to me. For I have found only:

Summa studentium: three books. This little work into three.
On the Trivium also: one book. In three it has pleased.
A reading on Job: one book. It will endure until the time.
Sermons also: one book.

He is also said to have written commentaries on several books of the Bible, which, however, have not yet come into our hands. This man is also reported to have been the first to arrange concordances of the whole Bible, of the New and Old Testament.

Alchardus of Middletown, English by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in divine Scriptures, excellently instructed both in philosophy and in canon law, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, subtle and ready in resolving doubtful questions of the Scriptures. He is said to have written many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have found only the following:

On the Sentences: three books. He brought hidden things into the light.
On the distinctions of the Decretum: one book. All our fathers under the cloud.
On the order of judgments: one book.
On the four Gospels: three books.
On the Epistles of Paul: fourteen books.

It is said, moreover, that he published commentaries and readings on several books of sacred Scripture, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished in the times of Emperor Adolf and Pope Nicholas IV, in the year of the Lord's nativity 1290.

Nicholas of Bibra, German by nation, a man nobly learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, an outstanding philosopher and poet, who in his time, being held in esteem at Erfurt, acquired great glory for his learning. He wrote not a few treatises both in verse and also in prose, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have seen only the work whose title is hidden, which he composed at Erfurt in verse and in prose:

On Avoiding Evil: one book. Hearer and reader of the song.
Letters to various persons: one book.

He flourished at Erfurt under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

John of Paris, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a theologian, very learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, since in both sciences, having ascended the magisterial chair in the university of Paris, he taught publicly for a long time and left not a few disciples. He is said to have written certain excellent treatises, by which he made himself useful to those then present and later known to those to come. Of these I have found only one work, not to be scorned:

On the Sentences: four books.

Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.
