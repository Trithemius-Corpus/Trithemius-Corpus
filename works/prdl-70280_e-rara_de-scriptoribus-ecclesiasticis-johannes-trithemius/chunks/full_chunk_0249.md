Alanus de Rupe, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, of most excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people, in life and conduct devoted to God and a special lover of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin, and a venerable promoter of her rosary and service; by her, too, he sometimes deserved to be refreshed with a singular gift of heavenly consolation, and now to be revived in eternal happiness. Among the other works of his talent he wrote:

A Compendium of the Marian Psalter, one book. It begins: “Because of catholic truth.”
On the Miracles of the Rosary, one book. It begins: “[unclear].”
Also Various Sermons, one book.
And certain other things.

He died in his convent at Zwolle, under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pope, in the year of the Lord 1479, in the seventh indiction, on the vigil of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary. Her rosary, and the psalter commemorative of Christ’s Passion, which had long now been neglected, he recalled to the minds and practices of men; in its honor he founded a confraternity, which, confirmed by the apostolic see, increases daily and provides many with a salutary exercise.

Conradus of Zabern, a German by nation, a man studious and practiced in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, especially music; teachable in talent and exemplary in life, distinguished and devout in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote in plain speech certain little works not to be despised, by which, usefully and fruitfully exercising his talent, he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among their number the following are extant:

On the Monochord, one book. It begins: “Since, as a certain wise man...”
On the Method of Singing Well, one book. It begins: “In favor of the whole clergy...”
On the Ending of Collects, one book. It begins: “Although very many concerning...”
Many and Various Sermons, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 1470, under Pope Sixtus IV, under whom he also died.

Laurentius Calcaneus of Brescia, a noble man, a knight of the golden spur, and a most celebrated doctor of laws, a most lucid philosopher and historian, distinguished in talent and eloquence, and a very acute disputant of questions. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity; among these they report the following:

On the Commendation of Studies, one book.
On the Seven Mortal Sins, one book.
On the Conception of Saint Mary, [unclear] book[s].
Also Several Counsels, [unclear] book[s].
And certain other small works.

He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pope, in the year of the Lord 1478, in the eleventh indiction.
