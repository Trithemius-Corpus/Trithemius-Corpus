Jacobus de Gruytrode, German by nation, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of All Apostles near Liège, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, excellent in talent and sweet in speech, wrote certain distinguished and devout little works, the reading of which is considered not unprofitable for religious persons and lovers of their own salvation.

Jacobus Zenus, a Venetian patrician, bishop of the church of Padua, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, ready and circumspect in counsel, and distinguished by experience of many things, wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory. Among these there is a most elegant work:

On the Deeds of the Supreme Pontiffs, 1 book, and certain other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1476, in the ninth indiction.
