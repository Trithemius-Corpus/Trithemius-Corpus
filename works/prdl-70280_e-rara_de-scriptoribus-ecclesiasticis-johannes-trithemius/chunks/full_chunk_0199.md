Lasius Andernarius, a Gaul by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, nobly exercised in the arena of scholastic discipline, distinguished in talent, an outstanding disputant. He wrote some notable volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following distinguished, splendid work excels:

On the Sentences: four books.
Questions and Collations: one book.

He is also said to have written some works on moral philosophy, but they have not come to me.

Bartholomaeus de Saliceto, jurist, the most celebrated of all in his time, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, whose sayings are held in great authority among professors of civil law. He published certain splendid little works on laws, by which he consecrated the memory of his name to immortality among posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code: nine books.
On the Institutes: four books.
On the Use of Fiefs: one book.

And many others. He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and of Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Johannes, abbot of the monastery of Saint Bavo, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, an outstanding doctor and professor of the divine Scriptures, subtle in talent, polished in eloquence, distinguished no less for religious conduct than for knowledge of letters. He wrote certain notable little works for the instruction of monks. Of these the following are extant. A work which he entitled:

On the Eating of Meat: one book.
Explanations on the Rule: one book.
Questions on the Sentences: one book.

Besides these, he is said to have written various and numerous treatises; but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands, except that little book which he wrote on the eating of meat, in which he beautifully sets out and rightly concludes that, for a healthy monk professed according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the eating of meat is illicit and therefore wholly forbidden.

Richardus Lauinham, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, an Englishman by nation, a man excellently learned in the holy Scriptures, an eminent interpreter of secular philosophy, and also of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He also wrote some volumes, of which these are reported:

Against the Lollards: one book.
On the Foundation of His Order: one book.
For the Book of Bridget: one book.
Various Sermons: one book.
Also On Logic: one book.
And Various Questions: one book.

He flourished in the University of Oxford, especially at the time when the heresy of John Wycliffe and of the Lollards began in Bohemia.
