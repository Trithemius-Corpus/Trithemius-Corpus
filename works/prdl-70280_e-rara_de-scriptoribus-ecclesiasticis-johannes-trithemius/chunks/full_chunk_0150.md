And not a few other things.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

Goffredus of Trani, subdeacon of the Roman Church and, as they report, chaplain of the supreme pontiff, a man nobly learned in canon law and secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and renowned for eloquence, who in his time was considered not the least among the doctors of this faculty, wrote little works of reading not to be scorned, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there survives a notable summa, sufficiently widespread:

On the Titles of the Decretals: five books. “The diversity of glosses [unclear].”
Various Questions: one book. Other works which he is said to have composed have by no means come to our knowledge.

Guido de Baysio, archdeacon of Bologna, a man very skilled in both laws, sharp in talent and renowned for eloquence, whose authority among professors of this science has remained great down to the present day, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he rendered the memory of his name, so to speak, immortal. Of these only a few have so far come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following. A very large and notable volume which he entitled the Rosarium:

“To the reverend father in Christ.”
On the Decree: two books. On the Decretals: five books.
