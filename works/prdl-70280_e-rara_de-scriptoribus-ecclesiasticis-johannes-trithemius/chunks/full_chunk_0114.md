William, abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, in the diocese of Speyer, of the Order of Saint Be[unclear], wrote some letters: a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, in secular letters inferior to no one among the Germans in his time; a philosopher, astronomer, distinguished musician, most holy in life and conduct, who is read to have shone with many miracles. He reformed regular discipline, which had long since collapsed in many monasteries of Germany. Therefore, for the preservation of monastic observance, he wrote from the ancient statutes:
Constitutions of Monks: one book.
On the Tones of Music: one book.
On the Clock: one book.
On the Correction of the Psalter: one book.
To Saint Anselm of Canterbury: a letter.
A book of letters to various persons.
He also published some other things, which through the negligence of the ancients have fallen into oblivion. He died under the emperor Henry III, but as king of the Romans the fourth, in the year of the Lord 1091, in the fourteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Nones of July. He was buried in the middle of the church of the apostles, with a great reputation for holiness.

Gilbert, provost of the monastery of West[unclear] in England, of the Order of Saint Benedict, hearer and disciple of Anselm of Canterbury, a man learned in the divine writings, not ignorant of secular philosophy, ready in intellect, cautious in speech, made his name known to posterity by writing, when he composed certain necessary little works. Of these the following are reported:
A Disputation against the Jews,
To the blessed Anselm, bishop: one book.
On the Prophet Isaiah: one book.
And certain other things. He flourished under Henry IV with Anselm, in the year of the Lord 1090.

Ontherus, a monk of Elwangen, of the Order of Saint Benedict, skilled in the divine Scriptures by constant exercise, nobly taught in the disciplines of secular letters, a famous theologian and poet, subtle in intellect, polished in speech, wrote in meter and prose certain excellent volumes. Of these few have come to my knowledge. There is extant a volume of his in elegant meter:
On the Passion of Saint Cyriacus: one book.
And certain letters. He flourished in the times of King Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1100.

Theoderic, abbot of the monastery of Saint Trudo in the diocese of Liege, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose. In both styles he wrote certain excellent little works, in which he exercised his talent splendidly. Of these the following, written in elegant meter, are reported:
Life of Saint Trudo: one book.
Life of Saint Bavo: one book.
Life of Saint Remold: one book.
And many other things. He flourished in the times of Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1100.
