Johannes Tinctoris, German by nation, of Cologne, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy; acute in intellect and an outstanding disputant. He taught theology publicly for a long time in the University of Cologne, and gained great praises for his learning. He wrote little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported:

Dissensions of Thomas and Scotus, which he began to reconcile, in one book.
Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Also various collationes.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the aforesaid university under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Nicolaus Perotus of Sassoferrato, and bishop of Siponto, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to no one in his time in secular letters; an outstanding theologian and a most eloquent rhetorician; keen in intellect and distinguished in life. He wrote certain lucid little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there survives a notable work divided into two volumes, on the poet Marcus Valerius Martialis, very beautifully comprising all the elegance, method, and explanation of Latin speech, which he entitled The Volume of Commentaries on the Language, or, as it is commonly called:

Cornucopia, two books: “Barbara and [unclear] flatters [unclear].”
On the Education of Boys, one book.
On the Kinds of Metres, one book.
Orations and various letters.

He is said to have written many other things besides, both in divine letters and in humane letters, and to have translated some things from Greek into Latin; but they have not come into our hands.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and the Roman pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Antonius Rosellus, Tuscan by nation, an eminent professor of both laws and a very celebrated interpreter, and greatly adorned with the discipline of other good arts; outstanding in intellect and eloquent in speech. He taught law for many years in the University of Padua. He wrote many distinguished little works, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. But I do not recall that I have ever seen any of them. There is reported, however, his notable work:

On the Monarchy of the World, three books.
And many other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III, in vigorous old age, and was buried at Padua.
