Juvencus, a priest, Spanish by nation, a noble and most learned man, a philosopher, poet, rhetorician, and distinguished theologian, venerable to the Church no less for his way of life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, published for the edification of readers many excellent volumes, both in meter and in prose; but few of them have come to my knowledge. I have read the distinguished work which he composed in hexameter and pentameter verses:

On the Four Gospels, 3 [4?] books. “Nothing immortal is held in the structure of the world.”
On the Sacraments, 2 books.

The rest which he is said to have composed have not come into our hands.

He flourished under Constantine the Great and his sons, in the year of the Lord 330.

Eustachius, by birth a Pamphylian, first bishop of Beroea in Syria and then twenty-third bishop of Antioch after Peter, a learned and holy man, endured many hardships from the Arians for the Catholic faith. For, banished into exile to Trajanopolis of the Thracians, he died there; after the Arians invaded his church, they held it for many years. He wrote very many short works, chiefly against the Arians, and various other diverse treatises, namely:

Against the Arians, 3 books.
Against Origen, 1 book.
On the Soul, 1 book.
Almost innumerable letters, 1 book.

He flourished in the times of Constantine and his sons, in the year of the Lord 330.

Athanasius, nineteenth bishop of the city of Alexandria, a holy man and most learned in the divine Scriptures, endured many persecutions from the Arians for the Catholic faith. Indeed, the whole world was narrow for him, with the Arians persecuting him everywhere, and nowhere did he seem safe, nowhere secure. For a man’s enemies were those of his own household. Fleeing, therefore, from the face of the emperor of the Arians, he came to Trier, a city of Gaul, where he was honorably received by Saint Maximinus the archbishop and hid with him for seven years in a cistern without water, which to this day is shown in the crypt at the monastery of Saint Maximinus in Trier. While lying hidden in this place for many years, as we have said, he did not see the sun; and there he composed the creed “Whoever wishes to be saved,” until at last, betrayed by a certain girl, he escaped again by flight. If anyone desires to know his deeds more fully, let him read the Tripartite History. He wrote almost countless short works in Greek. Among these I have found the following among the Latins:

You are one God: God the Father; God the Son.
Now concerning the individual names of the persons.
How in the one God.
Here now concerning the several names.
Here the person contradicting is.
I ask of you concerning the unique deity.
For which reason most earnestly.
Without beginning of times, above all.
When you have read this faith.
To those who say that the Son of God was created.
To the most blessed lord, and deservedly.
Their first objection is.
When at the city of Nicaea.
