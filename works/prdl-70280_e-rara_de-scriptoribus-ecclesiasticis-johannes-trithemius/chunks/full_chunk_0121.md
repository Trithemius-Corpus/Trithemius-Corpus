He flourished in the times of Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Gratian, a monk of Bologna or Classe, of the Order of Saint Benedict, by birth, as some maintain, an Etruscan, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and burning with admirable zeal, ready in intellect and clear in eloquence, also distinguished in life and conduct, wrote, after Ivo, bishop of Chartres, a notable volume of Decrees from the statutes, enactments, and writings of the ancients, which professors of canon law use in the schools down to the present, approved by Pope Eugene III. Its title is:
The Concordance of Discordant Canons: four books. The human race by two...
He also composed certain other things.
He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Hugh, monk of Corbie, of the Order of Saint Benedict, called de Fouilloy, a man rich and learned in the Holy Scriptures through reading the ancients, distinguished in intellect and sweet in eloquence, and no less venerable for conduct than for knowledge, made his name known to posterity by his writings. For he wrote a notable volume, not so much useful as delightful to all religious persons and those fearing God, divided into four little books, designated beforehand as follows:
On the material cloister: one book. On the arrangement of the cloister.
On the spiritual cloister: one book. The contemplation of the soul’s cloister...
On the cloister of the soul: one book. Into the heavenly [unclear] cloister.
On the cloister of paradise: one book. Behold, a virgin shall conceive, etc.
And certain other things.
He flourished in the times of Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Richard, monk of Saint Victor in Paris, of the Order of Regular Canons of Saint Augustine, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, outstanding in morals and conduct, wrote many and almost innumerable little works, by which he greatly benefited the usefulness of believers and consecrated his name to immortality. Among these the following are reported:
On the Holy Trinity: five books. My righteous one lives by faith.
On contemplation: one book. Arise, Lord, into your rest.
On the twelve patriarchs: one book. Benjamin, a little youth.
On the mystical ark: five books. That mystical ark of Moses.
On the Song of Songs: one book. On my bed, etc.; the soul which God...
On the extermination of evil: one book. What is it to you, sea, that you fled?
On the promotion of good: one book. Every head is sick, etc.
Against the Judaizers: one book. Behold, a virgin shall conceive, etc.
On the tabernacle of the covenant: one book. Whatever you shall bind upon...
On the forty-two stations: one book. In the regeneration, when he shall sit...
On the study of wisdom: one book. Since to your question, etc.
On the perfection of monks: one book. Bring to the Lord, sons of God, etc.
On mental prayer: one book. Those things in the Gospel.
On the threefold holocaust: one book.
On the authority of Peter: one book.
On judicial power: one book.
On the difference of sins: one book.
On the instruction of novices: one book.
On the spirit of blasphemy: one book.
