This is Jerome, sincere lover of Jesus Christ, who, letting no time pass idly, meditated day and night on the law of the Lord, and, scarcely granting his body the sleep necessary for it, always, by day and by night, wrote, read, taught, or prayed; this is attested by the almost countless books which he published. This defender of truth, cultivator of virtue, most bitter enemy of vices, when he was a priest of the Roman Church and steadfastly rebuked the crimes of the clergy, withdrew from the city because of their envy; and coming to Judea he visited the glorious sepulchre of the Lord, and, establishing for himself a place of habitation beside the Bethlehemite manger, became known as famous to the whole world, divine providence surely arranging this: that he who was to be a light of the world should rest there where the true Sun of justice had made himself known to men by being born. A holy and fitting inhabitant of the most holy place, firm in faith, kindled with charity, mature in his way of life, who could not be seduced by prosperity, whom no adversity ever cast down from his holy purpose. But why, a foul praiser, do I touch the holy head of the man with impure lips? It would have been more fitting for me humbly to keep silent than shamelessly to speak of so holy a man. Now therefore let us record in order what we have found concerning his writings and minor works:
