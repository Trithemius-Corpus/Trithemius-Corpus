Rainerius Pisanus, Italian by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man zealous and learned in the sacred writings, and sufficiently skilled in canon law, prompt in intellect, scholastic in speech, cautious in counsel, and no less venerable in [unclear] religion: in order to provide a path for his brothers less experienced in law in hearing confessions, he wrote a very great and distinguished summa on cases, in two large volumes, according to the order of the alphabet, which he entitled:

*Pantheologia*, two books. “Absolution. Concerning absolution.”

And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Erardus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, German by nation, lector of the convent of Liège, a man zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, and an outstanding preacher in making sermons to the people, distinguished in intellect and adorned with scholastic eloquence. He wrote a very useful treatise for devout and religious persons, teaching seven dispositions to be made concerning the heart, gathering here and there with discernment from the divine scriptures many things pertaining to edification, which he entitled:

*On the Teaching of the Heart*, one book. “Prepare your hearts.”

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

Besides these he published certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Jacobus de Arena, from Parma by nation, a very celebrated consultant in both laws, profound in intellect, scholastic in speech; his opinions in explaining the laws, as Bartolus testifies, were so proper and fitting that he seemed to be writing from the mind of the founder of the law at the time.
