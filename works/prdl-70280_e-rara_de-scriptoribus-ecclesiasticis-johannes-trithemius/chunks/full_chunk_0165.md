He also composed the office of the stigmata of Saint Francis, founder of the Order of Minors. Finally, he is said to have published commentaries on many books of the Bible, which have not yet at all come into our hands. He flourished under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Nicolaus de Lyra, English by nation, of the Order of Minors, a man most devoted to the sacred Scriptures and skilled through long practice, perfectly instructed in the Hebrew language, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech, and no less venerable in manner of life than in knowledge, wrote many distinguished little works on the sacred volumes, by which his name came to the knowledge of posterity. And how useful and necessary they are for all who desire to learn sacred Scripture, no one knows better than one who has exercised himself in reading them. For there are some who judge him contemptible because of the simplicity of his eloquence; they seem to me both vain and unskilled, and unworthy of sacred knowledge and salutary philosophy, since they censure ecclesiastical simplicity and embrace words, not things.

Concerning the little works of this devout and learned man, the following are reported:

On the Sentences: three books.
On Genesis according to the literal sense: one book. “In the beginning,” etc. “Omitting.”
On Exodus: one book. “According to what Isidore says.”
On Leviticus: one book. “And he called Moses,” etc. “As the Lord spoke,” etc. “Ex.”
On Numbers: one book. “The declaration of your words.”
On Deuteronomy: one book. “You will bring them in,” and [unclear].
On Joshua: one book. “The Lord raised up judges.”
On Judges: one book. “In the days,” etc. “Here follows.”
On Ruth: two books. “Through me kings reign.”
On Kings: two books. “Gather up the fragments.”
On Chronicles: two books. “He will be priest over Solomon.”
On Ezra and Nehemiah: two books. “These things ought to have been done,” etc.
On Tobit: two books. “Arphaxad,” etc. “After the histories.”
On Judith: two books. “In the days,” etc. “After.”
On Esther: two books. “Have patience with me,” etc.
On Job: two books. “A great prophet has arisen.”
On the Psalter: two books. “Behold, I have described it to you threefold.”
On the Song of Songs: two books. “The words of Ecclesiastes, as it says.”
On the Book of Wisdom: two books. “Let him kiss me,” etc. “It is expedient.”
On Ecclesiasticus: two books. “All wisdom,” etc. “Here begins.”
On Isaiah the prophet: two books. “Jerusalem, evangelist, give.”
On Jeremiah: two books. “I have made you a prophet to the nations.”
On the Lamentations of Jeremiah: two books. “How she sits.” “In the preceding.”
On the Book of Baruch: two books. “These words,” etc. “After the book.”
On Ezekiel: two books. “The heavens were opened,” etc.
On Daniel: one book. “But to Daniel God gave.”
On the Twelve Minor Prophets: one book. “The bones of the twelve prophets.”
On Maccabees: two books. “And it came to pass,” etc. “After the history.”
On the Gospel of Matthew: one book. “Four faces to one,” etc.
On Mark: one book. “The face of a lion on the right.”
On Luke: one book. “The face of an ox on the left.”
