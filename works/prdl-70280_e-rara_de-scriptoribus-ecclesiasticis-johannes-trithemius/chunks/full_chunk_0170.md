Alvarus, of the Order of Friars Minor, Spanish by nation, a hearer of John Scotus, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, held in esteem by John XXII, acted against William Ockham the Englishman in writings and in words. He published certain distinguished volumes of his talent, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is a great and notable work, describing the defects of ecclesiastical persons, which he entitled:

*On the Lamentation of the Church*, one book.
Also *The Mirror of Kings*, one book.
*On the Sentences*, one book.
*An Apology*, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished at the same time as Ockham, under Louis, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Matthew Silvaticus, Mantuan by homeland, noble by birth, a physician by art and profession, in which faculty in his time he was considered both learned and experienced before many others; subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote in that science volumes not to be despised, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these I have seen only a notable work, addressed to Robert, king of Sicily and physician, on all simple medicines and their properties, which he entitled:

*Pandects*, one book.
*Aaron* in Greek; in Arabic.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Louis, the fifth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1420.

John Canonicus, of the order, as they say, of the Friars Preachers, and afterward, as [unclear]; once a hearer and disciple in the academy of Paris; a man learned in the divine Scriptures, and subtly instructed in secular philosophy; also not ignorant of both pontifical and imperial law. He is said to have composed certain distinguished little works, by which, exercising his talent, he also made his name known to posterity. But I have seen none of them. They are said to be:

*On the Sentences*, one book.
*Disputed Questions*, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Durandus of Saint-Pourçain, of the Order of Friars Preachers, and afterward, as [unclear]; and not meanly instructed in secular philosophy; ready in intellect and distinguished in eloquence; an excellent declaimer of sermons; at first a defender of the writings of Saint Thomas Aquinas, but afterward a very fierce assailant of them. A certain shifting cause is reported for this change, to which I ought neither readily to give credence nor rashly to deny it. He wrote certain volumes not to be spurned, by which he made his name known. Among them are reported:

*On the Sentences*, one book.
*On the Instruction of the Clergy*, one book.
*A Notable Summa*, one book.
*Various Questions*, one book.
*Diverse Sermons*, one book.

The other things which he is said to have composed have not yet come to my knowledge.
