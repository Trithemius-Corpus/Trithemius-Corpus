Johannes Noblet, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, French by nation, of the Paris convent and school, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular disciplines, since before entering religion he had been, by art, practice, and profession, a health-giving physician of sick bodies, wrote several distinguished little works, of which the following are reported: *On the Sentences*, three books; *On the Canonical Epistles*, six books; *A Centiloquy of Enigmas*, one book; *Various Sermons*, one book; and many others.

"Noble in the gates is her husband."

Besides these, he is said to have written wonderful and most elegant commentaries on many books of the Bible, which by no means have come to our knowledge.

Johannes de Ragusio, of the order of the brothers preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, distinguished in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, published certain distinguished little works of his talent, by which he transmitted his name to knowledge. Among them I have read a work addressed to the fathers of the Council of Basel, *Against the Hussites*, one book: "Most reverend and venerable..." Of the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished in person at the Council of Basel, where he publicly delivered the said treatise against the heretics to the fathers, under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1438.

Blondus Flavius of Forli, secretary of Pope Eugenius IV, a man skilled in every discipline of the liberal arts, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, an outstanding historiographer, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, second to none in grace of expression, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported: three decades entitled *On the Deeds of the Romans*, thirty-one books: "The origin of the empire of the Romans..."; *On Rome Triumphant*, ten books; *On Rome Restored*, three books; *On Italy Illuminated*, eight books; *Letters to Various Persons*, one book; *Various Orations*, one book; and many others.

"The origin of the empire of the Romans..."

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund, under Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1435. He left five sons from a legitimate marriage, cultivators of the liberal arts.

Neymericus de Campo, German by nation, vice-chancellor of the University of Cologne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent, an outstanding disputant of difficult questions, wrote many distinguished little works, of which, however, few have so far come into our hands. He was personally sent to the Council of Basel by the University of Cologne, where, at the urging of Master Nicolaus de Cusa, later cardinal, he published: *A Compendium of Questions*, one book: "After many [unclear] by occasion..."; *On the Sentences*, three books: "As Augustine says in the book On..."
