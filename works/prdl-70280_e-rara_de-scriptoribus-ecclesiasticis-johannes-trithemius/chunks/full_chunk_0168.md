Albertus de Beka, a German by nation, from Guelders by homeland, of the Order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior of Lower Germany, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and outstandingly learned both in canon law and in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence; he shone in his time no less brightly for his manner of life than for his learning. He wrote certain commendable little works, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these are reported:

On the Sentences;
A Summa of the censures of the new law;
On the rule of his order;
And certain other works.

He also corrected the ordinal of his order with discerning care. He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Clement V, pope, born in Gascony, formerly called Bernard, bishop of Bordeaux, sat after Benedict XI in the chair of Peter for eight years and ten months. He was a learned and energetic man. By his command the Order of the Templars was destroyed throughout the whole world in one day. He celebrated the Council of Vienne, in which he enacted many things for the benefit of the Church. He composed the seventh book of Decretals, which John XXII, his successor, published, as is contained at the beginning of the same work. It is commonly called:

The Clementine Code;
Also letters to various persons.

He died while Henry VII was governing the Roman Empire, in the year of the Lord 1313, in the twelfth indiction.

Petrus de Perpiniano, called Riminatione, a Catalan, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the sacred Scriptures and a subtle expounder of scholastic philosophy, quick in intellect and an outstanding declaimer, produced many little works of reading not to be despised; by exercising in them the abundant vein of his talent, he made his name known also to posterity. Among these are reported:

Questions on the Sentences;
On the whole Psalter;
Various sermons.

Concerning the other works that he composed, however, nothing has come to my knowledge.

Derricus, of the Order of Friars Minor, a holy and learned man and an outstanding preacher of the divine word, assuming a pilgrimage on account of his most fervent love for it, penetrated Asia and India, where by preaching and teaching the evangelical faith of Christ he converted many from iniquity to the path of justice. His most holy life was confirmed by many signs both in the flesh and after death. Among other little works of his talent he wrote:

The history of his pilgrimage;
Also various sermons;
And certain letters.

He flourished in the times of Louis IV, emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.
