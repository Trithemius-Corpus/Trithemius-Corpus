Nicolaus of Wachenheim, a German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and renowned for eloquence, who, in the Heidelberg academy, for almost fifty years, by teaching, lecturing, and disputing, was as it were the chief among theologians and was held in the highest esteem, wrote some little works not contemptible in reading, by which he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these a few have come down to me:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Many sermons, one book.
Several conferences and orations, one book. “Because, with the approval of the sacred [unclear].”
And certain other things.

He flourished at Heidelberg under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1480, already very old, and under Pope Sixtus IV; he died almost seven years before this.

Michael of Milan, of the Order of Friars Minor, studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, distinguished in life and conduct, of very celebrated reputation in preaching sermons to the people, wrote, for the benefit of those who devote themselves to preaching, some little works not to be despised, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these there are extant:

Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Sermons for Advent, one book.
Sermons on penitence through Lent, one book.
Sermons on sins, one book.
Sermons on the Ten Commandments, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1480, and died under Pope Innocent VIII.

Sixtus, Pope IV, a Ligurian by nation, born in Savona, a maritime city, formerly called Franciscus, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, sat after Paul II in the chair of Peter for thirteen years. He was a man learned in the divine Scriptures, an excellent disputant, a lover of the clergy, a promoter of religious, and a singular benefactor, especially of those whom a life of observance commended. When Pope Paul once heard him disputing, he admired him so much that he deemed him worthy of the honor of the cardinalate. Before his pontificate he wrote certain distinguished little works, among which these are reported:

On future contingents, one book.
On the power of God, one book.
On the blood of Christ, one book.
On the conception of Saint Mary, one book.
And certain other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1483, in the second indiction.
