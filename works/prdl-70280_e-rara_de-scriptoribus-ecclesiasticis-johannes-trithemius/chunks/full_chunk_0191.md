Johannes Fust, a German by nationality, from the town of Kreuznach in the county of Sponheim, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Strasbourg, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote, while he was still lector in the convent of Kreuznach, some not contemptible little works for the instruction of simple preachers, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these we have seen:

Sermons on the Season: one book.
Sermons on the Saints: one book.
Weekday Sermons for Lent: one book.

But of the other things which he composed, nothing has yet come into our hands. He is said to have flourished at Kreuznach around the time of Emperor Charles IV. The convent at Kreuznach has the originals of the sermons of the said Fust.

Gerardus Magnus of Deventer, a German by nationality, of the diocese of Utrecht, a man excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in canon law, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, who first established at Deventer the house of the brothers whom they call “of the Common Life.” He wrote several little works of not contemptible reading, by which, exercising his talent, he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. For he was renowned throughout all Germany not only for learning, but also for religious life and praiseworthy conduct. Of his little works they report these:

On Contracts and [Usury?]: one book.
On the Instruction of Novices: one book.
On the Letting of Pastoral Care: one book.
On the Interior Life: one book.
On the Government of Nuns: one book.
On the Disadvantages of Marriage: one book.
Letters to Various Persons: one book.
Various Sermons: one book.
And many other things.

He died under Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1384, in the seventh indiction, in the forty-fourth year of his age.
