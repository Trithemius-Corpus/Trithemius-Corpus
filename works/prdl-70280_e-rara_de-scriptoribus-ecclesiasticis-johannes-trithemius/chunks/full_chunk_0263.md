Laurentius Burellus of Dijon, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, very excellently instructed in secular letters, outstanding in talent, distinguished in eloquence, practiced in verse and prose, wrote certain notable volumes in both kinds, by which he made his name known. Among these the following are reported: a very celebrated work in praise of Elijah, patriarch of the Carmelites, which he entitled:

*Helias*, 1 book.
On the illustrious men of his order, 1 book.
And very many others.

He is still living, in great esteem among his own, writing and composing many things, under Maximilian, most unconquered king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Christophorus Landinus, an Italian by nation, a citizen of Florence, a man especially learned in secular letters, not ignorant also of philosophy and sacred Scripture, for many years presided over the chair of the Muses and by public office taught poetry. Distinguished in talent, he wrote works held in great esteem by all, especially: commentaries on P. Vergilius Maro; likewise commentaries on Q. Horatius; *Camaldulenses*; *Ethics*.

He is reported still to flourish among his Florentines, whom he adorns with all the flowers of literature.

Jacobus of Bergamo, of the order of the brothers of Saint Augustine, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, a very celebrated historiographer, wrote certain very lucid little works, by which he made posterity beholden to the memory of him. Among these there is extant a work of histories from the beginning of the world down to the years of the Lord 1486, a large and notable volume, which, bringing together material from diverse sources with much long labor, he entitled: *Supplementum chronicarum*, 11 books. *Pro Ciceronis vestri* [unclear]. Of the others I have learned nothing.

He is still living among his own people of Bergamo, in esteem, nearly sixty years old, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Baptista de Sancto Blasio of Padua, a man very skilled in both laws, distinguished for the learning of the philosophers, subtle in talent and adorned with eloquence, who in the Paduan gymnasium publicly taught laws excellently, where he instructed a great number of pupils, wrote in that faculty certain notable little works, by which he showed the force of his talent to those present and to posterity. Among these I have read:

On dotal privileges, 1 book. “Since many things concerning dowries...”
Also *Correlatives*, 1 book. “In this work of correlatives...”
Questions on arbitration, [unclear] book. “In the name of Christ...”
On the first part of the Decretals, 1 book. “In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ...”
