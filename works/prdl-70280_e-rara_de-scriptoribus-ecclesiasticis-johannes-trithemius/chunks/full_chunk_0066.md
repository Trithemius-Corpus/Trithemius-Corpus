Paschasinus, a Sicilian bishop, learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, worthy of reverence no less for his conduct than for his knowledge, is said to have presided in place of the blessed Pope Leo the Great at the Council of Chalcedon, in which, together with the holy fathers, numbering two hundred, he condemned the heretics Eutyches and Dioscorus. Eutyches was a priest and abbot of the very celebrated monastery of Constantinople, who asserted that Christ was only God and not man; and he wrote various works on behalf of his error, all of which were condemned together with their author. Paschasinus, however, wrote to Pope Leo:

On the question of Easter: an epistle.
To the same, on the miracle of the font: an epistle.
The acts of the Council of Chalcedon: a book.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 450.

Leo, the first pope, by birth a Tuscan, after Sixtus III sat in the chair of Peter for twenty-one years: a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in delivering homilies to the people so celebrated and eloquent that he had no superior in the Roman Church, as his very famous sermons testify. For he was the Cicero of ecclesiastical speech, the Homer of sacred theology, the Aristotle of the arguments of faith, the Peter of apostolic authority, and Paul in the Christian pulpit. His sermons penetrate the heart, move the mind, and confirm the Catholic faith with sweet brevity of speech. Nor is this surprising: bearing the fire of divine love kindled in his breast, what else could he speak but burning words? He wrote many short works. Of these I have found only the following, namely:

Against Eutyches the priest: a book.
On the Nicene Creed: a book.
To Emperor Leo, of testimonies: a book.
To the inquiries of Bishop Januarius: a book.
On the Passion of the Lord: 24 homilies, a book.
Many homilies to the people: a book.
To Flavianus the bishop, against Eutyches: a book.
To Emperor Leo, against Eutyches: a book.
To Bishop Turibius, against the Priscillianists: a book.
To Rusticus, bishop of Narbonne: a book.
To Anastasius, bishop of Thessalonica: a book.
Many letters to various persons: a book.
Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

At length he died, after many labors for the benefit of the Church, under Emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 461, in the fourteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Kalends of July.

Peter, bishop of Ravenna, a learned and very holy man, shining in life with many miracles, was of excellent talent in delivering homilies to the people, and converted many to the path of truth as much by word as by example. He wrote many things for the edification of the faithful. Of these there are circulated:

Several sermons and homilies: epistles.
To Eutyches the priest: an epistle.
He also wrote several other letters.
He flourished under Emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.
