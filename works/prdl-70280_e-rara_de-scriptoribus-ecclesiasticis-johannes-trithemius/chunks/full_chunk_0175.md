Lapus de Castellione, abbot of San Miniato, of the Order of the holy father Benedict, a jurist most learned among all men of his time, trained from his earliest age, as they say, in the disciplines of the courts, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, and an outstanding disputant of scholastic questions, whose authority among jurists is to be reckoned by no means slight. He wrote several treatises in his field. Of these there is extant a distinguished work:

His Allegations, one book.
On the Clementines, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished in the times of Louis IV, emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Guericus, abbot of Igny, a man by no means lowly learned in the divine Scriptures, easy in talent, sweet and composed in eloquence, sufficiently apt for persuasion, distinguished no less by his life and love of regular observance than by knowledge of the Scriptures. For the edification of simple devout brothers he wrote treatises not to be despised; in them, showing himself a useful and faithful shepherd of Christ’s sheep, he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have found only a work of sermons on the temporal cycle and the saints through the circuit of the year, which he delivered:

To his brothers, one book. “We await the Savior.”

He also wrote letters.

As for the other things which he is said to have composed, I have so far been able to find nothing.

Henricus de Vrimaria, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, by nation, as they report, a German, a man from boyhood most studious and very learned in the holy Scriptures, second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy. For many years he was an outstanding doctor in the Parisian school; he shone forth, renowned for celebrated learning. He wrote many excellent volumes, among which the following are said to be:

On the Sentences, four books.
Also Quodlibets, one book.
On the Ten Commandments, one book.
Flowers from the Conferences of the Fathers, twenty-four books. “Hear, O Israel, the commandments. In [unclear].”
On the Perfection of Man, one book. “Because many things suitable for preaching.”
On the Incarnation of the Word, one book.
On the Fourfold Instinct, one book. “Other seed fell into the earth.”
Exposition of the decree on the celebration of Mass, one book.
Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book.
On the Books of the Ethics, ten books.
And many other things.

He flourished in the times of Louis IV, emperor, surnamed the Bavarian, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Bartholomaeus de Osa, by homeland Bergomensis, a man studious in the divine Scriptures, notably learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and historiographer, outstanding in talent and bright in eloquence. He is said to have produced some volumes of excellent learning, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following stands out:

A distinguished work of Histories, sixteen books.

He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.
