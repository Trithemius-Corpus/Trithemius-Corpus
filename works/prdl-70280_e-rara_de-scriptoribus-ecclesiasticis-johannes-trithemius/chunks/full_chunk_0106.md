Guido, monk and abbot of the monastery of the Cross of Saint Leufredus, of the order of the holy father Benedict, by homeland an Aretine, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular disciplines, an outstanding dialectician and musician. In this too he is to be preferred to the others: that, through his rule on the hand, even boys and girls may learn unfamiliar chants by themselves more easily than by the voice of a master or by the use of any instrument. Among other little works of his talent he wrote: against Berengar, cleric of the church of Tours, *On the Body and Blood of the Lord*, one book; *On Music* also, two books, “Therefore those who [unclear] our discipline.” And other works which I have not seen. He flourished under the emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1030.

Egelnoth, archbishop of the church of Canterbury in England, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, most holy in life and conduct, a most merciful advocate of the poor; at his urging Cnut, king of England, gave many alms, especially to the church of Chartres, over which at that time presided Fulbert, a most learned man, of whom we made mention above. He is said to have written, to the honor of almighty God, a fine work: *In Praise of Saint Mary*, one book; Letters to various persons, one book; and certain other things. He flourished in the times of the emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year 1030.

Adelmann, grammarian, from a cleric of Liège bishop of Brescia, once a hearer of Fulbert, bishop of Chartres, a man devoted to and learned in the divine writings, a philosopher and dialectician famous in his time, excellent in verse and prose. He wrote in both styles certain commendable little works, of which only a few have come to my knowledge by reading. He wrote to Berengar of Tours, formerly his fellow student under Fulbert, then beginning to err and to teach depraved doctrine, at great length, correcting him in friendly fashion: *On the Body and Blood of the Lord*, one book, “I call you a collector because.” Letters to various persons, one book. He is also said to have written many other works, both in verse and in prose, which I have not seen. He flourished under King Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1040.

Hermann, who was called Contractus, monk of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the order of the holy father Benedict, German by nation, son of the count of Veringen in Swabia, a man most learned in the divine writings, most universally trained in secular letters, philosopher, poet, astronomer, rhetorician, musician, second to none in his time, most skilled in three languages, Greek, Latin, and Arabic. He wrote many distinguished volumes in verse and prose, of which I have found only the following, namely: *On Music*, one book, “In considering the monochord”; *On the Monochord*, one book, “The first is the age of this world”; *On the Six Ages of the World*, two books, “In measuring, therefore, most subtly”; *On the Composition of the Astrolabe*, one book, “Whoever [unclear] of astronomical [unclear]”; *On the Usefulness of the Astrolabe*, one book, “He who is skilled in arithmetic”; *On Computus*, one book, “Of a distinguished and prudent man”; Lives of several saints, one book; various hymns and proses. He also composed chants and very beautiful proses in sweet and regular melody; among them is that very sweet one concerning the blessed Virgin Mary: *Salve regina*.
