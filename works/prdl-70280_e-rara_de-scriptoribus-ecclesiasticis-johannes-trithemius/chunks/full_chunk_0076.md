But Marianus Scotus, an excellent computist, writes otherwise, saying as follows: If the holy father Benedict, abbot, as some write, died on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April, on the Saturday of holy Easter, he seems to have died in the year of the Lord 601. For unless [unclear] in the ninety-sixth year before that year, his death does not fit, because then, if born, he was very young. Therefore, according to this calculation of Marianus, the holy father Benedict died in the last year of the emperor Maurice, namely in the year of the Lord 601, at the age of more or less ninety, in the fourth indiction, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April.

Boethius Manlius Severinus, ordinary consul, Roman, a philosopher, orator, and distinguished poet, learned in the divine Scriptures and, in secular letters, the most erudite of all men of his time, was the son-in-law of Symmachus the patrician. He was a man most richly imbued with Greek and Latin eloquence, since he translated many volumes both of Aristotle and of other philosophers from the Attic tongue into Latin; subtle in intellect, ready in understanding, and Catholic; in speech not inferior even to Tully. It is established that he had the friendship of Saint Benedict, and ate at his table on Monte Cassino with Tertullus, the senator and father of Placidus. He wrote many splendid volumes.
