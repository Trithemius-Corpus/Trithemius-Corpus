He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Franciscus de Zabarellis, by nation a Paduan, cardinal priest of the Church, a man very learned in canon law, and nobly instructed both in divine Scripture and in secular letters, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote in law some works not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Decretals, one book, “Nothing new is [unclear].”
On the Clementines, one book, “About to repeat the decretal.”
On the canonical hours, one book.
Various repetitions.
And many other things.

He flourished at the Council of Constance, under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418. There also he died, buried among the Friars Minor. In his praise Poggio delivered an oration.

Petrus Maurocenus, a Venetian patrician, cardinal bishop of the most holy Roman Church, a man very learned in divine Scripture and canon law, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect, composed in speech, distinguished in life and conduct. He is said to have fashioned many excellent works, in which the liveliness of his intellect is recognized to shine forth. But I, up to the present, have not deserved to see any of them. There is reported of his an outstanding volume:

On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, one book.
And various determinations.

He flourished in person at the Council of Constance, under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418.

Nicolaus Dinckelspule, by nation a German, glory and rector of the Viennese university in Austria, very learned in the holy Scriptures, much instructed in secular philosophy, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, notable in life and conduct, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory. Among these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, three books.
Questions on the Sentences, one book.
On the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, one book.
On the Ten Commandments, one book, “He writes on Matthew 22, q.”
On the Lord’s Prayer, one book, “If you shall ask the Father anything.”
On the eight beatitudes, one book, “In the Gospel which is read.”
On the three parts of penance, one book, “It should be noted that, as the master says.”
On the eight capital vices, one book, “There are two commandments of charity.”
On the seven mortal sins, one book, “Consequently it must be said.”
On the precept of charity, one book.
On the sins of the tongue, one book.
Sermons for the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Moral sermons, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished in person at the Council of Constance, sent by the University of Vienna, under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.
