And that great prophet Isaiah, when he had seen the mystical vision of almighty God seated on a lofty throne, mindful of his own frailty, trembled, nor did he consent to thrust himself further into the divine spectacles until one of the seraphim, flying to him, had cleansed the mouth of the fearful man with a burning coal. And we, fathers, whenever we are about to speak or hear of the highest and immutable good, let us put off the shoes of worldly affections, fearing that which God threatens against vicious men who dare to scrutinize his majesty, saying: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” Let us also lift up our mind through desire of inward love, so that, touched by the coal of divine fervor, it may be made worthy to be filled with the mystical beholding of divine spectacles. For I say to you who have assembled in this audience of an unlearned man: God can truly be loved by no one without some prior knowledge; but knowledge, the maker of love, comes to no one who has not first been purified in affection from all hatred or love of those things that are in the world and that follow the movement of the flesh. Knowledge of the highest good causes love in us, and love also perfects knowledge. For the Son of God said to the Father concerning believers: “This is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” Therefore from knowledge love arises for us, and from love the everlasting enjoyment of the highest good. That highest immutable good, which we call God, from whom every good in heaven and on earth exists and is named, far and incomparably transcends both our intellect and the angelic intellect, since indeed it is above every affirmation; and toward knowledge of it our mind, aided by it, is advanced rather by affection than by intellect. For our soul, duly cleansed from all these earthly passions, ascends to God by loving, to whom it cannot come by understanding, since it leaves far behind it all images and likenesses both of those things that are and of those things that are not; and alone with love it enters the darkness of divine contemplation, in which no idol is admitted, no form, no mass ever set before the senses or the intellect. For as Saint Dionysius the Areopagite, disciple of Paul, says in the Mystical Theology: that divine majesty, existing far beyond every affirmation and negation, according to our intellect, is neither soul, nor sense, nor imagination, nor opinion, nor reason, nor understanding; nor does it have reason or understanding; nor is it spoken or understood. Nor is it number, nor order, nor greatness, nor smallness, nor equality, nor likeness, nor unlikeness. Nor does it stand, nor is it moved, nor does it enact rest. Nor does it have power, nor is it power, nor is it light, nor life, nor substance, nor age, nor time. Nor is there a spiritual touching of it; nor is it knowledge, nor truth, nor kingdom, nor wisdom. Nor is it one, nor unity, nor deity or goodness; nor is it spirit, insofar as we ourselves are able to know. Nor is there a denomination of Son or of Father; nor is it anything of those things that are known to us or to anyone in the world. Nor is it anything of those things that are. Nor [unclear] error, truth, nor is [unclear]. We neither seek [unclear], nor do we make negations or [negations?]; we neither affirm God himself nor remove him. For the highest ineffable good is above every affirmation, the perfect and singular cause of all things, and its excellence is above every negation; and it is simply free from all things that can be thought, understood, or spoken by us or by anyone in the world, and beyond all things.
