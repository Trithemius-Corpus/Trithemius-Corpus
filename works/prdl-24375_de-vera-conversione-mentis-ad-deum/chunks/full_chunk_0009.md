When this first shines upon the soul, now serene and purified from lower things, it kindles a wondrous love: namely, when, transcending in mind all forms and intentions, not only imaginable but also intellectual, it has stripped them away, then through love of the infinite good, despising itself, it rejoices with ineffable joy over all things that can either be said or thought. This indeed the soul can attain not only after the present life, but sometimes even in this life, when, purified from lower things and freely restored and united to itself, it is so borne upward through the desire of love, [unclear] affectionately into God, that, utterly forgetful of all things that are in the world, and not even knowing itself, [it rejoices] with inexpressible joy. Those who have experienced these things know them; if time allowed, their virtues could be thrown in the face of those who, since they are carnal and given over to vices, having a veil spread over their heart, dream that the mystical theology of the saints and the contemplative life contain nothing of truth, and think that it is fabricated according to the quality of affections in the opinion of an unlearned man, as they themselves are accustomed to say. Of these the holy Apostle said: The animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit, for it is [unclear] to him; and he cannot understand, because [they are examined?] spiritually. And Saint Jude the Apostle: “These are,” he says, “they who separate themselves, animal men, not having the spirit.” Unhappy are those who immerse themselves in earthly cares and the pleasures of the flesh, who hunt after the vain knowledge of this world, together with false honors; just as their mind, always occupied with lower things, has never learned to shake itself free from sensible things, so it does not rise to the sweetness of divine love. You err, O mortals, and indeed you err greatly, who, intent either on vain studies, or sunk in the cares of the world, or oppressed by the pleasures of the flesh or by evil passions, think that you either know or love God. For it cannot happen that a mind which loves any other thing, about which it is occupied, can purely love the highest and unchangeable good, which is God, or ever be joined to him through desire; but it must have nothing else, neither of good nor of evil, at hand, so that, free and released from all things, it may someday grasp, alone, the One alone. For union between God and the mind cannot come to be unless, all things having been cast aside that are wont either to stain the affection or confound the intellect, the mind itself is joined through love, pure to the most pure, clean to the most clean, one to the One, and alone to the Alone.

Now, man, approach the deep heart; and after such cultivation of your soul, whereby through the study of letters and the various traditions of masters, from youth down to old age and extreme age, you have labored for a little knowledge, at last cast the seed of divine sweetness into the field of your heart, so that someday you may receive the fruit of most holy love, without which no one will arrive at the enjoyment of the unchangeable good. The studies of letters are indeed beautiful; to devote labor to receptive theology is pleasant; to have knowledge of law, astronomy, poetry, music, rhetoric, arithmetic, philosophy, and the other arts is certainly delightful. Yet one must not grow old in them in such a way, not linger, not consume all the time of life; rather, from these one must ascend by the ladder of love to the highest beauty, in which the true blessedness of the soul infinitely transcends every created beauty. Otherwise, O learned men, what does it profit to cultivate the field of the mind laboriously through the daily study of letters, as though by plowing, if either, hungry, it is not sown with divine seed, or the slender fruit of inward love is choked among the thorns of riches and the briars of pleasures? If we believe the proverb, or rather, if with open eyes we see what experience teaches us, integrity of life is rarer among the learned: the brighter they are in intellect through acquired knowledge, the more foul they are in affection. In their person, Saint Augustine, having heard of the conversion of Saint Anthony and moved by just grief, cried out: “The unlearned rise up and seize heaven, and we, with our doctrines, serving flesh and blood, descend to hell.” Turn back to the heart, O wise men; leave vain cares; purify your souls from every carnal affection; drive out hatred together with love [of the world]; cleanse your minds of all things that can impede the sweet divine influx, so that you may be able truly to love God. To his unsurpassable enjoyment the soul can in no way be admitted after the present life if, while set in the flesh, it has not loved him above all things. Therefore, so that a graded ascent toward true conversion may be prepared for the mind, since no one suddenly becomes highest, we shall erect, in a few words, a ladder distinguished by seven steps. By it the mind itself, ascending in some measure to the knowledge of the highest good, and devoting itself meanwhile to holy exercises of divine love, may deserve to obtain sweetness.
