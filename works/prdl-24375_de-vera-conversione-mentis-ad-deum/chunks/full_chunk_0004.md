Since, therefore, that highest good, God, the immutable principle of all things, is none at all of those things which we either understand or are able to understand, which it happens that we understand only through the service of the senses, which are fed upon the mass of bodily objects; and since everything spiritual is most remote from those senses themselves: whoever still clings to the senses, whoever, blinded by the passions of his flesh, sinks the concupiscible power of the soul into the chains of evil affections, it is not possible that he should ever perceive this gift of mystical illumination himself, or be able usefully to persuade others about it. For only the soul of a clean heart, raised from the lowest things not through intellect but through affection, can now, while established in the body, sweetly see God by loving him, in a mirror and in an enigma; and when at last, released from the flesh, free and absolved from every disordered affection, it has returned to its principle, then, more united to God through the grace of love, it will know him perfectly, as the Son of God promised us, saying: Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God. But now, proceeding to the examination of the senses, we shall see how far their consideration is from the knowledge of God.

That great prince of theologians, the holy evangelist John, at the beginning of his theology, thus beautifully concludes concerning the highest good by positions nearer to sight: In him was life, and the life was the light of men; and the light shines in darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it. Come now, man, you who trust in sensible things, who have learned to exercise your mind for understanding from nowhere except through sensible things; come, I say, and question your senses themselves, so that they, which report to you almost countless other things, may teach what the light is that shines in darkness and cannot be comprehended. First test your hearing.

Have you heard what light is? Mother [unclear], show me what light is, if you know, so that through it an ascent may be prepared for me to the spiritual light which I have sought in all things. Hearing will answer you, saying: I am airy; be content if I report airy sounds to you. I have not heard light. But if I have ever heard anything about it, it was not light itself, but air moved by the tongue, very far removed from the truth of light, which began and passed away. Such is not the incomprehensible light. Smell will say: I indeed am not so luminous; I belong to vapor; learn vapors from me. Why do you seek from me things that are foreign to me? If light is not vapor, it is nothing to me. Taste, when questioned about light, will answer: I move among liquids. If the light you seek is not a liquid, either sweet or bitter, or at least mixed, the judgment of it does not pertain to me. Touch will answer: Do not try to force from me what I cannot comprehend. I am corporeal; I report corporeal things. Seek from others; I do not know what light is. Turn at last, finally, to the shining eyes; by the light through which, above the other senses, indeed through which alone, they are delighted, call them to witness. Let them indicate to reason what light is. Sight will suddenly answer: I am a splendid spirit, and I am a spiritual splendor; when questioned, I shall gladly set forth my proper offices. Light is a certain spiritual, sudden, and very broad emanation from bodies, without detriment to their own nature: namely, of a certain brightness from transparent things, but of color from the opposites of these; of quantity, figure, and motion from all things. Now gather into one genus every kind of color. What will this whole be, except a certain many-colored light, or light made opaque in the more solid and darker matter of earth? Separate out the earth mixed with it. What will remain, except a certain quality, indeed a brightness and act of the transparent, just as color is the act of the opaque? Color indeed is opaque light; light, however, is clear color, indeed a certain flower and vigor of a transparent body and of colors, as if one-colored in act and many-colored in power. What is this that sight has reported to you about light? Is this the light that it described? I have never heard anything defined more obscurely.
