Therefore, despising the blind report of the senses, let us ascend from here to the lofty watchtower of the mind, to see whether perhaps the mind itself can suggest to us what the light is which is said to shine in darkness.

O mind, you who measure all things rightly, tell me: is perhaps the light that shines in darkness God himself, than whom nothing is clearer and nothing more obscure? Nothing is clearer than that God exists, and that he is supremely powerful, wise, and good; for everything made by him proclaims this to our ears, so that no one is deafer than he who does not hear so great a sound and noise of all things. On the other hand, nothing is more obscure than what God is, so that nothing is darker than the man who thinks this is utterly clear. But the mind, illuminated by the light of the Catholic faith and raised by intellectual knowledge to cognition of heavenly things, will answer: God is the father of lights, with whom there is no change by which he may be extinguished or diminished, nor shadow of alteration by which he may in turn enter night or suffer any defect. Again it will say that God is light in which there is no darkness at all; form in which there is nothing formless; beauty also in which there is nothing deformed.

But what are these things to him who is above all things and transcends every positive assertion infinitely far; who made from nothing those things that exist; uncreated, eternal, immovable, incomprehensible, always existing; who is none of the things he made, he who made all things? Therefore, O mind eager for heavenly things, leave the senses behind, pass beyond reason, and transcend even yourself, withdrawing yourself into the affection of the heart. Even if not for others, at least for yourself do not be slow to gain, by loving, a taste of the highest and unchangeable good, leaving behind you all images and every likeness, so that by freely, absolutely, and purely going beyond yourself and all things, with all things removed and yourself released from all things, naked and alone, you may be able to fly up to the supersubstantial ray of the divine darkness. Meanwhile lead your intellect captive; indeed, allow it to be buried. Take care to enlarge your affection as much as you can by loving, since you can by no means comprehend God by understanding, for he stands far above every intellect. Yet access to him lies open to you by loving, if you are pure, if gentle, if tranquil, if released from all things and rightly united to yourself.

I would not have you be deceived by taste, since whatever you see, whatever you grasp, whatever you say, is not God; and what he is, you, clothed in a frail little body, cannot know, nor understand, nor in any way express, even if you were endowed with all the tongues of the world. Strive nevertheless with prudent sobriety, only as one wholly purified from the affections of the flesh, loving no one in this world except God alone, actually stripped of passions, calm and tranquil, quiet without any disturbance. The ray of the highest good has poured its goodness into you, by which God himself is shown to you to be invisible, incomprehensible light, the truth of every truth and the cause of all things. Its splendor, or rather its shadow, is that visible and finite light, the cause of visible things, whose nature is to disclose all other things to the rest. But to God, the invisible light, all things are truly and clearly visible through himself: he enlightens every man coming into this world, through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made that was made.

But, O reason, do not trust the senses when you go on investigating, with mystical concern, the principle of all things, God, the unfailing light. For all the senses of the body are dulled, and perceive nothing of the highest truth. Sight did not sufficiently report to you about light; the others did not report at all. Everything spiritual is far removed from the senses.

Ascend to that lofty watchtower of the mind, O reason, friend of truth, once the senses have been entirely lulled. Since it is a certain intellectual light and luminous understanding, indeed illuminated by the invisible light through a certain participation, whose object is intelligible light itself, it can lead you by the hand to the summit of the divine darkness, in which the highest Truth inhabits inaccessible light.
