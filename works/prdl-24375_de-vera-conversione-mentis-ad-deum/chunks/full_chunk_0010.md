*The first step, therefore,* is the purification of the soul itself from every attachment to corruptible things, actions, and desires: so that it may be clean, still, tranquil, released from cares, empty of evil desires, free from every carnal love, and, as far as possible, united to itself in its innate beauty. For the soul’s natural beauty, formed by the supreme Beauty, is defiled by admixture with the body it has assumed, just as ugliness befalls gold if it is covered with clods of earth. If someone shakes these off, pure gold at once remains; and it is now beautiful when it has been separated from what is alien, content in its own purity for beauty. In the same way the soul, separated from those desires which it conceived in itself through excessive commerce with the body, and freed from other disturbances, the stains now erased which bodily familiarity had burned into it by long habit, and thus left alone to itself, has without doubt cleansed away all ugliness previously imbibed from an alien nature; and, having found again its lost beauty, it now first ascends the step of divine knowledge, and that more difficult step itself. For this reason the oracles of the Scriptures rightly prophesy that the soul not purified in the present life from sins contracted in the flesh, after death, lying in filth among the dead, is tormented. For an impure soul, on account of its depravity, is very like filthy swine in the body: they delight in filth and dirt. What else is true temperance, by which alone the monastic religion is especially adorned, than not to indulge bodily pleasures, but to flee them far away as the foulest diseases of the soul? Fortitude, however, is to fear none of the things that are transitory, nor even death itself, which is nothing but a certain separation of the soul from the body, which the mind does not fear when it desires to be alone and to be happily united forever with its own beginning. Moreover, what else is magnanimity than contempt for mortal things for the love of the divine? And prudence is understanding that shuns lower things and raises the soul to higher things. When the soul has been instructed in these doctrines and already purified from the filth of vices, reason and beauty become united to it, so that thereafter it yields to no pleasures of the flesh that are opposed to the highest principle; wonderfully restored to beauty and understanding, it advances, illuminated by the ray of divine splendor.

*The second step* is knowledge of divine works. When the mind considers their beauty with admiration in many and diverse forms, it cannot fail at some time to lift itself upward with the desire of love to the author of so great a beauty. Hence also the holy Apostle says to the Romans that the invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, are perceived by understanding through the things that have been made. For all the beauty of the world has been formed by the supreme Beauty, and by its variety invites us to knowledge of him, to such a degree that nothing is more foolish than one whom so great a variety of beautiful things does not kindle to contemplation of the supreme Beauty. What else, too, does the very sequence of the Scriptures suggest to us than that that highest, unchangeable good, God, in the beginning created heaven and earth, and all things contained within the circuit of that heaven; and, through the miracles made by him, marvelous and countless as they are, which by their cry compel us, as if by hands, to knowledge of the highest truth, unless we are harder than stones.

*The third step* is contemplation of the order by which lower works are brought back step by step to higher ones. For since the supercelestial world, and also the celestial, is most orderly, the sublunary world too is necessarily ordered; and since, in it, things lower than man are ordered, therefore human things are much more so.
