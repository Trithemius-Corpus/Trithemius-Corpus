Therefore God himself is the beginning above all things, who made all things; whom no understanding can comprehend, because he is above every understanding; who created every understanding; who is worshipped in spirit and truth, and, invisible, is seen by the love of the purest affection; who, always remaining unchangeable, causes not himself, but us, to be changed by our affection according to the image of piety. Since he made all things and can do all things, the ineffable principle of all things, he is not circumscribable, but immense, incomprehensible, including all things, filling all things, containing all things. And because he is immense, he has no magnitude, since magnitude has place only in external things. Indeed, the author of magnitude himself must not be a participant in magnitude. When he is called great, there is no quantity at all; rather, his magnitude is that nothing is more powerful than he, and nothing more sublime, to whom there is nothing either in heaven or on earth that can be compared. Moreover, when we say that God himself reigns through all ages of ages and everywhere throughout the universe, this signifies neither measure in him nor, again, vast immensity; otherwise by what means would he measure the remaining things? Nor is it permitted to imagine any figure or form in him, since that incomprehensible divinity and the immense majesty of beauty, by infinite comparisons and beyond measure, far surpasses everything that can be imagined, thought, comprehended, or spoken; nor is there anything of those things which mortal man has ever understood or could understand, concerning which the prophet Isaiah rightly cried out: Truly you are a hidden God, the God of Israel, the savior, who makes darkness your hiding-place and dwells in inaccessible light; by whose wisdom every man has been made foolish. O immense beauty of the divine majesty, able to be tasted by loving affection alone, desirable to the mind above all things; of which we can receive no figure and no form, to be supremely desired and loved by us; whose love is immense, whom to know and love is eternal blessedness. For this love is not finite, since even the beloved himself is not finite; but his love is infinite, always [unclear] with eternal felicity.

Therefore also his beauty is lovable above every beauty: for indeed he created every beauty; and since he is both its beginning and its end, he is not a participant in it, but, existing without comparison beyond measure above every position in his own beauty, goodness, truth, and eternity, he transcends it.

¶ But now you will have to consider further how the mind itself ought to conform itself to its own principle; by what inquiry it must return to that principle after the allurements of pleasures; what necessity or what utility is imposed upon those returning; and finally what is the manner of purging the affection from the lowest and carnal passions, as it seeks the highest and unchangeable good, so that, free and released from desires opposed to reason and to the rectitude of the mind, it may take up the upward passage of love. For our soul itself, made in the image of God, as Scripture bears witness, always indeed, stirred by the natural love implanted in it, desires to have recourse to the fountain of its beauty and to be united to its own principle, which reason has most excellently persuaded it must be loved; and the nature of loving, surpassing every appearance, consists in the one object of blessedness itself. Whatever you show it by referring it, in respect, to that object, is not enough, since in this very thing it always seeks something else that formed it. For reason teaches that everything which has form, and the form and appearance itself, is something measured, and for that reason is neither universal, nor sufficient of itself, nor beautiful by its own nature, but rather is mixed, not pure, not simple, and made by another. Therefore these things must be beautiful, but measured. But the highest good itself, which is truly beautiful, or rather above beauty, is circumscribed by the limit of no measure. Certainly it is not formed; likewise it is neither appearance nor something participating in any of those things which are known to us or to anyone in the world. Therefore that which is lovable first and exists first utterly surpasses every appearance; and there the beauty of intelligible beauty, above every understanding, and its comeliness, is the highest goodness itself, free from all things that are not, always existing absolute, the living principle of all things, of the rational soul
