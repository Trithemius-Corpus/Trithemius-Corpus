*An elegiac poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on holy mother Anne.*

How great are the rewards Anne brings back to her devotees no one grasps in mind, nor can he speak of them by mouth. She drives off hardships, obtains pardon, increases the adornments of honor for her own, and causes them to be in heaven.

Conversely, many very bitter things attend the despisers of mother Anne. She casts the powerful down from honor, tramples the proud, casts [the fierce?] down from honor, and permits them often to [bear?] great scandals.

Therefore, men, I advise you: serve so great a patroness, and let the company of women be present likewise with love. What we write is clearly established also by examples; let open ears receive what helps pious hearts.

*From Sponheim, on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August, 1494.*

*A Sapphic poem of Rutgerus Sicamber, for a hymn in praise of Saint Anne.*

We sing your praises, venerable Anne; favor those who praise you with melodies, obtaining pardon for all and granting the rewards of salvation.

You, Anne, parent of Christ's mother, shining in the highest citadel of heaven, obtain whatever you ask of the Son of the Almighty.

You heal the weak, cleanse what is stained, make faithful servants obtain their wish, and at last grant them to ascend the heavenly kingdom.

By approved conduct, Anne, and by many prayers, you moved the highest Lord to grant that you become the mother of so great an offspring in the world.

Let honor and power, brightness, strength, piety, and vigor be to the highest Father, to the heavenly Son, and to the Holy Spirit for all eternity.

*Another by the same.*

Let us all resound the assiduous praises of Anne, mother of the Virgin Mother, with all our powers, from the marrow of the soul, with sonorous voice.

She brought forth for wretched humankind, soiled by the stain of mother Eve, the noble offspring who brought forth to the world the radiant light.

This light drove out the darkness of the blackening world, shining on all with heavenly light, as Saint John proclaims in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine girl, whose Begotten One you call your grandson according to the flesh only, do not despise the praying people.

O Virgin, [favor?] the prayers of mother Anne, who asks earnestly for us; you, grandson Jesus, easy to entreat, fulfill her request.

To the Son of the daughter of mother Anne, with God the Most High, be honor and power; equal honor is paid to the blessed Spirit for all time.

*Another poem by the same, in iambic dimeter, which is sung to the melody of the hymns of Ambrose.*

Bowing down, let us honor the feast of blessed Anne, singing due thanks with our voices and our innermost hearts.

She is the mother of the gracious Virgin, who brought forth the radiance of the world; she is the one who by her virtues rendered herself most pleasing to God.

She drives away [harms?] and confers grace by her prayers. She relieves the most dreadful diseases, both of body and of spirit.

In praise of her, O King of heaven, to you, to the Son, and at the same time to the Spirit, we utter pious praises with sonorous songs. Amen.

*An [Alcianic?] poem by the same, which is sung to the melody of 'Crux fidelis' or 'Pange lingua.'*

This company, with voice, prayers, and pious hearts, longs to set forth the feasts of Anne, mother so renowned. Be gently present, we beseech you, to those who praise you.

You, parent of Mother Mary, you, most pleasing to God, preserved the [commands?] of the Law, which Moses once delivered to the ancient fathers and to the Hebrew peoples.

Obtaining wondrous, gracious gifts for the faithful by prayer pleasing to the Son of the high Father, you heal the sick and grant that hearts be chaste.

You, Father, and the Son with the Spirit, we ask, that you give joyful things to us who sing the renowned praises of most sweet Anne, mother of the gracious Virgin. Amen.

*Poem of Rutger [Venral?], a German canon regular of [Heynen?], on the book On the Praises of Saint Anne.*

Whoever you will be, reader of this excellent book, put away vain fear: it contains nothing frivolous.

Abbot Trithemius, by his art, has brought forth this book, pregnant with wondrous meaning and sweet with charm.

Watching over books, the keeper of the Sponheim fold, who cultivates the sacred writings of the threefold language--you will find them; only turn them over in a pious heart.

He does not pursue vain and everywhere doubtful little tales; he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence.

Therefore, I pray, read with love the worthy praises of venerable Anne, which the learned man has brought forth for you in love.

*Printed in the noble city of Mainz by Peter of Friedberg, in the year of the Virgin birth 1494, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of August.*
