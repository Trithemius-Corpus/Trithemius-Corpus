*The song or hymn of Johannes Herst of Lauterburg on Saint Anne. To the Reverend Father and Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.*

Rejoice, Anne, refuge for the wretched; we pray, we sing, we bring forth songs. On your feast, with your offspring, be gracious; now we speak praises to you.

Rejoice, earth shining with fertile grass and, drenched, moist with deific dew, from which was born, purer than the ether, the Virgin unknowing of sin.

Rejoice, you who had been of royal seed, and now, conceived by a man, had entered upon famous marriage, long without offspring, made a mother by a vow.

Rejoice, you who were confounded, reddening at the pontiff’s reproach; you call upon God with tears. He hears you; you receive your husband coming to meet you at the gate called Golden.

Rejoice, you who, glad, embraced Joachim, your husband, coming to meet you; and, instructed by the heavenly divinity, thus the mother of your Jesus is conceived in your little womb.

Rejoice, simple prudence, angelic in discernment, you who, praised by the choir and divided threefold in portion, divide your goods among the temple and the poor and a part to your servants.

Rejoice, Anne: the people of the faithful celebrates your feasts, that, as patroness for them, by your prayers you may render gracious the Father, the Spirit, and Christ begotten. Amen.

*An elegiac poem of Rutger Venrai, regular canon in Haina, in the diocese of Worms, on Saint Anne.*

Hail, splendid mother of the Virgin mother, of the brightness of the Father, of the radiance of uncreated light.

You, holy one, bore for the Lord a divine girl, from whom came forth Jesus, the gracious light of the world.

Constantly call the son born of your daughter your grandson, though he himself made the heavens and the earth.

In flesh your grandson, Jesus himself, joined supreme divinity to flesh; for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always been, nevertheless he chooses and deigns to be called your grandson.

Do not hesitate to strike such a grandson with prayers, Anne; we ask that you will bear all the things asked.

If he has not bent easy ears at first, quickly have recourse to your daughter.

Let your daughter show her breasts to Christ her son; straightway the gracious parent will bring what is desired.

If the matter has been very hard to obtain by prayer, let the daughter seek help with her mother.

My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that the Lord will straightway hear both women praying with a gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers it is fitting to seek the help of Anne and Mary together.

It is certain that, if you honor the mother of the Lord’s mother, you also cultivate the titles of the Virgin Mother herself.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it becomes clear that you are preparing praises for the Lord in his mother.

To the son of the daughter of Anne be praise, virtue, and honor, and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*A Sapphic poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the day of Saint Anne.*

Let us celebrate the feasts of holy Anne, the exalted mother, rich in merits, who brought forth for the world the parent of the Son of God the Father.

Her ever-venerable life is rightly praised by us as blessed; by the gift of signs you increase relief for wretched minds.

She heals the sick, cleanses away all evils, brightens the hearts of the sorrowful with joy, sets fitting things in order, rules and governs, and makes rough places smooth.

Most powerful, dwelling in the hall of the highest King, she wipes away stains by prayer, bestows grace, and leads faithful souls to the stars.

Here, as the brothers frequent your feasts, bringing forth vows and praying good things of you, help, preserve, restore, govern, renowned mother.
