Anna, heavy with child, you bear the garden which the flaming sword of the cherubim guards, burning night and day.
Hail.
Anna, you bear a flower that surpasses white lilies, humble violets, and the crimson rose.
Hail.
Anna, in you is found the Mosaic ark, and the golden urn of heavenly bread lies there.
Hail.
Anna, you bear the bride of the true Solomon, and the house adorned within and without with every gem.
Hail.
Anna, you bear the temple whose inner chambers sweet sounds have filled, and all music rejoicing.
Hail.
Anna, you bear the veil, beneath a beautiful image of the fathers, holding in itself the things done, to cover the lofty things of God.
Hail.
Anna, you bear offspring: the golden flame of Phoebus clothes the head, and the bright moon the snow-white feet.
Our Father. Ave Dei.
Anna, now made possessor of the longed-for Mary, you scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have arisen.
Hail.
Anna, at Mary newly born all the stars applauded, and an unwonted radiance flowed from the pole.
Hail.
Anna, it is right to believe that angelic choirs around you celebrated the birthday of your offspring.
Hail.
Anna, the deep shades felt your new childbirth; then a new light itself terrified the Stygian pools.
Hail.
Anna, mother, you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands, more radiant than the sun and the lights of heaven.
Hail.
Anna, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl? She smiles at her mother; she causes no sadness.
Hail.
Anna, take courage: behold, your daughter reaches for the milky breasts, and with tender hands seeks your neck; you receive her tender limbs in your tender lap.
Hail.
Anna, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast she draws sweet juices with rosy mouth, sounding pleasing murmurs; [unclear].
Our Father. Ave Dei.
Hail.

Anna, it is your heart's desire to adorn your daughter with approved manners and to prepare her for the highest God.
Hail.
Anna, you prepare to bring your daughter into the lofty shrine; but the blessed girl of her own accord reached the upper step.
Hail.
Anna, you follow the swift one with slow step and effort; the eager little virgin comforts her weary mother.
Hail.
Anna, why do you marvel at strength in the tender virgin? You see not human things, but holy signs of God.
Hail.
Anna, you are accustomed to give gifts to the needy and to sacred temples; now you give that than which no woman has given more.
Hail.
Anna, where you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell, she willingly entered the cloisters and the virginal house.
Hail.
Anna, about to send away the pious one, you waste your cheeks and eyes with weeping; deep love holds you motionless.
Hail.
Anna, return home; leave your daughter to the Thunderer. Let her think of nothing but the Jesus who is to come.
Hail.
Anna, it is enough for you to serve two Marys; they too will honor you with no small devotion.
Our Father. Ave Dei.
Hail.
Anna, by your weeping you have learned to succor one who weeps. Therefore you too readily favor the wretched with your prayers.
Hail.
Anna, you are accustomed to overturn the established laws of nature; and, if you will it, let the barren woman now be a joyful mother.
Hail.
Anna, you even stay the gifts of fleeting fortune, lest, slipping suddenly, they flee from our laps.
Hail.
Anna, you put disgrace to flight; you give the gifts of fair fame. You relieve griefs, and love yields to you.
Hail.
Anna, you help either one whom hostile force terrifies, or one whom the cruel bonds of a dark prison hold.
Hail.
Anna, you are present as faithful relief for sick mortals; with you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing.
Hail.
Anna, mother, the sailor calls upon you on the swelling wave, if winds and water toss his shattered boat.
Hail.
Anna, mother, you are always to be feared by the black tyrant; as ice flees fire, so that guilty one flees you.
Anna, mortal hearts cannot ask anything of you in vain. Whatever you will, your daughter and God too will will.
Hail.
Anna, therefore blessed by merits and by so great an offspring, great mother Anna, look upon our prayers.
Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary.*
Almighty and most merciful God, who deigned to choose blessed Anna as the mother of the mother of your Son: graciously grant that we who honor her memory with faithful devotion may obtain, by her merits and prayers, the helps of eternal life. Through the same our Lord.

*Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beissel[ius?], professor of both laws, patrician of Aachen, to the Reverend Father and Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim of the Order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1494.*

*A poem of Adam Werner of Themar on most holy Anna. To the Reverend Father and Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.*

For the praises of the illustrious grandmother we compose [songs]. Dear grandson, move the plectra of the [unclear]; nor you, her daughter, chaste parent, be absent from the singing choir.

Anna, offspring of kings, is given to a husband; barren for a long time, she laments that she is sterile. Weeping, she prays for offspring, nor do her tears flow in vain.

Heavenly messengers promise a child. Pregnant, joined to her husband, and then brought to childbed, she is proved by the offspring born, through whom our salvation came.

Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green; a fruitful rod comes forth with fertile shoot. Refreshed by its flower, death conquered, we live again.
