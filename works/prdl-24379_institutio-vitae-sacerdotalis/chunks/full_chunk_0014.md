Therefore temptation must be resisted at its beginnings, because lust is never better conquered than if it is avoided as a fugitive. For if once it has entangled you through habit, it never willingly lets you go without the special grace of God. If you hesitate to give credence to our words, read the examples of the elders. But there is no need to seek examples in books, since you can find lessons before your eyes. Behold, we know many aged priests today whose life we know to be incontinent. We see that in them age grows old, yet the vices of youth by no means cease. And when they come to the last stage of old age, they do not abandon their depraved habits; but such as they always were from the beginning, such also they go to death. You would see among them priests already decrepit who have drunk in the indulgence of lust to such a degree that, when because of old age they can no longer act wantonly in deed, they burn for the accustomed thing in thought, word, and gestures. Of such persons the Lord says through the prophet: If the Ethiopian can change his skin, and the leopard his spots, then you also will be able to do good, you who have learned to do evil. Intercourse with women can cease in old men, but the desire for the act remains as formerly. Therefore, as you ought to be as an old man, so also now live as a young man. For it is difficult for an old man to correct his habits and to think new thoughts in an old mind. Indeed, old age has habits; it does not receive them. He who wantons with a woman when young is mad when old. Therefore it is good for a man when he has borne the yoke of the Lord from his youth, since if anyone has been evil in youth, rarely will he be good in old age.

*On the remedy against lust. Chapter 3.* Physicians are accustomed first to show the sick the seriousness of their disease by subtle discussion, and only then to apply help with remedies. For once the gravity of the illness is known, the bitterness of the draught terrifies less. This I shall do with you also: first I have disclosed to you the danger of cohabitation with a woman by manifold demonstration, and, so far as I could, I have declared what is the end of this disease. It follows now, therefore, that we should consider the remedy. For the flesh always lusts against the spirit, and unless it is restrained by wholesome exercise, it casts both itself and him into the depth of damnation. Hence the poet rightly admonishes, saying: If you take away idleness, the bows of Cupid perish, and his despised torches lie without light. Holy Scripture also forbids it, since every idle person is in desires.
