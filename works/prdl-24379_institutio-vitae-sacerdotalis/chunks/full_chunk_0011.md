With dreadful evil arts and superstitions they entangle priests in their lusts, until, their strength gradually drained away, they render them utterly useless. Blessed is the man whom God has kept from an evil and lustful woman. There is no wickedness [greater than?] the malice of a woman who enjoys evil loves. Whatever she devises, she tries; whatever she can do, she dares. No one believes what devices, ways, and inventions she uses, so that she may lay low, with an indissoluble bond of her pleasures, the man whom she plans to deceive. She attaches herself as devoted to her lord, not because she loves him, but because she desires him. For the crowd of concubines follows the prey, not the priest. As long as he is rich, he has abundance in everything; she does not leave her lord's side. But if he begins to lack even bread, the concubine [unclear] herself to another [unclear]. They are glued together so that by no means can they be separated from one another, except by death. It also often happens that some prefer to give up their prebends rather than their concubines. You are not ignorant of what happened to your first preceptor. He, miserably seduced by the love of that harlot whom you knew, since because of fear of her husband he could not live with her publicly, despising his relatives and the benefices which he had, in order that he might enjoy her freely, went away with her, fleeing secretly. And to this very day no one knows where he has gone. I know a certain priest from among those who keep concubines; when, for his sin, I was at one time rebuking him privately, I finally brought him to this point, that he was willing to promise perpetual continence thereafter into my hands. And when I pressed him to cast out the concubine at once, he answered: “I cannot.” “Why?” I said. “If I put her away,” he said, “who would take care of my little children?” For he had two boys by [Caiʒ?]. “I am poor,” he said; “I am not able to hire them out for wages. Therefore I will not cast out the mother until the little children grow up, lest I become guilty of blood. Yet I will be chaste with her as with a chaste woman, and will not henceforth be joined to her in any union.” “And what will you do,” I said, “if, before these children have grown up, others arise in their place?” He swore that he wished to take care that this should not happen. “You will not amend the fault,” I say, “unless you cast out the concubine. It is difficult to abandon accustomed things, and, when the occasion is present, to abstain from former vices. No one will nourish a serpent in his bosom unharmed.” The man went away to his house, and to this day he has not abandoned his domestic crimes. I have considered all the works of God, for the man whom He has despised, no one can amend. So difficult is it for a priest, once fallen into fornication, to be able to rise again to true chastity. For according to Scripture: He who commits sin is the slave of sin.
