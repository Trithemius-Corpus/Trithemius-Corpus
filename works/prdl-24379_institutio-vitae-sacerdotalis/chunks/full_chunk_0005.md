It is now your part, O Nicholas, to rebuke the vices of others with diligent admonition, and to show yourself free from vices, lest, if you denounce crimes while vicious yourself, you render yourself liable to condemnation. For no one can confidently reprove in another what, with conscience as witness, he remembers himself to have committed. Therefore he who must judge the sins of others must be irreproachable, because the man who commits in himself what he denounces in others does not amend but provokes. But today many among priests are found like hypocrites: they grow angry with brothers who offend in lighter matters, and do not wish anyone to be indignant against themselves. Miserable is the condition of human frailty: a sinner condemns a sinner, and in the same crime delivers an unequal sentence. I should lie if an adulterer did not very often rebuke his neighbor for fornication; a judge vicious in many things condemns a few vices in another, and, so that he may be able to hide his own depravity, he reveals the transgressions of all. Against this it is said by the Lord: Hypocrite, first cast out the beam which you carry in your own eye, and then you may see the splinter in another's. Why does the sacrilegious man reprove a thief? Why does the criminal accuse the vicious man? Every correction will be wholly fruitless which an equal foulness of life stains. Therefore, so that you may lack the reproach of hypocrites, beware, as one who corrects another, of offending in a similar sin. Correction will never become useful among subjects while he who presides bends his neck to public sins. Indeed, the right of correction is lost when the conduct of the corrector is polluted by the vices of his subjects. The authority of the priesthood grows cheap if public iniquity corrupts the honorableness of conduct. Therefore let every correction take its beginning in its own author, and only then will it proceed safely into public view. Let him whom his own conscience gnaws in a similar crime suspend for a time the sentence of rebuke. The state of believers is now for the most part corrupt, and as the people are, so also is the priest; all are devoted to avarice, from the greater down to the least. Woe to the sinful nation which always slips down into graver things, while it beholds its judges guilty of an equal fault. Ecclesiastical authority has now come down to such misery that we are commanded to embrace the words of priests, not their deeds. For the unhappy priests say of themselves, bearing witness to their own defilement: 'Attend, O Christian faithful, to the words of our instruction; beware our examples.' Words most worthy of everlasting confusion: for as much as by their doctrine they ought to have instructed the people of God, so much by their perverse works they scandalize them.
