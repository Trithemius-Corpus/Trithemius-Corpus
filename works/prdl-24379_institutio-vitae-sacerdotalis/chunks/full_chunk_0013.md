Little by little they grow accustomed to touching one another with their hands. At last, through mutual laughter, future unions are prepared; and while by custom they live familiarly together, they suddenly fall into the pit of the devil. But afterward, once they have fallen into mutual guilt, what has been done seems somewhat abhorrent to them, but they cannot easily draw back from what they have begun. Therefore they continue on the path they had entered; and although at the beginning the conscience abhors such zeal [unclear], nevertheless by habit it gradually bears it more lightly. And at first indeed they begin to behave wantonly with a certain shamefaced fear, but thereafter, blinded by depraved habit, they are no longer ashamed. Meanwhile the belly, conscious of the secret, begins to swell, and to reveal publicly the crime that had lain hidden. Finally a little child is born; for a short time the father and mother are ashamed, and at once they return to their accustomed crimes. From then on, indeed, all shame is cast far away; the eye of conscience is blinded; the fear of God and of hell is utterly driven from memory. They now live licentiously in sins, joined by diabolical glue; they live defamed before men; they live alienated from God. Soon the long-established crime turns into despair; the remedy of repentance is derided; the long-suffering patience of God is despised. Meanwhile the priest becomes so much a slave to lust that he seems to himself unable to live without a concubine. He can indeed amend his sin, but he refuses, because he feels himself delightfully entangled in the bonds of lust. He promises himself that in the future he will take up the resolve of repentance; he promises to amend his life. But while, amid so many crimes, they put off the will to repent, they rarely come to the remedies of salutary repentance, because they hope that their life will still be prolonged for a long time. Indeed some are so maddened by diabolical enticements that they are able to think of nothing but carnal things and care nothing for future retribution. They love a criminal concubine more than eternal happiness. What repentance, do you think, do these people perform at the end, when they are pressed not by will but by necessity? Those who have spent the whole time of their life in lust rarely obtain true repentance in the final moments of their life. For even if the late repentance of such persons, through God’s mercy, is sometimes true, nevertheless they will not be able to reach the kingdom of heaven without the most grievous torments of purgatory.
