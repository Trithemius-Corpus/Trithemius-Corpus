I know by experience that the study and reading of the Scriptures is also the most effective remedy against all temptations. For blessed Jerome says: “Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” For Scripture alone is what promises us a heavenly remedy in tribulation. What could we know or believe about our heavenly homeland if we did not have the Scriptures? For the Apostle says: “All Scripture is divinely inspired,” so that “through patience and the consolations of the Scriptures we may have hope.” Therefore I admonish you to take up the study of the holy Scriptures as a consolation in this pilgrimage, since if you are constant in reading them, you will easily despise the world with all its vanities.

For nothing so raises the mind from vain anxiety to heavenly things; nothing so allays the fires of the flesh as devotion to sacred reading. Finally, study is especially suited to your office, as Malachi says: “The lips of the priest shall keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law from his mouth, because he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts.” For, as we said a little earlier, the priest of Christ must be learned in the divine law, so that he may know what there is from which he may bring forth discourse. For he who has undertaken to govern souls, for which he will have to render an account to God the supreme judge, must necessarily read and understand many things. Indeed, as that great Gregory Nazianzen said, and after him the Roman Pope, the governance of souls is the art of arts. But he who has learned nothing in the holy Scriptures, what will he teach others? Or what benefit will a priest who does not know the Scriptures be able to bring to his hearers in preaching?

Woe now to our times, in which as the people are, so is the priest. Our priests cast aside the study of the Scriptures; they neglect learning. Instead of books of the Scriptures they keep birds and dogs. Let the bishops who promote such idiots and ignorant men to the dignity of the priesthood see to it, those who entrust the care of Christ’s sheep to unskilled shepherds. Withdraw from the company of those who cast aside knowledge, who scorn study. They savor nothing but the worldly; they love nothing but vanity; they understand nothing except what is carnal. They will quickly overturn you if you often pass into their assembly. Sitting in ambush with drinkers in taverns, they devote themselves to games and feasts. The fear of God is not before their eyes. They grow angry if anyone begins to speak about the Scriptures in their presence; they disdain to hear a reading, but turn instead to fables. What have you to do with them? Are they not priests, asses in their conduct? They understand absolutely nothing of the Scriptures; they despise learning. They do not know how to write or speak in the Roman tongue; scarcely have they learned to expound the Gospels in the vernacular. How many errors, fables, and heresies they announce to the people by preaching in the church, who could believe unless he had experienced it? Instead of books they acquire children for themselves; instead of study they love concubines. These are blind guides of the blind, who do not instruct the people of God in righteousness, but rather lead them astray. O how many poor Christians dwell in the countryside who, because of the ignorance of their priests, do not know what befits a Christian. Amid these things there is no one who grieves over the danger to souls, no one who applies a remedy. All those who are earthly savor their own things; all seek their own; all run after profits. But what are these things to us? Each one stands or falls to his own lord. And although it does not pertain to me to correct these things, nevertheless I cannot fail to grieve when I see every evil in the church proceeding from the ignorance of priests.
