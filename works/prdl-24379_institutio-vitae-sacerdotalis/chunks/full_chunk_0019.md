Nothing is better for a person than that, by long-continued reading, he should fix his mind there whence life comes to him. Therefore continual study must be devoted to reading the Scriptures, because reading nourishes the mind and supplies supports for the virtues.

Therefore, so that you do not lose the value of time, read the sacred Scriptures, write books, persist in devout prayers, and at the proper times labor also with your hands. Do not be ashamed to learn what you do not know, because then you will be able usefully to instruct others, if you always seek to be informed by teachers. Do not be ashamed to assume the name of disciple, if you desire to attain to the honors of the master’s office. For the Holy Spirit, who teaches man knowledge, is a lover of humility. When you sit at table to take food, remember that first the sacred writings are to be sounded forth, so that, while the flesh is fed, the mind may by no means be defrauded of its own nourishment. For in the Council of Toledo it is thus read: “Out of reverence for God and for priests, the whole synod established this: that, since idle tales are often customarily introduced at meals, the reading of the divine Scriptures should be mixed into every priestly banquet. For by this both souls are built up toward the good, and tales that are not of necessity are prohibited. For the word of God is food of the spirit, because man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.” The ancient fathers and priests devoted the greatest diligence to the holy Scriptures, so that they might healthfully feed the mind and provide useful things for the instruction of posterity. Let us cling to their footsteps, so that we may deserve to be found worthy ministers before God.

*On fleeing avarice and covetousness. Chapter 5.*

Although at the beginning of our discourse we disputed against avarice and covetousness, nevertheless we are led by a certain reason to speak again in this place about this very matter. For one never acts enough against this vice unless it is utterly rooted out. But our Savior, knowing how difficult it is for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God, said to those who followed him: “Unless someone renounces all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple.” Hence the psalmist admonishes us, saying: “If riches abound, do not set your heart upon them.” And the apostle says: “Having food and clothing, let us be content with these.” What more do we seek, who are ministers of God? Covetousness is disgraceful in a priest of God; avarice is disgraceful, and the continual hunger for gold is damnable. Therefore, most beloved brother, if you desire to live in the freedom of the spirit, do not seek perishable money; do not love riches. Cast your thought upon the Lord, and he himself will nourish you; he himself will feed you when you are hungry; he himself will guard you.
