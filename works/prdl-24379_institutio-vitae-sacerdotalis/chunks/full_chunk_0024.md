For it is better to send a penitent with compassion to purgatory than, by the austerity of the confessor, to consign one in despair to hell. Do not neglect to pray your canonical hours with reverence and devotion at the times appointed by the Church. Likewise, do not omit to read the hours of blessed Mary ever virgin, the vigils of the dead, together with the other things owed according to the priestly order.

Have care of your house in the fear of God. If you have a household, persuade them by word and example to preserve modesty and honorableness. For indeed it damages the reputation of a priest among the people when the populace speaks ill of his household. Let your speech be strengthened by the testimony of the Scriptures, because whatever it enjoins apart from the holy Scriptures is despised with the same ease with which it is approved. I do not want you to imitate those who preach in church without learning, who frequently cite fables and phantoms of dreams, and are ignorant of the mysteries of the divine Scriptures. Brawling and garrulous priests, speaking without reason, do not edify the people, but labor to draw them into admiration of themselves with empty speeches and Aesopian fables. For it is a foolish and damnable abuse, when the Scriptures and the Gospel have been omitted, to teach the Christian people the fables of Aesop and the fabricated deeds of the Romans.

Nor should one wonder that the unlearned populace hears these things more gladly than the Scriptures, since the Apostle foretold this when he says: “There will be a time when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires they will heap up teachers for themselves, itching in their ears, and they will indeed turn away their hearing from the truth and be turned to fables.” Hence Jerome says: “To roll out words and by rapidity of speaking to create admiration of oneself among the unlearned crowd belongs to ignorant men. The shameless brow often interprets what it does not know, and when it has persuaded others, it also usurps for itself the knowledge which it does not have. Nothing is so easy as to deceive a base little crowd and an unlearned assembly by volubility of tongue, which admires all the more whatever it does not understand.” But you, speak the things that befit sound doctrine, and do not seek the applause of the unlearned if you desire to find a place among the learned.

Do not imprudently desire the care of souls, unless you have first learned to govern yourself and others justly according to ecclesiastical sanctions. For the office of teaching the people subjects a man to many dangers and is troubled by daily anxieties. For the ruler of souls must be without crime, as a steward of God: not proud, not wrathful, not given to wine, not a striker, not greedy for shameful gain, but hospitable, kindly, sober, just, holy, continent, embracing that faithful speech which is according to doctrine, so that he may be able to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict it.
