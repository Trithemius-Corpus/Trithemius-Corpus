…of all evils is greed, which does not allow a man to live for God in freedom of spirit, but always twists the mind with bitter cares and does not permit him to think of anything except riches. Moderation, however, is the safest condition: it is not oppressed by necessity, nor lifted up by abundance into pride. We see among the priests of God worshippers of idols, who are always watchful in gathering money, who devote themselves to avarice, who think nothing, or very little, about eternal life. They live as though they had been born for gain; they cast piety toward the poor far away. They desire nothing else than to seek and find gold. You would see among them certain avaricious and wretched rich men, who have a very great supply and abundance of temporal things, but lack the ability to use what they have acquired. All the days in which they dwell on earth they live wretchedly. They do not presume to eat and drink according to the necessity of the body; they desire always to add to the heap of money; they keep watch over profits; they care utterly nothing for the salvation of souls. They grieve more over a lost penny than if ten souls entrusted to them were to perish. But it is no wonder if he neglects another’s soul, who damns his own. For whoever has once subjected his heart to avarice and greed will never, or rarely, be free. He always burns for greater profits of money; he always pants for an increase of gold; and the desire of having grows so much as the money happens to have increased. Thereafter one benefice, however rich, is no longer enough. To obtain others also the avaricious priest runs about; right and wrong he labors to obtain what he wickedly desires. Meanwhile he runs to the apostolic see; he relates the causes of his petition, which, if they are examined with the truth, are at once found false. Then one man obtains many prebends; he hires out the care of the sheep to hirelings; he receives wool and milk from the sheep, but he is content not to feed the flock. That prophet will inquire against the shepherds who feed themselves. They think themselves free from fault who obtain many benefices by dispensation of the supreme pontiff, but they do not consider what is required for that obtaining. But you, stand firm and do not transgress the ancient boundaries which your fathers set; instead, set a limit to desire. I want you to be content with one benefice, which is sufficient for you according to the moderation of your state, lest, if you take on more without just necessity, you be charged more gravely for it before God. Do not take as your example the multitude of those who act otherwise; do not walk on the broad way of sinners, if you wish to arrive at life. Let him who serves the altar live from the altar; but he who neglects to feed the sheep, by what merit does he require wool and milk from them?
