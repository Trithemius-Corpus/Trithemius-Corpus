And the Lord says: “Wash yourselves; be clean; remove the evil of your thoughts from before my eyes; cease to act perversely; learn to do good,” says the almighty Lord.

As for what pertains to the outer man, the Apostle, instructing a bishop, says: “A bishop must be blameless, sober, chaste, kind, adorned in conduct, hospitable, teachable.” Whatever the Apostle prescribes to a bishop in this place is also commanded to the other priests and presbyters. And first, when he says that he must be blameless, he declares him free from all vices and from the slanders of the people. For the life of a priest is set before the people as an example, and is compared to a lamp, so that just as light shines in darkness, so the conduct of a presbyter may shine among the crowds. For the Lord says in the Gospel: “Let your light so shine before men that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven.” Then indeed a priest walks worthily before God, when [unclear].

For he is the servant of the Lord, and this above all is required of him: that he carry out the will of God in words and deeds. But no one fulfills the will of God except he who, after expelling vices, makes his heart a dwelling-place of virtues. Take care that you do not stain the spouse of Christ, the Church, by the baseness of your life. Take care that you do not scandalize anyone by bad example, because for as many as you have led into sin by your fault, for so many you will be tormented in punishment. Let yourself be seen rarely in public; accustom yourself to remain at home and to devote yourself to readings. Be quiet in mind and body. Do not run about through the streets and the houses of neighbors. Do not take part in the tales of rustics. For too much presence diminishes reputation, and excessive familiarity of a priest among the people breeds contempt. But going out rarely in public will show for you among the people a great reverence of maturity. Live in such a way that many may be edified by your example. Among elders speak little, because talkativeness is often reproved where silence is praised. Indeed, among old men it befits a young man to listen, not to speak.

Do not desire, in the college of priests, to appear wise, lest you be confounded before them as foolish. Do not be wordy and arrogant. When questioned, answer with humility and goodwill, and do not, in the manner of wranglers, be loud in voice while you speak. Do not delight in immoderate laughter, and keep yourself always and everywhere from every unseemly levity. Avoid useless conversations and empty words, knowing that for every idle word you have spoken you will have to render an account to God. Let your speech be moderate and sparing; let every lie be far from it, and let there be no oath made in vain. Do not enter taverns with rustics, because that is not permitted to your honor.
