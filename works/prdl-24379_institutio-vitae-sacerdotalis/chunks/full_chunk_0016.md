Slippery is the serpent of lust, and unless it is crushed in the head, it is not easily destroyed. While the adversary is still weak, he is easily overcome. Nor, if you have sometimes conquered, should you think yourself secure, because victory is not complete where a daily battle is being waged. Therefore, so long as you can be subjected, do not believe that you have conquered. Do not trust in your own strength; do not claim the victory for yourself, but know that your hope rests in the help of God alone. For no one is chaste by his own powers. For with the grace of God helping, a good will conquers the flesh. But against the temptations of the flesh one must pray to the Lord without ceasing and say: “Pierce my flesh with your fear, for I have feared your judgments.” Do not love the delights of this world, which will quickly make you pass away and easily set you aflame with the heat of lust. In your food let excess be avoided, and let not variety of dishes stir appetite, but hunger. Let not incitements to lust be set before you, but things that nourish the body and do not inflame it. Wine, in which there is wantonness, should always be suspect to you, lest you be inflamed by a double heat, namely that of drink and of youth. He who uses wine constantly nourishes the fire of lust in his bosom. The uncovering of Noah’s shameful parts and the incest of Lot warn you always to fear wine. For drunkenness is the burial of a living man: it destroys the senses, extinguishes reason, binds the mind, wounds the conscience, diminishes the body’s health, and kills the soul. Drunkenness is a spring of evils: it rouses the flesh to lust, stains the heart with the worst thoughts, and loosens the tongue into the vilest words. This is the saying of most holy Jerome: “A full belly easily foams into lust.”

Therefore let a chaste man’s refreshment be moderate; let his drink be sparing and light; let his speech be modest. Let him avoid familiarity with women; let him guide his senses with every guard, lest he fall into the pit of lust. Whenever a foul thought begins to beat upon the mind, let the memory of the Lord’s passion enter; let him place his thought in the wounds of Jesus Christ. For it is written: “Blessed is he who shall take and dash his little ones against the rock.” And the apostle says: “The rock was Christ.” For the recollection of the Lord’s passion is the most effective remedy of all temptations. Indeed, he whom Christ’s passion does not soften, no exercise will cure. Therefore, as soon as the lust of the flesh begins to attack the spirit, let the cultivator of chastity lift his hands to heaven and say: “Lord Jesus Christ, lover of chaste counsel, you know that my powers for resistance are slight. Help my weakness through your bitter passion.” I believe in the Lord that, if he persists in prayer with devotion, he will easily obtain victory against the flesh. But I wish you, most beloved brother, to avoid not only foul deeds but also foul words.
