But he who flees the burden of solicitude does not without fault receive the profit of the milk. If, therefore, you refuse to feed Christ's sheep, by no means seek wool and milk from them. For the sheep were not appointed for the sake of the shepherd, but the shepherd for the sake of the sheep. Nor is the care of the sheep to be sought so that they may have food; rather, food is to be sought so that due care may be applied to all. But by many now the order is perverted: they seek prelacies and benefices for this reason, that they may have money in the chest, not that they may feed souls for the love of God. O shepherd and idol, how miserably you deceive your very self. You take care only that you may eat, whereas you ought to have eaten so that you might be able to care for souls.

Covetousness is the root of evils, and of all evils: the mother of transgression, the teacher of harming, the charioteer of wickedness, the inventor of deceit, the origin of sedition, the pit of scandal, the promoter of falsehood. It commits sacrileges and thefts, carries out plunders and arsons, wages wars and murders. Finally, covetousness alone is what taught men to buy and sell simoniacally; it seeks and receives wickedly, trades unjustly, relies on deceits, and nourishes falsehood and perjury. It is this that breaks faith, dissolves a pact, corrupts testimony, and violates an oath. Covetousness also extinguishes charity between God and man, interrupts brotherly love, disturbs concord, makes counsel foolish, perverts the judgment of equity, destroys unity. If, therefore, you desire to show yourself a minister worthy of God, pull this root of all evils out of your heart as quickly as possible. Brief is every possession of earthly wealth; brief is the action of mortal life; daily pressing is the procession of the human race toward death. Why, therefore, do you delay, for the love of God, to despise the things that, overtaken by death, you will shortly be forced to leave behind, even unwillingly? For we do not have here an abiding city, but we seek the one to come. Where your treasure is, there also will your heart be. Do not seek the perishable riches of the world; do not bind your heart with the chains of avarice. For it befits a priest of Christ to be a meek and merciful disciple, who does not desire the goods of others, but generously distributes his own among the poor.

*On the honorable outward conduct of priests. Chapter VI.*

Consider now what sort of conduct you ought to establish for yourself, and how you ought to live, so that you may be able to please almighty God, whose minister you are, with your whole heart. But as far as inward conduct is concerned, you must shine, clean and pure in conscience, who minister before the Lord.
