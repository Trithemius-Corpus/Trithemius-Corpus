For who would not grieve that those men are ignorant of the commandments who have been appointed to instruct others? Behold, in the ordaining of priests no learning is sought; those not called are permitted to approach; the life of the man to be ordained is never examined, as above all it ought to have been.

For this reason the Christian people is led away captive by the cords of sins; the ardor of charity is extinguished; the concord of brotherly love is violated. Our priests are given over to worldly desires; they do not presume to correct the vicious, because they recognize that they themselves have sinned in greater matters. For there are few who duly instruct the flock by word and example. Nor is it any wonder if lesser men are free from the study of the Scriptures, when among the prelates you find few who cleave to the examples of the ancients. For they are chosen, for the most part, not as the more learned, but as [unclear] readier for gathering riches. They have either no codices of the Scriptures or few, since they pursue learning with hatred. Nothing so diminishes ecclesiastical dignity and renders it contemptible as the ignorance of prelates. When formerly bishops, abbots, and the other prelates and priests directed their mind to the Scriptures, so easy an entrance did not lie open to crimes and sins. For love and knowledge of the Scriptures teach one to despise the world, to flee vices, and to love the most holy virtues. So long as the Israelite people remained in the law of the Lord, no power of the enemy prevailed against them. But after it cast aside knowledge of the law, it was subjected to many adversities. For Scripture says: Because they had no wisdom, they perished through their own foolishness.

Today also the priests and rectors of the churches are in great number, but few of them give attention to knowledge of the law and of the Scriptures. And because, as the Lord speaks through the prophet, the priests have cast away knowledge of the law, therefore they have been made contemptible among the people. He indeed cries to the Lord in tribulation, but he does not hear them, because he who turns away his ears so as not to hear the law, his prayer will be abominable. Therefore let priests not wonder that laymen despise them, since they themselves spurn the commandments of Christ. But I greatly fear that more grievous things will soon come against the clergy. But enough of these matters. Consider, truly, not what others do, but what is to be done by you. For many have been called to the priesthood in the church of God, but few are chosen. We must therefore take the greatest care that we who are born into the lot of the Lord may also be chosen. What does it profit to discharge the priesthood here for a very brief time, if it should happen that one is separated from the sight of God? Therefore nothing is better than to fix desire on heavenly things, to devote oneself to the study of sacred reading, to despise present things for the love of God, and to think on things to come.
