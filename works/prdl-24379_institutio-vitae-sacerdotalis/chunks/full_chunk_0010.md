You would also see the unhappy father, rising from bed, receive his child on his knees, and, after kisses have been given, place him on his right knee and the breviary on his left. While praying, he smiles at the smiling little child; when he cries, he coaxes him; when he is silent, he addresses him with frequent words and gestures. Meanwhile the fond mother comes up. From the father’s hand she snatches the sweet pledge, the book; she handles the leaves, tears them, and, with saliva flowing from his mouth, by the parent’s permission soils them. Amid all this there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention is given to the words, but all his concern is spent in consoling the crying child. The words fall confused from the mouth of the one praying, and are interrupted by the frequent kisses of the little child, so that when the prayer is completed he scarcely remembers that he has prayed.

Meanwhile the sacristan pulls the bell; the altar is prepared for sacrificing. He is awaited by the people in the church [unclear]. The priest runs up, his hands washed in place of the boy [unclear]; he puts on the sacred vestments with all the haste he can, and distractedly completes the sacrifice of God. O conscience, where meanwhile do you remain? Where do you lie despised? O wretched man, you who handle the Son of God with polluted hands, how long do you wound, tear, and destroy your soul? With what rashness, finally, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul? Unless the great mercy of almighty God spared you for a time, the angels, provoked by indignation, would strike you heavily at the altar. Where is the vow of perpetual chastity that you promised into the hands of the bishop when he ordained you? Where is the reverence due to the office of priesthood you assumed? You have sinned; why do you not do penance? How long do you despise the long-suffering of God? Or do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the just judgment of Jesus Christ. I suppose indeed that you are thinking of repenting in the future, but you give no thought at all to how near death, the cutter-off of repentance, may be. For truly the merciful God who promised that he would pardon the penitent did not promise tomorrow to the one who delays. Moreover, the cleric whom a cohabiting woman has once led away captive, she never, or rarely, releases free unless death intervenes. I pass over the fact that many, through the deceits and evil arts of their concubines, are often driven to such madness that they can by no means extricate themselves from the most shameful love of those women. The hearing shudders at the crimes of prostitutes, nor does the integrity of chastity allow one to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by priests’ concubines.
