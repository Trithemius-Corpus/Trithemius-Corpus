Therefore the man who desires to extinguish the vice of lust must continually exercise himself in some holy work. For idleness is the enemy of the soul. Hence blessed Jerome admonishes a certain man, saying: Always do something good, so that the devil may find you occupied. For occupation in some holy work is the best remedy against evil thoughts. Indeed, when the mind is pleasantly intent upon some good exercise, it does not think of other things. Access will not lie open to the devil if the mind has been occupied in some exercise. Take an example in holy David, who, when occupied with affairs of war, subdued lust; but when he remained idle at home, committed adultery. When the hand is free from work, the mind grows weak in thought. If therefore you desire to conquer lust, flee idleness. But in what exercises you ought to employ your time, receive briefly, if you please. In the morning accustom yourself to rise from sleep before daylight, and to give some time to prayers, both canonical and votive. And when by praying devoutly you have completed the appointed measure of your service, thereafter you will give yourself to sacred readings, until you understand that by your office you are called to celebrate Mass. After Mass one must again give time to prayers and sacred meditations, and [unclear] devoutly; the business of the household must be attended to, and whatever things must be arranged out of necessity should be ordered with the fear of God. But the manual work of a priest is to write, to bind books copied by himself or by others, or printed books, and to apply himself to other honorable labors according to his condition or necessity. Finally, after food has been taken, praises must be offered to God, who feeds us here on earth; and again one must pray that after this life he may feed us without end in heaven. When these things have duly been completed, one must not at once go off to chatter, but must again give oneself to sacred letters. Finally, one must work with the hands until evening, which should not be extended beyond the fourth hour in manual work. But when, the day now declining, it is time to sleep, one should first have recourse to most devout prayers. When these have been completed, you should make an account of your actions during the day, and should declare yourself guilty before God for your negligent way of life, and ask for pardon and forgiveness. For if you have judged yourself, you will be absolved by God. And unless you have held yourself constantly in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overturned. But if you have been caught in some offense, immediately have recourse to the lamentation of penitence, and such a one should be approved as suitable by knowledge of the Scriptures and recommended by his way of life. For rarely does one who lives with a blameworthy way of life truly open the way of salvation to those who confess.
