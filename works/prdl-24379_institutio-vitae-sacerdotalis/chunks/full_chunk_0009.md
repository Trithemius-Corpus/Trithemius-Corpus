But hurry, and do not put yourself off until the coming hours. He who is not ready today will be less fit tomorrow. “Do not delay to be converted to the Lord,” says Scripture, “and do not put it off from day to day,” since the longer you postpone repentance, the less fit you will be for repenting. Now ascend the watchtower of your mind, and see the madness of foolish priests, who are led captive by worthless concubines, so that they do not do whatever they themselves wish, but what is commanded them by harlots. Meanwhile you would see a [stolid?] cleric sitting beside his concubine, gazing at her with open mouth; and if she smiles at him, he laughs; if she is angry, he flatters her until he is restored to favor. The wretched priest is led captive by a woman, and he who ought to have ruled others is overcome by the dominion of his maidservant. Oh, who would believe what groans the wretch utters amid these things, whenever he thinks of casting off this very harsh yoke of the arch-devil, what griefs he bears in his heart. Indeed, sometimes he is touched by such displeasure against his beast that he plans to drive her away from himself on the next day. Meanwhile he gives and receives insulting words, and at last after words they come to blows: he strikes and is struck back; he curses and is blasphemed. O astonishing spectacle: the head of a priest, which it is not permitted a prince to touch, the hands of a prostitute tear, strike, and bruise. At last, after curses and blows, the woman turns herself to weeping; with tears poured out by the devil [unclear], until at last she recalls the unhappy captive to pity and makes him favorable to herself. You would then see the cleric suddenly changed, and so moved by tears [unclear] that he who had suffered the injury first begs pardon. Then the table is set; the banquet is restored; sighs are mingled with words; the dealings of new marriages are negotiated. Amid these things the [stolid?] priest sits, discoursing on patience and meekness; he sighs that he has offended against his mistress; he labors with all his strength to recover her favor. What is done after friendship has been renewed, it will be more permissible for you to think than for me to say. There is no fear of God before the eyes of a priest bound by the chains of a concubine; no dread of the punishments of hell; no thought of priestly dignity; no memory of public honor.
