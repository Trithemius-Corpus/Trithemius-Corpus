*Magic.*

*Treatise II.*

*The Images, or Seals, of Chael.*

Chael, one of the sons of Israel, a most ancient teacher, when he was in the desert, observed, and had many figures carved according to the course of the signs of the planets. He recognized a very great effect in them, and, so that these powers of images might be known to posterity, he composed a book, from which the following things have been excerpted. And blessed be God, who gave such powers to the earth for the salvation of the human race. (These words are found at the very beginning in the book itself written by Chael.)

The figure of a man sitting behind a plough, with a long beard and a long face, with curved eyebrows, around whose neck there are four men lying down, who holds in his hands a fox [and] a vulture: if such a stone, on which these things have been carved, is hung from the neck, it is effective for all plantings and for finding treasures; for if, when going to bed, you place it beneath your neck, in sleep you will see treasures and the manner of extracting them. It has another power, namely to be beneficial in the illnesses of brute animals, when they drink water in which this stone has been washed. It must be carved on the day and hour of Venus.

The image of a man having a shield on his neck and a helmet on his head, holding a sword in his hand and trampling a serpent under his feet, carved on red jasper: if it is hung from the neck, he will overcome all in battle, especially if the battle is joined on the day of Mars.

The image of a horse having a crocodile upon it, carved on a hyacinth: it has the power to render the wearer victorious in civil cases; it makes him loved, compliant, and lovable, and it must be set in gold, because its power is increased by gold.

The figure of a seated man, and of a woman standing before him, her hair spread toward her loins, and of a man looking upward, carved on carnelian: its power will be to acquire goodwill from all who are touched by this stone. A little amber and turpentine must be placed beneath the stone.

The figure of a foaming and fiery horse, upon which a black man sits with a sceptre in his hand, carved on hematite: it grants the power of ruling and restores lost favor; it must be set in equal weight of gold and silver.

The image of a seated man with a lit candle in his hand, if it has been carved on chrysolite, makes the wearer rich; it must be set in the purest gold.

The carved figure of a stag, or of a hunter, or of a hare, has the power of restraining demons, lunatics, and the frenzied.

Know that the carved image of a woman having in one hand a bird and in the other a fish has power in catching fish and birds; it must be set in silver.

An image whose front part is a horse, but whose rear part is a goat, if it is carved on some stone, gives good fortune in feeding and arming animals or beasts of every kind, and it must be set in lead.

The figure of a woman sitting with a trumpet on a horse, or of a soldier running with a horn at his neck and with a tree before him, carved on some stone, grants hunters favor in hunting, if they carry it with them.

The figure of a man with bent knees looking upward and holding a cloth, if it is carved on a stone and someone carries it with him, gives favor and riches in buying and selling things.

The figure of a scorpion and a Sagittarius fighting with one another, if it has been carved on some stone, will make the malicious and discordant concordant, and enemies friends. It should be set in silver.
