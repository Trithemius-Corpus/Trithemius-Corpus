And this is the method of sharpening the intellectual power and memory from the powers of the heavens, and also from plants and animals. I hope that I shall win no small favor among those who have a fragile and fleeting memory, although they are stirred to understanding, if I bring forth certain remedies for strengthening the memory, remedies which I know very distinguished men have used successfully and which until now have lain hidden among concealed secrets, not begrudging things which can be of public use. But you should know that there are many things in them which adhere neither to temperament, nor to matter, nor to any manifest quality, but must be ascribed solely to a certain occult property.

Take of the hearts of swallows, still beating, no. 7; of turtle-doves, no. ij; of dried mint, pennyroyal, vervain, eyebright, prepared coriander, [galtrichum?], each one ounce; of rosemary flowers, half an ounce; of aromatic calamus and cinnamon, each [unclear]; of cloves, one and a half ounces; of musk and musked [galia?], each six grains; of anacardium honey and storax calamita, each half an ounce. Let those of these which admit of grinding be reduced to a fine powder, and with fennel water let them be made into pastilles in the shape of a date-kernel.

The person who is to use this will observe this order. After the body has been purged, for [unclear] nights he will eat nothing for supper except raisins with a few almonds and a little bread; his dinner, however, will be a roasted hen, or a young chicken, a turtle-dove, a partridge, or something similar; his drink will be wine of one year, moderately diluted. But every night near the hour of sleep, a pastille will be inserted into the right nostril and stopped with cotton, and thus you will keep it until you have recited no more than twenty verses of Virgil. After some days have passed, put the pastille, moistened with rose-water, into the left nostril, and you will retain everything that you are going to read, or you will quickly be affected with understanding.

But he who altogether desires the memory thus to be fervent must abstain from drunkenness, sex, and especially rawness of the stomach, from all satiety, green fruits, vegetables, salted meats, and everything that generates wind. At the last course prepared coriander seed is [unclear]. Above all, too, it will be harmless if it is diligently and properly prepared. Nor will it be harmless [unclear] to hold the pastille in the mouth at intervals and swallow it. It will also help to throw the pastille upon coals, so that the smoke may be properly received into the nostrils and, once received, retained, the nostrils being stopped with cotton lest it exhale; or also to dissolve some pastilles in lavender water, or rose-water, or anise water, and frequently morning and evening to moisten the forehead, temples, nostrils, and occiput around the capsule of memory, and to put into the nostrils pure little linens soaked in it. And yet this remedy has been disclosed to few.

But to return to celestial matters: in precisely the same way and form as those of Mercury, the powers and influences of the remaining planets can be procured; as, for honors and dignities, and for obtaining the favor and goodwill of princes and great men, an image of the Sun when it is in Leo, in the tenth house, in trine with Jupiter or the Moon. Yet in this year 1608 the solar influence is impeded because of the opposition of Mars from Aquarius, as the remarkable and unusual changes of the very air plainly demonstrate. Hence in forming these figures one must look solely to this: that the planet whose power and influence someone desires to procure for himself be as free as possible from every impediment.

But why and whence rings, images, and figures engraved in a certain metal or stone possess the powers attributed to them must be assigned not to temperament, not to manifest qualities, not to the whole substance, but to divine magic, that is, to a certain higher and more sublime cause, which some have not inappropriately been accustomed to call a spiritual vehicle and bond. By its mediation, body and soul, substances mutually distant, are joined to one another; by it the magnet draws iron, and chicory follows the motion of the Sun.

With this vehicle and bond coming between or intervening, Mercury is efficacious in quicksilver, in sharpening or weakening the memory and the cogitative faculty, in the alectorius stone, in mixed color and taste, in Egypt, Greece, Flanders, and Silesia.
