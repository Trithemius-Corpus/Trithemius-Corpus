*Magic.*

[unclear] the head of a dragon, if it has been carved in crystal or in some precious stone, and is set in orichalcum; beneath the stone let there be musk and amber. If anyone carries such a stone with him, he will have obedient spirits and will accumulate great wealth.

If the image of a man sitting upon an eagle and holding a rod in his hand has been carved in crystal and set in a bronze or copper ring: if anyone looks upon this ring on Sunday before sunrise, he will conquer and overcome all his enemies; if he looks upon it on Thursday, he will be victor in war, and all people will willingly obey him. But the wearer must be clothed in white garments and abstain from dove flesh.

If the figure of a man riding and holding a bridle in one hand and a drawn bow in the other, having a sword girt on, has been carved in pyrite and set in a gold ring, whoever carries it with him will be made victorious in battles; nor will anyone be able to resist him.

If the image of a woman having her hair spread over her breasts, and in her sight a man as if approaching and making some sign of love to her, has been carved in jacinth or crystal and set in gold; beneath the stone let there be amber, aloe, and artemisia. All will obey the one who carries this stone in a ring; and if you touch a woman with it, she will love you; and if you place it under your head when you go to sleep, you will see whatever you wish.

*Seals.*

If the image of a man sitting upon a fish, and above the man’s head there is a peacock, has been carved in a red stone, and you place it beneath the table at a banquet, no one eating with the right hand will be satisfied.

The figure of a naked man standing, and at his right a girl standing whose hair is bound around her head; and let the man hold his right hand upon the girl’s neck and his left upon her breast, and let him look at her face, while she looks at the ground. When it has been carved in some stone and set in an iron ring, and beneath the stone there is a little of the tongue of a sparrow, hoopoe, myrrh, alum, and human blood, whoever carries this ring will be unconquered, nor will anyone be able to resist him, nor will any wild beast harm him. It is also effective for epileptics, if you mark red [unclear] with it; and if it is placed at a dog’s neck, it does not allow it to bark.

If the image of a man holding flowers in his hand has been carved in carnelian and placed in a tin ring, and the ring has been made on Monday or Friday, at the fourth, eighth, or twelfth hour, touch whomever you wish with this ring, and he will immediately obey you.

*Magic.*

Know that the figure of a bearded man having a long face and curved eyebrows, sitting upon a plough between two bulls and holding a vulture in his hand, carved in a stone, has power in the planting of trees and the finding of treasures, and makes one victorious in battle. Serpents are driven from the face of the wearer; it heals epileptics and removes every affliction of evil spirits. It must, moreover, be set in an iron ring and carried.

If the figure of a man holding a sickle in his hand above his head, with a crocodile beneath his feet, has been carved in a stone and set in a leaden ring; beneath the stone let there be a little root of squill. Whoever carries this ring with him will be safe from enemies, and on a journey from robbers.

If the figure of a man holding a sword in his hand and sitting upon a dragon has been carved in amethyst and set in a leaden or iron ring, and is worn on the finger, spirits will obey him and will reveal to him treasures of every kind.

If the figure of a standing eagle has been carved in aëtites stone and set in a leaden ring, it has the power that whoever carries it with him will catch many fish; nor will any wild beast harm him, and he will be loved by people.
