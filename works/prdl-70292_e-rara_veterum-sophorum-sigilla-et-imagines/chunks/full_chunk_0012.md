The figure of Saturn engraved on stones is of this sort: an old man holding a sickle in his hand, having a beard not very hairy. This figure, carved on a stone of the nature of Saturn, makes the bearer powerful, with continual increase of power.

The image of Jupiter is of a man seated on a throne or on a four-footed chair, holding a rod in one hand and a globe in the other; or holding an image of Cancer or a fish, and having an eagle before his feet. But among the teachers of magic he is represented differently: for they carve the figure of a man with a ram’s head, wrinkled heels, and a slender chest. If this is carved on a stone, especially [Krabatus?], it makes the bearer fortunate, lovable, pleasing to religious men, and raises him to honors and dignities.

The image of Mars is represented on stone in many ways: sometimes with a banner in his hand, sometimes with a lance or another instrument of war, always armed, and sometimes on horseback. The virtue of this stone so carved makes the bearer victorious, bold, and warlike, especially if it is carved on a stone of similar virtue.

The figure of the Sun is found in various ways: sometimes like the Sun, with rays all around; sometimes

like a man seated on a throne, with loose hair and flowing garment, and also in a chariot, as if he were being carried by a four-horse team; so that around the chariot are the twelve signs of the Zodiac. The virtue of this figure carved on some stone makes the bearer commanding and pleasing to great men.

The images of Venus likewise are many; yet the following is more often carved on stones: a woman with a large garment, a robe, and holding a laurel in her hand. Its virtue is to give agility in affairs to be done, to bring all things to the desired end, to remove fear of drowning in water, and to bring honors from women.

The figure of Mercury is a man with a slender chest, having a handsome beard and talaria, holding the caduceus-rod, with a cock before his feet, or with a serpent beneath his feet. Its virtue is to assist in acquiring oratorical knowledge, and it also gives very great help in enriching merchants.

The images of the Moon are various: for sometimes she is represented in the proper figure of the Moon, with horns; sometimes in a chariot, as a girl with dogs and a quiver, with dogs following a stag. The virtue of this image is to provide expedition in legions, from these good things, etc.,

goods and honors, and in all affairs to be done to provide speed and ease, and to bring them to the desired end. But it must be carefully observed, as was also warned above, that the planet whose image is carved on a suitable ring ought to be very well disposed, that is, not combust, retrograde, or oppressed or touched by the rays of malefics, but in a good place of the sky: in the Ascendant or Midheaven, with Jupiter or Venus, and in its essential dignities. Otherwise the image will be vain and of no importance.

Moreover, the ancients formed the image of the Bear thus.

They carve two Bears wound around by a serpent: the Greater Bear is contained at the head of the coil, the Lesser at the tail. This image, carved on some stone, makes the bearer knowledgeable, crafty, and powerful.

The image of the Crown has the likeness of a royal crown with many stars. Sometimes it is represented in the likeness of a king with a crowned head; it exists in the northern part and in the sign of Sagittarius, and is of the nature of Venus and Mercury. When this image has been carved on a stone, it gives bearers honors, knowledge, and royal favor.
