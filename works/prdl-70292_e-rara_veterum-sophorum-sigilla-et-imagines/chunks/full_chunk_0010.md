The figure of a man wishing to play on instruments, carved in sapphire, makes one most pleasing to all.

The image of a cock, or of three maidens, when found in agate, makes a man favored before God and men.

The figure of a lion carved in garnet provides riches and honors, gives joy, and drives out sadness.

The image of a stag or a dove carved in onyx provides courage against demons to the wearer.

The figure of a man in the likeness of a merchant carrying wares for sale, or of a man sitting beneath a centurion, when found in emerald, gives riches, makes one victorious, and relieves one from hardships.

The image of a bull or calf found in magnet-stone brings good fortune to the bearer through all places, and is effective against all enchantments.

The figure of a horse or wolf found in jasper drives away fevers and stops blood.

The figure of a crowned man in topaz makes the wearer good, obedient, and beloved by God and men, and provides honors and dignities.

*Magic*

The image of an armed man with a sword in his hand, when found carved in sardius or amethyst, causes the bearer to acquire a perfect, good memory, and makes him prudent.

The figure of a stag or he-goat found carved in chalcedony gives the power of increasing riches, when it is kept in a money-box.

The image of a hare in jasper does not allow the bearer to be harmed by any demon.

A sea-locust carved in beryl reconciles quarrels and joins marriage in love.

The figure of a night-raven, because it is the bird of Pallas, carved in a stone that serves for knowledge, makes a man most learned and eloquent.

The image of a peacock carved in a suitable stone makes the wearer rich.

The figure of a man killing a lion, or another beast, with a sword makes the bearer clever, victorious, and lovable.

The image of a man holding a laurel or palm in his right hand makes the bearer victorious over caves [unclear] and prizes, lovable, and powerful in his region.

*Seals*

The figure of a winged man having a serpent beneath his feet and holding its head in his hands makes the wearer rich, prudent, and lovable to all.

The carved image, in some stone, of an ant having an ear of grain or a grain is effective for acquiring riches.

The figure of a cock holding a crown or belt in its beak will give victory in a duel, especially if it is in [gallinaceus?].

When you find, carved in some gem, the figure of a standing man whose head is that of an ox but whose feet are those of an eagle, and impress it into wax, no man will speak ill of you.

When you find a basilisk and a dragon carved in carnelian, fighting with each other, and the head of an ox is there, apply it to your neck when you are about to fight, and you will conquer all beasts, both marine and terrestrial.

The figure of a naked, swollen, and crowned man, holding in one hand a cup and in the other a branch of an herb, carved in jet and fastened in a ring made of any metal: when the wearer has carried it, it immediately frees him from fever.

The figure of a man sitting upon a fish, carved in a red stone and placed on a cloth [unclear], if the diner eats with his right hand, will make him not be sated.

The figure of a woman having disheveled hair or naked breasts, and in her sight there is a man looking upon her face, when you find it carved in hyacinth, garnet, or crystal, and bind it in a gold ring of the same weight as the stone, and beneath the stone there is amber, aloes, and polypody, will make the wearer lovable to all; and when it is placed beneath his head, in sleep he will see whatever he wishes.

The figure of a man riding and holding in one hand a bridle, and in the other a bow with a dark-blue sword, when you find it carved in pyrite and it is bound in a gold ring, makes the wearer victorious in battle, whom no one will be able to overcome; and if anyone submerges this ring in musk oil, all will [unclear] him, if he has anointed his face with that oil, nor will they be able to oppose him.

The figure of a standing man who is naked, and at his right there is a naked maiden standing, and her hair is unbound around her head: when you find it carved in magnet-stone, and the man holds his right hand over the maiden’s neck, but his left over her breast, and the man looks at the maiden’s face, while she looks at the ground, place it in an iron ring
