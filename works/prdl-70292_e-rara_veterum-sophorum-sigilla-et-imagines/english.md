*The seals and magical images of the ancient sages, or the carvings of stones and gems according to the name of God, the Tetragrammaton, with the signature of the planets and according to certain regions of the heavens and constellations, for producing astonishing and marvelous effects.*

There is added the true method of making them, and the astonishing erection of the Signature of Mercury for sharpening memory and intellect.

By the authors:
Zoroaster, the Chaldaean;
Solomon, King of the Jews;
Raphael, the Jew;
Chael, the Egyptian;
Hermes, the Persian;
Thetel.

Drawn from a manuscript of Johannes Trithemius.

Published in the year of restored salvation 1612.

*The Magical Seals and Images of the Ancient Sages, established according to the names of God and the constellations of the stars, with the signature of the planets.*

*Treatise I.*

*The Seals of Raphael.*

The image of a beautiful dragon, if it has been carved in ruby, or in another stone of similar nature, know that its power is to increase the goods of this world, and to make the wearer cheerful and healthy.

The image of a falcon, if it is made in topaz, serves to acquire the goodwill of kings, princes, and magnates.

The image of an astrolabe, if it has been carved in garnet, its power will be to restore the wearer to health and to free him from all illnesses and adverse accidents on a journey.

The image of an ass, if it is made in chrysolite, its power will be to prognosticate and predict future things, that is, insofar as it can pass into human weakness; for truly to predict future things belongs to God alone.

The image of a ram, or of the head of a man with a beard, carved in sapphire, has the power of healing and freeing from many infirmities, from prison, and from every trouble; and it is a royal image, for it gives dignities and honors, and raises one on high.

The figure of a frog, if it has been carved in beryl, has the power of reconciling enemies, and friendship among men, if you touch those at discord with it.

The image of a camel’s head and of two goats among [a myrtle tree?], if it is made in onyx, has the power of summoning, gathering, and binding demons, if someone carries it with him; and in sleep it makes one see terrible and wondrous things.

The image of a vulture, if it is made in chrysolite, its power will be to bridle and gather demons, to defend the place in which it is put from evil spirits and from their infestation, and it makes demons obedient to the wearer.

The image of a bat, if it has been carved in heliotrope, gives the wearer power to resist demons.

The image of a well-adorned man having something beautiful in his hands, if it has been carved in cornelian, has the power of stopping blood; it contributes to honors.

The image of a lion or of Sagittarius, if it is made in stone, is effective against poisons; it frees from fever.

The image of an armed man with a bow and arrow, if it has been carved in iris, has the power to protect the wearer and the places in which he stays from evil.

The image of a man with a sword in his hand, if it is made in cornelian, has the power of preserving the place in which it shall be from lightning and tempest; it guards the wearer from vices and from fascination.

The image of a bull, if it is made in prase, is said to help against sorceries; in masteries it provides favor.

The figure of a hoopoe with the herb dragonwort before it, if it is made in beryl, will have the power to summon aquatic spirits, to restrain speech, and to receive answers from them concerning the things asked.

The image of a man with his right hand raised toward heaven, if it is made in chalcedony, its power will be to win civil lawsuits, to render the wearer healthy, and to preserve him on journeys and from harmful causes.

If the names of God have been carved in ceraunia, it will have the power of preserving the places in which it shall be from tempests, and it gives power and victory to wearers against their enemies.

The image of a bear, when it is made in amethyst, has the power of driving demons away, and of defending and preserving a man from drunkenness.

The image of an armed man carved in magnet makes wearers victorious in war.

*The Images, or Seals, of Chael.*

Chael, one of the sons of Israel, a most ancient teacher, when he was in the desert, observed, and had many figures carved according to the course of the signs of the planets. He recognized a very great effect in them, and, so that these powers of images might be known to posterity, he composed a book, from which the following things have been excerpted. And blessed be God, who gave such powers to the earth for the salvation of the human race. (These words are found at the very beginning in the book itself written by Chael.)

The figure of a man sitting behind a plough, with a long beard and a long face, with curved eyebrows, around whose neck there are four men lying down, who holds in his hands a fox [and] a vulture: if such a stone, on which these things have been carved, is hung from the neck, it is effective for all plantings and for finding treasures; for if, when going to bed, you place it beneath your neck, in sleep you will see treasures and the manner of extracting them. It has another power, namely to be beneficial in the illnesses of brute animals, when they drink water in which this stone has been washed. It must be carved on the day and hour of Venus.

*Treatise IV*

*The Images, or Seals, of Thetel*

Thetel, a very ancient doctor, treating of carvings on stones, says that these carvings, when found on suitable stones, possess very great power.

The image of a man carved on jasper, holding a shield in his left hand and in his right an idol or something warlike, with vipers in place of feet and, in place of a human head, the head of a cock or a lion, will give power against enemies and renders one victorious; it is effective against poisons, and also stops blood from whatever part it flows.

The figure of a man having a bundle of herbs at his neck, when carved on jasper, has the power of discerning or recognizing illnesses; it restrains blood from whatever place it flows. They say that Galen possessed this stone.

They say that a cross carved on green jasper has the power of freeing the wearer from drowning in water.

A woman having a bird in one hand and a fish in the other: when carved on chrysolite, it is of great value for doing business.

The figure of a wolf carved on jasper is effective against cunning, and prevents one from uttering insane words.

The figure of a stag carved on jasper has the power of healing and freeing lunatics and phrenetics.

The figure of an emperor carved on jasper, with head held upright, renders the wearer lovable to every creature, and whatever he seeks he will obtain.

The figure of a maiden with flowing garment, holding laurel in her hand, when carved on jasper, makes the wearer safe from drowning in water; nor will he be troubled by a demon, and it makes him powerful and able to obtain all things.

The figure of a man having in his hand an inscribed palm, when it is found on jasper, renders the wearer powerful and pleasing to princes. Thus far Thetel.

*On the Method of Making Seals and Magical Images. Treatise VI.*

Having set forth the magical seals and images, and the method of making them, insofar as it has indeed been handed down by the most ancient writers themselves, it remains that in this place there also be added a more exact method of making them, and that the foregoing be explained more accurately.

And indeed, before all things, in their composition the time must be considered: I mean the time of election, to be carefully observed in inscribing or engraving figures on stones. For they receive greater virtue from heaven if they are engraved at fixed and opportune times, since in this way the stars exert more influence. Hence one wishing to stir up love uses benevolent aspects, but one wishing hatred to approach uses adverse aspects. Therefore they wish the image of Saturn to be fashioned when he himself is in Capricot-

... in Capricorn or Aquarius; Jupiter's in Sagittarius and Pisces; Mars's in Aries and Scorpio; the Sun's in Leo; Venus's in Taurus and Libra; Mercury's in Gemini and Virgo; the Moon's in Cancer. Thus the image of Leo or Cancer ought to be set into a ring or stone while the Sun is traversing Leo and the Moon Cancer, yet in such a way that each is free from every impediment, especially from the rays of Saturn or Mars.

It must also be held that any sign, so long as the Sun is in it, becomes living, rules over the others, and has its effect felt before the others; and therefore the planet must always be directed to the place, that is, the sign, face, and degree, which the Sun illumines.

Thus far concerning the magical images of the ancient sages, which I have chosen to enumerate here chiefly for this reason: that it may be clear that they were constructed from magical philosophy, that is, from the principles of true natural magic, not of goety or necromancy, although it is quite certain that superstition has crept into many of them through abuse: Use has praise; abuse has blame. For if anyone should wish, with superstition, to refer them to evil action, he will be contrary to the divine order of the art itself and to the principles from which they flowed; and he must beware lest, ensnared by delirious superstitions, he become wholly delirious and superstitious.

*The erection of Mercury's signature, for sharpening the memory.*

Let Mercury serve as an example. If anyone desires to procure its influence for sharpening understanding, talent, and memory, let him observe at what time of the year Mercury, in Gemini or Virgo, in which signs it has its domicile, is strong and fortunate in the east or in midheaven. It has been noticed that it performs its operation chiefly at night, and more evidently at that hour and time at which the engraving was both begun and completed.

Therefore to seek the principle of this hidden influence from those principal and manifest qualities, from temperament or from the whole substance, is not so much absurd as ridiculous, since the actions of those things from which these virtues flow forth are not corporeal, and are poured in from heaven without a body. Therefore here there is no need of any measure taken from the senses, no ambitious battle of words and empty attempt at demonstrations. That the beginning of every philosophical demonstration cannot be sought from the senses and qualities, but must be sought elsewhere, is clear even from this common example: if someone asks concerning the color of a white rose, namely whence it derives its origin, and wishes to assign its cause to a colder temperament, what, I ask, will he say about the red rose? Will you say that species of endive pour forth milky juice for this reason, because they are cold? But very many very hot plants, such as the tithymali, swell and drip with the same juice. And is aloe bitter for this reason, because it is hot? Yet in opium, which kills by destructive cold, there is remarkable bitterness. And although it may be granted that such things flow from a property of the whole substance, nevertheless in our mystery a more sublime and more excellent craftsman, engraver, and former must be placed and sought above that substance. Therefore the Alexandrians have labored in vain, attempting to refer all things to manifest qualities; Scaliger says in Exercise 318 that this is the height of impudence, and at least in poisons it was fitting that they should stop. We see certain things burst forth from the womb and bosom of nature whose causes, although they are plainly inexplicable to us, meanwhile it is useful and pleasing to the lover of medicine and to the industrious and sagacious investigator of natural things to devise probable reasons; and if he does not fully attain them, nevertheless he does not take credit away from manifest things, nor does he slander their effects, but follows nature and its maker with admiration.

From this it is clear that the things elicited are not altogether vain: what is said about characteristic cure, which by engraved images hung from the neck accomplishes natural operations through the heavenly properties of the stars, agreeing with our bodies by a wonderful influence; I say, the things are not wholly vain which the most ancient explorers of nature have consecrated in writing and monuments concerning the written word, or characters and seals, fashioned at fixed times according to the virtue and influx of heaven, without any superstition which ignorance alone of this mystery has invented, without profanation of the divine name and without offense or any violation of faith and religion.
