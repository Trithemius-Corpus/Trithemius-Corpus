*Of Steganography*

Gediel asiel modebar mopiel, casmoyn, rochamurenu proys nasaron atido casmear vearfy maludym demosarotiel masdurym soduuiel mesray seor amatlum, laueur pealo netus fabelron. When these have been completed, the two princes called by name will be present, good, benevolent, safe; you must not fear them, but confidently entrust to them whatever you wish. They will faithfully carry out your command. This sign will be red.

Roth. KEEP IN YOUR MIND SUCH A SECRET AS you do not dare to commit to writing.

I am indebted to my lord for many benefits bestowed upon me; I have secretly learned the plan of his enemies, through treacherous ambushes, to seize the castle this night. I desire to warn my prince; by letters I cannot, since a search of travelers is being made by the enemy on the road; by messengers I cannot, since I know absolutely no one to be entrusted with my dangerous matter. I shall summon a spirit, entrust the secret, and send any letters I wish, merely for form’s sake, so that the prince may know who is to be summoned from the number of the spirits, since otherwise, without letters, he would not know that a spirit has been sent by me. This one goes; he anticipates the princes and the castle.

Roth. LET THE FORM OF THE LETTERS BE THIS or another; when he has read it, let him forbid going.

The highest nobility and the highest virtue of the human race is to love God purely, to seek his glory, and to adore him with uprightness of mind. For the humility of a pure heart shall reign in heaven; patience shall exalt the mind alone of a bare man. He who fears God and is most diligent shall be established in true nobility. Love God, despise the world, flee the age of iniquity, love religion, brotherly charity, the bond of peace, and the unity of those who live rightly. Christ admonishes in the Gospel that we ought not to be anxious about superfluous human cares. “Having,” says the holy Apostle, “only what is necessary, let us be content.” Let us therefore have the love of God and zeal for justice, since truly solid riches are purity of soul, simplicity of heart, disregard of earthly honor, the cleansing of beauty, and the instruction of holy morals. Let us be intent upon good works, lest vices overwhelm us; let us live innocent in all uprightness, as though we were to die tomorrow. Let us love God with a most pure heart, carefully keeping [unclear] his with humility; let us mourn for sins, at all times beseeching the most pious Savior that he may forgive us our vices and pour into us his love and humility of heart. Let us defer the desires of this world and vain and false honors, diligently thinking upon our death. To live virtuously, to love one’s neighbor, to do injury to no one, to fear God, to give alms, to feed the hungry, to defend the good, to rebuke the vices of the depraved, to persist in prayers, to despise the glory of the world, and to devote oneself to readings: these are the treasures of Christians. For the vain glory of the world is about to fall suddenly, and riches too are false, which, when we shortly breathe our last, we shall leave behind unwillingly. Justice alone is our happiness. In our humility there is glorying, and the possession of riches that will remain with us for all time: the memory of death, fear of judgment, and recollection of our sins.

When my prince has received these, or any other letters containing no secret, on seeing the sign of Gediel he knows whither it is to be turned, and which spirit is to be summoned.

*Conjuration.*

Gediel aprois camoret y moschoyn diuial palorfan, sermel, afparlon Crisphe Lamedon ediur cabosyn arsy thamerosyn. When these words have been spoken, as is fitting, and certain things have been added which are necessary in the art, with the spirit revealing it, not the letter, he will know the secret of the [unclear] mind, and will know the hidden matter, and he anticipates the enemy.
