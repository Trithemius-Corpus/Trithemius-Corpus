Here I wish you to understand by east not where the sun rises on any given day, but that place in which it was created at the beginning, which we call the equinoctial. For unless you observe this consideration most diligently, you will not be able to make progress in this art. But now I shall also teach you the [sequence?] of this table, so that from one spirit you may understand all. Let us therefore take Malgaras as an example, through whom the operation of this art is carried out in the ninth chapter of this present first book. He is western; his sign is this: ⋒. He has under him thirty who preside over the day, and they have great command for putting to flight the spirits of darkness. Subject to these are another thirty, who preside over the night and dwell always in darkness; nor do they come to the light except by the command of their princes, to whom they are subject at all times. And these in turn have under them, as guardians and ministers subject to them by full right, sometimes thirty, sometimes twenty, sometimes ten, and sometimes all; they go forth together with all at the command of their principals, and in [unclear] order. Thus, as many of their number as have been summoned by the operator, at the command of the great virtues, from the second or third order, so many servants and guardians also come with them to the principal, who is Malgaras. Again, under these are others, as messengers and doorkeepers, without a fixed number, who often assemble mingled with them. We do not possess the names of all these spirits. But those which it is necessary for the operator to know we shall set forth in each chapter. And note that all these spirits are, each with his principal emperor, in the quarter of the world assigned to him, as is clear in the table.

*Book One. Chapter II. Whose prince is called Padiel. Subsolanus, having under him 10,000 daytime ministers, 200,000 nighttime ones, and many other subservitors, whose number is uncertain.*

Since, as we said in the preceding chapter, Pamersiel with his aforesaid spirits is implacable and unfaithful, and does not obey indiscriminately all who desire to work in this art, but scarcely even the most experienced in it, we wish in this present chapter to make the art safer, and to demonstrate the manner of working in it through better spirits, who are benevolent, prompt, and cheerful in obeying the one who calls; who, when called in the proper manner and at the proper time, come without delay, and faithfully carry out without any deceit whatever what has been entrusted to them. Now the first principal emperor and presiding spirit of this operation is called Padiel, by the name Subsolanus, in an orbicular mansion, which is the first mansion after the east. He has under him ten thousand who preside over the day and over all daytime operations of this art; they command and bring out the nocturnal spirits lurking in darkness, who number two hundred thousand. All flee light and day, except one who is a messenger to the presiding spirits. All are good and benevolent, and they do not harm the operator unless he is malicious, or less skilled in the art.
