Then, in the same second table, we have arranged the motion of Saturn in any one of the twelve signs, for all the quarters of the day and night, down to the minutes, so that, at whatever hour, once the degree of the ascendant in which Saturn himself is has been known by us, the precise root of it may be able to appear clearly. But once the calculation of something has been completed, to which I do not know whether our age can ever attain, the table must be begun anew from the head of its beginning. In the third table we have reduced to proportion the sevenfold conjunction of Saturn with the other planets, which is unique; through it all 120 conjunctions are easily reduced to the point. In the same table the pure motion of all the planets is known through the punctual division of each beginning, progression, and elongation from the point in any motion, if the mean motion has first been perfectly obtained. For without knowledge of the mean motion it is impossible for anyone to be able to approach the practice of this science. Again, even if you understand the mean motion and likewise the true motion most perfectly, it will profit you nothing for knowledge of this science unless you understand fully the three prescribed tables for all the points of the divisions in the motions of the planets, taken equally from unequal degrees, minute by minute. But now let us proceed to the operation.

When, however, all these things have been known, and those things which we have said, and, with God’s help, are going to say, have been perfectly understood, when you wish to work in this most profound speculation, first of all you must know the rising, elevation, and setting of all the stars of the eighth sphere, through which the operation is made, and how far each is distant from another. The stars through which we operate for announcing any secret whatever to an absent person, whether by words or by writings and without a human messenger, are seven hundred in number. Their names, places, risings, settings, elevations, distances, approaches, and recessions we have described in a special book; it would be too laborious to repeat them here again by inserting them.
