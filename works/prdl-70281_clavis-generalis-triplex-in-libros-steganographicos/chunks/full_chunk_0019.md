Of these spirits, who are chief among the forty, they have the office of announcing the secret counsels of princes to their subjects and friends; from the diurnal ones you have eight, and just as many from the nocturnal ones, with their many servants, who suffice for everything; nor is there need to call the others. Carefully observe that, when you call one or two princes from those named, you express in the verse the number of their servants; for they are proud, and it pleases them wonderfully to be said to preside over many retinues. Therefore, when you wish to operate through them in Steganography, turn your face to the south-west, and do diligently what you know must be done, so that you omit absolutely nothing of those things which belong to the art. The conjuration: Asiriel aphorsy Lamodyn to Carmephyn drubal asutroy Sody baruchon, viefer palormy thulnear asmeron chornemadusyn coleny busarethon duys marphelitubra nasaron fabelronty.

When these things have been completed, those who have been called will stand by there. To distinguish them, know that the princes are commonly accustomed to appear in an airy habit, that is, sapphire-colored; but the servants in white. Entrust your secret to the princes themselves, because they are faithful. All the leaders have among themselves this custom beforehand: when the operator addresses them, they immediately send away the servants; indeed, they withdraw of themselves, lest they hear the commission. When he ceases speaking to the princes, the servants are seen standing by again.

Let the secret of the prince be of such a kind that he entrusts it to no one to be conveyed.

Roth.

The counsel of a prince is great and most secret, concerning very great and difficult matters. He wishes some other prince, a friend, to know it, because he has cause; but he fears that it may come into public, with the greatest danger to himself and his people; if, however, it is entrusted to a messenger, he fears lest he betray it, or to letters, lest they be read by someone. Therefore he calls a spirit, who will not reveal the secret; he entrusts it, accomplishes it, and is secure.

He sends letters in no respect suspect, nor fearing public exposure in any respect. Roth.

Grant us the strength to overcome the temptations of the devil, so that, with present evils avoided, we may obtain rest. O sweetest Savior Jesus, forgive the sins of us, the servants who pray, since we desire to serve you with all our strength. Make us always have in ourselves the fervor of your love, adorned with good works. Give us the true operations of virtues, observance of justice, and inseparable love of uprightness; make us truly humble and vigorous despisers of worldly things, so that, always burning with your love, we may love nothing earthly, and esteem nothing temporal as pleasant to us. O teacher of humility, Jesus Christ, teach us to imitate your doctrine. We are, alas, entangled in many vices, bare of good works. Save us, Jesus Christ, lest we perish.
