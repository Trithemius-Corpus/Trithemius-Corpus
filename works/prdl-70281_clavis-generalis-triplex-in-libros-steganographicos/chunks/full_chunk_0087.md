First of all the person to be initiated in the art shall swear an oath: I, N., swear and promise by the power of almighty God, by the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, by the resurrection of the dead and the last judgment, and by the salvation of my soul in the holy Catholic faith, to almighty God, to the blessed Virgin Mary, to all the saints, and to you, N., that I will faithfully keep this art of Steganography hidden all the days of my life, nor will I teach it to anyone without your will and consent. Moreover, by the same power I swear and promise that I will not use this science against God and his commandments, nor against the holy Roman and universal Church or its ministers, nor against justice and equity. So may God help me, and so may he save me at the last judgment. Then the preceptor shall read the following conjuration in the mystical tongue, and shall interpret it, and all the conjurations contained in this work, before the disciple, lest he suspect that there is anything diabolical, superstitious, or contrary to God, or that any compact with demons, implicit or explicit, is in any way secretly hidden there.

*General conjuration to be spoken by the master before approaching the art.*

And after the master of this art has explained to his disciple the conjuration, order, and series of all the spirits, he shall reveal to him the most secret mysteries of this science, which are neither written nor to be written, forbidding him under the oath he has taken to reveal them to anyone as long as he lives, except to worthy persons sworn in the art, or to write them in any manner or for any reason. Then he shall teach him the natures, names, places, numbers, offices, and properties of all the spirits who are assigned to this art: how, when, and where work is to be done through them, and the remaining mysteries that pertain to the perfection of this art, concerning which nothing is to be written.

*Here begins the third book of the Steganography of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, addressed to the most serene prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and Prince Elector of the Holy Empire.*

*Preface.*

After, with the aid of our Lord Jesus Christ, Savior of faithful souls, we had completed the first two books of our art, in which we described our Steganography as best we could, we must join this third book to them, and link more marvelous things to marvelous discoveries. I found in a certain book of a certain ancient philosopher, who was called Menaistor, that it is possible, by a certain art, for us to make known the conception of our mind to a friend, however absent he may be, without hours, without words, without books, and without a messenger, most perfectly, most broadly, and most secretly. And since I was driven very vehemently by a desire to know, I began to wish to test the truth of the statements; and after many labors at last I found that very science proven by experience.
