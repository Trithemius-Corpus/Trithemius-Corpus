*Mode XXX. It proceeds by alternate words, and the significant letter is transferred to the eleventh.*

Mode 30, beginning from one vacant word, proceeds through alternate words, so that throughout the whole sequence of the narrative it always places, after one vacant word, another significant one, as we have also shown in the preceding mode and in others. Further, by the fourth letter of the significant word, the eleventh following after it is to be taken, so that for A, m is taken; for b, n; for c, o; and so consequently, as is clear in the alphabet.

S. D. I send you a prayer concerning [the mother?], Mary ever Virgin, as you recently requested: [unclear] most loving, kindly mother, nourishing Mary, virgin, most radiant parent. You also most pleasing to the highest unity; you, most faithful helper of all who invoke you; you, noble refuge of those seeking you. For daily [unclear] of holiness; save [us], most gentle advocate of the poor, consoler of those who groan, most beautiful storehouse of virtues, most adorned. Hail, most kindly comforter of the sorrowful, and most celebrated promoter of the faithful friends of Christ. Rejoice, [Anna?], solace of those who mourn; rejoice, summit of nobility and sure hope of the faithful who labor. Obtain pardon for us who ask; [mitigate?] the dread Judge of Christianity. Help those contrite in heart; take away our souls, we beg; sanctify [us] by your goodness. Blessed are you, most honorable virgin mother, who bore in complete integrity, and as virgin gave birth to the almighty Savior, the Son of God, yet always remaining also a most pure virgin. The messenger Gabriel greeted her as full of grace, mother in humanity. Hail, mother, or inexhaustible fountain of indulgence in piety, shining with piety, most reverend to all by the very great magnitude of your benefits. Intercede for your servants; adorn us with your virtues, that we may attain blessedness after the end of this life by the kindness of the Savior of all the faithful. And may he who by his blood gave life to us wretched ones, with you interceding, receive [us] piously in his kindness [when dead?]. Amen. Farewell. From Sponheim, the seventh day before the Ides of January, in the year of the Lord 1499. Joh. Trith., Abbot.
