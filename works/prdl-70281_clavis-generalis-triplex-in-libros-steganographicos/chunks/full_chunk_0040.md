*Book One.*

When the person to whom the letters are sent has received them, if he is skilled in the art, let him first do what the art itself requires; then, turned toward the north, as is customary, let him utter the conjuration.

*The Conjuration Charm.*

Demoriel osayr chumon chulty saue porean lusin dayr pean cathurmosomarson ersoy lamedon iothar bufraym suarmenadroy chilarso sabelmerusyn.

When the charm has been completed, as is prescribed in the art, the spirit that has been sent will be present, and will appear visibly to you alone, and to absolutely no one else; he will speak the secret into your ear.

*Chapter XXI, Whose supreme emperor and prince is called Geradiel, dwelling in no quarter of the world, but accustomed to be present everywhere as a wanderer.*

Besides those emperors, princes, and dukes of spirits whom we have named in the preceding chapters, who remain in fixed places throughout the visible world, many other wandering, unstable ones have been assigned, remaining fixed nowhere, whom the ancient wise magi called [astati?], that is, unstable ones, because they fly in the air like flies, without order, without restriction. Of these, some are not useless to us for Steganography, because they do not require the observance of places, but can be summoned by conjuration in every place. The first of these is called Geradiel; he does not have any [count?] or prince under him, but only many servants, almost innumerable, whom he takes with him for service according to the ranks that they have among themselves, according to the twenty-four hours of day and night. Their general office is to announce secretly whatever things you wish, to whatever quarter.

In this table you have, numbered according to the hours and arranged in their own order, 18,150 ministers and servants of Geradiel the great prince. You should know their arrangement and reckoning to be altogether necessary for the operation, so that you may know at any given hour with how many servants they are accustomed to come, or when he himself is not accustomed to come, but only the servants assigned to the hour. I advise you, however, to take care in your operations to observe the hour in which the prince himself comes personally with the servants of that same hour, who otherwise, when their emperor is absent, are not always willing to obey the operator in all things. Therefore, when you wish to operate through this prince, after first doing what must first be done, say the conjuration.
