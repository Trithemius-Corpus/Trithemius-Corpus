You send to your friend familiar letters or a devout prayer, which may bear being seen.

I had lent you my codex concerning the deeds of the Lombards, and Bede’s volume on the times, which you should return to me. For it befits a wise man, especially a Christian, everywhere to keep the faith he has promised. To forget benefits is held, by our divine and human institutions, to be a crime. Do not incur so sinful a guilt. I sent you my volumes so that you might restore them at some time. But I freely gave you the Greek books, in consideration of which you might deservedly show yourself more faithful to us by returning thanks. Farewell, from Spanheim, on the Ides of March, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When your friend has received these or any other letters sent by you, once he has recognized the sign of Cabariel, let him turn toward that quarter of the world from which the Circius wind proceeds; after first doing, according to custom, the things that must first be done, let him read this prayer in silence.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Cabariel afiar paremon chiltan amedyn sayr pemadon chulty mouayr sauepor peatha mal frimaston dayr pean cothur no fabelrusyn elsoty chelmodyn. *Chapter.*

*Of which the prince is called Raysiel, dwelling directly toward the North, under whom are fifty princes who preside over our daytime operations, and as many who [preside over] the nighttime ones; whose office is to announce transmissions.*

[unclear] The thirteenth mansion is in the North, whose spirit and supreme emperor is Raysiel, who has under his dominion fifty dukes who preside over daytime operations, with many servants, and as many in number who preside over nighttime operations with their attendants. Their office is, in these matters and transmissions that pertain to death, to notify the friends of the operator and announce the secret. The dukes who preside over daytime operations obey the caller readily and come cheerful, joyful, and most ready to serve; but those who preside over nighttime operations are somewhat insolent, because they greatly hate and detest the light, nor do they easily obey, especially novices less proven in the art, whom they often mock to their face, unless they are so constrained by the operator’s conjurations that they cannot speak against him. For they greatly fear conjuration, and [unclear], they are given to being conjured more than the daytime spirits, and yet they obey unwillingly. But now from the number of all these let us set down the names of a few and the number of their servants, as many as suffice us for the customary operation.
