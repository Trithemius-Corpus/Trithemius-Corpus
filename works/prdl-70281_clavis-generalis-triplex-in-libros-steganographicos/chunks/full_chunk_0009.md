... buried, you raised from the dead on the third day, and showed him alive for forty days, to the great consolation of his rejoicing disciples. With pure and most firm confession we believe that he, true God and man, reigning with you in your uncircumscribable majesty without end, will come again to judge the living and the dead, and will render to each of us according to his works. Through him we ask you, O most merciful Father: hear us and have mercy on us now and in that terrible hour in which we are to be judged through that Son of yours. Look upon us, most merciful God; for we are frail sinners deserving pity, bare of good works, who by sinning have offended your most holy Majesty in many things. But, turned by your mercy to repentance, we groan weeping for our sins, humbly beseeching through Jesus Christ your Son that you turn your indignation away from us, and mercifully forgive us our past crimes, bringing us, when this present exile is ended, to the delight of the heavenly fatherland. Johannes Trithemius wrote this.

A simple and open narrative of this kind, or any other you wish, which all may read and understand, which contains nothing secret, and which you are not afraid should come before the sight of everyone, you are to write on the aforesaid paper, which we have mentioned, with the conjuration of the spirits, your face turned toward the east. When it has been written, express before the spirits the secret intention that you wish the absent person to know, calling them to witness that they should act faithfully. *Book One. For Knowing the Places, Names, Signs, and Assigned Marks of the Spirits.*

But since it is necessary for everyone working in this science to know the places, names, and signs of the principal spirits, lest through ignorance he call from the north one who is dwelling in the south, which would not only hinder his purpose but could also injure the operator, I shall therefore set before you, in a circle, the regions in which the principal spirits dwell, with their names and signs.
