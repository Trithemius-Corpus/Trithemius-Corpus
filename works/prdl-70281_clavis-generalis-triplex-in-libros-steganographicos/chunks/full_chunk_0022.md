*Steganographia*

*Chapter IX. The prince of which is called Malgatharas, who dwells in the west, having under him thirty dukes for daytime actions and as many for nighttime ones; their office is to announce the private secrets of friends.*

[unclear]

The inhabitant of the ninth mansion, which is in the equinoctial west, is Malgaras, the greatest prince of the west. He has under him thirty dukes who preside over the day, and thirty others who preside over the night, together with their servants. The office of all these is to announce to friends the private secrets and hidden matters of friends. The dukes are good and very ready to obey the one who calls them, especially the daytime ones, since they are more accustomed to associate among men. The nighttime ones, though they are good, nevertheless do not willingly associate with men, but greatly flee and detest the light, wishing to hide in darkness; for this reason they often appear like bats to the one calling them by conjuration. From all these we shall place twenty-four, with their names, in the table; twelve of them by day and as many by night are sufficient.

*Book One*

You now have, among the Malgaretic princes, twelve for daytime actions and as many for nighttime ones, with their six hundred forty servants. Note that Aroyz and Basiel are always solitary; Misiel, Barfas, Asper, and Deilas can be called two by two, but the rest three at a time. When you wish to have three, or two, or one, beware lest you err in their names. As the table teaches you, so act, without alteration, lest you fall into danger.

*Song of Conjuration. Red.*

MALGARAS ador chamefo bulueriny marefo bodyr Cadumir auiel casmyo tedy pleotyn viordi care viorba, chameron vesy thuriel vlnauy, bueue meuo chasmironty naor ernyfo, chony barmo caleuodyn barfo thubra fol. Once the conjuration has been spoken, be a strong and steadfast man: those whom you have called will at once appear to you visibly. But if those called from among the nighttime ones do not come at once, do not for that reason interrupt your work, but press them with repeated conjuration until they obey. For they are somewhat sluggish; they do not willingly come among men, as we said before.

*Let there be a secret of the mind which one does not wish to entrust either to a person or to letters.*

Red.

In matters that pertain to household affairs, money, a deposit, a loan, trust, or any business of human intercourse, you have a secret which you desire an absent friend far away to know; but it is such that it would come under public examination with the greatest danger or loss to both parties. For that reason it must be entrusted neither to letters nor to men. Therefore, so that you may be secure and need fear no one’s awareness, you turn toward the west, call a duke, and entrust the secret: he goes and faithfully carries everything through; if anything has been entrusted for you, he brings it back.
