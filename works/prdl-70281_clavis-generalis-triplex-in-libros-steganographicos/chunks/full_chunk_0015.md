*Chapter IV. Whose prince, Aseliel, dwells toward the east-south, having under him forty principal substitutes with their servants; they preside over those matters that pertain to love.*

The fourth mansion from the East, in the east-south, is held by the great prince Aseliel with his spirits, of whom ten preside over operations in this art by day, twenty by night. The office of the power of all these is to announce those things that pertain to the love of women.

These thirty, moreover, as princes, have under them the same number of others, whom they send in order to their duties when they have been duly summoned by the operator. Again, under these there are still others, whose number is great. But when you wish to work in Steganography through the prince of this mansion, there is no need to call upon all the spirits subject to him; one or two is enough for you, whether the operation is nocturnal or diurnal. I shall therefore tell you the names of a few, with the number of those serving under them. From these you should always call upon one or two, whichever you wish, and you have no need of more, unless the operation is for different purposes. This is the table.

You now have, behold, sixteen princes from among the greater ones, who are under the great Afiel, of whom eight preside over the day and eight over the night. Each of them has twenty subject servants, whom they send to their duties whenever it is necessary. All the princes are good for obeying, benevolent and most ready; but their servants are not all so, since they are sometimes proud and implacable, especially toward those whom they judge to be less perfect in the art. These sixteen princes, with their subjects, are sufficient for announcing everything to you, and there is no need for you either to know or to call upon more. But when you wish to work in Steganography through Asiel, observe, according to the things customarily prescribed in the art, the place of his mansion, which is in the east-south, and call one of the spirits named above for his proper time, whichever you wish. This is the conjuration:

Aseliel aproysy. melym, thulnear casmoyn, mauear burson, charny demorphao, Theoma asmeryn diuel, casonti yearlybasamys, ernoti chaua lorson.

When the conjurations have been completed in the usual manner, add the other things that must customarily be done in the art, and the effect of your operation will most certainly follow. Let us set down an example.

Let us imagine the secret of your mind. You have a beloved woman, in whose love you are held, and she no less; but you cannot approach or speak to her because of the watch kept by her people. At last you have found a plan by which you can possess her, but it is necessary first to advise her of it. You cannot do so yourself; you do not dare by letters, lest they come into the hands of others; nor do you decide that the matter should be entrusted to messengers or old women, lest afterward they make you known. Therefore, conceal by art for a time the matter which you trust cannot be made openly. You call the invisible ministers of the art, write letters suspected in nothing, and send them to your beloved even through those same guardians; they praise the writing and give it to your beloved to read. Recognizing the sign, she calls the hidden messenger, hears the plan, sends back her consent; you approach, enter, obtain possession.
