*CHAPTER XXIII.*

*Whose spirit and supreme prince is called Hydriel, and dwells in waters, having under him 100 leaders, 200 companions, and almost innumerable servants.*

[unclear]

There is another prince among the principal spirits, who is called Hydriel, and he dwells in waters, fishponds, lakes, marshes, and the sea, and around springs, cisterns, and rivers, having under his dominion at least 100 leaders or princes, and 200 companions or subordinate leaders, and many servants. Their office is generally to announce all things, both by waters and by land, whether it be by day or by night. And know that the princes are quite benevolent and willing in all things, but they do not appear so when they come after being conjured. For they frequently appear in the form of a serpent, sometimes large, sometimes small, having a very beautiful maiden’s head, with hair spread out. None of them goes alone, but each prince brings with him at least two companions and 80 servants. So that you may not fall into error in the operation, I shall set down for you the names of some of the leaders.

Behold, you have from the princes and leaders of Hydriel 12, and from the companions and servants 1320, who suffice for your operations in Steganography according to the order of the 24 hours, so that each leader has 2 hours, and the companions and servants 110 in their orders, which you must know completely. When, through these princes of spirits, you wish to do anything in Steganography, do what you know must be done according to the art, and say the conjuration.

*Song.*

Hydriel, apron chamerote, satrus pean nearmy chabelon, vearchas, belta, nothelmy phameron, arsoy pedaryn onzel, Lamedo drubel areon veatly cabyn noty maleros haytny pe fary does, pen rasi medusan ilcohi person. When the song has been spoken, the spirit whom you have called will be present, with the servants assigned to him according to the hour, and he will be obedient to you in all things, faithful for everything to which you send him.

X.

*Let us imagine some secret matter.*

Let there be for me some very secret matter of any sort whatever for a friend, which I cannot safely make known to him either by letters or by [messenger?]. Therefore, lest the secret come to light, I summon the spirit; he comes, he obeys; I entrust the secret; he carries it faithfully.

X.

*Let us compose letters of any kind whatever.*

Johannes Tritt., Abbot of Spanheim, sends greeting to Brother Peter Marponius Gisemius, laureate, a monk of the Rhine.

Brother Nicolaus, Bishop of Trbusium and suffragan of the most reverend father and most illustrious duke Adam of Insignisburg in Pantomorania, a most honorable and distinguished archprelate, an indefatigable lover of books, seems most justly to compare an abbot, Vinilianus by name, to beasts, because he is one who feasts splendidly among the dung-heaps of beasts and prefers calves to volumes, as Reuchlin has affirmed. I ask that you imitate Nicolaus, the best and most gentle bishop. Farewell, 21 March 1505.

When you have received these letters, or any others, with the sign of Hydriel, with no consideration of the sphere observed, but only after the general preliminaries that must be prefaced in the art have been put first, as customary say the following.

*Conjuration.*

Hydriel omar, penadon epyrma narsoy greol fabelrusin adiel pedrufij nozeui melrays vremy pean larfoy naes chemetyn.

When this conjuration has been spoken in the proper way, according to the instruction of the art, the spirit will appear to you visibly, revealing in your ear the secret entrusted to him.

*CHAPTER XXIV.*

*Whose prince is called Pyrichiel, having under him many leaders, princes, companions, and servants, whose number is uncertain.*

Pyrichiel, a certain supreme prince of spirits, seems to us to have to be wholly rejected from our art, because he is said to be quite benevolent. He does not have under him leaders or princes in the manner of the rest, but only companions and subordinate servants, whose number is uncertain. There are those who say that he has leaders and princes under him; they seem to us not to understand his property and custom well enough, since they have imagined companions and servants as leaders. The name of Pyrichiel derives its designation from fire, because he is frequently accustomed to be occupied around fire.
