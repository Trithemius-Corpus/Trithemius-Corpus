*Preface.*

They handed them down, in a sequence of narration, by sagacious invention. Although, because of the dullness of my talent, I cannot perfectly imitate these most devoted lovers of wisdom, nevertheless I do not cease to admire them and to read them with whatever diligence I can. For when I consider that they, by their own study, devised great things beyond other men, I rub myself, as it were, with a certain force, so that I may grow warm, and I impel myself toward some imitation, even among the least, of those who have gone before.

Nor, as I think, has this opinion wholly deceived me; since through the continual pursuit of reading I have learned many things which I had not known before, and it has opened to my thoughts an approach to investigating matters more secret and altogether hidden from others. For although I am not of such learning or industry that I would dare profess that I have in every respect grasped the hidden method of the mysteries of the ancient sages, nevertheless I have found certain methods, many and various, not altogether contemptible, as it seems to me, by which I can safely and without deception, suspicion, or detection by anyone, through open messengers or letters, convey to another the most secret intention of my mind in this art of knowledge as widely as I may wish.

And at the request of the Most Serene Prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and Prince Elector of the Holy Roman Empire, always my most unwilling [unclear], the wisest Maecenas of all philosophers, than whom no one has seemed to me more worthy for this great secret to be revealed to him, I have committed to writing this new discovery of a most secret art, and not without the greatest labors have gathered it into the present volume.
