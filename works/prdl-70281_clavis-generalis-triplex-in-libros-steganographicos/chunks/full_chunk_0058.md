*Book Two.*

... in a familiar matter, which you seem to have managed most diligently with grave modesty, I have plainly understood; this deed I praise, and I give thanks that will never die. And so I would very much wish to comply with your requests, if I had the things asked for, Christ knows. I have lately seen various Latin translations of [Ficiatus?]; I have read many most brilliant volumes of Pico della Mirandola, a most excellent man. I have seen Hippolytus in Greek; whether he has been completely translated, I do not know. The highest Truth, Christ, knows, from whom no darkness hides anything, that from my heart I wish to comply with you when you ask. I beg you, with zeal for virtue and zeal for rectitude: despise praises and honors that will perish. Consider true praise to be the imitation of Christ, who goes before. See to it, I beg, that you are a priest in works, and that you love Christ throughout all the time in which you live. The true rest of the soul, and pious knowledge of the Savior, observance of Catholic tradition, [unclear] eternal glory prescribed for all. May Jesus Christ, the glorious giver of all good things, confer on you Catholic love of eternity, making you a priest of Christians. Farewell. 4 Kalends of April, in the year 1500.

When the person to whom the letters have been sent has received them, being skilled in the art, after the customary preliminaries have first been set forth in general, let him arrange the lord of the ascendant with the other things that are customary on the chart, and let him say the prescribed conjuration. Once it has been said, the spirit sent will appear visibly; turning to him, let him say fearlessly: Fabelmerufyn passoriel liertos ryneas melchus thyrmo nydran year padroys. When this song has been completed, the spirit will at once approach gently and will faithfully disclose the mystery entrusted to him. But everyone who is to work in this art should beware, both he who sends the spirit and he to whom it has been directed, lest they presume to perform anything in public or among others who are not skilled in this art; because all hourly spirits love secrecy and detest publicity, and they would not depart without danger.

*Steganographia, Chapter III.*

*Whose hour is called Danzur, and its spirit is Vequaniel, supreme emperor, who has under his dominion 20 dukes, 200 counts. Of servants there is no number.*

[unclear]

The third hour of the day is called Danfur, whose supreme spirit and emperor is called Vequaniel, having under his rule 20 dukes, 200 counts. The number of servants is uncertain. Their office is general for all things; and they are sufficiently prompt and benevolent in all things, whatever has been entrusted to them, and faithful and safe; nor are they unwilling to obey, but when called in public they can by no means do so without injury or danger to the caller. For they love secrecy, as do all the others who preside over the hours and their operations. I do not know the names of all of them, but only of a few, which, when they themselves revealed them, were set down in this table and are sufficient for us.
