Here we have five of Oriel’s dukes and ten of the counts, with the servants assigned to them in their turns, to the number of 1100, who suffice for us for every operation of this hour. But when you wish to work through them in this hour of the day, after first doing what must customarily be done beforehand, and after writing what must be written on clean paper, and arranging this conjuration on its back, do the rest as you know.

*Conjuration.*

Oriel burnadiel irasmy ersoty amear medusen. After you have completed this conjuration according to the proper instruction of the art, you will see the duke present with the other spirits called; entrust the secret to him.

*Formulation of the Secret.*

We have a friend whose welfare we favor; we fear that he is to be supplanted and circumvented by some wandering rogue. Therefore we desire that he be warned. We cannot do it in plain words by messengers; by letters, lest they fall into someone’s hands, likewise we dare not. Therefore, so that we may be safe and our secret may always remain hidden, let us call at least one spirit, the leader of those named above. He comes, obeys, and faithfully carries the message.

Let the letter be composed at pleasure.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, sends greetings to Heinrich von Bünau, golden knight, distinguished philosopher and mathematician.

Since you are a most devoted student of the humanities, you will rejoice, I think, exceedingly to have found a new companion in your studies. The man who will present this letter to you is a man of great knowledge, a supreme philosopher, a most subtle arithmetician, a most eloquent rhetorician, a very profound mathematician, and in every respect most excellently learned. He speaks with the greatest elegance, resplendent with Ciceronian eloquence. For if I were most especially your friend, I would have kept so great a man wholly to myself, since in using his services I have found him a most learned philosopher. I especially favor you; receive gladly the philosopher whom, moved by singular affection, I send to you. O would that all our students were such, who with timid heart scorn noble knowledge. This man constantly searches into the study of philosophy and the lofty sciences, and the most lofty and profound things of nature; he continually handles them and penetrates them most acutely. Therefore, Heinrich Bünau, remember for Christ’s sake to treat the man with a generous countenance. Farewell from Sponheim, 2 November, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When the friend to whom the letter is sent, skilled in this science of Steganography, has received the letter, let him do everything that according to the art must be done, and let him append the conjuration written above, as is customary. And when he has seen the spirit sent present, let him say these words to him, as the more powerful among the spirits: Camyn aparfy aslotiel omcar reneas vean triamy cralty penafon. When these have been uttered, the spirit will immediately draw nearer and will reveal the secret to him.
