*Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim.*

Princes have most wisely been accustomed not to shut up riches to be kept in treasuries, but to apply them to the use of letters, judging this the most honorable path for those tending toward happiness: the benefit of letters, by which the commonwealth is made immortal. For indeed the knowledge of writings from [unclear] is called the burial of the living. It brings ignominious calamity upon human beings and makes them like beasts; true nobility, by contrast, is accustomed to make the soul splendid with learning and virtues. Infinite is the fruit of letters, which wins glory and holiness for our souls in heaven and shows itself immortal to those born of earth. Add that love of writing, since it extinguishes the fires of the flesh, harmful to the spirit, alone conferred on those ancient philosophers the finest memory and immortality of name among all posterity, always enclosed as venerable to human beings. Philosophy also makes those devoted to the studies of the ancestors glorious and splendid, lovers of tranquility, just, gentle, conquerors of the passions of the soul, most steadfast despisers of luxury and baseness. For these there is an easy ascent to God; holy exercises are an example of honorableness and a rule for every virtue; it shines forth in contemplation of heavenly things, sweetly raised up. They attain divine illumination, dear to God in honor, reverenced by men. Imitate these things, devote yourself to letters, confirm the happiness of life by upright works. Farewell. I, Johannes Trithemius, abbot, have written this.

*Mode XIV. Between two nulls it encloses each mystical word with its signifying words.*

Mode 14 always takes its beginning from a null and, duly proceeding in order through signifying matter by means of one null, encloses at the end the word to be hidden. Moreover, students in this art will be able not only to vary but also to enlarge the preceding thirteen modes, so that, beginning with those things at the various number where we ended the demonstration, they may ascend, up to whatever number they wish, by 7, 8, 9, 10, or more alternately, wherever they wish.

*Example*

*24 Key of Steganography. Example XIV.*

Apply yourself to letters with humble submission; be kind; most diligently beware ignorance. For teaching, celebrated by training, exalted in honorableness, learning distinguished by morals, is to be preferred to all honors. Knowledge immersed in vices has little worth, but diligent learning always makes a noble life, marked by virtues and adorned with examples of learning; adorned innocence is given esteem, since heavenly honor must be preserved by holy actions. Unfruitful knowledge will be subject to torments, but in elevated sciences friendly letters [unclear] with living virtues, and pleasures that are stained and to be overthrown; one must insist on exercises of virtues, or sweetnesses must be granted. Farewell from Sponheim, 7 Kalends of January, in the year of Christ 1498. Johannes Trithemius.

*Mode XV. It begins from the unit that marks, and proceeds alternately up to the number eight.*

Mode 15 sets one idle word before, adds another signifying word, then two alternates, three follow, and so up to eight; moreover from the group of eight through seven, then through six, and so consequently down to the unit, the descent is again made; and again, in the same way, ascent and descent to the unit, as often as will be necessary.

Princes must rightly be instructed in good disciplines and with virtues and learning, and should justly hold and knowingly govern the commonwealth. For the most glorious authority of leaders is to apply themselves at once to arms and letters: to destroy the enemy in war, and by the arts of letters to adorn the mind, and to rule with courage and wisdom together.
