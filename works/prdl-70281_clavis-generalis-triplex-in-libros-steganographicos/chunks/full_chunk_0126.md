*Example VI.*
[unclear]
The glorious, merciful one, our begetter, founder, and maker of the universe, making Christian memory of the deeds of Christ, claims it at no time with humbled knees. Wherefore we are struck dumb. Let us behold Christ carrying the wood on his shoulders, redeeming his servants by majesty. Christ, fixed to the wood, dies for us. O Christ crucified, best glorifier of all believers, hear those who bear burdens; O Christ, shining light, O Christ triumphant, free Christians. In the year of the Lord M.I.D.[unclear], on the Kalends of March, Johannes Trithemius, abbot, composed it.

But [unclear] all [letters?] of the secret mystery are transposed into the seventh letter immediately preceding each one. *Example VII.*
*The joy of temporal goods*, with flattering and slippery evils, wearies Christian peace with mortal gladness and various wars. The flesh is the vanity of the age for the criminal; those who rashly hold cheap the blessedness of the future kingdom, given over to wickedness, good for nothing, [more savage?] than beasts, wretchedly do not seek the bright path of Catholic life. They swiftly flee good things; nevertheless they perceive all Christian things as false, and the devout will more swiftly hold them in the tranquility of life. The true Catholic, who will never endure changes, truly flees the gladness of the world, does nothing rashly, does not pursue gain, loves purity; you shine with nobility by virtue; mindful of future light, he esteems transitory goods as worthless. Johannes Trithemius.
