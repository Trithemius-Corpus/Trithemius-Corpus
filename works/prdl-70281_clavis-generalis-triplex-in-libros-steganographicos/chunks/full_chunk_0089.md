*Chapter I*

*On Orifiel, the first and supreme angel of Saturn, and on the operations that are performed through him.*

Saturn, the highest and supreme of all the planets, slow in motion and by nature, cold because remote, difficult, heavy, from the point of its beginning 650 years up to the point of its first station, remaining in each sign for 30 months, is moved by its natural proper motion 626. Its first and supreme angel is called, according to the opinion of the ancients, Orifiel, who has under him three other principal spirits, namely Sadael, Poniel, and Morisiel, of whom we shall speak in their proper place and order.

Through this Orifiel, the great angel of Saturn, many different operations are performed in steganography. Through him we can make the secret of our mind known to a friend who knows the art, wherever he may be: by letters, if one wishes, or even without letters, and do many other marvelous things, which no one else understands who is unskilled in this science of ours. Therefore, anyone wishing to perform anything through this angel in steganography, especially on the day of Saturn, in these matters, business, and causes that pertain to Saturn, must first know its various and diverse motions. And first the various motion: pure, proper, mixed, direct, retrograde, and confused. And in all these he must know not only the general rules handed down by the astronomers, because although they are very useful for this science of ours, nevertheless they do not wholly suffice, but also the particular rules.

Therefore, once the tables and rules concerning the motions of the planets have been laid down as a foundation, which anyone working in this art must know completely, we have added special rules and tables of punctual motions throughout the individual chapters of this work; without observing them no one will be able to operate in this art. And note that the day of each planet is divided into four equal parts, of which the principal angel of that planet obtains the first, that is, three unequal hours; and his substitutes in order take the remaining three parts. For example: on the day of Saturn, Orifiel, its first angel, possesses the three unequal hours from sunrise, which are called the hours of the planets; Sadael, his first angel, possesses the second three; Pomiel, the second, holds the third; and Morisiel, the third angel of Orifiel, holds the last three hours. In the first three hours of the day both of Saturn and of the other stars, in matters appropriated to Saturn, one must operate through Orifiel; in the second, through Sadael; in the third, through Pomiel; and in the last three, through Morisiel. And all operations, according to the motion of Saturn, receive either a prosperous effect or a weak one. But now let us set down the punctual table.
