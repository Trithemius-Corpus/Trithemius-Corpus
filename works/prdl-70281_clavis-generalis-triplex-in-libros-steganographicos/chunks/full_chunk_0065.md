John Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, sends greeting to Lord Conrad, Abbot of Saint Stephen at Wurzburg.

A report has gone out concerning the Abbot of Limburg, that he has dismissed Brother John of Feilsdorf, a faithful man, highly skilled in temporal affairs, and has introduced new [unclear] monks, and appointed them as his officials. I greatly marvel at the useless deed. Nevertheless, when I remember the custom of the Limburgers, I do not know what to say. For they are ignorant of temporal matters, and therefore they dismissed a faithful man, because he reproved their deeds; they ought to have given thanks to our Savior Jesus Christ for the fidelity of so great a brother, who could have revealed their necessities. Wretched men, why do you reject a good and necessary helper? What shall I say? I do not praise the departure of so useful a brother. They have appointed stewards over temporal affairs. Abbot Muchar, it did not befit a man to act thus. At first you praised him; now you have rejected a man very necessary to you, and to be placed after none. I confess that if Christ had given him to me, as befits a Christian I would now give the greatest thanks, and would take care that he never departed. But you, ungrateful to Christ, preferred, by the foolish counsel of wicked men, to dismiss a faithful man who had been wearied, lest, with your affairs abandoned, there should seem to be any hope of recovery. From Sponheim, 3 Nones of April, 1500.

When the person to whom these letters, or any others whatsoever, have been sent has received them, sealed with the sign of Osmadael, being skilled in the art, after the preliminaries have been performed, let him say the prescribed conjuration; immediately the spirit sent will appear visibly. When you have seen him, say: Menafon aproysy elmano thulneas assierto mauear veneas cralnoti permason. When these words have been spoken, the spirit-messenger will reveal the secret to you.

*Of Steganography*

*Chapter 9.*

*Whose hour is called Karron, and the name of its supreme angel is Quabriel, having under his command 10 dukes, 100 counts. There is no fixed number of servants.*

The ninth hour of the day is called Karron, and its principal spirit is called Quabriel, who has under his command many and almost innumerable dukes, counts, and servants. Of these, for the service of this art of ours of Steganography, 10 of the dukes and 100 of the counts have been assigned; they have many servants assigned to themselves in orders. In the first order there are 10, in the second 20, in the third 30, in the fourth 40, in the fifth 500, in the sixth 600, in the seventh 700, in the eighth 1,080, in the ninth 90,000, but in the tenth there are 100,000. But the spirits used rarely to be called through all the orders; and if they were called, not all, but some with their dukes and counts, according to the command of the emperor, were accustomed to come. Raziel the Great, however, said in his book on illusions and transformations that the number of these spirits is very great, and that each one most diligently keeps his own orders in all services; and Solomon Hermes the Jew says, concerning the nature of spirits, that these are accustomed to appear in that form which the one working in the art has described, according to his will.
