*Conjuration.*

PYrichiel marfoys chameron, nael peanos pury lames iamene famerusyn mearlo canonorson theory torsa, nealthis dilumeris maphtroy carful ameor thubra phorsotiel chrebonos aray pe-malon layr toysi vadiniel nemor rofeuarfy cabti phroys amenda machyr fabelronthis, poyl carepon vemij naslotyn.

When these things have been completed, you will see the spirits whom you have called present; and the chief one is always accustomed to appear in the form of a serpent, having a beautiful head like a maiden’s, with hair spread out. There follows.

*Let us posit any secret whatsoever.*

Let there be for me a secret for a friend far absent, which I cannot safely send by letters, lest they be read when [unclear], or certainly [unclear] on the road. I have less confidence in a man to carry it, since I know that trustworthiness in men changes with fortune. Therefore, so that all things may be safe, I call upon the spirit, friend of the secret: he comes at once, having heard the song, and faithfully carries what has been entrusted to him.

*Let us form the letters as one pleases.*

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Jacobus Dracomius, canon of the Premonstratensian order, greeting and charity.

There is with us a notable mathematician and excellent rhetorician, constantly devoted to the study of a most tranquil life. He has with him almost infinite books, and an inestimable treasure of diverse exemplars: a pleasant and kindly teacher of upright and honorable life and of honorable society; most experienced in resolving doubts. He explains great matters: his name is Orophanius, burgrave of the kingdom of Bosnia. Farewell, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April.

When the person to whom they are sent, skilled in the art of Steganography, has received these or any similar letters, signed with the character of Pyrichiel, let him do as he knows must be done according to the art; then, with a light lit, let him say this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

PYrichiel osayr Chamerosy culty mesano dayr fabelron cathurmo pean ersoty meor iather cabon Frilastro melrusy.

When the song has been said, the invisible messenger will make himself visible, and will faithfully reveal into the ear the secret entrusted to him.

*Chapter 25.*

*Whose emperor and prince is called Emoniel, having subordinate dukes, 100 princes, many counts as subservitors, who have general office over all things.*

[unclear]

The prince of this chapter, Emoniel, with his dukes and princes, is wandering and unstable; he frequently dwells in groves, does not flee the light, and is benevolent and ready for all things whatsoever you command him. Both by day and by night he gladly comes when called.

He has under his dominion 100 princes and dukes, no fewer than 20 counts or subdukes, and servants and slaves without any fixed number. From these we shall name some who may suffice for us in working, reserving the rest, with others, to be named in their place if there is need.
