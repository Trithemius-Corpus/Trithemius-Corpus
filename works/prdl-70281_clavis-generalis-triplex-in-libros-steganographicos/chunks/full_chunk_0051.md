*Chapter XXXI.*
*Whose supreme prince is called Bydiel, having under his dominion 20 dukes and 200 counts. The number of servants is uncertain: they are very many.*

[unclear]

In addition, one remains of the supreme Spirits, assigned to us for the operation of Steganography: last indeed in order, but among the first in dignity. His name, by reason of his office, is called Bydiel, having under his command 20 dukes, or 200 counts or sub-dukes, and many attendants and servants. These dukes, together with the counts, have a fixed order among themselves; and as often as they are called by the operator, two dukes come with 20 counts, and appear in human form, gentle and ready for all things. They alternate among themselves, so that when in the first year the dukes have been called, afterwards the counts are called; in the second year the counts are called in place of the dukes.

We now have here ten from the princes and counts of Bydiel, marked with their names, who suffice for us at present for every operation of ours in the art of Steganography, together with many servants, who know how to observe their order according to the command of the dukes and princes. But when you wish to operate in this art through them, after the preliminaries have been set forth, say the conjuration.
