Most Illustrious Prince, I inform Your Grace that Conrad Hummel, beneficed at the altar of St. John the Evangelist, died last night. But lest Your Grace, without deliberation, grant the said altar either to one of the unskilled or unworthy, of whom I think a great number will be present, as is usually the case, expecting the said altar, instead of reserving it for a more useful man, I beg that, although I am far too unworthy for my prayers to seem to obtain any favor in the sight of so great and noble a prince, Your Grace may deign worthily to grant the aforesaid altar to the bearer of the present letters, rejecting the less worthy. I have known him to be worthy of the best benefices, since I know him to be adorned with beauty of character, learned in every way in knowledge and letters, modest, continent, temperate, and one whose love of religion will be pleasing to Your Grace. And for the benefices bestowed he will not be ungrateful, but forever mindful. Given from Spanheim, on the day before the Ides of March, in the year of our Lord 1500. Johannes Trithemius.

When the prince, or another person to whom they are sent, skilled in the art, has received these letters of mine, or almost any others, once the sign of Dorothiel has been recognized, let him turn toward the choir, where that spirit himself dwells with his leaders and servants, doing all and each of the things that the art itself requires. Then let him silently call the spirit in a subdued voice, as he knows how:

Dorothiel onear chameron vlyfeor madufyn peony oriel nayr druse mouayr pamerson etro dumeson, dauor caho. Casmiel hayrno, fabelrunthon.

When this song has been completed, if the spirit delays in coming, let him read it again up to a third time; and without any doubt he will appear visibly and will reveal to the ear the things entrusted to him.

*Chapter XI.*

*Roth, whose supreme spirit is called Vsiel, dwelling toward that quarter of the world which, from the wind blowing thence, we call Subcircius; he has under him 40 leaders by day and as many by night, who are over treasure.*

The eleventh mansion of the whole world is called Subcircius, namely from the wind which is accustomed to come from that region. In this quarter dwells a great prince who is called Vsiel, having under him 40 princes who preside over operations by day, and 40 who preside over those by night. Their office is to announce hidden and subterranean treasures, and everything that seems to pertain to the reckoning of treasures. From these we shall name a few, and as many as are sufficient in this place for the operations of Steganography; the rest, together with the others, we reserve to be named in their proper chapter at the end of this book. And note that the leaders of this mansion, when they are called by the operator, willingly and under compulsion obey them in all things, if the operator is such that he knows how to call them duly, or even to compel them, if they should wish to appear too slowly.
