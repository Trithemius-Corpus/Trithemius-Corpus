*The tenth method begins by means of four nulls, proceeds, and ends with meaningful and idle letters.*

The tenth method begins from four idle letters. Through the same number of signs in order it proceeds after the likeness of the eighth in threes; that is to say, when the mystic word ends in a vowel, what follows is begun from the meaningful letters; but if it ends in meaningful letters, that is incorporated from four idle letters. But if fewer than four letters remain around the end, it can be concluded with the same number of idle letters, and also be omitted in the same number of meaningful letters. Let us set down an example of the tenth.

S.D. Since it is useful to know how to adorn a most honorable talent, and since we judge nothing more shameful to befall men than ignorance. The vanity of the world is always hostile to good and excellent talents, since it swallows a noble intellect in filth, clouds the mind, scatters memory, disturbs the senses, overthrows discipline, weakens the body, effeminates the spirit, and makes the citadel of religion and piety into woodland tales out of a man; it consumes all time in vain things, seeks the comfort of the flesh, and transgresses into destruction all the brightness of Catholic instruction; it does not revere God, it despises men, it forgets benefits, it is disturbed by the evils of conscience, it rejects eternal joys and venerates human glory, it feeds on vices, it is stained with filth and every kind of foulness,

*The eleventh method runs alternately through four, and concludes the word bound with meaningful letters by empty ones.*

The eleventh method is always begun from four empty letters, and, proceeding alternately through the same number of meaningful letters, is ended in idle letters equal to the last meaningful letters. For all things to be concealed take their beginning from four idle letters, and however many meaningful letters you find remaining beyond four, know that so many empty letters must be appended at the end. Let an example of this be set down.

*Example of the eleventh method.*

*S.D. I send you the oration that I delivered at the wedding of that same friend of mine: God, creator of all things, redeemer of the human race and best savior, consecrated even the use of marriage, lest the species itself perish. For we read that he also commanded marriage to the first man and said, Increase and multiply. He laid down the law for the preservation of humanity; and in the Gospel he deigned to sanctify weddings with miracles, those deserving well of God, preserving chastity.
