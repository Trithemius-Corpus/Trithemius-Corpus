He had a jealous wife, a lover of our religion, noble in humility and innocence, glorious in chastity. There survive some praiseworthy lucubrations of his of right instruction, and many sweet and abundant teachings: short letters, not useless, honorably and graciously exhorting us to seek eternal glory. On the eighth day before the Kalends of April, in the year 1500. Johannes Trithemius wrote this.

When you have received these letters, or any others whatever, he to whom they are sent, being beforehand skilled in the art of Steganography, once the sign of Macariel has been recognized, should do what is customarily to be done, adding the song.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Macariel osayr chamerose chulti pesano dayr fameron cathurmochamerose chault fameron cathurmo pean ersoty lamedon souapor casrea mafyr. Ianos tharfia. peathan onacri pean etion matramy. When the song has been completed, the spirit sent will appear visibly to him alone.

*Chapter 30.*

*Whose supreme emperor is called Uriel, having under his dominion 10 dukes, and 100 counts or sub-dukes. The number of servants is uncertain.*

But it is not fitting for us to omit Uriel, the great prince, whom we know, with experience teaching us, to possess no last place of usefulness among the supporters of this art. He has under his dominion and command 10 dukes and princes deputed to this art of Steganography; counts or sub-dukes, who always accompany those dukes, each in his own order, 100; many servants, whose number is still not certain to me.

These three letters are red.

Here we have the dukes named, with the counts and sub-servants, whose ministry is sufficient for us for the completion of this art of ours at present. And note that the first in order are dukes and princes. The second are counts, and they have two orders among themselves, which you must know before all else. Those who are in the first order, both dukes and counts, whenever they are called, always appear monstrous, namely with a maiden’s head, and with body and tail in the form of a serpent. Those who are in the second order are accustomed to appear to us in a familiar and usual likeness. Nor are fewer accustomed to come than one duke and one count following him, in either order. But when you wish to work through these spirits, first do according to what you know from the art must be done; afterward add this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Uriel marfoys lamedonti noes, chameron, anducharpean phusciel atsimony tuerchoy iameryn nairiel penos raseon loes veay fabeltruso cralty layr parlis meraij mear, thubra aslotiel dubyr reanu nauosti masliel pedonyto chemarphin. When the conjuration has been completed, the spirits called will be present, each in his order, ready and cheerful to obey in all things.

*A very great secret is made.*

Let there be some secret of mine for a prince or a friend, the disclosure of which would bring danger to me, no small loss to him. Therefore, so that all things may remain secret between me and him, I entrust the secret not to a man, not to letters, but to a spirit.
