*Method XL. It uses alternate words, and transfers each first significant letter into the twenty-first.*

*Method 40*, like the preceding ones, uses alternate words, empty and significant in the same place. The first letter of each significant word is transferred in order into the twenty-first, the next nearest following, which is the last of ours, so that for A, z is taken; for b, a; for c, b; and so consequently, as is clear in the alphabet of this fortieth method, which follows below. This method, however, like all the others, is varied into diverse other forms, so that it may use either single significant words, or alternate ones, or several.

Do penance, wretched people. Now, in a short time, you hardened ones will suffer the most grievous tribulations of our race from infidel men, and a most cruel death. The Turk, with a great army from Hungary, has attacked Christendom far and wide. Savage as beasts, the Turkish men [unclear] crossed the Danube, kindled as if by fire with zeal for impiety. Therefore let all men mourn, as those soon to die. Where is your zeal, most wretched Christians? Where your constancy and devotion? Far away, through wickedness, Christian kindness has [unclear]; [unclear] has abandoned the Catholic faith. Zeal and the fervor of divine love have gone far away; because of these things the enemies of the faith will kill and devour men like locusts. For we have heard how grievously the Turk, having invaded Hungary, Bohemia, and Poland, destroyed cities, and most shamefully violated infants, women, and gathered virgins, and consecrated women who out of zeal for God had preserved chastity, and horribly killed all Catholics whatsoever. Destroying all, his cruelty surpasses men and beasts. He will suddenly throw all Germany into disorder with his most warlike army unless a general defense of the princes resists without delay. Therefore, weeping, let us humble ourselves with the greatest contrition of souls, praying to God most earnestly that he convert us, mercifully forgiving [our sins?], freeing us from enemies who strive to hand our souls over to death. In the year of the Lord 1499, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of March.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim.
