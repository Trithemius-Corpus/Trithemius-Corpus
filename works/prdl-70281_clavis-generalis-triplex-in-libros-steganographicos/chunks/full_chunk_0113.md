*The hidden tenor of the foregoing.*

Do not heed my word; I do not mean it so harshly; it must now be so; I will tell you the reason.

*Mode XXV. Like the preceding, it admits all significant words, but transposes each to the sixth.*

Mode 25, like the preceding, admits no empty word, but all the words which it admits are significant, because there is no need to insert any idle words, since there is no one in this world who, without the art of this book, could discover the secret knowledge written in these modes. Nevertheless, whoever, for greater caution or for ease, wishes to insert alternately, or after two or more empty words, the same number of significant words, we do not forbid it, especially since we know the letter for each person, so that he may, at his own pleasure, either invent new ones, if he can, or alter these as he wishes. Yet we advise them not to neglect to put the signs of the modes in their proper place, lest he himself cause others to err, as we have done, if he writes in this mode; because in each one, and in this twenty-fifth, the letters are transposed to the following sixth, as we shall show in its place. There follow the twenty-five modes of the alphabet mentioned above, in which for A let g be taken, because it is the sixth after a; for b, c; i, d; k, e; l, f; m, g; n, h; o, i; p, k; q, t; r, m; s, n; t, o; u, p; x, q; z, r; a, s; b, t; c, a; d, x; e, z; f.

When, therefore, you wish to write something either in this mode or in the other modes, once the alphabet arranged for this purpose has been set before you, put the first significant letters, written on one sheet, before you, as we do below for an example.

*Example*

*Of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim*

*Example of the hidden secret.*

Keep all letters that I send you secret, and let no one see them. Now let us fashion a narrative for this hidden message.

*Example XXV.*

S D The humble, desiring grace with laments, obtain forgiveness; by the grace of redemption freedom is restored. But penitent men are mercifully set free. The precepts, the justification of piety by the most gracious giver of freedom, and all the things of the Catholic law, the most pious Savior, King of power, about to judge, has bestowed. To all who devoutly hold Catholic rectitude he has promised the joy of blessedness. Good power of freedom: those holding the holy law will rejoice at last as Catholics; they will kiss the most gracious liberator in tranquil light. Farewell, from Sponheim, 3 Nones of January, in the year of the Lord 1498. Jo. Trith., Abbot.

*Mode XXVI. It proceeds by alternate words, and each letter of the significant words is transferred to the second.*

Mode 26 proceeds by alternates like the second, first placing one empty word at the beginning, then another significant word, and so on successively to the end of the narrative. Moreover, for each first letter of the significant words, the seventh afterward in order is to be taken, namely: for A, h is taken; for b, i; for c, k; and so on in succession, as will be clear in the following alphabet, where the black letters are taken for the red.
