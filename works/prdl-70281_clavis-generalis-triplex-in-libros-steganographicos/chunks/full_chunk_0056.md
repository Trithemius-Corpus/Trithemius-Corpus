*Book Two.*

We now have, from the dukes of great Samael, four, and from the counts the same number; from the servants, from the threefold order, 4,440, whose ministry is abundantly sufficient for us for every operation of the art of Steganography, with respect to the first hour of the day.

Therefore, when you wish to operate in the art in the first hour, write first on a slip the character of the lord of the ascendant, then that of the moon; afterward those of all the remaining planets according to the customary order, and at the end the character of the sign ascending at that same hour. And when you have completed this, write on the back of the same slip this conjuration in silence, keeping silent:

*Conjuration.*

Samael afluar onayr myscolayar madiel cuhiel naniel nabruys satiel atharbiel nadian naslon, ranyalcoha pemarson.

When this conjuration has been written on the slip, as stated above, you will fit the letters to be sent, neatly, clearly, and distinctly, to him whom you wish to know the secret, in whatever form pleases you; for it makes no difference how the letters to be sent have been shaped, provided that they contain no secret in themselves. Then, once the letters too have been written, call one or two of the counts according to the order, if you are operating many times in this same first hour of the day, and say the conjuration prescribed from the same slip, with the strongest intention, fearing nothing. When it has been duly completed, the spirits called will immediately appear to you, visible, in a familiar form customary to themselves, peaceful, well-disposed, most ready for all services that arise. When they appear, entrust your secret to the chief one among them, saying: Ameniel, by the hidden power of this conjuration I command you to go as quickly as possible to Albert Goeler of Ravensburg, prefect in Kreuznach, and to tell him in my name thus or thus. When these words have been said, add the secret word, which must not be written; and immediately the spirit will fly away. Send the letters, sealed with the character of Samael, as is customary, by a messenger; let them contain no secret and fear no one’s inspection.

The secret is whatever occurs.

Let the secret be a great and difficult secret, whatever occurs at the time, and such that it ought to be communicated to no one, nor entrusted to letters or to messengers; for in this art, especially through the spirits of this second volume, one must not operate for small and trivial causes, which can otherwise be announced by letters.

*You will compose letters on these matters at will.*

The majesty of the Catholic faith, to be firmly preserved by all men, has promised ease of eternal life to those serving Christ. Hold the Catholic Christian faith; glorify the Almighty, fleeing the tumult of vanity. No one of depraved works will have the blessed life. Let the faithful man follow Christ in the path of virtues. Integrity is adorned by good works, and the Catholic name is preserved by Christian fidelity. For a depraved man darkens faith. Christ hates the rash, and loves Christians practicing Catholic works, venerating tranquillity. Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, published this on the Kalends of April, in the year of the Lord 1500[unclear].

When you have received the letters, let the person to whom they are sent, skilled in the art, first set forth those things that must be set forth from the general instruction of the art, and let him say the prescribed conjuration in that manner and rite in which we have said above that it was written on the slip, with the character of the lord of the ascendant and the other things expressed. But as soon as he has completed the conjuration, he will see the spirit present; when he has seen him, let him say fearlessly: + penador auenal + folmeniel phanu + fauear caschanti hernoty maduran. Amen. When these words have been said, let him be silent, and he will see the spirit approaching him and reporting the entrusted secret secretly and securely into his ear. Let him listen to him thus without fear and with great diligence, so that if anyone is sitting around him, he may not indicate by nod or word that the spirit is present.
