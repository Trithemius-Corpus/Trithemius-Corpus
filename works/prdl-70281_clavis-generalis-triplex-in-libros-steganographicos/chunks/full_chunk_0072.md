*Book Two.*

Here we have named, from among the dukes of Tartys, 6; from among the counts, 12; and servants also, numbered and distributed by turns and ranks, 1,320, who suffice for us at this hour for the experiment of the art. And note that, according to Solomon and Ratiel, all these dukes and princes of Tartys are benevolent and most ready for obedience; and in whatever form they have been ordered to appear, they gladly assume it. Therefore, when you wish to work in this science, observe carefully the lord of the ascendant, together with the other things which we prescribe, and write everything that must be written, with this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Tartys chrybes faziel yrsoti haelnot dreor adeuar afy mearo Veny satu pemerson. Then, when everything has been most diligently completed, and the conjuration read in the proper manner, the spirits summoned will soon be present, ready for your command.

*A secret is made, whatever occurs to us, great and arduous.*

The secret which we wish to communicate to a friend through spirits is made whatever may occur to us at the time and according to the variety of affairs, so that it is not trivial, nor of such a kind that it can otherwise be safely reported.

*Johannes Trithemius, to the nuns on Mount Saint Rupert, who adorn the Catholic faith with bright virginity, continuously to merit eternal happiness by the most holy zeal of religion.*

By the mercy of the eternal King, we providently send you, after he has been long awaited, a man of great experience in temporal affairs, Johannes, born of Raunenstat, for a long time reverend and zealous abbot of the monastery of Violsdorf, and freely resigning that same abbey. On his account, as I had promised you, I went to the monastery of Lunapurga, in which he was serving Christ, occupied with continual prayers and readings of the holy Scriptures, with the monks of that same monastery; and I asked that he come down to your convent and undertake the care of temporal affairs, and become procurator and cellarer. The abbot of Lympurg, with his usual instability, changed in mind, [unclear] I was able to obtain his consent to dismiss him. At last, by Christ’s inspiration, he consented, though fearfully, because he fears to serve nuns, who change very readily. He is not without reason afraid, since, as experience has taught us, nuns have sometimes repaid their stewards for benefits with a reward not praiseworthy. A feminine spirit suddenly [changes] into zeal of bitterness. Yet, with Christ granting it, I know that he will be useful to you. He is a Christian, he is a monk, he fears the Savior, and he is of good understanding. Therefore accustom yourselves to restrain feminine mutability, so that you may remember to be constant; receiving him with holy honor and sincere charity, laying aside every levity of feminine spirit, especially since, with the help of Christ the Redeemer, who is zealous for charity, he can assist you and restore the condition of temporal affairs collapsed in your monastery. Farewell. From Sponheim, 17 Kalends of May, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1500.

When the man to whom they are sent, skilled in our art, has received letters marked with the character of Tartys, let him observe the lord of the ascendant, and let him do, most diligently, each and every thing that must be done, and let him add the customary conjuration; and when he sees that the spirits sent are present, let him say these words to the principal one: Chabor maslotiel tuseuo reneas porean trismeny penarson. And when he has said these words with strong intention to the principal duke, immediately, approaching calmly, he will reveal the secret.
