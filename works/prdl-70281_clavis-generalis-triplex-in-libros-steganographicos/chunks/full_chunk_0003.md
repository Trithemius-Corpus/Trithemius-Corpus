*To the Reader, greetings.*

You are to be advised, candid reader, that of the letters R. and S. in this work, placed sometimes over numbers, sometimes over characters, sometimes over words, either superscribed on the page or added in the margin, the former is the mark of rubric, or red color, while the latter is the mark of ink, or black color. Moreover you will observe this too: the individual titles of the individual chapters are likewise to be depicted and adorned in rubric. You may seek the significance of this matter from the Key prefixed to the work. Farewell.

*Here begins the first book of the Steganographia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the diocese of Mainz, addressed to the most serene prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, and prince elector of the Holy Empire, etc.*

It is the opinion of the most learned that the most ancient sages, whom in Greek speech we call philosophers, if they had discovered any secrets either of nature or of art, concealed them by various modes and figures, lest they come to the knowledge of wicked men. That Moses too, the most famous leader of the Israelite people, in his description of the creation of Heaven and Earth covered over the ineffable secrets of mysteries with simple words, all the more learned among the Jews confirm. Saint Jerome also, most learned among our own, affirms that almost as many mysteries lie hidden in the Apocalypse of John as there are words.
