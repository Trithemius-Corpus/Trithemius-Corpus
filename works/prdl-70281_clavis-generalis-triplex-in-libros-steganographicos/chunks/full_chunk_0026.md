Of the forty princes of Usiel, who preside over daytime operations, you have 14, with their servants, 400 in number, and 14 who preside over nighttime operations. You see two signs set opposite one another, for testing and caution concerning the treasure, as you know, so that it cannot be found by thieves. We use them for guarding the treasure that has been found while the spirit is being sent to a friend. Therefore, when you wish to work in this business by means of spirits: if the business is very great or pertains to a prince, summon whatever dukes you wish from the order of fours; if it is your own, from the order of threes; if private and small, from the order of twos; if lowly and very slight, then let it be done at night in the order of solitaries only.

Vsiel parnothiel chameron briosy sthrubal brionear Caron sotronhi egypia odiel Chelorsy meair Chadusy notiel ornych turbelsi paneras thorthay pean adresmo boma arnotiel Chelmodyn drusar loy sodiuiel Carson, eltrae myre notiel mesraym Venea dublearsy mauecar melusyron chartulneas fabelmerusyn. After these verses have been spoken toward the north-west in silence, as time and place permit, do the other things that the art itself requires, and entrust the secret to the spirit now appearing, with the seal of the deep mystery, hesitating in nothing, since all those dukes are good and faithful.

Let us suppose that you have such a secret of the mind, to be entrusted to absolutely no one.

You have found a very great treasure in the coffers of the dead or in some other place, or you know for certain that it is secretly hidden. You alone are not able to carry it away, and yet you trust none of those dwelling nearby. You have a faithful friend, but absent far away, to whom you desire to reveal the mystery, but you trust neither letters nor messenger, lest through agitation you fall into exposure and danger, and lose the profit. Therefore you summon a spirit; he carries the message, summons the friend. He comes, helps, shares. Write the letters as you wish, in which there is absolutely nothing to be feared.

I send you an oration that I composed at the wedding of a certain friend of mine. God, creator of all things, redeemer of the human race, and savior of all, lest the human race should perish, also instituted the sacrament of marriage; for we read that he joined Eve as wife to the first man and said: INCREASE AND MULTIPLY, instituting the law for preserving humanity. Afterwards God incarnate deigned to sanctify marriages by a miracle. I shall praise those who serve God well by keeping the marital [unclear]; although I am not married, I shall nevertheless rightly praise marriages.

Indeed, if marriage were taken away, the whole human race would fail and collapse. For by the use of marriages the human race exists, although ruinous, weak, and corruptible; yet without marriages it would not endure. But act in such a way that the fruitfulness of your marriages is adorned with morals and conduct, holy, and placing a stumbling-block before no one. First of all love God, continually applying yourselves to works of mercy with justice; flee impiety, cultivate rectitude, love the good, always speak praises to God, assist the poor with alms, obey the commandments of God. Do not forsake truth; honor the ministers of God; pay tithes to the churches; receive pilgrims as guests; free the innocent; feed the hungry; obey your superiors; honorably love your neighbors, inflicting injury on absolutely no one. Cultivate the Christian religion most devoutly; abhor the vain, harmful, and superstitious customs of the gentiles. Keep the teachings of the Gospels and live according to the precepts of God. Flee the pleasures of the flesh, and show yourselves honorable and good; anxiously guard purity of mind
