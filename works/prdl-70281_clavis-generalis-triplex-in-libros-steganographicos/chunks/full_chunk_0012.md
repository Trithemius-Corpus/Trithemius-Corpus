*FIRST BOOK.*

*THE SECRET OF THE INTENTION, WHICH [unclear] is entrusted not to letters, but to spirits of the second dwelling.*

[unclear]

Someone has been denounced to the prince, against whom some crime of lèse-majesté, or any other crime, is alleged. He wishes him to be punished by his official, from whom he is far separated. But he does not wish to write this very thing to him, lest, if the letters are disclosed by any chance, the accused man be warned and flee, or resist after joining to himself the forces of his friends, or contrive some hostile act against the prince or the laws of the country. He writes this or any other narrative whatever to his prefect, one which he need not fear being read by everyone; but the secret he entrusts to a spirit, in the manner we have described above, which will faithfully tell his secret to the man to whom it is sent, who has first been instructed in this art.

Sincere charity. I send you a prayer, for the devotion of penitents, rather beautifully composed and brief, which the whole goodwill of my mind toward you did not wish to possess alone; and it is as follows. Deliver us, Jesus Christ, eternal Savior of all good things, and forgive the offenses of sinners, and hear our groans, calming the storms of vices and renewing the oldness of conscience; lead us back to eternal paradise. Good Jesus, forgive us our sins as we beseech you, since we have grievously offended and have often fallen back. Sweetest Savior, heal our weaknesses, we pray. Be kind to those who pray to you, most dutiful Jesus, and hear us, your servants, as we supplicate you. We are human beings, wretchedly entangled in continual miseries and sunk in the greatest storms. Rescue us, most victorious comforter of the afflicted, since our life, continually buried in frailty, is corrupted. Have mercy at last, most merciful Jesus, Savior; look upon the humility of those who love you, and do not permit us, exiles, to be drowned in the mire and foulness of vices, in the love of the world. Until now we have lain sluggish, and, bound by vices, we have made little account of our salvation and have been scarcely mindful of the coming judgment. Most kind Creator, be propitious to your servants, and save those weeping ones whom the most wretched vanity of the world has deceived. Alas, we have all neglected the way of Truth; but deliver us, fixed in the mire and exiles as we are, since you are our Savior, Jesus, who despise no one. You are the unfailing joy and everlasting happiness of the saints, our hope, the glory of Christians, the light of angels, the rewarder of undefeated martyrs, the crown of virgins, the honor of widows. Good Jesus, hear us, humble [unclear].
