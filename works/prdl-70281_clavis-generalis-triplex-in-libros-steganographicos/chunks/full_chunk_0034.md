*Book One.*

To those wandering [miserably?], what could be more salutary or sweeter than to apply oneself diligently to letters? Through the study of them our eyes are raised toward the truth that is to be sought, and our mind is lifted up toward desire for future blessedness; without them the soul, sunk in temporal and worldly affairs, is compared to [unclear], never to arrive at the knowledge of God. Therefore despise the pleasures and tumults of the world, and devote yourself day and night to letters, since both religion is [unclear] by virtues and virtues are illumined by commerce with letters. Thus neither letters without the adornment of noble virtues [unclear], nor good virtues without knowledge of the Scriptures can prevail. And so I exhort you always to give your effort to the study of letters. Farewell from Spanheim, on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of April, in the year of the Lord 1500.

After the one to whom the letters are sent has received them, when the sign of Barucha has been recognized, and after the things to be sent beforehand have been sent beforehand, let him, toward the quarter of Vulturnus, which is nearest before the eastern one, say this Conjuration in silence.

*Conjuration of the spirit.*

Baruchas Mularchas chameron noriel pedarfys phroys lamasay myar chalemon phorsy fabel ontho theras capean Vear alronym lierno medusan thersiel peatha thumar nerosyn cral nothiel peson segalry madon scoha bulayr. When the conjuration has been spoken according to the precept of the art, the spirit will appear and will bring the secret entrusted to him to the ear, without deception.

*Chapter 17.*

*Whose prince and supreme spirit is called Camesiel, and dwells in the East, having under him many dukes and princes with their servants, whose office is to announce everything that is sent toward the East.*

Since there are so many affairs of men, and more can arise, for the communicating of which to distant friends no prince or spirit has been specially appointed in the aforesaid mansions, lest any difficulty in the perfection of their work should arise for those operating in this art of Steganography, after we have made mention of certain special ones who preside over special secrets, we wish also to set forth certain general princes of spirits for announcing general matters. Thus, if perhaps something occurs to someone that must be announced, concerning which no commission is found in the aforesaid matters, it may be referred to these, who in their own way have commission over all things, yet in such a way that generality does not derogate from specialty, nor a specialty not sufficiently clearly expressed refuse the office of generality.

There are therefore four great princes who, with their dukes and subservants, preside over all secrets to be announced that have not been expressed above. Of these the first, Camesiel, presides over all things that we wish to announce toward the East; Demoriel over those things that are sent generally toward the North. Therefore, when you wish to announce some secret by means of a spirit to a friend far away, absent toward the East, call one or more, as many as you wish, from the princes of Camesiel, turning yourself throughout the whole operation toward the eastern quarter, as you know according to the art.
