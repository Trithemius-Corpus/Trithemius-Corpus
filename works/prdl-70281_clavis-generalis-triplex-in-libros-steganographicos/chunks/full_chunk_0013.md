*The First Book. Chapter III.* Whose prince [is] Camuel, having under him 10 spirits presiding over the daytime operations and 10 likewise over the nocturnal, with as many under-servants, whose office is to announce the coming of the one who sends.

The first and supreme of all the spirits of the third mansion, toward the south-east, is called Camuel, very benevolent and prompt to send his substitutes to him who operates in this science according to the due form. He has indeed few ministers, but all good, willing, and faithful—namely ten who preside over the day and as many who preside over the night—who are always sent in pairs to the operator from either order; since those who preside over the day neither flee the darkness, nor those who preside over the night the light. Always, where necessity demands, in every commission of the operator the one is helped by the other; and when they appear visibly, then one is seen in a beautiful habit of mixed color, and the other is wont to stand by in the form of a bright splendor. Often, however, they appear by a sensible effect only, and are not seen, except when it has so pleased the operator. This Camuel, with his ministers and under-servants, has the authority and power of announcing to an absent friend the state, will, condition, way, journeys, coming, and departure of the sender, whenever he himself who operates in this art shall wish. When, therefore, you wish, and operate according to your will, through the angels of this mansion, it behooves you to know first of all who are the ten angels of Camuel who preside over the day, and how many substitutes each of them has, and likewise so of the nocturnal [ones]; which, that you may know perfectly, you will consider the present table.
