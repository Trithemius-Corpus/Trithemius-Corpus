*Arrangement for Sending a Secret Message.*

When you see the spirits standing before you in a strange form, do not be afraid, because they are good and benevolent, harming no one, and they almost always conceal a monstrous form, lest they terrify anyone, appearing with a very beautiful face. Safely entrust whatever, in the hour of the night, you wish [to write to a friend?]. Let the letters be formed as you please.

O wretched man, and truly pitiable, why do you not attend to the instability of all temporal things? And why do you not consider that you will shortly die? Remember what the first man deserved because he violated the commandment. Would that, as Balaam wished, our soul might die the death of the righteous, so that, scorning present pleasures and the vain honors of the world, we may be unstained, keeping ourselves from every sin. But we do not take care, until at last we fall into the dreadful bonds of death, to be tormented forever. I consider happy the man who thinks of these things beforehand. Therefore, O man, cast away the world, as the Son of God did, and consider how many evils follow us from love of it. For many dangers are brought upon us, and because it is damnable to those who love it, it is rightly despised. Worthy of eternal death is the lover of the world. What does that world profit you, wretched man? *Chapter 25.*

*In which we shall teach the general method and form of approaching this art of Steganography, and of operating in it without danger, with the greatest usefulness.*

Whoever desires to approach the knowledge of this hidden art of ours; whoever wishes to work wonders through it, and things useful to many amid dangers: first of all he must be adorned with virtues, and be of clean conscience and good will toward God, toward himself, and toward his neighbor, so that he is not inclined to harm anyone, nor seeks dealings of baseness. Then it is necessary that he be somewhat imbued with the good arts of letters, and especially with the science of the stars, so that he may know the general motions, courses, wanderings, changes, orders, natures, positions, risings, settings, and effects of the stars, signs, and planets; because without competent knowledge of these no one can have access to the depths of this art. Consequently it is necessary that he have a teacher perfect and experienced in the art, because we believe it possible to come to this science without a teacher only for a few, and for those most learned in many things, and especially skilled in magical matters. But the teacher must be not only proven and experienced in knowledge, but also faithful, upright, and fearing God; because the purer he has been in good conduct with God, the safer he will be in the operation of this science.
