It is not fitting for a good and honorable man, born of noble parents and ancestors, to violate a promise. You had promised, however, to restore them very soon and with the greatest diligence. It is fickleness to put off a promise, as it were, to the Greek Kalends. Have I not waited long enough with great goodwill? To promise very great things is most shameful for one who returns the least. Good faith adorns a soldier. What more serious crime than to refuse a loaned thing promised for return? Nor is it fitting for a Christian to be unwilling to keep an agreement. The ordinance of Christian law commands one to keep a promise, to return a loan; what you desire to be done to you, you should gladly do also to others. I demand back my books, which I had lent to you at your entreaty. Put aside the longer excuses, which you promise for the Greek Kalends. You know my mind in brief. Consider your name and the honorable family of the Bunawenses, and send my volumes back to me. Farewell. From Spanheim, 2 April, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When you have received these letters, or any others whatsoever, from him who sent the spirit to you, after recognizing the sign of Barquiel, observe in that same hour the lord of the ascendant; write his character, together with the others, on a clean sheet. You will read secretly the written conjuration on the back, as is proper; at once the spirit that was sent will appear. As soon as you have seen him, say these words to him:

Mefarym burne theoralueas casuean cralti lierto aply charmoys.

When these have been said, the sent spirit, coming nearer, will faithfully reveal to you the secret committed to him. But see that you are strong, steadfast, and undaunted in all things.

*Chapter VIII.*

*Whose hour is called Iafanym, and whose angel is called Osmadiel; he has under his command 10 dukes, 100 counts. The number of servants is uncertain.*

The eighth hour of the day is called Iafanym; its principal spirit is named Osmadiel, according to Picatrix in the magical writings. He has under his command 10 dukes, 100 counts, and innumerable servants, assigned not only to the operation of our Steganography, but also to many magical experiments. And Solomon the Jew, surnamed Hermes, says in the book On the Nature of Spirits that all those dukes and counts obedient and subject to the aforesaid Osmadiel are accustomed to come in various transformations according to the will of the operator, just as they have been commanded by the operator. But now let us set down the names of some of them.

Sarfiel, Demaros, Mariel: 10, 10, 100, 100.
Amalym, Ianofiel, Remasyn: 10, 10, 100, 100.
Chroel, Larfuty, Theoriel: 10, 10, 100, 100.
Mesial, Vemael, Framion: 10, 10, 100, 100.
Lantribotz, Thribiel, Ermiel: 10, 10, 100, 100.

Of the dukes five, and of the counts ten, we now have named in the table; they suffice for us for every operation of this hour. When, however, you wish to work through them in Steganography, write on a clean slip with the customary characters and conjurations; and, first adding the following things to those that must be added, speak the written conjuration itself, which is as follows.

*Conjuration.*

O Smodael aneor ersotynes as, hays layr Caphrayn thelreas meair penarsy.

When this song of conjuration has been spoken, the spirits called will be present, ready and obedient for all things. Entrust the secret to the duke, not to the count, unless the former is absent, because it must always be entrusted to the more principal one among those who come.
