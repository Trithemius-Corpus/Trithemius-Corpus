*BOOK ONE.*

Let us set down as an example some secret that is to be committed to absolutely no one.

You have some very secret business for a friend, which concerns you and him; the disclosure of it would bring perpetual loss or disgrace upon you and him. Certainly it is not safely entrusted to letters, lest they be read by someone, nor is it entrusted to a messenger, who will spread it among many. You use our art; thus both you will be safe, and your friend will know the secret.

Let us form for this purpose whatever little letter we please, in no way at all suspicious.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, to the excellent young man Jacobus Trithemius, his very dear brother, sends greeting.

Devoting yourself to letters, preserve humility of heart. Good morals adorn young men and make them more renowned than the noble. The knowledge of letters adorns a young man who loves holiness; without morals, knowledge will lack the honor of justice. A young man immersed in the vanities of vice [unclear], a despiser of wisdom, does not love true wisdom, which you should remember must be sought through virtues. The instruction of youths to be educated is adorned by morals, since heavenly honor is conferred by holy arts, and fruitless knowledge will be tormented with eternal punishments. Knowledge advances through virtues and letters; each fails when stained by vices. Scorn pleasures, so that by experience you may understand the truth of wisdom. Farewell.

The recipient of these letters, skilled in the art of Steganography, as soon as he has seen the character of the prince Symiel, understands at once by comprehension what he must do; and, turning to the north, after placing before it what must be placed before it, he says the conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Symiel marlos chameron pyrcohi pean fruary fabelrontigacelto siargoti melassor hialbrany ordu Casmeron omer vemabon. When the song has been completed, the spirit that has been sent, appearing, will faithfully fulfill the office entrusted to him.

*CHAPTER XV.*

*OF WHICH THE SUPREME EMPEROR IS ARMADIEL, who dwells in that part of the world from which the north wind is accustomed to blow, having under himself many princes.*

The great emperor Armadiel inhabits the fifteenth mansion toward the northern region, having under his rule many dukes and princes with their servants, from whom one hundred have been appointed over operations of day and night without distinction, so that all preside equally over our nocturnal and diurnal actions in Steganography, according to the will of the operator. Their office is to announce the operator’s most secret mysteries to princes and great men with the greatest fidelity and mystery. From these we shall name some of the more important, who are sufficient for our purpose.

You now have, from the spirits of Armadiel, fifteen in number, with their subservants, 1260, who, according to the division of the twenty-four hours into six parts, are accustomed, when called, to come to the operator with their dukes, if he wishes, preserving their order in coming. Therefore, when you wish to perform some operation in Steganography through them, you must observe most diligently the division of time according to this art, without which you will achieve absolutely nothing; when it has been known, as it should be, you will turn yourself to the north, doing what the art requires.
