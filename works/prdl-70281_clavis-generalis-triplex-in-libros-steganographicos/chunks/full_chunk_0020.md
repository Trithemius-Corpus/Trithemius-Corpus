We send letters for this reason to him to whom the spirit also is to be sent: because none of the princes or servants from among those spirits comes unless he is called, either to the sender or to him to whom he is to be sent. Therefore, so that you may know to whom it is sent, from what quarter of the mansion, and what spirit he ought to call, the letters are sent sealed with the sign of the prince, and written in such a way that they delight the spirit and he desires to be refreshed by their sustenance. *Book One.*

*Chapter VIII.*

*Whose Supreme Prince, Maseriel, dwells toward that quarter which we call Favonius, having under him sixty chief dukes, with their many servants.*

Roth.

The eighth mansion is around the west, in that place from which the wind is called Favonius; and there dwells the great prince who is called Maseriel, with an infinite multitude of dukes, princes, and attendants. From these, thirty princes have been appointed to this art, who preside over daytime operations, and just as many who preside over nighttime operations, with many servants. All these are appointed to announce and report the secrets of the human arts, of Philosophy, Magic, Necromancy, and of all marvelous and most secret operations, which are known to very few men; and in these matters they are ready and very faithful, nor do they return unless they have completed everything that had been entrusted to them. They are quiet and come without noise, as they are commanded, by themselves with many or with few, or even without attendants, at least visibly; they terrify no one except him who, not instructed in the art, has presumed to call them by the true principles of the art, which no one easily reaches without an instructor. But so that you may have at hand whom you should call for the operation of the spirit, know that not all are necessary for you, but only some; and therefore twelve are sufficient for you for actions that are done by day, and just as many for those that are done by night, with their servants.
