*Argument of a secret message.*

Let it be any secret whatever, very great and secret, which you wish absolutely no one to know except one person. Call the spirits deputed for the hour, as many as you wish, and entrust the secret to the principal one. Write letters in whatever form you wish, in which nothing secret is contained, and which should fear no one’s sight at all.

John Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, to Rutger Vencaius the Sicambrian, canon of the order of the divine father Augustine of the monastery of Dameten, greeting.

Those matters which you proposed to me to be resolved, with Christ’s help, I have resolved. Whether well, you who proposed them shall pronounce judgment. I shall briefly report to you an astonishing event that took place. A certain brother recently reported to me, as did one friend, not the least, that a young man had been raised from the dead at the invocation of Christ, reporting wondrous and dreadful things among the lower regions, narrating the torments of wicked Christians, namely of those who by acting perversely crucified Christ; that they were especially guilty of this, that, forgetful of the benefit of most gracious redemption, they always showed themselves ungrateful to Christ; and that those suffer dreadful things who, known to be ungrateful for his benefits by not living well, and blinded by sin, neglected future joys while following the pleasures of the world. They, bitten by the negligence they had committed, cried out most bitterly: Deservedly we endure these things, we who were always ungrateful to the redeemer of the human race: Christians in name, heathens in deed. We wretches always sought earthly consolations; therefore we receive the due recompense of eternal damnation. Alas, most unhappy that we are: why did we offend Christ? Why did we despise the time granted to us? The raised young man reported to us many deep things, which I am utterly unable to write, especially being weighed down by serious business. And therefore, brother, let us zealously imitate the footsteps of Christ, always giving thanks to our most gentle Savior and asking that he forgive us the guilt we have committed. Farewell. 6 Kalends of April.
