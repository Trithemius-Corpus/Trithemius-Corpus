*BOOK TWO.*

*CHAPTER II.*

*THE SECOND HOUR OF THE DAY IS CALLED CEVORYM.* Its prince and supreme spirit is Anael, who has under his command 10 dukes, counts, 100 presiding spirits. There is no certain number of servants and sub-servants.

The presiding spirit and emperor of the second hour of the day is principally called Arael, who has under his command, deputed to the service of this art of Steganography, 10 dukes, counts, centurions, and 100 presiding spirits over all his spirits. As yet I have no certain number of the servants. The second hour of the day, however, is called Cevorym, in which changeable operations are performed, astonishing to all men. All the spirits of this emperor are quite benevolent, pleasant, cheerful, and most ready to obey the practitioner in the art, provided he is skilled in the art, strong in spirit, constant, and well understanding.

R. R. R.

Menarchos Orphiel Quofiel 10 100 G. n.
Archiel Cursiel Ermazsel 10 100 G. n.
Chardiel Elmeym Granyel 10 100 G. n.

In this table we have set down 3 from among the dukes of Arael, six from among the counts, and 339 from the number of the servants, who, by being sent through their orders, suffice for us for every operation of Steganography in this second hour of the day.

When, therefore, in this hour you wish to perform something in Steganography, write on a clean sheet the character of the lord of the ascendant, and, in the order following it, the other planets, and finally the sign of the twelfth house, with ink made from the material of this art. Then write on the back of the aforesaid sheet this conjuration in silence, with your face turned toward the sun.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Anael otiel aproisy rachymas thulnear layr meu ear theor cralnotiel amersoty moueay phroys lierto mear vrnesa elty famelron.

When the conjuration has been written, do the other things which you know must be done according to the art. Then speak the song in silence; you will soon see the spirit present, cheerful, pleasant, and benevolent in every respect; to him you may safely entrust the secret.

*Let us posit any secret whatsoever.*

Let the secret for us be of whatever kind occurs at the time, which seems neither profitably to be committed to letters nor to a courier. Let one duke-count from the spirits of Anael, or another of them, as you please, be summoned. Let the secret be entrusted to the chief one in the due manner, as is proper according to the precept of the art; he will carry it faithfully.

*The tenor of the letters is made familiar.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, to Wilhelm Veldicus, canon of Saint Augustine in the valley of Franconia, his fellow philosopher, greetings.

I would rejoice to answer your letter, so may Christ make me rejoice, and I would send the volumes requested, if at last I had them. For I have been zealous with a good zeal, such as the most ancient philosophers had, for the greatest and weightiest hidden treatises of nature.
