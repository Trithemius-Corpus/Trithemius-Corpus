*Precautions to Be Observed*

But it must be observed that the aforesaid method, as we have said, is not sufficiently safe in serious matters, since as soon as any suspicion begins to arise about the letters, the secret can easily be detected by a clever person. Therefore the following methods are safer, as is clear. Likewise it must be observed, if by this or other methods you write to someone not sufficiently learned in the Latin language, that he should not divide the words, lest [unclear]. If you wish, you may use two points; I fill each word of your secret so that, when you have made a complete sentence of the outer text, you may make a little stroke thus, or the reverse, as you like. Yet it seems safer that neither points nor little strokes be made; and let the sign of a period, of whatever method, be placed not in the word of the secret, but rarely; place it neither over the first letter nor after the last, just as I did above in the hidden tenor: Dear fa[unclear]ithful one, etc.

*Extension of This First Method*

The first method itself of writing secretly, like nearly all the others, can be varied with manifold diversity, so that a person who perfectly understands the aforesaid order of concealing may still be able to perceive only with difficulty other methods to be inferred from it. For anyone can vary this method, together with the others, for his own book: namely, so that the first line is conscious of the secret, the second not; or, if it is of such size, that one is not; and so consequently the concealer of secrets, as he wishes, is alternated in alternate lines, either whole or half, by descending from the beginning of his letters to the end, or by ascending from the end to the beginning. I add also that from either side, descending and ascending [unclear] another beside the sense of his hidden [unclear] can be varied by powers so many and so diverse that, in explaining them, there is need of an Oedipus. But my present purpose is to show first the simple methods of writing, and to give to those who are worthy and suitable to know many things an occasion by which they may be able elegantly to devise the greatest things from the smallest.

*The Second Method, Prudently Varied by Alternate Words, Easy and Very Safe in Every Language*

The second method of writing hidden things without suspicion is that the words of the narrative of the openly proposed text be varied by alternate position: namely, so that when the first letter of the first word bears witness to the hidden intention, the first letter of the following word is free from the duty of the secret office. Afterwards the third is not free from the ministry, the fourth remains idle, and so consequently the words, mixed always in alternate turns, serve the secret, while those suited to the public discourse withdraw in prudent order from the signification of hidden things. And this method, easy in every language of the world, contains [unclear] and seems to me sufficiently safe for announcing whatever secrets you wish. Its example is as follows.
