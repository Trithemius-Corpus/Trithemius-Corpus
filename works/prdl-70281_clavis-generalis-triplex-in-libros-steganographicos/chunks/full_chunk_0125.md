Z. As we are about to praise the exalter of our souls, let us continually approach in divine love, desiring whatever useless slanders, empty vanities, eternal joys; behold, all things pass away except God and justice. Why do we love this life and desire to be plunged into destruction? Why do we lead ourselves away? *The fifth method uses individual signifying letters, and each letter is changed into the fifth before it.*

The fifth method of this second chapter, like the preceding one, admits no empty words, but the mystery proceeds through each of the signifying words. Nor do I believe that anyone ignorant of this art can ever discover things hidden by these methods, since even for one who knows the art but is not well practiced it becomes difficult to penetrate everything. The letters hidden in all the words of the mystery run through to the fifth immediately preceding, so that for a is taken [unclear], which is in the circle of the alphabet.

62 *Key of Steganography: Example of the Fifth.*

G. Standing by the grains of the mind, we humbly [unclear] your letters; new ones. Zealots of the Trinity, grant us honorable good zeal; evil zeal pursues, does not work. Most merciful Christ, we humbly ask, father, come to our aid. Alas, I am heavily burdened by grave diseases; O best redeemer, grant pardon to the wretched. Johannes Trithemius, abbot, set this down on the seventh day before the Kalends of March, in the year 1500.

*The sixth method proceeds through all the signifying words, and each letter is placed in the sixth preceding.*

The sixth method of this chapter also proceeds through all the signifying words after the fashion of the previous one, admitting no word that is wholly empty; and all the letters of the secret mystery are transposed backward, so that before A the sixth preceding in the little wheel is taken, which is r; for b, s; for c, t; for d, [unclear]; for e, x; for f, z; for g, A; and so consequently according to the order of the alphabet set down in the little wheel itself. By means of the number placed beneath the alphabet it can easily and without labor be written how far each letter stands from another, and how far it is in the order of each alphabet.
