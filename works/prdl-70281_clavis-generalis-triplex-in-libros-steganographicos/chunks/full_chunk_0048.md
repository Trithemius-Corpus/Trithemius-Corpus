*Steganography.*

*Chapter XXVIII.*

*Whose supreme spirit and commander is Menachiel, who has under him twenty princes or leaders, one hundred counts, and servants of uncertain number, who obey their own leaders.*

[unclear]

Although the things we have said seem to us to suffice abundantly for every operation of Steganography, nevertheless, lest we consign to oblivion those things which we have experienced, it has pleased us to add still some further matters, which, by the revelation of the aforesaid spirits, we have learned to be most excellent for the perfection of this art, and have experienced quite fully. Thus, among the other spirits who favor this art, there is one who has obtained the name Menadiel by reason of his office, who has under him twenty princes and leaders, one hundred counts, and many servants, whose number is uncertain to me; all of them are faithful in commissions and the best messengers in the great affairs of kings and princes, through whom we shall accomplish wonders.

Now we have named six of the leaders, and the same number of the counts, and also, from the servants deputed to them by turns and in their own orders, 390 in number. They have this order among themselves: that at the first turn two leaders and one count are called; at the second turn he is called again, and now comes into the order of the leaders. At the third turn the man becomes count who had been the first leader of the first order, and so consequently. Therefore, when you wish to perform something through the spirits of Menadiel, consider carefully which leader and which count must be called by you according to the time and hours; and, after the preliminaries that must be performed have been performed, say the conjuration.

When this conjuration has been duly completed, the called spirit will be present, namely first one count, two leaders with servants.

*The secret is made, whatever presents itself.*

Let your secret be for a male friend or female friend, whatever presents itself according to the time or the variety of circumstances, which you do not wish to become shared with anyone else. Call the spirit, entrust the business to him, fear nothing; he is the most faithful of all, and will carry out well what has been entrusted.

*You will be able to compose letters on these matters, such as you wish, according to your pleasure.*

Great and eternal are the things that will be for the advantage of those who observe innocence: for men rejoicing to be present at angelic praises; to behold that lofty light; the splendor of eternal blessedness, and to see the face of majesty by its own light. Pay attention, all of you, and wipe away past negligences with tears; put off useless cares and harmful ones; shudder, O muddy men, at the deceptions of unstable things; at last seek the profit of souls.
