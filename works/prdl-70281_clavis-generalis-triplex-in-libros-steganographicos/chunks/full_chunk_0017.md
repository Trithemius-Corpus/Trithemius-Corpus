By living justly we shall attain happiness; for only the humble are to be exalted, only the rash to be condemned. If you seek happiness, hold fast eternal justice; you will obtain the highest glory. Be a most fervent zealot for truth; do not love falsehood, corrupting honesty and dissolving the moderation of the soul. Flee the vanity of the world, since it is very brief. Be a lover of Justice, supplanting no one unjustly. Cultivate the virtues, flee vices; justice [unclear] freedom; beware all insolence; always be humble. Love the Savior of our souls: he who loves him will by no means die unhappily. Despise glory that passes suddenly away; love the blessedness of the saints, not to be corrupted eternally. Carefully flee the snares of our ancient enemy, whom the humility of devotion, never to be interrupted, will conquer. Be watchful, so that you are not overcome by the heat of the flesh; defend manfully the paths of justice, and you know that the guilty man of rectitude is subject to everlasting evils. Every proud man is hateful to God: dying, he will be tortured without interruption and will never be consoled. What, finally, will the proud expect except lamentation? Therefore woe to you who are exalted, worthy of eternal punishment and reserved for everlasting darkness and horrors. O most pernicious vanity of vanities. Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, wrote this on the Ides of March, 1500.

When you receive the letters, once Barmiel’s sign has been recognized, let him immediately turn toward the south, or as soon as he can. For no danger can arise from delay, as regards the spirit’s obedience, even if much delay has intervened. For when they call, he is accustomed always to come, whether quickly or late, even if they call after many days. This is his Conjuration, after those things have first been done which the art requires to be done.

*Chapter VI.*

*Whose supreme prince, Gediel, dwelling in the south-southwest, has under him twenty by day, and as many by night, with their servants, who have the office of announcing those things which, foreseen, contain dangers.*

Over the sixth mansion, which is in the south-southwest, presides the great prince Gediel, who has under his dominion twenty other chief dukes by day, and as many by night, with their many servants. Their office is to announce all things that pertain to warning a friend of any imminent future danger whatever, especially to princes, for the defense of country, castles, and cities; to announcing all things that profit friends, those whom we favor, for their safety; and also against enemies and any adversaries whatever. For all these things it is not necessary to summon the twenty princes, but two suffice with their servants. So that you may know them by name, I shall accordingly list some of them, namely eight for the day, eight for the night.

First, before we proceed to working, you should know that at least two spirits must always be called, with their twenty attendants, without whom they proceed nowhere, although not always do all appear visible. But there is no need to be concerned about this, provided that through their invisible ministry you obtain what you intend. Therefore when you wish to work in Steganography through their ministry, turn yourself toward the south-southwest, since all of them remain there at all times with their prince Gediel; do what you know must be done according to order.
