*Preface.*

... the letters of the world, Latin, Greek, or barbarous, free from all suspicion, by which it would present one thing openly to those ignorant of the art, and another secretly to those who knew it. Nor, if this science were published among the reprobate, would the trust between married persons, contracted by sacrament, any longer remain safe thereafter: since a wife, even if hitherto ignorant of the Latin tongue, yet now sufficiently instructed, through modest, honorable, and most holy words of any language or idiom, could understand most fully the evil and immodest mind and intention of an adulterous lover or fornicator, even while her husband carried the letters and praised them as excellent; and she could, in the same way, as broadly and copiously as she wished, most safely communicate her own desire to him in the same or other letters, in a beautiful and quite highly adorned sequence. And public trust could be endangered; all letters, documents, writings, and finally the very knowledge of men could come [unclear] (which God forbid); the whole order of the commonwealth, with the passage of time, could be disturbed in no small degree.

Nor can anyone, even if he lived as many years, ever become so learned and experienced in this science with his teacher that there would not be left infinite methods, secretly, most hiddenly, and most safely sufficient in this very art of writing, and, at the will of any experienced person, of working for all purposes, which he himself has not yet grasped with his preceptor. For just as the aerial spirits, good and evil, created by the highest God for our ministry and benefit, through whose understanding all the secrets of this art are revealed, are countless, infinite, and utterly incomprehensible to us, so too [unclear] of this art of ours, which [is called] secret Steganography...
