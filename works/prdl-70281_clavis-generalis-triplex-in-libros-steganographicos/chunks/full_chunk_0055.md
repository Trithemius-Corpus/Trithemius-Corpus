Therefore, taking up, for each single hour both of day and of night, the principal spirits appointed by the secret of Solomon, surnamed Hermes, in his volume *On Magic*, as the supreme emperors of the art, together with the other officials necessary to us for the operation proposed, we shall proceed; and we shall give both method and instruction to all students in this art, by which at any hour they may disclose the secret and hidden conception of their minds to friends with safer counsel. For each prince presides like a watchman over his spirits, dukes, counts, and servants; at his command, when summoned by us with a lawful song, they come; they carry the entrusted secrets, and show themselves obedient and ready for us in all things. But since I am going to mention spirits again, I think I must preface once more: nothing in this art of ours is held to be frivolous; nothing contrary to evangelical tradition or the Catholic faith; nothing at all superstitious is handed down. For everything that we have said in the preceding volume, or shall say in those that follow, is supported by natural, lawful, and honorable principles; and the mystery veiled only by foreign institutions, and words wrapped in the names of spirits, require a learned reader. For we use the service of a spirit to veil a secret which, if published, would harm the reprobate. And lest anyone fail through error in the reckoning of the hours, let him know that we have accepted the hours of the planets both by day and by night.

*Chapter I.*

*Samael, supreme emperor, presides over the first hour of the day; he has under his command many dukes, counts, and servants, whom, when called in this hour only, he sends to one experienced in working in Steganography.*

The emperor-spirit of the first hour of the day, which takes its beginning at sunrise, is called Samael. He has under his command many dukes, counts, and subordinate servants; when summoned by the operator in this hour only, in Steganography, they come. They carry the secret that is entrusted to them, but they utterly refuse to come outside the hour. And note that Samael's first dukes, the princes, are rarely sent by him into the service of this science, since they hold office in stirring up men's magical nocturnal illusions. But the counts, with their attendants, have been specially appointed to the service of this art; into this they are also more often sent without the presence of the dukes. Since they are from the highest order among spirits, they are accustomed to show themselves somewhat proud and rebellious to those who summon them, nor do they easily obey anyone, unless he is most skilled in the art of Steganography, without labor and danger. For they are impudent, very ready for illusions and mockeries of men, and they especially deride and jeer at those whom they find to be less skilled in the art of Steganography. But those whom they consider bold, steadfast, and experienced in that art, they respect and fear, and they always obey their commands most promptly, with trembling reverence. And so now we shall report the names of those counts who are necessary to us in the operations of Steganography in this hour.
