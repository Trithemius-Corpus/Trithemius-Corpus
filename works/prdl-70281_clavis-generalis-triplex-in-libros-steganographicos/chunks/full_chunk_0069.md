*Chapter XII*

*Whose great emperor of spirits has under his dominion many princes, dukes, counts, and servants. The hour is called Naybalon, and its spirit Beratiel.*

[unclear]

The twelfth hour of the day is called Nahalon, and its supreme angel is called the emperor Beratiel, who, according to Solomon the Jew, has under his empire almost innumerable spirits appointed for various operations. From all these, different spirits in different orders are assigned to the operation of steganography. In the first order there are dukes and counts, 10; in the [second?], [20?]; in the third, 30; in the fourth, 40; in the fifth, 500; in the sixth, 600; in the seventh, 700; in the eighth, 800; in the ninth, 900; in the tenth, 100; in the eleventh, 200; in the twelfth, 300. All these, by their turns and orders, with an infinite multitude of servants, are assigned to the operations of this art; from them an intelligent and perfected operator in the art will be able to summon, in the twelfth hour of the day, whichever and however many he wishes. All the princes, dukes, and counts of this hour, according to the opinion of Raziel, assume forms at the pleasure of the operator.

Therefore, when you wish to operate through any of them in the last hour of the day, observe the lord of the ascendant of that hour and the house of the twelfth; and write the character of each, with the other necessities, on a clean little sheet, and on its back this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Berathiel Odiel irfotirodu dreor rauezo melros ethiel aty no diel hayres penafon. Then, after all things that must customarily be sent beforehand have been sent beforehand, say this conjuration in the proper manner, and immediately the spirits called will appear. Then entrust the secret to the more powerful one, according to the instruction of the art, and he will faithfully obey in all things.

*The theme of any secret whatever, at pleasure, that occurs.*

We have a friend absent far away. We know that a danger threatens him, of which he is unaware. We desire to warn him, but for certain reasons we do not dare to do so openly. Therefore we call the spirits assigned to this operation according to the hour, and safely commend the secret.

For the holy religion of the Christian faith forbids love of the world to its people, since he who loves worldly joys loses heavenly happiness. A Catholic who does not live as a Catholic is shown to have denied Christ; and he who loves temporal things is not called a good Catholic. He who loves the Creator seeks the joys of spiritual things.
