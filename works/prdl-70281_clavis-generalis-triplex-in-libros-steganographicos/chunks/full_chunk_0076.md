Here now we have twenty-four named from the spirits of Abasdarhon, of whom the first twelve are dukes in their orders, while the remaining twelve are counts; and there are two from each order, namely one duke and one count. We have 3,200 appointed ministers, whom they use by turns in various magical operations. Solomon and Raziel, wise men and most experienced in magic, say that the princes of Abasdarhon are accustomed to appear in whatever form they have been commanded by the one who performs the operation; and since we have frequently experienced this, we acknowledge that it rests upon truth. Therefore, when you wish to operate through them in Steganography in the fifth hour of the night, observe the lord of the ascendant, and write down both it and the whole disposition of the heavens on a clean sheet, together with the other things that we have taught, and on its back this conjuration.

139 *Argument of the Secret That Occurs in the Steganography.*

Whatever secret you have, which otherwise you cannot without danger make known to a friend who is far away or absent, entrust it to the more powerful of the spirits, in that rite and form in which, according to the art, it is fitting, fearing nothing, because they are benevolent and faithful.

The Catholic faith saves no one who despises the ardor of divine love. To believe and not to have zeal for divine love is of no use at all for the redemption of the soul. Moreover, many people are now found who boast of the Catholic faith, but few have the works of the Christian faith; but idle faith dies. Many believe, but very few observe the commandments of God. Alas, the zeal of believers is weak; even unbelieving people can have a stronger zeal than this, and many do have it. Gentiles and people who do not believe that our savior, made man, is the son of the Almighty will be tormented by the punishments of hell. But all those who believe that the Son of God came into the world and became man through zeal for human redemption, yet do not adorn the Christian faith with holy works, and, always entangled in sins, prefer the honors of the world to eternal blessedness, zealously pursuing impious actions and staining the purity of faith with corrupt morals against obedience, will be tortured with heavier punishments. But the lovers of God, who have the right zeal of the Christian faith, prefer nothing to God, imitate Christ, flee sins, and despise the riches and honors of the world. Now therefore attend to these words, you who glory in faith: love God, and you will have the reward of blessedness, pursuing righteousness. If you love the vanities of the age against the honor of God, you will receive the prize of those who pursue iniquity: the horrors of hell. Written on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of May, in the year of the Lord 1500.
