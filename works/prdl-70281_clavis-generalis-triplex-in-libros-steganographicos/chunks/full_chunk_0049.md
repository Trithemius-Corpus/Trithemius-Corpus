*STEGANOGRAPHY*

When he to whom these letters are sent has received them, being already skilled in the art: after recognizing the character ÷- of the supreme prince Menadiel, and after first doing those things which customarily must be done beforehand, let him say the conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Menadyel murty chamerose dayr pean cathurmo phameron ersoti pray faruepo, fabel merij rean, charon ietlas Meduse fayr lamerosyn alty merchahon.

When the chant has been spoken, the spirit that has been sent will appear visibly to the one who summons him, and will report to him faithfully and secretly in his ear everything that he has in commission.

*Chapter XXIX.*

*Whose supreme prince is called Macariel, having under his command many dukes, princes, counts, and servants appointed to various services.*

Macariel also, one of the supreme spirits who preside over the operations of Steganography, must not be omitted by us, together with his serving princes, because he is sufficiently useful, prompt, and faithful for all things whatsoever we may wish. He has forty princes; of these, never fewer than four are sent into service, namely three dukes and one count, with not a few servants. They observe this order among themselves, that in their turns one of the dukes always performs the office of count, according to the four parts of the year. And concerning all these it is necessary for the operator to have full knowledge. But now let us name some of the princes themselves, who will abundantly suffice us for the operation of our Steganography.

*Book One.*

We now have named twelve of the princes of Macariel, with 400 servants numbered according to their turns and orders. Therefore when you wish to operate through them in Steganography, take care that you summon no fewer than at least four from those named above; after first doing what customarily must be done beforehand, say this conjuration.

*Chant.*

Macariel myrno chamerosy purmy maresyn amos peanamolradu, chabor ianoes fabelron dearsy chadon vlyses Almos rutiel pedaron deabry madero neas lamero dearsy, thubra dorpilto melrosyne draor chalmea near, parmon dearsy charon alnodiel parsa radean, maroy reneas charso gnone, melrosin te dranso casmar ebroset. Landrys masfayr therafonte noel amalan.

When this chant has been spoken, the spirits summoned will be present, appearing in diverse form, and often indeed with a virgin’s head, while their body and tail end in the form of a dragon, twisting and turning themselves in a fourfold order.

*A secret matter is sent to the prince.*

The prefect of the king or prince, appointed in a land or province, has understood by a most secret report that the enemies shortly have the intention of breaking into the province. He wishes to warn the prince, but he cannot do so by messengers, because they are to be tortured by the adversaries on the road so that they may betray the secret; nor by letters, since all letters are opened by them. Therefore he summons a spirit, entrusts the secret, and composes unrelated letters.

*Let us compose letters conveying nothing.*
