*Example XXVI.*

S.P. I had entrusted my books to you under the condition of returning them, my very dear [unclear], but see, you have by no means returned them. You ought to have sent them back shortly; you act contrary to the law which the catholic faith has sanctioned concerning a promise. Do not disdain the condition longer than you promised. It does not befit a good and honorable man, sprung from a noble ancestry of elders, to violate a promise. You had promised to return them very diligently in a short time; it is a mark of levity to put off a promise every day to the Greek Kalends. Have I not waited long enough with great goodwill? It is disgraceful to promise great things and repay very small ones. A promise adorns a soldier, nor does it befit a Christian not to keep a condition. But the law and constitution, Christian, bid you keep promises, repay a loan, and do generously in turn to others the things which you desire to be done to you. I demand back my books, which I had entrusted to you with entreaties. I do not desire a longer excuse, which I stretch out to the Greek Kalends. Release my mind in brief. You possess the nobility of your most honorable family, Bunnesind; consider your lineage, and send my volumes back to me. Farewell.

The hidden tenor of the above:

*This man wants to catch you badly; beware of him; he is* full of wickedness and wants to test you. *This is the intention* of the secret.

*Example XXVII.*

S.D. A rumor has gone out concerning the abbot of Limburg, that he has dismissed the brother of [Fleisderfensem?], a faithful man and experienced in temporal affairs, and has introduced new monks, lacking resources and assigning themselves to that office. I am greatly astonished at the counsels of the brothers; nevertheless, when I recall the custom of the Limburgers, I do not know what to say. For they are ignorant of temporal affairs, and therefore they dismissed a most faithful man when their saint reproved them. They ought to have said thanks to our Savior Jesus Christ for the faithfulness of so great a brother, who could have recovered their necessities. Wretched men, why do you reject a good and necessary administrator? What shall I say? I do not praise the departure of so useful a brother, most skilled in temporal affairs; he would have aided the Limburgers most quickly, which I deeply grieve, that the Limburg monks have been so foolish: they dismissed an excellent man and appointed as stewards monks worth absolutely nothing in temporal affairs. Abbot Macharius, it did not befit a man to act thus: at first you praised him, now you drive away a far better man, to be placed after no one. For I confess that if Christ had given him to me, I would give him the greatest thanks, as befits a Christian, and would see to it that he did not depart, [unclear] Christ. You preferred, having lingered, to dismiss a faithful man, worn out by the depravity of false men, lest I should seem, your affairs having been abandoned, [unclear] hope of recovering. Farewell. From Sponheim. Nones of January, in the year of the Lord 1499. Johannes Trithemius, Abbot.
