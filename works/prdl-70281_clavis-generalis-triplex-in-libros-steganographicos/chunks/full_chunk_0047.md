Here you have twelve of the princes of Solouiel, of whom the first six are dukes in this year, and the remaining six counts; in the following year these will be dukes, and those counts, and so consequently, always by alternating turns. I have collected the number of sub-servitors as 1,840, although there are more whom I have not yet been able to number. But when you wish to operate through these spirits, be steadfast and strong in spirit, and do not be frightened by their appearance when you see them appear in the form of serpents with a maiden’s head. Then do what is to be done according to the art; once these things have been done first, add this conjuration.

When the song has been duly completed, the spirits that have been called will be present in the customary form; to them entrust the secret.

*Let us set down a secret at pleasure.*

I decide that a friend far absent, previously skilled in the art of Steganography, must be warned about certain imminent dangers. But I fear the public, since I would be making another’s danger my own; so that I may avoid this, I entrust the secret neither to letters nor to men, but to a spirit whom I know will be most faithful in all things.

*Let us form letters at pleasure.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, sends many greetings to the Reverend Father in God Rutger Sycamber, Archbishop of Narva.

The most reverend and most studious Nicolaus, bishop of Irburs, general governor of your ecclesiastical affairs, will explain to you that [unclear] has acted badly, by subjecting to bonds the vicar of our lord King Desiderius, a benign man, and has killed him, tormented by hunger. We are expecting the enemy Desiderius the king, breaking daily into the diocese. But we entreat you: come to aid shortly, so that you may not fall into the enemy. Farewell. 24 March 1503.

When those to whom these letters are sent have received them, provided he has previously been generally imbued in the art, let him do what is to be done, adding this conjuration with a steadfast spirit, fearing nothing.

*Conjuration.*

Soleuiel curtiel chamerusyn faty pemalros dayrianothycathurmo parmoy iotran lamedon frascu penoy ilthon fabelmerusyn. When the song has been spoken, the spirit sent by the operator will appear visibly, faithfully revealing everything committed to him; and if he wishes to send anything back to the operator, let him entrust it to the same spirit.
