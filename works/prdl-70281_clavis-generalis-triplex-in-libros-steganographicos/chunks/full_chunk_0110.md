*Mode XXIX. It uses alternate words, and the first letter of each significant word is shifted to the tenth.*

Mode 29 similarly uses alternate words, like the preceding modes, beginning with one empty word, to which another significant one is soon joined; again one empty word, and again one significant word, and so consequently to the end of the proposed narrative. *Example XXIX.*

SD. Since you are most devoted to the arts of humane learning, I think you will surely rejoice that you have found a ninth companion of your studies. This man, who presents these letters to you, is a man of much knowledge, a supreme philosopher, a most subtle arithmetician, a most eloquent rhetorician, a most profound mathematician, and excellently learned in every [unclear]. He speaks most elegantly, shining with Ciceronian eloquence. Were I not so much your friend, I would have kept such a man entirely for myself, since, having enjoyed his familiar company, I have found him to be most learned among philosophers. I am especially favorable to you, that you may receive so gratefully so great a philosopher, whom, led by singular friendship toward you, I send to you; and by showing yourself benevolent and familiar to him, you will prove him a supreme philosopher. I have seen certain secrets of the arts committed to most elegant letters, by which they accomplish wonders. This most learned man can answer ex tempore the very weighty questions proposed to him; in proposing, he uses a zeal most ready for disputation. Therefore I ask that you receive him liberally and humanely, a man most learned in all good arts by culture and familiarity, and a very weighty defender. Indeed, he will be able to instruct you in many things, since he is a philosopher of most elegant learning, an outstanding historian, a most skilled rhetorician too, ready, and a most acute geometer. O would that all our students were such, who with swollen breast despise necessary study. But this man, devoted to philosophy, and other sciences and arts, loves them deeply, treats them continually, and penetrates them most acutely. Receive him therefore with love; treat him with a generous countenance; strengthen the faithful Christian. Farewell, from Sponheim, on the Nones of January, in the year of the Lord 1499. Johannes Trithemius.

*Mode XXX. It proceeds by alternate words, and the significant letter is shifted to the eleventh.*

Mode 30, beginning with one empty word, proceeds by alternate words, so that throughout the whole sequence of the narrative it always places after one empty word another significant word, just as we have shown in the preceding mode also and in others. Moreover, by the fourth letter of the significant word the first following after it is taken as the eleventh, so that for A, m is taken; for b, n; for c, o; and so consequently, as is clear in the alphabet.

Am, bn, eo, dp, eq, fr, gs, ht, iu, kx, wz, ma, nb, oc, pd, qe, rs, sgth, ni, xk, zl.

*Content of the Secret.*

They have written to me that I should come to him, but I am not coming; you must first write to me beforehand.
