*Mode XXIII. With each letter shifted to the fourth, it proceeds by alternating turns or words.*

Mode 23 always begins with one idle word, to which it immediately joins one significant word, and so proceeds with alternating words. In the meaning of these, the first letters are always transposed to the fourth, so that for a, e is taken; for b, f; for c, g; and so consequently as far as x, for which c is taken, as in the following alphabet we shall clearly show by example.

A e, b f, c g, d h, e i, s k, h l, i k, o l, p m, q n, r o, s p, d q, u l, r x, s z, t a, s b, x c, z d.

This mode, however, like all the others, will be capable of being varied in many ways: as it now uses alternate words, so let it use pairs, threes, or more. Then the sign of a mode of this kind is to be sought; and it must always be observed immovably for the others, since one should look not to the variety of the mode but to the order of the mystery.

*Example XXIII.*

S. D. Those matters which I set before myself to be solved, with Christ’s help I have resolved. I would like you to write back to me briefly what you make of it, what then has benefited me, your opinion, and the whole matter. Brother Gratius, and also a friend, recently reported to me that then, among our people, a young man had been restored to life, recounting every invocation of Christ from the dead, and telling of the wondrous and dreadful torments among the lower regions of wicked Christians: namely, of those who, by acting perversely, crucified Christ; those especially guilty who, though they had obtained the most kindly benefit of redemption, nevertheless always remained ungrateful to Christ and, utterly blinded, neglected future joys. Following the pleasures of the world, they were most bitterly [unclear] with daily and continual neglects gnawing at them: “We shall be condemned; we are justly tortured; deservedly do we suffer cruel things. Cruel as we are, we sought earthly and worldly things ambitiously; we neglected Christ’s commandment; we despised the humble; we loved iniquity; and we receive the repayment of eternal damnation. Alas for us unhappy ones, why did we deny Christ, why did we neglect the time of indulgence?” etc. The man raised up related that he had found many other wondrous things, etc. I cannot write them on this occasion, especially in a few letters. Farewell.

*The hidden tenor of the aforesaid.*

Tomorrow at nine o’clock take heed of me at the gate. There I will appear secretly and silently with 200 horses.

*Mode XXIV. It takes all significant words, and transposes each letter to the fifth following.*

Mode 24, after the likeness of the first, takes all the words of the proposed narrative as significant; but it transposes the first letter of each word to the fifth next following: that is to say, so that for a, f is taken; for b, g; for c, h; and so consequently, as we shall show in the second alphabet, where for the red letter the black one immediately placed above it is taken.

A f, b g, c h, d i, e k, f l, g m, h n, i o, k p, l q, r u, x z, s t, b v, c x, d z, e [unclear].

Therefore, whenever you wish to write or read in this mode, place this alphabet before you, easily indicating all the signs of each mode.

*Example XXIV.*

S. D. Setting the honor of benefactions before attentive services, and not provoking the insults of benefactors, remember, I beseech you, the holy charity of your brothers, the good zeal and honor of those brothers of yours who continually adorn our Catholic religion with holy works. But I pray, kind brother, that I may cast away fear. Double-tongued brothers, the hatred of being loved, quarrels, and disputes take goodness away by evil works. Be splendid every day to the good also; aspire also to fame; adorn the soul with holiness, justice, continence; and have zeal for the name [unclear].
