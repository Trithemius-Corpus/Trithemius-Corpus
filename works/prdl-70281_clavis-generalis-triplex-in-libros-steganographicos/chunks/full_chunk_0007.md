For I too am a Christian, and voluntarily devoted to the monastic way of life; and I desire to live and conduct myself in no other way than befits a true Christian and a monk professed under the Rule of the blessed father Benedict. I received the Catholic faith itself, according to the tradition of the holy Roman Church, from the cradle, baptized in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. This faith I hold and believe together with that same universal Church of Christians; and, as long as I live, with God’s help always firmly preserving it inviolate, I shall hold it in heart, mouth, and deed, nor do I ever intend to deviate from it on any occasion whatever. Far be it from me, therefore, either to learn or to teach anything contrary to the Christian faith and its purity, harmful to holy morals, or in any way opposed to the regular profession. I fear God, and I have sworn myself to his worship; from him I shall not be separated while living, nor, as I trust, when dead.

I have placed this declaration of mine at the head of this whole work of ours not without cause, because I know that there will be many who, when they cannot understand what we have written, will turn to insults and will attribute our good and holy studies to evil arts or superstitious admonitions. To these, since they will exist, and to all who are going to read these synthemata of ours, we earnestly entreat that, if they understand this secret tradition of ours, they keep it forever hidden, and not pour such wondrous mysteries out into public. But if they do not understand it, as we know will happen to many, let them learn before they criticize. For indeed he shows himself a rash judge who, before he has known the truth of a case, pronounces sentence upon it. Learn this art first, and judge afterward; but if you cannot understand it, reject not the art itself, since it is good, but your own dull wits. For I know and am certain that no one of holy mind can criticize this work of ours unless he happens to be wholly ignorant of it. But those for whom it is more familiar to despise wisdom than to have learned it, I neither desire nor would wish to penetrate these mysteries of ours.

*Chapter I.*

*Whose key and operation are held by the principal spirit Pamersyel, anoyr madriel, through the ministry of ebra sothean abrulges utrasbiel. And nadres ormenu i tules rablion hamorphiel. To these the commission of all is made with an exorcism.*

[unclear]

The operation of this first chapter is very difficult and full of dangers, because of the pride and rebellion of its spirits, who do not obey anyone unless he is most experienced in this art. For they not only do not obey novices and those less proved in the art, but also, if they are pressed too much, they frequently harm them and offend them with various illusions. They are malicious and faithless above all other aerial spirits, and obey absolutely no one unless compelled by the greatest sacraments; and the secret entrusted to them they often faithlessly reveal to others. For as soon as they have been sent out with letters, they fly off, and, bursting in without order toward the person to whom they are sent, like a people fleeing from battle without a leader, they hasten furiously and, filling the air with their shouting, often disclose all the secrets around the sender. Therefore we advise that no one intending to operate in this art should compel them, nor anxiously seek their services. For they are insolent and faithless, since he can find many of those whom we shall mention next who are sufficiently well-disposed, and who of their own accord offer themselves ready for obedience. But if anyone should wholly wish to experience their insolence and to prove that what we have said is true, let him know that this manner must be observed. Let him prepare the paper on which he is going to write, with an invocation of the divine name, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.
