When he has said these things, and has seen that the spirits are present to him for obedience, let him continue the work begun. But if they have not yet appeared, let him repeat the things already said so many times until they appear; or else let him cease altogether, lest, compelled too much, they harm the operator. But when you have completed the letters intended, send them with a messenger to a friend approved in the art; when he receives them, let him say this conjuration: Lamarton anoyr bulon madriel traschon ebrasothea panthenon nabruilges Camery itrasbier rubanthy nadres Calmosy ormenulan, ytules demy rabion hamorphyn. When these things have been said, he will at once understand your meaning perfectly, the spirits now of their own accord offering themselves with force and crying out, so that very often even others present can perceive the sender's secret. But observe that in all letters which you send written by this art you affix your own sign, so that the man to whom you write may know through which spirits you have operated. For if he were to operate through some spirits for understanding, and you through others for sending, they will never obey him for all eternity; rather, because of the improper operation, they would both harm him and by no means reveal your secret. For all the spirits whom we use in this art guard only the ranks and offices committed to them; in no way do they interfere with others. Consider carefully all the things which we shall say in what follows. And, so that we may prove by example the things we have said, let us set out our intention in the vernacular tongue, which we shall subsequently make known to a friend in the aforesaid manner.

*Book One.*

*Let this be the secret intention of the mind of Roth, some prince, which he wishes his absent vassal to know.*

Dear faithful one, it is our will, for honest cause, to make war on those of Strasbourg and to fall upon them with all our force next Wednesday after Vitus and Modestus. Therefore it is our earnest desire of you, since you are bound to us by oath and vow and obliged to faithful services: that on that same day you should appear well equipped, according to your ability, about four after midday, by Stauffenburg in the forest [Here the letters were placed thus]; you would find further instruction there, should follow it, and should not fail to come. We shall recognize this graciously toward you, and keep this plan of ours with you in high secrecy, etc.
