*Clavis Generalis Triplex in Libros Steganographicos* — the "Threefold General Key to the Steganographic Books" — is Trithemius's own explanatory key to his most notorious work, the *Steganographia*. The *Steganographia* (composed around 1499–1500) circulated only in manuscript during his lifetime and earned him lasting accusations of necromancy, because its cipher instructions are dressed as conjurations of angels and spirits; it was printed posthumously in 1606 and placed on the *Index*. The *Clavis* is the antidote Trithemius supplied: a compact decryption key demonstrating that the "spirit names" resolve into a cryptographic system, not a magical one. It sets out, *modus* by *modus*, the explicit substitution alphabets by which each cipher is to be read.

This is the **1608 Frankfurt edition** — printed by Balthasar Hofmann for Johann Berner, the same partnership behind the 1621 *Steganographia* elsewhere in this corpus. The *Clavis* is the work from which the solved *modi* on the [cipher solutions page](../cipher-solutions.html) are drawn. Stephen Skinner and Daniel Clark's 2024 Llewellyn edition translates the *Steganographia* itself and its angelological framing; the *Clavis*, as Trithemius's own cryptographic key, has not previously been available as a complete, freely reusable English parallel text. The Latin of this witness is comparatively clean and the translation sits in the top tier (S).
