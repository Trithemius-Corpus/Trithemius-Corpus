*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Laurentius Justinianus, a Venetian by nation, of the Celestine order, and learned, distinguished in character and life, although he could have had the very greatest riches and honors of this world, despising all things for the love of Christ, entered the aforesaid order. In it, shining with great religious devotion and abstinence, he published, for the edification of the simple, very devout little works, whose titles are given below:

The Tree of Life, 1 book. On Monastic Discipline, 1 book.
On the Marriage of the Word and the Soul, 1 book. On the Agony of Christ, 1 book.
On the Interior [Conflict?], 1 book. On the Lamentation of the Church, 1 book.
On the Sacrament of the Altar, 1 book. On the Solitary Life, 1 book.
On Contempt of the World, 1 book. On the Lord's Sermon at Supper, 1 book.
On the Pastoral Office, 1 book. On Obedience also, 1 book.
On the Degrees of Perfection, 1 book. Forty Sermons, 1 book.

He flourished in the time of Rupert, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1410. He is buried at Venice in the cathedral church, at whose tomb many miracles are divinely performed. Bernardus Justinianus, his nephew, wrote his life lucidly.

Augustinus of Rome, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, prior general, a man most devoted to the divine scriptures and very learned, and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and renowned in eloquence, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, 4 books. On all the Epistles of Paul, 14 books.
On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book. On Free Will, 1 book.
On Original Sin, 1 book. On Christ and the Church, 1 book.
On the Charity of Christ toward the Elect, 1 book.
On the Perfect Justice of the Church, 1 book.
On the Power of the Pope, 1 book. On the Books of the Ethics, 8 books.

He is said to have written many other things besides, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the time of the emperor Sigismund and Pope John XXIII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Baldus of Perugia, once the hearer and disciple of Bartolus de Saxoferrato, the most celebrated jurist of all his time, and also nobly learned in secular philosophy, sharp in talent, profound in judgment, cautious and steadfast in counsel, wrote many distinguished little works in both laws, by which he obtained for his name everlasting memory among posterity.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Among their number the following are given:

On the Codex, 9 books. On the Old Digest, 14 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Institutes, 4 books. On the Use of Fiefs, 1 book.
On the Authentics, 1 book. On the Second Book of the Decretals, 1 book.
Additions to the Speculum, 1 book. Many Consilia, 1 book.
And several other things.

He died in the time of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1423, in the first indiction, and was buried with honor at Pavia, in the convent of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, in the month of July.

Angelus of Perugia, brother of the aforesaid Baldus and companion and associate in his studies, most learned in civil law, renowned in talent, most celebrated in fame and reputation among jurists, also composed many volumes in his faculty. By establishing monuments of excellent talent through them, he destined the memory of his name for the knowledge of many. Among their number the following most elegant commentaries are given:

On the Codex, 9 books. On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Institutes, 4 books.

Indeed he is said to have worked through the whole body of civil law, and certain other things as well. He died in the time of the emperor Sigismund, in the same year as Baldus his brother, in the year of the Lord 1423, in the first indiction.
