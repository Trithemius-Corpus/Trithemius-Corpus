*Abbot of Spanheim.*

[He] wrote certain distinguished little works for the instruction of monks, of which the following are extant: *On the Eating of Meat*, book 1; *Declarations on the Rule*, book 1; *Questions on the Sentences*, book 1.

In addition, he is said to have written various and numerous treatises, but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands, except that little book which he wrote on the eating of meat, in which he beautifully sets forth and soundly concludes that, for a healthy monk professed according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the eating of meat is unlawful, and therefore altogether forbidden.

Richard Lavinham, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, an Englishman by nation, a man excellently learned in the Holy Scriptures, an outstanding expounder of secular philosophy, and also of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote several volumes as well, of which these are reported: *Against the Lollards*, book 1; *On the Foundation of His Order*, book 1; *For the Book of Bridget*, book 1; *Various Sermons*, book 1; *On Logic* also, book 1; and *Various Questions*, book 1.

He flourished at the University of Oxford, especially at the time when the heresy of John Wycliffe, that of the Lollards, began in Bohemia.

Johannes de Werdena, of the order, as they say, of the Friars Minor, a German by nation, a man studious and learned in the sacred writings, rich in reading of the ancients, and sufficiently learned in natural philosophy, excellent in talent, composed in speech with scholastic simplicity, and in his time a most celebrated deliverer of popular sermons, wrote, among the other monuments of his talent, little works very necessary for preachers of the word of God, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant: *Double Sermons on the Temporale*, book 1, “Let us put on,” see; *Double Sermons on the Saints*, book 1.

But the other things which he composed have by no means come to our knowledge.

Jacobus, who is called Magnus, a native of Toledo, of the order of the hermit brothers of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine writings, distinguished in knowledge of natural philosophy, and abundant in reading of the ancients, is said to have issued various treatises and commentaries on the divine books, which have not yet come into our hands. Johannes Campsceñ, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, an Englishman by nation, long practiced in the Holy Scriptures and in continual reading of the ancients, rich in talent, an excellent deliverer of sermons, is said to have fashioned certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number, they report that there are extant very notable *Sermons on the Temporale*, book 1; *Sermons on the Saints*, book 1.

But of the other things which he composed I have not been able to find the titles of the works.

Thomas Gualensis, of the order of the Friars Preachers, an Englishman by nation, a man learned and very studious in the divine Scriptures, and nobly learned in secular letters, especially in scholastic philosophy, subtle in talent and clear in eloquence, also an outstanding deliverer of sermons and an acute disputant in scholastic lectures, wrote many distinguished little works on the Holy Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity; of these, however, few have come into our hands. For I have found only the following: *On the Whole Psalter*, book 1, “Blessed is he who keeps”; *On the Proverbs of Solomon*, book 1, “In hidden places I shall prepare”; book 1; *On Augustine’s City of God*, 10 books, “The force of the river makes glad”; *On the Song of Songs*, book 1.

He flourished at the University of Oxford, where he is said to have composed many volumes, and especially various lectures on several books of the Bible.

Philippus, abbot of the monastery of Otterburg, in the diocese of Worms, of the Cistercian order, a German by nation, a man studious in the divine Scriptures, learned through long practice, and not ignorant of secular literature, published for the edification of readers volumes not to be despised; by soundly exercising his talent in them, he also left to posterity the memory of his name. Of their number the following are reported: a distinguished and notable work *On the Song of Songs*, book 1; *Sermons on the Temporale*, book 1; *Sermons on the Saints*, book 1; *Letters to Various Persons*, book 1.

If he published anything more, it has still by no means come to my knowledge.
