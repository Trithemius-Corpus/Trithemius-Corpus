John de Bachone, an Englishman, of the order of the friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed both in canon law and in secular philosophy, shone brilliantly in the Paris school no less for his conduct than for his learning; and afterwards, promoted to the provincialate of England, he energetically governed his order by word and example. He wrote many notable volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Mark, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Luke, 1 book.
On the Gospel of John, 1 book.
On the Epistles of Paul, 14 books.
On the Acts of the Apostles, 1 book.
On the Canonical Epistles, 7 books.
On the Apocalypse, 1 book.
On the Sentences, 1 book.
Various Quodlibets, 1 book.
Mirror of His Order, 1 book.
Defense of His Order, 1 book.
Against the Jews, 1 book.
On the Poverty of Christ, 1 book.
On the Rule of His Order, 1 book.

He also published explanations on the books of blessed Augustine On the Holy Trinity and On the City of God, and on Anselm’s Why God Became Man and On the Incarnation of the Word; in all of these he exercised his talent splendidly.

He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

Rupert, a Frenchman by nation, of the order of the friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently instructed in scholastic philosophy, distinguished in talent, apt in speech, published various little works on the holy Scriptures; by splendidly exercising his talent in them, he made the memory of his name known also to posterity. He was an excellent preacher of the divine word, and wrote many things useful to composers of sermons. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 2 books.
On the Epistles of Paul, 3 books.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.

He composed many other works besides, which have not come to my knowledge.

Clement VI, pope, a Limousin by homeland, formerly called Peter Roger, abbot of Fécamp, of the order of Saint Benedict, sat in the chair of Peter after Benedict XII for ten years and six months. He was a man learned in the divine writings and not ignorant of secular letters, a notable doctor of canon law, venerable no less for his conduct than for his learning; in declaiming sermons to the people he was of excellent talent. He published certain distinguished little works, by which he showed the liveliness of his talent. Of these are reported:

Various Sermons, 1 book.
On the Canonization of Ivo, 2 books.
Rejoice and praise [unclear]
On the Approval of King Charles, 3 books.
Solomon will sit upon [unclear]
Many Collations, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.

He died and was buried at Avignon, under Charles IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1352, in the fifth indiction.
