*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

A poet laureate, also not unacquainted with the divine Scriptures, skilled in both languages, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, he wrote certain distinguished little works both in verse and in prose, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity. Of these he recently read the following to me:

To Maximilian, most illustrious King of the Romans, on both rhetorics of Marcus Tullius Cicero, an Epitome, that is, a Breviary, 1 book.
On the Praises of Eloquence, 1 book.
On the Art of [Versifying?], 1 book.
On Composing Letters, 1 book.
On the Site of Nuremberg, 1 book.
The Sarmatian Pilgrimage, 1 book.
Poems, 4 books.
Of Loves, which in German are entitled According to the Four Sides, 4 books.
A Panegyric to the Duke of Bavaria, 1 book.
On the Life of Saint Sebald, 1 poem.
The Site of Germany, 1 book.
Various Orations, 1 book.

He has published many very elegant letters to various persons in verse and prose, and various epigrams. In cosmography, of which he is very knowledgeable, he has written various works, which we trust will hereafter come to light. He is still living, being thirty-five years of age, and is writing various things under Maximilian, most illustrious King of the Romans, when Alexander VI was supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494.

Petrus Bonomus of Trieste, protonotary of the imperial court, a man very learned in the Latin tongue, both very fluent and very practiced in composing poems of every kind, as though the tuneful Muses poured forth from his storehouse, and for that reason most celebrated by the fame of a great name, wrote very many works in prose and in verse of various kinds. Of these the following are especially extant:

Books of Epigrams.
A tireless accumulation of Letters.

Nor does he ever cease writing. He is still living under his king Maximilian, devoted to letters and cultivating his talent with the good arts, in the year in which we write these things, 1494, the twelfth indiction.

Sebastian Ticio, otherwise Brant, a native of Strasbourg, German by nation, an eminent professor of both laws, excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in the other disciplines of secular literature, by no means unskilled in poetry, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, outstanding in counsel and action, who by his learning and night-labour wonderfully adorns Basel, a renowned city of Germany, wrote certain little works, both in verse and in prose, of reading not to be despised, by which he made his name well known. Of these the following are extant:

On the Titles of Both Laws, 1 book.
A Sapphic Rosary of the divine Mary.
In Praise of the Virgin, Enigmas of the Old Testament.
He Proves the Virginal Conception Possible.
The Wondering of Mary When the Angel Arrived.
The Salutation of the Angel.
On the Miraculous Places of the Blessed Virgin.
The Virginal Compassion.
The Consolation of Christ to His Mother.
On the Blows Given to Christ.
On the Ingratitude of Those Eating Manna.
In Praise of Saint Sebastian.
The Life of Saint Onuphrius.
In Praise of Saint Valentine.
In Praise of Saint Ivo.
The Life of Saint Bernard.
In Praise of the Carthusian Order.
On Contempt of the World.
Praises of Blessed Christiana near Basel.
Against Slanderous Advocates.
On the Captivity of King Maximilian.
An Elegy on the Death of Emperor Frederick.
To the Same King Maximilian.
Various Epigrams, 1 book.
Commendatory Poems for Various Printed Editions.

He has moreover compiled, with wonderful art and talent, though in the common and vernacular language, a certain little book which he called the Ship of Narragonia. In it he has so elegantly expressed the cause and root of all folly, censures the morals of men, and gives certain salutary remedies to individuals, that he would rightly have called that work not the book of fools, but rather a divine satire.
