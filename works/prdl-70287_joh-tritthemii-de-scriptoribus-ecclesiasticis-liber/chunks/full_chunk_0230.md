*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Jacobus of Bergamo, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, a most celebrated historiographer, wrote certain very lucid little works by which he made posterity beholden to his memory. Of these there is extant a work of histories from the beginning of the world down to the year of the Lord 1486, a great and distinguished volume, which, compiling it from various sources with much and long labor, he entitled *Supplement of Chronicles*, in fifteen books. *For Cicero’s [unclear]*. I have seen nothing of the others.

He is still living among his own people of Bergamo, held in esteem, nearly sixty years old, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Baptista de Sancto Blasio of Padua, a man very skilled in both laws and distinguished by philosophical learning, subtle in talent and ornate in speech, who taught law publicly and excellently in the Paduan gymnasium, where he instructed a great number of pupils, wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he showed the force of his talent to those present and to posterity. Of these I have read:

*On Dowry Privileges*, one book. *Since concerning dowries many things*.

Also *The Correlative*, one book. *In this correlative work*.

*Questions on arbiters*, [unclear]. *In the name of Christ*, [unclear]. *Since* [unclear].

*On the First Rubric of the Decretals*, one book. *In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ*.

He also wrote other things.

He also wrote on the difference between an arbiter and an arbitrator.

He completed these very works in the Paduan gymnasium, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480, indiction 13. And I believe that he is still alive.

Marcus Antonius Sabellicus, a man most learned in every respect, the most celebrated philosopher, orator, and poet of all our age, adorning Venice with his learning and books, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, skilled in both languages, for whom Hermolaus Barbarus, Venetian archbishop and patriarch of Aquileia, once composed a book on the precepts for writing history, wrote certain distinguished volumes both in meter and in prose, by which he secured everlasting remembrance for his name among posterity. Of these I have seen the following: three great volumes of histories from the beginning of the world. *To the Senate*, a most celebrated work, containing from the beginning of the city 33 books.

*The History and Deeds of the Venetians*. *And if from every writing*.

*On the Antiquity of Aquileia, His Fatherland*, one book. *It pours forth with Illyrian [unclear]*. *Where headlong fortune*.

*On the Sontian Fortification*, a poem, one book. *Now, the wheels having been spread, she set out*. *The kindly vessel tosses*.

*On the Sontian Slaughter*, a poem, one book. *Among the pleasing suns of study*. *These things of one walking upon the ground*.

*On the Burning of the Carni*, one book. *The kindly vessel tosses*.

*On the Shipwreck of Peter’s Boat*, one book. *Among the pleasing suns of study*.

*On the Inventors of Things and Arts*, one book. *These things of one walking upon the ground*.

*On the Origin of Udine*, a poem, one book. *The gracious day of the Virgin*.

*In Praise of St. Mary*, 14 elegies.

I have seen nothing of the rest.

He is distinguished today among the Venetians, held in the highest esteem, composing many and various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we are writing these things, 1494.

Hubertus Leonardus, bishop of Darien, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, excellent in talent and clear in eloquence, who once taught theology excellently in the Parisian gymnasium, wrote many highly commendable little works, by which he made his name known with glory. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Genealogy of the Nobles of the Franks and Their Deeds*, to Ludovicus, bishop of Liege, one book.

*On the Gospel of Luke*, one book.

*On the Government of Princes*, three books.

*On Ecclesiastical Immunity*, five books.

*Against the Heretics of Nivelles*, one book.

*Various Sermons*, one book.

*A Notable Lenten Collection*, one book.

And several other works.

When he was inquisitor of heretical depravity against certain heretics of Nivelles throughout the diocese of Liege, he confuted and defeated them with the sword of the Holy Spirit, writing the aforementioned very celebrated work, containing twenty-eight principal propositions; in it, vigorously and eloquently attacking just as many of their erroneous articles, he refuted them.

He flourished in Germany, from which he also came, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1490. And I believe that he is still alive.
