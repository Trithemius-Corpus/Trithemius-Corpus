Aldus Manutius, a Roman, very skilled in the liberal arts, wrote *Institutions of Greek Grammar* in pure Greek speech; *On the Various Inflections of Every Kind of Nouns and Verbs*. The same man wrote four books of *Institutions of Latin Grammar*. He also published another volume, *On the Kinds of Days*, and at the same time *On Shadows and Hours*, which are mentioned in Palladius. There are also extant his very elegant annotations on Quintus Horatius, and prefaces not to be despised, prefixed to many books printed by him. One letter of the same man to Angelo Poliziano. He composed several other little works, and translated not a few from Greek into Latin phrasing, among which he rendered Homer's poem on the battle of the frogs and mice in Latin words in prose, so that verse answers to verse. He translated in the same way also the compunctive poems of Damascenus, and well. He flourished at Venice under Maximilian, and died around the year of the Lord 1514.

Alexander the monk, by no means moderately instructed in the liberal arts, wrote the historical discourse, which is extant at Rome in Greek, *On the Discovery of the Holy and Life-Giving Cross*.

Alexander ab Alexandro, a distinguished jurist of Naples, published six books of *Genial Days*. He was illustrious in the time of Francesco Filelfo, Niccolo Perotti, and Ermolao Barbaro.

Alfonsus Ferrus of Naples, a notable physician, chief surgeon of Pope Paul III, brought to light four books *On the Manifold Medicine of the Holy Wood, and on the Administration of Wine*.

Aloisius Marlianus of Milan wrote an elegant letter on the disastrous voyage to Spain of Philip, king of the Spaniards.

Aloisius Mundella, a distinguished physician at Brescia in Italy, wrote twenty-three medical letters, containing an exposition of various questions and, besides, of difficult passages of Galen.

Alphonsus, a man most illustrious in every kind of good letters, made himself famous in his age by an excellent volume, which is inscribed *Against Heresies*.

Alphonsus, archbishop of Toledo, wrote several questions on Aristotle's books *On the Soul*.

Alvarus Gomes, a Spaniard, a man not unlearned, rendered the *Proverbs of Solomon* in verse.
