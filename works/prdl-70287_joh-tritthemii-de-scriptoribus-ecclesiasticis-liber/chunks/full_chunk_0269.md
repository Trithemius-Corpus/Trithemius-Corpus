*To the Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II*

He flourished at the time when Erasmus himself did, certainly at Louvain in the year 1520.

Clarundus, a man wonderfully experienced in affairs and in knowledge of the times, made this age illustrious with a splendid volume, which is entitled On the Genealogy of the Dukes of Burgundy, Brabant, Flanders, Holland, etc., from Hector the Trojan down to Charles V.

Ephraim, Greek by nation, a man well instructed in the liberal arts, wrote the lives of the emperors of Constantinople in iambic verse. He flourished 200 years ago.

Epiphanius, prelate of Selybria, or Olybria, a man not a little practiced in sacred letters, published a very useful book against the burners of images.

Erveus, a monk of the order of St. Dominic, very skilled in philosophy, composed commentaries, in a rough style but learned, on the logic and philosophy of Aristotle.

Eusebius, a Sardinian by nation, and, from being reader of the city of Rome, bishop of Vercelli, banished for confession of the faith by the emperor Constantius to Scythopolis and thence to Cappadocia, returned to the church under the emperor Julian and published commentaries on the Psalms by Eusebius of Caesarea, which he had translated from Greek into Latin. He died in the reigns of Valentinian and Valens.

Euthymius Zygadenus, a monk, a diligent compiler of sacred literature, wrote expositions on all the Psalms, which are extant in Greek at Rome. The same man carefully collected an interpretation of the epistles of Paul from Chrysostom and various other fathers.

Euzoius, while a youth, was educated at Caesarea under the rhetor Thespesius together with Gregory Nazianzen the bishop; and, as bishop of that same city, with very great labor he attempted to restore on parchment the corrupted library of Origen and Pamphilus. At the last he was driven from the church under the emperor Theodosius. Various and manifold treatises of his are reported, which were extant in the time of St. Jerome.

Fabianus of Monte San Savino, having a certain exact knowledge of jurisprudence, wrote on the alienation of ecclesiastical property in the treatise On Purchase. Likewise, On Buying and Selling.

Felix Malleolus, a Swiss of Zurich, doctor of decrees, once cantor of the church in his homeland, composed many little works not unworthy of the learned, of which we shall add the catalogue here:

Against the Strong Mendicants. A Recapitulation of the same.

Against anchorites, Beghards, and woodland Beguines.

A Description of the Lollards. On the Business of Monks.

A Gloss on the Bulls Obtained by the Beghards.

On Parish Priests and Mendicant Religious.

On Religious Proprietors, Preaching the Lord’s Precepts.

The Tenor of a Letter Sent from Heaven by the Patrons of the Church of Zurich, against those negligent in divine worship.

A Treatise on the Institution of New Divine Offices.

On Money Offered and Paid for Receiving a Prebend or Benefice in Churches or Monasteries.

A Dialogue on the Jubilee Year.

On Blessings of the Air to be Performed with the Sacrament.

A Treatise on Exorcisms. Another Treatise of Exorcisms.

On Credence to be Given to Demons.

On Drawing the Press-Tree on a Feast Day.

On Marriage or Betrothals.

On the Theft of Relics and Other Things Recently Miraculously Committed and Carried Out by Three Persons in the Monastery of the Blessed Virgin of the Place of the Hermits, and Finally Found and Recovered.

A Letter against a Certain Proud Cleric.

A Form of Appeal against a Certain Cardinal Who in Germany Wished to Enter a Cathedral Church.

On Ecclesiastical Liberty. On the Occasion of Good and Evil.

On Buying and Selling One for Twenty.

On Contracts Which Are Called Hostageships, and in the Vernacular Gisel.

A Doctorate in Folly. The Tenor of the Letter of Doctorate in Folly.

Against Unjust Judges.

A Dialogue on the Consolation of Those Unjustly Oppressed.

The Passionale of the same man. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1454.

Felix Petantius, chancellor of Segna, wrote a book to Vladislaus, king of Hungary and Bohemia, on the routes by which the Turks should be attacked. This book ought to be read by our princes in this unhappy age, so that the bloody enemy of Christians may be attacked to greater advantage than up to now.

Florentius Volusenus, a young man of keen intellect, wrote a theological oration, or a pious and learned commentary.
