*On Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

On faith and works,
On communion,
On satisfaction,
On the invocation of saints.

By the same man there is also a Little Summary on sins.

Thomas Linacre, English by nation, a professor of medicine, a man most highly skilled in both languages and of a very polished style, earned great praise and favor among the learned by his writings, so that he is regarded as an excellent grammarian and the most distinguished of Galen’s interpreters.

Rudiments of grammar.
On the Corrected Structure of Latin Speech, 6 books.
From the works of Claudius Galen he rendered into Latin:
On Temperaments, 3 books.
On Unequal Distemper, 1 book.
Method of Healing, or On Curing Diseases, 14 books, to Henry VIII, king of England.
On Preserving Health, 6 books.
On Natural Faculties, 5 books.
On the Use of Pulses, 1 book.
He also translated the Sphere of Proclus.

Thomas More, English by nation, notably learned both in Greek and in Latin, and a man of upright life, because of the remarkable dexterity with which he was endowed, was condemned to death by Henry VIII, king of the English, and became famous among posterity not only by martyrdom but also by many little works, of which I have seen the following:

Several dialogues of Lucian translated by him.
Utopia.
Many excellent epigrams by him are extant.
He flourished in the year, if I am not mistaken, 1535.

Thomas Murner of Strasbourg, bachelor of sacred letters at Krakow, of the Order of Minors, published many little works, full of jokes and witticisms, against the Lutherans, and certain others, namely these:

A Worthy Praise of Honorable Poems, a Wonderful Chastisement of Shameless Ones.
He wrote very many sermons; a Calendar, in which he graphically depicts the customs of the Lutherans according to the zodiac, namely in which sign someone could conveniently seize the goods of the Church, or deflower nuns.
He flourished in the year of salvation 1536.

Timothy, bishop, an elegant writer, very learned in [unclear] of all antiquity, wrote four books to the catholic Church spread throughout the whole world, in which he asserts that it is the duty of a Christian man, while he is in this life, to expend the riches of this world for the honor and name of God.

*Against the Abbot of Sponheim, On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Tilmannus Sibergensis, of the Order of Preachers, prior of Cologne, a most diligent inquisitor of heretical depravity and an outstanding professor of sacred scripture, a man acute in resolving questions, published many excellent little works displaying the acuteness of his talent, of which the following are reported:

On the Seven Sacraments, 1 book.
Annotations and Appendix to the Enchiridion of Johann Eck.
And certain other things he will write, if God grants him a longer life.
He is still living, a very old man, in the year of Christ 1546.

Torellus Sarayna of Verona, doctor of laws, wrote On the Origin and Antiquity of the City of Verona. Also:

On the Illustrious Men of Ancient Verona.
On Those Who Held Dominion over the City of Verona.
On the Monuments of the Ancients, arranged in alphabetical order, from which, with assistance, one may read most old inscriptions without difficulty.

Troilus Malvitius, jurist, wrote On Play, On Offerings, On [unclear], On the Dignity of a Bishop, On the Canonization of Saints.

An Opinion on the Commendation of Benefices, whose beginning is: A certain Urbanus in his treatise on etc.

Valescus de Taranta, chief physician of the king of France, a man, as is reported, very experienced in medical matters, wrote a little treatise On Plague and Epidemic Disease.

And a Practicum, commonly called Philonium, on the cure of diseases of every kind.
He is still living, as I hear, in the year of Christ 1546.

Varinus Phavorinus, bishop of Camerino, a man having exact knowledge of both the Greek and the Latin idiom.

He compiled a Greek Dictionary and commentaries from many and various authors.
By the same man there is a Thesaurus or Cornucopia and Gardens of Adonis.
He also translated certain apophthegms selected from the passages of Joannes Stobaeus.
