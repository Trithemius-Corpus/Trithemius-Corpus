*Servus Dei*, bishop of a certain church, whose city’s name I have not been able to find, a man learned through continual exercise in the divine Scriptures and rich in reading the ancients, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, is said to have fashioned some little works. Of these there survives a volume furnished with sacred Scriptures, against those who say that Christ, while living in this life, did not see the Father with his bodily eyes. The title of this work is:

That the Son Saw the Father, one book.

In this work he shows that the Son of God, even from the hour of his conception in the womb of the Virgin Mary by the Holy Spirit, always saw God the Father with his bodily eyes, and thereafter, and now, clothed in human flesh.

He also wrote certain other things.

He is said to have flourished under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 480.

*Victor*, bishop of Capua, learned in the holy Scriptures and sufficiently trained in secular letters, an excellent theologian and computist, sharp in intellect and renowned for eloquence; but very few of his works have come to my knowledge. He refuted the error of Victor of Aquitaine, who, at the order of Pope Hilary, while writing paschal cycles, taught that Easter was to be celebrated on 15 Kalends of May in the year of the Lord 455, although it should more correctly have been celebrated in that year on 7 Kalends of May. Therefore, when this occasion arose, he wrote:

On Easter, one book. One Gospel from the Four, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

*Cerealis*, an African bishop, a man very skilled in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular disciplines, was asked by Maximinus, bishop of the Arians, whether he could establish, with a few testimonies of divine Scripture and without, at least, the assertion of disputation, the consubstantiality of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. He replied not with a few, as the other had mockingly asked, but with many testimonies of both Testaments, composing a distinguished work:

Against the Arians, four books.

He also wrote other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

*John*, priest of the church of Antioch, rich in reading the ancients in the holy Scriptures, after he had taught grammar at Antioch for some years, began to wish to profess himself a cleric. Made a priest by legitimate succession, he devoted himself to the divine readings; made more learned by these, he became so powerful in delivering homilies that he had no equal in his time. He wrote a sufficiently well-instructed volume:

Against the Timotheans, one book. Several Homilies to the People, one book.

And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

*Theodolus*, priest, Italian by birth, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, coming from Italy into Coele-Syria, is recorded as having been ordained there as priest and finally as bishop; and by writing and composing many things he transmitted his name to posterity. Of his little works I have found only a metrical work on the miracles of the Old Testament and the fables of the poets, to which he gave the title:

Eclogues, one book. “The lands of the Ethiopians…”
On the Harmony of the Scriptures, one book.

Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 480, under the emperor Zeno, under whom he also died.

*Eugenius*, bishop of Carthage in Africa, a learned and holy man, a public confessor of the undivided Trinity, suffered many injuries at the hands of the Arians while they were persecuting the Christians in Africa. For with many other bishops, their tongues cut out, he was sent into exile. He wrote some little works full of faith and learning, of which the following are reported:

Exposition of the Catholic Faith, one book. Apology for the Faith, one book.
Disputation Held with the Arians, one book.
To His Church, when he was going into exile, one letter.

He is nevertheless said to have written other things, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.
