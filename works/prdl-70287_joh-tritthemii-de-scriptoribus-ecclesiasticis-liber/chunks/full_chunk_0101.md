*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

And certain letters to others. He died under Emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1031.

Burchardus, formerly a monk of the monastery of Lobbes of the Order of St Benedict, bishop of Worms in Germany, once a hearer and disciple of Albert of Gembloux, abbot at Lobbes, of whom mention is made in the canon, distinction 73, *In nomine domini*, a man most devoted to divine Scripture and very learned, wrote from the opinions of the holy fathers, of general councils, and of the supreme pontiffs a great and distinguished work after Isidore, namely the *Decrees*, which he divided into twenty books: 'In the New Testament after'. Letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished under the holy Emperor Henry, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, of the Order of St Benedict, in the diocese of Constance, German by nation, a man learned in divine Scripture and very nobly instructed in secular letters, a musician and poet, second to none in his time, outstanding in life and conduct, wrote certain excellent volumes both in verse and in prose; of these I have found only the following. For Piligrim, otherwise Peregrinus, archbishop of Cologne, he composed an excellent and useful volume: On music or tones, two books: 'Every regular mo[de?]'. On the instruments of music, one book: 'That music is not to be contem[ned?]'. On the coming of the Lord, to Aribo, one book: 'Of highest dignity'. Also on the office of the Mass, one book. On the fast of the Ember Days, one book. On the fast of Saturday, one book. On the measure of the monochord, one book. Letters to various persons, one book.

He also published many other things which have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of St Henry, the first emperor but the second king, because the first did not receive the crown of the Empire, in the year of the Lord 1026.

Adelboldus, bishop of Utrecht, formerly, as they say, a monk of Lobbes of the Order of St Benedict, under whose persona Abbot Heriger composed the dialogue on the coming of the Lord, a man learned in divine Scripture and nobly instructed in secular letters, held in great esteem at the court of the holy Emperor Henry, overthrew the unbelievers no less by arms than by learning. He wrote: The Life of the holy Emperor Henry, one book. On the praise of the Holy Cross. On the praise of the blessed Mary.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

But he also published several other works, both in verse and in prose, which have not come to my knowledge. He died under Emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1027, in the tenth indiction, in the eighteenth year of his episcopate, on the fifth day before the Kalends of December.

Osbertus, monk of Canterbury of the Order of St Benedict, a man learned in divine Scripture and highly skilled in the art of music, subtle in intellect and brilliant in eloquence, published various treatises in both literatures, by which he transmitted the fame of his name to posterity. Among other things he wrote: The Life of St Dunstan, archbishop, one book. Also On music, one book.

He flourished in the times of the holy Emperor Henry, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Odilo, the fourth abbot of Cluny, of the Order of St Benedict, a holy and learned man, presiding over the monastery for fifty-six years, directed the souls of many to Christ by word and example. Peter Damian, bishop and cardinal of Ostia, described his life, full of virtues and miracles, in a lucid discourse. He composed for the edification of his brothers: Elegant sermons, one book.

He died under Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord 1048, in the [unclear] indiction, in the eighty-seventh year of his age, on the first day of the month of January.

Fulbertus, bishop of Chartres, most learned in divine Scripture and, in the disciplines of secular letters, the most learned of all the learned men of his time, a famous poet and dialectician, presided for many years over the public school and trained very many most learned hearers. Most holy also in life, he is read to have shone with many miracles. Among other things he is said to have written: Letters to various persons, one book. In praises of St Mary, one book. Various hymns and prayers. And certain other devout works.

He flourished in the times of King Henry I, in the year of the Lord 1010.

Bruno, bishop of Würzburg, cousin of Emperor Conrad II, German by nation, a man devoted to divine Scripture, most holy in life and conduct, who, because of the excellence of his merits, was inscribed in the catalogue of the saints and is held to be the special patron of eastern Francia. From the writings of the ancient fathers, a beautiful exposition: On the whole Psalter, one book: 'Blessed is the man who has not gone' [unclear].
