Arnoldus Bostius, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Ghent, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, exceptionally well taught in secular letters, a theologian, philosopher, orator, and outstanding poet, keen in intellect, eloquent in speech, devout in life and conduct, of whom several letters to me are extant, wrote in verse and prose many distinguished little works, of which the following are extant: a notable volume composed in verse and prose, in which he clearly shows the prerogative, origin, and progress of his order in view of Marian protection; for this reason he was pleased to entitle it

*On the Patronage of Mary*, 1 book. “You asked, most distinguished man...”
*On the Illustrious Men of the Carthusians*, 1 book.
*Against Vincentius of Nieuwpoort*, 1 book. “Brother Arnoldus Bostius...”
Letters to various persons, 1 book. “How long always...”
*On the Four Last Things*, 1 book.
Various poems.

Henricus Euticius, an East Frank, an excellent professor of both branches of medicine, has published many things which he is preparing for posterity. Also, in diverse and elegant script, a very beautiful little work of poems, which he entitled

*Scommata, or Urbane and Witty Jests of Young Men*.
Several little works in medicine.
A satire against sophists and dialecticians, and enemies of the study of humane letters.
In praise of the Blessed Virgin.
And many other things.

He is still living when we wrote these things, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Etelwolfus de Lapide, a noble man, a German by nation and a Swabian, a man of great talent and a zealous emulator of Roman eloquence, and for that reason having obtained great honor with the Margrave of Brandenburg, wrote to Georgius de Lapide, his maternal uncle, a man most devoted to letters,

*On the Praises of Heroes and Illustrious Men*, 1 book.
A most polished letter collection.
And other things, by which he will win great praise for Germany among cultivators of the Roman tongue. He is still living today under King Maximilian, in the year in which we wrote this, 1494.

Rodulphus Langius, a German by nation, canon of the church of Munster in Westphalia, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a philosopher, orator, and very celebrated poet, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote, both in verse and in prose, many distinguished little works, by which he spread his name far and wide with immense glory. Of these the following are extant: To Hermannus Langius, dean of the same church,

*On the City of Jerusalem*, 2 books. “Once most splendid...”
To the dean of Cologne, poems, 1 book. “Whom bright virtue...”
*On the Siege of the City of Neuss*, 1 book.
A poem *On the Three Magi*, 1 book.
Several letters to various persons.
Also various poems.
And many other things.

He is still living, very old, in the city of Munster, composing many things under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.
