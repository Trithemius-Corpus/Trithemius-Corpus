*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

...divided into ten distinctions, which he entitled:

*On Medicine of Every Kind*, book I.
*To Pope John XXII*, book II.
*On the Book of John Mesue*, book III.
*On Aristotle's Problems*, book IV.
And certain other works.

He translated Galen *On Black Bile* and *On the Regimen of Health*, together with many others.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Sibertus de Beka, a German by nation, from Gelderland by homeland, of the Order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior provincial of Lower Germany, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained both in canon law and in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect and brilliant in eloquence, who shone in his time no less by his manner of life than by his learning, wrote certain commendable small works, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are reported:

*On the Sentences*, book II.
*A Summary of Censures of the New Law*, book III.
*On the Rule of His Order*, book IV.
And certain other works.

He also corrected the ordinal of his order with sagacious purpose.

He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and of Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Clement, the fifth pope, born in Gascony, formerly called Bernard, bishop of Bordeaux, sat after Benedict XI in the chair of Peter for eight years and ten months. He was a learned and energetic man; at his command the Order of Templars was destroyed throughout the whole world on one day. He celebrated the Council of Vienne, in which he established many things for the benefit of the Church. He composed the seventh book of the Decretals, which John XXII, his successor, published, as is stated at the beginning of the same. It is commonly called:

*The Code of the Clementines*, book I. *The foundations of the Catholic faith*...

He died while Henry VII was governing the Roman Empire, in the year of the Lord 1314, in the twelfth indiction.

Petrus de Perpiniano, called Rimi, a Catalan by nation, of the Order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the holy Scriptures and a subtle expounder of scholastic philosophy, quick in intellect and an excellent declaimer, published many small works of reading not to be despised. By exercising in them the abundant vein of his talent, he made his name known even to posterity. Of these there are reported:

*Questions on the Sentences*, book I.
*On the Whole Psalter*, book I.
*Various Sermons*, book I.

But concerning other works that he composed, nothing has ever come to my knowledge.

Dericus, of the Order of Friars Minor, a holy and learned man, an excellent preacher of the divine word, because of his most fervent love for it took up a pilgrimage, penetrated Asia and India, where by preaching and teaching and proclaiming the faith of Christ, he converted many from iniquity to the path of justice. His most holy life was confirmed by many signs both in the flesh and after death. Among the other small works of his talent he wrote:

*A History of His Pilgrimage*, book I.
Also *Various Sermons*, book I.
And certain letters.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Franciscus Marionis, of the Order of Friars Minor, formerly a hearer of John Scotus, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly trained in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, composed in style, constant in study, sufficiently acute and very fit for disputing and resolving questions of the Scriptures, wrote not a few distinguished volumes of his talent, in which he showed himself most learned and transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Sentences*, book I. *Concerning the proemium of the first*...
*On Formalities*, book I. *Various Questions*, book I.
*Sermons on the Season*, book I. *Sermons on the Saints*, book I.

He is also said to have composed certain commentaries on the divine volumes and some diverse treatises, which have not yet at all come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.
