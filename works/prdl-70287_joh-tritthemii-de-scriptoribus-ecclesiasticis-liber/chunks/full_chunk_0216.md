*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Distinguished for [sermons?] to the people, he wrote certain little works, of which the following are reported:

On the Epistle of Paul to the Romans, one book.
A Preceptory of the divine law, ten books. “Sacred Scripture of the divine...”
On the sacrament of the altar, one book.
Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Various disputed questions, one book.

He also published some other works, which have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in his convent at Tienen, in the year of the Lord 1476, in the ninth indiction, on the sixteenth day before the Kalends of August.

Bartholomaeus Cepolla, a Veronese by nation, a very swift jurist and an eminent cultivator of other disciplines of secular literature, who acquired great praise for his learning by lecturing regularly, disputing, and writing in the Paduan gymnasium, wrote many distinguished little works, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among these there is a truly most subtle work, which he entitled:

The Legal Precautions of Civil Causes, one book. “It is especially to be noted...”
On simulated contracts, one book.
On urban estates, one book.
Also on military affairs, one book.
And several others.

He died in the time of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1477, in the tenth indiction.

Johannes de Regio Monte, a German by nation, formerly a pupil of the astronomer Georgius Burbachius, no less than Anaximander of Miletus or Archimedes of Syracuse, most learned in Greek and Latin eloquence, the glory of the Germans, on account of astronomy and the other mathematical disciplines endowed with a perpetual stipend by Matthias, king of Pannonia, and by the magistracy and citizens of Nuremberg, obtained great praise for his learning not only among his own Germans, but also among the Italians and the French. For, summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus to correct the Paschal computation of Dionysius, while he was preparing many things for posterity, he died of disease, with very great loss to scholars. He wrote certain distinguished little works, of which the following are extant:

A very beautiful Calendar, one book.
Ephemerides, that is, a Diary, one book.
Against Gerardus Cremonensis, one book.
After we sent out the index of letters and orations, one book.
And several others.

He died at Rome under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1476, in the ninth indiction.

*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

[unclear], studious and learned, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, keen in intellect, distinguished in life, after he had gloriously taught theology for many years in the Cologne gymnasium, having held the vanities of the world in contempt, in his old age entered the Order of Preachers for love of God in the same city of Agrippina, and was soon appointed inquisitor and exterminator of heretical depravity. Among other things, he wrote, as they say:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Disputed questions, one book.
Sermons and conferences, one book.
Various things also in philosophy, one book.
And several others.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1475.

Alexander Tartagnus of Imola, a jurist, studious and learned, and well experienced in the institutions of other secular disciplines, excellent in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote, for the benefit of those who keep watch over the study of both laws, certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number the following are reported:

On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, one book.
Also on the Clementines, one book.
Also on the Code, nine books.
On the Old Digest, fourteen books.
On the New Digest, twelve books.
On the Infortiatum, fourteen books.
And certain others.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Johannes Soreth, a Norman by nation, the twenty-fifth prior general of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, sweet and clear in eloquence, in life [unclear].
