*On Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

Hadrianus, priest-cardinal of the title of St. Chrysogonus, [Batonien̄], composed many very distinguished books, since he was a man of great learning; of these the following circulate:

On Latin speech, or modes of speaking Latin, one book.
A Hunt addressed to Cardinal Ascanio, in Phalaecian verses.
The Journey of Julius II, Roman pontiff, in heroic verses.
On True Philosophy, four books, compiled from the four doctors of the Church.

He flourished under Julius II, supreme pontiff, and Maximilian.

Haymo and Strabo, brothers of Bede, also learned men, of whom the one wrote certain not unlearned homilies, the other on Genesis.

Heliodorus of Antioch, priest, extraordinarily practiced in sacred letters, published an excellent volume on virginity, furnished with holy Scriptures.

Henricus Bebelius of Justingen, German by nation, poet laureate, wrote an art of composing poems, corrected and enlarged by him, together with his [racamationes] or observations to be followed in verse. By the same: The Instruction of Boys; The Triumph. And many other things, among which are those scurrilous jests, by no means worthy of a free, much less a Christian and learned, man.

He flourished under Emperor Maximilian, in the year 1514.

Henricus Glareanus, a Swiss from the district of Glarus, poet laureate, was the first, or among the first, of the men of our nation successfully to learn good letters, and to teach them publicly. He professed for some time at Basel; then he moved to Freiburg, a town of Breisgau, where he is still living today, a very old man, if I am not mistaken, and still teaches. He is to be praised on this account, that he cultivated several liberal arts, antiquity, and histories excellently, and illustrated them by writings. Of those which have come into our hands, these are:

Scholia on Aelius Donatus’s method concerning the eight parts of speech.
An Introduction to Arithmetic.
A Description of the Situation of Switzerland and the Neighboring Peoples.
A Panegyric on the League of the Four Swiss Peoples.
A Panegyric for the Most Just League of the Swiss.
An Introduction to Music.
On Geography, one book.
A Judgment on the verses of P. Terentius throughout all the comedies.

*In Abbot Span[heim], On Writings.*

Annotations on Horace.
Annotations on Ovid’s Metamorphoses.
Annotations on Cicero’s book On Old Age.
Annotations on the fragments of Sallust’s Histories.
Annotations on the commentaries of C. Julius Caesar.
Annotations on Titus Livius.
Chronology for the whole of Roman history.
He revised Dionysius of Halicarnassus and illustrated him with annotations, and added a chronography of the times comprised in Dionysius’s history.
Two books of elegies. And he is said to have published certain other things.

He flourishes under Emperor Charles V, in the year 1546.

Henricus Suso, a Swabian, of the Order of St. Dominic, an excellent preacher, renowned for holiness of life and also for miracles, wrote some works both in Latin and in German which powerfully kindle the mind to the love of God. Of these there is extant in Latin:

The Clock of Eternal Wisdom.
And One Hundred Articles on the Passion of the Lord.

He flourished around the year of the Lord 1350.

Hermannus Torrentinus, a German, almost the most learned among the grammarians of his age and nation, wrote commentaries on the first part of Alexander’s Doctrinale, with the interpretation of words, some faulty, superfluous, and obscure verses being either rejected or changed into truer and clearer ones.

There are also extant by the same man commentaries on Virgil’s Bucolics and Georgics. He also published a Poetic Elucidarium.

He flourished under Maximilian in the year of the Lord 1500.

Hermas, whom the apostle Paul mentions when writing to the Romans, “Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermas,” etc., is said to be the author of the book which is called The Shepherd, and in certain churches of Greece it is even read publicly. It is truly a useful book, and many of the ancient writers have employed testimonies from it; but among the Latins it is almost unknown.

Hieronymus Cardanus of Castiglione, a Milanese physician, a man not unlearned, composed two books On Consolation.

There is extant by the same man a most complete Arithmetic.
