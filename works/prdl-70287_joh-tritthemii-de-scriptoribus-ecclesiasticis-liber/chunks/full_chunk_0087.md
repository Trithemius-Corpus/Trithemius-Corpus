By order of Charles he arranged histories and readings suitable for the individual feasts of the saints throughout the whole year. And he organized and wrote many other things. He flourished in the times of the same Charles the Great, in the year of the Lord 780.

Adrian, pope the first, a Roman by nation, after Stephen the Third sat in the pontificate for twenty-three years. A learned and most holy man, held in the highest honor by the emperor Charles, he established many things for the benefit of the Church, and condemned the Felician heresy in a synod of three hundred and fifty bishops, by order of the emperor Charles, at Frankfurt. Since he was studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, he abridged the thirteen books of letters of Saint Pope Gregory into two, selecting the more useful parts. He also wrote from his own inspiration to Leo the Younger and his son Constantine, emperors of Constantinople:

On the Veneration of Images, 1 book.
To the Emperor Charles, 1 book.
Letters to various persons, 1 book.

He also wrote quodlibets.

He died while Charles the Great was emperor, in the year of the Lord 795, in the third indiction, on the day of the Nativity of the Lord, the seventh day before the Kalends of January.

Ruggandus, bishop of Metz, grandson of King Pippin through his sister, noble in birth, made noble by holy manners and by study of the Scriptures, an outstanding venerator of the bodies of the holy martyrs, desiring to instruct the clergy and people committed to him in righteousness by word and example, wrote for the clergy of the church of Metz a sufficiently instructive and useful Rule:

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

The Rule of Canons, 1 book. And certain other things.

He transferred the bodies of the holy martyrs Gorgonius, Nabor, and Nazarius from Rome into France; the body of Saint Nazarius was placed in the monastery of Lorsch, then of the order of divine Benedict, in the diocese of Worms.

He flourished under King Pippin, father of the emperor Charles, in the year of the Lord 760.

Leo, pope the third, a Roman by nation, after the pontiff Adrian sat for twenty years. He was a man very learned in both divine and secular writings, and also most renowned for the holiness of his life and conduct. He anointed Charles, surnamed the Great in deed, to the empire of the Romans, and transferred this dignity from the Greeks to the Franks. Among other things he wrote:

Letters to various persons, 1 book.

He died under Charles on the Ides of June, in the year of the Lord 816, in the ninth indiction.

Haymo, the third bishop of the church of Halberstadt in Saxony, formerly abbot of the monastery of Hersfeld, of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, once a hearer of the monk Alcuin, for whom Rabanus, then abbot of Fulda, composed twenty-two books On the Universe, was certainly a most learned man in every respect, a most sagacious interpreter of the divine Scriptures, and of excellent talent in delivering homilies to the people. He was first a monk of the monastery of Fulda with Rabanus; afterward he became the third abbot of Hersfeld; later, consecrated the third bishop of Halberstadt, he vigorously governed that church by word and example for thirteen years.
