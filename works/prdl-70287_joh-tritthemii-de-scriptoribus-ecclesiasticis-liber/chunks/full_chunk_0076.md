Cassiodorus, monk and abbot of a monastery near the city of Ravenna, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and, in secular letters, easily the most erudite of all in his time, shone forth most illustriously no less in holiness than in knowledge. He was first chancellor of Theoderic, king of Italy, and a senator of Ravenna, and later consul of the city of Rome; afterward, despising the vain honors of the world for the love of God, he became a monk. From then on, applying the study of humane letters to the exposition of the holy Scriptures, he composed many very elegant treatises. He also composed many other works which have not come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished in the times of Justin the Elder down almost to the end of the reign of Justin the Younger, being more than 58 years of age, in the year of the Lord 575.

Arator, subdeacon of the sacrosanct Roman Church, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures and nobly learned in the letters of secular disciplines, powerful in verse and prose, subtle in intellect, clear and polished in speech, wrote a very notable volume, composed in meter, to a certain abbot Florianus:

On the Acts of the Apostles, 1 book. “That Judea of its crime”

To Abbot Florianus, 1 epistle. “They who flourish in merits”

To Pope Vigilius, 1 epistle. “With wave-beaten walls”

And certain other things which I have not seen.
