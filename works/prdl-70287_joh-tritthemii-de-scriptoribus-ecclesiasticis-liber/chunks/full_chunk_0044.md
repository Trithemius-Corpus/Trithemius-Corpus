*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...wrote for the edification of readers certain distinguished volumes, of which these are considered the more important:

Against Avarice, 2 books. On the Illumination of the Man Born Blind, 1 book. Several Homilies to the People, 1 book. And other works unknown to us. He flourished under the emperors Theodosius and Arcadius, in the year 390.

Macarius, an Egyptian monk and formerly a disciple of the blessed father Antony, a devout man of most holy way of life, about whom many miracles are told, and how he even obtained the voices of the dead from tombs for the confirmation of the faith, sent to all religious men a useful letter, which is: To Monks, as one book. “In the first place indeed [unclear].” And other letters to others. He flourished in great holiness under the emperors Constantine and Constantius, in the year of the Lord 350. His deeds are contained in the Lives of the Fathers.

Faustinus, a priest, a man very learned in the sacred scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, of the Luciferian party, and for that reason deservedly blameworthy, wrote many excellent volumes, which are judged useful for the refutation of heretics; of these I have found only the titles of a few:

Against the Arians and Macedonians, 7 books. An Apology to Valentinian, one book.

He flourished under Valentinian and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390.

Jerome, priest and monk, born of his father Eusebius, from the town of Stridon, a man learned in secular literature and, among all the doctors, most learned in the divine scriptures, remarkable for his knowledge of many languages, interpreter of the sacred law, hammer of heretics and most powerful assailant: he is the true evangelical steward, faithful and prudent, who, bringing forth from his treasury things new and old, and giving to his fellow servants in due season the measure of spiritual wheat, despoiled the proud Hebrews and enriched the humble Latins. He is the distinguished doctor of the Church of Christ, who, with his pious mother Rebecca, that is, the grace of God, brought forth the garments of Esau, wandering astray through the byways of errors, laid away idly at home, and clothed meek Jacob, faithful and serving Christ. He is Jerome, the sincere lover of Jesus Christ, who, passing no time idly, meditated day and night on the law of the Lord, and, scarcely allowing his body necessary sleep, always by day and night wrote, read, taught, or prayed, as the almost countless books that he published testify. This defender of truth, cultivator of virtue, most bitter enemy of vices,

*Abbot of Spanheim*

when he was a priest of the Roman Church and was steadfastly accusing the crimes of the clerics, withdrew from the city because of their envy; and coming into Judea, he visited the glorious sepulcher of the Lord, and, establishing for himself a dwelling-place beside the manger at Bethlehem, became known in glory to the whole world, divine providence arranging this, namely, that there the light of the world should rest where the true sun of righteousness had become known to human beings by being born. A holy and fitting inhabitant of the most holy place, firm in faith, kindled with charity, mature in conduct, who could not be led astray by prosperity, whom no adversity ever cast down from his holy purpose. But why do I, a foul praiser, touch the holy head of the man with impure lips? It would have been more humble for me to be silent than to speak insufficiently about so holy a man. Now therefore let us set down in order what we have found concerning his writings and little works; of these there are:

On Faith, to Damasus, 1 book. “We believe in God the Father.”

On the Faith of the Nicene Council, 1 book. “We believe in one God.” “Almighty God the Father.”

On the Essence of the Trinity, 1 book. “Very few days.”

Against Jovinian, 2 books.

Against Helvidius, 1 book. “Recently, asked by the brothers.”

Against Vigilantius, 1 book. “Many monsters in the world.” “It comes very near that he who.”

Against the Luciferian, 1 book. “Not boldly, as falsely.” “Already written to Ctesiphon.” “About ten years ago.” “If according to the apostle Paul.” “Both by your letters and those of many.” “Having read the letters of your prudence.” “That up to now to you.” “Never, Oceanus, would he himself.” “To the most blessed Pope Damasus.” “You sent me a volume.” “In the previous letter which.” “In the seventy-seventh Psalm, which.” “Among the letters of your Blessedness.” “My son Apodemius.” “You ask me that for you.” “Now in the very moment of departure.” [unclear]

On the Same, 1 book. On Virginity and Marriage, 1 book. On the Vision of Isaiah, 1 book. On Melchizedek, 1 book. On True Circumcision, 1 book. On the Forty-Two Stations, 1 book. On the Prodigal Son, 1 book. On Twelve Questions, 1 book. On Eleven Questions, 1 book. On Anna and Joachim, 1 book. On the Resurrection of the Flesh, 1 book.
