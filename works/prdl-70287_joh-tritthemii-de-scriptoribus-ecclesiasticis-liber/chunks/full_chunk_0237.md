Jacob Wimpheling of Schlettstadt, a German by nation, priest of the church of Speyer, a man most learned in divine matters and excellently instructed in secular letters, a very celebrated theologian, philosopher, orator, and the most outstanding poet of all, first at Erfurt and then in the school of Heidelberg publicly expounding philosophy and sacred Scripture, acquired great glory for his learning. A man, I say, most learned in every respect, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, excelling both in verse and in prose, he wrote in both kinds some distinguished little works, by which he spread his name far and wide. To this Jacob we long ago dedicated the book On the Luminaries, or Illustrious Men, of Germany.

He lives in manly age, being almost forty-three years old, in the renowned city of Speyer, and writes various works under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Baptista de Salis, of the Order of Friars Minor, of the Genoese province, a man studious and learned in divine Scriptures, excellently instructed both in both laws and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, devout in life and conduct, and distinguished in the observance of regular discipline, published certain little works necessary for the instruction of simple priests, by which he spread his name far and wide. Of these there is extant the Summa on Cases, very useful in the forum of conscience, which after the author’s name is called the Summa Baptistiniana, one book. And some other works.

He completed the same Summa under Emperor Frederick III. And he still lives while Frederick’s son Maximilian holds the kingdom, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Egelhard Funk of Schwabach, an East Franconian, German by nation, a learned man and skilled in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, orator, and poet, not ignorant of divine Scriptures and canon law, outstanding in intellect and eloquent in speech, excelling in verse and prose, is said to have fashioned certain little works in both kinds of style, by which he made his name known. In elegiac verse: On the Praise of His Fatherland, one book. On a Ring Given to Him, one book. Various epigrams. And several other works.

He is renowned at Rome as a pleader or advocate in cases, and he composes various works under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

John Langer of Bolkenhain, a priest of Wrocław, a man studious and learned in divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in intellect and eloquent in speech, excelling in verse and prose, worthy of the priesthood in life and conduct, wrote many distinguished little works in both kinds of style, by which he made his name known. Of these there is extant a notable work addressed to George de Lapide, lord of Steinau, which he entitled On Revenues and the Commonwealth, one book. [Incipit:] “Today I am oppressed by a great...” Letters to Various People, one book. Many poems. And several other works.

He still lives in Wrocław and writes various works under Maximilian, King of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.
