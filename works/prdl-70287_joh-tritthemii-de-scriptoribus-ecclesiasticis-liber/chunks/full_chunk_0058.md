Evodius, bishop in Africa, disciple of the blessed Augustine, bishop of Hippo, to whom Augustine wrote the treatise *On the Soul of Christ among the Dead* and many letters, a man learned in the holy Scriptures and most upright in the conduct of his life, who deservedly ought to be placed in the catalogue of the saints, is said to have written certain devout little works. Of these there is extant a volume on the miracles done in Africa through the relics, brought by Orosius the priest, of Saint Stephen the martyr, [unclear book-count]. And several letters to various persons, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Saint Augustine, in the year of the Lord 420.

Palladius, the first bishop ordained by Pope Celestine for the island of the Britons or Scots, Hibernia, Greek by birth, a learned and holy man, by his most sweet preaching converted to the faith of Christ the faith entrusted to him. After him Saint Patrick, a Briton by race, consecrated by Saint Celestine the pope as archbishop of Hibernia, shining for forty years with signs and miracles, converted the whole island to the faith. Palladius wrote:

*Life of Saint John Chrysostom*, [unclear book-count]. And certain other things.

He was sent into Scotland by Pope Celestine I in the year of the Lord 452, in the fifteenth indiction, according to Marianus Scotus.

Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, [unclear] by nation, once an outstanding [unclear] and cultivator of Carmel, a learned and holy man, by command of Pope Celestine I presided at the council of two hundred fathers at Ephesus, in which, condemning Nestorius and Pelagius together with their errors, he uttered many elegant opinions. Since he was most learned in the holy Scriptures, he wrote many little works, of which these are reported:

*On Faith against the Heretics*, one book. *Against Nestorius*, one book.
*On the Defection of the Synagogue*, one book. *On the Birth and Praise of Saint Mary*, one book.
*On the Paschal Cycle of Ninety-Five Years*, one book. *Commentary on the Gospel*, one book.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

Commentaries on Leviticus, arranged in sixteen books.
A work of treasures on the Trinity, extant in Greek in Italy.
Greek commentaries on several prophets, Isaiah, Hosea, Daniel, and certain very varied homilies, are preserved in Italy.
A Greek book on the fact that through all the books of Moses the mystery of Christ is signified enigmatically. Dogmatic solutions of questions which certain persons had put to him from the *Treasures*.
An exposition of the prayer of the prophet Habakkuk, in Greek.
A book *On Adoration and Worship in the Spirit of Truth*, and that the shadow is a type of life in Christ according to the Gospel, addressed to Palladius in dialogue form, with one interpreting and one responding, in Greek, in Italy.
Seven orations, in Greek. Certain Greek homilies on the sacred creed. A counsel on concord between Greeks and Latins, in Greek. Two letters of the same man to Successus, bishop of Diocaesarea in Isauria. One letter to Augustine on the miracles of Saint Jerome. A *Practica* collected by the same man before the third synod. A book entitled *Ta glaphyra*. A Greek vocabulary, and a little work on words which change their meaning by accent, with a Greek-Latin lexicon. All these are preserved in Italy.

Besides these he composed several letters, homilies, and sermons, and other diverse treatises, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished, presiding at the Council of Ephesus, under the emperor Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 432, in the fifteenth indiction.

Theodore of Ancyra, bishop of Galatia, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters, especially strong in dialectic, eloquent in speech and a keen disputant, being quite distinguished in the Council of Ephesus, wrote there, in dialectical fashion and very obscurely:

*Against Nestorius*, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under the prince Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 430.

Sozomen, Greek by nation, an excellent cultivator of secular letters and sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures, is said to have written some little works for the edification of the Church. Of these there is extant a distinguished work addressed to the prince Theodosius the Younger, from the third consulship of the Caesars Crispus and Constantine down to the seventeenth consulship of the same Theodosius:

*Ecclesiastical History*, nine books. “The thought once came to me...”

The other things which he composed have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the aforesaid emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 450.
