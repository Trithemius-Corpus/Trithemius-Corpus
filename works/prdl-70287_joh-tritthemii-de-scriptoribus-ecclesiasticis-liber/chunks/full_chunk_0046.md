Several sermons, one book. “Since the ancient east...”

These are the writings of the most blessed doctor Jerome which at present occurred to us to record. Nor do we doubt that he wrote more, which are not contained in this catalogue, chiefly for this reason: that they either remained unknown to us or dispersed among many things. For among all the doctors of the Church none was of more benefit than Jerome, who, besides innumerable small works, translated the Old Testament from Hebrew into Latin, corrected the New according to the Greek truth, and faithfully offered it to the Church to be read. He also made many volumes of the Greeks Latin, namely those of Origen, Josephus the Jew, Eusebius of Caesarea, Didymus of Alexandria, Victorinus the martyr, and others.

At last, after many labors, full of days, he died under the rulers Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 422, in the fifth indiction, on the last day of the month of September, at the hour of Compline, in the ninety-ninth year of his age; he was buried at Bethlehem, shining with many miracles. Eusebius, his disciple, described his life.

47 *The Abbot of Spanheim*

Audientius, a Spanish bishop, a man having a mind exercised in the divine Scriptures, wrote against the Manichaeans, Sabellians, and Arians, yet with his principal intention against the Photinians, who afterward were called Bonosians, an outstanding work, which is entitled *On the Faith against Heretics*, one book.

He also published certain other works, which have come to my knowledge.

Rufinus the monk, priest of the church of Aquileia, an eloquent man, very learned in the divine Scriptures, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, was of no small reputation among the ecclesiastical doctors. Although his writings are many, nevertheless they are counted among the apocrypha, distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*, because he wrote invective letters against the most holy man Jerome, as can be understood from the reading of them. But afterward, when they returned into the concord of brotherly love, the little works of Rufinus were made authentic, insofar as they were not contrary to the sayings of Jerome. Concerning the writings of Rufinus, therefore, the following are reported:

On the prophet Hosea, one book. On the prophet Amos [unclear].
On the prophet Joel, one book. “To me indeed, most faithful...”
On his faith, to Bishop Laurentius, one book. “I have read your writings...”
Invectives against Jerome, one book. “I heard certain persons, when...”
On his faith, to Anastasius, one book. On the sacraments, one book.
On the blessings of the patriarchs, one book. Additions to the Ecclesiastical History, two books.
An exhortation to Proba, one book. [unclear]. He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius.
Letters to various persons, two books.

He is said to have composed many other things besides, but they have not come into our hands. He also translated into Latin the volumes of many Greeks, namely of Clement, Origen, Basil, Gregory the Great, bishop, Pamphilus the martyr, Eusebius of Caesarea, Evagrius, Sixtus the philosopher, and many others, all of which he marked with his own prefaces and prefixed letters. He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius; he wrote many and various works, in the year of the Lord 400.

John, bishop of Constantinople, once, while still a priest at Antioch, a hearer and disciple of Eusebius, bishop of Nyssa, who because of the charm of his speech is named Chrysostom, that is, “golden mouth,” a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, was of excellent ability in delivering homilies to the people, distinguished no less for holiness than for learning, and was held by the people in great reverence and love. This most blessed bishop endured many wrongs from Theophilus of Alexandria, and by his fraudulent and deceitful machinations [unclear].
