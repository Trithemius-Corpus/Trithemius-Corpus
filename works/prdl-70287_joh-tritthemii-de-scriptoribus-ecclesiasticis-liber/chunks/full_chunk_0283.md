By the same man, *Paedologia*, or a little book on the conduct of boys.

Under his name there are also circulated *Nugae*, epigrams.

He flourished at Paris in the year of the Lord 1533.

Nicolaus Clenardus of Mechelen, a man most skilled in the three languages, for many years publicly taught humane letters at Mechelen, and, in order to benefit everyone, even those absent, brought to light certain works in Latin, Greek, and also Hebrew, among which the following are recorded:

Most complete and very brief *Institutions* in the Greek language.

To these have been added Greek meditations, for the benefit of those who lack the living voice of a teacher.

By the same man, a Hebrew grammar, or rather tables of that language.

I think that he is still alive.

Nicolaus Dathus, son of Augustinus Dathus, wrote a book *On the Praises of Eloquence*, and a life of his father, which is found prefixed to the works of Augustinus; but, overtaken by death, he did not complete it.

There are certain poems in that book in praise of his father; others are *On the Duty of a Scribe*.

Nicolaus Saguntinus, most eloquent in both languages, equally ready in talent and fluency, was an interpreter at the council which, under Eugene IV, was held between the Greeks and the Latins, both on other articles of the faith and on the procession of the Holy Spirit, at Ferrara and Florence.

He translated Plutarch’s book *On Civic Precepts*, and Onosander the Platonist’s *On the Best Commander*.

Nilus, a holy man, exactly learned in Greek, wrote monastic orations, together with fifteen theological little works, in φωτικοῖς chapters.

By the same man, a book to Charicles the bishop, too harsh toward sinners, who said that repentance alone did not suffice for confession.

Some works are extant at Rome, and others in Italy in Greek.

Nicolaus Villagogus, a Rhodian knight, published, in the form of a little book, a notable letter on the armed expedition of Charles V against Algiers, depicting that campaign, in which he himself took part, so graphically that the matter seems almost to be taking place, not merely to be narrated.

Because this letter was most elegant and bore before it, as it were, a certain majesty, it was inserted, together with other events done around that time, into the chronicles of Nauclerus printed at Cologne in the year 1544.

The author Nicolaus himself flourished under the emperor Charles V, in the year of the Lord 1541, perhaps still living, and likely hereafter to become more famous through the publication of similar monuments.

Octavianus Mirandula, an Italian by nationality, a regular canon of Saint Augustine of the Lateran, a man renowned for no common honesty and learning, collected the flowers of illustrious poets from the best and ancient poets, twenty-five in number.

The work is no less useful than laborious.

Olympiodorus, an outstanding paraphrast of sacred Scripture, wrote most learned scholia on Ecclesiastes, inserted with a new translation, unfamiliar to Latin ears, namely that of Aquila, Theodotion, and the Septuagint.

Olympius, a Spaniard by nationality, a bishop, a man learned in sacred letters, wrote a book on faith against those who blame nature and not free choice, showing that evil was implanted in nature not by creation, but by disobedience.

Orontius Fineus of Dauphine, royal professor of the liberal disciplines in the Academy of Paris, wrote *Protomathesis*, a varied work, no less useful than pleasant to know.

By the same man, a universal astrolabic quadrant.

Demonstrations on the first six books of Euclid.

A recent and complete description of the whole world, in the likeness of the human heart.

A new description of all Gaul.

He flourished, if I am not mistaken, in the year of salvation 1543.

Paduanus de Grassis of Barletta, master of sacred theology, regent in the school of the great house of Venice, wrote a scholastic *Enchiridion* of the quodlibetal contradictions of the subtle doctor Scotus.

Pacianus, [or?] Tatianus, father of Dexter, to whom Saint Jerome wrote the book *On Illustrious Men*, bishop of Barcelona in the ridges of the Pyrenees, distinguished for chastity and eloquence, and as much by life as by speech, wrote various little works, among which is [unclear], and *Against the Novatians*.

He died under the emperor Theodosius, already in extreme old age.
