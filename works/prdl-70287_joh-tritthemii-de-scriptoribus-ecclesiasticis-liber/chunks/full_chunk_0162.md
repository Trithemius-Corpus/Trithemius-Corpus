He died under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1358, in the eleventh indiction.

*Simon of Cascia*, of the order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man [unclear], zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, who indeed is said to have been illumined by the spirit of prophecy in the Florentine convent, published several volumes of great usefulness, in which he showed himself a learned and devout man. Among these I found an outstanding work on the whole Gospel, which is commonly entitled:

*The Deeds of the Savior*, one book. “From the good and super-good...”

*On Christian Doctrine*, one book. “The imitation of God in men...”

*On Patience*, one book. [unclear]

*Exposition of the Creed*, one book. [unclear]

*On the Mirror of the Cross*, one book. [unclear]

*On the Discipline of Spiritual Men*, one book. [unclear]

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book. [unclear]

And certain other works.

He ended his life under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 140[unclear].

*Simon of Speyer*, a German by nation, of the order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, master of Paris, most learned in the holy Scriptures, and nobly instructed in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse, taught theology for a long time with distinction in the school of Cologne, and won from all great praise for his learning. He wrote an outstanding volume:

*On the Sentences*, one book.

*Against the Jews*, also one book.

*A Postil Read at Cologne*, [unclear].

He published some other things recently, but they have by no means come to our knowledge.

*Sertorius*, whom some call Fortunerius, the nineteenth minister general of the order of Friars Minor, an Englishman by nation, from [Galenis?] by homeland, afterward archbishop of Ravenna, and finally patriarch of Grado and cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, published certain distinguished volumes by which he made his name known also to posterity. Among these there is extant an outstanding work:

*On Augustine’s City of God*, twenty-two books.

Concerning the rest I have found nothing.
