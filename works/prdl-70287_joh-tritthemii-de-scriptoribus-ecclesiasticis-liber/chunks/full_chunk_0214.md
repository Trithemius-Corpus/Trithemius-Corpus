*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

... won praise for his [unclear] from everyone; in the same university he wrote and lectured on certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, 4 books.
Sermons for collation, 1 book.
Various disputed questions, 1 book.
And many other things.

This man’s brother, Theodericus Grefmunt of Meschede, doctor of arts and medicine, a man most learned in every respect, still survives and resides at Mainz; he himself, among other things, elegantly composed a treatise On the Regimen of Health in Time of Pestilence. He has a son called by his own name Theodericus, scarcely completing three lustra of age, whose admirable talent, and singular learning as well as eloquence, far surpasses many of advanced age, even doctors; there is extant with me his notable book On the Seven Liberal Arts. For he has written both to me and to others almost countless letters, most ornate in whatever subject, and various speeches displaying Ciceronian fluency, to such a degree that, like another Adeodatus, once the son of the most holy Augustine, by his talent, readiness in writing and arguing, and subtlety, he is an object of admiration and astonishment to many, even learned men.

Godescalcus, moreover, the brother of his father, flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1470. And he died before this decade.

Stephanus Hoeft of Ladenburg, formerly a canon of the church of Speyer, German by nation, a man learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, sharp in intellect, fluent in speech, distinguished in delivering sermons to the people, since for some years he held the office of preacher in the castle of Heidelberg, wrote works not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there are extant:

On the Books of the Ethics, 10 books.
Sermons for collation, 1 book.
Certain poems and other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III, Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1471, in the fourth indiction.

Bernardus Justinianus, a Venetian by country, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, outstanding in intellect, fluent in speech, who on account of his singular grace in speaking was held in esteem not only before his own Venetian senate, but also before the Roman pontiffs, cardinals, the other princes of Italy,

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

and the senators of cities, wrote, among the other monuments of his talent, concerning Laurentius Justinianus, patriarch of Venice, his maternal uncle:

His Life and Most Holy Way of Living, 1 book.
Also Histories, 1 book.
Several elegant speeches, 1 book.
Letters and certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, Pope Paul II, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1470.

Enricus de Piro, German by nation, sprung from the city of Cologne, of the Carthusian order, professed of the house of St. Barbara in the same city, before his entry into the order a subtle and famous doctor of both laws, afterwards also exercised in the divine Scriptures, wrote certain excellent volumes, of which the following are reported:

On [initiotis], 4 books. “With all things read to me granting consent...”
Also To Novices, 1 book.
Counsels in Diverse Cases, 1 book.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
And certain other things.

There is frequent and celebrated mention of him in the Acts of the Council of Constance. He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III, Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Nicolaus Donis, German by nation, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man studious and learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished mathematical philosopher, the most watchful discoverer and most sagacious restorer of the Cosmography of Ptolemy, excellent in intellect and renowned in eloquence, wrote volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant a wonderful work, with new pictures and tables most elegantly ordered and most diligently corrected:

On the Cosmography of Ptolemy, to Pope Paul II, 7 books. “It does not escape me, most blessed...”
Also On Wondrous Places, 1 book. “Since, as Augustine says...”
Various letters to various persons.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 147[unclear].
