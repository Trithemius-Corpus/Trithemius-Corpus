Johannes de Ragusio, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, produced certain excellent little works of his ability, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have read a well-constructed work addressed to the fathers of the Council of Basel: *Against the Hussites*, one book, “Most reverend, venerable [fathers?] ...” Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished in person at the Council of Basel, where he publicly delivered the said treatise against the heretics before the fathers, under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1458.

B

Londus Flavius of Forlì, secretary of Pope Eugene IV, a man skilled in every discipline of the liberal arts, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, and an eminent historiographer, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, and second to none in grace of expression, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

*Three Decades*, so entitled.

*On the Deeds of the Romans*, three books, “Of the Roman empire ...”

*On Rome Triumphant*, ten books.

*On Rome Restored*, five books.

*On Italy Illustrated*, eight books.

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

*Various Orations*, one book.

And several other works.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund, under Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1435. He left five sons from a legitimate marriage, cultivators of the liberal arts.

Heimericus de Campo, a German by nation, vice-chancellor of the University of Cologne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent and an outstanding disputant of difficult questions, wrote many excellent little works; nevertheless, few of these have so far come into our hands. He was personally present at the Council of Basel, sent by the University of Cologne, where, at the urging of Master Nicholas of Cusa, later cardinal, he produced *Compendium of Questions*, one book. Afterward, on several occasions [unclear].

*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

*On the Sentences*, four books, “As Augustine says in the book ...”

*On Being and Essence*, one book, “Being and not-being are opposed ...”

*Compendium of Divine Things*, four books, “In this little work under ...”

*Various Questions*, one book.

*Many Sermons*, one book.

*On the Authority of the Council*, one book.

And several other works.

He flourished at the Council of Basel, where he wrote and disputed much, in the year of the Lord 1438, under Emperor Albert.

Georgius Trapezuntius, apostolic secretary, a man most learned in every respect, a very celebrated philosopher, rhetorician, and poet, who, having been put by Pope Eugene in charge of the public school at Rome, taught rhetoric and poetry for many years and left after him many disciples most skilled in every science, wrote certain excellent volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these are extant:

A very beautiful *Rhetoric*, one book.

A singular *Dialectic*, one book.

*On the Orations of Cicero*, one book.

Various orations and letters.

And certain other works.

He flourished at Rome in the times of Pope Eugene IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1435.

Florianus de S. Petro, an Italian by nation, a native of Bologna, easily the most learned of all men of his time in civil law, and not ignorant of either the divine Scriptures or secular philosophy, sharp in talent, distinguished in eloquence, a subtle disputant and cautious counsellor, wrote in that faculty certain very excellent little works, by which he commended the memory of his very celebrated name to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Code*, nine books.

*On the Old Digest*, twenty-four books.

*On the New Digest*, twelve books.

*On the Infortiatum*, fourteen books.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1435.
