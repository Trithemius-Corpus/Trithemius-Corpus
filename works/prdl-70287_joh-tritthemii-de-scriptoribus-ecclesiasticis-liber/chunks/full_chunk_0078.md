There is extant among the Eberbach books a brief and learned disputation of Gregory against the Arians, which begins: “For love of the Catholic faith.”

When he was still a levite, in the person of Pope Pelagius he composed a very distinguished letter to Elias, bishop of Aquileia, who refused to accept the Three Chapters of the Council of Chalcedon; this letter is not found among his letters. It is reported also that he composed many other works, namely on the prophets, on the Books of Kings, also on the books of Moses, and on other holy scriptures. After his death, before these had yet been multiplied, his most wicked rivals, envying his glory and sanctity, burned them with fire, as John the Deacon complains in the book of his life. The most blessed Pope Gregory died in the second year of the emperor Phocas, which was the year of the Lord 605, in the eighth indiction, on the fourth day before the Ides of March, shining with many miracles.

Leander, bishop of Seville, formerly a monk, by country from the province of Carthagena in Spain, born of his father Severianus, was a man most devoted to study and nobly learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, pleasing in speech, most excellent in talent, and also holy in life and conduct and beloved of God. By his preaching and industry Hermenegild, son of Leuvigild, the Arian king of the Goths, was brought back to the confession of the orthodox faith and, on the vigil of Easter, was killed by his heretical father and received the crown of martyrdom. His brother [Richardus?], as soon as he received the kingdom after his father’s death, at Leander’s urging and preaching renounced heresy and converted the whole people of the Visigoths subject to him to the Catholic faith. To this Leander blessed Gregory dedicated the volumes of his commentaries on Job. Leander himself wrote several little works, of which the following are reported. When, on account of the Catholic faith, he had been banished into exile by the Arians, he wrote:

Against Heretics, 2 books.

Against the institutions of the Arians, 1 book.

On Virginity, to Florentina, 1 book.

Prayers in a double edition on the Psalms, 1 book.

On Contempt of Death, to his brother, 1 book.

On the praise of sacrifices, 1 book.

Letters to various persons, 1 book.

But he published some other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the year of the Lord 590, under the emperor Maurice. He died under [Richardus?], the most devout prince of the Goths, renowned for miracles.

Priscian the grammarian, by country from Caesarea, a man most learned in secular letters, skilled in Greek and Latin eloquence, held in great esteem at the court of Chosroes, king of the Persians, wrote certain distinguished volumes in the grammatical art, which among the ancients were held in great authority. Of these the following are reported. To Julian the patrician:

On the Parts of Speech, 2 books.

On Construction, 1 book.

On Accents, 1 book.

On Cosmography, 1 book.

On Natural Questions, to Chosroes, 1 book.

He is said to have written some other works also, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished, as some hold, in the times of Chosroes, king of the Persians, and of the emperor Heraclius, in the year of the Lord 610.

Marcus, a monk, disciple of the holy father Benedict and very dearly beloved by him, a man learned in the divine scriptures, most pious in secular letters, a distinguished orator and poet, eminent in morals and conduct, wrote after blessed Pope Gregory, adding certain things in a most elegant flowing heroic verse:

The Life of Saint Benedict, 1 book. And certain other things.

He was illustrious down to the reign of the emperor Heraclius Augustus, in the year of the Lord 610.
