*Abbot of Sponheim.*

He is said still to be living and to be composing various works under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Hieronymus of Padua, an Italian by nation, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and remarkably learned in secular letters, a philosopher and distinguished poet, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote certain little works of no contemptible reading, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there is extant a very fine little book, entitled *Iesuida*, addressed to Petrus Donatus, bishop of the city of Padua, in verse, one book: “Greatest of the heavenly ones, [unclear].”

Other works of his are also extant.

He is said still to be living and to be composing various works under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Franciscus Mataracius of Perugia, a man learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, orator, and most celebrated poet, and highly skilled in both languages, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of these there is extant a very celebrated little work addressed to the young Petrus Paulus Cornelius, *On Composing Verses*, one book: “The poetic art the ancients...”

*Letters to various persons*, one book.

Many epigrams.

And several other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick and Pope Sixtus IV.

Dominicus Mancinus, a man very learned in the divine writings and remarkably learned in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain approved little works, by which he made his name known. Of these I have read only that treatise which he composed in elegant verse, *On the Passion of the Lord*, one book: “Not the praises of men, nor...”

He also wrote other works.

He is said still to be living and to be writing various things under Maximilian, most noble king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Ludovicus Brunus, an Italian by nation, from the marquisate of Montferrat, a man most learned in every respect, a professor of each law and a very celebrated interpreter, a laureate poet, a distinguished philosopher and orator, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote many distinguished little works in both kinds of writing, but I do not remember that any of them came into our hands except that treatise which he set out in elegant verse, *On the Coronation of King Maximilian*, one book: “Holy through the eternal [unclear].”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He is still living; he is said to be writing various works under the same King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Jacobus Sprenger, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, provincial of Cologne, a man studiously learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, clear in eloquence; since he had previously been appointed by Pope Innocent VIII as inquisitor of heretical depravity, together with Henricus Institoris, a theologian of the same order, he gathered together and wrote, with the help of the same colleague, a volume against witches, those little women who are instruments of the devil, for the precaution of the simple, which he entitled *The Hammer of Witches*, three books: “Whether to assert [unclear] witch...”

If he wrote anything further, I do not know.

He is still living today in his convent at Cologne, under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote this, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Johannes Tholofus, a German by nation, originating from East Francia, which is called Franconia, canon of Regensburg, provost of Forchheim, a man studiously learned in the divine Scriptures, remarkably learned in secular letters, a distinguished doctor of pontifical law, an astronomer, cosmographer, and most celebrated poet, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote certain distinguished little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he commended his name to posterity. But I do not remember that any of them came into our hands. Those who have known the man well report that there are, by him, on various subjects:

*Elegant Letters*, one book.

And certain works on mathematics.

And certain other works.

He is still living today in the city of Regensburg in the province of the Norici, and is writing various works; he is a very great patron of learned men, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493, in the twelfth indiction.
