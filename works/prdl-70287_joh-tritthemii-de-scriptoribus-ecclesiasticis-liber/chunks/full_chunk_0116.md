Peter, the Parisian dialectician, called Abelard, a Gaul by birth, a man most learned in secular philosophy, attempting to investigate higher matters, overwhelmed by glory, fell into many errors; through the action of the holy abbot Bernard, he was condemned with his depraved doctrines in the council of Sens. From this time secular philosophy began to defile sacred theology with its useless curiosity. This Peter wrote some little works which, if he had not mixed errors into them, could have benefited posterity. Of these there survives a volume overshadowed by dialectical obscurity, varied with many opinions, which he wished to entitle

His Theology, 1 book.
Know Thyself, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Conrad, the third emperor, in the year of the Lord 1140.

Adelphus, an abbot of the order of Saint Benedict, a man skilled in divine scriptures through long practice, not ignorant of secular letters, an indefatigable cultivator of regular observance, so that by word and example he might show himself a useful shepherd to the sheep of Christ entrusted to him, is said to have forged some little works for their benefit, in which a rich vein of talent commends an adorned eloquence. Of these there survive elegant

Sermons to the Brethren, 1 book.
Against the Saracens, 1 book.

The rest which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

William, abbot of Saint-Thierry, afterward, as he says, a monk of Clairvaux under the divine father Bernard of the Cistercian order, a man studious and learned in divine scriptures, and also distinguished by the holiness of his life and conduct, and for that reason singularly beloved by him, published little works not to be despised in reading; of these few have come to my knowledge. He began to write, but, forestalled by death, did not complete,

The Life of Saint Bernard, Abbot, 1 book. Incipit: “Scripturus vitam servitui.”
The Mirror of Faith, 1 book.
The Enigma of Faith, 1 book.
Sentences of Faith, 1 book.
To Theophila, on the Nature of the Soul, 1 book.
On Contemplating God, 1 book.
On the Nature and Dignity of Love, 1 book.
On the Sacrament of the Altar, 1 book.
Against Peter Abelard, 1 book.
On the Song of Songs, unfinished, 1 book.
On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, 1 book.
And certain other things.

There are some who say that this William, while he was still abbot of Saint-Thierry, wrote that well-known letter to the Carthusians whose beginning is “Fratribus de monte Dei orientale lumen,” which is ascribed by many to blessed Bernard. He flourished under Conrad, the third emperor, in the year of the Lord 1140. And he died before Bernard, when he had completed one book on his life; Arnold, abbot of Bonneval, added four other books to it.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Conrad, a monk of the monastery of Hirsau on the borders of the Swabians, of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, of the diocese of Speyer, a man most learned in divine scriptures, very skilled in secular letters, an eminent philosopher, rhetorician, musician, and poet, composed many splendid little works under the name Peregrinus. In them the adorned arrangement of the thoughts and the charming cultivation of speech declare their author inferior to none of the ancients. Of these, only the following have come to my knowledge:

A distinguished work of dialogues, under the name of Peregrinus the priest, addressed to Theodora, a virgin consecrated to God, which he entitled
The Mirror of Virgins, 1 book. Incipit: “Collaturus tecum, o Theo-.”
On the Gospels through the Year, 1 book. Incipit: “Quia in littera veteris te-.”
On the Life of the Spirit, the Fruit, and Death, 1 book. Incipit: “Cum omnis divinae paginae.”
The Matricularius, 1 book. Incipit: “Cum manifestum sit, mun-.”
On the Praises of Saint Benedict, in verse, 1 book. Incipit: “Luce velut solem.”
Didascalon, 1 book. Incipit: “Quia te video diu va-.”
On Music and the Tones, 1 book. Incipit: “Musica est secundum cui-.”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.
