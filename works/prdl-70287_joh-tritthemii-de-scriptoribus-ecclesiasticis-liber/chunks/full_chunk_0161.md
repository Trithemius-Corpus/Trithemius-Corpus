*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

On the Sentences, 4 books. "Concerning the first [unclear]"
Quodlibets also, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished at Paris and completed his lecture under Emperor Louis the Bavarian, the fourth, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1344, in the twelfth indiction. And not long after that same lecture of his he died at Paris and was buried there in the convent.

Bernardus Parentinus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and skilled through long practice, and not moderately learned in the scholastic disciplines, subtle in intellect and distinguished in speech, of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people, wrote for the instruction of simple priests certain volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant:

An Exposition of the Mass, 1 book. "Since the Wise Man cries out"
Various sermons, 1 book.
And several other things.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord [1340?].

Osbertus, English by nation, of the Order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man certainly most learned in the holy Scriptures, and exceptionally learned in secular philosophy, of acute and forceful intellect, scholastic in eloquence, subtle in debating and resolving questions of the Scriptures, wrote certain excellent little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, 4 books. "One conclusion having been proposed"
Various determinations, 1 book. "It is asked whether [unclear]"
Various sermons also, 1 book.

The rest that he composed have not been able to come to the knowledge of our reading.

Johannes Estuidi, English by nation, a man easily the most learned of all his time in secular letters, a philosopher and astronomer second to none, outstanding in intellect and sweet in speech, wrote certain excellent little works of his intellect, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of a later age. Of these there is extant a notable work of judicial astrology, concerning the accidents of the world, which is commonly called:

The English Summa, 2 books. "My intention in this book"

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished, and completed the same Summa, as he states at its beginning, under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1347, in the fifteenth indiction, on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of January.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Bernardus, abbot of the monastery of Monte Cassino, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in life, and a notable cultivator and propagator of regular observance, wrote for the edification of monks certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are extant:

The Mirror of Monks, 1 book.
On the Rule of Saint Benedict, 1 book. "It is read in Proverbs"
On Regular Precepts, 1 book.
Sermons to the Brothers, 1 book.

He is also said to have composed many other things, which have by no means reached us.

Symon de Cremona, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of the liberal arts, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in speech, quite excellent in delivering sermons to the people, since indeed at Venice he labored for a long time in this office, where by word and example he instructed very many, wrote for the instruction of simple preachers little works not to be despised, of which the following are extant. To Antonius, his brother, a priest, sermons:

On the Gospels, 1 book. "Lift up your heads, because"
On the Epistles also, 1 book. "Let us cast away the works of darkness"

The rest that he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading.

Petrus Raimundi de Insula Grassa, French by nation, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the fifteenth Prior General, who after Petrus de Casa, from Limoges, vigorously governed his order for fifteen years, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, wrote elegantly:

On the Sentences, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished and was elected general from the convent of Carcassonne in the province of Narbonne, under Emperor Louis IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1343, in the eleventh indiction.
