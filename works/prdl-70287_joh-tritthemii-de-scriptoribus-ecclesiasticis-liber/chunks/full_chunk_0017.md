*Prologue of the Author to the Catalogue or Following Book*

Often, at the urging of many people, I have been asked to complete, when at last an opportunity should present itself, the book on ecclesiastical writers which I had once begun, for the purpose of gaining knowledge of books. I confess that I delayed for a long time, lest, if I presumed to undertake so arduous a matter unskillfully, I should provoke the hatred of rivals through my own levity.

But now, since to the request of friends there has been added the counsel of learned men, who by their arguments encouraged me to take up this task, I could no longer resist their petitions, especially since this little work of ours is supported by the patronage of such great men. For why should I fear the hatred of detractors, when I see that a refuge among the learned lies open to me? There will be, I do not doubt, some who will judge this labor of ours worthy of praise; there will also be some who, with a zeal of rivalry, will disparage it. What then? Shall we therefore abandon the exercises of good works, lest we provoke the hatred of rivals? Far from it. Whoever does not imitate the wicked offends them; but whoever fears to offend the envious does wrong to the good. And indeed it is praiseworthy to have displeased the wicked, who are accustomed to love nothing unless it is matched to their own morals.

Now therefore, my friends and timely exactors, receive the book composed on ecclesiastical writers, which with diligent prayers you so greatly desired to be brought to completion. I have done what I could, and have expended my ready strength on this work, although in many things the capacity itself is less than the desire. For I, after the manner of those who see dimly, entered this wood under the guidance of the fathers who went before, and, gathering from their little works the several things suited to my purpose, I carried them together into this bundle. Many writers also, found by long investigation, I have inserted into our Catalogue with great toil and expense.

Do not be surprised, excellent reader, if in the annotations and titles of books my opinion does not always agree with yours. Who, amid so manifold a variety of books, could enumerate the opinions of individuals, let alone make them agree? You will therefore grant pardon if at times you find a volume designated in one way or another differently from what your opinion holds. For it was not possible for me everywhere to express the differences of individuals. For, as I have already said, I followed the footsteps of the fathers, and where they disagreed, I embraced what I believed to be truer. But if you find something else that rests on a more solid truth, I shall gladly yield to your judgment.

It pleased me, moreover, to proceed by a new manner of writing, so that the reader himself might more easily find knowledge of the books from their beginnings. Beginning, therefore, with the disciples of the apostles down to the last year of Emperor Frederick III, namely the year of the Lord 1493, I have carried down the catalogue of illustrious men; in it I have recorded not the names, ranks, writings, and times of all men, but only of those whom I found, placing opposite them the beginnings of the titles of the volumes; those not seen I have passed over with a blank space. The times in which certain men flourished I could not find at will; nevertheless, as it seemed to me, I placed them in a suitable position. And in each defect let the knowledge of one who knows supply what is lacking, namely that he may compute the times of individuals and restore those transposed to their proper places.

I confess that I have also deliberately passed over several men whose writings are not easily found, nor their times. I know, too, that there are still many very learned men living, who are said to be publishing various little works; but since I do not have certain knowledge of them, I prefer to pass them over in silence rather than think uncertain things. And, to use the words of Jerome, those still-living writers who have been omitted by me in this catalogue should blame not me but themselves, since they preferred to hide their writings rather than have them come into the light. But I believe that, content with their writings and talent, they will not care about the losses caused by my silence. For even of those whom I found, I wished to portray not praises, but books. This, indeed, the labor of a more prolix volume would demand.

Go now, therefore, into the world, work completed by our labor; go, and dispense to lovers of books the treasure which you contain. What do you fear? Why do you tremble? Why do you delay? Do not be afraid to go forth in public; do not fear the hatred of rivals, supported by the patronage of so many saints. Come, book, slight indeed in talent, but a revealer of great matters: why is it that, as though sluggish and useless, you are afraid? Go forth confidently. A thousand will praise you, while scarcely one disparages you. The love of God, with which you were written, I pray may mercifully excuse you, together with your author, always and everywhere. *End.*
