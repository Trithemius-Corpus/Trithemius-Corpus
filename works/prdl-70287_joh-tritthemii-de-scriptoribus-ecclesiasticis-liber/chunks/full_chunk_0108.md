*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

not unacquainted with literature, and also conspicuous for religion and uprightness of morals, at the command of the venerable abbot Hugh, gathered all the customs of the monastery of Cluny into one volume in lucid language, giving the work the title:

*The Customs of Cluny*, one book.

He wrote other things. He flourished under the emperor Henry III, in the year 1050.

Bernard, monk of Corvey in Saxony, of the order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, a man nobly learned in the divine Scriptures, highly skilled in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote to Hartwin, archbishop of Magdeburg, in language indeed lucid but bitter, a volume:

*Against Henry IV*, one book.

And other things which I have not seen.

He flourished in the times of the same emperor Henry, in the year of the Lord 1070.

Ivo, bishop of Chartres, formerly provost of Saint-Quentin of Beauvais, of the order of regular canons of the divine father Augustine, a man trained in the divine Scriptures, and a most careful interpreter of the canons of the holy fathers and of general councils, revered no less for sanctity than for learning, magnificently reformed his order, which had long fallen into decay. After Burchard, bishop of Worms, he wrote from the canons of the saints a compendious decree, which jurists used before the times of Gratian, which he entitled:

*Panormia*, [unclear]

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book. “Ivo of the church of Chartres” [unclear]

And certain other things.

He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1090.

Hildebert, bishop of Le Mans, afterwards archbishop of Tours, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, acute in intellect and very eloquent in speech, once a disciple of Berengar of Tours but a persecutor of his error, who endured many persecutions and tribulations for ecclesiastical liberty, even to imprisonment, wrote in both styles, that is, metrical and prose, certain excellent little works, of which as yet only a few have come to the knowledge of our reading. He wrote in elegant meter a very beautiful and useful exposition:

*On the Whole Office of the Mass*, one book. “I have proposed to write what...”

*On His Exile*, one book. “Recently I was wealthy...”

*On the Misery of This Life*, one book. “We are taken from the midst...”

*On the Sacrament of the Altar*, one book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

*Hymns and Prayers in Various Meter*, one book.

He comprised the epitaph of Berengar, once his teacher, in fifty-two verses.

He wrote many other things, still unknown to us.

He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1090.

Anselm, archbishop of Canterbury in England, formerly abbot of the monastery of Bec in Normandy, of the order of Saint Benedict, once hearer and disciple of Lanfranc, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in his time in skill in secular letters, whole and most holy in life and conduct, devout in mind, eloquent in speech, vigorous in action, angelic in countenance, mature in gait, exemplary in progress, continual in the study of the Scriptures, was adorned with every notable virtue. He wrote almost countless little works, of which the following are reported among the number:

*Against the Gentiles: Why God Became Man*, one book. “Often, earnestly, by many...”

*On the Holy Spirit against the Greeks*, one book. “Come now, little man...”

*On the Fall of the Devil*, one book. “If anyone [says] one nature...”

*On the Diversity of the Sacraments*, one book. “It is denied by the Greeks that...”

*On Leavened and Unleavened Bread*, one book. “That saying of the Apostle, ‘What do you have...’”

*Monologion*, one book. “To the lord and friend Walram...”

*Proslogion*, one book. “Anselm, servant of the church...”

*On the Will of God*, one book. “Human reason, when concerning...”

*On the Holy Trinity*, one book. “Since free choice is permitted...”

*On the Song of Songs*, one book. “Concerning those three questions...”

*On Peace and Concord*, one book. “Since God is truth...”

*On Free Choice*, one book. “The will in three ways...”

*Dialogue on Truth*, one book. “Since, at the command of your son...”

*On Similitudes*, one book. “Lord my God, give a heart...”

*Meditations*, one book.
