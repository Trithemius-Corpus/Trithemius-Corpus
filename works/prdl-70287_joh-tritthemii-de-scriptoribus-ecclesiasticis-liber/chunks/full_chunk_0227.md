*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

He flourished in Cologne under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1490, a very old man, being about eighty years of age. And I believe that he is still counted among the living.

Marsilius Ficinus, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by homeland, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, most skilled in both languages, the singular glory, ornament, and crown of Platonic theology: he wrote in an ornate style many excellent little works, by which he showed to posterity the vein of his excellent intellect. Of these the following are reported:

A notable *Theology*, one book. “In what manner in [unclear]...”

*On Intellect and Intelligence*, one book.

*On Plato’s Symposium*, one book. “Plato, first among philosophers...”

*Letters*, several books.

And many other things.

From Greek, moreover, he translated many works into Latin. For Piero de’ Medici of Florence he translated fifty-six treatises of Plato. Also Hermes Trismegistus, *On the Power and Wisdom of God*. The book of Xenocrates the Platonist *On Death*, for the same Piero de’ Medici. For Giovanni Cavalcanti he translated the little works of Speusippus, Alcinous, and Pythagoras. For the same man, the book of Alcinous the Platonist *On the Doctrine of Plato*. He wrote, besides, many other things, which have by no means come to the knowledge of our reading.

He flourished at Florence under Emperor Frederick III and the Roman pontiffs Sixtus IV and Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1490. And I believe that he is still among men.

Rupertus of Lecce, bishop of Aquila of the Marsi, or of the city of Abruzzo, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, excellent in intellect, sweet and composed in speech, outstanding in life and conduct, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, famous throughout the whole Christian world, who by word and example converted many from iniquity: he wrote, for the edification of readers, especially preachers of the divine word, certain excellent volumes, of which the following are reported:

*Sermons on the Temporale*, one book. “The night has passed...”

*On the Praises of the Saints*, one book. “Let not the wise man glory in...”

*On Penance through Lent*, one book. “Many are called... because...”

Also *On Sins*, one book.

And certain other things.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Popes Sixtus and Innocent, in the year of the Lord 1486. I do not know whether he is still living.

Johannes Pheffer of Widenberg, a German by nation, an East Frank by homeland, a man learned through long study in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect, devout, gentle, and outstanding in life and conduct; sent from the University of Heidelberg, he illuminated by his learning the beginning of the Freiburg school, in which he has already for many years gloriously taught theology and has properly instructed many of his hearers. He wrote certain excellent little works, among which there exists a notable work on the Epistles of Paul, which, read publicly, he entitled

*Priestly Directory*, thirteen books. “Concerning the reader of the epistle...”

And many other things.

He flourished still among men under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1490. He may still be alive.

Johannes Britannicus, born from Palazzolo, a town of the Brescian district, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished philosopher, notable poet, and lucid rhetorician, who today is held in esteem among his own people of Brescia, whom by inestimable learning, teaching, and writings he makes daily more illustrious: he wrote many and various works, by which he made his name known to posterity and venerable to those present. Of these the following are reported: a very celebrated work of commentaries

*On Persius*, one book.

Also *On Statius*, one book.

*On Ovid’s Metamorphosis*, one book.

Also various *Letters*.

And many other things.

He is flourishing in the city of Brescia and composing various little works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1495.

Baptista of Ferrara, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine writings and excellently instructed in secular literature, excellent in verse and prose, and skilled in both languages, very celebrated and of almost divine intellect in declaiming sermons to the people: he wrote many excellent volumes, by which he rendered himself useful to those present and known to posterity. Of these the following are reported: a most lucid work of histories from the times of Christ down to us, which he entitled

*Florida*, one book.
