The same man wrote, by means of figures, a brief Monotessaron of the Gospels, which is wonderfully useful to preachers of the word of God for aiding the memory; printed at Cologne in the year of the Lord 1542.

Antonius Cermisonus, a man fully instructed in healing bodies, the teacher of Savonarola, is said to have written many little works on medicine, which have not yet come into our hands. I have read only that notable work which he wrote in the form of counsels, one book. He wrote On Urines.

Antonius Codrus Urceus, a man of distinguished learning, was born at the little town of Herberia in the territory of Reggio, which is about seven miles distant from Modena, in the year of salvation 1446. He left to posterity several immortal monuments of his mind; although he was by no means able to put the final hand to them, nevertheless for the most part they show how great was his faculty of expression, how great his talent, and finally how great his learning. In composing and arranging poems, both in Greek and in Latin, he was very ready; in this kind there survive very many of his elegies, epigrams, and Sylvae. Those who read them will see how highly the acuteness of his wit and his judgments are to be valued. To the Greek vocabulary he added many things of his own and corrected many things. He wrote sermons or orations filled with much learning, etc., and many other works, which you have appended below.

Letters, two books. Sylvae, two books.
Satires. One Eclogue.

Antonius Eparchus of Corcyra teaches Greek literature at Venice. He translated into the Latin tongue several books of Polybius’s Histories, not yet published either in Greek or in Latin.

He is still living in the year of the Lord 1546.

Antonius Fumanellus of Verona, a physician, by far the most practiced in the use of the art throughout all Italy, published a complete method for diagnosing and curing all fevers.

By the same man there is a surgical counsel on fracture of the skull, a wound of the breast and lung, and inflammation.

A history and method of cure for difficulty and constriction in passing urine.

Likewise:

Whether mineral water is suitable for difficulty of urination.
On the powers of the iron-bearing bath and on the nature of iron.
On the baths of simple water.

Antonius Gazius of Padua, an illustrious physician, in the year 1490, as he himself testifies at the end of his book, wrote a book On Preserving Health, which he entitled The Flowering Crown, evidently because from the chief opinions of physicians, as though from little flowers, he arranged this work like a certain garland.

He flourished under the dominion of the Venetians in the year of salvation 1504.

Antonius Goveanus, a Portuguese, wrote certain epigrams and four letters.

The same man restored not a few things to their former splendor, among which Virgil and Terence are especially reckoned.

On the same Terence he published very elegant commentaries. He also translated Porphyry’s Introduction on the Five Predicables very elegantly. And he brought to light an exposition on Cicero’s speech Against Vatinius the Witness; the critical part of logic.

Antonius Lodovicus, a physician of Lisbon, a most learned man, composed certain works on medical matters; among these the following are the chief:

On Occult Properties, five books. Problems, five books.

Antonius Mancinellus, a distinguished grammarian, flourished and wrote many works at Venice around the year of the Lord 1490. All his works are comprised for those who choose them in these verses:

The Government of Words, Variation, Ear of Grain, Figure,
Thesaurus, Flowers, these first kept vigil here, that is, in his homeland.
Afterwards Rome gave the Gymnasium, the commentaries on Maro,
Then on the Satirist, thence on Flaccus, and the Versilogus likewise.
Brought to the Venetian shores at the urging of Pomponius,
You write the file of Laurentius, and thence the Method.
You explain the Dreams, who were seen greater, Scipio;
And at the same time the Rhetorician, or the work of Cicero.

Antonius Marinarius, of the Carmelite profession, a most celebrated theologian, composed three books On the Consonance of Jesus Christ and the Prophets. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1545.
