Theophilus, sixth bishop of the church of Antioch, a man very devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in talent and speech, and an excellent declaimer, wrote many volumes for the benefit of the faithful, and shone forth as an excellent expounder of the Lord’s discourses. Of his little works the following are reported:

*Against Marcion*, one book. *Commentaries on the Gospels*, four books.

*To Autolycus*, three books. *On the Proverbs of Solomon*, one book.

He also composed several other short treatises pertaining to the edification of the Catholic Church, which among the Latins are rarely found together. He was ordained bishop in the year of the Lord 173, indiction II.

Apollinaris, bishop of Hierapolis in Asia, a learned man and distinguished defender of the Christian faith, eminent in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote little works of reading not to be despised, in which he showed himself a man not so much learned as zealous. Among them these are reported:

*Apologetic for the Christians*, two books. *On Truth*, three books.

*Against the Cataphrygians*, five books.

But he also published some other works, knowledge of which antiquity has taken from us. He flourished under Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 165.

Dionysius, bishop of the church of the Corinthians, a most eloquent man and very learned in the divine Scriptures, a most vigilant shepherd of Christ’s flock, was of such concern and diligence that he instructed not only the people of his own city with salutary teachings, but also, by letters, summoned the peoples of foreign cities to constancy in the faith. These letters are indeed reported to be so useful and lengthy that they may not improperly be called books; of them the following are listed:

*To the Lacedaemonians*, one letter. *To the Athenians*, one letter.

*To the Nicomedians*, one letter. *To the Cretans*, one letter.

*To the Gnosians*, one letter. *To the Pope and the Romans*, one letter.

*To the Amastrians*, one letter. *To Chrysophora, a woman*, one letter.

He flourished under the rulers Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius, in the year of the Lord 170, not without a reputation for sanctity, and wrote various works.
