He wrote commentaries on the books of Aristotle, and composed many other works which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished at Paris in the year of the Lord 1257.
He died under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280, in the eighth indiction, in the eightieth year of his age, and was buried in the middle of the choir of his convent at Cologne. And after him there arose no man like him, who was so learned, educated, and expert in all letters, sciences, and matters. But as to his being accused of necromancy, this man beloved by God suffers injustice.

Simon of Tournai, priest, learned in the divine Scriptures and subtly instructed in secular philosophy, a very acute dialectician and most skilled in all the liberal arts, having been made master of the theological school, taught for many years in the university of Paris, where he instructed very many hearers. But while he follows Aristotle too much, he is found to have erred in certain matters. He wrote, however, a certain little work of his own talent:
On Athanasius, 1 book.

He also published some other works which have not come to my knowledge.

Thomas, of the counts of Aquino, of the Order of Preachers, hearer and disciple of Albert the Great in the convent at Cologne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, notable in life and conduct, easily obtained primacy among all the doctors of his time. Like another Augustine, he gave himself wholly to the study of Scripture, spending day and night in its love and practice, and wrote so many and such great volumes that among the moderns he has few equals either in number or in usefulness
