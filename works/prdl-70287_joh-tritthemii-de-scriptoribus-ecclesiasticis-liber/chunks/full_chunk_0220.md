Theoderic of Herxen, a German by nation, visitor of the order of the Brethren Clerics Living in Common, a devout man, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, clear in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, wrote many devout volumes for the edification of simple brothers. By usefully exercising his talent in them, he left remembrance of himself to posterity. Among these are reported:

On the Whole Psalter, one book.
On the Lord’s Prayer, one book.
On the Ave Maria, one book.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book.
On the Desire of Dying, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1476.

Dominicus de Dominicis, a Venetian by homeland, first bishop of Torcello and afterward bishop of Brescia, and apostolic referendary, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, excellently adorned with studies of philosophy, dialectic, astronomy, and eloquence, who, because of his great virtues, was held in high esteem by the Roman pontiffs Pius, Paul, and Sixtus, and often fulfilled the office of legation, wrote several small works of excellent talent, but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands. His elegant works are reported:

Orations on Various Subjects, one book.
Various Sermons also, one book.
And many treatises.

He died at Brescia under Emperor Frederick III and Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1478, in the eleventh indiction, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of March, in the sixtieth year of his age.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Ludovicus Donatus, Venetian patrician and bishop of Bergamo, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of scholastic philosophy, also notable for piety, religion, clemency, and gentleness, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, wrote certain splendid small works, by which he left remembrance of himself to posterity. Of these I have never deserved to see anything anywhere. An outstanding work of his to Pope Paul II is reported:

On the Sentences, four books.
Various Orations also, one book.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484, in the second indiction, and was buried in the church of Saint Vincent the Martyr.

Conrad of Rodenberg, a German by nation, abbot of the monastery of St. John the Baptist of Rheingau, in the diocese of Mainz, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a devout and learned man, and an especially zealous guardian and lover of regular observance, who previously for many years held the office of prior at St. Matthias the Apostle in Trier and St. Martin in Cologne: since he was a most ardent lover of the Blessed Mary ever virgin, he wrote in her honor, in plain discourse, a very celebrated volume, taking from each of her members, under the figure of a cluster of grapes, the material of praise; to it he gave the very apt title:

On the Vineyard of the Lord Sabaoth, one book. *Its foundations are in the mountains*.
The Exercise of Novices, one book. *Consider your calling*.
On Preparation before Mass, one book. *Our Lord Jesus Christ*.
On the Ruin of the Order, an oration, or one book. *The crown has fallen from the head*.
On the Causes of Ruin, an oration, one book. *Catch us the little foxes*.
On Pastoral Care, an oration, one book. *Blessed is the servant whom he finds*.
And various conferences in chapters.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1486, in the fourth indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of January, and was buried in the right apse of the choir before the altar of Saint Benedict, toward the ambulatory.
