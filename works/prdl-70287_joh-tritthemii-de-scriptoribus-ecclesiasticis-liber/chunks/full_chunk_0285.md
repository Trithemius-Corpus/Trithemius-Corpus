Under his auspices the books of D. Dionysius the Carthusian, and many other pious and learned books, were brought to light. He himself wrote:

An *Introduction to the Contemplative Life*, 1 book.

*On the Outpouring of the Heart*, 1 book.

A *Handbook for Priests*, 1 book.

*On Divine Goodness*, 4 books.

*Exhortations to Convents of Monks for the Chief Feasts of the Whole Year*, 1 book.

The *Evangelical Candle*, 1 book.

And some other works against the Lutherans.

He died in the year of the Lord 1536, on the very day of St. Michael.

Petrus Crinitus, a very learned man, distinguished for an approved life, left to posterity works not to be despised; from these I shall set down certain ones:

*On Honest Discipline*, 25 books.

*On the Latin Poets*, 5 books.

He also wrote numerous letters.

He flourished in the year of restored salvation 1505.

Petrus Galatinus, of the Order of Minors, a most accomplished doctor of arts and sacred theology, and a member of the household of Cardinal Laurentius Puccius, composed a work, in the manner of a dialogue, *On the Secrets of Catholic Truth Found in the Hebrew Books, especially in the Talmud*, in defense of the most excellent man Johannes Capnio, against the Jewish obstinacy of our age.

He flourished under Maximilian, emperor of the Romans, and Leo X, supreme pontiff, in the year of salvation 1516.

Petrus Gylius, a Frenchman by nation, a man exquisitely learned in Greek and Latin, very highly deserving of good letters, translated Theodoret’s commentaries on the Twelve Prophets; he also promises this in the preface to Philo, book 36.

He gave Latin form to Aelian’s sixteen books *On the History of Animals*, and everywhere added corollaries in their proper places from Porphyry, Heliodorus, and Oppian.

There is also his book on the French and Latin names of the fish of Marseille. He flourished in the year of salvation 1542.

Petrus Martyr of Anghiera, a Milanese orator, formerly [unclear] of Ferdinand and Elisabeth, monarchs of Spain, composed five Decades on the lands recently discovered, or on Oceanic matters and the New World; in these, whatever has been handed down about the lands recently discovered is taught copiously, faithfully, and learnedly, so as to hold the reader eager for new things.

In addition, there are three books of his *Babylonian Embassy*.

He flourished in the year of the redeemed human race 1516.

Petrus Mosellanus of Proteg, a German by nation, and, as I think, to be counted among the foremost restorers of the more polished literature in our age.

Among his writings I found the following:

A *Paedology* written for the use of boys. There are 57 dialogues.

Tables on figures and tropes.

Precepts on the first exercises before the rhetor.

An oration on acquiring knowledge of various languages.

Annotations on Aulus Gellius’ *Attic Nights*, and on book 7 of M. Fabius Quintilianus’ *Rhetorical Institutes*.

He wrote scholia on the *Dialectic* of Georgius Valla.

From Greek he made Latin: Gregory Nazianzen’s sermon on the feast of Christ’s Nativity, and Isocrates’ oration on avoiding war and preserving peace.

He flourished at Leipzig around the year of the Lord 1518.

Petrus Numagen of Trier, a priest at Zurich around the year of the Lord 1483, wrote about Nicolaus Subsylanus, a Swiss, who is piously believed to have lived holily as a solitary for a long time without food.

The same man composed a treatise on apparitions of spirits and phantoms.

Petrus Tataretus of Paris, a man excellently learned in secular philosophy and endowed with remarkable talent, wrote on almost the whole philosophy of Aristotle: on logic, physics, *On the Heavens and the World*, generation and corruption, the *Meteorology*, *On the Soul*, *On Sense and the Sensible*, memory and recollection, sleep and waking, length and shortness of life, and certain matters of metaphysics.

Likewise an exposition on the *Summulae* of Petrus Hispanus with the text, together with additions.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 1509.

Petrus Victor, otherwise Victorius, a very learned man in our age, living at Rome, published a little book on the districts and places of the city of Rome.

The same man corrected Cicero’s books, and added explanations of the corrections.
