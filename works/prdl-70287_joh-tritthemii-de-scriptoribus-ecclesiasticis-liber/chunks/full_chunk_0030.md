He is said to have composed letters to various people and also many other works, which have not come into our hands. The little book On the Twelve Abuses of the Age, ascribed by many to Cyprian, does not have the flavor of his style. At length he suffered martyrdom under Valerian, on the same day as Cornelius at Rome, but not in the same year, namely in the year of the Lord 249, in the twelfth indiction, on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of October.

Pontius the priest, hearer and disciple of blessed Cyprian, a learned and holy man and an outstanding declaimer, after the glorious triumph of his master came into Gaul and at the city of Cymerla won the palm of martyrdom for the faith of Christ. This Pontius, by his honey-flowing preaching and industry, converted the two emperors Philips to Christ, and persuaded them, the first of the Roman rulers, to renounce idols. *Abbot of Sponheim*

He flourished under the emperor Philip, in the year of the Lord 240.

Dionysius, the thirteenth bishop of the city of Alexandria, once a hearer and disciple of Origen, while he was still a priest held the public school under Heraclas, and gained great glory for his learning. For he was a man most learned in every science of doctrines, a distinguished philosopher, rhetorician, and theologian. Agreeing with the doctrine of Cyprian and of the African synod concerning the rebaptizing of heretics, he wrote very many letters on that matter to various people, of which the following are reported among the number:

An outstanding letter of his against Paul of Samosata is extant, which he had written a few days before his death. He also composed many other works for ecclesiastical utility, which are unknown to us. He flourished under the rulers Gallus and Volusianus, in the year of the Lord 255.

Novatian, priest of the Roman church, attempted to seize the episcopal chair against Cornelius; when he could not do so, he stirred up the first schism in the Roman Church and established the heresy of the Novatians, refusing to receive penitent apostates and rebaptizing those duly baptized.
