He also wrote one book *On Logic*; one book, *Introduction of Dignities*; one book *On the Two Wills and Operations in Christ*, etc.; an epistle or one book *On the Trisagion*; one book *On the Hundred Heresies*; one book *On the Dispute of a Christian and a Saracen*; one book of very compunctive hymns; one sermon *On Those Who Have Departed Hence in Christ, That They Are Greatly Helped by Sacred Offices and by the Good Deeds of the Living*. Of all these works, together with certain others, recently printed at Cologne by the Quentel presses, a good part had nowhere been printed before these days, so that you may readily believe the Abbot when he says that he had not seen them; but as to the time at which he asserts this author lived, and likewise as to the other Damascene placed below in his own place, you will not readily believe him.

He flourished under Theodosius, that most devout prince, in the year of the Lord 390.

Ticonius the African, whom blessed Augustine, in the first book *On Christian Doctrine*, records as having belonged to the Donatist party, although in certain matters he disagreed with them, was certainly a sharp-witted man and very much practiced in the divine Scriptures. He wrote not a few little works, of which these are reported, namely: one book *On Civil War*; one book, an exposition of diverse causes; one book *On the Apocalypse of John*; one book of rules for sacred Scripture; several letters to various persons; and many other things.

He flourished when Theodosius and his sons were ruling, in the year of the Lord 390.

Heraclides the monk, a man holy in conduct and distinguished in learning in the divine Scriptures, a most diligent investigator and imitator of the holy fathers, anchorites and monks, who, like a resourceful bee, gathering from the way of life of each one the flowers of virtues into the little hive of his breast, produced the honey of spiritual sweetness. Among other things, for the edification of readers, he wrote an outstanding work on the life and conduct of the holy fathers living in the desert or in a monastery under a rule, whose title is:

*Paradise*, one book.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Gratian, in the year of the Lord 580.

Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, a man very studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently imbued with secular letters, and worthy of the priesthood no less by his way of life than by his knowledge of the Scriptures, wrote, among other things, a useful work *On the Holy Spirit*.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

One book *On the Holy Spirit*.

Of the other things which he is said to have composed, nothing has come to my knowledge.

There are, however, extant in Italy sermons by the same author, which follow: *On the Life and Miracles of Basil the Great*; *On the Sinful Woman Who Anointed the Lord, Against the Pharisee*; *That One Must Not Despair*; certain orations and hymns, all in Greek.

He flourished in the times of the devout emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390.

Epiphanius, bishop of Salamis, a city of Cyprus, a man sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures, was of such sanctity that he deserved to be illumined by the spirit of prophecy and to become a worker of many miracles. He was a very close friend of blessed Jerome and wrote several letters to him. If anyone desires to know his deeds, let him read the *Tripartite History*. He composed some little works, of which these are reported:

One book *On the Eighty Heresies*. “The Simonians, from Simon.” Two books *Against All Heresies*. One book *On the Errors of Origen*. One book *To Theophilus of Alexandria*. One book of letters to various persons. “To the lord, most beloved son.” And certain other things.

He flourished under Theodosius, that most religious prince, in the year 390.

There is extant by the same Epiphanius a Greek book, translated by Albanus Torinus, *On the Life of the Prophets and Evangelists*.

Theophilus, the twenty-first bishop of the church of Alexandria, a man devoted to the Scriptures, a very fierce opponent of the Origenists, who is said to have had great discord with Saint John Chrysostom and, after he had been driven from the episcopate by intrigues, to have compelled him to die in exile, as is contained more fully in the histories of the ancients. He is reported to have written certain little works, of which few have come to my knowledge. There is extant his one book of letters to various persons on various matters. “To the lord most beloved, a.” One book *On Easter*.

He also produced many other things, as is gathered from the letter of blessed Jerome directed to him, in which he asks that they be sent to him either to be read or to be translated. He flourished under the princes Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year 390.
