Johannes of Düsseldorf, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Strasbourg, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, a very celebrated interpreter of the theological faculty, outstanding in talent and illustrious in life, is said to write many things, but to conceal them. I have seen certain treatises of his, whose titles I have not now been able to recall to memory.

He lives to this day at Strasbourg, under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.

Theodericus Ulsenius, a Frisian man, of singular learning and skill in poetry and oratory, has published some things, among which there are extant his

And certain epigrams.
And many other things, which will come down to posterity.

He is still living under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Hartmann Schedel of Nuremberg, a German by nation, doctor of arts and medicine, not ignorant also of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, compiled and wrote, among other little works of his talent, adding some things from Jacobus of Bergamo and other historiographers, especially on the affairs of the Germans, a large and notable work, which contains

Histories of the times, one book.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He lives to this day at Nuremberg, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

402 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Johannes, abbot of the monastery of Saint Martin the bishop, in Spanheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, of the diocese of Mainz, a German by nation, a Mosellan by homeland, born from the village of Trittenheim in the diocese of Trier: although I am not worthy that my name should be placed among the ecclesiastical writers, on account of the poverty of my talent, nevertheless, compelled by urging friends, I have been forced at the end of this catalogue to place the titles and beginnings of my own lucubrations also, after the example of the holy fathers Jerome, Gennadius, Honorius, priest of Autun, and Sigebert, monk of Gembloux, who also placed themselves at the end of their treatises published on these same matters. I have therefore written, besides this work, also the following. And first, by command of the presidents of the general chapter, I abridged

The statutes of the same chapter, one book.
On the visitation of monks, one book.
On the manner of holding a chapter, two books.
On the Rule of Saint Benedict, two books.
An exhortation to monks, two books.
On temptations, two books.
On the life of priests, one book.
On the vice of ownership, one book.
On the misery of this life, one book.
On the ruin of our order, one book.
On the illustrious men of our order, four books.
Praises of the Carmelite order, two books.
On the praise of scribes, one book.
On the luminaries of Germany, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
On Saint Anne, one book.
Various sermons, one book.

As for conferences, whether delivered in general chapters, or in observantial ones, or elsewhere, they are without fixed number; some are preserved, some have been removed. These, then, are the works, besides those which are still unfinished in my hands, which I presently recall having written, in the thirty-fourth year of my age, the tenth of my assumption of the office of abbot, and the twelfth of my monastic life, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and the supreme pontiff Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which I have now for the third time corrected, enlarged, and completed this catalogue, having labored on it for almost seven years, 1494, in the twelfth indiction.
