The same man rendered into Latin the liturgy of Saint Chrysostom.

There is added a Doxology in Greek and at the same time in Latin, which was also in use among the ancients both in the Mass and in morning prayers.

Likewise the Nicene Creed, made Latin from the Greek by the same man.

All according to the exemplar of the library of the monastery of Saint Simeon, which is held to be very ancient at Belgian Trier, with brief annotations by the same Ambrose.

On the celibacy of priests he published a little book filled with learning.

Besides these works that have been enumerated, he also wrote innumerable sermons, and very learned exegeses on various books of the Bible, but not yet brought to light.

He enjoys the air above in the year of the Virgin birth 1546, intending to write many more things for the sake of students.

Ambrosius Spiera of Treviso, of the order of the brethren of Saint Mary, wrote a Lenten book On the Flowers of Wisdom.

Amedeus, bishop of Lausanne, wrote eight homilies on Mary the virgin mother, filled with much learning.

Anacletus, Roman pontiff, a man of most holy life and of learning not to be despised, published decrees and a letter, which is found in the volume of councils.

*To the Abbot of Spanheim, On Writers.*

Five Greek sermons of Saint Anastasius of Antioch, perhaps bishop or presbyter, are preserved there:

1. On the correct dogmas of truth among us.

2. παρ᾽ ἐκπεμψθαι, that is, On the uncircumscribed.

3. On the divine economy or dispensation.

4. On the suffering or impassibility of Christ.

5. On the resurrection.

Anastasius, monk of Mount Sinai, wrote commentaries on the sixth psalm. He also published other things, of which the following are held:

An oration at the beginning of Lent.

On the diverse ways of the soul's salvation.

All these, the catalogue of the Palatine library bears witness, are preserved in Greek at Rome.

Andreas Albius, a physician of Bologna, published an excellent book on medicine, and, if the powers above favor him, will soon give six dialogues on waters. He is still flourishing in the year of the Lord 1546.

Andreas Alciatus, jurisconsult, most famous in our age, wrote a book of Emblems, which contains about one hundred various pictures as if hieroglyphics; their meaning, which follows each one, is acutely explained by elegiac epigrams. The inscriptions are of this kind: The insignia of the duchy of Milan. The treaties of the Italians. On silence. That even the fiercest are tamed. That gratitude is to be returned. Concord. Love the most powerful passion.
