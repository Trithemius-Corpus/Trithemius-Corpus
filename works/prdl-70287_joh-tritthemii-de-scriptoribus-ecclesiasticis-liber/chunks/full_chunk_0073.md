*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Benedict, the first abbot and founder of Monte Cassino, by country a man of Nursia, the foremost founder of the monks of the West, a man of most holy conduct, illuminated with a prophetic spirit, from whose order many very learned and most holy men thereafter shone forth, converted countless peoples from iniquity to Christ by word and example. From his order the Roman see has had eighteen pontiffs, more than two hundred cardinals, sixteen hundred archbishops in various churches, and almost four thousand bishops. Distinguished abbots who were famous for learning, writings, or life: fifteen thousand seven hundred. Those canonized or entered in the catalogue of saints, as we learn from ancient records: fifteen thousand six hundred, apart from those whose names in all these categories have not come down to us. And concerning these illustrious men of our order we have already written four special books, in which we have recorded the names of some, if not of all.

Saint Benedict wrote, in a clear style and with excellent discretion:

*The Rule of Monks*, 1 book.

It has seventy-three chapters.

Under this rule twenty orders serve, which we have set out one by one in the first book *On the Illustrious Men of Our Order*, of which some are rare among us. He flourished under Justin the Elder, in the year of the Lord 530. And he died, as some hold, in the year of the Lord 542, in the fifth indiction, on the twenty-first day of the month of March, when he was sixty-two years old, on the Saturday of Holy Easter. But Marianus Scotus, an excellent computist, writes otherwise, speaking thus: If the holy father Benedict the abbot, as some write, died on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April, on the Saturday of Holy Easter, he is seen to have died in the year of the Lord 601. For unless [unclear] in the ninety-sixth year before that year, his death has no [unclear]; for if he was born then, he was very small. Therefore Saint Father Benedict died, according to this reckoning of Marianus, in the last year of the emperor Maurice, namely in the year of the Lord 601, at about the ninetieth year of his age, in the fourth indiction, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of April.

Boethius, an orator and distinguished poet, learned in the divine Scriptures, in secular letters the most learned of all in his time, was the son-in-law of the patrician Symmachus; a man most abundantly imbued with Greek and Latin eloquence, since he translated many volumes, both of Aristotle and of other philosophers, from the Attic tongue into Latin; subtle in talent, ready in understanding, and Catholic, in speech not inferior even to Tully. It is certain that he had the friendship of Saint Benedict, and that on Monte Cassino he ate at his table with Tertullus, the senator father of the monk Placidus. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which I have found the following. To Symmachus, his father-in-law, patrician of the city of Rome:

*On the Holy Trinity*, 1 book. *Long investigated*.

1 book. *I ask whether the Father, Son*.

*Indeed anxiously and long*.

*I have known the Christian faith*.

*Unity is that by which one*.

*In the winter season of this year*.

*This second [unclear]*.

*When these matters have been dispatched, which belong to*.

*The great things indeed of the book*.

*Alexander in the commentaries*.

*How great things for the studious*.

*Of speaking and disputing*.

1 book. *Many ancient philosophers*.

*At your exhortation, father*.

*Every method of making distinctions*.

*Many ancient Greeks*.

*When all the philosophers*.

*You ask that from the hebdomads*.

*Among all the authors of antiquity*.

*Indeed the perception of all*.

*Songs which certain*.

*But since, my patrician*.

*Your [unclear] knows the intention*.

*On the Predication of Power*, 2 books.

*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.

Besides these, the common Latin translation into two books of Aristotle’s *Prior Analytics* is believed to be by Boethius.

He is also said to have written some other works, but they have not come to my knowledge. He died in the same year as the patrician Symmachus and Pope John, under the emperor Anastasius, by order of King Theoderic at Ravenna, after a long exile, slain in prison, in the year of the Lord 524, in the second indiction.
