He died under Emperor Leo I, who ruled in the year of the Lord 460.

Claudius (others call him Claudianus), bishop of Vienne, to whom Salvianus the presbyter composed one book, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most highly trained in secular letters, famous no less for holiness than for learning, shone in his times. Because of frequent earthquakes he was the first to institute litanies, which the Roman Church thereafter undertook to continue. He wrote On the State of the Soul, three books; also letters, and certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480. And lest the name Claudius trouble anyone, [unclear], let him know that the same man is called Mamertus.

Sidonius, bishop of the Arverni, learned in the divine Scriptures and, in the disciplines of secular letters, in the opinion of all men of his time most erudite, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote many distinguished little works both in meter and in prose. Of these I have been able to find only those which I set down below, namely: Panegyric to Anthemius Augustus, one book: ‘With auspice and number’; Panegyric to Julius Valerius, one book: ‘Conceive the former things, Phoebus, having traversed [unclear]’; Panegyric to his father-in-law, one book: ‘Speak, speak what I ask, [unclear]’; To Felix, one book: ‘Among the dark-blue [unclear]’; Epithalamium for Ruricius and Hiberia, one book: ‘By chance, stormy [unclear]’; Epithalamium for Polemius, one book: ‘Phoebus and the thrice three’; Eucharisticon to Faustus, one book: ‘Four days before’; also Epigrams, one book: ‘The stable of the Bistonian king’; To Pontius Leontius Burg[unclear], one book: ‘Hail, mighty Narbo’; Praise of the city of Narbonne, one book: ‘Long do you command, lord [unclear]’; Letters to various persons, nine books.

He also wrote many other works, short indeed, but sufficiently learned, whose titles I have passed over for love of brevity. He flourished in the times of Zeno Augustus, in the year of the Lord 480.
