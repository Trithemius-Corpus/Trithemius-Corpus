*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Sermons on the saints, one book.
Lenten sermons, one book.
He also published many other things besides: lectures on the sacred books, collations, determinations, and various treatises, which have not come into our hands.

Conrad of Alzey, a town of the diocese of Mainz in the Palatinate, three [unclear] miles distant from Worms, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, a philosopher, poet, and mathematician, in his time most celebrated among the Germans, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, excellent in verse and in prose: he wrote in both styles certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known. Among these there is extant a volume, adorned with beautiful variety in verses and rhythms, entitled On the Most Holy and Most Pure Mary, Mother of God, and the Redemption of the Human Race:

The Work of Figures, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Also Poems, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished around the times of Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370.

Stephen Petringon, an Englishman by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, a most acute disputant, and very distinguished in delivering sermons to the people, published works not to be despised, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of many. Among these are:

Against the Wycliffites, one book.
A Repertory of Arguments, one book.
A Certain Notable Lecture, one book.
Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book. And not a few other things.

He flourished in the University of Oxford at the time when John Wyclif was burned.

Bertramus, bishop of Thefelicensis, of the order of Preachers, a German by nation, suffragan vicar in pontifical matters of the bishop of Metz, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most famous among the doctors of his time, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, also an excellent deliverer of sermons: he wrote to Cuno, archbishop of Trier, a distinguished work entitled:

On the Illusions of Demons, one book. "To the most reverend in Christ..."
To the same, On the Schism, one book. "The wise seeking glory..."
Various sermons, one book.
And several other things.

He died under Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1387, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of February, in the tenth indiction, and was buried in his convent at Koblenz.

Thomas Lombe, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, an Englishman by nation, a man learned in the holy Scriptures through long practice and continual study, and well instructed in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle and keen in intellect, who, having obtained the magisterial chair in the Oxford school, earned great praises for his learning, wrote an excellent work:

On the Sentences, four books.
Against the Lollards, one book.
On the Incarnation of the Lord, one book.
Also Sermons, one book.
And various questions. And certain other things.

He flourished in the University of Oxford, chiefly at the time when John Wyclif in England was exhumed and publicly burned.

Philip Riboti, a Catalan by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, and prior provincial of Catalonia, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, venerable also in life and conduct, and an excellent lover and guardian of the Carmelite order, wrote for the edification of his brothers works not to be despised, in which, showing the fervor of his spiritual zeal toward his religion, he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among these there is extant a volume not to be rejected on the first institution, expansion, and confirmation of his order, which he entitled On the Special Deeds of the Carmelites:

On the Special Deeds of the Carmelites, ten books. "When the Israelite people..."
On the Illustrious Men of the Carmelites, one book.
Sermons and letters.
He composed many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge.

Nicholas Ritzonis, by homeland a Toulousain, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, and prior provincial in the kingdom of Sicily, a learned man, distinguished in life and conduct, and an excellent deliverer of sermons, subtle in intellect, brilliant in speech, published some works on Scripture, the reading of which will not be contemptible to preachers of the word. Among these are reported:

Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book.
