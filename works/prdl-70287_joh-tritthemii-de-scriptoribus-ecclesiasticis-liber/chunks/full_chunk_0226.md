Of these there is extant a large and distinguished volume addressed to Henry, archbishop of Acherontia [Acheruntia?], confessor of Ferdinand, king of Naples, and principally to the king himself, which, gathering from the books of the ancients, he entitled:

On the Property of Ancient Words, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I am uncertain whether he is still alive.

Benedictus Capra of Perugia, a man most celebratedly learned in both laws, and well exercised both in the divine scriptures and in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle in ability and an outstanding disputant, distinguished for knowledge of many ancient and modern matters, taught law publicly for a long time in the Perugian school and acquired a great reputation for his learning. Among other things he wrote:

On the Decretals, 5 books.
Also several Counsels, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480.

Johannes Andreas, bishop of Aleria, secretary of the apostolic library, formerly a companion of Cardinal Nicholas of Cusa; a man devoted to the divine scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a jurist and most eloquent orator, excellent in ability, distinguished in life and conduct, skilled in both Greek and Latin speech, wrote certain little works of no slight authority, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Among their number the following are given:

On the Fourth Book of the Decretals, 1 book.
On the Use of Fiefs, 1 book.
On Appeals, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
And very many others.

He died under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1493, in the eleventh indiction.

Johannes Campeius of Bologna, a most celebrated jurist, and not moderately learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular philosophy, excellent in ability and renowned for eloquence, taught law publicly for a long time in the Bolognese school, where, training many and very distinguished pupils, he gained great fruit and praise from his learning. In that faculty he wrote many outstanding commentaries and treatises, as they report, by which he commended his name to posterity. But none of them has come into our hands. A distinguished work of his is reported:

On Dowry, a treatise, 1 book.
And very beautiful Counsels, with many other works.

He flourished under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484. And I do not know whether he is still alive.

Dominicus Bolanus, Venetian by nation, a man exercised in the divine scriptures and excellently learned in secular letters, a philosopher and rhetorician not to be rejected, sharp and vigorous in ability, composed in speech, published some distinguished little works of his ability, by which he showed himself a devout and faithful worshipper of the Christian name and commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have seen only a distinguished little work against detractors of Marian purity, which he dedicated to Nicolò Marcello, the seventy-first doge of the Venetians:

On the Conception of Saint Mary, 1 book. Not with small grief...

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished at Venice under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484. I hear that he is still held to be alive.

Franciscus Niger, a Venetian priest, a man [unclear] in the divine scriptures, a poet and orator not worthless, outstanding in ability and eloquent in speech, wrote various little works of his ability, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there is extant a work not to be despised, but to be raised up with great praises, addressed to Leonardo Botta, a Milanese knight, entitled:

Grammar, 14 books. From Annaeus Seneca [unclear]

His letters are also reported.
And certain other works.

He flourished at Venice under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. I do not know whether he is still alive.
