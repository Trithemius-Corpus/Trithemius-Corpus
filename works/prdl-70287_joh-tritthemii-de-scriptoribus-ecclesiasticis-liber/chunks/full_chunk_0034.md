Firmianus Lactantius, once a hearer and disciple of Arnobius the rhetorician, a man abundantly learned in secular letters and nobly instructed in the divine Scriptures, so that in the art of speaking he easily held the first place after Cicero, taught rhetoric gloriously, first at Nicomedia, then at Rome under Diocletian, having been summoned by him. There, when through lack of pupils he had fallen into poverty, at last, already an old man, he became tutor in Gaul to Caesar Crispus, son of Constantine. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which these are reported:

He says that he wrote not a few other things besides, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished under the emperor Constantine, to whom he was very dear, in the year of the Lord 320.

Eusebius, bishop of the church of Caesarea in Palestine, surnamed Pamphilus, once a disciple of Dorotheus the eunuch, a very learned man, most devoted to the divine Scriptures and, together with the martyr Pamphilus, a most diligent restorer of the ecclesiastical library: although he was an Arian, when he came to the first council of the Fathers held at Nicaea, he laid aside his error; and, following the opinion of the Fathers, by their command he composed the creed which is called Nicene and offered it to them to read and confirm as testimony of his own most pure faith. In it he vigorously crushed the errors of the Arians, and nothing has been either added to or subtracted from his edition, but to the present day it is publicly sung in the Church of God. Thereafter, persevering in the orthodox faith, he was most holily brought to his end. He wrote in Greek many excellent volumes, of which the following, that is, are current among the Latins:

Against Porphyry, 30 books.
On Isaiah the Prophet, 10 books.
Books of Gospel Expositions, 20.
On Evangelical Preparation, 14 books. “Since what becomes Christians...”
Ecclesiastical History, 10 books. “The successions of the saints...”
Chronicle Epitome, book 1. “Moses of the Hebrew race...”
Theophanies, 5 books.
Topics, book 1.
On the Life of Pamphilus, 3 books.
Martyrology, book 1.
On the Disagreement of the Evangelists, one book.
Apology for Origen, 6 books.
On the whole Psalter, book 1.
He also composed many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Constantine the Great, to whom he was very dear because of his exceptional learning and holiness of life, in the year of the Lord 320.

Rheticius, bishop of the Aedui at Autun, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and in his time a teacher of renowned reputation, is said to have written not a few little works, and especially commentaries on various books; but we remember that few have come to our knowledge, namely:

On the Song of Songs, book 1.
Against Novatian, book 1.
Saint Jerome censures his exposition on the Song as inept. He flourished in Gaul under Constantine the Great, in the year of the Lord 320.

James, surnamed the Wise, bishop of Nisibis, a city of the Persians, a man of great learning and incomparable holiness, one of the number of the confessors victorious under the persecutor Maximian, attended the Council of Nicaea and condemned the most impious Arius by opposing the homoousion. This man of most holy conduct often delivered the city entrusted to him from many dangers by his prayers. He wrote, for the edification of the faithful, not a few little works in the Syriac tongue, of which these are reported:

On Faith, book 1.
On Love of Neighbor, book 1.
On General Charity, book 1.
On Prayer, book 1.
On Fasting, book 1.
