He had nothing to leave, apart from the church library, which even at his death he most diligently commended to the brothers to be guarded. Poor in possessions, rich in merits and knowledge, Augustine the bishop rested in the Lord; Possidius, bishop of Calama, wrote his life.

Atticus, bishop of the city of Constantinople after blessed John Chrysostom, a man learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, was nevertheless blameworthy in this, that he allowed himself to be ordained in place of a living bishop who had been unjustly expelled; from this matter many scandals arose in the Church of God. He wrote little works not to be despised by us, among which there is an outstanding work to the queens, daughters of the emperors of Constantinople:

*On Faith and Virginity*, two books.

And not a few other things.

He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Paulinus the priest, disciple of Saint Augustine of Hippo, afterward bishop of Nola in Campania, to whom many letters and various treatises of Augustine and Jerome are said to have been addressed, a man most learned in the holy Scriptures and second to none in his own time in secular letters, shone forth no less in holiness than in learning. Concerning him blessed Pope Gregory writes in the third book of the *Dialogues* that he was of such piety and mercy that, after he had spent all his possessions for the use of the poor and the liberation of captives, he even handed himself over into slavery for the son of a certain widow; from that slavery he was finally released, together with many captives, on account of the merit of his life. He wrote many volumes, as much in merit as in number, of which the following are reported. First, at the request of Saint Augustine, he wrote:

*Life of Saint Ambrose of Milan*, one book. *Life of Saint Felix, bishop of Nola*, one book.

*On the Sacraments*, one book. *On the Death of a Baptized Infant*, one book.

*Panegyric of Theodosius*, one book. *Hymns in Various Metre*, one book.

*On Contempt of the World*, one book. *On Penitence*, one book.

*On the Blessings of the Patriarch*, one book. *On the Praise of the Martyrs*, one book.

*To Severus Sulpicius*, four letters. *Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

He also published some other works which have come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 420.
