*[page] 83. ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

Avitus, bishop of Vienne, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a theologian, rhetorician, and most renowned poet, who in his time was held in esteem no less for the sanctity of [his] life than for the erudition of the Scriptures, wrote, both in verse and in loose prose, not a few little works; but very few have come to my knowledge. Of these, the following, composed in heroic and elegant meter, are reported:
On the origin of the world: 1 book. On original sin: 1 book.
On the sentence of God: 1 book. On the flood of the world: 1 book.
On the passage of the Red Sea: 1 book.
On virginity, to his sister Fuscina: 1 book.
Of letters to various [persons]: 9 books. Of many homilies: 1 book.
And many others.
He flourished under the princes Zeno and Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 500.

Godelbert, presbyter, learned by continual exercise in the divine Scriptures and rich in old reading, and moreover most erudite in secular letters, a profound theologian, renowned orator, and distinguished poet, subtle in talent and adorned in eloquence, wrote in heroic meter a most renowned work, running most elegantly through histories and allegories from the beginning of the world up to the childbirth of the Virgin; the title of which work is:
Allegories of the Scriptures: 1 book.
But he wrote also several others.
He flourished, as they report, in the times of Anastasius Augustus, in the year of the Lord 500.

Eugypius, presbyter and abbot, erudite in the holy Scriptures, and singularly devoted to the reading of the books of Saint Augustine, and sufficiently instructed in the letters of the secular arts, illustrious also in life and in the conversation of monastic institution—to whom several letters of Saint Fulgentius of Ruspe are reported [addressed]—wrote certain necessary little works, of which I have found only the following. He culled a great volume, having three hundred chapters, from the books of blessed Augustine the bishop, [dedicated] to Proba; which begins thus:
The Prologue (letter 1): "To the lady deservedly venerable…"
But the book thus (book 1): "What I wrote to you…"
The Life of Saint Severinus the monk: 1 book.
He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, according to Honorius the presbyter; but according to Sigebert, he flourished then under Tiberius Constantine, in the times of Pope Pelagius II, in the year of the Lord 580.
