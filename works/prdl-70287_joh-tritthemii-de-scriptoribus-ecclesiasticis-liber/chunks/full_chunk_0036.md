Juvencus, a priest, a Spaniard by nation, a noble and most learned man, a philosopher, poet, rhetorician, and distinguished theologian, venerable to the Church no less in manner of life than in knowledge of the Scriptures, published for the edification of readers many excellent volumes, both in verse and in prose; but few of them have come to my knowledge. I have read the notable work that he composed in hexameter and pentameter verses:

*On the Four Gospels*, 4 books.

*On the Sacraments*, 2 books.

The other works that he is said to have composed have not come into our hands.

He flourished under Constantine the Great and his sons, in the year of the Lord 330.

Eustathius of Antioch, a Pamphylian by birth, first [bishop?] of Beroea in Syria, and then [unclear], learned and holy, endured many troubles from the Arians on behalf of the Catholic faith. For, having been banished into exile to Trajanopolis in Thrace, he died there; when the Arians invaded his church, they held it for many years. He wrote many small works, chiefly against the Arians, and various other diverse treatises, namely: *Against the Arians*, 5 books; *On the Soul*, 1 book; *Against Origen*, 2 books; Letters, almost innumerable, 2 books.

He flourished in the times of Constantine and his sons, in the year of the Lord 340.

Athanasius, the nineteenth bishop of the city of Alexandria, a holy man and most learned in the divine Scriptures, endured many persecutions from the Arians for the Catholic faith. Indeed, the whole world was narrow for him, with the Arians persecuting him everywhere, so that he seemed nowhere safe, nowhere secure. For a man’s enemies were those of his own household. Therefore, fleeing from the face of the Arian emperor, he came to Trier, a city of the Gauls, was honorably received by Saint Maximinus the archbishop, and for seven years [was hidden?] in a crypt at the monastery of Saint Maximinus at Trier. In this place, hiding for many years, as we have said, he did not see the sun, and there he composed the creed *Whoever wishes to be saved*, until at last, betrayed by a certain girl, by the will of God he escaped in flight. If anyone desires to know his deeds more fully, let him read the *Tripartite History*. He published several letters to various persons, and also sermons and various treatises, which either have not yet been translated into Latin or have not come to my knowledge. He died in the time of the emperors Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 379, in the forty-sixth year of his priesthood, on the sixth day before the Nones of May, in the seventh indiction.
