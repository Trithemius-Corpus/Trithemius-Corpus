He is renowned down to the present day at Ferrara, where, succeeding his father, he has now for thirty-three years taught both languages most flourishingly, and writes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, the twelfth indiction. He himself has a son learned in the Greek and Latin language, following his ancestral discipline.

Angelo Poliziano, a man skilled and most learned in every respect, a celebrated grammarian, philosopher, orator, and distinguished poet, perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, excellent in talent, who for a long time illuminated Italy by his learning and teaching, wrote various illustrious volumes, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among them the following are reported:

Certain *Centuries*, one book.

*On Errors of the Latin Language*, one book.

Various letters to diverse persons, two books.

And he is said to have written certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He dared to present Homer’s *Iliad* in verse for Latins to read. He is still renowned in Italy, as I have heard, writing various works, under the king of the Romans Maximilian and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Baptista Mantuanus, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in knowledge of secular letters, doctor of sacred theology, distinguished philosopher, very celebrated poet and orator, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, outstanding in life and conduct, an ornament of the Latin language, a renowned interpreter of Greek, excelling in verse and prose, wrote in both styles many excellent volumes, by which he acquired for his name a celebrated reputation throughout the whole Christian world. Among these the following are reported:

To his father:

*On the Blessed Life*, a dialogue, one book. “Brother Baptista, to Peter …”

*Suburbanum*, on the Bucolics, two books. “Faustus approaches the fields …”

The life of Parthenice, three books. “Holy [Palestine?] …”

*On Saint Catherine*, one book. “[unclear] having begun [unclear] …”

The life of Albert the Carmelite, in Sapphic meter, one book. “Greatest of pontiffs …”

*On the Calamity of His Times*, one book. “The month of August returns …”

*Panegyric of Robert*, one book. “We are forced by the evils of this …”

*A Dream Concerning the Same*, one book. “The ancients [marked?] the demigods …”

To Falco, of epigrams, one book. “Greatest worshipper of justice …”

*Consolation on Death*, one book. “I owe it, but cares prevent …”

*Delights of the Sacred Font* [unclear]. “Delights of the sacred font …”

*On Contempt of Death*, one book. “Why do you grieve, and fear fate …”

*Against Foul-Mouthed Poets*, one book. “There are those to whom eloquence is given …”

*On the Conquest of Granada*, four books.

*On Patience*, in prose, one book.

*On the Origin of Religions*, one book.

*Apology for Brother Peter*, five books.

*Introduction to the Subtle Scotus*, one book.

Most elegant orations, one book.

Many letters to diverse persons, one book.

Poems of various kinds.

And many other things.

He is still living in Italy, everywhere named with a very celebrated reputation, and is writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we completed these things, 1494.

Johannes Bertram of Neuenburg, German by nation, formerly a pupil of Georgius Molitoris of Neuenburg, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, first at Erfurt and then in the school of Mainz publicly teaching theology and instructing many, wrote in that faculty certain illustrious little works, by which he made his name known. Among these there is a large work:

*On the Prologue of the Bible*, one book. “As I was thinking in [unclear] …”

Many addresses to the clergy, one book.

*On the Value of Masses*, one book. “Most reverend in Christ …”

Various questions.

And certain other things.

He is renowned in his manly age in the school of Mainz, teaching theology in ordinary course and composing various works, under the most illustrious Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494.
