*Responsive letter of the reverend father and lord Johannes of Trittenheim, abbot of Sponheim, to the venerable and most learned man Father Albert Morderer, giving an account why he placed many professors of secular letters among ecclesiastical writers.*

[unclear] Brother Johannes of Trittenheim, abbot in Sponheim, of the Order of St. Benedict, sends greeting to Brother Albert Morderer, of the Observant Order of Minorites, his friend. You say, most beloved Albert, that you wonder that I have placed professors of secular letters, who wrote nothing ecclesiastical, among the illustrious ecclesiastical men whom I have called writers, since Jerome, Gennadius, Isidore, Honorius, Sigebert, and the remaining learned censors of writers on such matters are found not to have done this at all; judging it unworthy that those devoted to secular disciplines, who labored in nothing for the edification of the universal Church, should obtain a place among ecclesiastical writers. I do not wonder that you wonder; the others wonder too. Therefore, by answering you, I shall satisfy all who hold the same opinion.

And first, indeed, why others did not do what you wonder that I have done does not concern me; but why I did what they did not do, I shall briefly explain. Indeed, I by no means consider professors of the good arts, remaining in the Catholic faith, philosophers, orators, and poets, who have written anything useful and honorable in their field, unworthy of the catalogue of illustrious men, for I know that they assist the Church by their lucubrations. Nor do I think that anyone can be called sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures when he is ignorant of the discipline of secular literature. But how will a theologian learn it, if there is no one who has transmitted it? Who does not know how much a Christian and faithful philosopher can do for the Church, one who destroys the errors of the gentiles, exposes arguments and fallacious syllogisms, and confounds the doctrines of unbelievers? Read the histories of the ancients, and you will find that deeds confirm faith by words. How often have orthodox philosophers destroyed the cunning of heretics by disputation, overcome gentiles, conquered schismatics? Why should I use many words? Every school of theologians now takes the beginning of study from philosophy, to such an extent that one cannot be a theologian unless he has first been a philosopher among them.
