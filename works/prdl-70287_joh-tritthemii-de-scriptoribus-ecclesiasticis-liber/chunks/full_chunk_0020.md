These are the works of Seneca’s writings that I have been able to find at present. He is also said to have written some other little works, which have not yet come into our hands. He was certainly a most ardent lover of wisdom, and a zealous seeker of it, and of so retentive a memory that, when two thousand names had been read before him, he soon recited them from memory. Moreover, when a certain poet had composed two hundred verses and, after putting them onto a sheet, was reciting them in order before Nero, Seneca at once, using his memory instead of the volume, repeated the same verses one by one, beginning from the last and proceeding in reverse order as far as the first. He is read to have held in memory the names of all his fellow citizens. He flourished at Rome in the times of the apostle Paul, under the emperor Nero, in the year of the Lord 50.

Marcellus, son of Marcus, prefect of the city of Rome, once a disciple of Simon Magus, sufficiently instructed in secular letters, converted to the faith of Jesus Christ by the preaching of the blessed apostle Peter, composed many things for the benefit of the Church of God. For, at the request of Christians, he wrote:

On the Conflict of St. Peter and Simon Magus, one book.
To the saints Nereus and Achilleus, one book.
On the Acts of Peter and Paul, one book.
On the Arts of Simon, one book.
On the Death of Saint Petronilla, one book.
On the Passion of Saint Felicula, one book.
On the Passion of Saint Nicomedes, one book.

He also published other things concerning the deeds of the saints, which have not come to our knowledge. He flourished at Rome in the times of St. Peter the apostle, in the year of the Lord 50.

Linus, the first bishop of the city of Rome after blessed Peter, a Tuscan by nation, a man of most holy conduct, held the pontificate for eleven years, three months, and twelve days. Since he was a very learned man, beloved by God, he wrote in the Greek language concerning the holy apostles Peter and Paul, who suffered on one day:

The Martyrdom, one book.
And certain letters.

At length, by Saturninus the prefect, whose daughter he had freed from a demon, he was punished by a capital sentence and became a martyr on the eleventh day before the Kalends of October. He flourished at Rome under the emperor Galba, in the year of the Lord 70.

Philo the Jew, an Alexandrian by nation, of the race of priests, is placed by blessed Jerome among ecclesiastical writers for this reason: because in his little works he is very often engaged in praise of Christians. When he had been sent to Rome as envoy of his people, he is remembered as having had the friendship of the blessed apostle Peter; for this reason also he adorned with his praises the followers at Alexandria of Saint Mark, Peter’s disciple. He was a man most learned in every respect, regarded as another Plato, so that it was commonly said: either Plato imitates Philo, or Philo imitates Plato. For so great a likeness of thought and expression between them is reported. He wrote very elegant books, of which the following are given below:
