*[page] 216. 178. ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

Azo, an Italian by nation, drawing the origin of [his] life from the city of Bologna, once hearer and disciple of John Bassiani of Cremona, a most learned man; a most famous interpreter of the civil law, a fountain of laws, an abundance of words, a lamp of law, who by his erudition and knowledge easily excels all the jurists who preceded him; he published many little works pertaining to the civil law, by which he acquired immortal praise and glory:
A notable Summa of law: 1 book.
On the Old Digest: 24 books.
On the Code also: 9 books.
Besides, he composed excellent apparatus on the laws, and appended additions to several Summas, and wrote various [things] which are not unknown to learned jurists.
He flourished in the times of Frederick II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1250, and left after him many distinguished disciples.

Giles de Fuscariis, a citizen of Bologna, a man most learned in canon law, and nobly erudite as much in the imperial laws as in the other disciplines of humanity, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence, a doctor of great authority among the ancient professors of this faculty, wrote certain illustrious volumes in that discipline, of which I have seen only:
On the office of the Notary: 1 book. ["It remains now that, concerning the office…"]
Various Questions of law.
The rest which he composed have as yet by no means come into our hands.

Francis, founder and prince of the order of Minors, an Italian by nation, sprung from Assisi, a city of Italy, a man of most holy conversation and a most ardent follower of evangelical poverty, who by word and example turned many from iniquity and directed [them] into the way of catholic justice; he was so great a lover of Christ that in contempt of the world and of transitory consolation he scarcely had an equal. In whose order many most learned men have thereafter become illustrious, who composed little works almost innumerable for the usefulness of the faithful. But Francis himself wrote, briefly:
The Rule of the Minors: 1 book. ["The Rule and life of the Minors…"]
A brief Testament: 1 book. ["The Lord gave to me, brother…"]
He flourished under Frederick II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1220.
