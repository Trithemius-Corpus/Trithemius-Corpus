*Abbot of Spanheim.*

A double Lenten work, 2 books.
Of the other things that he composed, nothing has yet come to my knowledge.

Henry of Kalkar, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, and prior of the house of St. Barbara in Cologne, a man who had an enlightened mind in the divine Scriptures, and was also not ignorant of secular letters, notable in life and conduct, published little works not to be despised in reading, the use of which is judged very useful for religious and devout brothers. Among them are reported:

*The Monastic Exercise*, 1 book.
*Loquagium* on rhetoric, 1 book.
*Cantuagium* on music, 1 book.
On the origin of my order, 1 book.
And certain other works, especially pertaining to the purification of consciences.

He flourished under the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Richard Maidstone, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and remarkably learned in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, and an outstanding declaimer, wrote many commendable little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them I have found:

Against the Lollards of Wycliffe, 1 book.
Sermons on the season, 1 book.
Sermons on the saints, 1 book.
The rest are hidden from me.

He flourished in the Oxford school, especially at that time when the heresy of John Wycliffe began to sprout in England, and he wrote various things.

Marsilius of Inghen, English by nation, a man in the divine Scriptures, the first planter of the Heidelberg school, which Rupert, duke of Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine, established and magnificently endowed, and which Pope Urban VI confirmed on the model of Paris; also, as Wimpheling testifies, canon and treasurer of the church of St. Andrew at Cologne: he published little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among them, in the Heidelberg library, written in his own hand, are held today:

Questions on the Sentences, 4 books.
A notable dialectic, 1 book.
Several books of commentaries on Aristotle,
And certain other things.

He died under the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1394, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of September, in the second indiction, buried at Heidelberg in the church of St. Peter. There are some who write that he was German; I grant them freedom of judgment. It is established, however, that he was called from the Paris school to Heidelberg for the beginning of the university.

John, monk of the monastery of Castell, in the diocese of Eichstatt, of the order of St. Benedict, German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in intellect and eloquent in fluent speech, a distinguished theologian, philosopher, and rhetorician, wrote many and splendid volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them I have found only the great and very famous work, divided into two volumes, which he published with immense and commendable zeal:

On the Rule of St. Benedict, 2 books.
A Breviary of the Bible, 1 book.
Sermons on the season, 3 books.
Sermons on the saints, 4 books.
On the Passion of the Lord, 42 sermons, 5 books.
Letters to various persons, 6 books.
Besides these he published many other things, which have not yet come to my knowledge.

Conrad of Monte Puellarum, German by nation, canon of the church of Regensburg, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence: first absorbing the rudiments of the liberal arts in the Erfurt school, he then betook himself to the University of Paris, where, lecturing publicly to students on philosophy and sacred letters for eight years, he obtained the doctor's cap. He wrote for lord Lupold, bishop of Bamberg, a large and noble work, which he entitled, according to the Greeks:

*Economics*, 3 books. All of moral philoso[unclear]
*Monastics*, to the duke of Austria, 4 books.
And also *Politics*, 5 books.
Various Questions, 6 books.
And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1390. Hen
