Lanfrancus de Oriano, a very celebrated jurist and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent and renowned for eloquence, wrote in his profession certain volumes not to be despised. By them he transmitted the memory of his talent to posterity, and proved himself in study in many and splendid ways. Of their number the following are extant:

*H*

Johannes Kanneman, of the Order of Friars Minor, a German by nation, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent, renowned for eloquence, was outstanding in delivering sermons to the people. Thinking wrongly about ecclesiastical power, when he was sought by the minister of Saxony for prison, he escaped by flight and fled to the Observantines; thereafter he amended his error along with his life. Johannes de Hagen, the Carthusian, a very learned man, had already reproved him and recalled him to the paths of equity. He wrote:

A defense of himself, one book. *Multi famosi viri... Egredimini filiae Sion...*

Certain questions, one book.
And many other works.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.
