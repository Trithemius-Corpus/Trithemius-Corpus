*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Sermons on the saints, one book. Moral sermons, one book. And many other works.

He was personally prominent at the Council of Constance, having been sent by the University of Vienna, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Peter of Rosenheim, a monk of the monastery of Melk in Austria, of the Order of St. Benedict, German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and magnificently instructed in secular letters, an outstanding philosopher and poet, ready and distinguished in talent, produced both in verse and in prose little works not to be cast aside, in which he properly exercised his talent. Among these there is extant a metrical work which he wrote, at the urging and request of Nicholas [Dinkelsbuhl?], to Lord Branda, cardinal of St. Clement, apostolic legate in Austria, on the chapters of the whole Bible, which he entitled

The Rosy Memorial of Divine Things, one book. The star-ruling [one], heaven, earth...

A Notable History of the Times, one book.

Various Sermons, one book.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1420, and composed many and various works.

Raphael Fulgosius, from Piacenza by nation, a man most learned in both laws and well instructed in the precepts of secular philosophy, excellent in talent, cautious and steadfast in counsel, produced certain distinguished volumes in civil law, by which he displayed the force and liveliness of his talent even to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books. On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.

On the New Digest, twelve books. Many Legal Opinions, one book.

And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1416.

John of Palomar, archdeacon of the church of Barcelona, an eminent doctor of the Decrees, and nobly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, produced little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is extant a distinguished treatise to John, abbot of the monastery of the Scots of the Order of St. Benedict at Vienna:

On Abstinence from Meat, one book. The sons will rise in judgment...

Sermons and Questions.

The rest that he composed have by no means come to the knowledge of my reading.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

He flourished at the university of Vienna in Austria, and composed many works in the time of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Raphael Cumanus, eminent professor of civil law and a very celebrated interpreter, excellent in talent and outstanding in counsel, whose authority among jurists is by no means slight, wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books. On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.

Also Legal Opinions, one book. And several other works.

He flourished under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1416.

John of Teneramunda, a man zealous and very learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, distinguished in talent, ordered in speech, produced very useful little works for the edification of religious brothers, by which he instructed not only those then present, but also those who would come after him, in the love of God. And they are these:

On the Knowledge of God, one book. On the Nature and Fall of Man, one book.

On the Restoration of the Fallen, one book. On the Love of God, one book.

On the Joy of Man, one book. On the Sacrament of the Altar, one book.

On the Christian Faith, one book. On the Honor of God, one book.

On the Conception of Saint Mary, one book.

He also elegantly composed many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Gasparinus, a very celebrated grammarian, from Bergamo by native place, born in the little village of Barzizza, a man most learned in secular letters, a most outstanding philosopher and rhetorician, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, who called back from the dead the elegance of Latin speech, half-dead as it was: he wrote many distinguished little works in that faculty, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these there are extant:

On Orthography, one book.

Letters to Various Persons, one book. I rejoice very greatly and [am glad]...

Various Orations, one book.

On the Letters of Cicero, one book.

On the same author’s On Duties, one book.

On the same author’s On the Orator, one book.

On the same author’s On Old Age, one book.

And certain other works.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.
