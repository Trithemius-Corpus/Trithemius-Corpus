[Guido?] de Suzaria, a doctor most skilled in law and sufficiently instructed in the other disciplines of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, ready and cautious in counsel, among the ancients expert in these matters enjoyed great authority. He is said to have produced certain distinguished little works in law, among which there exists a notable Summa, which he entitled, in 4 books, On the Ordering of Cases.
Various Declarations also.
And many other works, which have not yet come into our hands.

Bartholomaeus Brixiensis, professor and interpreter of canon law, most [unclear] and excellently learned in the other disciplines of secular literature, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote various distinguished little works both in law and on other matters, by which he gave his name to immortality. Of these the following are reported:
A Repertory of the Decretum, 3 books. “Since with favoring [unclear]…”
Disputations on the Decretals, 5 books.
A Chronicle of Italy, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
And certain other works.
The same man wrote additions to the glosses of the Decretum.
Also Sunday and Friday Questions, 91 in number.
He also produced a history of times.

Johannes de Deo, professor and eminent expositor of both laws, and sufficiently learned in the disciplines of philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, who among the ancients was of great authority, wrote many various little works in both laws, through which the memory of his name came to the knowledge of many. *On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Hugo Barchinonensis, of the Order of Preachers, cardinal priest of the title of Santa Sabina of the holy Roman Church, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, who before his entry into the order had been master of sacred theology and had had many distinguished pupils, wrote several excellent little works, by which he consecrated his name to immortality. Shining no less brightly in conduct and religion than in knowledge, he remained the same in dignity as he had been before. Of his books these are reported:
On Genesis, 1 book. On Exodus, 1 book.
On Leviticus, 1 book. On Numbers, 1 book.
On Deuteronomy, 1 book. On Joshua, 1 book.
On Judges, 1 book. On the Books of Ruth, 1 book.
On Kings, 4 books. On Chronicles, 1 book.
On Ezra and Nehemiah, 5 books. On Tobit, 1 book.
On Judith, 1 book. On Esther, 1 book.
On the Books of Job, 1 book. On the Psalter, 1 book.
On Proverbs, 1 book. On Ecclesiastes, 1 book.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book. On the Book of Wisdom, 1 book.
On Ecclesiasticus, 1 book. On Isaiah the Prophet, 1 book.
On Jeremiah, 5 books. On Ezekiel, 1 book.
On Daniel, 1 book. On the Twelve Minor Prophets, 12 books.
On the Books of Maccabees, 2 books. On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Mark, 1 book. On the Gospel of Luke, 1 book.
On the Acts of the Apostles, 1 book. On the Epistles of the Apostle Paul, 2 books.
On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book. A Summa on Cases, 1 book.
He also composed several other works, which have not yet come to my knowledge.
He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1240.

Jacobus Baldeuini, formerly a hearer of Odofredus Beneventanus, a very distinguished man, professor and eminent interpreter of civil law, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, outstanding in counsel and action, wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:
On the Code, 2 books. On the Old Digest, 11 books.
On the New Digest, 3 books. On the Infortiatum, 4 books.
And certain other works.
He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1240.
