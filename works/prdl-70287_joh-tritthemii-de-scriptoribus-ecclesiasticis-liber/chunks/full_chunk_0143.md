218 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Garcias, Spanish by nation, a man most practiced in both laws, subtle in intellect, ready and cautious in counsel, acute in resolving questions of cases, held in esteem among the professors of this faculty in his time, extended his name by teaching and writing. For he is said to have written several small works both in the laws and in canon law, which have not come into our hands. There are extant by him:

*On the Decretals, five books.*

And several works in the laws.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

[Nicolaus?] IV, pope, of the order of the Friars Minor, [Hieronymus?] [unclear], sat after Honorius IV in the chair of Peter for four years: a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not meanly instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and venerable no less for goodness of life than for learning. He wrote a sufficiently excellent work:

*On the Sentences, four books.*

And certain sermons.

He is also said to have published useful commentaries on several books of the Bible. He died at Rome, the empire being vacant after Rudolf’s death, in the year of the Lord 1291, in the fourth indiction.

Boniface VIII, pope, Campanian by nation, previously called Benedict, sat after the renunciation of Celestine V in the chair of Peter for eight years and nine months; a man indeed learned, but excessively arrogant and ambitious. By what means he acquired the pontificate, the chronographers have not passed over in silence. Since he was very skilled in law and saw that new varieties of cases were daily arising in the contentious forum which could not be determined through the Decretals, he composed, through three men most learned in law, new constitutions, which he wished to be called:

*The Sixth of the Decretals of the Most Holy Roman [Church], one book.*

Letters, several, to various persons, one book.

He died under the emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1305, indiction 1. Concerning him it had previously been prophesied: “He entered like a fox, lived like a lion, died like a dog.”

Guilhelmus Mandagoti, archdeacon of Nimes, afterward archbishop of Embrun, and of the most holy church
