He taught first at Venice, afterwards at Ferrara, and in both places had very celebrated pupils. He wrote to Prince Leonello the work entitled:

Also a brief *Grammar*, one book.

Several *Orations*, one book.
Many poems.
And not a few other things.

By order of Pope Nicholas V he translated into Latin Strabo’s *Geography*, a work no less useful than pleasant. From Plutarch he made Latin versions of the lives of many illustrious men. But he also translated Plutarch himself *On the Education of Children*, with many other works, most elegantly; these are partly extant.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460, in the eighth indiction, on the day before the Nones of December; buried at Ferrara in the convent of the Carmelites, honorably given a marble tomb.

Henry Kalteisen, archbishop of the churches of Nidaros and Caesarea, of the Order of Friars Preachers, German by nation, from Koblenz by homeland, where he also entered the order; first made lector of the sacred palace, preacher of the Holy Cross, and inquisitor of heretical depravity; and afterwards, as we have said, consecrated by apostolic authority as chief bishop of two provinces, he became known as renowned throughout the whole world for his learning and preaching. Since he was a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, he wrote, among other things, notable works:

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.
*Sermons through Lent*, one book.
*On the Magnificat*, one book.
*Various Questions*, one book.
*Conferences to the Clergy*.
And several other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1465, in the thirteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Nones of October; buried in his own convent at Koblenz.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Johannes Bertachinus of Fermo, a very celebrated legal scholar, and singularly devoted to that faculty, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain small works in law, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among them there is a most famous work, divided into three large volumes, which he compiled very diligently and in fine order with much labor, and entitled:

*Repertory of Both Laws*, five books. “Princes are wont [unclear] immortal...”
*Various Questions*.

He published besides several other things, which have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

Pope Pius II, Tuscan by nation, Sienese by homeland, previously called Aeneas Silvius, sat in the supreme pontificate after Callixtus III for six years. A man learned in every respect, an outstanding philosopher, orator, and poet, he left many monuments of his talent to posterity. He, poor, served a certain prelate in the Council of Basel for a time. Then, received into the chancery as a scribe, he was created first bishop of Trieste, afterwards of Siena, thereafter cardinal, and finally was elected pope by the votes of all.
