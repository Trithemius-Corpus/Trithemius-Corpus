*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

He flourished under the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1400. And, dying as a young man, he was buried in the sacristy of his order at Venice.

James of Cessolis, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in divine Scripture and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent, polished and well-composed in speech, second to none in his time in delivering sermons to the people, published certain distinguished volumes, by which he left to posterity a praiseworthy memory of his name. Of these there is extant a notable work, entitled *On the Morals of Men and the Duties of Nobles and Commoners*, treating the subject *On the Game of Chess*, in 4 books. “Among all kinds of [unclear].” *Various Sermons*, 1 book.

Other works, however, which he is said to have written have by no means come to our knowledge.

Thomas of Haselbach, German by nation, learned in sacred Scripture, not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, scholastic in speech, but excessively diffuse and prolix, and for that reason liable to the criticism of certain people. For when he was ordinary lecturer in sacred theology at the university of Vienna, lecturing on Isaiah the prophet for twenty-one years, he did not reach the end of the first chapter. He wrote some treatises on the divine Scriptures, of which the following many volumes are reported:

*On Isaiah the Prophet*, 1 book.
*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*On the Ten Commandments*, 1 book.
*On the Five Senses of Man*, 1 book.
*Sermons on the Season*, 1 book.
*Sermons on the Saints*, 1 book.
And many others.

He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and of Pope Gregory XII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Of their number especially the following are reported:

*Sermons on the Season*, 1 book.
*Sermons on the Saints*, 1 book.
