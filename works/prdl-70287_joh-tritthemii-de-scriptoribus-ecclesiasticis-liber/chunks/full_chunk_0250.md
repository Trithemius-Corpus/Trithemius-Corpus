He published annotations on twenty-four books of the Pandects or Digest, and will publish them on the remaining books, as he himself testifies in the letter which he writes to Johannes de Ganayo, great chancellor of France. How great the learning and doctrine of the annotations may be is left for each person to consider.

He lives at Paris, to the great expectation of all; from him greater works will soon come to light.

Wilhelmus Castellus ([Ieiunio Castalius?]), a Frenchman, a most distinguished poet and orator, of Tours, was born at Tours in the year of the Lord 1468, his father being Christianus Castellus, a doctor [Paxisien?] and royal physician, and his mother Martina of Bourges. There, after being taught the first rudiments of letters, he was sent to Paris, as is customary, to take up more fruitful and more solid disciplines, to his maternal brother Johannes Anglicus, a man excellently learned in divine and human letters, to enter the Navarre bursary, as the ordinary word has it. There he devoted such labor to liberal studies, especially the humanities, that in a short course of time he not only equalled all his companions who preceded him in age and learning, but even far surpassed them. So great was his thirst for letters, so great his love, so great his care, so great his zeal. Hence, suddenly known by fame above the heavens, he was summoned to the Burgundian college, to instruct youth in the better and more polished letters, by Bernardus Roillete, a man richly furnished with all good disciplines, the chief rector of that college, and was put in charge of the first class in the humanities. Then, after enduring the contest of dialectic and after his pupils had been presented with the laurel, he returned to the Navarre college, intending to join divine studies, for which he had always yearned, to human disciplines. At last, having obtained the doctorate in the most sacred faculty of theology, he withdrew to Tours, where, honored with honorable benefices of the church, the archdeaconry and a canonry, he lives with his own people, intent on prayer and preaching.
