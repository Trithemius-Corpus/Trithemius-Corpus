*On Ecclesiastical [Writers], Addition II.*

Johannes Bernardus Felicianus, a Venetian, a man learned in Greek and Latin, translated many things from Greek physicians, philosophers, and theologians, and is still continuing in translation, namely in the year 1546. For he has almost finished that “golden chain,” as they call it, or the Greek interpretation of Oecumenius on the Acts of the Apostles, on Paul’s epistles and those of others. He translated Galen’s books On the Doctrines of Hippocrates and Plato.

On the anatomy of the womb, one book.

On the formation of fetuses, one book.

He translated book 6 of Paul of Aegina, on surgery.

He made Latin Alexander of Aphrodisias’s explanation on the first book of Aristotle’s Prior Analytics.

Likewise he endowed with Latin Aristotle’s ten books of Ethics, with the explanations of Eustratius, Aspasius, Michael of Ephesus, and some other Greeks.

Johannes Caesarius of Julich, a German by nation, helped the cause of letters not inconsiderably by his studies, and entered into great favor among all students. He is still living, in the year 1546, a very old man, in Cologne. The catalogue of all his books and commentaries is this:

A learned Dialectic. A most elegant Rhetoric.

Commentaries on Jodocus Clichtoveus’s Introduction to the Knowledge of Terms. A geometrical epitome.

He corrected the work of Diomedes the grammarian.

He corrected and illustrated with scholia the books of C. Plinius Secundus on the medicine of fishes.

Likewise Paulus Jovius’s book on Roman fishes.

He rescued the whole work of C. Plinius Secundus’s Natural History from innumerable errors.

He revised the five books of Severinus Boethius On the Consolation of Philosophy.

And many other works still unknown to us.

Johannes Chrysostomus Zanchus, an Italian by nation, brother of Basilius Zanchus, wrote five books On the Origin of the Orobians or Cenomani, addressed to Pietro Bembo, and a panegyric to Charles V, emperor, in an elegant style.

Johannes Cochlaeus of Nuremberg, a German by nation, applied himself to theology in Italy, where he also obtained the insignia of the doctorate, as they call it. He was once dean at Frankfurt, at the church of the Blessed Virgin, in the year 1520. Today he resides in the city of Eichstatt, now advanced in age.

*In the Abbot of Spanheim, On Writers.*

I hear that many works have been written and published by him. But those which I have seen are these:

Rudiments of grammar.

An expostulation against the new reformation in the Church of the senate of Bern among the Swiss.

A little book On the Authority of Canonical Scripture and the Catholic [Church], against Heinrich Bullinger.

A brief reply against Heinrich Bullinger’s lengthy response on the authority of Scripture and the Church.

A defense of the priesthood and sacrifice against the gnawings of Wolfgang Musculus.

A skirmish against Andreas Osiander’s four conjectures about the end of the world.

On the purgatory of souls, one book.

Six Philippics against Philip Melanchthon.

A discussion on the new Hebrew translations of Holy Scripture.

One little book On the Power of a General Council.

A refutation of the fifty articles that were disputed at Wittenberg.

Seventy propositions in a general council.

Nycticorax.

He is still living as an old man, composing many things, in the year of salvation 1546.

Johannes Cono of Nuremberg, a German by nation, a monk of the Dominican order, added Greek to Jerome’s books when they were first being printed at Basel. He translated Gregory Nazianzen’s oration on Gregory of Nyssa, and Basil the Great’s little book On the Difference between Essence and Hypostasis, and restored Gregory of Nyssa’s book On Man to its native splendor.

Johannes Despauterius of Ninove, a German by nation, published a most elegant grammar and a Recrimination against an adversary. He flourished in the year of salvation 1539.

Johannes Dietenberger, a most profound professor of theology, for so the inscription has it, and inquisitor of heretical depravity, brought to light the Phimositomum or Confutatory of the Scripturarians and Lutheran brethren. A treatise of the same man on divorce was added to it. He also translated the Bible into German. He flourished in the year of salvation 1527.
