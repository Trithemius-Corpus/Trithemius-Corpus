Cyrillus, priest and monk of Mount Carmel, the third prior general of the same order, by nation, as they report, a Greek, a holy man illumined by a prophetic spirit, to whom almighty God is said to have disclosed his will through angelic ministry, while he is honored with silver tablets written by the finger of God, wrote, among the other little works of his talent, to an abbot, *On the Angelic Oracle*:

*On the Course of His Order*.
Several letters to various persons.

He flourished under Philip and Otto, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Helinandus, a studious man learned by long practice, and nobly instructed in secular literature, acute in intellect, eloquent in speech, because of the excellence of his learning and the integrity of his life, appeared not the least among the ecclesiastical doctors of his time. For he wrote several little works, by which he gave his name to immortality. Of these the following are reported: from the beginning of the world down to his own age, a great, distinguished volume:

*Histories*, 48 books.
*On the Restoration of the Fallen One*.
Several sermons, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1209.

Gilbert, a monk of the Cistercian order, afterwards abbot of a certain monastery, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, excellently learned, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence, wrote after the death of Saint Bernard the abbot, where he had left off, namely from the place, “In my bed by night,” down to the end, forty-six subtle and most elegant sermons:

*On the Song of Songs*, one book. “Various are [the things] of lovers.”
And certain other things.

He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1190.

Aelred, abbot of the same monastery, of the Cistercian order, a man exercised by constant study in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, gentle in intellect, sweet in speech, outstanding in devotion, wrote for the usefulness of his brothers a number of little works, by which he benefited them and transmitted the knowledge of his name to posterity.
