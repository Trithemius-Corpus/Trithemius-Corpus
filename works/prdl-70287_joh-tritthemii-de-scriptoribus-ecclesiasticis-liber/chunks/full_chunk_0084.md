He flourished under the emperor Tiberius, in the year of the Lord 700.

Bede, monk and priest of the monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, which is in England, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures and very learned, and very skilled in secular letters, an eminent philosopher, astronomer, calculator, and poet. Not ignorant of the Greek language, outstanding in talent, not over-elaborate in speech, yet sweet and well ordered, he wrote many volumes, in which the keenness of his intellect is proved. When he was seven years old, by the care of his relatives he was given to be educated by Abbot Benedict, and then by Ceolfrid of the said monastery of [Wearmouth?]; and spending all the time of his life thereafter in residence in the same monastery, he devoted all his effort to meditation on the Scriptures, and, amid the observance of regular discipline and the daily care of singing in church, he always found it sweet either to learn, or to teach, or to write. In the nineteenth year of his age he received the diaconate, and in the thirtieth the rank of priesthood, both through the ministry of Bishop John, at the command of Abbot Ceolfrid. From the time when he received the priesthood until the end of his life he composed the following little works:
