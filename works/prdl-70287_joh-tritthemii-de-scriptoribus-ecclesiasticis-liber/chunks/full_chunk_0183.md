Bartholomaeus of Urbino, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, a diligent investigator of the works of the holy fathers, wrote volumes not to be despised, of which the following are preserved: from the minor works of Saint Ambrose and Augustine he compiled into one work, with singular zeal, according to alphabetical order, to which he gave the title

*The Thousand Sayings of Augustine*, one book.

*The Thousand Sayings of Ambrose*, one book.

*On Spiritual Warfare*, one book.

And several other things.

He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and of Pope Gregory XII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Franciscus de Platea, of the Order of Minors of St. Francis, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, notably instructed both in law and in secular philosophy, excellent in talent, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, is said to have composed by lamplight many distinguished minor works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity. Of their number the following are reported: an excellent and distinguished work which he entitled

*On Restitutions*, one book.

*Whether Restitution Is [unclear] from Censures*, one book.

*Various Sermons*, [unclear] books.

But the rest that he composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

Peter of Candia, Cretan by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, first bishop of Novara, then archbishop of Milan, afterward cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, finally supreme pontiff, named Alexander V, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, magnificently instructed in scholastic philosophy, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, who once presided over the public school in the University of Paris, by teaching and writing acquired great and immense praise. From his minor works there survive clear and useful commentaries:

*On the Sentences*, four books.

*Various Sermons*, one book.

And certain other things.

He died in the last year of Rupert, king of the Romans, and was buried in the convent of the Minors at Bologna, in the year of the Lord 1409, in the third indiction,

Franciscus de Bachone, a Catalan by homeland, of the Order of the Brothers of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Gerona, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, was so strong in teaching and disputing in the University of Paris that he was surnamed the Sublime Doctor. Among other monuments of his talent he wrote a great and distinguished work from the writings of the holy fathers, quite useful to declaimers of sermons to the people, which we may entitle

*Repertory of Preachers*, four books.

It is reported besides that he wrote certain other things also, but they have not come into our hands.

Michael Herbrant of Düren, of the Order of the Brothers of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, German by nation, lecturer in sacred theology and prior of the convent of Kreuznach in the diocese of Mainz, a learned man and so distinguished a declaimer that, summoned not only in the vicinity but also to remote places and cathedral churches, he preached while held in admiration by all. He enjoyed great intimacy and authority with Johann, the last count of Sponheim, and obtained from him many benefits for his convent. Among other things he wrote elegant

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

*Synodal Collations*, one book.

And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Peter of Speyer, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and magnificently instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and clear in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in declaiming sermons to the people, wrote certain distinguished minor works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of their number are reported

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

*On the Books of the Ethics*, two books.

But the other things that he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading.
