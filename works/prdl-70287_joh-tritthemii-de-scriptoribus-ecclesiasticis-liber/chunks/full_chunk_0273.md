*On Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

*457*

On the question of the fire of Purgatory.

The cause of the so long-lasting calamity of the ecclesiastical estate in Germany.

A dialogue, whose name is καλοσώες.

A postil for the seasons and saints through the whole cycle of the year, in German and Latin.

Likewise a postil for the whole of Lent, in German and Latin.

And many other works, either in German alone, or in German and Latin alike.

He is still living in the year of the Lord 1546, intending, if God gives him a longer life, to write certain other works for students.

Germanus, archbishop of Constantinople, adorned himself with sacred letters while he was still a boy; whence it came about that he published many little works not unworthy of reading.

A Greek sermon of the same man, on the limits of life, is extant in Italy.

Germanus Brixius of Auxerre, canon of Paris, a man learned in both languages, wrote many things, of which the following are reported:

Antimorus, a poem against Thomas More, the Englishman.

Four congratulatory letters.

An elegy on the death of Francis Deloinus.

And certain poems and letters.

He translated the dialogue of St. John Chrysostom on the dignity and burden of a bishop, in 6 books.

Eight homilies on Paul’s Epistle to the Romans.

He is still flourishing under Francis, king of the French, in the year 1546.

Gertrude, a learned virgin, joined familiarly to God by the perfection of outstanding holiness and accustomed to heavenly revelations, published, at the institution of the Holy Spirit, the Book of the Insinuations of Divine Piety, very consolatory to pious minds, divided into 5 books.

And she flourished in the monastery of Helfede at Eisleben, about the year of the Lord 1300.

Gervasius Riccobaldus of Ferrara, as a man in his time most devoted to antiquity, wrote a history of the pontiffs down to his own times, that is, to the year of the Lord 1300.

Gratiadeus, a monk of the Order of St. Dominic, and skilled in sacred Scripture and philosophy, wrote a summa of the whole of logic. The same man also wrote great and small questions in physics.

Gregory, archbishop of Constantinople, since he had imbibed sacred Scripture from the cradle, in mature age offered a brilliant proof of this matter. He wrote 29 elegant sermons, and 18 other addresses.

*In the Abbot of Sponheim, On Writers.*

A collection and exposition by the same man, [unclear], are extant in Greek at Rome in the Vatican Library.

Gregory Baeticus, bishop of Elvira, composed various treatises in a moderate style up to extreme old age, and an elegant book on faith.

He flourished in the time of St. Jerome, about the year of the Lord 400.

Gregory, patriarch of Cyprus, collected proverbs in alphabetical order; these are extant at Rome in the Vatican Library.

By the same man: Against Beccus Joannes, The Tome of the Holy Synod, or Pillar of Orthodoxy; On Measures; On Faith; On the Predicaments.

Gregory, bishop of Antioch, a most learned man and of a certain singular integrity, wrote certain sermons in Greek, namely: On the Burial of Christ; On Joseph of Arimathea; On the divine resurrection of the Lord after three days. They are preserved at Rome.

Gregory, metropolitan of Corinth, wrote on the canons of the κοιμήσων of the divine Virgin and of the Annunciation, moreover on the feast of the Lord’s Antipascha, that is, on the touching by Thomas, on the assumption of the great God and Savior Jesus, and on Pentecost.

A poem by the same man on the first feast of the venerable death of Cosmas the monk.

A Greek volume with this inscription is preserved at Rome in the Vatican Library.

Gregory Olympiadas, a man well learned in Greek literature, left to posterity splendid monuments of his talent, among which there is extant a Greek exposition on the Song of Songs and the Proverbs of Solomon, which is still found in Italy, not yet printed.

Guido Bonatus, otherwise Bonacius, a mathematician of Forlì, learned in the knowledge of the stars, wrote ten treatises on astronomy. He flourished under Martin IV, supreme pontiff, in 1282.

Guido of Ravenna, a priest six hundred years ago, wrote a history of the pontiffs.

A history by the same man on the war of the Goths is extant.

Guilhelmus Puteanus Blangiacus, a physician and citizen of Grenoble in France, a man of much learning and excellent talent, wrote a defense of John Mesue the physician, that aloe opens the mouths of the veins, and no few other similar things to be said, against John Manardus and Leonardus Fuchsius and many other newer physicians, a work not a little useful for knowing the powers of simple medicines.
