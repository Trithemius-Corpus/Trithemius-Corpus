*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...sent to Pope Leo testifies, he accomplished and endured many things for the extirpation of heresies. He wrote in the Greek language certain little works not to be despised; of these there are cited:

*Acts with Eutyches the Presbyter*, one book.
*Against the Errors of the Same*, one book.
*To Pope Leo on the Same Matter*, one letter. “No diabolical thing...”
*A Book of Letters to Various Persons*, one book.
And some other things which we do not know.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger and Marcian, in the year of the Lord 454.

Hilarius the pope, a Sardinian by birth, after Leo the Great sat in the pontificate for six years and as many months. He was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and distinguished by the adornment of many virtues; constantly intent on alms and holy works, he shone with a great reputation for holiness [unclear]. At Rome, when a synod of fifty bishops had been held, he made a number of decrees for ecclesiastical propriety. He wrote certain necessary little works; of these I have found only:

*A Synodal Decree*, one book. “Since a religious [unclear]...”
*To the Bishops of the Province of Tarragona*, one letter. “After the letters of your...”
*To Ascanius, Bishop of Tarragona*, one letter. “Of divine grace toward us...”
*On the Council of Nicaea*, one letter.
*On the Council of Ephesus*, one letter.
*On the Council of Chalcedon*, one letter.
*A Book of Letters to Various Persons*, one book.
He died under Leo Augustus, in the year of the Lord 468, in the sixth indiction, on the ninth day before the Kalends of March; Simplicius succeeded him.

Eusebius, bishop of Milan, very learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, both familiar and dear to the most blessed pontiff Leo, fighting and writing bravely for the holy Catholic faith against the heretics, is said to have published some little works for the edification of the faithful. Among the things he is said to have written in matters of faith is:

*To Saint Leo the Pope*, one letter. “Having returned [unclear]...”
He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Asclepius the African, bishop of the territory of Vaga, a studious and learned man, an outstanding declaimer of homilies to the people, and a most vigorous assailant of heretics, wrote many volumes for the edification of the people. By their publication he was named glorious in every land, since he showed himself a useful teacher not only in words but also in works. For many most learned men made his age famous. Of his writings there are cited:

*Against the Donatists*, one book.
*Against the Arians*, one book.
*Countless Homilies to the People*, one book.
And many other things unknown to us.
He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

Prosper, bishop of Reggio after Faventius, an Aquitanian by birth, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, eloquent in speech, profound in sense, subtle in intellect, vigorous in assertion, and most holy in life and conduct. He was once notary of the blessed Pope Leo the Great, and composed many letters in his person. He also wrote from his own vein many splendid little works; of these I have found only the following: from the Sentences of the blessed bishop Augustine, in hexameter and pentameter verses, mixed with prose, a most beautiful work, which he wished to entitle:

*Epigrams*, one book. “When it pleases to direct the mind to sacred things...”
*On the Contemplative Life*, three books. “The contemplative life in...”
*On Free Will*, one book. “To the lord and most beloved brother Ru...”
*Responses to the Chapters of the Gauls*, one book. “The doctrine which the holy...”
*To the Objections of the Vincentians*, one book. “Certain Christian...”
*To the Extracts of the Genoese*, one book. “In the books of blessed memory...”
*A Great Sum of 350 Propositions*, one book. “True knowledge is that which...”
*Against Cassian on Free Will*, one book.
*A Chronicle of Histories*, one book.
*A Book of Letters to Various Persons*, one book.
*On Illustrious Men*, one book.
*On the Captivity of Rome*, one book.
He says that he wrote some other things besides, but they have not come into our hands.
He flourished in the times of the great emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460. His feast is celebrated on the seventh day before the Kalends of July.

Victor, bishop of Cartenna, a city of Mauretania, learned in the holy Scriptures and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, a notable persecutor of heretics, is said to have written many volumes, in which he both instructed the devout people and powerfully crushed those who erred concerning the faith. Of these the following are cited:

*Against the Arians*, one book.
*Against the Donatists*, one book.
*Consolatory Address to Basilius*, one book.
*On Penitence Also*, one book.
*Homilies to the People*, two books.
He published not a few other things as well, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished under Marcian Augustus, in the year of the Lord 450.
