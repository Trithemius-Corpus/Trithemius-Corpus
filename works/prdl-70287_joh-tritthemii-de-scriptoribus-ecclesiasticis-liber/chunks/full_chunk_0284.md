Paulus Belmissenus, physician, lecturer and doctor of Bologna, and a man fully learned in all the arts, rendered the first two books of Aristotle *On Animals* in Latin elegies, which correspond to their chapters.

Of the same man, three elegies: one to Francis, king of the Gauls; another on the pope’s departure into Gaul; the third to Clement, supreme pontiff, on undertaking war against the Turks.

An epithalamium for Henry, brother of King Francis.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 1543.

Paulus Cortesius, apostolic protonotary, a remarkable lover of the liberal arts, used a style beyond measure elegant in the commentaries which he wrote on the four books of the *Sentences*.

It is also reported that he wrote certain letters.

He flourished in the time of Julius II, supreme pontiff.

Paulus Ricius, an Israelite, a very learned man converted to the Christian faith a few years ago, and physician of Emperor Maximilian, composed many excellent little works, of which these are extant:

*The Balance of the Wise*.

A book *On the Six Hundred and Thirteen Ordinances of the Mosaic Sanction or Pentateuchal Edict*.

*A Prophetic, Philosophical, and Talmudic Disputation on Behalf of Christian Truth*.

*An Introduction to the Learning of the Kabbalists or Allegorists*, with a letter against the letter of Stephen the priest, detractor of the Kabbalah.

*A Compendium on the Nine Orders of Doctrines and the Connection of the Whole Peripatetic Dogma*.

*A Resolutive Oration on the Threefold Order of Doctrine*.

*Conclusions by Which You Will Be Able to Judge That Aristotle Practiced a Threefold Doctrine and to Discern the Connection of His Whole Dogma*.

*On Celestial Agriculture*, book 1, [unclear], to Emperor Charles V and Ferdinand his brother.

*On the Common Principle of the Sacraments*, a sermon.

*On the Hidden Providence of God*, on the Psalm *Blessed is the man*.

*A Compendium on the Soul of Heaven, According to the Tradition of the Holy Philosophers*.

*On the Method of the Parts of the Proem According to the Rhetoricians*.

*On the Method of Investigating and Establishing the Subject of Any Science*.

*A Learned and Elegant Oration Against the Virulent and Most Savage Frenzy of the Turks, to the Princes, Magistrates, and Peoples of Germany*.

*A Medley Excerpted from the Talmudic Code, at the Request of Emperor Maximilian*.

He flourished, as is clear, under Charles V, most unconquered emperor of the Romans.

Petrus de Alvernia, of the Order of Preachers, a disciple of Saint Thomas Aquinas, a man learned in the divine Scriptures by long study and acute in intellect, wrote commentaries on several books of Aristotle, namely:

*On the Motions of Animals*.

*On Length and Shortness of Life*.

*On Youth and Old Age*.

*On Respiration*.

*On Death and Life*.

*Additions to the Commentaries of St. Thomas Aquinas on Aristotle’s Books On Heaven*.

Petrus Bembus, a Venetian cardinal, now an old man, is still living at Padua, unless I am mistaken; a man highly adorned with the liberal arts, the greatest imitator of Ciceronian eloquence, he commended the acuteness of his intellect to mortals in numerous little works, of which they report these:

Quite a few letters.

*On the Imitation of Speech*, one book.

*Benacus*, a heroic poem.

A little book *On Aetna*.

I hear that at this time he is committing the history of the Venetians to writing.
