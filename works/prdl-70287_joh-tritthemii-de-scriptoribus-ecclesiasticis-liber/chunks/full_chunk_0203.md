*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1446, in the ninth indiction, on the fourth day before the Ides of July.

Henry of Vuerlis, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, minister of the province of Cologne, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, excellent in talent and scholastic eloquence, an acute disputant and an outstanding preacher of sermons, composed certain lucid volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there is extant the notable work:

On the Sentences, four books.
On Ecclesiastical Power, one book.
Various Sermons also, one book. And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Andreas of Traiectum, a German by nation, a monk of our monastery of Spanheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, which is situated within the bounds of the diocese of Mainz, between the Rhine and the Hercynian forest which, called Saonus, extends westward toward Trier, a man certainly studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant also of secular letters, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote, both in verse and in prose, not contemptible little works, of which the following are held:

Soliloquies of a Man to God, one book. “When once the divine [unclear]...”
On the Progress of Virtues, one book. “Having gone out perhaps [unclear]...”
On the Use of [unclear], one book. “Your fraternity’s [unclear]...”
Letters to Various Persons, one book. “Reverend father, recently from...”
Various Conferences, one book. “Although among all who wrongly...”
Poems and rhythms. And certain other brief works.

He flourished in the aforesaid monastery of Spanheim, before the introduction of the very recent Bursfeld reform, in the year of the Lord 1445.

Antoninus, of the Order of Friars Preacher, archbishop of Florence, an Italian by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence, famous and of celebrated reputation in preaching sermons to the people, and no less venerable in life and conduct than in learning, wrote many splendid volumes, by which he labored magnificently for the edification of the Church militant. Of these there is extant a great and very celebrated work, divided into four not small volumes, which he entitled Sum[unclear].

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

Summa of Cases of Conscience, four books.
A Vast and Notable Chronicle, three books.
Manual for Confessors, one book.
On Ecclesiastical Censure, one book.
Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book.
And certain other works.

“How greatly magnified are...” “I shall speak propositions from [unclear]...” “Those searching have failed in [their search?]...”

He died, after many labors in sacred study, not without a reputation for holiness, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1459, in the seventh indiction, on the sixth day before the Nones of May, and was buried at Saint Mark’s, a convent of his order. He was inserted into the number of the saints by Pope Adrian VI; his feast is kept on the second day of May.

Leonard of Utino, of the Order of Friars Preacher, a man indeed studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, of excellent talent and outstanding reputation in preaching sermons to the people, wrote for the use of preachers certain splendid little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

Sermons on the Season, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book. “Interpreter of divine things...”
On Laws through Lent, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1445.

John of Capistrano, a town in Abruzzo not far from the city of L’Aquila, of the Order of Friars Minor, formerly a disciple of Saint Bernardino of Siena, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently taught in canon law, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence, and devout also in life and religious conduct, a very celebrated preacher of the divine word, who by word and example converted many from iniquity, wrote certain little works of no small usefulness, of which few have come down to me:

On Covetousness, three books. “Do not lay up treasures for yourselves...”
Against the Hussites, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
Some Letters. And certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1450, and not without a reputation for holiness.
