John Valeis, otherwise Galensis, an Englishman by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, who for a long time taught theology with distinction in the school of Paris, was of such great learning and eloquence that he was called the Tree of Life. He wrote many excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. But few of them have come into our hands. For I found a notable volume, which I judge very necessary and useful for preachers of the word of God, which most people call the *Communiloquium*, because it contains something concerning every estate; but he himself entitled it:
*Sum of Collations*, 7 books. "Since the doctor or preacher ..."
*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*On the Ordering of Life*, 1 book. "Since in every undertaking ..."
*Handful of Flowers*, 1 book. "Abstinence is good in ..."
*On the Apocalypse of John*, 1 book.
*Various Sermons*, 1 book.
*On Diverse Subjects*, 1 book.
Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

And note that this book of John is called the *Handful of Flowers*, whose prologue begins, "Poor little Ruth"; he began it, but, prevented by death, did not finish it, but Brother Thomas of Ireland afterwards completed the same work. The book itself begins thus, according to alphabetical order: "Abstinence is good in food to receive with thanksgiving," etc. He flourished in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William Durandus, bishop of Mende, once a hearer and disciple of Henry of Ostia, whom they call the Speculator, a most pious doctor in law and very zealous for ecclesiastical observance, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, wrote to Ottobono, cardinal of St. Hadrian, afterwards supreme pontiff, Hadrian V, an outstanding work divided into three volumes, which he entitled:
*Mirror of Law*, 3 books. "Reverend in Christ ..."
*Rationale of Divine Offices*, 8 books. "Whatever things in the ecclesiastical ..."
*Repertory of Law*, 1 book. "By the rust of the first-formed man ..."
And certain other works.
He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, and completed his work the *Rationale of Divine Offices*, as he himself testifies, in the year of the Lord 1286.

James of Belusio, a very distinguished doctor of both laws and a careful interpreter, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, profound in counsel and knowledge, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, is said to have written certain excellent little works in law, by which he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity; of these I have not yet been able to see any. His works are said to be:
*On the Authentics*, 1 book.
*On the View of Fiefs*, 1 book.
*Additions on the Novels*, 1 book.
And many other works.
And various disputations.
He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William of Lyons, of the Order of Friars Preachers, as they say, bishop of the same church, a man much learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, of sweet intellect and subtle speech, not over-curious, yet orderly, was quite outstanding in delivering sermons to the people. He is said to have written certain little works, by which he magnificently instructed both those then present and those who would come afterwards; of these they report:
*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*Sum of Virtues*, 5 books. "Since concerning individual useful things ..."
*Sum of Vices*, 8 books. "About to speak of individual vices ..."
*Sermons to the People*, 1 book.
He is said to have written many other works also, which have not come to my knowledge.

William of Ware, an Englishman by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, teacher of John Scotus of the same order, a man sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures and, through long study, very skilled in Aristotelian philosophy, in the manner of modern theologians, wrote certain works of his intellect not to be despised, by which he made himself known to posterity. Of these there are extant:
*On the Sentences*, 1 book. "Since, according to the testimony of Blessed Augustine ..."
Concerning the rest I have seen nothing.
