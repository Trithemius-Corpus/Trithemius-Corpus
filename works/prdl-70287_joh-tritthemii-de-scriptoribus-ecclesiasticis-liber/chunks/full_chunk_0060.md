Victorinus of Marseilles, rhetorician, a man most learned in secular letters and very devoted to the holy Scriptures, attempted to expound the holy Scriptures; but, being occupied with secular literature and having entered the field of the divine utterances without a teacher, he did not accomplish what he wished. Hence the little works that he wrote are indeed distinguished by Christian feeling and pious zeal, but are shaped with a lighter weight of opinions. He published, addressed to the person of his son Ethernius, commentaries from the beginning down to the death of the patriarch Abraham:

On Genesis, four books.

And certain other things which are hidden from us. He flourished under Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 430.

Philippus, monk and priest, once a hearer and disciple of blessed Jerome, a studious and learned man, an outstanding lover of voluntary poverty, who by word and example instructed many in piety, after the death of his master published certain commentaries on the divine Scriptures, among which there survives, as they say, an excellent work:

On Job, one book.
A collection of letters, one book.

He flourished under the princes Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 430. And he died under Marcian.

Vincentius, monk and priest of the monastery of the island of Lerins, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, distinguished in life and character. There survives an outstanding work of his, which he composed under the name Peregrinus, very famous among the ancient doctors and worthy of praise, on avoiding the errors and gatherings of heretics:

Against all heretics, two books.

It is also read that he wrote a number of other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the reign of Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 429.

*Abbot of Spanheim*

Paulus, priest, a Pannonian by nation, which is now called Hungary, a man devoted to sacred letters and sufficiently learned, venerable no less for the cultivation of religion than for knowledge of the Scriptures, a zealous defender of the Christian faith, is said to have written not a few volumes, the reading of which is considered quite suitable for confounding heretics. There survive his works addressed to Constantia, a virgin dedicated to Christ:

On virginity and contempt of the world, two books.

Concerning other works I have so far found nothing. He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 430.

Isaac, formerly a monk, priest of the church of Antioch, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, of very celebrated reputation for delivering homilies to the people, beloved by God in life and conduct, venerable to men, wrote in the Syriac tongue, in imitation of Saint Ephrem, several devout little works, the reading of which is judged very useful for religious men. Among these I have found the following among our materials:

Against the Nestorians and Eutyches, two books.
An exhortation to the spiritual life, one book.
On the struggle against vices, one book.
On access to God, one book.
On the difficulty of the virtues, one book.
A dialogue on spiritual progress, one book.
On the order of the monastic life, one book.
On violent operation, one book.
On humility, one book.
On the three orders of those making progress, one book.
On the solitude of monks, one book.
On various temptations, one book.
On the instruction of novices, one book.
On penitence, one book.
A poem on the devastation of Antioch, one book.

He delivered several homilies to the people, but they have by no means come into our hands. He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Eucherius, bishop of Lyons in Gaul, a learned man, imbued from youth with monastic discipline, ready in talent, composed in speech, pleasing to God and men in life and conduct, since indeed he turned many away from iniquity by word and example, wrote several excellent volumes, but so far few have come to my knowledge.
