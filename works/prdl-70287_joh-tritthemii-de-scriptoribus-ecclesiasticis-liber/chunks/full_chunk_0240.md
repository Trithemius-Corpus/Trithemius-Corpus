*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Of letters to various persons, one book. And many other things.
He translated into Latin John Damascene on the immaculate mysteries; also Dionysius on the divine names, and certain letters of his; also the volume On the Soul by Alexander of Aphrodisias the philosopher; and many other things.

He lives to this day at Venice, thirty-seven years old, writing and translating various works, under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Jodocus Badius of Ghent, a German by nation, a man most learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, rhetorician, and most distinguished poet, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech, who formerly at Ferrara heard Baptista Guarinus teaching Greek letters and in that science also became learned, now adorns Lyons with his learning, reads the poets publicly and privately, instructs several sons of magnates, and has written in verse many distinguished little works, of which the following are extant: a notable metrical work against the vices, which he entitled

The Moral Wood, one book. And many other things.
Also Against Vincentius, one book. “Unhappy Vincentius, which …”
The Psalter of Mary in sapphics, one book. “What the chorus of bards …”
Also On Grammar, one book.
Several epigrams, one book.
Various letters.

He lives to this day, thirty-two years old, composing many different things under Maximilian, the renowned king, in the year of the Lord 1492, indiction 12.

Elias Regnier, a very celebrated jurist, excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, distinguished in speech, cautious in counsel, who for a long time publicly taught law in the university of Poitiers, wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have seen only the following: a celebrated work which contains

Cases of the Sixth Book of the Decretals, one book. “Boniface VIII [unclear] …”
Cases of the Clementines, one book. “John, bishop [unclear] …”

Of the others I have seen nothing.

He is still living in the University of Poitiers, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Rutgerus Venray, a German by nation, of the order of the regular canons of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of the divine apostle Peter, which, situated not far from Worms, is called Heyne, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, practiced in metre and prose, wrote in both kinds certain little works not to be despised, of which the following are extant: a Tetralogue, which he entitled

On the Instruction of Children, two books. “When boys [unclear] …”

On the Varied Appearance of Letters, two books. “Although, excellent Peter …”
On a Marvelous Thing, two books. “For knowing [unclear] in verse …”
On New Things, two books. “I say that very many things [unclear] …”
On the Kinds of Metres, one book. “Most familiarly in [unclear] …”
On the Accents of the Latins, two books. “This Tetralogue, which on …”
A Panegyric to Me, one book. “To the reverend [unclear] …”
Various epigrams. And certain other things.

I hear that he is fashioning for me a work on the calamities of these times, which I have not yet seen.

He has written very many letters, both to me and to others, in verse and in prose, not inelegant.

He lives to this day and composes various things, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Jodocus Beisselius, patrician of the royal city of Aachen, a German by nation, a man devoted to the holy Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, fluent orator, and notable poet, who, although he is necessarily distracted by care for his wife and household property, nevertheless burns with incredible love for the Scriptures, writing and composing many things daily, by which he spreads his name everywhere with great glory. Of these the following are extant: a most beautiful dialogue addressed to Hermolaus Barbarus, in the manner of the Platonists,

On the Best Kind of Musicians, one book. “I have led you, O Hermolaus …”
