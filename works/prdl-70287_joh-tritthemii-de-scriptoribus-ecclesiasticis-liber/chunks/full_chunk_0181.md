He flourished under Rupert the Bavarian, king of the Romans, and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1400.

Hugo of Schlettstadt, a German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and notably learned in the disciplines of the philosophers, subtle in intellect, and a distinguished disputant of scholastic questions, wrote, as they say, certain excellent volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name. Of these there is extant a work of *Questions on the Sentences*, four books.

And certain other works.

He was, moreover, a follower and clear interpreter, in his determinations, of the holy father Bonaventure, of the Order of Minors, explaining those things which in that man’s distinctive philosophy might have seemed obscure and rather rare to the simple.

Paulus Venetus, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and, in Aristotelian philosophy, second to none in Italy in his time, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, and an outstanding declaimer, composed many little works, especially in philosophy, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name.
