*Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

He is still living in the year of the Lord 1546; from him we expect great usefulness that will come to scholars, if the powers above prolong his life.

Franciscus de Accoltis of Arezzo, well trained in jurisprudence, wrote on the whole civil law.

He also wrote *Consilia*.

*On the Difference between an Interlocutory and a Definitive Sentence*.

He rendered the *Letters of Diogenes* into Latin.

He dedicated to Pope Pius a little book by an uncertain author, *On the Baths of Pozzuoli and the Neighboring Ones*, found and published by him in Italy.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 1300.

Franciscus Capuanus of Manfredonia, formerly public professor of astronomy in the University of Padua, wrote commentaries on the *Theoricae planetarum* of Georgius Purbachius.

Franciscus Craneveldius of Nijmegen, J.C. and councillor of the Imperial Majesty, in his preface to the books *On the Truth of the Christian Faith* by Ioannes Lodovicus Vives, discusses a number of matters concerning the excellence of those volumes, the manner of writing, and also the author himself.

The same man translated into Latin style the six books of Procopius the rhetor and historiographer *On the Buildings of the Emperor Justinian*.

He is still living under Emperor Charles V, in the year of the Lord 1546.

Franciscus Emericus of Opava, a Viennese physician, having exact knowledge of the medical art, among other things published a book *On the Proper Use of Medical Aids*, according to the true mind of Hippocrates and Galen.

Franciscus Macerata, of the Order of Minors, composed summaries of the individual chapters in the seven books of Josephus *On the Jewish War*, which serve in place of an epitome.

Franciscus Maturancius of Perugia, highly skilled in every kind of liberal art, wrote commentaries on the Philippic orations of M. Tullius Cicero, and on the books *On Duties*, also on the *Paradoxes* and the books of *Rhetoric*.

Franciscus Romeus of Castiglione, of the Order of Preachers, who, having concern for his profession, imbibed sacred letters from his earliest years, wrote a learned and elegant book *On the Freedom and Necessity of Works*, against Christian pseudo-philosophers.

The same man published a brief annotation concerning the immortality of the soul, derived in a Christian and Peripatetic manner, as the title of the book has it.

Franciscus Titelmannus of Hasselt, of the Order of the Friars Minor, a most vigilant professor of sacred Scripture among the Louvain scholars, commended himself to posterity by many excellent books, among which we have been able to find these:

An *Apologetic Epistle*.

A *Compendium of Natural Philosophy*, gathered into twelve books.

*On Dialectical Consideration*, 6 books.

Commentaries on the Epistle to the Romans.

Paraphrases on all the epistles of Saint Paul.

Most lucid commentaries on the whole Psalter.

And he published very many other things, which are found here and there in booksellers’ shops.

He flourished at the time of Desiderius Erasmus of Rotterdam.

Franciscus Valleriola, a very celebrated physician of Avignon, wrote commentaries on the six books of Galen *On Diseases and Symptoms*. He is still living under Francis of Valois, king of the French, composing many things.

Franciscus Vatablus, royal professor of the Hebrew language at Paris, a man most adorned with liberal arts, rendered into Latin Aristotle’s *Little Natural Works*, as they are called. He also revised the paraphrases on all the physical works of Aristotle published by Jacobus Faber.

The same man wrote annotations on the Pentateuch of Moses.

He is still living in the year 1546.

Fridericus Nausea, theologian and J.C., now bishop of Vienna in Austria, an outstanding cultivator of profane letters and sufficiently learned in sacred letters, wrote many little works. Among them these are reported:

Lenten sermons, earlier, later, and Advent sermons.

Prague sermons.

A homiliary of four centuries, on the season and on the saints.

Commentaries on Tobias.

*The Life of the Apostles*.

*On Marvels*.

*Fragments*.

*Continuations*.

*What Councils Are, and Whence*.

*Panegyrics*.

A *Monody on D. Erasmus of Rotterdam*, together with a catalogue of all Erasmus’s lucubrations, and his life and death.
