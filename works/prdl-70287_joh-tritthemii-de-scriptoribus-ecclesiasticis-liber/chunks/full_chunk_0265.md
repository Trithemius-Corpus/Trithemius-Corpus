*On ecclesiastical writers, Addition II.*

The Dialectic and Rhetoric of the same man.

Commentaries on several speeches of Cicero.

Annotations on Cicero's Paradoxes, and on the books On Duties, On Friendship, On Old Age, On the Dream of Scipio, on Cicero's Topics addressed to Trebatius, and on the Oratorical Partitions of the same author.

He also wrote in the year of salvation 1544 to Martin Bucer, in the form of a little book, a lengthy letter. Then in the following year he published an elegant book against the same Bucer, in which the following headings are treated:

On the administration of the Eucharist.
On the invocation of the Church.
On the celibacy of priests.

And he is still meditating certain other works, renowned under Charles V, emperor of the Romans.

Bartholomaeus Merula of Mantua, apostolic protonotary and very skilled in sacred and profane letters, wrote learned commentaries on Ovid's books from Pontus.

Bartholomaeus Vespuccius of Florence, distinguished doctor of arts and medicine, composed some scattered annotations on the Sphere of Johannes de Sacrobosco. A very elegant oration by the same man is extant.

Basilius Zanchus of Bergamo, canon of the Lateran order, composed an epitome of Latin words from various authors.

By the same man, an appendix of the words which are lacking in the observations of Marius Nizolius on Cicero.

By the same author, commentaries on epithets have appeared.

Bassianus Landus of Piacenza, a learned young man, composed Iatrologia, or two dialogues, addressed to Hercules Este, duke of Ferrara, in which is contained a method for recognizing and curing diseases.

The same man wrote two books On Human History.

And he rendered several speeches of Demosthenes into Latin.

Beatus Rhenanus of Selestat, a man abundantly learned in secular letters and very well instructed in the divine Scriptures, reviewed many books of the orthodox fathers, worn away by age, and himself composed not a few volumes, among which these are reported:

On German Affairs, 3 books.

Annotations on the extant books of Titus Livius.

Annotations on Cornelius Tacitus, with a preface.

He revised the two books of P. Velleius Paterculus' Roman History.

Very lucid commentaries on the works of D. Tertullian, together with arguments and a preface.

*442 Against the Abbot of Sponheim, On Writers.*

He prefixed a letter to the works of Origen.

On all the works of Erasmus of Rotterdam, a letter to Charles V, most unconquered emperor.

Commentaries on Seneca's play on the death of the emperor Claudius.

The Life of Johannes Geiler of Kaysersberg, written by the same man.

A preface to the Theophrastus of Aeneas, and to the gnomology of Xystus.

He translated two letters of Gregory Nazianzen to Themistius.

Preface and correction in the books of Pliny's Natural History.

He is still living today in his own homeland, namely in the year of salvation 1546, already an old man, and perhaps, as we hope, he will still deserve even more credit for good letters.

Beda Natalis, a Parisian theologian, wrote against certain false opinions of D. Erasmus of Rotterdam, and also of the Lutherans.

By the same author there appeared certain books on the single Magdalene, against Jacques Lefevre d'Etaples.

He flourished under Paul III and Charles V, in the year of the Lord 1536.

Benedictus Aretinus de Accoltis, a man greatly adorned with more polished learning, composed four books on the war waged by Christians for many years against the barbarians, for the recovery of Judaea and the holy sepulchre of Christ.

Benno the cardinal, archpriest of the Roman church, wrote the life and deeds of Hildebrand, who afterwards, having been made Roman pontiff, was called Gregory VII, in whose time he lived. There it is narrated why he was deposed, and certain other things are reported which no historian has yet published, neither Platina nor anyone else.

He flourished under Gregory VII, in the year of the Lord 1080.

Bernardinus Carvajal, bishop of Badajoz, a learned man and distinguished orator of the king of Spain, delivered at Rome, in the church of Saint Peter, before the senate of cardinals, after the death of Innocent VIII, an oration on electing the supreme pontiff.

Bernardus de Busti, a pious and learned man, wrote the Defence of the Mount of Piety, and the things that follow:

A counsel on buying back.

On the pact of buying back, in the first part of the Rosary.

Bernardus Lutzenburgus, monk of the Order of Preachers and prior of Cologne, wrote a catalogue of almost all the heretics who down to these times have everywhere been handed down in literary monuments, indicating their names, errors, and the times in which they lived, in [unclear] books.
