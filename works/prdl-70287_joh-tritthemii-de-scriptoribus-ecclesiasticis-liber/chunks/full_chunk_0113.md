For many years he devoted himself to secular philosophy in the school of Paris, and became a renowned philosopher. But, considering the philosophy of the present life to be vain, he abandoned the study of vanity; returning to his homeland, he became a monk in the said monastery and [then?] was made abbot. He wrote, moreover, some little works not unworthy of reading, of which I have found only the following:

On the Song of Songs, 4 books.
On Contempt of the World, 2 books.
On St. John the Baptist, 1 book.
Of His Revelations, 1 book.

He wrote certain sermons on the saints whose bodies or patronage they had in the same monastery of Limburg. He flourished under Emperor Henry V, not without a reputation for sanctity, in the year of the Lord 1124.

Rudolf, abbot of Saint-Trond in the diocese of Liege, of the order of the holy father Benedict, a German by nation, a man skilled in the divine Scriptures through constant exercise, not meanly learned in secular letters, strong in verse and prose, an excellent musician, and no less renowned for work than for knowledge, wrote in both styles little works not to be despised, of which there are reported:

Against the Simoniacs, 7 books. Also a Chronicle, 1 book.
Many poems.

He also composed several chants in praise of the saints, with sweet and regular melody. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1115.

Franco, second abbot of the monastery of Affligem, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, sufficiently instructed in secular doctrine, strong in talent and renowned for eloquence, wrote certain commendable little works both in meter and in prose, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. When he was still a simple monk in the aforesaid monastery, at the command of his abbot Fulgentius, he began a notable work, which afterward, having been made abbot, he completed, adorned with knowledge and eloquence:

On the Grace of God, 1 book.
On the State of Future Glory, in meter, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
Sermons on the Blessed Virgin, 2 books.
And certain other things.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

He was elected abbot of the aforesaid monastery of Affligem under Emperor Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1109, in the thirteenth indiction.

Gilbert, bishop of Poitiers, formerly a Parisian scholastic, surnamed Porreta, a Frenchman by nation, a man much exercised in the divine Scriptures and magnificently learned in secular letters, subtle in talent and confused in speech by the novelty of his terms, was accused of heresy in certain matters by Blessed Bernard, and, convicted in the council of Reims celebrated by Eugenius, is said to have renounced his error. There are those who excuse him, saying that the authorities introduced in his expositions were understood by some in a less sound way. But we, agreeing with the Roman Church, confess that what has once been concluded by it may be infringed by no one. Gilbert wrote many little works, of which the following are reported:

On the Whole Psalter, 1 book.
On All the Epistles of Paul, 14 books.
On the Gospel of John, 1 book.
On Boethius, On the Trinity, 1 book.
Books of Questions, etc., 1 book.
Several sermons.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Conrad III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1140.

Lucas, abbot of Mount Saint-Corneille, of the order of the holy father Benedict, learned in the holy Scriptures by continual exercise, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters for his station, having obtained a noble talent in expounding the divine books, transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity by writing various treatises. But I do not recall that any of them has come into our hands. There is reported a notable work of his addressed to Milo, bishop of the church of Therouanne, a man likewise most learned, which I have not yet seen:

On the Song of Songs, 1 book.
Several sermons and letters.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Lothar, the last emperor, in the year of the Lord 1130.
