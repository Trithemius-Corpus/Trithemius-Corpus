Flavaldus, a monk of Reims, of the Order of St. Benedict, learned in the divine scriptures and exceptionally instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote little works not unworthy of reading; by his zeal he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among these there is said to be a distinguished work, from the founding of the city of Reims, which they relate was built and so named in memory of their lord by the soldiers of Remus, who had fled after Remus was killed by his brother Romulus, down to his own times; in it, inserting many things about the holy martyrs and other matters and deeds, he wished to give the volume this title: *The Deeds of the Bishops of Reims*, one book. Also *Lives of Many Saints*, one book. He is said besides to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Albertus, a monk of Lobbes and afterwards abbot of the monastery of Gembloux, teacher of Burchard, bishop of Worms, a man learned in the divine scriptures and very well instructed in secular letters: while he was still a monk, he kept the public school of the monks at Lobbes, in which he instructed Burchard, together with others, in both kinds of literature. Among other works of his talent he wrote *Lives of Several Saints*, one book; *Chants in Honor of the Saints*; and certain other things. He flourished in great religious devotion and discipline in the times of the emperor Otto II, in the year of the Lord 980.

Aribo, archbishop of Mainz, [unclear] after St. Boniface the martyr, to whom Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, wrote a treatise on the coming of the Lord, was a man devoted to and learned in the divine scriptures. Having held a synod at Seligenstadt with Burchard, bishop of Worms, and his other bishops and abbots, in the year 1023, indiction 6, he established many useful things, which are contained in authentic writings.
