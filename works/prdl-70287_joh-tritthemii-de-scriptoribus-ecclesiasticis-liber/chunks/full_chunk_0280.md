He is still flourishing, as I think, namely in the year of the Lord 1546.

Johannes Ruellius, a physician of Soissons, by nation a Frenchman, wrote three books *On the Nature of Plants*, than which, in this subject, nothing, so far as I know, has appeared down to this day more learned or more copious.

He also translated not a few works, of which you have the following:

He translated the six books of Pedanius Dioscorides *On Medical Matter*, and one book of Actuarius *On the Composition of Medicines*.

He rescued from decay, with very great diligence, Scribonius Largus’ book *On the Composition of Medicines*.

The same man gave Latin form to two books of veterinary medicine collected from Apsyrtus and others.

He flourished in the year of salvation 1539.

Johannes Simoneta, by nation a Calabrian, a most diligent man in composing histories, wrote thirty-one books on the deeds of Francesco Sforza, duke of the Milanese.

He flourished around the year 1486.

Johannes Stella, a priest in rank, but a Venetian by homeland, diligent in writing the lives of the supreme pontiffs, wrote a book on the life and morals of the Roman pontiffs, from Peter the Apostle down to Julius II, that is, to the year of the Lord 1503.

Johannes Tagautius Ambianus Vimacus, a Parisian physician, very celebrated in our age, published six books *On Surgical Instruction*.

The same man wrote two books *On Purgative Medicines*.

And certain other works known to physicians.

He is flourishing in the year of the Lord 1546.

Isaac, a monk by profession, highly skilled in Greek literature, composed a Greek scholium on the first figure of a description on a plane, and verses on the twelve winds.

He wrote out certain other things, the catalogue of which I shall append:

Various astronomical works in Greek, and *The Apparatus of the Astrolabe*.

*A Method of Geodesy*, that is, of the division of the earth.

*The Solution of a Certain Palamedic Question*, [unclear].

*On the Abbot of Spanheim, On Writers.*

An encomium of the martyr Demetrius. And on the saying, “These things I command you, that you love one another.”

At what time he flourished, I have nothing to offer.

Johannes Thaulerus, of the Order of Saint Dominic, a most profound theologian and a support of the city of Cologne, excellently illuminated by the anointing of the Holy Spirit in delivering sermons, wrote sermons on the perfection of the inner life, very useful.

A treatise on the virtues.

And various letters, redolent of the spirit of devotion.

And with prophetic spirit he foretold the scourges and heresies against the sacraments and doctrines of the Catholic Church that would arise, which now in our times are devastating the Church.

He flourished at Cologne around the year of the Lord 1350.

His works, now translated into Latin, are expected to appear any day.

Lambertus de Monte wrote a sufficiently acute magisterial question, showing by the authorities of divine Scripture what, according to the sounder opinion of the doctors, can more probably be said about the salvation of Aristotle the Stagirite.

Landulphus de Columna, a Roman, canon of Chartres, a man not idle in collecting histories, wrote a history of the Pontiffs, not long after Vincent of Beauvais.

Lazarus Baynius, by nation a Frenchman, in our age ambassador among the Venetians in the name of Francis, king of the French, a very learned man, wrote *Annotations on the Second Law, On Captives Returned by Right of Postliminium*.

By the same man, annotations on the treatise *On Gold and Silver Bequeathed*, in which the kinds of garments and vessels are explained.

The books are altogether most learned, philological, and very useful for the knowledge of authors in both languages, especially historians.

Leonardus Iacchinus, or Giachinus, formerly a Florentine physician, but now, as I hear, a professor at Pisa, an exceptionally learned man, wrote a book *On the Number and Being of Indications*.

Also a treatise against Avicenna, Mesue, and all the common physicians.

He also translated Galen’s little book *On Prognosis*, to which is added an apologetic oration.
