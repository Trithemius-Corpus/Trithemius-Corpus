John of Schoonhoven, a German by nation, of the order of the Canons Regular of St Augustine, from the monastery in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, which is commonly called Groenendaal and in Latin Viridis Vallis, in which John Ruusbroec formerly held the office of prior, was a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, cautious in intellect, composed in speech, and no less distinguished in conduct than in knowledge. He wrote to Simon, his nephew:

A Spiritual Exhortation, 1 book. “Great joy to me”
On the Course of a Monk, 1 book. “That to you, dearest” [unclear]
On the Progress of a Monk, 1 book. “Fight the good fight”
A Defense of John Ruusbroec, 1 book. “When a certain doctor” [unclear]
Various Sermons, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
And many other works.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Constance, under Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Peter de Colle, of the order of the Friars Minor of St Francis, a German by nation, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, and eloquent and lucid in delivering sermons to the people. He published several volumes for the edification of readers, by which he transmitted the memory of his distinguished name to posterity. Among these there survives a well-arranged work, which he recited before the fathers at the Council of Basel:

On the Authority of a Council, 1 book. “The Church fought many” [unclear]
Questions on the Sentences, 1 book.
Various Sermons, 1 book.
And many other works.

He flourished at the time of the same Council of Basel, at the beginning of the reign of Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1440.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Hermann, a monk in Campo, of the Cistercian order in the diocese of Münster, a German by nation and a Westphalian by homeland, was a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently instructed both in canon law and in Aristotelian philosophy, ready in intellect and clear in speech. He wrote several little works of his practiced talent, by which he labored considerably in defense of the Council of Basel. Among these I found a little work that he entitled On Definitive Voices:

In a General Council, 1 book. “Now after very often repeated” [unclear]
On the Power of the Pope and of a Council, 1 book. “Blessed Athanasius”
Also On Schism, 1 book.
On Neutrality, 1 book.
Of the rest which he composed, although many are reported, nothing has come down to me.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, in the year of the Lord 1440.

John Gauer, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, lector of sacred Scripture, of the convent of Mainz, was a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently capable in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote little works of his practiced talent not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there survive:

On the Sentences, 4 books. “To the praise of almighty God”
Also On Exodus, 1 book. “The law for the common utility”
A Harmony of the Evangelists, 1 book. “Because God sent his Son”
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
A Triple Lenten Work, 3 books. “When you fast, know” [unclear]
And many other works.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Basel, under Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1440.

Bernardino, of the order of the Friars Minor, an Italian by nation and from Siena by homeland, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures, the most celebrated preacher of all his time, most holy in life and conduct, inasmuch as both living and dead he shone with countless miracles. He wrote certain little works for the edification of the faithful, by which he also showed the power of his talent to posterity. Of these there survive:

On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book. “Blessed [unclear] threefold”
On the Eternal Gospel, 1 book. “For I did not come to dissolve the law”
On the Christian Religion, 1 book. “Now abide faith, hope”
