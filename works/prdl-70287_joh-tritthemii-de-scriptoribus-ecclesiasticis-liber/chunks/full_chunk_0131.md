*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

He flourished under Frederick, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 1250.

Henry, archbishop of Embrun and cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, commonly called the Ostian, a man most skilled in both laws, who, on account of the excellence of his talent and learning, was held in esteem by Pope Alexander IV, wrote certain little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only the distinguished work known to all, *On the Titles of the Decretals*, in five books. “Alpha and omega, one in [unclear].” Also a concise *Summa*, in one book.

The other works that he composed have not yet come to my knowledge.

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1259.

[Unclear], a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in the disciplines of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, was of excellent industry in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported: for at the command of Pope Alexander IV he arranged, in a very useful style, through the cycle of the year, *Sermons*, in two books; *On the Sentences*, in four books; *Quodlibets*, in one book. “It is asked how virtue...”

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1250.

[Unclear], a man exercised by long study in the divine writings and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and clear in eloquence, who in his own time, because of his learning and erudition, was of great reputation, wrote at the request of the provost and clerics of the church of Lille, *On the Psalter*, in one book, and certain letters.

He also published several other works, which have by no means come to our knowledge.

Alexander, called de Villa Dei, of Dol as his native place, of the order, as they say, of the Friars Minor, a man certainly trained in every direction, a grammarian, philosopher, astronomer, and distinguished calculator, wrote not a few little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity. Of these I have so far found only the following: a volume written in leonine verse on grammar, whose use today is constant in boys’ schools, [unclear] so useful, because of the weakness of the younger age and the sloth of teachers.
