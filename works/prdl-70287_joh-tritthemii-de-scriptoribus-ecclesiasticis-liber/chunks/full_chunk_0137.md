Rupert of Rusia, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, inasmuch as he taught theology excellently for many years and left after him many distinguished disciples, wrote several excellent little works by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant, addressed to Haymo the Englishman, minister general of his order, a man likewise most learned, a Declaration, one book.

On the Rule of St. Francis, four books.
Also on the Sentences, four books.
Various sermons, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Udalric of Argentina, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, formerly a hearer and disciple of Albert the Great, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, composed little works not to be despised in reading, by which he became known to both present and future generations. Of these there are extant: A Summa of Theology, one book.

On the Sentences, four books.
Also On the Soul, one book. And many other things.

He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

Haymo, an Englishman by nation, minister general of the order of friars, a man trained by long study in the holy Scriptures, who, having obtained the magisterial chair of the theological faculty, instructed many disciples in it, and who, by order of Pope Alexander IV, corrected and rubricated the Roman Breviary, is reported, among other things, to have written briefly:

On the Sentences, one book. And certain sermons, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1279.

*Abbot of Spanheim*

He flourished while the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord 1260.

Peter the Spaniard, a Portuguese, bishop of Tusculum and cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, and finally supreme pontiff after Hadrian V, a man very learned in medicine and magnificently instructed in secular philosophy, sat in the pontificate for only eight months. He wrote little works not to be despised by physicians, of which the following are reported:

Thesaurus pauperum, one book. On Problems, one book.
Canons of Medicine, one book. Letters to Various Persons, one book.
And certain other things, namely several treatises in dialectic, already long very well known commonly.

He flourished in the pontificate under the name John XXI, in the times of the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1275.

William, a citizen of Piacenza, a learned doctor of the art of medicine and not ignorant of other humane letters, who, hired at a public salary at Verona, maintained a school in that faculty for many years and instructed several excellent disciples, wrote little works not to be despised by professors of this science, by which he made his name known to both present and future generations. Of these there is extant a distinguished volume which he wished to be entitled:

On Universal Medicine, four books. Also On Surgery, one book.
Concerning the rest I have found nothing.

He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.
