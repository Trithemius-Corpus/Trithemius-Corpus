*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Alanus de Insulis, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular letters second to none in his time, a very celebrated theologian, philosopher, and poet, who for a long time gloriously presided over the ecclesiastical school at Paris, where, shining brightly in every divine and human science, he deserved to be called Universal, wrote in verse and prose almost innumerable little works, by which the memory of his name was made immortal among posterity. But few of them have come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following under their titles:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Quodlibets also, 1 book.
The Eye of Sacred Scripture, 1 book.
On Doctrine also, 5 books.
On the Five Books of Moses, 5 books.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book.
On Penance, to the father of Bourges, 1 book.
On Mystical Equivocal Terms, 1 book.
On the Six Wings of the Cherubim, 1 book.
On the Complaint of Nature, 1 book.
A Summa of Virtues and Vices, 1 book.
On Parables, 1 book.
On the Art of Preaching, 1 book.
Anticlaudianus, 1 book.
On the Natures of Animals, 1 book.
Several Sermons, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.

To the most reverend father and...
Every science rests upon its own rules.
Since many, for the adorning...
By the grace of God, to the father of Bourges [unclear]
From Phoebus, Phoebe [unclear]

And he wrote some dialogues.

Finally, he is said to have produced notable commentaries and lectures on many books of the Bible, especially on the prophets, on the Gospel, and on the Apostle; concerning these I have so far been able to find nothing. Certain marvelous things are told about him, to which I neither lend credence nor rashly deny it. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.
