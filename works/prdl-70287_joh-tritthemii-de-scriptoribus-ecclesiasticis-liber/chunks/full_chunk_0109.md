One homily is extant, which is found with the homilies collected by Alcuin.
Likewise clear expositions on all the epistles of St. Paul, which lay hidden for almost 450 years, now in our age rescued from decay and filth by the industry and labor of D. Renatus Casteneus.
A dialogue on the Passion of the Lord.
He flourished under Emperor Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1080.

William, abbot of the monastery of Hirsau in the diocese of Speyer, of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, to whom the blessed Anselm, archbishop of Canterbury, wrote several letters, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, inferior to no one among the Germans of his time in secular letters, a philosopher, an outstanding astronomer and musician, most holy in life and conduct, who, as one reads, shone with many miracles, reformed regular discipline, long since collapsed, in many monasteries of Germany. Therefore, for the preservation of monastic observance, from the statutes of the ancients, he wrote:

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Constitutions of Monks, twelve books.
On music and tones, thirteen books.
On the clock, fourteen books.
On the correction of the Psalter, fifteen books.
To Saint Anselm of Canterbury, one letter.
Letters to various persons, seventeen books.
He also published some other works, which through the negligence of the ancients have fallen into oblivion.
He died under Emperor Henry III, but under the fourth king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1091, in the fourteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Nones of July, and was buried in the middle of the church of the apostles, with a great reputation for sanctity.

Gelselbert, provost of the monastery of West in England, of the order of Saint Benedict, hearer and disciple of Anselm of Canterbury, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, quick in intellect and cautious in speech, made his name known to posterity by writing, when he composed certain necessary little works. Among these the following are reported: a disputation against the Jews,

To Blessed Anselm the bishop, eighteen books.
On the prophet Isaiah, nineteen books. And certain other works.
Certain homilies on the Song of Solomon are also circulated under his name, which you will find among the Eberbach monks.
He flourished under Henry IV with Anselm, in the year of the Lord 1080.

Gunther, monk of Ellwangen, of the order of Saint Benedict, skilled in the divine Scriptures through constant practice, and nobly learned in the disciplines of secular letters, a distinguished theologian and poet, subtle in intellect and polished in speech, wrote certain excellent volumes both in verse and in prose, of which few have come to my knowledge. His volume in elegant meter is extant:

On the Passion of Saint Cyriacus, twenty books. And certain letters.
He flourished in the times of King Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1100.

Theoderic, abbot of the monastery of Saint Trudo in the diocese of Liege, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, wrote certain excellent little works in both styles, in which he magnificently exercised his talent. Among these the following are reported, written in elegant meter:

Life of Saint Trudo, thirteen books. Life of Saint Bavo, fourteen books.
