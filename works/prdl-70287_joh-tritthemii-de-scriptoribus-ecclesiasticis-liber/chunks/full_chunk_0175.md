*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Henry Boyc, a Breton by nation, from the diocese of Leon, as he testifies at the beginning of his work on the Decretals, a man most learned in canon law and nobly trained in scholastic philosophy, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, expansive and abundant in judgment, wrote in that faculty volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Decretals, 5 books. “To the venerable and [unclear] ...”
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, 1 book.
On the Clementines, 1 book.

He is also said to have published commentaries on the Decrees and various other treatises on different subjects, which have by no means come into our hands.

Johannes Rusbrock, prior of the regular canons of Groenendaal in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, a German by nation, a man, as they say, devout but little learned, wrote in his native tongue, for the edification of the simple, certain little works which after his death were translated into Latin by another. In some matters they are excellent, but in others they are said by doctors to be erroneous. Concerning these Johannes Gerson, chancellor of Paris, composed advisements. Of them the following are reported:

On the Adornment of Spiritual Marriage, 5 books. “Behold, the bridegroom comes; go out ...”
On the Progress of Religious, 1 book.
Exhortatory Letters, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Johannes Schodehoven, a German by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Mechelen, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote for the use of preachers of the word of God volumes not to be despised. Of these the following are extant:

A distinguished work on vices and virtues and every matter suitable for preaching, according to the order of the alphabet, in two volumes, which he entitled Polypodium, 2 books. “Many hide themselves ...”
Various Sermons, 1 book.

He also published some other works, which have by no means come into our hands.

Michael Augrianus of Bologna, an Italian by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and nobly trained in secular philosophy and canon law, whom Pope Urban VI first appointed vicar of the whole order, and who, finally elected general in the year of the Lord 1381, governed the order under obedience to Pope Urban for nine years. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which the following are reported:

To the Cardinal of Santa Maria in Trastevere, a distinguished and excellent work.
On the Conception of Saint Mary, 1 book.
On the Whole Psalter, 5 books.
On the Sentences, 4 books.
Questions on the Sentences, 1 book.
Table of the Sentences, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Luke, 1 book.
Table of the Moralia of Gregory, 1 book.
Table of the Decretum, 1 book.
Lenten Sermons, 1 book.
He began a Dictionary, 3 books.
On Valerius Maximus, 1 book.
On the Ethics of Aristotle, 1 book.
And certain other works.

Prevented by death, he did not complete this divine work of the new dictionary, but brought it only from A to C inclusive, in three volumes. He died in the last year of the emperor Wenceslaus, under Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1396, on the calends of December, in the eighth indiction, and was honorably buried before the high altar of his convent at Bologna. May he rest in the peace of Christ.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Philippus Ferrarien[sis?] Tholosanus, bishop of the church of Pax in Spain, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine scriptures and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, quick in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, formerly preaching in the kingdom of Sicily, converted many from iniquity and brought forth for God great fruits of his learning. He wrote elegant:

Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
And certain other works.

There are some who write that he was raised to the honor of the cardinalate because of his extraordinary learning and the uprightness of his life; about this I am not certain.
