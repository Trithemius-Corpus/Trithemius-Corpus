*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Letters to various persons, one book.
And many other things.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1417.

John Dominici, cardinal of the title of Saint Sixtus, commonly called Ragusan, a Florentine by birth, of the Order of Friars Preachers, who was sent by Pope Gregory XII by provision to the Council of Constance, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote certain distinguished volumes by which he made his name known to posterity. Among them the following are reported:

On Ecclesiastes, one book.
On the Song of Songs, one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, one book.
On the Magnificat, one book.
On the Epistle to the Romans, one book.
Entitled Lucula noctis, one book.
Itinerary of Devotion, one book.
On the Love of Charity, one book. “If I speak with the tongues of men”
Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Sermons through Lent, one book. “Blessed is the man who has not gone away”
And certain other things.

He died in the Council of Constance under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418, in the second indiction.

John, bishop of [Lombaria?], a German by nation, a native of Basel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and renowned for eloquence, a very celebrated deliverer of sermons, produced little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number the principal are reported:

On the Sentences, one book. Various sermons, one book.
And certain other things.

Besides this, he is said to have delivered many speeches and conferences to the clergy, and also before the supreme pontiff and the assembly of cardinals, which I have not seen.

John Zacharias, a German by nation, a native of Erfurt, a man very studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, also an excellent interpreter of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, a most acute disputant, very suitable in delivering sermons both to the clergy and to the people, wrote certain distinguished little works by which he made his name known. Among them are reported:

On the Sentences, four books. On Genesis, one book.
On Exodus, one book. On Leviticus, one book.
On the epistles of Paul, fourteen books. Various sermons, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, and in the Council of Constance was magnificently praised for disputing against the heretics, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Antonio de Butrio, an Italian by nation, a native of Bologna, easily the most celebrated jurist of all in his time, and excellently learned in other writings, both divine and human, renowned in talent and eloquence, and venerable no less for honorable conduct than for learning, published several little works in law, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among their number the following are reported:

On the Decretals, five books.
On the Clementines, one book.
Repertory of Canon Law, one book.
Repertory of Civil Law, one book.
Most elegant Counsels, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of the general Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Gabriel of Spoleto, of the Order of Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent and an outstanding deliverer of sermons, wrote some commendable little works, by which he showed also to posterity the knowledge of his lively talent. But I have not deserved to see any of them up to now. Nevertheless, the principal works of his are reported:

Against the Heretics, one book. Against the Fraticelli, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Constance and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Francesco Zabarella, a Paduan by nation, cardinal priest of the Roman Church, a man most learned in canon law, and nobly trained both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, subtle in talent and renowned for eloquence, wrote in law little works not to be scorned, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity.
