Johannes de Bromyard, of the Order of the Friars Preachers of St. Dominic, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and learned through long practice, not ignorant also of secular letters, excellent in talent and an outstanding preacher, wrote certain distinguished volumes by which he secured the memory of his name among posterity. Among these the following are reported:

The *Summa of Preachers*, one book. “The life of preachers,” etc.

The *Summa of Moral Law*, one book. “What in the following work [unclear].”

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

He is said to have written many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge.

Johannes Gluel, German by nation, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Cologne, born in Aachen, an imperial city, a man excellently learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, of excellent and distinguished talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote, among the other little works of his study, a volume not to be rejected by devotees of this religious order, on the origin and advancement of his order, which he entitled:

*Mirror of the Carmelites*, one book.

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Lenten Sermons*, one book. “Store up for yourselves.”

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

He flourished in his convent at Cologne, writing and composing various little works, where at last, failing, he passed his day and was buried in peace.

Henricus Euta, or Oyta, German by nation, the unique ornament of the University of Vienna in Austria, [unclear] from the beginning, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly learned in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, an outstanding preacher of sermons, wrote many distinguished volumes, of which very few have so far come into our hands. For I have read only:

*On the Sentences*, four books. “Concerning the prologue of the first [unclear].” “On [unclear].”

*On Contracts*, one book. “Love justice, you who [unclear].”

*On the Conception of Saint Mary*, one book. “A virgin shall arise from Israel.”

And certain sermons.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished in the University of Vienna, under Emperor Wenceslaus, together with Henricus de Hasia, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Henricus de Andernaco, German by nation, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and an outstanding interpreter of Aristotelian philosophy, excellent in talent and famous among all men of his time as a preacher of sermons, published little works of no slight authority, of which the following are reported:

A distinguished work *On the Sentences*, four books.

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

*Questions of Philosophy*, one book.

And certain other things that have by no means come to the notice of our reading.

He flourished especially in the convent at Cologne, where he is also said to have been buried.

Blaius Andernarius, French by nation, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the holy Scriptures and nobly trained in the arena of scholastic discipline, clear in talent and an outstanding disputant, wrote several distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these an illuminating and notable work excels:

*On the Sentences*, one book.

*Questions and Collations*, one book.

He is also said to have written some things in moral philosophy, but they have not come to me.

Bartholomaeus de Saliceto, the most celebrated jurist of all his time, subtle in talent and clear in eloquence, whose sayings are held in great authority among professors of civil law, published certain illuminating little works on the laws, by which he consecrated the memory of his name to immortality among posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Code*, one book.

*On the Institutes*, one book.

*On the Use of Fiefs*, one book.

And several other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.
