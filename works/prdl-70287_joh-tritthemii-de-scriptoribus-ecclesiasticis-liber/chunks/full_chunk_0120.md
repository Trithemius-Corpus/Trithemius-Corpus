*Abbot of Sponheim.*

... certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name also to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have read only the excellent work which he entitled:

*On the Prevarications of the Greeks*, 2 books. “The intolerable [unclear] of the Greeks...”
*Against the Errors of the Greeks*, 1 book.

He flourished at Constantinople in the times of Emperor Frederick I and Pope Alexander III, in the year of the Lord 1170.

John, archdeacon of a certain church of [Horit?], a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, skilled in the Greek and Latin languages, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain little works not to be despised for the edification of the faithful, by which he made his name known. Of these there exists an excellent and distinguished work, addressed to Stephen, a certain royal notary, against the Greeks:

*On the Procession of the Holy Spirit*, 1 book. “Many things in rounded Greek...”
Also various letters.

But he says that he wrote certain other things as well, which have not come into our hands.

Odefridus of Viterbo, priest, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent and distinguished, practiced in verse and prose, wrote little works of reading not to be despised, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of these there exists a large volume addressed to Pope Urban III, mixed in verse and prose, on the beginning of the world, on the whole Old and New Testament, and on the ages of the world, times, deeds, and all histories of kings, which he entitled:

*Pantheon*, 18 books. “On the beginning of the world and...”
I have seen nothing of the others.

Eckebert, the second abbot of the monastery of Saint Florin, of the order of the divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and venerable no less in conduct than in knowledge, was the brother of blessed Elizabeth the virgin, abbess of Schonau. He had formerly been a canon of the church of Bonn; called to Schonau by his holy sister, he first became a monk and then abbot. He wrote little works of reading not to be disregarded, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found the following:

*Against Heresies*, 1 book. “The prophecy long ago...”
*On the Beginning of the Gospel of John*, 1 book. “The mysteries of the divine word...”
*On the Magnificat*, 1 book. “The spirit of the Lord upon you...”
*On ‘The Angel Was Sent’*, 1 book. “The evangelical sermon for every...”
*On the Death of His Sister*, 1 book. “To virgins consecrated to God...”
*Meditations*, 1 book. “I have a word for you...”
*Sermons Through the Year*, 2 books. “There shall be signs, etc., Advent...”
*Praises of the Savior*, 1 book.
*A Book of Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.

He also published several other things, which have not come to my knowledge. For he was an excellent declaimer, learned and eloquent, so much so that he overcame in a single disputation a certain heretic whom no one could refute. He flourished in the aforesaid monastery of Saint Florin, which is called Schonau, in the diocese of Trier, under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1160; and on account of his religion and learning he was held in esteem.

Elizabeth, nun and abbess of the sisters of the monastery of Schonau, of the order of Saint Benedict, for there are two monasteries, one of monks and the other of nuns, situated at the end of the diocese of Trier, not far from the Rhine in the Hercynian Forest, sister of the aforesaid abbot Eckebert, a virgin of most holy conduct, received many revelations from God, which she committed to writing for the edification of posterity. Of these I have read the following:

*The Ways of God*, a useful work, 1 book. “It happened at the beginning...”
*On the Eleven Thousand Virgins of Cologne*, 1 book. “To you who [unclear] pious affections...”
*Of Various Revelations*, 2 books. “All who are going to read...”
*To Her Brother Eckebert*, 1 book. “You ask of me, brother...”
*A Book of Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book. “From the bishopric of Metz...”
*To Saint Hildegard*, 1 book.
And certain other things.

She died under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1165, in the fourteenth indiction, in the thirty-sixth year of her age, the twenty-fourth of her conversion, and the thirteenth of the visitation of the heavenly splendor, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of July, and was buried in the church of the monks of Saint Florin, in the middle of the choir.
