Albericus Roxiatus, otherwise de Rosate, a native of Bergamo, a distinguished professor of both laws and a very renowned interpreter, excellently learned also in the other secular letters, quick in intellect and polished in speech, produced many instructive volumes on the laws, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Code, 12 books.
On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 13 books.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Institutes, 4 books.
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, 1 book.
On the comedies of Dante, 1 book.

He also composed some other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord [1340?].

Johannes de Saxonia, of the Order of Friars Minor, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people, produced certain distinguished volumes of his intellect, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Among these there is extant:

A Summa on cases, 1 book.

And other works which I have not seen. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord [unclear].

Gerardus of Siena, Italian by nation, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man not moderately learned in the holy Scriptures and skilled in both laws by long practice, subtle and acute in intellect, whose learning his contemporary Johannes Andreas often praises and cites, wrote some excellent little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported as the more important:

On the First Book of the Sentences, 1 book.
On contracts and usury, 1 book.
On prescriptions, 1 book.

I have found nothing about the rest. He flourished with Johannes Andreas, under Louis the Bavarian, Emperor IV, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Johannes de Rupe Scissa, of the Order of Friars Minor, very zealous in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, led astray by I do not know what spirit, not to say led on, began, as though a prophet, to predict the desolation of the Catholic Church and certain other terrible things; he was cast into prison by the ministers of his order, and a lying spirit was found in his mouth. In chains he wrote to a certain cardinal:

His Revelations, 1 book. Most reverend in Christ...
Also on the Sentences, 4 books.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Gerardus, bishop of Savona, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a native of Bergamo, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in canon law, subtle in intellect, distinguished in speech, worthy no less in life than in doctrine, composed for the benefit of readers little works not to be despised; by usefully exercising his intellect in these, he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Two quodlibets, 1 book.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book. Two wings of the Cherubim...
On the Epistle to the Hebrews, 1 book.
On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, concerning religious houses, 1 book.

He says that he wrote many other little works, which have not come to my knowledge. In the Parisian school he ascended the magisterial chair. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Thomas de Garbo, a native of Florence, son of the former physician Dino, a man learned and skilled in medicine, nobly instructed in the disciplines of the philosophers, adhering to the footsteps of his most learned father, produced in his faculty little works not to be scorned by physicians, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among these there is extant, very noteworthy:

A Summa of Medicine, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, fourth Emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1340.
