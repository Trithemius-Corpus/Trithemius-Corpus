He flourished in the times of the emperor Constantine, son of Heraclius, and of his sons, in the year of the Lord 670; under them he gloriously presided over the synod of Arles.

Audoenus, also called Dado, bishop of Rouen, a Gaul by nationality, brother of Saint Ado the monk, a learned and most holy man, founder of the monastery of Resbacum, since he was learned both in the sacred Scriptures and also in secular letters, wrote in a most elegant style to Rupert, bishop of Paris:

The Life of Saint Eligius, bishop of Noyon, 3 books.

And certain other works.

He flourished in much holiness, virtues, and signs, in the times of the aforesaid emperor Constantine, in the year of the Lord 660.

Theodorus, seventh archbishop of Canterbury, consecrated by Pope Vitalian, a Greek by nationality, from Tarsus of Cilicia, a monk of our order in the city of Rome, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in his time in secular letters, very skilled in Greek and Latin speech, also excelling in verse and in prose, no less distinguished for holiness than for learning, held a synod in England and established many things for the advantage of the English church. Among the other monuments of his talent he wrote an excellent work on the character of the penance to be imposed on those who had offended in many crimes; the title of this work is:

The Penitential of Theodorus, 1 book.

Also some letters to various persons.

He died, not without a reputation for holiness, under Justinian the Younger as emperor, in the year of the Lord 690, in the third indiction, in the seventy-eighth year of his age and the twenty-second of his episcopate.

Leo, the second pope, a Sicilian by nationality, sat in the chair of Peter after Agatho for only eleven months, a man most learned in divine and secular writings, sufficiently instructed in the Greek and Latin language, subtle and eloquent, no less holy than learned. He translated from Greek into Latin the sixth synod, which in the time of Pope Agatho, his predecessor, had been celebrated in the city of Constantinople, in which Macarius, bishop of Antioch, was condemned with his accomplices, the bishops Cyrus, Sergius, Honorius, Pyrrhus, Paul, and Peter, who taught as doctrine that there was only one will and operation in our Lord Jesus Christ. He also wrote:

Many letters to various persons, 1 book. And certain similar works.
