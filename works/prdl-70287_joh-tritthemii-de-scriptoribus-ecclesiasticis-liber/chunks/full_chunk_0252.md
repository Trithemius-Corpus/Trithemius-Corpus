He also published three books of sermons, namely on the saints and on other extraordinary matters.

Carolus Bovillus Beromundius, of Samarobriva, a man adorned with rhetorical richness, poetic variety, and the most [unclear] splendor of all philosophy, and an outstanding geometer: indeed nature is believed to have brought him forth rather to investigate the divine secrets of things and the breadths and essences of all beings, than that, enclosed in a mortal body, he should lead a human life. For he was endowed with such an elevated keenness of intellect that not only did he bring into the light the natures of earthly beings, formerly covered in blind darkness, but also, with philosophy as his guide, he penetrated most deeply the star-bearing spheres and the open structure of the heavens; by the philosophical proportion of worldly things he foreshowed the ineffable trinity of the heavenly court, and exactly prefigured it by the philosophical [display?] of numbers and by the most unified investigation of earthly things. He was so wholly and especially devoted to contemplative philosophy and geometrical abstractions that often for nine hours, leaning on the trunk of a tree, with unmoving eyes and unmoving body, forgetful of himself, his own people, and human sustenance, and freed from the mass of the body, he seemed to contemplate the natures of things, so that his soul seemed rather, released from the body, to dwell in heaven than, bound by human chains, to draw vital breaths in this sensible world.

*Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition I.*

He discovered many things by the sharpness of his intellect, unseen in earlier ages. For, by means of his geometrical devices, he constructed the quadrature of the circle, which none of the philosophers before had discovered with sufficient exactness. With great wonder on the part of all, he wrote with marvellous ingenuity a book *On Nothing*, and he left to posterity very many other philosophical works which none of mortals in past ages had foreseen.

Six introductory books on geometry.
One book *On the Quadrature of the Circle*.
An introduction to the science of perspective.
A book *On the Divine, Angelic, and Human Intellect*.
A book *On Sense*. A book *On Nothing*.
The *Art of Opposites*. A book *On the Twelve Numbers*.
A book *Of the Divine Darkness*. Several philosophical letters.

Guilhelmus de Quercu, an outstanding doctor of theology, most eminent in our age in sacred eloquence; when that majesty of the French was being oppressed by the dreadful assault of the supreme pontiff, the English, and the Spanish, and by the grim preparation of wars, he alone, by his counsel and sound judgment of mind, most graciously came to the aid of the distress and necessity of the French. For he crossed the Alps and came to the city of Pisa, where by the sole sagacity of his mind he strengthened the very great assembly of cardinals and bishops, wavering over the French matter and over so great a portent of wars, and claimed for himself the more eminent roles in so great a council. Nor was he of less power in the people, when they had to undertake divine offices, by his eloquence. For he was cultivated with such gravity of judgment and knowledge of divine counsels that by his easy eloquence he set right the untamed manners of mortals and barbarous peoples, and easily led them back to the bright recognition of the supreme creator, with a kindly voice, gracious eyes, measured gesture, and pleasing comparisons.

Renatus Garnerius of Luxeuil, a doctor of both laws, most skilled in our age; because of his distinction in every kind of discipline, and then especially in the excellent faculty of laws, he spread his reputation and honor through various regions of the earth, and deservedly, in establishing and interpreting laws everywhere, easily claimed for himself the roles of Lycurgus and Socrates.
