*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Various sermons, 1 book.

He says that he wrote many other things also, but they have by no means come into our hands.

Petrus Boherius, abbot of Saint Avianus, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a jurist and an outstanding cultivator of the sacred Scriptures, subtle in intellect, renowned in speech, a most celebrated governor of monastic discipline: for the edification of his brothers he published several little works, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these I have found only:

On the Rule of Saint Benedict, 1 book.
On the Mirror of Monks, 1 book.
On the Signs of Speech, 1 book.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

Since he was a most celebrated doctor of decrees, he commented on the Rule of the holy father Benedict in a new and unusual kind of exposition, comparing all its chapters, opinions, and teachings, and even, what belongs to greater diligence, its words, counsels, and precepts, with pontifical law; with admirable subtlety he approved them by the canons of the fathers.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Jacobus de Altavilla, a German by nation, from Rinaugia, of the diocese of Mainz, a most learned professor of sacred letters, nobly instructed in secular philosophy, most subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse: he published several distinguished little works on the divine Scriptures, by which he destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have hitherto been judged worthy to see only a distinguished volume:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Also Various Questions, 1 book.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Johannes de Imenhusen, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, ready in intellect, circumspect in knowledge, scholastic in speech, a distinguished teacher no less by example than by word: he wrote certain excellent volumes, of reading not to be despised, by which, exercising his magnificent intellect, he also made known to posterity the memory of his name. Of these I have found only:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Elegant Sermons, 1 book.

Of the others I have seen nothing.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Petrus Berthorius, otherwise Bercharius, a Frenchman by nation, from Poitiers, prior of the monastery of Saint Eligius at Paris, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man most devoted to and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and outstandingly learned in secular letters; in preaching sermons to the people, a philosopher of excellent intellect, a distinguished historian and cosmographer: he wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported. To Johannes de Pratis, bishop of Palestrina and cardinal of the holy Roman Church, he wrote a great and outstanding work, which has now been printed in three volumes, not useless to the simpler preachers; they call it the Dictionary, but he himself wished it to be entitled:

Moral Repertory, 5 books.
Also Moral Reductory, 4 books.
Moral Inductory, 3 books.
Breviary of the Histories of the Bible, 1 book.
He wrote a Cosmography, 1 book.
Various Sermons, 2 books.
And many other things.

Finally, at the request of John, king of France, he translated Titus Livius into the French language. And it should be noted that of the three moral works which we have set down in the first place, each consists of three large volumes; of these, one part of the Moral Reductory, embracing morally all the historical books of the Bible, has been printed, and it begins from Genesis thus: “God in the creation of the world,” etc. His letters to various persons are reported, and small but many treatises.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1369.
