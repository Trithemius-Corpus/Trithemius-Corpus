*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...freed by [Martin?] from pain in his eyes, he came to Tours to his tomb to give thanks; and, proceeding to Poitiers, he was ordained there first priest and then bishop, and was distinguished for great learning and religion. He also wrote many useful and devout little works in elegant verse, of which I have seen only the following:

To Saint Radegund, daughter of the last king of the Thuringians, captive of the king of the Franks, later abbess of the monastery of Poitiers, in hexameter verse: *The Destruction of the Kingdom of the Thuringians*, one book.

*To [Iuininius?] on Radegund*, one book.

*To Justin the Younger*, one book.

*To Sophia Augusta*, one book.

*In the Person of Radegund*, one book.

*In Praise of Bishop Martin*, one book.

*On Leontius, the Venerable Bishop*, one book.

*In Praise of the Same*, one book.

*Hymns*, seventy-seven, one book.

*On the Miracles of Saint Martin*, four books.

*The Life of Saint Hilary of Poitiers*, [unclear] book.

*To Oporicus, on His Life*, one book.

*The Life of Saint Maurilius, Bishop*, one book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

*Poems to Friends*, one book. Incipit: “Rex regionis apex.”

He composed many other works also, but since they are brief, I did not wish to record them under the name of books. For he wrote many epitaphs and short verses, which I have seen. He flourished under Justin the Younger Augustus, in the year of the Lord 570.

Gregory, bishop of Tours, formerly a monk, once a disciple of Saint Avitus, bishop of Clermont, a learned and noble man, also gentle and humble, was of such sanctity and purity that even in life he shone with miracles. Visiting the thresholds of the apostles Peter and Paul, he was received by the blessed Pope Gregory with the greatest reverence and charity, as truly a saint, and was loved by him as a singular and unique friend. He wrote several volumes in a simple and open style, of which there are extant:

*On the Praise of the Holy Martyrs*, one book. Incipit: “Hieronymus presbyter Statueram illa tantum scrib[ere?]”

*On the Miracles of Saint Julian the Martyr*, one book. Incipit: “Magnum in nobis quo Omnibus sanctis in Chri[sto?]”

*On the Miracles of Saint Martin*, one book.

*Abbot of Sponheim.*

*History of the Franks*, ten books. Incipit: “Scripturus bella regum.”

*Epicoma of the Same*, one book.

*Chronicle of Ecclesiastical History*, one book.

The other things which he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished in the time of the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 600.

Martinus, bishop of Dumium, once also rector and abbot of a monastery, a man learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, coming from the regions of the East into Gaul, converted the people of the Sueves by teaching and miracles from Arian impiety to the right faith; he established rules of orthodox faith, founded monasteries, and reformed churches. He wrote to Mito, king of Galicia, an instructive volume on the four virtues which they call cardinal, which he wished to entitle:

*Formula of an Honorable Life*, one book. Incipit: “Gloriosissimo et tran[quillissimo?]”

*Moral Letters*, one book, and certain other things which I have not seen.

He flourished under Teudemir, king of the Sueves, while Justinian Augustus governed the empire of Constantinople, in the year of the Lord 540.

Paterius, of Rome, notary and secundicerius of the church, a man trained by constant study in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, collected all the testimonies of divine scripture by which the blessed Pope Gregory elucidated the obscure points of his exposition, and published three books: namely two on the testimonies of the Old Testament and one on the testimonies of the New Testament. He entitled this codex:

*Testimonies*, one book. Incipit: “Dum beatissimi atque apostolici.”

It exists among the Eberbach monks.

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished at Rome at the same time when Pope Gregory presided over the universal church; and at his urging he brought together the aforesaid volume, while Maurice Augustus held the empire, and afterward, with him, Phocas, in the year of the Lord 600.

Columbanus, first founder and abbot of the monastery of Luxeuil, a Scot by nation, a man of distinguished sanctity, merit, and learning, father of many thousands of monks and founder of several monasteries, an outstanding sower of the divine word, coming from Ireland into Gaul and Germany, increased divine worship, monasteries, and servants, like once the chief of the Druids. When he was still rather young, he devoted great study to sacred letters, so that in an elegant style he wrote:

*Commentary on the Whole Psalter*, one book, and certain letters to the brethren.

He died under the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 598, in the first indiction, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of December.
