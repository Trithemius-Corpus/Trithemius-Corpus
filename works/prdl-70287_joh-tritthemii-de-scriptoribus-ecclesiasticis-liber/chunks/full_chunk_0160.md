Ludolphus, of the Carthusian order, in the house of Strasbourg, a German by nation, sprung from Saxon land, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, gentle in disposition, sweet and composed in speech, and also distinguished in life and conduct, published certain excellent little works of great devotion and deep learning. By these, nobly exercising his talent, he bestowed his name upon immortality. Of these I have seen only the following:

A large and distinguished volume on the Gospel, which he entitled *The Life of the Lord Jesus*, 2 books.

William de Kaiotho, of the order of Friars Preachers, provincial prior in France, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, subtle in talent and clear in speech, of excellent ability in delivering sermons to the people, wrote certain little works not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. He abbreviated the *Summa* of Johannes Teutonicus, which is called the *Summa of Confessors*, 1 book. *What is simony, say...* [unclear]

Various sermons, 1 book.

The rest of what he composed has by no means come to my knowledge.

Peter de Casa, of Limoges, of the order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the fourteenth prior general, and afterwards bishop of Vaison, and finally, as they report, patriarch of Jerusalem, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in talent and clear in speech, learned and eloquent in delivering sermons to the people, published certain excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these are reported:

*On the Sentences*, 1 book. Sermons for the season, 1 book.

Sermons on the saints, 1 book.

And certain other works.

He flourished, and was elected prior general of his order from the convent of Limoges, under the emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1350, in the thirteenth indiction. He ruled his order energetically for twelve years.
