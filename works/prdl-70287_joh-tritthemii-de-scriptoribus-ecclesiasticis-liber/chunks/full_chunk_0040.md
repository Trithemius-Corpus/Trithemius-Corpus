*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Eutropius, monk and priest, a man of most religious manner of life, very zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, and fully instructed in secular letters, is said to have composed some little works, of which the following alone survive for us, known by their titles:

To two virgins, sisters, 2 books. And a Chronicle down to his own times, 1 book.

He flourished under the rulers Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 370. There are also some who write that he was a disciple of St. Augustine, and flourished in the year of the Lord 430.

Sychius, or perhaps more correctly Hesychius, priest and monk, once a hearer and disciple of the blessed bishop Gregory of Nazianzus, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, admonished by the example of his teacher, is said to have written many small works of explanation on the divine books; for he composed various volumes on both the New and the Old Testament, which have not yet come into our hands. I have read only that notable work of great size which he wrote to Eutychianus, the venerable deacon:

On Leviticus, 1 book. Venerable deacon ...

I have seen nothing of the rest.

He flourished under the rulers Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 400.

Ambrose, bishop of Milan, Roman by birth, a man most celebrated for faith and holiness, easily the most learned of all in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, fully instructed in the Greek and Latin tongue, whose merit the whole world proclaims, whose books holy mother Church confirms with a great commendation of authority. In expounding and explaining the divine scriptures he surpassed all teachers; no earthly power could ever break his faith, so that when many bishops were banished into exile for the faith, no one ever dared to touch Ambrose. For he was like a hammer of heretics, a most steadfast assailant, and while his tongue spoke in the faith of Christ, no human authority could ever impose silence on it. Always ready to undergo any dangers for the faith, with the Lord protecting him, he escaped everywhere free and unharmed.
