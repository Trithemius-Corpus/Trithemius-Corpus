He lives to this day at Aachen, a most distinguished interpreter of both laws, and is writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Hieronymus Balbus, a man of very celebrated reputation, and excellently learned in all the disciplines of the liberal arts, a distinguished philosopher and eloquent rhetorician, excellent in verse and prose, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, who by teaching, reading publicly, and writing earned great glory among the French and the Parisians, wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, by which he commended his name to posterity. Of these I have so far seen only a volume seasoned with great wit of eloquence, in which he responds to the slanders of a certain Guillaume Tardif, a rhetorician, in the manner of a dialogue, under the names of himself, Charles Fernand, Pierre Cohard, and the assailant himself; to it he gave the title *The Glorious Rhetorician*, one book.

He still lives in France, writing and composing various works, under Maximilian, the most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Aegidius Nettelet, dean of the church of Cambrai, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, distinguished in life and conduct, and conspicuous for singular devotion, published certain distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known. Of these there exists a very celebrated work, necessary for every estate, which with remarkable industry and very beautiful sequence he gathered into one from the epistles of St. Jerome the priest, four books.

The rest are unknown to me.

He lives to this day in the city of Cambrai, very celebrated for life and learning, and is composing various works, under Maximilian, the most distinguished king of the Romans, and the supreme pontiff Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Philippus Callimachus, Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, secretary of the king of Poland, most learned in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, eloquent orator, and notable poet, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, is said to have produced many distinguished little works, by which he left remembrance of himself to posterity. Of their number the following are reported: A notable work on the deeds of the impious king Attila, which he entitled *Attila*, one book.

*Polish History against the Turk*, one book.

*The Abbot of Spanheim*

*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

*On the Customs of the Tartars*, one book. *Love Poems in Various Metres*, one book.

And very many others.

He lives to this day with the king of Poland, in the highest esteem, and writes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1490, the twelfth indiction.

He lives to this day in Germany, composing various works, under Maximilian, the most illustrious king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Bohuslaus of Hassenstein, German by nation, a noble man and most learned in every respect, a notable orator and poet, who, contrary to the custom of the other nobles of this age, both handles arms and cultivates letters with remarkable zeal; he fosters and celebrates them, so that, like a second Pliny the Younger, even while serving as a soldier he composes books and poems. He wrote certain distinguished little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he made his name known. Of these the following exist: A distinguished work on the proper inventions of the Germans and Italians, one book.

Also *Poems*, one book.

Various letters.

And very many others.

He lives to this day in his castle Hassenstein, and writes various works, under Maximilian, the most excellent king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494, the twelfth indiction.
