*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Sebastian Murrho of Colmar, a German by nation, priest and canon in Colmar, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and most learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, orator, poet, and cosmographer, most skilled in the three languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, renowned for his knowledge of laws and canons, second to none in architecture, music, and the whole quadrivium, wrote, among other things, to Jakob Wimpheling of Sélestat, whom we mentioned above, a brief but very beautiful work:

An epitome on the praises of the Germans, 1 book.
On the works of Baptista Mantuanus, 1 book.
Several letters to various persons.
And certain other things.

He lives today in Colmar, where he is a canon, and is writing various works, which we hope will be published day by day, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Carolus Fernandus, a German by nation, born at Bruges, a town of Flanders, deprived of sight from early childhood, and for that reason utterly ignorant even of the elements of learning, displays so great a marvel of himself that, like another Homer, he has become a most celebrated poet, musician, philosopher, and orator, also a most acute interpreter of the divine Scriptures, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, most upright in life and conduct. Having been granted a public and perpetual stipend by the king of the French, he taught humane letters excellently for a long time in the very celebrated school of Paris, and gave most beautiful expositions of numerous authors. At length, having despised everything, he abandoned the world together with its stipends, and almost two years ago entered the order of our holy father Benedict, in a well-reformed monastery called Casale Benedictum, situated in a certain very dense wood, about ten miles distant from the city of Bourges. In it he perseveres to this day, burning with great zeal for souls, so much so that, ordained deacon by apostolic dispensation, he publicly preaches the word of God with the greatest fruit and admiration of all. He wrote, both in meter and in prose, many excellent volumes, by which he commended his name to posterity. On the metrical contest of Robert Gaguin concerning the most pure conception of the holy Mother of God and Virgin Mary, against Vincent de Castronovo of the Order of Preachers, a most elegant work:

Commentaries, 1 book. “We do not doubt from the Apostle...”
Praises of the Order of Carmelites, 1 book. “While the Parthian turns his weapons...”

*Concerning Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Odes in praise of Christ, 4 books.
On the conception against Vincent, 1 book.
An iambic poem on the same subject, 1 book.
An oration on the conception, to the Carthusian, 1 book.
An oration on Saint Catherine, 1 book.
Many letters, 2 books.
Almost innumerable poems, 1 book.
Elegies on contempt of the world, 1 book.
And many other things.

He lives today blind, but most learned, in the aforesaid monastery, composing many and various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494, in the [unclear] indiction.

Johannes Fernandus, brother of the aforesaid Carolus, himself a German by nation, a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher, a very celebrated orator and poet, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech. The king of the French, granting him a perpetual stipend, took him into his service as a musician. He wrote, both in meter and in prose, many useful little works suited to devotion, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of many. Of these the following are extant:

On Saint John the Baptist, 1 book. “With what fitting plectrum, what...”
Many various orations, 1 book.
Letters to various persons, 1 book.
Several poems and epigrams.
And many other things.

He also wrote the Hours of the Holy Cross and of the compassion of Saint Mary the Virgin in elegant verse, which, recently printed at Paris, are in circulation.

He lives to this day with the king of France, in the highest esteem, and composes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Hieronymus Donatus, a Venetian patrician, a very distinguished man, zealous in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a very celebrated interpreter of both languages, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech. Although he is now distracted by care for wife and children, nevertheless with wonderful diligence and incredible zeal for letters he is fervent, writing many things day by day, translating many from Greek; if another man, even released from every care, produced these, he could be regarded as a marvel. Of these there are extant:

An oration to the king of Portugal, 1 book.
