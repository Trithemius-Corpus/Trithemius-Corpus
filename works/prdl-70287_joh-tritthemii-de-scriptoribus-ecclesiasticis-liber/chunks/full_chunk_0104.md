*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

He flourished in the times of Emperor Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1060.

Leo, the ninth pope, previously bishop of the city of the Leuci, which is called Toul, under the archbishop of Trier, sat in the chair of Peter after Damasus for seven years. He had earlier been called Bruno, a learned and holy man. He wrote several distinguished short works, in which elegance of speech and sublimity of thought are deservedly commended. Among them are these:

To Emperor Constantine Monomachus, *On Extirpating Heresies*, one book.

*To the Patriarch of Constantinople*, one book.

*Against the Errors of the Greeks*, one book.

*To the Bishops of Africa and Egypt*, one book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

He died under King Henry III, the second emperor, in the year of the Lord 1054, in the seventh indiction, shining with many miracles.

Humbert, a monk of Toul, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, distinguished in intellect and eloquence, was brought with him to Rome by Pope Leo IX because of his knowledge of letters, and was ordained cardinal bishop [unclear]. Afterwards, sent by the same pope to Constantinople to refute the heresies of the Greeks, he so refuted and defeated Nicetas, a [unclear] monk, in the presence of Emperor Constantine and Michael the patriarch, that Nicetas anathematized his own book, which he had written against the church of the Latins, and with his own hand threw it into the fire to be burned. And whatever he there said or did in disputation, he wrote up clearly under the name of the Roman and the Constantinopolitan, in the manner of a dialogue, one book. He also wrote certain other works.

He flourished under Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord 1059.

Anselm, a monk of Reims, of the order of Saint Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man whose mind was practiced in the divine Scriptures, wrote certain short works of his study, which have not yet at all come into our hands. And among other things he gathered into one volume the deeds of Pope Leo IX, done throughout Gaul in councils and assemblies, in order to make known with how great authority, when he had come to Reims, he held a synod of other cities; with how great industry and subtlety he examined ecclesiastical cases; with what severity he corrected sinners; and in what way divine power cooperated with him.
