And a treatise to Antiochus, on very many necessary questions which are doubtful in the sacred writings and ought to be known by all Christians.

Theodore, bishop of Heraclea in Thrace, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and an excellent declaimer of well-composed speech, published commentaries of no contemptible reading on the sacred volumes, of which the following are reported:

On the Psalter, one book. On the Gospel of John, one book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. On the Epistles of Paul, fourteen books.

He is said also to have written some other works, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Constantine II, in the year of the Lord 340.

Eusebius, bishop of Emesa, a man most learned in the divine writings, and in declaiming homilies to the people second to none in his time, a rhetorician and theologian most celebrated in the opinion of all, is said to have written many, almost innumerable, little works; but we have been able to find only the titles and numbers of a few, namely:

Against the Gentiles, one book. Of many homilies, one book.
Against the Jews, one book. Sermons to the people.

He flourished while Constantine and his brothers were reigning, in the year of the Lord 550.

Triphyllius, bishop of Ledra or Leucotheon in Cyprus, a man most learned both in divine and in secular writings, and easily the most eloquent of all prelates in his age, is said to have written not a few volumes and to have expounded the divine writings in many ways; but I do not recall that anything of his works has come into our hands. His exposition is reported:

On the Song of Songs, one book.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Constantius, in the year of the Lord 340.

Serapion, formerly a monk, bishop of Thmuis, and a very close friend of the blessed abbot Antony, a man of such great learning and eloquence that, on account of the excellence of his talent, he obtained the surname Scholasticus, is said to have written many little works, of which the following are extant:

Against the Manichaean, one book. On the Titles of the Psalms, one book.
Several letters to various persons. He flourished under the prince Constantius, in the year of the Lord 540.

Acacius, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, a man indeed practiced in the divine Scriptures, but corrupted by Arian impiety, was held in such reverence by the emperor Constantius that he obtained the appointment of Felix, an Arian presbyter, as bishop at Rome in the place of Pope Liberius. This Acacius, because he was one-eyed, was called Monoculus. He wrote many varied treatises, which would truly have been useful if they had had a Catholic author. Of these, the more important are reported to be those which we set forth below:

On Ecclesiastes, seventeen books. Sethematon, sixteen books.
Symicton, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Constantine, in the year of the Lord 350.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Aquitaine, a man very venerable in morals, life, and doctrine, endured many wrongs from the Arians for the faith of Christ. For, through the faction of Saturninus, bishop of Arles, he was banished from the synod of Beziers into Phrygia, and for four years bore a hard exile with steadfast spirit; there, by his prayers, he is read to have called a dead man back to life. Since he was a man most learned in every respect, he composed many little works, of which the following are reported in number:

Against Dioscorus, one book. Against Valens and Ursatius, one book.
On the Holy Trinity against the Arians, twelve books. “As I look about me”
A Defense of the Catholic Faith, one book. “God, who is everywhere”
Against the Arians, one book. “To the most beloved brothers”
On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. “The step which Matthew”
On the Synods, to the bishops of the Gauls, one book. “I had resolved”
On some Psalms, forty-two treatises.
On the Psalm “Blessed are the undefiled,” twenty-two treatises.
To the emperor Constantius, fifteen books. “The time for speaking now”
Of Hymns, one book.
Of Mysteries, one book. On the Book of Job, one book.
Against Auxentius, one book. “To the most blessed and glorious”
On Heresies, one book. On the Song of Songs, one book.
Chronicles, one book. Letters to various persons, one book.

He also published some other works which have not come into our hands. At length, after returning to his city from his exile, he died under the emperor Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 371, in the fourteenth indiction; his feast is celebrated on the Ides of January.
