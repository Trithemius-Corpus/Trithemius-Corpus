*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Victorinus, an Aquitanian by nation, a man very devoted to the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, is said to have left certain little works of his own genius, which have not yet at all come to my knowledge. At the command of Pope Hilarius he wrote, for finding the date of Easter, *The Great Cycle* of 532 years, in four books.

This is called the great cycle of 532 years; when these have run their course, all the computations of the heavenly bodies return to the same point. Dionysius the abbot afterwards corrected it, and down to this our own age it has endured, although in some matters it has failed. This Victorinus also wrote certain letters.

He flourished under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460, and wrote his cycle under Pope Hilarius, in the third year of his pontificate.

Peter, priest of the church of Edessa, learned and practiced in the holy Scriptures, endowed with an outstanding power and talent for declaiming homilies to the people, so that in his own times he had no equal, wrote in the Syriac language many very distinguished volumes, of which only very few have come to my knowledge:

Commentaries on the Psalms, four books.

He also played in meter on the Psalms, five books.

He dictated, and delivered, almost countless homilies to the people, which are found among us rarely. But neither is the number of his treatises certain to us.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 300.

S[unclear], learned in [unclear] and sufficiently trained in secular letters, subtle and keen in intellect, often fought against the force of heretics, both by writing and by disputing. Hence, at the request of a certain Secunda, a virgin dedicated to Christ, he wrote a distinguished and memorable volume:

Against Marcion and Valentinus, one book.

Against Eunomius and Aetius, six books.

He published some other things as well, but they have not yet come to my knowledge.

He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, around the year of the Lord 440.
