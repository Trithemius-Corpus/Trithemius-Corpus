298 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Antonius of Genoa, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, and a professor and very celebrated interpreter of canon law, outstanding in ability and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain small works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with praise to posterity. But I have not had the privilege of seeing any of them, except that which is entitled: *Compendium of Moral Figures*, one book. The rest have not come to me. “To religious men [in Christ?]...”

He flourished in person at the general Council of Constance, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418.

Johannes de Mola, by homeland a Bolognese, a very celebrated doctor of both laws, excellent in ability and distinguished in eloquence, whose learning and eloquence in his time splendidly illuminated all Italy, is said to have published many distinguished volumes in both laws, whose authority is extolled with the highest praises among the professors of this faculty. Among these the following are reported first:

*On the Three Books of the Decretals*, one book.

*On the Sixth Book of the Decretals*, one book.

*On the ff. Infortiatum*, fourteen books.

*On the ff. Novum*: “Before we come to...”

And certain others.

He died at Bologna in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1435, indiction 14.

Antonius of Parma, prior general of the Camaldolese Order, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, excellent in ability, composed in speech, sufficiently fit in delivering sermons to the people, wrote for the direction of preachers of the word of God several distinguished small works, by which he made his name known. Of these the chief are reported:

*Sermons on the Season*, one book. “When he had drawn near...”

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.

*Sermons through Lent*, one book.

And certain others.

He was present in person at the Council of Constance, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.
