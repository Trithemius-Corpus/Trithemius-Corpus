340 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

John, abbot of the monastery of Niuicella, devoted to and learned in the sacred Scriptures, nobly trained in canon law, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, wrote many different little works of his own, by which he transmitted the memory of his most celebrated name to posterity with glory. Of these the following are extant: an illustrious and distinguished work entitled *The Concordance of the Bible of the Canons*, one book: “In the beginning God created.”

*Sermons on the Temporale*, one book: “Look, lift up [your] head.”

*Sermons on the Saints*, one book: “Having left the nets, [unclear] Peter.”

*Letters, Various Questions*.

The rest which he composed have not come to the notice of our reading.

Jacob of Gruytrode, a German by nation, of the order of the Carthusians, prior of the house of All the Apostles near Liège, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in the conduct of his life, excellent in intellect, sweet in eloquence, wrote certain distinguished devout little works, the reading of which is not deemed unprofitable for religious persons, lovers of their own salvation. Of these the following are reported:

*On True Virtues*, three books.

*On the Four Last Things*, one book.

*On the Life of the Lord Jesus*, one book.

*On Preparation before Mass*, one book.

*On the Definition of the Name Monk*, one book.

*Meditations on the Passion of the Lord*, one book.

*A Lectionary for the Table*, one book.

*Mirror of Prelates*, one book: “Eat, friends, [unclear].”

*Mirror of Priests*, one book.

*Mirror of Old Age*, one book.

*Colloquy of Jesus and the Monk*, one book.

*Colloquy of Jesus and the Prelate*, one book.

*Colloquy of Jesus and the Old Man*, one book: “Do not be indignant, I pray.”

*Colloquy of Jesus and the Hermit*, one book.

*Colloquy of Jesus and the Disciple*, one book.

He died most devoutly under Emperor Frederick and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1472, in the fifth indiction, on the day before the Ides of February.

A man devoted to and learned in the sacred Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, ready and circumspect in counsel, and distinguished by experience in many matters, wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory. Of these there is extant the very elegant work *On the Deeds of the Supreme Pontiffs*, one book.

And certain other things.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1477, in the ninth indiction.

William Forleon, a Breton by nation, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently trained in secular philosophy, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, who in the Parisian school, by teaching, by disputing, and also by writing, won great praise for his learning, published certain distinguished little works on the Scriptures. Of these there is extant the very celebrated work *On the Sentences*, one book; *Various Questions*, one book.

A hearer and disciple of his was once that most learned man, and easily the foremost of the theologians of our age, Master Stephen called Brulefer, a Frenchman by nation, of the same order, who taught theology in the Paris convent; afterwards betaking himself to the observance of the order at Mainz in Germany, he there began to lecture on the *Sentences*, which he now continues with praise in the convent at Metz. This William flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.
