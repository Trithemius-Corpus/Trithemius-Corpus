*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

If he wrote anything else, I do not know. He died under Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord 1045, in the thirteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Kalends of June.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Cassino, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and notably learned in secular letters, zealous for regular discipline and a strenuous governor, so that for the sheep entrusted to him, by word and example, [unclear], wrote beautiful and elegant

Sermons, one book.

He published also some other things, which have not yet come to our knowledge.

Guido, monk and abbot of the monastery of the Cross of Saint Leufredus, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, by homeland an Aretine, a man practiced in the divine writings and most learned in secular disciplines, a notable dialectician and musician, to be preferred to others also in this, that through his rule on their hand even boys and girls more easily learn unknown chants by themselves than through the voice of a teacher or by the use of some instrument, wrote, among other little works of his talent, against Berengar, cleric of the church of Tours,

On the Body and Blood of the Lord, two books.

Also On Music, [unclear]. And other things which I have not seen.

He flourished under Emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1030.

Egelnoth, archbishop of Canterbury, learned and not ignorant of secular letters, most holy in life and conduct, a most merciful advocate of the poor, at whose urging Cnut, king of England, made many alms, especially to the church of Chartres, over which at that time the most learned man Fulbert, bishop, presided, of whom we made mention above, is said to have written, in honor of almighty God, a beautiful work,

In Praise of Saint Mary; Letters to Various Persons, one book.

And certain other things.

Clarmannus, a grammarian, from a cleric of Liege made bishop in the times of Emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1030.

A Brescian, once a hearer of Fulbert, bishop of Chartres, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, a philosopher and dialectician famous in his time, excellent in verse and prose, wrote in both styles certain commendable little works, of which few have come to the knowledge of our reading. He wrote to Berengar of Tours, formerly his fellow student under Fulbert, then beginning to err and to dogmatize perversely, correcting him at great length in a friendly way,

On the Body and Blood of the Lord, one book. I call it a collection.

Letters to Various Persons, one book.

Moreover he says that he wrote many other things both in verse and in prose, which I have not seen.

He flourished under King Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1040.

Hermann, who was called Contractus, monk of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, son of the count of Veringen in Swabia, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, most universally learned in secular letters, a philosopher, poet, astronomer, rhetorician, and musician, second to none in his time, most skilled in three languages, Greek, Latin, and Arabic, wrote in verse and prose many distinguished volumes, of which I have found only those set down below, namely:

On Music, one book. In considering the single...

On the Monochord, one book. The first is of this world...

On the Composition of the Astrolabe, three books. Therefore in measuring below...

On the Usefulness of the Astrolabe, one book. Whoever of astronomy...

On Eclipses, one book.

On Computation, one book.

On the Squaring of the Circle, one book.

On the Conflict of Rithmomachy, one book. He who is skilled in arithmetic...

On Physiognomy, one book. Of a clear-sighted prudent man...

Lives of Several Saints, one book.

Various hymns and proses.

He also composed, in sweet regular melody, most beautiful chants and proses, among which is that very sweet one concerning the blessed Virgin Mary, Salve regina, and Alma redemptoris mater, and the others of which there is an abundant number. He also translated into Latin not a few volumes of Greek philosophers and Arab astrologers. In his limbs he was, in accordance with his name, contracted, but he always persevered in thanksgiving; whence through the blessed Virgin, Mother of God, he merited to be inwardly wonderfully consoled and illuminated. He flourished in the times of Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord 1050.
