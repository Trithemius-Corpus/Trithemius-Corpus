John Tacephale, an Englishman by nation, of the Order of the Brethren of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, a doctor learned in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, composed in speech, the most celebrated preacher of sermons of all in his time, produced little works not to be despised in reading; by them, usefully exercising his talent, he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in his convent at Norwich, where, buried, he rests in peace.

Robert Holkoth, an Englishman by nation, of the Order of Preachers, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and also an excellent preacher of sermons, wrote many excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books. “Whether every wayfarer...”
On the Book of Wisdom, 1 book. “The Lord is my rock...”
On the Seven Mortal Sins, 1 book. “This is the way; walk...”
Moralizations, 1 book. “Theodosius concerning the life...”
On the Immortality of the Soul, 1 book.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book.
On Ecclesiasticus, unfinished, 1 book.
And many other things.

He died of the plague after he had begun a reading on the Book of Ecclesiasticus, under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1349, in the second indiction.

Nicholas Dorhin, an Englishman by nation, of the Order of the Brethren of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine scriptures and excellently taught in secular philosophy, who in the University of Oxford, by reading and disputing, earned great praises from all, wrote little works not to be scorned in reading, by which he showed the rich, all-round grace of his very lively intellect. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, [unclear], 4 books.
Determinations of Questions, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the school of Oxford, and wrote many various volumes.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Paul of Perugia, professor of both laws and a very celebrated interpreter, and excellently learned in other humane letters, perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, was for a long time keeper and master of the library of Robert, king of Sicily, a most learned and studious prince. He wrote certain excellent little works by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there is extant a distinguished volume, in which there are many pleasant and useful things concerning the gods of the gentiles and of others, which he entitled:

Collections, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Bonominus, by nation a [Permonensian?], born from the town of Clusone, a learned physician and easily the most skilled of all in his time, and not moderately learned in the other disciplines of philosophy, most experienced in healing human bodies, wrote in that faculty little works not to be scorned, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. But I do not remember having seen any of them. The following, however, are said to be his:

On Poisons, 1 book.
Of Various Questions, 1 book.
And certain other things.

Tilmannus of Aachen, a German by nation, of the Order of the Brethren of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, distinguished professor of sacred letters, outstanding in intellect and an excellent preacher, and also sufficiently learned in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, wrote, for the benefit of readers, compilations not to be despised, by which he sent the fame of his name to distant places. Of these there is reported one notably remarkable reading, which he read publicly at Cologne:

On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
Also on the Sentences, 4 books.

He is also said to have composed sermons and various works on the Bible; since I am uncertain about them, I can make no judgment.
