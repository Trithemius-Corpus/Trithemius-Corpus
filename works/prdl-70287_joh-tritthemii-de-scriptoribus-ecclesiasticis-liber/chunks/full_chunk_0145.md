Peter de Bella Parthica, otherwise Pertica, a Burgundian by nation, a man most skilled in both civil and canon law, subtle and acute in intellect, and very ready in disputing and resolving doubtful questions of law, wrote many excellent volumes on the laws, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

On the Code, 9 books.
On the Institutes, 4 books.
On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
One Hundred Disputations, 1 book.
Several Opinions.
And certain other things.

He flourished at the same time as Dinus Mugelanus, under Emperor Albert and Pope Boniface VIII, in the year of the Lord 1300.

[Rainerus?] of Pisa, otherwise Rainerius, an Italian by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and sufficiently skilled in canon law, quick in intellect, scholastic in speech, cautious in counsel, venerable no less for religion than for learning, in order to provide a path in hearing confessions for his brothers who were less experienced in law, wrote a great and distinguished *Summa* on cases, in two large volumes according to alphabetical order, which he entitled:

*Pantheologia*, 2 books. 'Absolution. Concerning abso[unclear]'
And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Gerard, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a German by nation, lector of the convent of Liege, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, an outstanding declaimer in making sermons to the people, distinguished in intellect and adorned with scholastic eloquence, wrote a very useful treatise for devout religious persons, teaching the seven dispositions to be made concerning the heart, and sagaciously gathering from the divine Scriptures on every side many things pertaining to edification, which he entitled:

*On the Teaching of the Heart*, 1 book. 'Prepare your hearts.'
Sermons for the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.

In addition he published certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

John of San Gimignano, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man studious and especially learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in intellect and clear in speech, of excellent industry in declaiming sermons to the people, acquired great praise and fruit of doctrine in the salvation of souls. He wrote, among other things:

On the Similitudes of Things, 1 book.
Sermons for the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
Various Conferences, 1 book.

In addition he is said to have written certain other things, which have not come to me.

Peter of Dacia, a philosopher, calculator, and astronomer, the most excellent and celebrated among all men of his time, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures and of the Greek language, is said to have written many little works in astronomy and the art of calculation, which are judged not useless for ecclesiastical computation, by which he destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. But few of them have come to my knowledge.

On Calculation or Computation, 1 book.
Also a Calendar, 1 book.
And of his tables, 1 book.

The rest, although there are many things that he composed, have nevertheless not come to me.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.
