*Abbot of Spanheim.*

Non-useless sermons, one book.
Visions of a certain nun, one book.
On the life of Saint Lutgardis, one book.
But other things which he composed have not yet come at all to my knowledge.

Franciscus Accursius, Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, son of the most learned man Accursius, himself a man excellently learned in civil law and much instructed in the other disciplines of secular philosophy, whose works survive with great authority among the professors of this faculty, made his own times illustrious by teaching and writing, and gave his name to immortality. Of his little works I have as yet been able to see nothing. Various works of his are reported:

Disputations also, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Martinus de Fano, a professor and interpreter of both laws, most celebrated among all in his own time, the teacher of Johannes Andreae of Bologna, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence, and most acute in pleading legal cases, wrote distinguished volumes in his faculty, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have found only:

On actions, one book. “I indeed, Martinus...”
On judgment and cause, one book. “Since very many principal...”

Of the others I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Albertus Patavinus, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, once a hearer and disciple of Aegidius Romanus, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, and by his preaching converted many from iniquity to the path of justice. He wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he made himself useful to those then present and memorable to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On Genesis, one book. On Exodus, one book.
On Leviticus, one book. On Numbers, one book.
On Deuteronomy, one book. On the Gospel, one book.
On the Epistles of Paul, one book. Sermons for the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Sermons throughout Lent, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Dinus de Garbo, Florentine by homeland, a most skilled doctor of the art of medicine, and nobly instructed in the institutions of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and renowned in eloquence, published in his faculty little works not to be despised by students, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these are reported:

Commentaries on Galen.
On the malice of varied complexion, one book.
On Hippocrates, On the nature of the fetus, one book.
A book of various questions, one book.
On Avicenna also, one book.
Various things also in philosophy.
And certain other things.

His father Brunus was a physician most skilled in the art of surgery; he entrusted this Dinus to the Florentine physician Thadaeus to be instructed in that science.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Guilielmus de Cumo, French by nation, a most learned interpreter of civil law, subtle in intellect and renowned in eloquence, who in that faculty had few more learned than himself in his own time, magnificently adorned the school of Orleans by teaching and writing. He published little works not to be scorned by professors of the laws, by which he made his name known to posterity:

On the Old Digest, 24 books. On the Code also, 9 books.
And various other things.

He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Jacobus de Viterbio, archbishop of Naples, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence, and no less venerable in conduct than in knowledge, wrote little works not to be despised for the edification of readers, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity with glory. I have as yet deserved to see none of these. But the following work of his addressed to Pope Clement V is reported:

On the government of Christians, one book. On the Sentences, 4 books.
Quodlibets also, one book.
And some other things.

He flourished in the times of Henry of Luxembourg, the seventh emperor, and of Pope Clement V, in the year of the Lord 1316.
