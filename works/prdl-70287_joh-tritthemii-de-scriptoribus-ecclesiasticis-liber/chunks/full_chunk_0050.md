*ABBOT OF SPONHEIM.* [running head]

…held [silence] until death, that the sin which he had contracted by speaking he might wholesomely amend by keeping silence. He wrote little works of no contemptible reading, of which the following are reported:
On the life of St Martin the bishop: 3 books. ["Very many of mortals…"]
The Conference of Postumianus and Gallus: 4 books. ["When into one place I…"]
Of letters to [his] Sister: 1 book. Of letters to various [persons]: 1 book.
To Paulinus, bishop of Nola: 2 books.
He is said to have written also some others, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished with Saint Martin, archbishop of Tours, under the princes Arcadius [and] Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Ausonius, bishop of Bordeaux, a man most erudite in the divine Scriptures, second to none of his time in secular letters, excelling in verse [and] prose, shone forth renowned no less for sanctity than for erudition; who at Trier, in a council under the prince Maximus, together with the saints Martin, Ambrose of Milan, [and] Jerome the presbyter, did many illustrious things. He wrote there, among other metrical [works], a distinguished little work, which is entitled:
The Moselle: 1 book. On the praise of the same homeland: 1 book.
But he is reported to have written many other little works, which have by no means come to us. He flourished under Maximus, in the year of the Lord 410.

Augustine, bishop of Hippo, a Carthaginian by nation, in Africa, a man most erudite of all the learned, both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, eloquent in speech, renowned in eloquence, profound in sense, most holy in life [and] conversation; he wrote so many little works that I should believe no one able either to read or to have them all. Whence without doubt he is convicted of lying who confesses that he has found the whole of Augustine. For he precedes all writers in the number [and] usefulness of [his] books, who composed more than a thousand distinguished treatises. No one so profoundly explained sacred Scripture as Augustine. No one so usefully instituted Christian morals, nor did anyone defend the catholic faith more ardently than he. The king led [him] into the wine-cellar, and clothed [him] with such fortitude that he strongly laid low all the adversaries of the faith with the sword of his mouth. If you have him as a teacher, even with the rest lacking, he himself suffices you. Now therefore, of the many books which he published, let us mark down even a few:
On the Holy Trinity: 15 books. ["[You who are] about to read these [things]…"]
On the City of God: 22 books. ["The most glorious city…"]
On the predestination of the saints: 2 books. ["[That] the Apostle indeed said…"]
