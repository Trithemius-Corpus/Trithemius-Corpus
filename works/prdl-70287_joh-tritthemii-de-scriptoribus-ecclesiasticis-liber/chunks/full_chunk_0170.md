*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

On the sensible delights of paradise, one book, beginning: “In the delights of the paradise of God.”
He published Sermons, one book.
And certain other works.
He flourished under the emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord [1360].

Leonard of [Giffoni?], of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, the twenty-fourth minister general, and afterward cardinal priest of the Roman Church, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and very skilled in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and renowned in eloquence, is said to have left many monuments of his intellect, of which I have found very few:

On the Sentences, four books.
On the Song of Songs, one book.
A Notable Summa, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370.

John Balistarii, a Catalan by birth, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, the seventeenth prior general, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, who governed his order sternly in deed and speech for sixteen years, composed distinguished monuments of his intellect, among which are reported:

On the Sentences, one book.
On the War of the Church Militant, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
And certain other works.

The aforementioned notable work on the mighty war of the Church militant and of Antichrist attacking it he sent dedicated to Pope Gregory XI.

He died in the times of the emperor Charles IV and Pope Gregory XI, in the year of the Lord [1374], in the second indiction, in his convent of Majorca.

John Boccaccio of Certaldo, Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, easily the most learned of all men of his time in secular literature and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated poet, philosopher, and astronomer, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, published many distinguished volumes, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following are reported. To Robert, king of Sicily:

On the Genealogy of the Gods, one book, beginning: “If sufficiently from the things related, Lord...”
On Illustrious Men, one book, beginning: “When I was inquiring...”
On Famous Women, one book, beginning: “On the day before, of eminent women...”
On the Heresies of the Bohemians, one book.
On the Victories of Sigismund, one book.
On the Wars of the Emperors, one book.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

On Mountains, one book, beginning: “I had arisen, indeed weary...”
On Springs, one book, beginning: “After concerning mountains and...”
On Rivers, one book, beginning: “By poetic invention...”
On the Wars of the Florentines, one book.
On the Rebellion of the Lands of the Church, one book.
On the Victory of the Tartars, one book.
On Pools and Marshes, one book, beginning: “Although sometimes from pools...”
On the Names of the Sea, one book, beginning: “A modest amount of labor having been undertaken...”
Of Bucolics, one book, beginning: “Tyndarus, it was not enough...”
On the Capture of Constantinople, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
And many other works.

In the vernacular language, moreover, many books of his are extant, full of jests and vanities, such as the book of the Hundred Novels, Corbaccio, and Philostratus.

He died under the emperor Charles IV and Pope Gregory XI, in the year of the Lord 1375, in the thirteenth indiction, at the age of 62.

John of Hildesheim, German by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, learned in the sacred Scriptures, nobly taught in secular literature, a theologian, philosopher, and distinguished poet, was of excellent intellect in declaiming sermons to the people. He was formerly at Avignon the familiar and devoted servant of Blessed Peter Thomas, the general and most holy man of whom we made mention above. He wrote many distinguished little works in verse and prose, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among these the following are reported. To Florentius, bishop of Münster, he published a lengthy volume:

On the Three Holy Kings, one book, beginning: “When of the most venerable...”
A Chronicle of Histories, one book.
A Defense of His Order, one book.
On Monsters in the Church, one book.
On the Fountain of Life, one book.
On Antichrist, one book.
Against the Jews, one book.
Against One Painting Shameful Things, one book.
Eighty Letters, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1370.
