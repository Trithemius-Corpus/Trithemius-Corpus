*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

A venerable man, he wrote several little works of no negligible usefulness, by which his name became known to posterity: To Hermann, archbishop of Cologne, *The Life of Saint Lambert*, one book. To Rupert, bishop of Metz, one book. And certain other things.

He also wrote a chant on the Holy Trinity from Alcuin, which we use in the octave of Pentecost; and on the discovery of St Stephen; and a nocturnal chant on St Lambert, bishop and martyr, all of which, together with certain others, he composed in an authentic and sweet melody.

He flourished in the times of Louis, son of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 910.

Radulphus of Flavigny, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and in all humane letters the most learned of his time, subtle in talent, profound in understanding, sweet in speech, ornate and eloquent, acute and very outstanding in expounding the sacred books, wrote two great and distinguished volumes:

*On Leviticus*, twenty books. “When at some time a discourse concerning Judah...” These, never sufficiently praised, are available at Cologne from Quentel.

*On the Epistles of Paul*, fourteen books.

*A History of the Franks*, one book.

Also a short *Chronicle*, one book.

In addition he published many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

There are some who write that he was a monk of the monastery of Fulda in Germany.

Smaragdus, abbot of the monastery of St Michael, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and exercised in constant study of them, a driver of regular discipline, made his name known far and wide by expounding the holy Scriptures, and acquired a great reputation for knowledge and for his manner of life. For he wrote many distinguished little works, of which I have found only the following:

*On the Rule of St Benedict*, one book. “Since the disturbances of very many...” *The Diadem of Monks*, one book. “This little book...” *On the Whole Psalter*, one book. *On the Epistles and Gospels through the Year*, two books.

Also several sermons, one book.

Concerning his other little works, however, I have been able to find nothing anywhere. It is said, however, that he published many commentarial treatises on the divine Scriptures. But he also expounded the *Donatus Major*, and explained many authors of secular literature.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Widukind, a monk of the monastery of Corvey in Saxony, of the Order of St Benedict, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, ready in talent and clear in speech, excellent in verse and prose, wrote in alternating style many distinguished little works, of which I have so far found only the following. From the beginning of the Saxons down to the death of Otto, the first emperor, he wrote to Mechthild, his daughter,

*A History of the Saxons*, five books.

*The Passion of the Virgin Thecla*, in verse, one book.

*The Life of Paul, the First Hermit*, one book.

*The Deeds of Otto the First* “With virginal flower...” [unclear]

He flourished under Otto, the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 950.

Eutrandus, deacon of the church of Pavia, an Italian by nation, originally from the city of Pavia, formerly scribe and secretary of Berengar, king of Italy, a man very learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, keen and forceful in talent, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, and no less excellent in verse than in prose, is reported to have written some distinguished little works, by which he made his name known. Driven from Italy by King Berengar, he came into Germany; and while staying at Frankfurt, he wrote a notable work containing the history of his own time and the tyranny of that same Berengar, which, as if in return for the injuries received from him, he entitled *Antapodosis*, that is, *Retribution*, seven books. “When there were reigning, indeed...” [unclear] I have seen nothing of the others.

He wrote the aforesaid volume to Regimundus, bishop of the Spanish church of Elvira, in which he sometimes uses verse, sometimes Greek speech, interspersing many things concerning the deeds of the Germans.

He flourished under Otto, the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 950.

Abbo, abbot of the monastery of Fleury, of the Order of St Benedict, a man exercised in the divine Scriptures by continual reading and study, and excellently learned in secular disciplines, also distinguished in life and conversion, and proved worthy even to the crown of martyrdom, while he was preaching the word of God in Gascony, became a witness of the Christian faith by the shedding of blood. He wrote *On the Computus of Victorius*, one book. *Additions to the Same*, one book. And certain other things.
