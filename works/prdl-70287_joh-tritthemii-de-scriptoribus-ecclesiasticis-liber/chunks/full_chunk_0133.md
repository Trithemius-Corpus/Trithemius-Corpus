*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Albertus, bishop of Regensburg, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a German by nation, a Swabian by homeland, originating from Lauingen, a man very studious and learned in the divine scriptures, and in secular philosophy second to none in his time; subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, devout and most holy in life and conduct. Because of his teaching and learning he was called Magnus. He taught sacred letters and Aristotelian philosophy for many years, first at Paris, later at Cologne, where among others he had Thomas Aquinas as an excellent pupil. Then, having been assumed to the episcopate of the church of Regensburg, after he had governed it for some years, he left it, and out of love for saving study returned to his convent at Cologne, where he continued teaching and writing until the end of his life.
