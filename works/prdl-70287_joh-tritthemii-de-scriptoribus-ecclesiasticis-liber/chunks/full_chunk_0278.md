*To the Ecclesiastical [Writers], Addition II.*

On grace and free will, book 1. On the harmony of free will. On the captivity and redemption of the human race, 4 books. On ecclesiastical writings. He flourished in our century under Charles V, around the year of the Lord 1550.

John Eck, a Swabian, German by nation, doctor and sincere and indefatigable defender of theology, died recently after writing and publishing many things in Latin and German, among which are these:

Homilies throughout the whole year, on the temporal cycle and the saints.

A commentary on Haggai.

On the primacy of Peter, or of the supreme pontiff, against Luther.

A letter on the method of his studies.

On the sacrifice of the Mass, to Sigismund, king of Poland, 2 books.

The same, on the sacrifice of the Mass. A handbook of commonplaces against Luther.

Chrysopassus, or On Predestination, 6 centuries.

He also translated the Bible into the German language.

He also wrote many letters to various persons.

Reply.

An apology by the same against the filth of Bucer.

He is said to have written certain other innumerable works, a man worthy of immortality, and one who would have published several more, had not jealous death denied him to us. He died in the year of the Lord 1543.

John Fabri, German by nation, bishop of Vienna, a very learned man, who died a few years ago, wrote many excellent works, of which this is the catalogue:

Commentaries on the Institutes.

On books 1 and 2 of the Code.

On faith and works, book 1.

Against Doctor Balthasar Pacimontanus, the first author of the Anabaptists of our age, a defense of the orthodox Catholic faith.

A little book on the victory won among the Swiss.

A refutation of the six articles of Huldrych Zwingli.

A German letter to Huldrych Zwingli, book 1.

On the Eucharist. The Hammer of Heretics.

Sermons on the Eucharist, Victory against the Turks, Patience, and the Baptism of Little Children.

*In the Abbot of Sponheim, On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

A funeral oration by the same in praise of Lady Margaret, duchess of Austria, etc.

He composed very many other works, which were consigned to Vulcan by the heretics. He flourished, if I am not mistaken, in the year of the Lord 1536.

John Fisher, bishop of Rochester in England, a very learned man and a most keen champion of the Catholic faith, endowed also with the crown of martyrdom under Henry VIII, king of the English, and made famous because of his constant profession of the Catholic faith, wrote against Oecolampadius for the truth of the body and blood of Christ in the Eucharist, and against Luther for the Mass, and against Jacques Lefevre d'Etaples on the single Mary Magdalene.

And many other works, although not yet brought to light because of the savagery of the storms of the present age. He flourished in the year of Christ's birth 1535.

John Hoffmeister, a Swabian by homeland, of the institute of Saint Augustine, and a well-deserving provincial of the same order, commended himself to our age by innumerable little works. From these the following are listed:

A dialogue against the followers of the Lutheran gospel.

On the sacrifice of the Mass, book 1.

Commentaries on two epistles of Paul addressed to the Corinthians.

Expositions on Paul's Epistle to the Philippians.

Commentaries on Matthew.

Exegeses on Tobias.

Three sermons, in German.

And certain other works, which, if those above prolong his life, he will publish for the benefit of students. He is still living, a man worthy of ambrosia and nectar, in the year of Christ 1546.

John Ulrich of Basel, of the Order of Preachers, a man whom you would call learning itself and sincerity of faith, while still a young man acquired such knowledge of Greek expression as scarcely one or another of his age possessed.

He detected innumerable errors in the translation of Theophylact.

He also wrote Greek commentaries on Paul's Epistle to the Hebrews.

He died at Colmar in the year of salvation 1536, a young man.
