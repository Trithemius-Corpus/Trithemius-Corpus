124 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

He flourished in the times of the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 830.

Remigius, a monk of Auxerre, of the Order of St. Benedict, and afterwards, as they say, bishop of that same city, a man most learned in divine and human writings, a distinguished writer in verse and prose, teacher of St. Odo, first abbot of Cluny, renowned for life and doctrine, published many volumes in both styles, of which the following are reported:

On the Song of Songs, one book.
On the whole Psalter, one book. “Divine prophecy [unclear].”
On the Canon of the Mass, one book.
On the divine offices, one book.
On the festivals of the saints, one book.
To the bishop of the Aedui, one book.
Also several sermons, one book.

In addition he composed other works, both in sacred literature and also in secular writings, expounding Donatus and the ancient authors, which are possessed by many. He flourished under the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 880.

Constantinus, a monk of the monastery of Monte Cassino, of the Order of St. Benedict, an African by nation, a man very devoted to the divine Scriptures, and most learned in secular letters, especially in philosophy and medicine, fully trained in Greek, Latin, and Arabic eloquence, wrote many small works in medicine, which are held in esteem among physicians. He also translated many volumes of ancient physicians from Greek and Arabic into Latin, which he prefaced with his own prefaces and letters. Of his small works I have seen these:

Universal Diets, one book. “What first compelled [unclear].”
Particular Diets, one book. “We have completed in the first book [unclear].”
On Urines also, one book.
Medicinal Weights [unclear].

As for the rest of his volumes, of which he both wrote and translated many, I do not recall that any has come into our hands. They are better known to physicians.

Ambrosius Ansbertus, a monk and priest of the Order of St. Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine writings, of agreeable talent and sweet in speech, published many commentaries on the sacred volumes, by which he transmitted the fame of his name to later generations. Yet few have come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following:

On the Song of Songs, one book. “The gift of wisdom with [unclear].”
On the Apocalypse of John, ten books.
On Cupidity, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 890.
