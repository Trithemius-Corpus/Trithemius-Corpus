Josephus the Jew, by nation a Jerusalemite, by religion a priest, son of the priest Mattathias, a philosopher, rhetorician, and distinguished writer of the times, whom I would not place in our catalogue unless the Church read his writings and Blessed Jerome had done so before: after the destruction of Jerusalem he was captured by the emperors Vespasian and Titus and brought to Rome; because of the excellence of his talent he was magnificently honored by the senate and people of Rome. Although he remained a Jew, nevertheless he often praised the race of Christians, and in the eighteenth book of the Antiquities he set down a notable testimony concerning our Lord Jesus Christ. His volumes were held in such authority among the Romans, while he was still alive, that they were handed over to the public libraries; and after his death he was granted a bronze statue by the senate and people. Papias, hearer and disciple of Saint John, bishop of Hierapolis in Asia, a most steadfast propagator and defender of the Christian faith, disciple and zealous imitator of the holy apostles, wrote little works of authority not to be despised. Among these there exists a distinguished work, divided into five volumes, which he titled thus: Explanation of the Sayings of the Lord, books 5. Other things which he wrote we have not seen. He first invented the novelty of the thousand years, after which he dreamed that Christ would reign again in the flesh with the elect upon the earth; those who afterwards spread this error were called Chiliasts, from the thousand.

He flourished in the times of Caesar Trajan, in the year of the Lord 100 and thereabout.

Hegesippus, a Jew by nation, converted to the faith of Christ, shone forth as admirable in teaching and holiness in the Church of God; his feast has a place in the martyrology with the other saints. He wrote several little works of excellent talent, of which I have found only this: from the time of the Lord’s Passion down to his own age, a distinguished work, History of Ecclesiastical Acts, books 5. Justin, converted from being a philosopher to the catholic faith, a distinguished lover and worshipper of Christ, when in the city of Rome he rebuked Crescens the Cynic, who was blaspheming Christians, as gluttonous and afraid of death, and as a follower of luxury and lusts, was accused before the senate through that man’s plots, because he was a Christian, and was crowned with martyrdom for Christ.
