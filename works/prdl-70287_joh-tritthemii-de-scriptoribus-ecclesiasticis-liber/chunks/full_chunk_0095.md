*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

Paschasius Ratbertus, abbot of Corbie, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, nobly trained in Greek and Latin eloquence, acute in intellect and composed in speech, wrote not a few little works in verse and prose. Of these I have found only the following: to Placidus, abbot of our order, an outstanding work, *On the Sacrament of the Altar*, one book, “Whoever of Catholics”; *The Life of Saint Adelhard the Bishop*, one book; *On the Lamentations of Jeremiah the Prophet*, one book. He also translated some things from Greek into Latin, and composed many other things. He flourished in the time of the emperor Charles the Fat, in the year of the Lord 880.

Heiricus, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the monastery of the city of Auxerre, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in the disciplines of secular letters, excelling in verse and prose, was an outstanding teacher in homilies delivered to the people. He wrote in polished metre: *The Life of Saint Germanus of Auxerre*, six books; *Homilies to the People*, one book; many sermons and letters. He also composed many other things in both styles, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished around the time of the emperor Charles the Fat, in the year of the Lord 880.

Otfrid, a monk of the monastery of Weissenburg, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, formerly a hearer and disciple of Rabanus Maurus, abbot of Fulda, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, notably learned in secular letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, distinguished poet, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote many brilliant little works both in verse and in prose, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Following the example also of the emperor Charles the Great, he attempted to reduce the barbarism of the German tongue to grammatical rules, which he partly accomplished. Hence those things which he wrote in his native speech cannot easily be read and understood in our age, even by a man however skilled in the German language. Of his little works the following are extant: to Luitbert, the eighth archbishop of the church of Mainz, a great and outstanding work on the Gospel, which he entitled: *At the summit of dignity*, one book; *To Louis of the Easterners*, one book; *To Bishop Salomon, Otfrid*, one book; *Grace, Theotiste*, two [unclear]; to [unclear] [unclear].
