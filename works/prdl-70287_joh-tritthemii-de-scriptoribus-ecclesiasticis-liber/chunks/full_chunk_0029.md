*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

He suffered at Caesarea under Decius, in the year of the Lord 253, in the first indiction.

Julius Africanus, a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher, historiographer, and distinguished theologian, and a most ardent lover of divine Scripture and of books, founded by his own efforts and at his own expense a notable library at Caesarea in Palestine, in which he gathered many volumes both of secular and of divine writings. He also wrote several very useful little works, of which the following are reported:

Five books of *Chronicles*. One book *On Easter*.

One book *On the Holy Trinity*. One book *On the Sabbath*.

One book *On Circumcision*. One book *To Origen on Susanna*.

One book *On Attalus*. One book *To Aristides the Philosopher*.

And he is said to have composed many other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Marcus Aurelius, in the year of the Lord 220.

Gregory, bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, a hearer and disciple of Origen, a learned and holy man, both wrote many things and, while living, shone forth with miracles. While still a young man, seeking the study of secular philosophy with his brother Athenodorus, he placed himself under the school of Origen; by him, together with his brother, he was gradually, as if imperceptibly, converted to the faith of Christ, and became a most learned and most holy man. Of his writings there survives:

One book, a *Panegyric* addressed to Origen. One book of *Interpretations on Ecclesiastes*.

And several letters. He flourished under the emperor Gordian, in the year of the Lord 240.

Cornelius, the twenty-second bishop of the city of Rome after Peter, a Roman by birth, his father being Castinus, was made pope after the glorious martyrdom of Fabian and sat in the chair of Peter for two years, a learned and most holy man, until at last he too, crowned with martyrdom, passed, released from the flesh, to those above. He wrote several letters for the benefit of the Church; of these I have found only the following:

To Fabius, bishop of Antioch:

One letter *On Synods*. One letter *On Novatian*.

To the same, one book *On the Novatians*. One letter *On the Acts of the Synod*.

He wrote several letters to Cyprian, bishop of Carthage, and others to other persons. He flourished under the tyrant emperor Decius, in the year of the Lord 255.

Cyprian, bishop of the church of Carthage in Africa, a learned and most holy man, taught rhetoric gloriously for many years, and was a magnificent teacher in all secular literature. Then, at the persuasion of the presbyter Caecilius, from whom he obtained his surname, he was converted to the faith of Christ, [unclear] all his substance to the poor
