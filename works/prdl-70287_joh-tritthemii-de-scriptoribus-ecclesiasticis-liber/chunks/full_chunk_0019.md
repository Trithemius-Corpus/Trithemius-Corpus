These are the writings of blessed Dionysius that I find among the Latins; whatever more he published has remained among the Greeks. But since the aforesaid little works were covered with great obscurity, they were made clear by the explanation of the following fathers. Of these John Scotus, a monk of our order, was the first to translate and polish them; then Hugh of St. Victor, Rupert of Lincoln, Albertus Magnus, Dionysius the Carthusian, and many others, of whom no recollection is now at hand.

Blessed Dionysius suffered in the territory of Paris, together with the companions named above, under the emperor Domitian, in the year of the Lord 96, in the ninth indiction, and in the ninetieth year of his age. He was buried at Paris in a church consecrated to his name, shining with miracles.

Lucius Annaeus Seneca of Corduba, philosopher, disciple of Sotion the Stoic, uncle of the poet Lucan, was a man of most continent life. Since he is celebrated by the testimony of blessed Jerome, I have therefore placed him in the catalogue of Ecclesiastical Writers, because he was a very close friend of the apostle Paul, and composed many elegant works for the instruction of moral life. This man Nero Caesar, that ill-starred ruler, killed two years before Peter and Paul were crowned; Nero had once been his most unworthy pupil. Of his little works I have found the following:

On the Training of Character, one book;
