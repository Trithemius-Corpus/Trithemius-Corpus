*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

On not rebaptizing heretics, one book.
He flourished, as I have found, under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 490.

Julianus, bishop of the church of Toledo in Spain, a Moor by nation, to whom Saint Prosper is said to have written three books On Contemplation, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular studies, scholastic in speech, clear in understanding, wrote many notable volumes, of which the following are reported:

On holy virginity, one book. On the nature of the soul, [8] books.
On the resolution of questions, one book. On contempt of the world, one book.
Prognosticon of the future age, three books. The faith of the nascent Church [unclear].
Letters to various persons, one book.
On vices and virtues, two books. Letters to various women, one book.

He is said to have composed some other works as well, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Victorianus Buconius, bishop of the city of Castelliana in Mauretania, a man trained by constant study in the divine writings and by no means moderately learned in the disciplines of secular letters, a most powerful hammer of heretics and an outstanding teacher of the faithful peoples, is said to have written many little works in defense of the Catholic faith, of which the principal ones are reported as follows:

Against the Jews, one book. Against the Arians, one book.
Against all heretics, two books. On the sacraments also, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 460.

Museus, priest of the church of Marseille, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and polished by very subtle exercise in their meanings, scholastic and elegant in language, at the urging of Saint Venerius the bishop excerpted from the holy Scriptures lessons and responsories suited to the several festive days of the whole year, and arranged psalms suitable for singing in the church at the several times. He also wrote to Bishop Eustasius an excellent work:

On the sacraments, seven books. Several homilies to the people.
And certain other things.

He flourished under the rulers Marcian and Leo, in the year of the Lord 440.

Vincentius, a priest, a Gaul by nation, much practiced in the divine writings, having a tongue polished by practice in speaking and by greater reading, and not ignorant of secular letters either, wrote, as our Gennadius reports, an excellent work, to be very greatly embraced by devout men:

Commentaries on the Psalms, one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

*80 On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Cyrus, Alexandrian by birth, a physician by art, having from a philosopher become a monk for the love of Christ, a man very learned in secular letters, remarkable in skill of speaking, in his writings using syllogisms more than the authorities of the divine readings: while he inveighs too much against Nestorius, he is alleged to support the Timothean doctrine, and being uncertain of himself, he suspends his mind from agreement with the Council of Chalcedon, nor does he think one should assent that the Son of God after the incarnation is confessed in two natures. Of his writings, however, the following is reported:

Against [unclear], one book. Against heretics, one book.

He flourished under Leo Augustus, in the year of the Lord 460.

Salvianus, priest of the church of Marseille, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, teacher of the bishops of his time, wrote in scholastic and clear speech many varied little works, of which the following are reported. To Claudius, bishop of Vienne, he wrote several letters, seasoned with the salt of wisdom. To Marcellus the priest:

On the good of virginity, three books. Against avarice, four books.
To Claudius, bishop of Vienne, one book.
On the beginning of Genesis, the Hexameron, one book.
On the final part of Ecclesiastes, one book. Letters to various persons, one book.
Homilies made to bishops, one book. On the sacraments also, one book.

He also published many other works, which have by no means come to my knowledge.
He flourished under the emperor Zeno, in the year of the Lord 480.

Samuel, priest of the church of Edessa, learned in the divine Scriptures by constant exercise, an outstanding declaimer of homilies to the people, skilled in the Greek and Syriac language, first flourished at Edessa; later, coming to Constantinople, and there held in high esteem, he closed his final day. He wrote, both in Greek and in Syriac, almost countless little works, of which very few have come to my knowledge. For I found only:

Against the Nestorians, one book. Against the Eutychians, one book.

He flourished under Zeno Augustus, in the year of the Lord 480.
