*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

By the publication of books he entrusted his name to immortality. But I have been able to find only the titles of the following:

On the Code, 9 books.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.

Of the other things which he is said to have composed, I have so far been able to find nothing certain. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Hubertinus of Casale, of the Order of Friars Minor, formerly a hearer of Johannes of Parma, minister general, a devout man, sufficiently learned in the holy Scriptures, when he saw that his order was falling away from its first institution, began certain new separations under the appearance of reform, and obtained that they be confirmed by the supreme pontiff. Yet at the petitions of the custodians and ministers they were immediately revoked by the same pope, because they appeared to have been undertaken to the grave scandal and division of the order. Therefore Hubertinus, moved by this, entered the Order of the Carthusians. He wrote a very distinguished and notable volume, useful to religious and devout persons, which he entitled:

The Tree of the Life of the Crucified, 5 books.
Sermons; Letters. To all true [servants?] of Christ. And certain other things.

Certain Catholic doctors warn us that Hubertinus, in the aforesaid volume, in certain places, especially where he takes up the Canticle of Simeon, must be read cautiously, because on certain articles he thinks otherwise than the Church, mother of us all. Yet the author himself is to be excused, because it had not yet been determined. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Jacobus of Ravenna, born in the province of Lorraine, a man most learned in civil law and the sacred canons, and easily the most skilled of all in his time both in divine Scriptures and in secular letters, professor of sacred Scripture, a distinguished philosopher and dialectician, who, as Cinus says, converted what had been handed down by the elders in a simple sense into the dialectical mode of arguing. He wrote many small works in both laws, of which the following are reported:

On the Code, 9 books.
On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
Various Disputations, 1 book.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.
