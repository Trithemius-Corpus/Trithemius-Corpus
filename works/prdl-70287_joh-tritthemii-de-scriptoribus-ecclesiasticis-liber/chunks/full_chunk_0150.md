*Abbot of Spanheim.*

Various *Declarations*. Moreover he published many other small works, especially in law, and arranged various glosses on the canons, about which I have nowhere been able to find anything.

Vido, priest of the church of Ferrara and afterwards, as they say, bishop, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures, rich in reading of the ancients, and nobly learned in secular letters, an eminent philosopher and poet, wrote several small works, both in verse and in prose, not to be despised in reading, by which he made his name known. Of these I have found only a volume written in verse to Pope Clement V, on the New and Old Testament, imitating Peter of Riga, which he entitled *The Pearl of the Bible*, one book. Its noble beginning [unclear]. I have seen nothing of the others.

He flourished in the times of Henry of Luxembourg, the seventh emperor, and Pope Clement V, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Peter of Saxony, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, and nobly learned in canon law, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, an excellent preacher of the divine word, wrote certain excellent volumes of his own ability, which in his time were held in esteem. Of these there is extant a work useful for confessors: *A Summa of Cases*, one book; *Sermons on the Season*, one book; *Sermons on the Saints*, one book. The rest that he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

Crufianus, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by homeland, once a hearer and disciple of the physician Thadaeus, a man learned and instructed in philosophy and medicine, but by no means fortunate, and for that reason bidding farewell to the world and entering the Carthusian order, usefully exchanged the art of the flesh for the medicine of the spirit. He had previously written some small works in his own faculty, by which he made his name known. Of these there is extant a commentary *On the Techne of Galen*, one book, and certain others.

He flourished in the Carthusian order in the time of Albert, Roman emperor, in the year of the Lord 1300. And afterwards he is said to have written various treatises pertaining to spiritual edification.

Peter of Verberia, called Aureoli, of the Order of Friars Minor and archbishop of Aix, doctor of the Parisian school, very learned in the divine Scriptures and very skilled in Aristotelian philosophy according to the custom of this time, was subtle in intellect, an acute disputant, and excellent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote many excellent small works, by which he magnificently spread his name. Of these there are extant *On the Sentences*, four books; [unclear], *Come, let us go up*, one book; *The abysses were not yet*, one book; *Sermons on the Season*, one book; *Sermons on the Saints*, one book; and others that I have not seen.

He flourished before his pontificate, reading the Sentences at Paris, in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1317.

Gentilis of Foligno, an eminent professor of medicine and magnificently learned in the other disciplines of secular philosophy, whose skill in that faculty was not slight, wrote in medicine many excellent volumes, as they say, by which he made his name known with praise not only to those then present but also to posterity. But I have seen none of them. There are reported to be: commentaries *On Avicenna*, one book; *On the Degrees of Medicine*, one book; and certain others. *Counsels on All Diseases*, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Dantes Aligerus, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by homeland, a man in his time most studious of all in both the divine Scriptures and secular letters, wrote many excellent volumes in verse and prose, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Driven from his homeland for all his days, he lived in exile in Gaul for some time; afterwards, at the court of the king of Aragon, he composed various works about his calamity. Of his small works these are reported: *Comedies*, one book; *On the Monarchy of the World*, one book; several letters; and certain others. Also by him, a disputation *On Water and Earth*.
