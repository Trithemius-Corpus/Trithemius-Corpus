*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Rationale of divine works, seven books. “I should wish it to be declared how...”
Also, On contracts, one book. On the transfer of [unclear].
On the celebration of Mass, one book.
To the bishop of Warmia, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Sermons, conferences.
He first flourished in the school at Prague; then, when he was driven out with other Germans by the Hussites, he taught letters for a time in the Parisian academy.

Johannes Fustinione, a German from the town of Kreuznach, born in the county of Spanheim, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Strasbourg, a man learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent ability in delivering sermons to the people, while he was still lector in the convent of Kreuznach wrote, for the instruction of simple preachers, little works not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Weekday sermons through Lent, one book.

But of the rest that he composed, nothing has ever come into our hands. He is said to have flourished at Kreuznach around the times of Emperor Charles IV. His aforesaid sermons, Fustgin, are held in the original in the convent of Kreuznach.

[Gerardus?] Magnus of Deventer, a German by nation, of the diocese of Utrecht, a man excellently learned both in the divine scriptures and in canon law, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, who first founded at Deventer the house of the Brothers whom they call “of the Common Life,” wrote several little works of no contemptible reading, by which, exercising his talent, he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. For he was famous throughout all Germany not only for learning, but also for a religious life and praiseworthy conduct. Of his little works these are reported:

On contracts and usuries, one book.
On the instruction of novices, one book.
On the leasing of pastoral care, one book. “It is asked whether someone can...”
On internal conduct, one book.
On the governance of nuns, one book.
On the inconveniences of marriage, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book. Various sermons, one book.
And many other things.

He died under Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1384, in the seventh indiction, in the forty-fourth year of his age.

William of Califford, an Englishman by nation, of the order of the brothers of most blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, in [unclear], distinguished, of excellent and vigorous ability in delivering sermons to the people, wrote little works not to be despised for preachers of the divine word, by which he made his name known to many. Of these there are extant:

A certain notable lecture, one book.
Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.

He flourished in the academy of Cambridge in England, where by writing, teaching, and disputing he won great glory for his learning.

Franciscus Martini, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a Catalan by nation, a man learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, an outstanding declaimer, published with much diligence an excellent volume, furnished on the holy and most pure conception of the glorious Virgin Mary, mother of God, which he entitled:

Compendium, seven books.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the convent of Barcelona, where at the beginning of the aforesaid golden work he states that he compiled it in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Jacobus de Lusanna, of the order of the Preaching Brothers of St. Dominic, bishop, as they report, of a certain city, a man most devoted to the divine scriptures, skilled by long practice, prompt in intellect, scholastic in speech, sufficiently outstanding in delivering sermons to the people, wrote little works of no contemptible usefulness, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there are reported:

On Genesis, one book. “In the beginning God created...”
On Exodus, one book. “I will come to you and...” On Numbers, one book.
On Deuteronomy, one book.
Sermons on the season, one book. “As in the day, honorably...”
