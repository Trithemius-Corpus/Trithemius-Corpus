Alexander, the third pope, by homeland a Sienese, after Adrian IV sat in the chair of Peter for twenty-one years. He was a learned and vigorous man, who suffered many injuries from the emperor Frederick, while he forcefully crushed the three antipopes set up by him against himself; and at last, overcoming the emperor himself, as a sign of subjection he trampled his neck with his foot, saying: “It is written: You shall walk upon the asp and the basilisk, and you shall trample the lion and the dragon.” He wrote elegant

Letters to various persons, one book,
and On Ecclesiastical Power, one book,
and certain other things.

He died in the times of Frederick, the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 1181, in the fourteenth indiction. [unclear]

[unclear] Risolanus [perhaps Chrysolanus], archbishop of the church of Milan, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, distinguished in Greek and Latin eloquence, produced certain works of great authority, but none of them has come into my hands. There is reported to be his On the Holy Spirit Against the Greeks, one book; On the Holy Trinity, one book; letters, sermons, and various other treatises, which are unknown to me.

Hugo Eterianus, by nation a Tuscan, easily the most learned of all the doctors of his time in the holy Scriptures, and excellently instructed in secular letters, a most eloquent philosopher and rhetorician, perfectly trained in Greek and Latin speech, whom all Greece admired as a writer, very subtle in talent and second to none as a disputant, wrote many distinguished volumes both in Greek and in Latin, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have found only one work, short indeed, but not to be despised, on the heresies which the Greeks hurl against the Latins:

To Cardinal Ardoinus, one book. “Diligent prudence ...”
On the Immortal God, one book.
Of the rest I have found nothing.

He flourished in the times of Manuel, emperor of Constantinople, with whom he was of great authority, under Frederick, the first Roman Augustus, and Alexander, the fourth pope, in the year of the Lord 1160.
