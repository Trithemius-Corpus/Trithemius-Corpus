John Danck of Saxony, German by nation, contemporary of the aforesaid John of Lignières and his companion in studies, a man most learned in every respect, the most celebrated philosopher and astronomer among all men of his time, excellent in subtle intellect, published many excellent and admirable short works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. But thus far I have found very few:

Eclipse Canons, one book. “The quantity of an eclipse of the sun ...”
Canons of the tables, one book. “The time of the fourth aspect ...”
On the astrolabe, one book.
On burnings, one book.

He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, and of Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Peter de Palude, French by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy and canon law, an acute disputant and distinguished preacher, published certain excellent volumes on the holy Scriptures, by which he bestowed his name upon immortality. Among these are reported:

On the Sentences, four books.
Various Questions, one book.
And various Sermons, one book.
And many other things.

He is also said to have adorned many books of the Bible with his commentaries, that is, to have expounded them.

John Calderini, Italian by nation, from Bologna, once a hearer and disciple of John Andreae, professor of canon law, a very celebrated interpreter, not ignorant of secular philosophy and of the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, apt in speech, wrote no few volumes in his faculty. By these, proving his intellect through diligent exercise, he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity. Among these I have found a great and notable work, divided into three not small volumes, which he entitled:

Repertory of Law, one book. “A. This word a, sometimes ...”
On the Decretals, one book. “Although many doubtful matters ...”
On ecclesiastical interdict, one book.
Many opinions, one book.

Of the other things I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Paul de Liazariis, a hearer of John Andreae of Bologna and fellow student of John Calderini, a man most skilled in both laws, nobly instructed in the precepts of the philosophers, distinguished in intellect and eloquence, who among the doctors of law in his time was not [unclear], wrote, among the other short works of his intellect, a notable volume which is called:

On the Clementines, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and of Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Lupold of Bebenburg, bishop of Bamberg, German by nation, as they report once a hearer of John Andreae of Bologna, a very celebrated jurist, nobly learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, published short works not to be despised in reading, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with great glory. Among these there is extant a notable work:

On the Zeal of the Christian Religion of the Old Princes of the Germans, one book.
On the Rights of the Roman Empire, one book.
And certain other volumes on diverse subjects, which have not come to me.

He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, and of Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Nicholas of Modena, a man nobly learned in both laws, especially in the civil laws, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, cautious in knowledge and counsel, in order to leave to posterity monuments of his intellect, wrote certain excellent volumes in civil law, by which he had made himself useful to those then living, and known to those to come. Among these are reported:

On the Code, nine books.
On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.
