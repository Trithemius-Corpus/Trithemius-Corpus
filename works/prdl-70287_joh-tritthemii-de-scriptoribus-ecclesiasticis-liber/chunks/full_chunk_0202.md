Antonius Guainerius, an Italian by nation, from Pavia by homeland, a most celebrated doctor of the art of medicine, and nobly learned in the precepts of the philosophers, excellent in ability and notably distinguished by skill in his profession, wrote in that field of learning certain lucid little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On disease of the stomach, one book. On fever also, one book.
On diseases of the groin, one book. On pleurisy, one book.
On the womb, one book. On fluxes, one book.
On arthritis in the joints, one book. On the sufferings of stone, one book.
On the third book of Avicenna, one book. And many other works.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Gerardus Stredam, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of All the Apostles near Liege, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures and excellently learned through long practice, also venerable in religion, prudence, and authority, wrote for the edification of simple brothers certain little works, of which the following are reported: To the abbot of Saint Adrian of Geraardsbergen,

On pastoral care, one book. On the Ten Commandments, one book.
On the Seven Sacraments, one book. Mirror of religious, one book.
Dialogue on the virtues, one book. Dialogue on the vices, one book.
Sermons for the season, one book. Sermons on the saints, one book.
And certain other works.

He died at the beginning of the reign of the emperor Frederick III, under Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1445, in the sixth indiction.

Matthaeus Palmerius, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by homeland, a historian, philosopher, and most eloquent orator, who at the Council of Florence, which Pope Eugenius IV held against the synod of Basel, was counted among the distinguished men, wrote a book full of error concerning the angels; and, stubbornly defending it, he was condemned as a heretic and burned at the city of Corna. Carefully reviewing the Chronicle of Eusebius of Caesarea, he corrected it and, completing it down to the year of the Lord 1448, entitled it to Petrus Cosmas de’ Medici, one book, “Innate in our minds.”
Letters to various persons, one book.
Various orations also.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1440.

321 *The Abbot of Sponheim*

Johannes Ernesti, a German by nation, once the ornament and rector of the school of Heidelberg, learned in the holy Scriptures and rich in his reading of the ancients, subtle in ability, eloquent with singular grace of speech, an outstanding disputant of questions, wrote, as they report, certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of their number the following are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
A question in disputation, one book.
Many sermons, one book.
An oration on the Nativity of the Lord, one book, “Although of speaking concerning the greatest...”
On fasting, to the university, one book, “Most sacred desires...”
Many and various orations, one book.
And many other works.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, at the beginning of the reign of the emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Bartholomaeus, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Bethlehem in the town of Roermond in Guelders, a German by nation, an acute, devout, and celebrated interpreter of sacred letters, wrote for the edification of his brothers certain distinguished little works, by which he magnificently exercised his ability and left to posterity the memory of his name. Of their number the following are reported:

Exercise of devotion, one book.
Sum of vices, one book.
On abstinence from meats, one book.
On the proclamations of the brothers, one book. On an oath, one book. And certain other works.
On rash judgments, one book.
