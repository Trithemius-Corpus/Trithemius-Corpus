*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

*Raymundus Sebeide, a Spaniard by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, and also exceptionally well taught in secular letters, a distinguished doctor of arts and medicine, who by teaching and writing in the university of Toulouse gave great proof of his erudition, is said to have written many excellent little works. Of these I have seen only a large volume, which he had read publicly in the aforesaid university; at its beginning he promises many glorious things, and some applaud themselves concerning the truth of them as though experienced, because he had noted beforehand:*

*Of Creatures, or On Man*, one book.

*Disputed Questions*, one book.

To the praise and glory [unclear]

The rest are unknown to me.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1450.

Franciscus Barbarus, a Venetian by birth, a disciple in Greek letters of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine scriptures, a clear philosopher and a supreme orator in both languages, while still a young man wrote certain little works not unworthy of reading, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there is extant a work which he entitled:

*On Marriage*, one book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, sixteen books.

And certain other things.

From Plutarch he translated into Latin, for his brother Zacharias, the life of Aristides and of Cato, and of many illustrious men. Likewise also other things.

He died in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Nicholas V, in the year of the Lord 1454, in the second indiction.

Thomas Walden, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, and prior general of the English province, a man most learned in the divine writings and nobly taught in Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect and quite fit for disputation, distinguished no less in conduct than in knowledge, also an excellent preacher of sermons, and formerly confessor and secretary of Henry V, king of the English, wrote, among the other monuments of his talent, a distinguished work divided into three great volumes against the infamous Wycliffites and Hussite heretics, which Pope Martin V, after examination, confirmed by apostolic authority.
