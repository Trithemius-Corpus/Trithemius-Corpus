Prefaces to various books in mathematics, lectured on by himself.

Determinations of certain quodlibetal questions.

Andreas Thurinus of Pescia, apostolic physician, a man most learned in medical matters, wrote a letter against Matteo Curti of Pavia, physician, on the vein to be cut in the treatment of pleurisy.

And likewise to Paul III, On the Perfection of the Treatment of Pleurisy.

Responses by the same author against Matteo Curti, on the place of the vein to be cut in disease of the ribs.

The same man wrote On the Goodness of Waters, of spring-water and cistern-water, which is [unclear]; and a dissertation on the new embrocha, or artificial shower-bath.

There is also extant, by the same author, a Defense of Hippocrates and Galen against Girolamo Fracastoro, on the causes of critical days.

Andreas Vesalius of Brussels, public professor of the school of physicians at Padua, composed paraphrases on the ninth book of the very famous Arab physician to King Almansor, on the treatment of affections of the individual parts of the body.

There survives a letter of the same man, which teaches that the axillary vein of the right elbow is to be cut in pain of the side, and that melancholic juice is purged from the branches of the portal vein pertaining to the seat.

There are also extant seven books, On the Fabric of the Human Body, with most elegant figures of all kinds, by the same author.

Angelo Aretino, J.C. (whom they otherwise call Angelo Gambiglioni, or de Gambiglioni of Arezzo), wrote two volumes of commentaries on the Institutes of civil law, addressed to Leonello d’Este, marquis of Ferrara.

Likewise:

On Testaments, and the Clauses of Testaments.

On Crimes in Law, and the Clauses of Inquisitorial Procedure.

On the three books of the Code.

Two volumes of Counsels. Certain singularia.

A Repertory of Law.

A repetition, for the third time, on C. De bon. vac., book 10, l. Si vacantia.

Angelo Bolognini publicly taught surgery from Avicenna at Bologna, and wrote two books on the care of external ulcers, in an uncultivated style. In the first part of these are set forth the things that impede consolidation, what and how many they are, how they do harm, and how they are recognized. In the other, medicines are administered both generally and individually.

The same man composed a book on common ointments, in the solution of continuity.

Angelo Cospi of Bologna, a man thoroughly learned in both Greek and Latin literature, translated the sixteenth and seventeenth books of Diodorus Siculus.

Antiochus Tibertus of Cesena, professor of the liberal disciplines, wrote three books on chiromancy to Ottaviano Ubaldini, count of Mercatello.

Antipater, bishop of Bostra, composed a sermon on the Nativity of Christ. It is extant in Greek in Italy.

Antoninus, bishop of Constantina, driven into exile under Arcadius by Genseric, king of the Vandals, wrote an exhortation.

Antonius of Brescia worked out a learned work of sermons, which is extant in Italy.
