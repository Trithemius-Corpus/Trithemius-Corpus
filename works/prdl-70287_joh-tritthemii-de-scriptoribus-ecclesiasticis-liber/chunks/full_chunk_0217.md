He died at Angers under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1471, in the third indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of August.

Theodore Gaza of Thessalonica, a man excellently learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the holy Scriptures, very learned in Greek and Latin, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote many little works and translated many things from Greek into Latin, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of his own writings nothing has anywhere come to the notice of my reading; but among the translated works the following are reported:

Theophrastus On Plants, 1 book. Aristotle’s Problems, 1 book.
The same, On Animals, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He died at Rome under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1478, in the eleventh indiction.

John of Lutria, a German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently learned in scholastic philosophy, keen in talent, distinguished in life, and a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, who first taught theology gloriously in the school of Erfurt and afterward vigorously carried out the office of preacher in the church of Mainz, wrote certain little works not contemptible for reading, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books. Various sermons, 1 book.
Disputed Questions, 1 book. And certain things in philosophy, 1 book.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1479, in the twelfth indiction.

Alanus de Rupe, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, of the highest reputation in delivering sermons to the people, devout to God in life and conduct, and a special lover of the blessed Mary ever virgin, and a venerable promoter of her rosary and service; by her also, through a singular gift of heavenly consolation, he deserved at times to be refreshed, and now to be restored in eternal happiness. Among other works of his talent he wrote:

Compendium of the Marian Psalter, 1 book. “Because of the Catholic truth,” 2 books. “Although beautiful praise” [unclear]. Various sermons also, 1 book. And certain other things.

He died in his convent at Zwolle, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1474, in the seventh indiction, on the vigil of the Assumption of the blessed Virgin Mary. Her rosary and the Psalter commemorative of Christ’s Passion, which had long been so neglected, he recalled to the minds and practices of men; in whose honor he established a confraternity which, confirmed by the Apostolic See, increases daily and provides many with a salutary exercise.

Conrad of Zabernia, a German by nation, a man zealous and practiced in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, especially music, teachable in talent and exemplary in life, renowned and devout in delivering sermons to the people, wrote in plain discourse certain little works not contemptible, by which, usefully and fruitfully exercising his talent, he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of their number the following are extant:

On the Monochord, 1 book. “Since, as a certain wise man...”
On the Method of Singing Well, 1 book. “In favor of the whole clergy...”
On the End of Collects, 1 book. “Although very many...”
Many and various sermons, 1 book. And certain other things.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 1470, under Pope Sixtus IV, under whom he also died.
