Ansegisus, abbot of the monastery of Lobbes, of the Order of St. Benedict, later archbishop of Sens, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, distinguished in speech and talent, arranged and gathered together, for the use of the catholic Church, the laws of the emperors Charlemagne and Louis, which I have seen, as follows, namely:

Ecclesiastical statutes of Charles, 2 books. “When our Lord Jesus was reigning.”
Secular laws of the same, 2 books. “Concerning peace we admonish.”
Ecclesiastical statutes of Louis, 2 books. “To all of you either by sight.”
Secular laws of the same, 2 books. “If servants through contumacy.”

He published besides not a few other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Lothar, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Amulus, bishop of Lyons in Gaul, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, learned by reading the ancients, and nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent, distinguished and eloquent, wrote little works of no contemptible reading, of which I have read only the notable work addressed to Charles:

Against the Jews, 1 book. “The detestable things of the Jews.”

Of the rest I have as yet seen none. He flourished under Louis, son of Charlemagne, in the year 850.

Bertramus, priest and monk, very skilled in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in the letters of the secular disciplines, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence, and no less notable in life than in doctrine, wrote many excellent little works, of which few have come to my knowledge. To King Charles, brother of the emperor Lothar, he wrote a commendable work:

On Predestination, 1 book.
On the Body and Blood of the Lord, 1 book.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 830.

Turpin, archbishop of Reims, friend and secretary of the great emperor Charles, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, having a talent exercised in verse and prose, advocate of the poor, beloved by God in life and conduct, who declares that he often fought the Saracens with Charles by his own hands and arms, wrote elegantly:

The Deeds of Charlemagne, 2 books.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.

He flourished under the same Charles and Louis his son, in the year of the Lord 850.

*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Anastasius, keeper and most sagacious restorer of the apostolic library, learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, very skilled in Greek and Latin speech, is said to have composed not a few little works, but none of them has come into our hands. He translated many volumes of the Greeks into Latin; for, at the command of Pope Nicholas I, he translated:

The Life of John the Almsgiver, 1 book.
One Hierarchy of St. Dionysius, 1 book.
The Seventh Universal Synod, 1 book.
The Lives of Many Holy Fathers, 1 book.
And many other things.

He also wrote an Ecclesiastical History down to his own times, at the request of Charles II, to whom he was especially dear. He flourished under Louis II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 860.

Nicholas, the first pope of that name, a Roman by birth, sat in the pontificate after Benedict III for eight years; a man magnificently learned in the holy Scriptures, most holy in life and conduct, who established many things for the use of the catholic Church. By his permission and authority, through the efforts of Notger, abbot of St. Gall, the use of sequences first began in the Gallican churches. He wrote, among other things, in the person of his pontificate, an excellent volume:

Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.

He died under Lothar or Louis II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 869, in the second indiction, on the 8th day before the Ides of December.

John, deacon of the Roman Church, a man learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, venerable in life and conduct no less than in doctrine, wrote for the edification of many, to Pope John VII, in a sufficiently illustrious style, a large volume, which contains:

The Life of St. Gregory, Pope, the first of that name, 1 book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Charles the Bald, emperor, in the year of the Lord 870.
