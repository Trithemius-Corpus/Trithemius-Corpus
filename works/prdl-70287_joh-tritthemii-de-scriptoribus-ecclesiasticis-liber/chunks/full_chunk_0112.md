He flourished under Emperor Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1130.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Deutz near Cologne Agrippina, of the Order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures through the Holy Spirit enlightening him by vision, and not ignorant of secular letters, a monk of Saint Lawrence at Liège, on account of his incredible learning was taken from there by Frederick, archbishop of Cologne, and raised to be abbot of the monastery of Deutz. He composed many distinguished little works. Indeed, from the time when he entered the monastery in his youth, he had exercised himself so much in the Holy Scriptures that, even when sleeping, you would have seen that he could not be free from meditating on them, since his tongue and lips moved as if he were reading. Of his little works I have at present found only the following: To Cuno, abbot of Siegburg, afterward bishop of Regensburg:

On the victory of the Word of God, thirteen books.

Finally, he is said to have written many other things, and to have explained the New and Old Testament, for the greater part, in many books; concerning these we have seen nothing up to the present except the things we have marked.

He flourished with great holiness, shining with learning and miracles, under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1114. He died under Lothair III.
