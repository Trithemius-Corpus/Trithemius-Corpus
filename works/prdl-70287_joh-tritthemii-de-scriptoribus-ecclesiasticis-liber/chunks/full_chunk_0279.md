*Ecclesiastical [Writers], Addition II.*

Capitular sermons on the principal feasts of the whole year.
On the struggle, or passion, of the Lord, 3 books.
On the true Christian religion, addressed to the divine Charles, the fifth emperor of this name, ever Augustus, 1 book.
The Handbook of Christian Warfare, 1 book.
The Scholastic Quiver, 1 book.
The Mystical Quiver of Divine Love, 1 book.
A work of letters to various persons, 1 book.
A Dialogue between a Monk and a Soldier, 1 book.
The Mirror of Christian Perfection, 1 book.
The Life of the Savior, with various rosaries.
He also wrote some things in German against the Lutherans.
He further published learned dedicatory letters, although under the names of others, before the books of D. Dionysius the Carthusian.
At length he died in the Cologne Charterhouse, on the very day of Saint Tiburtius, after long and most patiently endured severe illnesses, in the year of the Lord 1539.

Johannes Ludovicus Vives of Valencia, Spanish by nation, within a few years published several very distinguished works, written learnedly and with full polish; in them he so joined wisdom to eloquence that no one, even an erudite man, would not admit that he had read them with some profit. He died recently, leaving behind a great longing for him among students. Moreover, whatever I have found published under his name are these:

Two letters On the Method of Boys’ Studies.
Introduction to True Wisdom.
A golden little book On Writing Letters.
On the Method of Studying, Reading, and Interpreting.
On the Method of Instructing Pupils.
On the Method of Collecting Examples.
Exercise in the Latin Language.
An interpretation of Vergil’s Bucolics, chiefly allegorical.
A preface and exposition on the Dream of Scipio, which he entitled The Vigil.
Rhetoric, 13 books.
On the Disciplines, 20 books.
And he produced very many other vigils, which are known to the learned.

Johannes Manardus, a physician of Ferrara, a man in his own age the most celebrated, and most deserving, of all professors of medicine throughout Italy in Galen’s doctrine and in his censure of the Arabs, composed eighteen books of medical letters.

His annotations, with censure, on the simple and compound medicines of Mesue.
He also composed commentaries on the first book of Galen’s Little Art.
He flourished in the year of salvation 1539.

Johannes Murmellius of Roermond wrote many little works, especially grammatical ones, not unlearned, and the following other works:

An Isagoge on Aristotle’s Categories.
Sentences drawn from the elegies of Tibullus, Catullus, Propertius, and Ovid.
Tables of Grammar.
Tables on the rudiments of the art of composing verses.
Three little works of tables.
Commentaries on the Satires of A. Persius.
Commentaries on the five books of Boethius On the Consolation of Philosophy. And many other things, namely various epigrams, Scoparius Pappa.
He flourished in the year of the Lord 1504. He died in 1517 at Deventer.

Johannes Nauclerus, provost of Tübingen, wrote Chronicles briefly comprising the memorable affairs of all ages and peoples, from the beginning of the world down to the year 1500 from the birth of Christ, with an addition by Nicolaus Basellius from the year of the Lord 1501 to the year 1514, and with a new appendix, briefly noting the events meanwhile carried on from the year 1515 down to the year 1544, the compilers being partly Conrad Tigemann and partly Bartholomaeus Laurens.

Johannes Pesselius Tilanus, by homeland a Geldrian, an outstanding professor of sacred scripture and a most worthy provincial of the institute of D. Dominic, both restored certain works of doctors from errors and himself composed works of his own, of which this is the catalogue:

He revised certain works of the divine Augustine corrupted by our heretics.
Gorra’s Expositions on the Four Gospels.
A Compendium of the New Treasury, on the Temporale and the Sanctorale.
And certain other things, if heaven favors, he will publish.
The pious orthodox man is still living in the year of Christ 1546.
