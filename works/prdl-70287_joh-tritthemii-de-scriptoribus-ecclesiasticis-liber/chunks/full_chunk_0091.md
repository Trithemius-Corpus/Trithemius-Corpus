*The Abbot of Spanheim.*

Ellator, a priest of a certain church, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, rich in reading the ancients, distinguished in talent and eloquence, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, is said to have expounded many volumes on the holy Scriptures, in which he splendidly displayed his ability. There survives, among other things, a notable volume of his, which he published at the request of friends:
On the Book of Wisdom, 7 books.
I have found nothing about the others.
He is said to have flourished in the times of Emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Strabus, a monk of Fulda, a German by nation, once a pupil and scribe of Abbot Rabanus, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly trained in the studies of secular letters, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote not a few little works in exposition of the sacred volumes; in these, showing himself a learned man, he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Thus, imitating his master Abbot Rabanus, he wrote:
On Genesis, 1 book. On Exodus, 1 book.
On Leviticus, [unclear]. And many other works. This Strabus, finally, is remembered as the first to have assembled, from the sayings of the holy Fathers, the gloss on the whole Bible which is now called the Ordinary Gloss; others afterwards enlarged it by adding many sayings of the Fathers.
He flourished under Emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Lupus Servatus, a priest, to whom Abbot Rabanus of Fulda is read to have written eight books on the epistle of Paul the Apostle to the Romans, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and, according to the age of his time, abundantly skilled in secular letters, wrote some little works of his own talent, of which very few have come to my knowledge. There survives a volume of his:
On Three Questions, 1 book.
And certain letters, as well as other works.
He flourished under Lothar, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 850.
