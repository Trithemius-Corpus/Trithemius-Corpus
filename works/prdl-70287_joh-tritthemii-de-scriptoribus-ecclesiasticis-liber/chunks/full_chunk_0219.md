Moreover he translated many things into Latin, of which the following are reported. Among the Venetians, afterwards among the Florentines, the Sienese, the Bolognese, and finally the Milanese under four dukes, with an annual stipend, he taught Greek and Latin letters gloriously to very many. He died at Bologna, a nonagenarian and poor, indeed as a Christian philosopher, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1481, in the fourteenth indiction, in the year after the death of his son Marius.
