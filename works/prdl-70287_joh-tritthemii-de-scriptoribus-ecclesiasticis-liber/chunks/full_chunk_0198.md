Rudolphus of Bruxella, a village of Swabia, German by nation, of the diocese of Speyer, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and notably learned also in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, an eminent professor of sacred theology, who in the Heidelberg school acquired great praises for his learning: in that faculty he wrote works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity, namely:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Very useful sermons, one book.
Various questions, one book.
Several orations to the fathers, one book. Although holy mother
And many other things.
He flourished in the Heidelberg school at the time of the Council of Basel and thereafter, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Hugo of Siena, Italian by nation, a man very learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished philosopher and very celebrated physician, excellent in talent and outstanding in eloquence, wrote volumes in medicine not to be lightly esteemed by students, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name. Among these the following are reported:

On the first [book] of Avicenna, one book.
On the Aphorisms of Hippocrates, one book.
On the Techne of Galen, one book. And many other things.
He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1450.

Henricus de Gauda, German by nation, magnificently learned in the holy Scriptures, subtle in talent and a very acute disputant of scholastic questions, in the Heidelberg school, both by reading, by teaching, and also by composing, easily held first place in his time. *The Abbot of Sponheim*

Various orations. Disputed questions. And certain other things.
He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1435.

Santes de Arduinis, born from the city of Pesaro, a man most learned in philosophy and medicine, and most experienced in healing human bodies, held in great esteem among the Venetians in his time, published certain excellent works of this faculty, by which he destined the memory of his most celebrated name for the knowledge of posterity. Among these there exists a notable work on all natural and artificial poisons, one book.
On smelling also, one book.
On procreation, one book.
And certain other things.
He flourished under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Nicolaus de Susato, German by nation, a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, and most learned both in secular philosophy and in the divine Scriptures, held in esteem by Ludovicus, duke of Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine, and in the Council of Constance joined with the other cardinals from the German nation for electing the supreme pontiff, as is found in the acts of the same council: he wrote several excellent works of his talent, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name. Among these are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, one book.
Many sermons, one book.
Various questions. And many other things.
He flourished in the Council of Constance under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Johannes de Marliano, a very celebrated philosopher and physician, excellent in talent, distinguished in experience of this art, and not among the lowest of the physicians of his age, published certain excellent and lucid works both in philosophy and in medicine, the reading and study of which are said to be of no little use to students of these subjects, namely:

On reaction etc., one book.
On antiperistasis, one book.
And many other things.
He flourished in the times of Sigismund, the most Christian emperor, in the year of the Lord 1430.
