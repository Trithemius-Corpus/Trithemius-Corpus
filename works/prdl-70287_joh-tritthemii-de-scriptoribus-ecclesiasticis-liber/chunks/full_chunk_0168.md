*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Bartholus of Sassoferrato, once a hearer and disciple of the doctors Cinus and Jacobus Butrigarius, a most celebrated jurist, one to whom earlier ages had had no equal in that faculty, subtle and keen in intellect, polished and eloquent in speech, published certain distinguished volumes, especially on the laws. How great an authority these have today among jurists, no one even lightly learned and experienced is ignorant. Among these are cited commentaries on civil law, as follows:

On the Code, 9 books.
On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Institutes, 4 books.
Also the Tiberiadis, 1 book.
Also Councils, 1 book.
On the Guelphs and Ghibellines, 2 books.

And many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1355, in the eighth indiction, and in the fifty-sixth year of his age.

Petrus Thomas, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, first bishop of Patti, then archbishop of Crete, and most recently patriarch of Constantinople, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, most holy in life and conduct, since he is read to have shone forth, living and dead, with many signs and miracles, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, of which these are cited:

On the Sentences, 3 books.
Also various sermons, 2 books.

And certain similar things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350, and was buried in Cyprus.

Bartholomaeus, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, English by nation, a man most studious in the divine Scriptures and not meanly learned, and sufficiently imbued with secular letters, and distinguished no less by religious conduct than by knowledge, published little works of no negligible authority, the reading of which is judged necessary for simpler brothers.
