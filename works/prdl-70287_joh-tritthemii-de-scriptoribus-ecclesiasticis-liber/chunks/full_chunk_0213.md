Albertus de Eyb, a German by nation, canon of the church of Bamberg and chamberlain of Pope Pius II, a man zealous and learned in secular letters, not unacquainted with the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, an outstanding rhetorician and poet, wrote little works not unworthy of reading. Of these the following are extant: to lord Johann, duke of Bavaria, bishop of Munster and later primate of Magdeburg, a distinguished work entitled:

*The Poetic Pearl*, one book. “You have very often desired…”
*On the Married State also*, one book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.
And many other things.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Rodericus, bishop of Zamora, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not unacquainted with secular letters, excellent in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, wrote little works of no contemptible authority for their talent, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have so far seen only one volume, which he entitled:

*Mirror of Human Life*, one book. “Above all human…”
The rest I have not seen.

He flourished in the times of Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Ambrosius Coriolanus, of the order of the hermit friars of St. Augustine, prior general, a Roman by homeland, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and distinguished in knowledge of secular letters, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech, learned and eloquent, wrote certain notable little works by which he commended his name to immortality. Of these, however, few have come to my knowledge by reading; I have set them below:

*On the Rule of Saint Augustine*, one book. “From every learned parent…”
*A Commendation of the Same Rule*, one book. “After blessed Augustine…”
*On the Second Rule*, one book. “Keen in intellect, foremost in life…”
*On the Praises of the Same*, one book. “Very often, when in…”
*Various Orations*, one book. “Most blessed father, they have been accustomed…”
He also wrote many other things.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Benedictus Stendel, from the town of Halle in Saxony, a German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect and clear in speech, who for many years gloriously taught theology in the school of Erfurt and shone forth distinguished as much in life as in doctrine, wrote certain little works not unworthy of reading. From these he lectured ordinarily in the said university on the following:

*On Genesis*, one book. *On Exodus*, one book.
*On Leviticus*, one book. *On Numbers*, one book.
*On Deuteronomy*, one book. *Various Sermons*, one book.
*Diverse Collations*, one book.
And certain other things in logic, philosophy, and the other studies and scholastic exercises.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Sifridus, titular bishop of Cyrene, of the order of Friars Preachers, a German by nation, vicar in pontifical matters of Theoderic, archbishop of Mainz, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not unacquainted with secular letters, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin languages, subtle in intellect and clear in speech, a keen disputant and outstanding preacher of the divine word, ready in counsel, distinguished in life, wrote certain little works worthy of reading, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are extant: to the aforesaid Archbishop Theoderic,

*On the Usury of the Jews*, one book. “It is inquired in the forum of conscience…”
*On Rents and Redemption*, one book. “It was asked first whether…”
*Sermons and Collations*, one book.
*Various Questions*, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and under Pius and Paul, Roman pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 1470.
