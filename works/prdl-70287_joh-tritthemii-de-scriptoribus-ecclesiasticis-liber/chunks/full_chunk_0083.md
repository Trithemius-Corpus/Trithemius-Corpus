*Abbot of Spanheim.*

He died under the emperor Constantine, son of Constantine, in the year of the Lord 677, in the fifth indiction, on the fifth day before the Nones of July.

Adamannus, priest and abbot of the monastery of Hii, which Saint Columbanus once built on an island of Scotland, a man very devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, cautious in intellect and sweet in eloquence, distinguished in life and conduct, not without a reputation for holiness, was the father of many monks. Among other things, in a sufficiently well-composed style, he wrote a volume

*On the Places of the Holy Land*, one book.

There survives in the library of the abbey of Eberbach part of that book, on the location of Jerusalem,

and certain letters.

He flourished under Justinian the younger, Augustus, in the year of the Lord 690.

Benedict, an abbot in Britain, a man both devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and an outstanding preserver and extender of regular discipline, wrote a distinguished little work for monks, in which, comparing all the rules of the holy fathers with the Rule of our most blessed father Benedict, he shows that the rule of all the others agrees with it, and that it is at variance with none at all. For this reason he entitled his book

*The Concord of the Rules*, one book. He also wrote other things.

He flourished under the emperor Constantine, in the year of the Lord 670.

Aldhelm, bishop of the West Saxons, formerly abbot of the monastery of Malmesbury in Scotia, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a devout man and of exceptional holiness, devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and in secular letters the most learned of all in England in his time, excellent in verse and prose, a most fervent preacher of the divine word, wrote certain instructive volumes in both styles; of these I have found only the following. While he was still abbot, at the command of the synod of his people, he wrote against the error of the Britons a distinguished and useful work:

*On the Celebration of Easter*, one book. “Almighty begetter [unclear]”

One book. “Most reverend man of Christ [unclear]”

One book. Many letters to various persons.
