Anselm, bishop of Lucca, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not at all poorly instructed in secular letters, the tireless co-worker of Pope Gregory VII and an outstanding defender against Guibert, set up under him by Henry, so that he defended all his deeds, sayings, or writings against Emperor Henry by the authority of law and of the Scriptures, left very famous monuments of a great intellect, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant:

*On Jeremiah the Prophet*, 1 book. Also *On the Psalter*, 1 book.
*Against Guibert the Antipope*, 1 book.
And several letters to various persons, 1 book.
He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1080.

Willeram, abbot of Mersburg, of the Order of St. Benedict, German by birth, a man learned in the divine writings and not ignorant of secular letters, practiced in verse and prose: after he had for some time devoted himself to literary study at the University of Paris, he returned to his homeland and was made scholasticus of the church of Bamberg. At length, considering that the glory of the world passes away with time, he despised the world and, for love of Christ, put on the monk in the monastery of Fulda; and afterward, as his merits increased, he was consecrated abbot in the monastery of St. Peter near Mersburg, and by writing made his name known to posterity. Of his little works I have read only a volume composed in meter and verse mixed with prose, with beautiful variety, on the marriage of Christ and the Church.

*On the Song of Songs*, 1 book. “Since the studies of the elders…”
Of the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished under Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1070.

[unclear]

Franco, scholasticus of the church of Liège, German by birth, a man splendidly learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in the discipline of secular letters, a philosopher, astronomer, and distinguished computist, and venerable no less for religion than for knowledge, is said to have composed some treatises on the deeds of writers, which have not yet come to my knowledge. He wrote to Hermann II, archbishop of Cologne, a subtle and excellent work:

*On the Squaring of the Circle*, 1 book. *On the Ecclesiastical Computus*, 1 book.
And many other things. He flourished under Emperor Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1060.
