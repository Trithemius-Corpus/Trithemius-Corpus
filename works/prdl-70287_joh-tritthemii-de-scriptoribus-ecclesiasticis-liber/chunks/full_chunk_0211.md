under Frederick, emperor the third, and Sixtus, pope the fourth, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1473, in the sixth indiction.

Johannes Hagen, otherwise de Indagine, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, of the house of Saint Salvator near Erfurt, prior in Eisenach and in Stettin, a man most devoted to study in the divine Scriptures and very learned, and an excellent doctor in canon law, distinguished in talent, ready and provident in counsel, wrote in plain style many distinguished volumes to princes, bishops, and other prelates of churches, having been questioned on various and diverse matters; of these I recall that few have come into our hands.
