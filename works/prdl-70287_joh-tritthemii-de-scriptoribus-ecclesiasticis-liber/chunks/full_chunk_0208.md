Johannes de Turrecremata, of the Order of Preachers, bishop of Sabina, and cardinal priest of the most holy Roman Church, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, quick in intellect and clear in speech, wrote many little works of his talent, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are reported. To Arsenius, a monk of the monastery of Saint Benedict, which he himself held in commendam, he composed a great work, and one not to be rejected:

On the Rule of Saint Benedict, one book, “Since, with the help of our Lord”;
On the whole Psalter, one book, “To the most blessed father and cle…”;
Questions on the Sunday Gospels, one book, “To the holy and most revered…”;
Questions on the Gospel readings for the saints, one book;
On holy water, one book, “The reverend father recently enjoined me…”;
Sermons on the season, one book, “Divine Scripture teaches us…”;
Sermons on the saints, one book;
and very many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and the supreme pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes of Mechelen, a German by nation, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures, not moderately learned in scholastic philosophy, excellent in intellect, distinguished in knowledge, publicly taught theology for many years in the school of Cologne and acquired great praise for his learning. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

A notable work on the Sentences, one book;
On the Psalter, one book;
Various questions, one book;
Sermons and collationes, one book;
and very many other things.

He flourished in the time of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 146[unclear].

Dionysius Rykel, otherwise de Leuwis, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, of the house of Bethlehem at Roermond, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures and skilled through continual exercise; *The Abbot of Sponheim* skilled also, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, singularly devout, so much so that he was even considered worthy of divine revelations, to whom there are extant several letters of Nicholas of Cusa, a most learned cardinal, wrote so many and such great works that, in number of little works, apart from Augustine, he had no equal among the Latins. He devoted himself to contemplation and prayer with such fervor that one would think he could never have written anything. Again, he was so diligent in writing and reading that one would suppose he could nowhere have been able to pray or contemplate. He slept very little, and his abstinence from food and drink was admirable; after the example of blessed Jerome, he judged it sweet to meditate day and night on the law of the Lord, always either writing something useful or reading, so that sacred reading frequently took its turn.
