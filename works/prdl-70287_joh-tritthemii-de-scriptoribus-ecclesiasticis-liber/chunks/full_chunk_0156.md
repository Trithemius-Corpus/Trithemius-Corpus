Erenus de Boyo, a Briton by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man in every respect most learned, an eminent professor of sacred scripture, of sharp and vigorous talent, quite capable in solving questions of the scriptures, is reported to have written some distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is found, as his people report, an excellent work:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
And Various Questions.
But he is said to have composed certain other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Rupert, of the order of the Preaching Friars, a man learned through long practice in the divine scriptures, a subtle and profound interpreter of scholastic philosophy, an acute disputant, an outstanding declaimer, sharp in talent, scholastic in speech, published certain little works not to be despised; by exercising his talent fruitfully and usefully in them, he made his name known not only to the present but also to the future. Of these there survive:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
In addition, he adorned many books of Aristotle with most elegant commentaries.
He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Stephanus de Ast, of the order of the Friars Minor, a man nobly learned in the divine scriptures, an eminent philosopher and jurist, distinguished in knowledge, profound in talent, cautious in counsel, firm in judgment, composed in speech, is reported to have forged various little works of his excellent talent, by which he made his name known to those present as well as to those to come. Of these I have seen only the necessary work:

A Summary on Cases, 8 books. “Therefore, about to speak in this first [unclear]”
Of the rest I have seen nothing.
He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1550.

Johannes de Alerio, a Toulousain by nation, of the order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the thirteenth prior general, a man zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, and venerable no less for fervor in religion than for learning, governed the Carmelite order vigorously for nine years. He published several distinguished volumes, by which he showed the abundant grace of his talent. Of these there survive:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
A Reading on Ecclesiasticus, 1 book.
And several others.
He was elected general of his order at Montpellier in the general chapter, on the day of Pentecost, under Louis the fourth emperor and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1321, in the fourth indiction.

Johannes de Regno, a Frenchman by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the Toulouse convent, a man learned in the holy scriptures and not ignorant of secular learning, ready in talent, scholastic in speech, outstanding in delivering sermons to the people, published besides many volumes, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
A Lenten Work, 1 book.
The rest, however, which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

Stephanus de Provincia, professor and excellent interpreter of both laws, and sufficiently instructed both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, distinguished in talent and eloquence, whose learning was approved among his contemporaries, wrote certain volumes [not?] to be neglected, by exercising his talent gloriously in which he also transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is an excellent work:

On the Clementines, 1 book.
Various Questions, 1 book.
Of the rest I have found nothing.
He flourished around the times of Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1330.
