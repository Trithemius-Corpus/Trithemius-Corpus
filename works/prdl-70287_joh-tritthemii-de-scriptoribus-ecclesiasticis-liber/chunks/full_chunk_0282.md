*Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

Martinus Gregorius, a physician at Paris, a man skilled in both languages, corrected according to Greek exemplars several translations of Galen's books; among them there is extant Method of Healing, and the following:

Introduction to Pulses.
On the Motion of the Muscles, 2 books.
On the Use of the Parts, 17 books.
On the Faculties of Foods, 3 books.
On the Regimen of Attenuating Diet, 1 book.
He flourished two years ago.

Martinus Polichius Mellerstadius, a most learned professor in the university of Wittenberg thirty years ago, published collected notes of commentaries for a course in logic, on all Aristotle's books of logic and the book On the Six Principles.

Collected notes of the same man's excellent course in physics.

Matthaeus, monk of Aula Regia, a man of notable life and by no means ordinary learning, often disputed at Prague with Ioannes Huss and his followers.

He also wrote on the four books of Peter Lombard.
On the Psalter. On the Gospel of Matthew.
Commentaries on the hymns, and many other things.

Matthaeus Curtius of Pavia, a physician by far the most learned in Italy, an excellent instructor in the old method of healing according to Hippocrates and Galen, wrote on venesection in pleurisy, twice, I think.
He died, as I hear, in the year of salvation 1544.

Matthaeus of Philadelphia, metropolitan at Ephesus, a learned and honorable man, wrote prayers, in the first place laying out a compendious path to repentance, and contributing not a little to the reception of the Lord's body. They were translated by Alardus Aemstelredamus.

Michael Ritius of Naples, skilled in both laws, a very celebrated councillor of the king of the French, composed three books On the Kings of the Franks, and is said to have written very many other volumes, of which these are extant:

On the Kings of Spain, 5 books.
On the Kings of Jerusalem, 1 book.
On the Kings of Naples and Sicily, 4 books.
On the Kings of Hungary, 2 books.
And certain other things.

Mechtildis, a learned and devout virgin of the order of St. Benedict, familiarly united in friendship with her heavenly bridegroom, and renowned for divine revelations, published a book abundantly full of the new wine of the divine sweetness of spiritual grace.

And she flourished in the monastery of Helfta in Eisleben, otherwise Isleben, a town of the county of Mansfeld, around the year of the Lord 1300.

*On the Abbot of Sponheim, On Writers.*

Nemesius, philosopher and bishop, adorned with excellent learning in sacred letters, wrote summary discourses On the Nature of Man. They are extant in Greek in Italy. They have also been printed in Latin, with Georgius Valla as translator.

Nicephorus Blemmydes, who was both monk, solitary, and priest, surnamed [unclear], a man learned in good letters, wrote many and various works, whose titles we have seen; they are these:

An inquiry On the Five Words, and why they are only five, neither more nor fewer.
A Method concerning the Compendious Art of Disputing.
A Summary, or Introductory Epitomes to Logic, 2 books.
Epitome of Philosophy.
A commentary On Sophistical Refutations.
Book of Laws; On the Soul; On the Body.
Thirty-two chapters On Meteors.
On Natural Principles and Causes.
A discourse, What Sort of Man a King Ought to Be.
Canons on Urines.
[unclear Greek title].
A demonstration that life is not predetermined for individuals.
Forty-one chapters; I do not know on what subject.
And he is said to have elucidated many other things.

Nicolaus Beraldus Aurelius, a man most ardently devoted to more refined literature, wrote a very elegant dialogue in which certain methods are explained by which the ability to speak extemporaneously can be acquired, and also concerning that extemporaneous faculty itself.

He composed an elegant oration On Old and New Jurisprudence, with a learned exhortation to the reading and study of the ancients.

He is also said to have written on Politian's Nutricia.

He flourished in the year of salvation 1533.
