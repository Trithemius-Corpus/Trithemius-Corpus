Jacobus Faber Stapulensis, a most celebrated philosopher of our age, the unique ornament of Belgium, indeed of all Gaul, given in our time by a certain divine gift as a remedy for the republic of letters, rescued all parts of philosophy from the murky barbarism of certain sophists. And those things which by now had altogether disdained to speak Latin, and had gone far away from the mind of Aristotle and of the rest who truly philosophize, he restored to their former majesty both of language and of doctrine, with such structure and sequence that nothing is lacking that should be added, and nothing offers itself to be removed without fault; with so great industry, so much art in all his works, so great dignity in speaking, that anyone even moderately learned, together with philosophy, could most richly attain eloquence, which had not been possible before. For he was the first among the Gauls, as Cicero was among the Romans, to join philosophy, still rough and unpolished, with eloquence. For what had previously been wandering as a stranger at Paris he endowed with citizenship. Hence those who are untrained not only in philosophy but also in learning think that they have acquired something, both for judging and for adjudging. For in speaking he is lofty, in his thoughts weighty, in invention refined, in composition careful and exacting, and [unclear] not only his own resources but also those of others. To this one purpose indeed he seems to have been born: to look after the literary [palaestra?], restore the good arts, and bring them back to their ancient splendor. Such is this man's care, such his delight, this the study toward which all my thought directs itself, toward which his mind, toward which his spirit directs itself. So that this might be done more freely, he not only, like Demetrius, gave over to his own people the patrimony, not to be despised, which he had at Etaples, but also cast aside dignities, offices, benefices, and honors,

Other, greater works in the faculty of the liberal arts are indeed awaited. But not content with these, this most religious man has directed his mind to divine matters, and has given himself wholly to them; he is about to bring a great ornament and aid to our theologians, searchers of the divine word, as one who explains what is tangled, illuminates what is obscure, unties what is knotted, and everywhere restores what is broken and mutilated with his craftsman's skill. This can easily be perceived from the things which, in this most sacred discipline of divine wisdom, he has either translated, or written, or caused to be printed more correctly and more accurately. They are written below:
