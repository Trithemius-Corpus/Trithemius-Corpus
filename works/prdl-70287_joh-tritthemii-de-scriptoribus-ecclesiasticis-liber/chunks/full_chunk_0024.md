Tatian, however, wrote almost countless volumes, though they have not come to my knowledge. For because of the teacher's error, even his writings, despised, perished. There survives, with Jerome as witness, a distinguished volume of his, which he entitled *Against the Gentiles*, book 1. He flourished under Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 170.

Philip, bishop of Crete, that is, of the city of [Gortyna?], whom Dionysius, bishop of the Corinthians, placed above, mentions in his letter which he had written to the church of that same city, a noble and learned man, published for the benefit of believers volumes sufficiently instructive, as they say, which have by no means come into our hands. There survives one book of his, *Against Marcion*. He also wrote certain letters to various persons and various treatises, which have still by no means come into our hands. He flourished in the year of the Lord 170, in the times of the emperor Marcus Antoninus Verus.

Modestus, a man indeed of flourishing talent, although it is unknown to us what dignity or rank he held, wrote, as blessed Jerome witnesses, little works not contemptible in their reading, in which the abundant vein of his talent is seen. Of these there survives a distinguished volume, *Against Marcion*, book 1. The rest are unknown to us. He flourished under the rulers Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius Commodus, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 175.

Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon in Gaul after the triumph of blessed Photinus the martyr, formerly a hearer of Polycarp the martyr, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and very learned, sent as legate to Rome under Pope Eleutherius, was approved as most illustrious no less in holiness than in doctrine.
