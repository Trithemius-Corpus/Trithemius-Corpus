Julian, bishop of Campania, a man of sharp and vehement talent, learned in the holy Scriptures, trained in Greek and Latin speech, before he revealed in himself the impiety of Pelagius, was distinguished among the doctors of the Church. Afterwards, however, [embracing?] that man’s heresy, he wrote against the holy doctors little works suited to his own opinion, of which there are extant:

*Against St. Augustine*, 4 books. *Against the Same*, another 7 books.

*A Dialogue of the Disputation of the Parties*, 1 book.

And many other things, all of which, because of the author’s errors, have rightly been rejected from the ecclesiastical library. In a time of famine and distress, he distributed all his possessions in alms to the poor; he associated many noble religious men, deceived under the appearance of piety, with his heresy.

He flourished under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Innocent, the first pope of that name, a learned and most holy man, Alban by nation, the forty-second after blessed Peter, presided over the universal Church for fifteen years, and gloriously ordained many things for its benefit. To him, while he was still a priest, blessed Jerome composed the history of the woman struck seven times, as the prologue of that same work shows. Since he was most learned in the divine Scriptures, he wrote several excellent little works for the instruction of the faithful, of which I have found only these:

*A Decree against the Pelagians*, 1 book.

*Against the Bonosiaci*, 1 book. *We Have Long Wondered*.

Many letters to various persons, 1 book. *In Inquiring about These Things*.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He died in the year of the Lord 417, in the fifteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Kalends of August.

Eusebius of Cremona, a monk and disciple of blessed Jerome the priest, a studious and learned man, venerable in life and conduct, continually intent on holy prayers and divine readings after the example of his most reverend master, attained a great reputation for holiness. He wrote to Damasus, bishop of Porto, and Theodosius, senator of the city of Rome, a sufficiently elegant and lengthy volume on the passing of St. Jerome, 1 book: *To the Most Reverend Damasus*. He flourished in the time of St. Jerome, in the year of the Lord 420.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Possidonius the African, bishop of Calama, once a hearer and disciple of blessed Augustine, a man studious in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, having lived on familiar terms with St. Augustine for many years, shone forth brightly among ecclesiastical prelates in life and doctrine. Among other things, for the edification of the faithful, he wrote:

*The Life of Saint Augustine*, 1 book. *At the Inspiration of the Maker of All Things*.

*An Index of His Books*. *Against the Pagans Concerning* [unclear].

He flourished under the emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 430.

Siricius, pope, Roman by nation, sat after Damasus in the pontificate for fifteen years, a learned and holy man, a very keen defender of the Catholic faith. All the Manichaeans whom he found in the city he condemned to exile. Converts, however, for the doing of penance, he relegated to a monastery until the end of life. Among other things, he wrote to Himerius, bishop of Tarragona:

*A Decretal Work*, 1 book.

*Against Jovinian and Auxentius*, 1 book.

He died under the emperors Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 398, in the sixth indiction, on the ninth day before the Kalends of March.

Fastidius, bishop of the Britons, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, an excellent preacher of the word of God, illustrious also in life and conduct, distinguished in speech and talent, wrote several devout little works. Among them a certain volume of sound Christian doctrine is said to be addressed to Fatalis, 1 book. *On Preserving Widowhood*, 1 book.

He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 420.

Coelestine, the first pope of that name, Roman by nation, a learned and holy man, by whose order blessed Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, held at Ephesus a synod of two hundred fathers, in which Nestorius and Pelagius were condemned with their wicked doctrines. There too it was decreed that the most blessed Virgin Mary should henceforth be called Mother of God. Blessed Coelestine wrote, among other things, a solemn decree:

*Against Nestorius*, 1 book. *Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.

And certain other things.

He died under the emperor Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 435, in the tenth year of his pontificate, in the first indiction, on the Ides of April.
