*Abbot of Sponheim.*

[unclear], and in the regions of Gascony, and in the other lands and kingdoms, preaching the Cross against the infidel schismatics, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known. Of these there are extant:

Against the Lollards, one book. Also On the Schism, one book.
On Some Psalms, one book. Sermons de tempore, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book. And certain others.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Gerard of Zutphen, German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant also of secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and to be preferred no less for his manner of life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, published certain little works pertaining to the instruction of devout brothers, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

On Interior Reformation, one book, "A certain man descend[ed?]."
On Spiritual [Ascension?], one book, "Blessed is the man whose..."
Various sermons, one book.

John de [Hildinio?], of the order of the brethren of the Hospital of St. John of Jerusalem, a man learned in the divine writings, not ignorant of secular philosophy, an eminent doctor of sacred Scripture, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, sharp in intellect and composed in eloquence, published many excellent volumes and delivered several elegant lectures in the University of Paris. Of these the following are extant:

On the Gospel of Mark, one book, "He was made like a lion."
On the Gospel of John, one book.
On the Epistle to the Romans, one book.
On the Epistle to the Corinthians, one book.
On the Epistle to the Galatians, one book.
On the Epistle to the Ephesians, one book.
On the Epistle to the Philippians, one book.
On the Epistle to the Colossians, one book.
On the Epistles to the Thessalonians, one book.

*On Ecclesiastical Writers.*

On the Epistles to Timothy, two books.
On the Epistle to Titus, one book.
On the Epistle to Philemon, two books.
On the Epistle to the Hebrews, one book.
On the Annunciation of the Lord, one book, "Hail Mary, full of grace."
Various sermons, one book, "Instruct your son, because as..."
And many other things.

He is said also to have written commentaries and lectures on many books of the Bible, which have not yet at all come into our hands.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Philip of Monte [Calerio?], of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man practiced in the sacred Scriptures, a great and outstanding preacher of the word of God, wrote for the usefulness of those who serve in declamations little works not to be scorned; by nobly exercising his talent in them, he also destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only the following:

Sermons de tempore, one book, "There will be signs in the sun and [moon]."
Sermons on the Saints, one book.
Lenten sermons, one book.

He is said besides to have composed various other treatises and to have explained some of the divine books, but none of them has reached me.

John, surnamed Grossus, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, the nineteenth prior general - some have the twenty-first - a native of Toulouse, a studious and learned man, wrote in plain speech volumes not to be despised; by exercising his talent magnificently in them, he made his name known also to posterity. Of these I have seen only the excellent work which, containing the institution and progress of the Carmelite order, is entitled Viridarium, three books, "By what things [unclear]."
His sermons, one book, and certain other things are also reported.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1400.
