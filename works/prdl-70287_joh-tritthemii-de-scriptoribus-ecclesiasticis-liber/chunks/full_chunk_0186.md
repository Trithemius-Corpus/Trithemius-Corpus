Johannes de Aurbach, a German by nationality, priest of the church of Bamberg, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, published little works of no slight authority, by which he preserved the memory of his name for posterity. Of these I have seen only the distinguished work:

On the Sacraments of the Church, 1 book, “To the praise of God, of souls ...”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished in the city of Bamberg and wrote various little works.

Dionysius de Burgo sancti sepulchri, of the order of the hermit brothers of the divine father Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, subtle in talent and eloquent in an accomplished style, wrote little works not to be despised in each branch of literature, by which he left to posterity lasting monuments of his talent. Among them the following are reported:

Very beautiful and elegant commentaries:
On Valerius Maximus, 1 book.
On the Metamorphoses, 1 book.
On the works of Virgil, 1 book.
On the tragedies of Seneca, 1 book.
On the Politics, 1 book.
On the Rhetoric, 1 book.
And certain other works.

Moreover he reduced all fables to a pleasing tropological sense.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1412.

Petrus de Aliaco, bishop of Cambrai and cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, formerly chancellor of the Paris academy, teacher of Johannes de Gerson, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, especially in Aristotelian philosophy, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence, and also an outstanding declaimer of sermons, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name and talent. Of these I have found the following:

On the Sentences, 2 books, “What doctrine is this ...”
On the Praise of Holy Scripture, 1 book, “Upon this rock I shall build ...”
On the Beginning of Mark, 1 book, “What doctrine is this ...”
On the [unclear] of the Church, 3 books, “Whether the church of Peter ...”

Nicolaus Clemengis, archdeacon of Bayeux, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, having obtained the mastership of sacred page in the Paris academy, gave great evidence of his learning. He wrote many distinguished volumes in both styles, by which he left the memory of his most brilliant talent. Among them the following are reported:

To the lord Petrus de Aliaco, bishop of Cambrai, the aforesaid cardinal, a notable work which he entitled:

On the Fruit of the Hermitage, 1 book.
On the Corrupt State of the Church, 1 book.
On New Feast Days, 1 book.
On the Matter of the Schism, 1 book.
