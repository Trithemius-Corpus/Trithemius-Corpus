Hambaldus, a Roman by nationality, professor of sacred theology and an outstanding interpreter of the Scriptures, subtle and forceful in intellect, and quite capable in disputing and solving their difficult questions, is said to have written certain excellent little works for the instruction of his disciples, by which he made his name known. Among these there are extant: On the Sentences, one book. Quodlibets, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolph, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Henry of Ghent, archdeacon of Tournai, German by nationality, a man among all the doctors of his time most learned in the divine Scriptures, and very subtle in Aristotelian philosophy, was of such reputation and authority in the University of Paris that he was called the Solemn Doctor throughout the whole Christian world. For he was subtle in intellect and most acute in scholastic disputations, as his excellent works testify. Of these I have found only the following at present:

Summa of Theology, one book. On the Sentences, two books. To the Sacred Virgins, beginning “Concerning virginity,” one book. On Penance, one book. On the Metaphysics, three books. On the books of the Physics, five books. On Metaphysics, four books. He indeed composed many other things, but they have not come to my knowledge.

He died under Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1293, in the sixth indiction, on the feast of the apostles Peter and Paul, and was buried at Tournai in the church of Saint Mary, Mother of God.

Hugolinus, patriarch of Constantinople, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man trained by long study in the divine writings and sufficiently learned, subtle in intellect, almost scholastic, noteworthy in character and life, is said to have written certain excellent little works, of not contemptible reading, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following, among others, are reckoned the better ones:

On the Sentences, three books. Quodlibets, six books. On the Difference of Idioms, one book. Sermons on the Season, one book. Sermons on the Saints, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Arlotus, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, Tuscan by nationality, a man excellently learned in the divine writings and sufficiently instructed in secular philosophy, a subtle dialectician and a most acute disputant, who won great praise for his learning by writing variously, was of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people. He is said to have assembled the Concordances of the New and Old Testament into one volume, one book. Various Sermons, one book.

And certain other things. He flourished in the times of Emperor Adolf, in the year 1290.

*The Abbot of Sponheim*

James, bishop of Genoa, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man trained in the divine Scriptures, who because of his frequent citations of them was called Vorago, quick in intellect and clear in speech, was quite capable in declaiming sermons to the people.
