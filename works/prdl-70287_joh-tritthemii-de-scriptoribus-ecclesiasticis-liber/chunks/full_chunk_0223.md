Tillmannus, of the order of the regular canons of Saint Augustine, provost of the monastery of the holy martyr Christopher in Ravengirsburg, at the edge of the diocese of Mainz, near the Hercynian forest, which from the river Rhine extends its tail toward the Mediomatrices and the Treveri, in a place which from antiquity is called Recesius of the Huns, a German by nation, studious in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, wrote, in a simple and plain style for the edification of the brethren, certain little works full of devotion and charity, in which he exercised his talent. Of these the following are extant:

On the Spiritual Vineyard, one book.
On the Instruction of Novices, one book.
And certain small exercises.

He died old and full of days, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1485, in the third indiction.

Henricus Arnoldi, a Saxon by nation, by profession a Carthusian monk of the Basel house, a man devout in religion, distinguished in talent, sincere in affection, weighty in morals, sweet in speech, affable in conduct, pious, gentle, shining in example, very much exercised in sacred letters and not ignorant of pontifical law; when for many years he held the office of notary in the holy Council of Basel, he was approved by the testimony of all as faithful, just, and prudent. At length, seeing that all things are vanity, he despised everything for the love of Christ, choosing and entering the Carthusian enclosure; there, appointed prior of the same house, for many years he laudably ruled in peace and tranquility the monks subject to him, the conversi, and the rest, treating them with paternal love. He wrote many very devout and salutary little works, of which those that have come to our knowledge are set out below:

Many devout sermons on various saints, one book.
Several sermons, one book.
Histories of various saints, one book.

Jodocus de Calwe, a German by nation, a Swabian by homeland, a man exercised by long study in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of scholastic philosophy, prompt and forceful in talent, was for many years held in esteem in the school of Heidelberg by lecturing and preaching, where also, according to custom, for some time he held the office of preacher in the church of the Holy Spirit. He wrote certain treatises, as they say, not to be rejected, but they have not come to the knowledge of our reading. There are extant from him various sermons on diverse subjects, one book; A Vocabulary of Preachers, one book; and various treatises.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1480. And under Pope Innocent VIII he died before this three-year period.

Nicolaus de Crutzenach, a German by nation, from the diocese of Mainz and the county of Sponheim, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, distinguished in life, an acute disputant and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, who for many years publicly taught theology in the school of Vienna, and both among the scholars there and with Emperor Frederick III obtained great praise and reward for his learning, wrote certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity.
