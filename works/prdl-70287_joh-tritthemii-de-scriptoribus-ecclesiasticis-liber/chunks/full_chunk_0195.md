Petrus Paulus Vergerius, by country a Justinopolitan, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters, a philosopher and very celebrated rhetorician, perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin speech, once a hearer of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher teaching Greek letters at Venice, wrote certain distinguished little works of excellent talent, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Among these there is extant a notable work, containing, concerning the deeds of the lords of Carrara:

A very beautiful History, 1 book.

On Noble Manners, 1 book.

In Praise of Saint Jerome, 1 book.

And many other things.

He flourished at the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Maphaeus Vegius, by country a man of Lodi, datary of Pope Martin V, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and outstandingly learned, and very learned in secular letters, a very celebrated orator and poet, and no less distinguished in conduct than in knowledge; through whose care and expense the remains of Saint Monica, mother of Saint Augustine, were translated from Ostia Tiberina to Rome and were laid in a new chapel built at great cost by him, in which he himself also was buried: he wrote, both in meter and in prose, certain distinguished volumes, among which there is extant a very celebrated work, necessary for all religious persons, composed from divine and human literature, addressed to certain sisters, which he entitled:

On Perseverance, 7 books. “Since grave matters often, as happens”

On the Penitential Psalms, 1 book.

In Praise of Saint Monica, 1 book.

The Thirteenth Book of the Aeneid, in heroic verse, 1 book.

Many Epigrams, 1 book.

On Military Affairs,

A Dialogue, which is called Philaletes,

Many Letters.

And very many other things.

*ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Johannes Placth, a German by nation, ornament and pillar of the Heidelberg school, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly instructed in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life and conduct insofar as the secular estate permits, an outstanding teacher in delivering sermons to the people: among other things he wrote a great and notable volume, which he lectured on regularly and publicly in the aforesaid University of Heidelberg:

On the first [unclear] of the Decretals, 1 book. “Venerable fathers”

Against the Bohemians, 1 book. “As the wise man says”

Questions on the Sentences, 1 book.

Several Sermons.

Various Questions.

And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1439.

Johannes Dieppurg, called de Franckfordia, a German by nation, a doctor of Heidelberg, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, a keen disputant and an outstanding expounder of sermons, formerly chaplain and secretary of Ludwig, duke of Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine, and often an envoy in many difficult cases: he wrote little works of reading not to be scorned, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

Against the Hussites, 1 book. “When recently in the year of the Lord”

Against the Feymeros, 1 book. “The law of the Lord outwardly [unclear]”

On Contracts, 1 book. “As in the forum of the soul where in”

On Predestination, 1 book. “Glory to God in the highest”

Many Sermons, 1 book.

An Oration before the King of England, 1 book.

An Oration before the Duke of Savoy, 1 book.

An Oration before the King of France, 1 book.

Various Questions, 1 book.

Several Letters,

Diverse Orations.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1430.
