Of the same author: four Eclogues, and certain Distichs.

Raphael Volaterranus, a man of varied learning, widely extending among others, carefully wrote thirty-eight books of the *Urban Commentaries*.

The same man published one book, *On the Office of a Prince and Leader*, and gave Latin form to not a few books of both sacred and profane authors; among these the following are said to have been translated by him:

Xenophon’s *Oeconomicus*.

Gregory Nazianzen’s *Monody*.

Ten short orations of Basil, and the *Institutes of Monks*.

Two books of Procopius of Caesarea *On the Persian War*, and the same number *On the Vandalic War*.

He flourished in the time of Julius II, Supreme Pontiff.

Raymundus of Barcelona, chaplain and penitentiary of Gregory IX, Supreme Pontiff, was a man most skilled in theology and pontifical law; with his assistance, as Platina testifies, he compiled the Decretals. He also wrote a *Summa* called *On Penance and Betrothals*.

Raymundus Lullius, a man learned in medical matters and long exercised by study in sacred and divine letters, became a hermit, and afterward an outstanding preacher of the divine word; he wrote many things in theology.

And various other little works; those which we have seen are these:

*On the Praises of Mary*, book 1.

A wondrous and very celebrated work *On Fevers*.

*New Metaphysics*, and a *Remonstrance of Philosophy against the Averroists*.

*Proverbs*, 3 books.

*On the Cabalistic Art*, or *Introduction to All the Sciences*, 4 books.

*Isagoge into Rhetoric*.

*Astrology*.

*On the Secrets of Nature*, or the fifth essence, which they commonly call distillation, 2 books.

*Lily of Understanding*.

*The Great General and Ultimate Art*, the attainer and key-bearer of whatever arts and sciences.

He flourished in the year of Christ 1311.

Richard, a brother of the Order of Preachers, a Florentine by homeland, about 200 years ago a man of no contemptible learning, when he was traveling in Asia, learned the Qur’an and translated the Arabic language; and against it he published a commentary, which Demetrius Cydonius afterward rendered into the Greek tongue.

*On the Abbot of Spanheim, On Writers.*

Richardus Perusinus, a wonderfully ardent lover of more polished literature, composed twelve books of the *Austriad* in not inelegant hexameter verse.

He flourished under Maximilian Augustus.

Robertus Ketenensis, otherwise Remensis, a Briton from the Anglo-Saxons, and finally archdeacon of Pamplona, translated into the Latin language the four books of the Qur’an, reduced into an epitome, from Arabic.

At the urging of Peter, abbot of Cluny, the authentic codex of the doctrine of Muhammad was translated by the same man.

He flourished in the time of the emperor Adelfonsus.

Rudbertus of Mosham, a distinguished doctor and dean of Passau, counselor of Ferdinand, king of the Romans, published a book whose first inscription is as follows:

*The Little Synod of Nuremberg, Roman, Not German*, which Rudbertus of Mosham celebrated with the ecclesiastical senate of Nuremberg, with the highest zeal for piety and truth, for the sake of correcting and amending Lutheranism. In it at last the great Babylonian whore, and her firstborn Antichrist, sitting and ruling in the doctrine and church of the Lutherans, is revealed and slain with the sword of the spirit, and [unclear] reply is made to the letter of the Nuremberg ecclesiastics.

A book by the same man *On the Supreme Article of Justification*, otherwise *On Faith the Monarch, the Nun*.

*Considerations, Axioms*, that just as penance ought not to be separated from the remission of sins, nor the word Charity from faith, nor the cross from the Gospel, so also the law ought not indiscriminately to be separated from justification.

He flourished in the year of Christ 1540.

Robertus Stephanus, a Frenchman by nation, a Parisian printer, in this age has offered to students very many books from his press, worked out with elegant type and with the greatest correctness.
