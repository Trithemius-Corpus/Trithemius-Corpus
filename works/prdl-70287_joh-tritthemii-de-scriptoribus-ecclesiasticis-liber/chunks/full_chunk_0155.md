*Abbot of Spanheim.*

learned in the Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, ready in talent, distinguished in eloquence, no less reverend for his manner of life than for his knowledge, of great reputation in delivering sermons to the people; and, since he most vigorously defended the liberty of the Church against tyrants, he was at last pierced by an arrow by the lord of Carrara and was crowned with martyrdom. He wrote very useful and elegant works:

Sermons for the season, one book. “The desired one will come [unclear]”
Sermons on the saints, one book. “But far be it from me to glory”
And certain other things.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.

William Ockham, English by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, formerly a disciple of John Scotus, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly taught in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain small works on the Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Acting and writing against John XXII, who condemned the heresy of the Fraticelli, together with Peter of Piceno, whom Emperor Louis had set up as an idol and had caused to be called Nicholas V, and declaring him to be a heretic, he was excommunicated by him. Fleeing to Emperor Louis, likewise excommunicated, he said to him: “O Emperor, defend me with the sword, and I shall defend you with the word.” And so he remained excommunicated with the excommunicated man until the end of his life; at last, at his death, he was absolved. He was a most bitter assailant of Scotus, his master, and attacked almost all his sayings. He wrote:

Against John XXII on the poverty of Christ and the apostles
An Apology, one book.
On the Sentences, four books.
Quodlibets, also one book.
On the Supporters of Heretics, seven books. “In all curious matters”
On the Sacrament of the Altar, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320. And he was buried in the convent of his order in the province of Noricum, which is now called Bavaria, of which Emperor Louis had previously been duke.

Alvarus, of the Order of Friars Minor, Spanish by nation, a hearer of John Scotus, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, held in esteem by John XXIII, acted against William Ockham the Englishman in writings and in words. He published certain very distinguished volumes of his talent, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there survives a great and notable work, describing the defects of ecclesiastical persons, which he entitled:

On the Lamentation of the Church, one book.
Likewise The Mirror of Kings, one book. “In the name of our Lord Jesus”
On the Sentences, four books.
An Apology, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished at the same time as Ockham, under Emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Matthew Silvaticus, of Mantua by homeland, noble by birth, a physician by art and profession, in which faculty in his time he was held to be both learned and experienced before many others, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote in that science volumes not to be despised, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only the notable work, addressed to Robert, king of Sicily and a physician, on all simple medicines and their properties, which he entitled:

Pandects, one book. “Aaron in Greek, in Arabic”
I have seen nothing of the rest.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

A certain hearer and disciple of John Scotus in the schools of Paris, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and subtly taught in secular philosophy, and also not ignorant of both pontifical and imperial law, is said to have composed certain distinguished little works, by which, exercising his talent, he also made his name known to posterity; but I have seen none of them. They are said to be:

On the Sentences, one book.
Disputed Questions, one book.
And certain other things.
