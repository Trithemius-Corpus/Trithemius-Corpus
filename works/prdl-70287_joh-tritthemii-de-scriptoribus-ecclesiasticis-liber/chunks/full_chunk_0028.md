*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

The eighth is entitled, one book. *On Fate, against the Astrologers*, one book. He flourished at Rome under the emperor Alexander, in the year of the Lord 230.

Beryllus, bishop of Bostra in Arabia, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally instructed in secular letters, after he had governed the church gloriously for some time, at last fell into the heresy of the Paulianists, who denied that Christ existed before Mary; but, corrected by Origen, he wrote many things, of which there survives a *Dialogue* held among them, one book; *Letters to Various Persons*, one book; and certain other works. He flourished under the emperor Alexander, in the year of the Lord 254.

Hippolytus, bishop of a certain city whose name Jerome did not specify, certainly a man both studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, wrote many and almost incredible volumes, so that Ambrose, of whom we have already made mention above, used to urge Origen to imitate him, and strongly encouraged him to expound the divine Scriptures, providing him, as we have said, with seven copyists, the same number of very swift girls for writing, and a considerable number of book-copyists, with their costs and expenses. You would have seen a beautiful and delightful contest, in which each of the two greatly desired to surpass the other in the publication of books. The following are reported among the works of Hippolytus: *On the Hexameron*, six books; *On the Apocalypse*, one book; *On Genesis*, one book; *On the Proverbs of Solomon*, one book; *On Exodus*, one book; *On Ecclesiastes*, one book; *On the Song of Songs*, one book; *On the Antichrist*, one book; *On the Prophet Zechariah*, one book; *Against Marcion*, one book; *On Daniel*, one book; *Against All Heresies*, three books. He also published many and varied treatises on the Psalms, as well as homilies and sermons almost innumerable, and many other works still unknown to us. He flourished under the prince Alexander, in the year of the Lord 220.

Alexander, bishop of Cappadocia, when out of desire for the holy places he was traveling to Jerusalem, was there, by divine revelation, ordained bishop after Saint Narcissus. Illustrious for much holiness and learning, he shone forth, and at last, for the faith of Christ, under Decius, a most impious tyrant, completed his martyrdom. He wrote many elegant letters to various persons, among which the following are chiefly reported: *On the Peace of the Church, to the Aaronites*, one letter; *For Origen against Demetrius*, one letter; *To the Antiochenes*, a letter; *To Origen*, several letters.
