Georgius of Peuerbach, German by nation, a man most skilled in the knowledge of secular letters and not ignorant of the sacred scriptures, easily the most brilliant philosopher and astronomer of all in his time, who for a long period publicly taught philosophy and the science of the stars in the University of Vienna, where among countless other pupils he instructed Johannes de Regio Monte, afterwards a most learned man: he wrote certain distinguished little works of keen intellect, of the number of which I have seen only:

*The New Theorics of the Planets*, one book. “The sun has three orbs ...”

Of the others I have as yet seen nothing.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pius and Paul, Roman pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Domitius Calderinus, of Verona by homeland, a most brilliant rhetorician, thoroughly imbued with the disciplines of all the good arts, outstandingly skilled in Greek and Latin speech, subtle in intellect and eloquent in expression: he wrote many distinguished little works in humane letters, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among them the following are reported:

*On Juvenal*, one book.
*On Martial*, one book.
*On Suetonius*, one book.
*On Ovid’s Ibis*, one book.
*On the Letters of Cicero*, one book.
*Against Brotheus the Grammarian*, one book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.
*Various Orations*.
And several other works.

He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Sixtus IV, whose secretary he had been, in the year of the Lord 1477, indiction 10.

Johannes Tinctoris, German by nation, of Cologne by homeland, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, and nobly learned in secular philosophy, sharp in intellect and an outstanding disputant, who publicly taught theology for a long time in the University of Cologne and acquired great praises for his learning: he wrote little works not to be despised, among which the following are reported:

He began to reconcile the disagreements of Thomas and Scotus, in one book.
*Questions on the Sentences*, one book. Col[unclear]

*Abbot of Sponheim.*

Also various *Collations*. And certain other things.

He flourished in the aforesaid university under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Nicolaus Perottus of Sassoferrato, and bishop of Siponto, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, and in secular letters second to none in his time, an outstanding theologian and most eloquent rhetorician, keen in intellect and distinguished in life: he wrote certain brilliant little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these the following are extant:

A notable work in two volumes, on the poet Marcus Valerius Martial, very beautifully comprising all the elegance, method, and explanation of the Latin language, which he entitled *A Volume of Commentaries on the Latin Language*, or, as it is commonly called, the *Cornucopia*, two books.
“Barbara etc. Blādit” [unclear]
*On the Kinds of Metres*, one book.
Orations and various letters.

He is said also to have written many other things besides, both in divine letters and in humane letters, and to have translated several works from Greek into Latin, but they have not come into our hands.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pius II and Paul II, Roman pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 147[unclear].

Antonius Rosellus, otherwise Roxellus, Tuscan by nation, an excellent professor of both laws and a very celebrated interpreter, and greatly adorned with the discipline of the other honorable arts, outstanding in intellect and eloquent in expression, who taught law for many years in the University of Padua: he wrote many distinguished little works, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. But I do not remember ever having seen any of them anywhere. Yet his notable work is reported:

*On the Monarchy of the World*, one book.
Various treatises on the laws. And several other works.

He also wrote on successions and on the legitimation of bastards.

He died under Frederick III, emperor, in vigorous old age, buried at Padua, in the year of the Lord 1467, indiction 15.
