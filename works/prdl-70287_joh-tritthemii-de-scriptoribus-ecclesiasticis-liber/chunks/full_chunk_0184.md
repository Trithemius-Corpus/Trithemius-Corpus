John Capreolus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, by country a Toulousain, a man learned in sacred writings, excellently trained in the disciplines of Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect, sufficiently fit for disputation, a most zealous champion and defender of the sayings of St. Thomas Aquinas, wrote very distinguished memorials of his talent, by which he made his name known. Of these many volumes are extant:
On the Sentences, 4 books.
And various sermons, 1 book.
And certain other works.
He flourished under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1415.

Henry of Coesfeld, by nation a German, of the Carthusian Order, prior of the house of St. Mary near Mount St. Gertrude in Holland, a man most learned in divine scriptures, devout in life and conduct, of great reputation, subtle in intellect, clearer in speech, and also a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, wrote many excellent volumes, the frequent reading of which is judged useful for devout brothers who love their salvation. Of these the following are reported:
On Exodus Mystically, 1 book.
On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, 1 book.
On the Three Substantial Vows, 1 book.
Do Not Love the World.
Against the Vice of Possession, 1 book.
On the Annunciation of the Lord, 1 book.
A Eulogy of Paul the Hermit, 1 book.
The Mystical Circumcisorium, 1 book.
On the Sacrament of the Altar, 1 book.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book.
And several other works.

The work of William, bishop of Paris, On the Bodily and Incorporeal or Spiritual Universe, which surveys it with wonderful industry, he beautifully brought to a conclusion in a brief compendium. He died in the house of the Valley of Grace near Bruges, beneficially occupied in the act of visitation, in the times of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 140[unclear], in the seventh indiction, on the Ides of July.

Henry of Hachenburg, of the Order of Friars Preachers, by nation a German, a man magnificently exercised and well instructed in divine writings, of excellent talent and very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, wrote volumes not to be rejected for the guidance of simple priests. Of these there are extant:
Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, 1 book. “I shall come and heal.”
Sermons on the Saints, 2 books.
He flourished, as his associates report, in the convent at Koblenz, where he composed various works.

[unclear] Jordanus, of the Order of the Friars of St. Augustine of the Hermitage, by nation a German, a devout man and an excellent expounder and interpreter of the divine scriptures, of most excellent talent in delivering sermons, wrote several elegant and devout treatises useful to the brothers, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, however, few have come into our hands. The following are reported:
On the Apocalypse of John, 1 book.
An Apology for His Order, 1 book.
On the Passion of the Lord: “Look and do according to...”
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
A Lenten Book: “Behold, he will give to his voice...”
Various Questions, 2 books.
And many other works.
He flourished under King Rupert, in the year of the Lord 1410.

[unclear] Peter, bishop of Novigrad, by nation an Italian, from the town of Bruniquellus, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man most learned in divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, published several useful little works, by which, through his magnificently exercised talent, he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported:
A certain notable work, in which he arranged all the histories of the New and Old Testament in alphabetical order and disposed them according to individual subjects.
On the Sentences, 1 book.
On the Proverbs of Solomon, 2 books.
Also on Ecclesiastes, 2 books.
On the Song of Songs, 2 books.
And many other works.
Moreover, he is said to have adorned almost the whole Bible with excellent commentaries. He flourished under Rupert, king of the Romans, in the year 1410.
