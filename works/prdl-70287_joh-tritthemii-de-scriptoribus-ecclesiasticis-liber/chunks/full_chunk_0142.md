He is also said to have written commentaries on several books of the Bible, which, however, have not yet come into our hands. He is also said to have been the first to arrange concordances of the whole Bible of the New and Old Testament.

Richard of Middleton, English by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently instructed both in philosophy and in canon law, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, subtle and ready in resolving questions and doubts concerning the Scriptures, is said to have written many very distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have found only the following:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books. “He brought forth hidden things ...”
*On the distinctions of the Decretum*, 1 book. “All our fathers under ...”
*On the Order of Judgments*, 1 book.
*On the Epistles of Paul*, 4 books.

It is said, moreover, that he issued elegant commentaries and lectures on several books of sacred Scripture, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished in the times of the emperor Adolf and Pope Nicholas IV, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Nicholas of Bibra, German by nation, a man nobly learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher and distinguished poet, who at Erfurt in his time was held in esteem, acquired great glory for his learning and wrote not a few little works both in meter and in prose, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have seen only a work whose title is hidden, which he composed at Erfurt in verse and in prose:

*On Avoiding Evil*, 1 book. “Hearer of the poem ...”
*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.

He flourished at Erfurt under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

John of Paris, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a theologian most learned in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, since, indeed, having ascended the magisterial chair in both branches of learning in the university of Paris, he taught publicly for a long time and left not a few disciples, is said to have written certain distinguished little works, by which he made himself useful to those then present and afterwards known to those to come. Of these I have found only a work not to be despised:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.

Of the rest I have found nothing. He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

*Abbot of Spanheim*

Nicholas the Frenchman, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, seventh prior general, a learned and holy man, who, after he had ruled his order vigorously for almost twenty years, as far as he was able, yielded his office for love of Christ and returned to the quiet of the hermitage, where he persevered in holy conduct until death, wrote, among other things, a mournful work lamenting the state of his order, when the brothers had been expelled from the Holy Land, which he entitled:

*The Fiery Arrow*, 1 book.

He wrote other things also. He flourished in the times of the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

Matthew of Aquasparta, otherwise Aquasputa, of the Order of Friars Minor, twelfth minister general, and afterwards bishop of Porto and cardinal of the holy Roman Church, a man most learned in these divine Scriptures and, according to the custom of this time, magnificently instructed in secular philosophy, composed certain distinguished little works of excellent talent, by which he made his name known far and wide. Of these the following are extant:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*Inventory of Sentences*, 1 book.
*On the Epistle to the Romans*, 1 book.
*Several Sermons*, 1 book.

And certain other things. He flourished in the times of the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1390.

William Peraldus, French by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and also sufficiently excellently instructed in secular letters, especially in Aristotelian philosophy, ready in talent, scholastic in speech, was of excellent industry in delivering sermons to the people. He issued, in both faculties, several instructive volumes, by which he made his name known not only to those then present, but also to those to come. Of these there are extant:

*Summa of Virtues and Vices*, 2 books.
*On the Instruction of Religious*, 1 book.
*Sermons on the Season*, 1 book.
*Sermons on the Saints*, 1 book.

And very many other things, which have not yet come to my knowledge.
