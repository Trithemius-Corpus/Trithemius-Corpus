*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Of an exhortation to the candidates, one treatise. On the ascension of the Lord, two treatises. On Saint Perpetua and Felicity, one treatise. On Saint Vincent, two treatises. On spiritual almsgiving, one treatise. On bodily alms, one treatise. On Samson, one treatise. On Susanna and Joseph, one treatise. On the dedication of the temple, two treatises. Sermons on various subjects, 200, one book. Letters, more than 200, one book.

There is extant also a certain sermon on the mercy of God at Mount Saint John, a monastery of the Benedictines in the Rheingau.

He published many other works as well; if anyone desires to know them more fully, let him read the list of his books edited by Possidius, from which we have taken many items. Jacobus de Voragine, bishop of Genoa, writes in his treatise which he composed on the books of Augustine that the number of his books, treatises, sermons, and letters rises beyond one thousand thirty. And he concludes thus: And therefore no one has ever been found who was able to find all the books of Augustine. And Possidius says: He lies who declares that he has read you entire, Augustine.

But in this work I have deliberately omitted certain treatises which are ascribed by many to blessed Augustine. Among these is the book *On the Spirit and the Soul*, in whose seventh chapter Boethius is cited, though he was born a long time after Augustine. But the style itself does not have the savor of Augustine. Likewise the book which has now been printed under Augustine’s name, *On True and False Penitence*, is falsely ascribed to him, since in the seventh chapter, toward the end, Augustine himself is cited. The book *On the Conflict of Virtues and Vices*, which begins “The apostolic voice,” echoes the style of Pope Gregory, and agrees with the older copies. Likewise the book *On the Ladder of Paradise*, whose beginning is “When on a certain day,” is in the ancient copies inscribed to blessed Bernard, whose spirit and manner of speech it seems to express.

Why should I strive at length? So many treatises and sermons are falsely inscribed to blessed Augustine that a review of them would require its own volume. Nor is it my place to sit as judge and deprive of their titles things that belong to another. Let the prudent reader carefully consider what is to be done or held by him in these matters.

Saint doctor Augustine died in the city of Hippo when it was being besieged by the Vandals, in the year of the Lord 430, in the thirteenth indiction, in the seventy-sixth year of his age and the fortieth of his priesthood, according to Marianus Scotus, on the fifth day before the Kalends of September, in the third month of the city’s siege. While answering, amid the attacks of the besieging Vandals, the books of Julian the Manichaean at the very end of his days, he persevered gloriously in defense of the Christian faith. He made no testament, because there was nothing from which he might make one
