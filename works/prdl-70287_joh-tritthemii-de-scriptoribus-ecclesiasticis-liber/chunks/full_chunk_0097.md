*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

He flourished under Emperor Arnold, in the year of the Lord 910.

Hildouin, formerly abbot of the monastery of Lobbes, of the order of Saint Benedict, first bishop of Liège, then driven from there, came to Hugh, king of Italy, to whom he was joined by a line of kinship; by him he was appointed bishop of Verona, and at last, after Lambert, was made archbishop of Milan. He ordained the monk Ratherius as bishop for the church of Verona. A man sufficiently learned in the divine Scriptures, he wrote, at the request of the brothers, while he was still abbot:

The Deeds of the Abbots of Lobbes, one book.
Sermons to the Brothers, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished under Hugh, king of Italy, in the times of Conrad, emperor of the Romans and Germans, in the year of the Lord 920.

Ratherius, formerly a monk of the monastery of Lobbes, after Hildouin bishop of Verona, a man learned in the divine writings and highly skilled in the arts of humane learning, which they call liberal, simple in spirit, mild in disposition, and scholastic in eloquence, came with Bishop Hildouin to Hugh the king in Italy. After Hildouin's transfer to the archbishopric of the church of Milan, Ratherius was appointed bishop of the city of Verona; not long afterward he was driven from there by Hugh, because he had admitted into Verona Arnold, duke of Bavaria, Hugh's enemy, and was banished into exile at Pavia. At length, having returned to his church, he was driven out again, I do not know whether for the same reason; and for his own consolation he composed various works in both places, which could no less delight the understandings of readers than profit them. Among these the following are reported:

On His First Exile, one book.
Another, on the Second, entitled Phrenesis, one book.
Another, which is called Inefficax, one book.
On the Body and Blood of the Lord, one book.
Against the Anthropomorphites, one book.
Agonisticon, that is, of Preludes, six books.
Also Confessions, one book.
The Life of Saint Ursmar of Lobbes, one book.

He is also said to have written several other works, which have not come to my knowledge. And at last, as the monk Sigebert writes, having been made bishop of Liège, he was driven from there also. He flourished in the times of Henry, the first emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 930.
