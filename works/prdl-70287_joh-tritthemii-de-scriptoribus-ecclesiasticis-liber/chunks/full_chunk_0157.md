His distinguished volumes testify to this, by which he secured eternal remembrance for his name. Of these I have had the privilege of seeing a few:

On the Decretals, 5 books.
On the Novellae in two forms, 1 book. “Because it is preposterous...”
Additions to the Speculum, 2 books.
The work entitled Mercuriale, 1 book. “I, Johannes And...”
A Summa on betrothals and marriage, 1 book.
On the Clementines, 4 books. “Since frequently in the forum...”
On the praises of Saint Jerome, 1 book. “Johannes Gratiosum...”
On the tree of consanguinity, 4 books. “Jerome continually...”
Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He died at last of the plague and was buried at Bologna, under Charles IV, emperor, and Clement VI, pope, in the year of the Lord 1348, in the first indiction.

Monaldus, of the Order of Friars Minor, a theologian and very learned jurist, subtle in intellect, cautious in knowledge, scholastic in speech, of excellent industry and virtue in delivering sermons to the people, published, for the benefit of the simple, little works not to be despised even by the learned, by which he usefully provided both for those then present and for those to come long afterward. Of these the following are reported:

A necessary and very beautiful Summa of canon law, 4 books. “Since one ignorant...”
Questions on the Sentences, 4 books.
Various sermons, 1 book.
And many other things, which have by no means come to our knowledge.

[Unclear], an Italian by nation, formerly hearer of the very learned man Dinus of Mugello, and a very close friend of Johannes Andreae, a man very skilled in both laws, and also excellently imbued with the disciplines of philosophy, who for many years was a consistorial advocate and acquired great praise and glory for his learning, wrote some distinguished little works in both laws, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

A volume of consilia, 1 book. “In the name of the Lord, Amen...”
Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He is said to have left after him many lectures in civil law, which I have not yet seen.

He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.

[Unclear] Johannes de Blomendal, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and clear in eloquence, was an eminent teacher in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, for the edification of the faithful, volumes not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these I have so far found only:

Sermons on the temporal cycle, 1 book. “Take your weapons, your quiver...”
Sermons on the saints, 1 book.
Sermons for Lent, 1 book.

He flourished under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330.

[Unclear] Guido de Monte Rocherii, a man very studious in the sacred Scriptures and rich in reading of the ancients, clear in intellect, scholastic in speech, wrote little works of his talent not to be despised, by which he made the memory of his name known also among posterity. Of these I have seen only that well-known work circulated in print, which, composing it for the guidance of simple priests, he wished to be entitled:

The Manipulus curatorum, 3 books. “Since, as the prophet says...”

He flourished in the times of Louis IV, emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330.
