*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

*Chronicle of his order*, one book. *On the fall of the impious Roman*, one book. *On Mount Sinai*, one book. *Life of Mechtild*, one book. *Chronicle of Ferrara*, one book. *Various Sermons*, one book.

He also published very many and varied epigrams, which we hope will be published day by day. He also translated from Greek into Latin several sermons of Blessed John Chrysostom, and many other things unknown to us. He is still living at Ferrara, and is writing various works under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494.

Ubertinus Clericus of Crescentino, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, rhetorician, and distinguished poet, and most skilled in both languages, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, who in the very celebrated school of Pavia lectured publicly for seven years, on a stipend, with the greatest attention and benefit of his hearers, wrote many excellent little works both in verse and prose, of which the following are extant:

*On the Letters of Cicero*, sixteen books. “The letters of this volume...”

*On the Duties* of the same, one book.

*On the Metamorphoses of Naso*, one book.

Also *Letters*, one book. *Many Poems*, one book.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I do not know whether he is still living.

Wilhelmus of Aachen, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, who once held the office of preacher in the church of Basel, wrote many commendable little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these he publicly lectured on some in the school of Erfurt, with the attention and praise of all. He wrote:

*On the Gospel of John*, one book.

*On the Canon of the Mass*, one book.

*On the Passion of the Lord*, one book. “Jesus handed over willingly...”

*Disputed Questions*, one book.

*Itinerary of the Holy Land*, one book.

*Various Sermons*, one book.

And several other things.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1483. And I believe that he is still living.

Johannes de Lapide, German by nation, of the Carthusian order, a monk of the Basel house, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct: once, in the school of Paris, he obtained the mastership in the faculty of arts, governed in the same for several years, and, diligently pursuing the study of sacred letters by teaching, lecturing, and disputing, deserved to be adorned with the dignity of doctor in the school of the theologians. He first transferred to the University of Basel the teaching of those Parisians who are called Realists, and there planted, strengthened, and increased it. He was also one of the principal initiators and authors of the studies of Tübingen. Finally, after freely resigning several benefices, he was called to the canonry and office of preaching of the distinguished church of Basel, and at last, after a few years [unclear], leaving everything for the love of Christ, he put on the habit of the sacred Carthusian order. He wrote many excellent little works, by which he made himself useful to the present and memorable to the future. We have obtained copies of few of these. Yet the following are extant:

*Dialogue on the Art of Punctuation*. “The disciple [unclear]...”

*On the Art of Resolving Sophistical Arguments*, one treatise. “Since for every...”

*Explanation of All the Books of Aristotelian Logic*, one book. “To investigate...”

*Explanation of the Books of Physics* of the same.

*Explanation of the Six Books of Metaphysics* of the same, one book. “Because first...”

*On Those Things Which They Require for a Worthy Priest*, one treatise. “Man to the image...”
