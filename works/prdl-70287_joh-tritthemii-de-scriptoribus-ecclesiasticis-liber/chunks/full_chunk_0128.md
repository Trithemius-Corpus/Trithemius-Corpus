Albertus Galeoti, by country a Parmese, a professor of canon and civil law and a famous interpreter, subtle in intellect, cautious in counsel, and scholastic in speech, who, because of his learning and experience in affairs, was counted among the chief doctors by the old jurists, wrote certain minor works in law not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with immortal praise. Of these there survives:

The Summa Margarita of Questions, one book. “When I, Albertus Gal...”
And Declarations of Judgments, one book.
And certain other diverse works, unknown to us, are held by various persons.

Odofredus of Benevento, a hearer of Azo of Bologna and afterwards the teacher of Jacobus Balduini, a man most skilled in both laws, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, who left many distinguished disciples after him, was held in great authority among the ancients because of his teaching and erudition both in law and in the other humane letters. Many minor works of his are reported, by which he made his name known to posterity:

On the Formation of Libels, one book. “After the work of the notary...”
On Judicial Order, one book. “As Christ...”
On the Art of the Notariate, one book.
And other works which I have not seen.
He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1250.

Johannes de Blanoso, Burgundian by nation, a man most learned in both laws and sufficiently instructed in the precepts of the secular philosophers, keen and ready in intellect, wrote in clear speech minor works not to be scorned by the professors of this faculty, by which the fame of his name became known far and wide both to those then present and to those who would come after. Of these there survives a volume of great utility, which he entitled:

On the Actions of Advocates, one book. “I, Johannes de Blanoso...”
And Various Questions.
But the other works which he composed have not come to my knowledge.

Johannes, Teutonic by name and nation, born from the city of Freiburg, bishop of Bosnia in Hungary, of the Order of Preachers, a man much exercised in the divine Scriptures, learned in law, and excellently skilled in secular philosophy, who because of his learning and holiness of life had been taken from the order to the episcopate, which he later abandoned and returned to his order, and after Raymond was appointed master general of the order, did many good things. He wrote a notable Summa for Confessors, five books. “Since new matters of doubts...”
And certain other things.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1150. He was buried in his convent at Strasbourg, once shining, as they say, with many miracles.

Innocent, Pope IV, previously called Symbaldus, an Italian by nation, born from the city of Genoa, sat in the pontificate after Celestine IV for eleven years, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and in law, a lover of religious, who celebrated the general Council of Lyons, in which, with the consent of the fathers, he deposed Frederick from the empire and established many things for the utility of the Church. He nevertheless wrote certain excellent minor works, by whose publication he is worthily numbered among ecclesiastical writers, to be transmitted with them to the knowledge of posterity. Against Petrus de Vineis:

On Ecclesiastical Power, one book.
An Apparatus on the Decretals, five books. “It is read in Ezekiel...”
The Rule of the Poor Clares, one book. “The form of life of the order...”
The Authentics also, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
And certain other things. He flourished under Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1250.

Rolandinus de Romanciis, a most celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, and nobly learned in secular philosophy, who was not among the least with the old jurists, wrote in that faculty certain excellent minor works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I found only the Summa:

On the Order of Crimes, one book. “The little book begins...”
Determinations and Questions.
The rest, which he is said to have composed, have not come to my knowledge.
