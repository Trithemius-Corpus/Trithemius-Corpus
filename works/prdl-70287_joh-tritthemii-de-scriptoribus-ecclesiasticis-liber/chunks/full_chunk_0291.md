*On Ecclesiastical Writers, Addition II.*

Victor, the fifteenth bishop of the city of Rome, a holy and learned man, wrote on the question of Easter and certain other little works. Among them the following are recorded:

Certain decrees.

An account of the faith, rendered to Huneric, king of the Vandals, when he demanded it.

He flourished under the emperor Severus, around the year of the Lord 200.

Victor, bishop of Utica, wrote three books of a History of the Vandal Persecutions, which Christians formerly endured in Africa around the times of blessed Augustine, about the year of the Lord 430, under Genseric and Huneric, kings of the Vandals.

Vigilius, martyr and bishop of Trent, published five pious and elegant books, about a thousand years ago, against Eutyches and other heretics, who thought with too little piety about the property of Christ’s natures and the unity of his person.

Vigilius, a bishop most skilled in divine letters and a most ardent emulator of martyrdom, wrote to a certain Simplicianus a little book in praise of martyrdom, and a letter containing the deeds of the martyrs of his time among the barbarians.

I think he is the same man who, a thousand years ago, wrote against Eutyches and other heretics, who thought with too little piety about the property of Christ’s natures and the unity of his person.

Vitus Amerbachius, German by birth, a very learned man of our own time, a few years ago a most vigorous champion of the Lutheran sect, now led by repentance, has betaken himself to the Catholic Church; and he succeeded Johann Eck, a man worthy of immortality, in the academy at Ingolstadt. He composed distinguished little works of learning and doctrine, some of which are entitled as follows:

Antiparadoxa, with orations on the praises of his homeland and on the method of studies.

Commentaries on the three books of Cicero On Duties.

A paraphrase of Cicero’s speech for Archias the poet.

A double interpretation of the poems of Pythagoras and Phocylides.

An interpretation of the Meteora of Joannes Jovianus Pontanus.

On the Soul, four books.

He is still living under Charles V Caesar, in the year of salvation 1546, and will compose many things for the benefit of students.

Ulpius Franekerensis, a Frisian, a man most learned in both languages, rendered Hesiod’s Works and Days in verse, in such a way that he expressed each Greek verse with a corresponding Latin one.

*On the Abbot of Sponheim, On Writers.*

He also wrote certain collections of proverbs not noticed by Erasmus.

Urbanus of Belluno, a Franciscan monk, instructed in the Latin and Greek languages to no ordinary degree, learnedly and accurately wrote two books of Grammatical Institutes for the Greek language.

Xystus, bishop of the city of Rome, a learned and upright man, describing in a volume the decree of the synod held against Nestorius, sent it to the churches of East and West, confirming that, while two natures remain perfectly in Christ, one person of the Son of God is to be believed.

Likewise also Xystus, the successor of Celestine, sent opinions on the same matter to Nestorius himself and to the bishops of the East, to cut down his error.

(What agreement there may be between these two Xystuses, let Gesner’s Bibliotheca see to it; from there these things, with many others, have been taken.)

Zacharias Scholasticus, otherwise called the Sophist, bishop of Mytilene, renowned for his knowledge of human and divine letters, made known to posterity the sharpness of his talent in certain little books, namely in a Dialogue, which is inscribed Ammonius, or The Philosopher. It is reported that something in Greek under his name is extant at Rome.

*To the Reader.*

Somewhat above, where Heinrich Herp is treated, namely on page 331, let the following be inserted, if you please, not because they were carelessly omitted there, but because, after Herp’s section had been completed, they were meanwhile incidentally suggested to us by certain studious and pious men:

On the Song of Songs of Solomon, concerning the virtues formed like Christ, one book. “Let him kiss me with the kiss...”

Directory of Contemplatives, one book. “Who [unclear] of our Lord Jesus Christ...”

Conferences on the Perfection of Life.

Eden, or The Paradise of Contemplatives, one book. “Friend, come up more often...”

*The End.*
