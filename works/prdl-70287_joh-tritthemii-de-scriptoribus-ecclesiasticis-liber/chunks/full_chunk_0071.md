*The Abbot of Sponheim.*

The decretals of Pope [Anastasius?], and one letter, which is found in the second volume of the works of Saint Jerome.

Paschasius, deacon of the Roman church, a learned and holy man, father of the poor, wholly intent on the distribution of alms, was of such virtue and holiness that Pope Gregory writes, in the fourth book of the Dialogues, that after his death a demoniac was cured by the touch of his dalmatic. Saint Germanus, bishop of Capua, saw his soul in the pains of purgatory, tormented for no other reason than that, in the election of Pope Symmachus, he had preferred Laurentius, and had persisted in this opinion, though peacefully, until death. He wrote, as Gregory attests, a notable volume against the errors of the Greeks:

On the Holy Spirit, 3 books. And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 500.

Symmachus, pope, a Sardinian by birth, a learned and holy man, obtained the pontificate after Anastasius, not without discord among the factions of Laurentius, and held it for fifteen years and six months. A man merciful and generous toward the poor, he loved the clergy, enriched the churches, and drove heretics out of the city. After holding a synod at Rome of one hundred and twenty bishops, he canonically cleared himself of the charges brought against him, and drove Peter, bishop of Ravenna, with his accomplices into exile. He wrote certain letters, works not to be despised, of which I have found:

Decree of the first synod, 1 book. It begins: “Because frequent...”

Decree of the Roman synod, 1 book. It begins: “When in one place at blessed...”

Acts of another Roman synod, 1 book. It begins: “The fourth synod held...”

On the transgressions of clerics, 1 book. It begins: “Indeed well, brotherly...”

Letters to various persons, 1 book.

He died under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 515, in the eighth indiction, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of August; Pope Hormisdas succeeded him.

Symmachus the patrician, senator and Roman consul, father-in-law of Boethius, a distinguished philosopher and rhetorician, and a magnificent worshipper of the Christian faith, was banished into exile at Pavia together with Boethius by Theoderic, king of Italy, on suspicion of having aimed at liberty; and not long afterward he had him cruelly slain. Since he was a learned and most eloquent man, he composed several works, of which I have been able to find only a certain short but elegant volume:

Letters to various persons, 1 book. It begins: “Surely you laugh because...”

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He died in the reign of Anastasius, killed by Theoderic, king of Italy, at Ravenna, in the year of the Lord 524, in the eleventh indiction.
