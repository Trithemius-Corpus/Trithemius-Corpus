He also published some other works, which have not come to my knowledge. Finally, he is said to have made Hegesippus and certain others Latin from the Greek. He flourished under the most merciful emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 380.

Philastrius, bishop of Brescia, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, known and familiar to blessed Ambrose of Milan, whom Saint Augustine mentions in a certain letter to Quodvultdeus, wrote for the common benefit certain excellent little works, of which I do not recall that any has come into our hands. There is said to be an outstanding work of his, as blessed Augustine, in the place cited above, attests, which he wrote:

On 156 Heresies, one book. “The Simonians from Simon [unclear].”

The rest I have not seen. He flourished under the most Christian emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 380.

Evagrius, monk, disciple of Saint Macarius the Egyptian, afterward bishop of Antioch, a man in every respect most learned, instructed in both secular and divine writings, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, even in the episcopate preserving the austerity of his former life, was of such abstinence that he was regarded as a marvel. He was the first to commit the life of the holy fathers to writing, and from his own vein he published many little works. Of these, however, very few have come to my knowledge. The following are reported as his:

To the Anchorites, very useful, one book. On the Eight Principal Vices, one book.
