Michael of Cesena, the seventeenth minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, and venerable no less for religion than for knowledge, produced in the holy writings works of exposition not to be despised, by which he became known far and wide both to those then living and now to posterity. Of these I have so far been able to find very few, namely:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
On the prophet Ezekiel, 1 book.
Sermons for the season, 1 book.
Sermons on the saints, 1 book.
He produced readings and commentaries on many books of the Bible, which I have not yet seen.

He flourished under the emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Bertrand, of the Order of Friars Minor, by country a Milanese, born of a noble family, and, as some think, cardinal priest of the most holy Roman Church, a man certainly exceptionally learned in the divine Scriptures and not moderately instructed in secular philosophy, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote several works of great usefulness, among which I have found:

On the Sentences, 1 book.
Sermons on the Gospels, 1 book. “They shall see the Son,” etc. [unclear]
Sermons on the saints, 1 book.

The rest that he composed have not yet come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1325.

Gerard of Bologna, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel, the eleventh prior general, doctor of Paris, most learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, venerable no less for his way of life than for his knowledge, who governed his order vigorously for twenty years, wrote several volumes of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name also to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 1 book.
Quodlibets, 1 book.
Ordinary Questions, 1 book.
Sermons for the season, 1 book.

He began a very distinguished Summa of theology, which, however, overtaken by death, he left unfinished.

He died under Louis the Bavarian, emperor IV, in the year of the Lord 1317, in the fifteenth indiction, and was buried at Avignon.

John of Colonna, by country a Roman, of the Order of Friars Preachers, archbishop of Messina, a man possessing a practiced intellect in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, a very sagacious investigator of the histories of past time, wrote works of noble intellect not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these there survives the volume which he entitled:

Sea of Histories, 10 books.
Letters to various persons, 1 book.

He also produced some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Martin, of the Order of Friars Preachers, penitentiary of the lord pope of the city of Rome, and afterwards, as they report, bishop of a certain city, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have seen only:

A Table of the Decretum, 1 book. “Among any other things” [unclear]
A Brief Chronicle, 2 books. “Since to know the times is a very great” [unclear]
Sermons for the season, 1 book. “Knowing that it is the hour”
On Various Miracles, 1 book. “Confession is made with the mouth unto salvation”

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under the emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

There are some who write that this Martin belonged to the order of our holy father Benedict. In my opinion the likeness of the name of Marianus Scotus, of our order, whom I mentioned above, has deceived them, for he too wrote a chronicle. There are likewise others who think that he should be assigned to the community of the Cistercian Order; these either ignorance of matters has darkened, or envy.

Gerard Odonis, of the Order of Friars Minor, the eighteenth minister general, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in scholastic philosophy, subtle in intellect, apt in speech, and quite fit for disputation, is said to have composed certain works in the holy Scriptures, of which few have become known to me:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Also On Logic, 1 book.
Various Questions, 1 book.
