John of Dorsten, German by nation, of the Order of the Hermit Brothers of Saint Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, well suited to disputing and resolving scriptural questions, and also of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people; he was held in esteem at the Erfurt school for teaching and preaching. He wrote works not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there are reported:

Sermons on the season, one book.
Sermons on the saints, one book.
And certain other works.

He flourished in the convent at Erfurt, under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pope, in the year of the Lord 1475.

Angelus Saxo of Brunswick, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, keen in intellect, distinguished in life, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people; after obtaining the mastership for teaching at the Erfurt school, he held the office of preacher at Mainz for a long time. Among other things he wrote a certain notable work:

On the Canon of the Mass, one book.
Various sermons, one book.
Diverse questions, one book.
And certain other works.

He died at Strasbourg, and was buried in the monastery of the penitents, under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1481, in the fourteenth indiction.

Franciscus Philelphus, born from the ancient town of Tolentino, a knight decorated with gold and poet laureate, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, formerly in his youth at Venice a pupil of Chrysoloras the Byzantine philosopher, from whom he learned Greek and Latin eloquence most perfectly; a man so zealous and so indefatigable a lover and cultivator of the good arts that, like another Pliny, amid the continual occupations of wars he was always either reading something for himself or dictating something to others.
