Hieronymus Fracastorius, physician, very elegantly wrote in heroic verse three books of *Syphilis, or On the French Disease*, addressed to Pietro Bembo. By the same author: *Homocentrica*; and *On the Causes of Critical Days, Through Those Things Which Are in Us*. He is still distinguished under Emperor Charles V, in the year 1546.

Hieronymus Gaebuilerus, outstanding in knowledge of sacred and profane letters, composed many excellent works, of which a great many are still extant, namely an *Exhortation to Holy Communion*; an *Epitome of the Royal and Most Ancient Origin of Emperor Charles V and of Ferdinand, King of Hungary and Bohemia, and of all the Archdukes of Austria and Counts of Habsburg*. He flourished in the year 1530.

Honoratus, bishop of the church of Marseille, an eloquent man and without any impediment of speech, an extempore declaimer in the Church, and, because from boyhood he grew up in the fear of God and was also practiced in ecclesiastical affairs, opens his mouth like an armory of the Scriptures, and, in the manner of homilies, composes many things for the profit of readers, and especially for cultivating the reasoning of faith and refuting the perversity of heretics.

He adapts the lives of the holy fathers to be read for the edification of later generations, especially that of his nurturer, Saint Hilary, bishop of Arles; he performs litanies for supplicating the clemency of God with the people entrusted to him, as far as he is able. He flourished at the time when Gennadius lived.

Jacobus Hochstratus, of the order of St. Dominic, Prior of Cologne and a most diligent inquisitor of heretical depravity, brought forth several productions against the enemies of the Church. He wrote an *Apology* and *Destruction of the Cabala* against Johannes Reuchlin. The same man wrote *On Faith and Works*, 1 book; *On Purgatory*, 1 book; *On the Veneration of the Saints*, 1 book; *On Christian Liberty*, 4 books, against Luther; *Against Concubinary Priests*, 1 book; also the *Pearl of Moral Philosophy*; and many other little works against heretics. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1512; he died in 1527.

Jacobus Monachus, a pious and learned man, wrote certain Greek sermons which are preserved in Italy, namely: *On the [unclear]*; *On the Conception of the Virgin Mary*; *On the Twelve Tribes of Israel*; again, *On the Conception and Generation*; *On the Holy of Holies*; “[unclear],” etc.

Jacobus Henrichmannus of Sindelfingen, at the rebirth of good letters, published *Grammatical Institutes* for the benefit of students, with an exhortation to tender youth that it eliminate barbarism and study eloquence. He flourished in the year of salvation 1506.

Jacobus Locher Philomusus, a German by nation, highly skilled in both languages, wrote various works in verse and prose. Of those which we have seen published, these are: *Compendium of Rhetoric*; *Syntax on Composing a Funeral Oration*; *Epode on the Death of Pluto and the Other Demons*; *Poem on Night, Wine, and Woman*; *Dialogue on the Eminence of the Four Doctors of the Church*. He flourished at the time when Reuchlin lived.

Jacobus Picoloninus, Cardinal of Pavia, wrote very elegant letters, very full of recondite matters, together with most lucid commentaries containing the events of his own time, along with certain other works not previously printed.

Jacobus, Count of [Purililiatum?], a most learned man, wrote a little work on the generous education of children, no less delightful than useful. He flourished around the year of the Lord 1480.
