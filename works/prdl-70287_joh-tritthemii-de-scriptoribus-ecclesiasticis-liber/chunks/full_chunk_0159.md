*ABBOT OF SPANHEIM.*

On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
And certain other works.
He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Lapus de Castellione, abbot of San Miniato, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a jurist most learned among all men of his time, and imbued from early youth, as they say, with the disciplines of the philosophers, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, and an outstanding disputant of scholastic questions, whose authority among jurists is to be judged by no means slight, wrote several short works in his faculty, among which there is an excellent work:

Of his Allegations, 1 book.
On the Clementines, 1 book.
And many other works.

He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Guerricus, abbot of Igny, a man by no means slightly learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, sweetly composed in speech, fit for persuading, distinguished no less by love of regular observance in his way of life than by knowledge of the Scriptures, wrote, for the edification of simple devout brothers, short works not to be despised; by these, showing himself a useful and faithful shepherd of Christ’s sheep, he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have found only the work of sermons on the temporal cycle and on the saints through the circuit of the year, which he delivered:

To his brothers, 1 book. We await the Savior.

He also wrote letters.
Concerning the rest, however, which he is said to have composed, I have not been able to find anything anywhere.

Henry of Urimaria, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, by nation, as they report, a German, a man most devoted to the holy Scriptures from boyhood and very learned, second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy, who for many years was an outstanding doctor in the Parisian academy, distinguished by celebrated doctrine, wrote many very famous volumes, among which the following are said to be:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
Quodlibets, 1 book.
On the Ten Commandments, 1 book.
Flowers from the Collation of the Fathers, 2 books. Hear, Israel, the commandments.
On the Perfection of Man, 1 book. Because many [unclear].
On the Incarnation of the Word, 1 book.
On the Fourfold Instinct, 1 book.
On Exemption, 1 book.
Exposition on the Creed, on the celebration of Mass, 1 book.
Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
On the books of the Ethics, 10 books.
And many other works.

He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, surnamed Bavarian, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Bartholomaeus Pisanus, of the Order of Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most skilled in both laws, and nobly learned in the knowledge of secular philosophy, distinguished in intellect, open in speech, cautious in knowledge and counsel, is said to have written certain very famous short works. Of these I have seen only the Summa, which he calls the Pisan, a work certainly not to be rejected, which a few years ago was in great use among confessors of the common people, which the author entitled:

Summa magistrucia, 1 book.

Concerning the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, and completed his Summa, as he himself testifies, in the year of the Lord [1338?].

Bartholomaeus Brixianus, of the Order of Preachers, a man devoted to and learned in the holy Scriptures, and by no means slightly learned in both laws, also an outstanding declaimer of sermons, and no less holy than learned, since he is said to have shone with miracles even in life, wrote, among the other monuments of his talent, a notable:

Summa on Cases, 1 book.
Various Sermons, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord [1340?].
