84 *On Ecclesiastical Writers*

He condemned the Eutychian bishops, banishing them into exile. For this reason, holding a synod at Rome, he established many things for the benefit of the Church, and wrote:

A synodal decree, one book. *To Flavius Boethius* [unclear].
Several decretal letters, one book.

He died under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 495, indiction 1.

*Gennadius*, priest of the church of Marseille, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, fully trained in both Greek and Latin eloquence, wrote many little works, and translated not a few volumes of the ancient fathers from Greek into Latin speech. Of his own writings he himself, at the end of *Illustrious Men*, listed the following:

On his faith, to Pope Gelasius, one book.
Against Nestorius, six books.
Against Eutyches, nine books.
Against Pelagius, three books.
Against all heresies, eight books.
On illustrious men, one book. *James, surnamed* [unclear].
On the heresy of the thousand years, one book.

He wrote these and certain other works which have not yet come to me. He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 490.

*Gelasius*, the first pope of that name, an African by birth, sat after Felix in the pontificate for four [unclear], a holy man and most learned in the divine Scriptures. As soon as he entered upon the pontifical office, he sent into exile the Manichaeans whom he found in the city, and publicly burned their books at the basilica of Saint Mary Major. He established many things for the benefit of the Church, and wrote very many works against heretics. Of his little works the following are reported:

A synodal decree, one book. *The necessity of things* [unclear].
Against the Arians, two books.
Hymns in the manner of Ambrose, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Several weighty prayers.

And certain other things. He arranged Graduals, Alleluias, and Tracts, with prefaces appropriate for the Mass, and separated the authentic books of the holy fathers from the apocryphal, as is found in distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*.

He died while Anastasius Augustus was emperor, in the year of the Lord 426.
