*Ecclesiastical [writers], Addition II.*

Tables on Galen’s books *On Diseases and Symptoms*.
And he wrote certain other works, which have not come into our hands.
He flourished in the year of Christ 1543.

Symphorianus Campegius, otherwise Champerius, of Lyon, a Frenchman by birth, a knight, and distinguished chief physician of the Lorrainers, divided into four books the *Castigations or Emendations* of the apothecaries and of the Arab physicians Mesue, Serapion, Rasis, Alpharabius, and other more recent physicians.
To these is added the workshop of apothecaries and more recent physicians.
Various minor works by the same man. These are:
*On Famous Writers of Medicine, Ancient and More Recent*, one book.
*On the Founders of the Divine Laws, together with a Refutation of the Mohammedan Sect, or of the Koran*, one book.
A dialogue on the Mohammedan law.
*On Diseases and Remedies of Bodies and Souls*, [two?] books.
A confirmation of the evangelical and Christian religion from the writings of gentiles, poets, and philosophers.
Medical aphorisms.
*The Gallic Garden*, a book on medicine so entitled.
*The Monarchy of the Gauls*.
He also excerpted four books from all the books of Galen.
On dialectic, rhetoric, geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, music, natural philosophy, medicine, theology, likewise on laws, politics, and ethics.
By the same man, [two?] books on an apologetic dispute between Andreas Thurinus and Matthaeus Curtius.

Synesius of Pentapolis in Africa, a Christian philosopher, made bishop of Ptolemais, wrote books on grammar and philosophy, royal, panegyrical, or demonstrative speeches.
An encomium of baldness.
He composed a remarkable discourse, in Greek style, *On Providence*, and very many other books of diverse kinds, and admirable letters.
Of these the following are extant in Italy in Greek:
*On the Interpretation of Dreams*, one book.
Letters *peri ten pistin*, that is, concerning the faith.
Scholia on the book of Democritus.
Greek discourses on kingship, on the household, on his own life. Two monostichs.
And he is said to have published many other works. Suidas mentions him.
At what time he flourished, however, I could not easily say.

Theodoricus Adamaeus Salembergius published annotations on the six books of Procopius the rhetorician concerning the buildings of the emperor Justinian.
By the same man, a congratulatory panegyric on the concord of the Christian world.
A book on the island of Rhodes and the institution of the military orders.
Likewise he rendered the Tablet of Cebes into Latin.

Theodorus the deacon, a man not ignorant of the liberal arts, with the utmost zeal composed an excellent book of the sacred canons, both of the Apostles and of synods, both ecumenical and particular, and of other holy fathers, etc. It is extant in Italy.

Theophanes, bishop, otherwise metropolitan, of Nicaea, since he was a very great lover of the Christian religion, composed certain chapters against the impious Jews.
Two letters of his are also extant in Greek: one of them, which is called didascalic, he wrote to priests and the rest of the clergy; the other to the Nicaeans, in which he teaches how a Christian ought to hold all present things, both pleasant and troublesome, of no account.

Theophilus, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, which was formerly called the Tower of Strato, under the emperor [Seno?], composed, together with the other bishops, a very useful synodical letter against those who celebrated Easter with the Jews on the fourteenth moon.

Theophylactus, archbishop of Bulgaria, a man most accomplished in every kind of letters and of a more sincere life, published in Greek most elegant expositions on the four Gospels and all the epistles of Saint Paul. Commentaries by the same man on the prophet Hosea and on all of Micah, recently discovered, have been brought from the island of Patmos.

Theotimus, bishop of Tomi in Scythia, a man distinguished by very elegant talent, published short and concise treatises in the manner of dialogues and of ancient eloquence. And he was writing certain other works in the time of Jerome, as Jerome himself testifies.

Thomas Branduandinus, otherwise Brauardinus, an Englishman, an outstanding theologian, wrote *On the House of God*, otherwise *On the Causes of God*, and *On the Virtue of God, the Cause of Causes*, against the Pelagians. And certain other works.
He flourished in the year of the Lord 157.
