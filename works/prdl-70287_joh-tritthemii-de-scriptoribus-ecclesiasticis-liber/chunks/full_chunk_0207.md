Nicolaus Dorbellus, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, of the province of Tours, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and second to none in scholastic philosophy, distinguished in talent and sufficiently fit for disputing and unraveling questions of the scriptures, wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity; and in the school at Poitiers, both by disputing and by teaching, he earned great praise for his learning. He was a profound interpreter and very keen defender of the sayings of John Scotus; expounding philosophy from his writings, and dividing this very work into three parts, mathematical, theological, and ethical, he named it the Compendium.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Callixtus III, in the year of the Lord 1455.

Petrus de Unzola, a citizen of Bologna, a very celebrated jurist and well instructed in the disciplines of the liberal arts, outstanding in talent, is said also to have written some other works, but they have not come to my knowledge.

Henricus de Herph, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, German by nation, of the province of Cologne, a man zealous in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of pontifical law, outstanding in talent and clear in speech, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, to be revered no less for his religious life than for his knowledge of the scriptures, wrote works of not negligible authority, by which he made his name known to posterity.
