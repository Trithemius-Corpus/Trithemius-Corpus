*Ecclesiastical [Writers], Addition II.*

The New Graecismus has about 10,560 verses.
A little book On the Sacraments, about 4,000.
The Passion of the holy martyrs Felix and Regula and Exuperantius, about 3,000.
A little book On the Proper Names of Rivers and Mountains, about 1,500.
The Seats of the Romans, about 1,150.
Praises of the Blessed Virgin, about 900.
The [Clipearium?] of the Teutons, about 160.
A Catalogue of the Roman Popes and Emperors, about 1,640.
A Commendation of Rudolf, King of the Romans, about 800.
He flourished in the year of the Lord 1275.

Conrad Peutinger, doctor of both laws, wrote Table-Talk Sermons, in which many things concerning the wondrous antiquities of Germany are related.
The same man published an epitome On the Emigrations of Certain Peoples.

Conrad Summerhart of Calw, a Swabian, principal founder of the University of Tübingen and a theologian, wrote many works most pleasing to the learned, among which these are reported:
Commentaries on the whole of physics.
On Contracts, a large volume.
Conclusions on the Sentences of Peter Lombard.
Treatise I: That God Willed to Become Man.
An oration to the fathers in the Hirsau chapter of the Order of Saint Benedict, On the Twelve Monastic Abuses.
He flourished in the year 1501.

Cosimo Pazzi, archbishop of Florence, a man powerful in both languages, made Latin the 41 discourses of Maximus Tyrius the Platonist.

Cuthbert Tunstall, bishop of London, singular ornament and pillar of good letters, composed four books On the Art of Calculation.
He flourished under Charles V Caesar.

Cinthio Giovanni Battista Giraldi of Ferrara, a man suited to the making of poems, wrote certain poems, and they are these:
An Epicedion on the death of Lord Alfonso d’Este, prince.
Hercules d’Este hailed as duke.
One book of Elegies. Two books of Silvae.
An Epithalamium on the marriage of Johann Sinapius.
Three Eclogues.

*On the Abbot of Spanheim, On Writers.*

Certain things On the Parts of the Human Body.
A letter On Change.
And many other things which have not come down to us.

Cyril, bishop of Jerusalem, often driven from the Church and received back, at last under the emperor Theodosius held the episcopate unshaken for eight years.
His Catecheses are extant, which he composed in his youth.

Cyrus Theodorus Prodromus, a man zealous and learned in the divine scriptures, composed ancient and pious Greek epigrams.
Arguments or chapters of each testament were composed by the same man in iambic tetrastichs of six-foot verses, and in heroic tetrastichs, which everywhere are joined beneath the iambics of the same meaning, not indeed on all the books, but on the Pentateuch, Joshua, Judges, and Kings.
Likewise on the four Gospels and the Acts of the Apostles.
And he composed very many other things, of which I shall append certain ones which have come down to us:
Iambic and heroic tetrastichs on the three holy hierarchs, Gregory, Basil, and Chrysostom.
Elegies in which he addresses the apostle Paul.
Similar ones to Gregory the Theologian.
Others on Basil the Great.
Others on Chrysostom.
Others to Gregory of Nyssa.
Others on Saint Nicholas.
He wrote An Exile of Friendship in six-foot iambics.
Expostulatory verses On Providence.
On Bareus, by whom he had rashly been called a heretic.
On the Image of Life.
Admonitory verses to the envious.

Daniel Barbaro, a Venetian patrician, doctor of arts, great-nephew of Ermolao, recently wrote refined commentaries on the five words of Porphyry.
Likewise he published commentaries on the three books of Aristotle’s Rhetoric to Theodectes.
Elegant letters of the same man to various persons are also extant.
He is still flourishing in the year 1546.
