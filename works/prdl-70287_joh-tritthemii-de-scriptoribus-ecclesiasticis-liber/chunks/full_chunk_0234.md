Haringus, son of Sifrid Sinnama, born at The Hague in Frisia, who is commonly called Hermannus, a German by nation, a very celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, excellently learned both in divine scripture and in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, ready and steady in counsel, whose authority is held in great esteem among princes and prelates of churches, easily holds first place in the university of Cologne in teaching, writing, and disputing. Among other things he wrote a great and very famous work, explaining legal titles with the utmost clarity, very useful and necessary for all who are beginning to study in both laws or in either of them:

On the title of the Authentics, 1 book. “Although the constitutions...”

On the Uses of Feuds, 1 book. “Because this book of feuds...”

On the Peace of Constance, 1 book. “Because the emperor Frederick...”

On the title of the Institutes, 4 books. “Although from this book...”

On the title of the Decretals, 5 books. “As, as the Emperor says...”

Certain other works are extant.

He is living in manhood’s prime in the city of Cologne, illuminating it with his learning, under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year in which we wrote these things, 1494, the twelfth indiction.

Raphael Regius, an Italian by nation, a man most learned in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, a celebrated poet, and an eminent orator, skilled in both languages, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech, who in the university of Padua won the highest praises for his learning by teaching, disputing, and composing, is said to have produced many splendid little works, by which he made his name and talent known with glory. Of these the following are extant:

Corrections on Quintilian, 1 book. “Great-souled men are accustomed...”

On one letter of Pliny, 1 book. “In the inscription of this work...”

Against Johannes Calphurnius, 1 book. “Although by a very serious...”

A dialogue against the same man, 1 book. “Yesterday you disturbed the boys’...”

Various orations.

Also many letters.

And many other things.

He is still living at Padua, as I have heard, and is writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Laurentius Bonicontrius, an eminent philosopher and astronomer, and excellently learned also in the other disciplines of the liberal arts, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, strong in verse and prose, is said to have produced many splendid little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of many; but I have not yet been able to see any of them. His notable work over the cycle of the year is said to be:

The Fasti, with preliminary notes, 1 book.

And certain commentaries.

And certain other things.

He is still living in Italy, writing many and various works, both in verse and in prose, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Johannes of Keysersberg, a German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in divine scripture, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect, distinguished in life and conduct, a very celebrated preacher of sermons, who for many years has held the office of preacher in the cathedral church of Strasbourg, wrote some commendable little works, but they have not yet come to the knowledge of my reading. There are said to be his elegant:

Sermons to the people, 1 book.
