The *Steganographia* is Trithemius's most famous and most notorious work: a treatise in three books, written about 1499–1500 and circulated for a century in manuscript before it was first printed (Frankfurt, 1606). On its surface it is a manual of angel-magic — long conjurations addressed to spirit-princes and their subordinate spirits, each governing a quarter of the heavens, a wind, or an hour of the day. Beneath that surface it is a cryptographic system: the "conjurations" are cover-text, and the real message is carried by the spirit-names and the numeric keys tabulated for each prince. It was the disguise that undid him. Manuscript copies reached readers they were not meant for — Carolus Bovillus among them — who took the spirit-magic at face value and accused Trithemius of necromancy; the book was placed on the *Index*, and the third book's reputation as genuinely demonic persisted until its cipher was solved — first by Thomas Ernst (1996), then independently by Jim Reeds (1998). Trithemius's own answer to the charge is the *Trithemius sui ipsius vindex* of 1616, also in this corpus (`prdl-32287`).

The Latin source here is the **1621 Darmstadt edition** — printed by Balthasar Aulaeander (the Latinized name of Balthasar Hofmann) at the expense of Johann Berner — recovered from the Bibliothèque nationale de France / Gallica. The work was absent from this corpus's original acquisition pass and was reassembled from its 168 IIIF page-images. It was re-OCR'd with the same vision model used across the corpus and translated by GPT-5.5 with a Fable&nbsp;5 review pass against the page scans; the review recovered the cipher tables and the Early-New-High-German cover-text letters that a first pass tends to flatten into placeholders. **The cipher is decoded** — in a separate cryptanalysis (eleven *modi*, II–XI and XXXII–XXXIX, decoded by inverting each *modus*'s printed alphabet) on the [cipher solutions page](../cipher-solutions.html). In the body translation here the Latin and its German cover-text are rendered as they stand, not decoded inline. For the cryptographic machinery see the companion *Clavis* texts (`prdl-70281`, `prdl-70282`), Ernst (1996) and Reeds (1998), and for the comprehensive English edition Stephen Skinner & Daniel Clark, *Steganographia: Books I–IV* (Llewellyn, 2024).
