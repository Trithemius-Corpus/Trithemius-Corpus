*Polygraphiae libri VI* — "Six Books of Polygraphy" — is the **1518 first edition** of Trithemius's open cryptographic manual, the first printed book on cryptography in the West. He completed the work late in life — the dedication to Emperor Maximilian I is dated 1508 — and it was printed posthumously, two years after his death. Where the *Steganographia* hides its ciphers behind angel-magic, the *Polygraphiae* teaches the craft in the open: substitution tables, transposition grids, the "Ave Maria" cipher in which any plaintext becomes a pious Latin sentence, and numerical codes. Its six books move from simple to complex, culminating in the polyalphabetic *tabula recta*.

This witness is the 1518 edition printed by Michael Furter and Adam Petri for Johann Haselberg — the long-standing attribution to Oppenheim / Peter Schöffer has been corrected to Basel (Adams T-979, refined by Christie's and Brill). It is one of three *Polygraphiae* witnesses in the corpus (1518, 1550, 1600).
