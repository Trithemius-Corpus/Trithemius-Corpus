*Reverse table of transposition.*

[table of letters and numerals]

In this reverse table, you have just as many alphabets through the transposition of letters as in the preceding one, which we also set down as the direct table. For, as you see, under metathesis there are twenty-four toward the right. Likewise the same number toward the left; downward, twenty-four; and upward, ninety-eight. Multiplied four times twenty times, reduced in the form of a cross to the four sides, they make no fewer than one hundred and twelve alphabets.

The same table follows, expanded.

*First figure of the expansion of the direct table.*

[expanded table of letters]

This is the first figure of the expansion of the direct table: five alphabets by metatheses, as is seen, with transposition of the letters. For in the first alphabet b is taken for a, c is taken for b, and so on consecutively, as is clearer to one who looks closely. In the second alphabet g is taken for a; in the third, d; in the fourth, e; in the fifth, f, as the placement of the order teaches.

The expansion is continued.

[expanded table of letter-pairs]

In this second figure of the expansion of the direct table there are likewise contained five alphabets in number, from which by metathesis many others also can be made; their arrangement, for the sake of brevity, we have omitted. [unclear].
