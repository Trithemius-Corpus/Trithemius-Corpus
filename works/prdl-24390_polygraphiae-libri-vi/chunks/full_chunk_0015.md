Again I warn you in advance, lest you understand us to have promised, or to be promising, otherwise. The third benefit is that you will teach one ignorant of the Roman speech, not only in a few hours, as we promise, to write and read, but also to understand, if you prudently apply the duty of the office; this we have thought should not be rejected without reasonable cause. For the disciple of the new art will write in whatever tongue he has known, and, with the Latin discourse composed under regulated instruction, he will understand and interpret it, if you yourself help him word for word; that will suffice. Finally, this instruction can be extended to all the things and affairs of the world. How these things can be done, if anyone wishes to know from me, let him first remember to show himself such that he deserves to receive them. For all things are not owed to all, but one man is to be preferred to another. The fourth benefit is that, by extending the likeness of the thing received, all these things can be extended into any language whatever, the binary instruction being reduced to unity. Yet not just any man can do this, but only he whom God, going before him with his special grace, and art and nature have dignified. The fifth benefit is that, by imitation of likeness, we can communicate by the art of our invented knowledge whatever secrets of our mind we wish, to whomever we wish, in many ways and very widely, by various methods, which can be known or observed by no other person. Here is given a manifold speech without voice, through which safely, securely, very widely, with all suspicion removed, we shall be able to indicate most fully to those who know the art all the conceptions of our mind, however difficult. And this marvelous invention has innumerable progressions, capable of all mysteries. The sixth benefit is that, through understanding this work, you will be able to discover any regulated metathesis, or transposition, of letters, and nevertheless to form them in such a way that it serves only those who know the art, and absolutely no one else in the world. And great is the power of metathesis in form, and likewise in operation, since it both has several modes and is varied in many ways. The seventh benefit is that, through understanding this volume, a very great faculty is given to those who desire to enlarge their memory without labor, whenever the seeds of minutiae are placed in their proper degrees. All things, for the sake of goodness, lie hidden, wrapped in the secrets of words and figures; if anyone desires to understand them more fully, let him call to mind that unrelenting labor conquers all things. For it will have been enough for me to have handed down the principles of the invented art, and the rude, and deliberately unformed, method of writing symbolically. For there was neither time nor reason for our inventions to be shaped in a more polished form, lest honey produce vomiting in those who are nauseated, or truth produce hatred in those who are indignant. Every time must be valued according to its own merits, and swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor loaded with precious gems. All things are unsuitable that do not accord with customs and the time.
