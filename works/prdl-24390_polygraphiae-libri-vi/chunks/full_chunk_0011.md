I have placed the letters of the Latin alphabet in order before the individual words, so that the operator may have an easy method in the proposed matter, and the reciprocal party, in reporting, may be relieved of excessive labor. The paraphrases that are placed outside the straight order of the lines are not devoid of the mystery of the art, since they serve light-flowing propositions for the consummation of necessary perfection, not obscure ones. Therefore whatever are employed in the former are neglected in the latter. For these are the certain means by which every suspicion of an adversary is removed. All things have been set down with reason, so that not even a proposition is introduced idly. The farther, for example, the sun advances from the equinoctial circle, the more the day is lengthened for those dwelling in those regions. Therefore, so that unity may advance in the right order, by means of the ternary, into the binary, the paradigm that we hand down must be observed rather carefully. And first, with prior consideration, let the intelligent operator of his intention observe what he is going to assign to the ternary through the binary; let him divide day with night into minute parts, and distinguish the minutiæ of the winds by a supposition of phrase that is not foreign but proper. In any mystical business let him take careful heed not to overturn the established order of the art, nor put one paraphrase in place of another, nor grant to one what is due to another; otherwise unity does not admit the binary, and the whole operation will be confused and useless. Let night not be wandering through excessive length, unstable, advancing beyond the limits appointed to it by art; let curiosity not change water into snow, nor let him sleep by day who keeps watch at night. Let the mind, solicitous in intellect, also use art, lest the hand by its own levity commit an error, which, slight and small in the monad, is accustomed to appear [always?] great and very large in the binary. One paraphrase from any order is admitted only into the service of the secret; for it, after the example of the keeper, whenever a particle of daylight has required it, one phrase similarly adhering is taken up, not several. These things must be [commended?] to a vigilant mind, and care must be taken that the wind not be animated. After this a rule is prescribed for the one approaching the practice of the art: that, so far as he can by mystical art, he imitate the one person of a human being, consisting of two natures, namely the soul surpassing visible things and the flesh remaining visible. For just as the soul is not seen, although it is placed in a visible body, so the secret of the mind, in order that it may remain secret and nonetheless operate at will, requires the guidance of light, while continuing in its workshop of darkness. Therefore, as often as the body serves the soul, the servant the master, the will reason, see that the virtue of the principal power in the light of the paraphrase is preserved in its natural vigor.
