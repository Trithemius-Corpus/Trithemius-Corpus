For there is a vicissitude of all things; variety succeeds to the simplicity of minds. Who will be able to bring back this continual flowing-away, so to speak, to the concord of unity? For many times pass, as Scripture says: knowledge will be manifold. But that to the two preceding books of the Polygraphy we add a third has not lacked a special reason. For, since the wills of human beings are varied, varied methods are rightly given for honorable operations. In the former, night illumines the days; in these, however, day is without light. There light leads shadow around in light; here, however, a blind man illumines a blind man, and, being and remaining sight without eyes, ministers to the darkness what he does not have. Here night darkens the day with perpetual mist; walking in darkness, it offers blind guidance without light. These paraphrases serve all people throughout the world; in every language they transmit any secret safely, securely, and without danger to the cabalistic binary. Latinity moves barbarians to laughter; barbarism itself generates suspicion in Latins. Yet it matters nothing, or little, since however great the suspicion may have been, it will nonetheless contain every mystery most secretly. Let the diligent operator call back to mind the rules and precepts which we have handed down, lest he labor uselessly and in vain for himself and for others. For understanding is needed; without it the utility of no art will stand firm. In a few words we have shown the path to the greatest things; for finite things we have given a method toward infinite things. From things given and received, greater things are easily prepared for friends. Reader, remember that the retrograde sign can be fixed in every place, until you attain the desired rest. Farewell. From Peapoli, on the first day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
