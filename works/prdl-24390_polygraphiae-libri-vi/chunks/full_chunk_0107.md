But, I pray, the readers will forgive me; for my own inclination indeed drew me back from these childish studies, not to say [unclear], vain ones, but the insistence of certain friends compelled me to write them, to whom I neither could nor ought to deny this without injury to the bond contracted. Nevertheless, led by their entreaty, I also had to join the following little chapter to the preceding ones, and to place numbers in place of letters. The ancients established various and multiple modes of writing by means of figures of numbers, by which, for subtle discovery, they safely transmitted their secrets to one another. Therefore, persuaded by the prayers of friends, we have placed at the end of this sixth book a few methods out of many, by which a way to knowledge of others may lie open to those who do not know them. For if anyone takes pleasure in knowing these things, he will easily be able, once these are known, to climb to higher things, and to vary the discovered methods in manifold alternation according to his own judgment. As a beginning for this matter we have placed a table beneath the figure, which, varied [unclear], will contain in itself an alphabet in the form of a cross, ninety-six.
