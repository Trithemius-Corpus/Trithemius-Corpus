Once the demonstration of our possibility has been shown as briefly, and no less sufficiently, as possible, I do not think that I should weary myself longer in this same matter, since the method provides a path without end, if anyone should need an example for further things. Nor do I think it at all necessary to recall to memory the things that have been set out in the preceding pages, since a keen reader, if he is studious, will also be able, with the Lord’s help, to find the greater things that lie hidden in them. We have handed over to the wise and studious man both the material and the cause; if he wishes to use these prudently, by his own talent he will surpass ours. But whoever desires this arrangement to be more beautiful in its shadow, let him join hand and mind to talent, and make for himself a path more beautifully adorned by paraphrase. Let anyone whom these things delight keep firmly in memory that he must always remain within the prescribed circle; if he passes beyond it in any way, the whole operation vanishes uselessly. There are many names of men; finally, there are almost infinite words for things, villages, cities, provinces, herbs, stones, and words, all of which serve our purposes. From these it is clear that it is not impossible for a book to be made according to the method, if talent is present with diligence, which may surpass our discoveries without limit. But he who is of such dullness that he cannot understand these childish things of ours, when will he penetrate the subtler things that remain? We set an end to the fourth book of the Polygraphy, to which we join a fifth in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
