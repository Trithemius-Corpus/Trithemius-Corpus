But whenever the discourse that precedes is more obscure, it is done for this reason: lest a madman abuse, to destruction, the sword of defense which a man sound in mind and spirit carries for his own protection. Yet for that reason nothing evil is contained in it, since the perverse abuse even good things for evil. Whoever, moreover, presumes to condemn this edition of ours is himself conscious and witness against himself that he has never truly understood it. Mysteries are hidden under [unclear], lest the secrets of owls be made manifest in any way to [unclear]. If it delights anyone to know these things, let the reader approach with envy put far away. For I am a Christian, I am a priest under the rule of the divine father Benedict and a monk; I love Christ, and with whatever sincerity of mind I can, I devoutly adore him always. I have no dealings with demons, I have had none, and with God as my protector I shall have none; no pursuits in magical, necromantic, or profane arts; no occupations, no teachings. Whoever thinks otherwise of me thinks wrongly, does me injury, and defends Bouillus’s open lie. But now let us dismiss these matters, and let us set forth in order what is contained in each book of our Polygraphy.

*Table Or Index Of The Things Which Are Treated In The First Book*

In the first book of this Polygraphy of ours we have arranged and distributed, according to the order of our ternary, the words of the Latin language, as many as are to be seen; by whose use and service all princes throughout the world will find safe obedience in any secret matters whatsoever, which, without danger to the mystery, without mark, and without suspicion of [unclear], will remain forever hidden. It is certain that by these means the journey is safe for owls, never to be violated by any investigation of anyone. Knowledge of the hidden matter is granted to two only, [unclear] when he knows, is brought back to the monad. Ignorance is the mother of error; and he who has scorned to learn the good thing which he does not know, loves blindness. The periphrases themselves are set forth in the manner either of one admonishing or of one supplicating; these we can vary for every visible material thing of this world. For individual [unclear] are suited to mysteries, and the secret matters of the Greeks are not followed but ours are preferred. When you have understood the things that you see, do not rashly despise the hidden things that you do not see. Accidents are perceived; the substance remains invisible. Hence greater things lie hidden, while lesser things are open to those who behold them. Since this is done with reason, no good man will take it for evil, since everyone suitable can from these things, if only he wishes, advance to higher things. But at this point it does not please me to bring forth higher things into the light [unclear].
