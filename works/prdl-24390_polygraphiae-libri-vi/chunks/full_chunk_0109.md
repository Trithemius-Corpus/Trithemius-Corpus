The sixth and last book of our *Polygraphy* we have, by God’s mercy, brought to its close. In it, concluding our discourse with the wandering stars, we warn the reader in certain points. First, that as often as he uses an alphabet, he should prudently add the character or mark of some sign or planet, for the sake of a twofold usefulness. For by this means excessive labor is removed from the friendly reader, and the mystery will remain secret, since anyone ignorant of the craft, seeing a table written with numbers and marked beforehand with the characters of signs or planets, will think that it pertains to the method of astronomy, and will suspect that no mystery at all lies hidden. For this reason we have not begun [unclear] with the official letters, but from a distance, lest we give anyone occasion for suspicion. Nevertheless prudence must be used by writers, since an astute reader who is touched by suspicion of something hidden, if he detects the function of one number, will easily compute the whole alphabet.

Second, let anyone who desires to be a shrewd practitioner of this matter remember that whenever in any word a letter is doubled, as often happens, it should be written only once and marked above with a point. For example, I wish to write “Ioannes” from the alphabet of Aries: I do not duplicate 18, which is n, but write thus, 14. 19. 6. 18. 10. 23., “ioanes,” “abas,” so that every sign of suspicion may be removed. In similar cases similar caution must be exercised; for unless the practitioner is endowed with skill, and knows that greater things are to be found from small ones, he will not be a fit person to whom these very small matters of ours ought to be revealed. Not everything is to be communicated indiscriminately to everyone, especially things whose use can be turned to good and to evil. Moved reasonably by this consideration, we have plainly handed down in these six books of the *Polygraphy* many things to those who wish to know them, but we have allowed far more secrets to lie hidden beneath things manifest. Finally, we have written a key that lays open the whole work, showing how each metaphor is to be understood; yet by the counsel of certain men we have ordered it to remain hidden, so that studious persons may not lack material for exercising their memory, and that the cause of knowing it may be taken away from the wicked, who are not willing to use it well. In each passage many mysteries lie hidden, and the greater the things you discover from these, the more ample are those that remain to be found.

A way is prepared for beginners; for those who advance in it, it will not be difficult, in the course of time, to discover many rare and very great things. Here he who did not know how will learn to write Latin, and prudently to wrap all the mysteries of his will in a foreign language. But so that the ignorant man may be able to understand not only his own mysteries which he wraps up, but also open speech of which he has until now always been ignorant, the matter is not one of great labor, if the paraphrast has fully followed our intention in this volume. For beneath the bark lie the kernels of our *Steganography*, buried by just reason, lest they lie open to unworthy babblers. Finally it was worth the trouble that one work should not prejudice the other, but that its own right should be distributed to each unimpaired.

We, weary after a long voyage, here cast anchor in the sea under the name of JESUS CHRIST, the savior of the faithful, on the twenty-first day of the month of March, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1508, in the eleventh indiction of the Romans, at [Paeapolis?], a city of the eastern Franks, which they call Würzburg, Herbipolis.
