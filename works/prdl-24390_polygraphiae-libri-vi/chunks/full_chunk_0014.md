*A Little Index, or Table, of the Things Contained in the Sixth Book*

In the sixth book, which is the last book of our *Polygraphy*, after introducing mental narration, we have set out in order almost all the minutiae of the preceding signs and degrees, and, with arithmetical distributions, have drawn the functions of the letters into the service of a cabalistic operation. One thing lies hidden here in the bark, and another is found in the common operation of the signs. For just as the spirit in the blood gives life to the human body, so the hidden power in the art commends the operation. Therefore whoever needs knowledge of this invention should open the eyes of his understanding, so that he may comprehend what is the property of each operation. For we desire all things to be pure for good men in such a way that, if it were possible, they should never become manifest to the wicked. A cause compelled us to fit Latin letters to numbers, lest, if we were too obscure, the intention of our mind should be plain to no one. Yet this novelty, lest it should become public without reasonable cause, had to be mystically concealed under unknown signs and marks of numbering. For [unclear], nocturnal things are stronger than diurnal ones; and just as night in its order overtakes day, so light shrinks from darkness. What nature denies, no one successfully dares. We ask the reader not to condemn this laborious work of ours before he understands it, but, that he may be a judge without rashness, let him prudently remove the bark, until he comes to the hidden kernel. The work is good and natural, capable of being turned to good use or bad, according as the desire of each one’s will is moved. The sword is a good instrument, and it too is turned to a good use whenever it is carried for defense without injury to one’s neighbor. But if it is stretched out unjustly to harm anyone, by abuse a good thing is relaxed into evil. Therefore, so that good men may have something by which they may prudently attend to their affairs, *Polygraphy* is written by us; and lest it furnish the wicked with an occasion for sinning, the kernel is hidden in the bark under various enigmas. He who is good does not abuse the good for evil; rather, if he can, he turns evil into good.

*What Are the [unclear] of This Work*

We undertook the labor of the present work, rare and arduous as it is, for no other reason than to provide for the utility of those in need. I say “in need” not of food, not of drink, but of assistance in the preservation of mystical things without danger. We therefore judge it worthwhile if we briefly note the uses and advantages that may come to those using these inventions of ours. For indeed, since in our private affairs there are no matters such that we need these doctrines for the concealment of secrets, we have certainly labored for those whose [command?] had to be obeyed. The first advantage is that a complete understanding of this work gives the person in need an easy method by which you will make the secret of your mind known, safely, secretly, and without any suspicion on the part of any observers, to one who knows the skill of the art, however widely there may be need, at whatever distance of places, by regulated speech. What lies hidden in these things, the work of *Steganography* brings out, for it does not require a messenger who knows the secrets he carries. It would be otherwise in matters admitting the light, according to the common custom of us all. Do not take the matter otherwise than it is.
