*The End of the First Book of Our Polygraphy*

We rejoice that the end of the first book of our *Polygraphy* is at hand; by it, made readier for the matters that remain, we judge that the powers of our talent must be tested. For newly invented things sometimes trouble the beginner, because the one whom all follow has no one preceding him. The first beginning of any invention is the more difficult; afterwards, when it has been made firm, it brings greater confidence to the author. Therefore, completing the first book of our invention with God’s favor and help, we have divided the whole into three hundred eighty-four minutiae, assigning to each, in order, as is seen, its proper degrees of periphrases. And these form a sufficiently beautiful Christian harmony: sixteen different stations, with two hundred and nine thousand phrases duly joined together in all respects. Nothing is lacking here for students of these inventions of ours, so that in mystical, secret, and hidden matters, even those to be announced more remotely with the greatest safety, they may have the fullest knowledge and faculty. All things that are contained in the circle of the art proceed from a point; their atoms constitute a circular line, and, by a multitude of light, a straight one. Whatever is outside the line is said always to be enclosed among the minutiae and paraphrases, and never goes out. Therefore, whenever you have crossed the straight line of the day and have not fulfilled the purpose of the nocturnal syntax, turn the head back to the tail, and, once a joining has been made, let there be a binding of both to one another. Arrange the shadow in such a way that it does not confuse the sun by similarity of sound. Nor should you fear shame if, in the first calculation, the day has not comprehended the shadow; count it together with the other until the necessary proportion follows according to your wish.
