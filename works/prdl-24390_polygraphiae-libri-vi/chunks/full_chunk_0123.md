[In great?] and difficult matters one must be on guard against uniformity, because this mode of writing, on account of that very thing, can easily be suspect. But whoever wishes prudently to use his natural industry will find subtler things from ours, and what we have handed down in a not sufficiently polished form he will easily make better. In the numerical orchema great secrets lie hidden; if anyone has properly understood it, he will penetrate many mysteries of numbers. For we had neither time nor reason on this occasion to publish everything, but in our judgment what we have done will be enough for the instruction of those to whom our discoveries will someday be pleasing, so that they may have many methods not only of writing secretly whatever they wish to absent partners in the art, but also one method of extending it into many better and subtler ones, according to each person's capacity of understanding. Likewise, since the numerical orchemata have been made sufficiently open and clear in their places, and at the same time their tables set forth, it was not necessary here to repeat things once said, lest excessive length produce weariness for the reader. But I did not think it necessary to set down an example of writing more secretly by means of numerical figures, since there is no difficulty in the method of knowing it for all who possess even moderate ability. With similar ease the very ancient signs of numbering that we have brought back to light can be understood and grasped; by them the knowledge of numbers is carried on to infinity. For whenever a stroke is placed above a smaller number, a thousand is indicated. Thus X [with a stroke] means ten thousand; C [with a stroke], one hundred thousand; M [with a stroke], a thousand thousand; XL [with a stroke], ten times a thousand thousand; CC [with a stroke], a hundred times a thousand thousand; MM [with a stroke] means a thousand times a thousand thousand, and so consequently as far and as much as you wish to advance in numbering, without limit.
