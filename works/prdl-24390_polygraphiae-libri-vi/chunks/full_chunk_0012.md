But if Phoebus has encroached upon night, or night upon the sun, through which the confusion of an eclipse would arise, either let unity resume the binary which it sent forth in the light, or let the shadow of night be brought back to the first line of the sun. Keep fixed in memory whatever has anywhere been produced in number, weight, and measure. Therefore, when you have cut the knot of the difficulty mentioned, count the minutiae of night, note also the paraphrastic expressions of daylight, and so adjust the one by the other that the necessary proportion may follow the operation. Take care not to interrupt the necessary labor while the eclipse of the sun lasts. Attend no less to being mindful of the ancient fathers, Thuyscon, who, Noah's grandson, is the father of the Teutons, and Marcomer, who is read to have first led the people of the Franks from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine. For the Franks are brothers of the Greeks, and, mingled with the Germans, once made two languages from one. Hence, according to the oracle, g.g.g. are taken up by those who have lost one g. Finally, student and [inquirer?] of polygraphy, you are warned that, after you, being eager for these inventions, have obtained the true method of operation, you should carefully guard everything, and not pour it out indiscriminately into the knowledge of all, especially filthy men. For this reason do not ask, since you know that there is nothing in the world so good and holy that the wicked do not rather often abuse it for evil.

*An index of the things treated in the second book of the Polygraphy.*

In the second book of the Polygraphy, nothing new is composed apart from the first; in it only the paraphrastic expressions of the day are varied. Therefore, whatever we said must be observed in that book, we warn must also be kept in this one. For the symbols of both books have been arranged in the same order under the path of light, and necessarily changed only in the signification of the paraphrastic expressions. In both books multiple progressions can be made, according to the capacity of human intelligence, when words are varied by metathesis. The [feronomata?] are also changed at will, whenever the minutes are converted into whole degrees. When this is done, a marvelous work in every respect will arise in the art, and the world, produced in an image in light, will stand, and there will be no fear at all of [unclear] for the operator. I could have given these matters a more beautiful face, if I had approached the task induced to write them either by some hope of profit or by the use of my own advantage. But since none of these things induced me to write, it seemed enough to have given those who desire it the occasion and the material to add more polished things to our inventions, and to adorn art with art.
