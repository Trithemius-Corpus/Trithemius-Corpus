Again, as Bede attests, there were some whose purpose, advancing to broader things, bound the wind, invisible to the eyes, to visible things, and compelled many growing things, and no less stones and shrubs, which lack voices, to speak with a human tongue. Why should I mention brute animals, since all things obey art? This secret is rightly preferred to all discoveries thus far, through which hidden things assume the strength and power of mysteries. Yet reason bids us hide the great in the small, and by no means entrust pearls to swine. From nothing nothing is brought back. The known multitude of visible things is great; but invisible things remain remote from us, if these are prudently fitted to them, the enlightened mind of man would know and behold wondrous things. But to discuss these matters at greater length is not the purpose of the present work; since they are greater and weightier, they require another work and another time. Matthias, the most powerful king of Hungary, and a hammer harder than adamant of the Turks, who in our age waged many victorious battles with the enemies of the cross of Christ, discovered many things by wonderful talent and art; he adorned owls with paraphrases, held his own mysteries, secret in manifold variety, and by manifest industry destroyed the counsels of his enemies. He preferred wisdom to gold; therefore he always triumphed as a glorious victor.

A sound mind is present in a wise man in a well-ordered body; borne upward by desire for higher understanding, when through the likeness of the binary it has returned from the ternary into unity, thereafter it will now easily rise to the denary. Pythagoras of Samos, that philosopher, warned his disciples not to forget the quaternary, in which the first perfection of numbering is comprehended. For thus we count: one, two, three, four. Halt your mind, for you have ten. For the denary is the head of perfection. What are eleven, except ten and unity? What is thirty? Three times ten. What is forty? Four times ten. You have a hundred, and you have by no means departed from the quaternary. And what is a hundred, except ten times ten? Deep mysteries lie hidden in these things, and they are manifest to few.

For love of brevity we pass over many in silence, who, assisting nature by art, applied prudence to secrets, and what seemed impossible to many who did not know, by keen study brought into easy use. A cultivated mind can do great things, and he who cleaves to God becomes higher than the world in understanding. Many are the mysteries of kings, and counsels full of cares, in which confidence and faith are required; and rare faith harms the believer, and doubt, even when present, more often admits [unclear]. Therefore it will have been most advantageous for all princes to be philosophers, and endowed with singular wisdom, just, gentle, benevolent, and distinguished by munificence, so that they themselves may not wish to deceive others, nor easily be able to be overthrown by others. Justice alone, the companion of prudence, makes a prince glorious. glorious.
