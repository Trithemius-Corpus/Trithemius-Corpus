*Preface to the explanation of the whole work of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James of Würzburg.*

I trust that no one who reads these things will judge me either arrogant or superfluous because I myself have written a commentary on the Polygraphy recently composed by me, especially if he has been able to weigh precisely what reason induced us to speak obscurely. For whatever can do harm when made public had reasonably to be concealed in mystical language. But the subject that we treat in the Polygraphy is, for the advantage of the commonwealth among good men, very good, useful, and honorable; and for that reason, persuaded by a reasonable cause, we wrote it, so that by its service we might make good men safe and secure from the investigation of the wicked when they communicate their secrets to one another. But lest an opportunity be offered to the wicked and perverse, who even more frequently misuse good inventions, to deceive the simpler and those less cautious in judging matters, we handed down in it mystical or hidden methods of writing, and covered the truth lying hidden in the marrow with certain coverings of figures. Yet we do not at all doubt that any industrious man, eager with desire for these things, will easily grasp everything, if he applies even a modest persistence in searching them out. For the things I have handed down are neither so difficult nor enveloped in such great mysteries that they cannot be wholly penetrated by a studious man. For everywhere we have so tempered the riddles of our words that they may easily be accessible to learned and studious men, and nevertheless remain inaccessible to sluggish children of darkness.
