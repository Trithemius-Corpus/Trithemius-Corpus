But because this method of writing secretly under a long narrative of open discourse contains rather few sentences of hidden intent, it will perhaps therefore be usable only in great and difficult matters, especially among those who like leisure and dislike labor. We shall therefore give, in the four following books, easier methods of writing, though indeed, when looked at, they are not wholly free from suspicion, because they elude the understanding of very many. But in the *Steganographia* the methods of writing most secretly and without any suspicion are richer, where a word does not represent a letter, but one whole word represents another whole word suited to the mystery. Let us set down an example of this matter: if you wish to warn a friend of some impending danger under such a narrative of words: Melancius, Tiberius, John, and Peter, servants of the count of [aloto], have conspired to kill you, and on the fourth day after Lawrence’s day, at night, they will surround your house; consider what you should do. Steganography thus veils this secret: I beg you, dearest friend, to send me by the bearer of the present letter ten florins as a loan, because they are very necessary to me for the construction of a certain building; I shall faithfully repay them to you. Who would seek or suspect that this secret lay hidden in the discourse written above? Let us set down the same thing also under another narrative: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who willed to become man for us, to suffer, and to die, have mercy on me, an unworthy sinner, now and in the terrible hour of my death. Behold, now you have one hidden sentence of your heart in two narratives, veiled within the bounds of steganography, in brief but faithful discourse, by which the whole method can not even be suspected, nor, if one should seek for a hundred years, could it be found [unclear], even if all the learned came together. Finally, steganography teaches you to hide this very secret sentence of your heart not only in one or two written narratives, but in almost infinite ones, by the method already mentioned, and to announce it to whomever you wish most safely, most secretly, and most securely. All methods of this sort can also be changed by metathesis into infinite [unclear], and all things can be written and announced through them. But why the work of the *Steganographia* remains buried in silence has been said sufficiently at the beginning of our *Polygraphia*; therefore it did not have to be repeated here. Let it lie hidden, then, lest it be seen by the ungrateful.

*Obscure Explanation of the Things Contained in the Third Book of Our Polygraphia*

In the third book of the *Polygraphia* we have handed down a newly devised method of writing secretly, quite secure and safe indeed, but, to all who look at it, suspect because of the hidden secret. For no one, however learned, will discover the hidden mystery without knowledge of the tradition, since he cannot understand even the open writing. We have arranged fictitious words, signifying nothing in themselves, but representing only the letters of the alphabet set before them, through which you may write and make known all the secrets of your mind to a friend most securely, however absent he may be. But the unskilled, ignorant of the art, when they have seen the writing, will think it is some foreign language; they will be wholly ignorant of what it contains. But when you wish to write or announce something to an absent person by the method of this book, take care first of all that you distinguish every single word properly from the others, so that each one can be very clearly discerned from another, lest the person to whom you write take one for two, or two for one, because that would give birth to a great error. Let us set down an example of this matter.

Abrin madon badis, cadulur pasu, adin loroff masara damis, boda omodromi drus uares mastron pha lares medas rassut mandebor tara, mesen baiu phosat, galos, prosol fane lemoros, aroso cabilneso, hamol heliet rhamil fesia doron hubur rodex. In this narrative of fictitious speech is contained this brief sentence of a secret: a friend warning a friend about danger. Do not come to court, because the king is angry with you. You see how easy, safe, and secure this method is for writing and announcing whatever you wish to a friend who understands it. In all cases, however, you should know that care must be taken, as was said before, that you do not take more than one word from any alphabet, nor again skip over any alphabet.
