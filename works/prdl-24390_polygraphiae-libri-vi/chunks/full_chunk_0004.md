In the year of the Lord 1514, when a fierce war had broken out between the realm and Philip, count palatine, Trithemius, through fear of the enemy, with his library and the goods of the monastery, [went] to Kreuznach, the nearest town; and I likewise returned to my own affairs. But in the year 1506, in the month of October, Trithemius himself, spurred by the envy of certain men, accepted the abbacy of Saint James at Würzburg and resigned that of Sponheim. In quiet he writes various works, whose titles, when asked, he recently sent me as follows. To Maximilian Caesar he added a wonderful work, which he entitled *Polygraphia*, six books. To the same, a key to the same *Polygraphia*, one book. Answers to eight questions of the same Caesar, one book. To the same, on the seven intelligences that move the spheres, one book. To Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg, prince elector, twenty books of various questions, speeches, and propositions. To the same, on foreknowledge lawful and unlawful for a Christian, two books. To the same he wrote that wonderful and laborious work of *Steganography*, eight books; which that rash Bouillus, not understanding it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely. To the same prince, a book of letters, one book. To the same, against malefactors and all arts and superstitions prohibited by the Church, five books. To the same, on the causes and cure of the falling sickness and of spells, three books. To Jacobus Trithemius, his brother, an itinerary of his life, two books. To Burghard von Horneck, physician, on the property of nuns, one book.

He wrote a history or annals on the origin, kings, and deeds of the people of the German Franks, three large volumes, beginning the narrative with King Marcomer, who in the four hundred thirty-third year before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine, down to the twenty-eighth or twenty-ninth year of Maximilian Caesar. To Laurentius, bishop of Würzburg and duke of Eastern Franconia, he wrote an epitome or breviary of the histories of those same three volumes, three books. To the same, on the dukes and bishops of the Franks of Würzburg, one book. On the miracles shown at the memorial of Our Lady Saint Mary at Dettelbach, two books. On the miracles of the same shown in the nettle-grove at Heilbronn, six books. An apologetic work against Carolus Bouillus, who slandered things he did not understand, one book. On the origin and kings of the Franks, one book. A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, of Saint James at Würzburg, one book. To Albert, of the margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, the life of the divine Rabanus, arch-prelate, three books. On the translation of the same from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To Johann, count palatine and duke of Bavaria, count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, on the origin, kings, and dukes of the Bavarians, together with the counts palatine of Sponheim, four books. He has in hand a laborious work on demons, profane arts, malefactors, and superstitious persons, which will have twelve books; in these he casts out, confounds, and overturns all the tricks of the diabolical arts. Of Würzburg letters, which he wrote in that abbacy, [unclear]: such as offices, a rosary, a missal office, various prayers, together with proses or sequences on Saint Anne, grandmother of our Savior. He wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene. A rosary on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord. A rosary on Saint Peter the Apostle. A rosary on Saint John the Evangelist. At the request of Johann, archbishop of Trier, a sequence on Anne, which begins, *Jesu coelorum regis*, to the melody *Ave praeclara*. Another to be sung on her feast, which begins, *Exultent in hac die*. A third for the Carmelite province at Augsburg; it begins, *Iubilemus in honore*. A fourth on Saint Hildegard, virgin, which begins thus, *Laus fit tibi rex aeternae gloriae*. A fifth on Saint Rupert, duke of Bingen, which begins, *Exultent in hac die*. A sixth on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord; it begins thus, *Cantemus inclyti*. A seventh on Saint Rabanus, archbishop.
