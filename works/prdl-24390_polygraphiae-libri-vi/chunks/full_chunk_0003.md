Also the anathemas of Saint Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria, against the heretics, promulgated in the third synod at Ephesus, and certain letters of the same synod. He also wrote, for the edification of monks, a notable work, which he entitled *On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons*, three books. He wrote certain other smaller works, the titles of which he did not indicate to me.

These, most beloved brother Nicholas, are the Trithemian little works which, while abbot for twenty-three years, he composed by lamplight at Sponheim, to the honor of God, amid cares and the continual occupation of household business, reading or writing day and night whenever he was able to steal leisure for himself. He brought into the Sponheim library the greatest treasury of books in every faculty during the years in which he presided over it, not only by the art of printing but also by the hand of scribes. For when I was with him for the space of one year, to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, I counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eight hundred had been decently written with the pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his mouth that he had paid out more than two thousand florins for books; he had not taken these from the substance of the monastery, which was always very meager, but, since he was known and dear to many princes, he paid out for books, for the most part, the gifts with which he was honored by them. For he is the greatest lover and guardian of books above all whom I know living in the flesh today. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot in Sponheim, has alienated many volumes; whether this is true, certainly, is by no means established to me.
