*The fourth book of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Saint James the Greater, Apostle, at Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict, begins.*

*The order of reason demanded* that the fourth book of our Polygraphy be made unlike the preceding ones in character, invention, and ease, so that those wishing to share in the secrets of our matters may attain this not in one way, but by various and different ways. We have extended [the practice?], comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that the word, through the minutia always joined to the monad, may be recognized in the light in any order [unclear]. No distinction is found here between unity and the binary; nor will one who wishes to work need to have the instrument of the art at hand, since what follows the first without an intermediary reveals the hidden mystery. Hence the prudent, intelligent person, experienced in the science, will satisfy the operation at every time and place. The binary received from unity, whence it produces the ternary. The first recognition of this cryptology is easy at first sight, after the moon has been illuminated by the sun even with a single point. [Unclear] and it can come about that the diligent knower may succeed without the guidance of a teacher. Yet I think this is to be understood only in this book, and in none of the preceding ones. Concerning the following ones, judgment will be given in its proper place. Among all the cabalistic methods of secrets that we have given up to the present, the easier progression of this fourth book can be extended to infinity. Therefore it is enough that I have handed down to those desiring fuller things both the form and the reason of an example. This light of understanding easily penetrates all things. I wrote at Peapolis, on the ninth day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
