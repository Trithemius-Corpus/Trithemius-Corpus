*An indication of the things that are contained in the third book of the Polygraphia.*

In the third book of the Polygraphia, having passed beyond the prescribed limits of our nation by the counsel of reason, we are led to foreign things; placed [unclear] in the midst of the Franks, we wander farther through images and likenesses of barbarians. For with almost four thousand mythological phrasmata arranged in order by art, we [unclear] a very easy path for the artificial unity, by which it will communicate symbolic notions to its binary more safely at every time. Here the binary is harder for the unity; unless our philologist is clear-sighted with prudence, he will make the binary contrary to the monad. For only the ternary, which proceeds from unity and the binary, connects both within itself as a palindrome. But if the necessary utility of reason extends beyond the limits of the proposed matter, then as often as necessary the paraphrasmata would [unclear] by minutes from the beginning of the day, until the minutes establish a degree or point in the smallest things. The face of this image knows perpetual silence, nor does it fear the deceits of the bacucei. It is extended in whatever abundance you wish; it is varied without number.

*A little index or tablet of the things that are treated in the fourth book.*

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia we have put much trust in the binary, which, after all obstacles have been removed, without the labor of difficulty, at first sight we have reduced to unity. Hence here we use a circle in great matters, a line or point in the smallest. Nor does it matter if night surpasses day, or if light does not accord with darkness, because a circle receives no end, and fullness admits no void. For they are contraries; as often as they succeed one another, one is corrupted by the other. He hands down in this fourth book of the Polygraphia a singular mystery useful to the binary, since that airy base itself, the old composer, is never designated in anything other than the binary. But in that part the levity of owls is always suspect to us in difficult matters. The construction of this thing makes paraphrasmata with infinite atoms.

*Index or tablet of the things that are treated in the fifth book.*

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have written in order on two tables the letters of the Latins, which we Germans use; one of these speaks straight, while the other is called reversed. Beneath these we have placed ancient dances, so that what we seek with labor among alien things we may see without difficulty in our own. For besides those things that have been placed openly by us, many more secrets still lie hidden in the inner parts; through understanding them the path for the worker is opened to greater things, which nevertheless are by no means proposed to everyone. We have subjected letters to numbers, by which hidden things may be made manifest according to our will. Hence, by the skilled operation of this book, they securely hand over all messages to cabalistic persons, although the mysteries are not free from suspicion of the bacucei. For every form of metathesis of signs generates suspicion in the observer, although it brings no harm to the owls. But you, who have attained the understanding of this work, will easily penetrate every transposition of letters among our Latins.
