What we promised at the beginning of the third book of our work Polygraphia, for obtaining the shadow and likeness of a new gloss before the inexperienced, we have sufficiently fulfilled by way of example in a compendium of labor. We have entrusted night, safely serving secrets, to be led through the shadows of periphrases; we have inserted numbers in the order of four lines down to the end, adapted for the use of those who wish. The places are degrees of minutiae, having fixed 3,150, although moments of hours remain, if you wish, infinite, for making secret in every nation and language of the world whatever secret matter may occur. The circle, arising linearly from a point, as often as night exceeds the image of light, by as much as you wish: if there is need, you will be able to bring the work back to the path by which you came. If there is much left over of night, bring back the dragon's tail to the original tail-place, into the head. But the pair must by all means be certified from the known signs of unity. He who hungers much should labor much, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is it a matter of great labor to lead wherever you wish that which advances of its own accord. Here, therefore, we set an end to the third book of our Polygraphia, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell. From Peapoli, on the seventh day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ our Savior 1508, in the eleventh Indiction.

*Here is the end of the third book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Peapoli, of the Order of Saint Benedict.*
