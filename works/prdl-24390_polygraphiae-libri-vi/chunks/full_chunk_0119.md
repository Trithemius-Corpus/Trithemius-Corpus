*Explanation of the fourth book of our Polygraphia.*

[unclear]

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia we have in like manner placed, in alphabetical order, words contrived by us, but in one point alone we deliberately differ from the preceding book. For in the preceding book the words of their own proper force contain nothing other than that they represent the letters set before them at the head. But in the fourth book, which we now have in hand, each word contains its own proper force, and nevertheless designates the letters placed before it. They contain within what they display without, because the second letter of each word always serves the hidden mystery, as we shall make clear by the following example: Ecorach banasar dulay semalas, chorion suriel anephor schabaras, busarat vimalo tumelech amasar. To understand the mystery of this, take only the second letter from each word; you will find this hidden sense: “Beware this man.” For the second letter in Ecorach is c, in banasar a, in dulay u, in semalas e. Join these four letters and they will make this word: “Beware.” And so one proceeds with the rest up to the end of the book; if anything of the hidden mystery remains to be written, one must return to the beginning as many times as necessary, until the secret has been wholly completed. In this very secret mode of writing, if someone has been trained by long practice, he has no need of a book, but will readily compose ex tempore words suited to his purpose. But someone less practiced in composing must have a copy of the book from which to write, since without the book he may easily find the hidden secret everywhere by means of the second letters. Yet in great, difficult, or dangerous matters, this method of writing is not to be trusted quite safely, because the industry of a skilled man could, by chance, discover the hidden mystery. This method of writing can also be altered by an intelligent man, so that the significant letters are placed in the third or fourth position of the word, with the words altered.

*Brief Explanation of the Fifth Book of our Polygraphia.*

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have transmitted various modes of writing secretly by transposition of ordinary letters, arranged in such coordination that under their veil we shall be able safely to write and announce everything to an absent friend. Now, to speak in our own manner, the progression of the transposition of letters is almost infinite, since from one alphabet innumerable alphabets arise by metathesis. First we described a straight table containing twenty-four alphabets; by understanding it, as many alphabets can be composed as there are stars counted in the firmament of heaven. For as many characters as there are in that table, so many times, by art and in order, do ten hundred thousand alphabets arise. After this we distributed an inverted table, which arises into another as often as you change the first letter at the head. The first letter in the straight table is b, in the inverted table z. Whenever you put any other letter in their place, you will find the table varied and entirely new in all respects, and so on to infinity. Then we expanded the first straight table, placing over the head of each black letter, in red, the letter which it represents, so that we might provide the reader with an easier mode of writing. This is the mode of writing: in the first black alphabet, take one letter of the hidden sentence; from the second, another; from the third, a third; and so consequently up to the end. When you have reached it, remember to return as often as necessary to the first order, until by concealing it you have completed the secret mystery of your mind.
