How just, holy, and acceptable to God was the life and conduct of the most blessed mother Anne. Chapter 6.

Whoever reads the records of the holy fathers with devout attention can discover with what great holiness the life of the most blessed mother Anne shone forth. Concerning her the holy bishop Epiphanius composed certain things; he records that her life was distinguished by justice, innocence, and purity, so that she had no equal in Israel. Honorius also, the priest of Autun, a holy and learned man, in the Mirror of the Church published in a clear discourse certain things in praise of the holy mother Anne, and showed in brief summary how holy she always was. Saint Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, once a devotee of the mount and order of Carmel, when writing to Pope Celestine I about the birth of the blessed Mother of God, the ever-virgin Mary, inserted many proclamations of praise concerning the life and conduct of Saint Anne. But why is it necessary for me to cite many teachers unknown to many, when blessed Jerome the priest, known throughout the whole world, is reported to have written on this very matter certain things worthy of praise, which are possessed by many?

Therefore the most blessed Anne, as far as her fleshly descent was concerned, was born of the tribe of David, from Bethlehem, her father being Solanus and her mother Emerentia, religious and devout parents according to the decrees of the law, who brought her up from infancy in the fear of the Lord together with her sister Ismeria. Moreover, Ismeria her sister gave birth to Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist. Joachim of Nazareth, of the tribe of Judah, received Anne as his wife; and she gave birth to the daughter who afterward became the Mother of God, the ever-virgin Mary.

O Joachim and Anne, parents worthy of eternal remembrance, who, serving the Lord God in simplicity, obtained an inestimable treasure from heaven! Who could sufficiently praise these parents worthily? Indeed, unless their life had been most holy, they would never have brought forth a child so pleasing to the Lord. They were of moderate but sufficient means, acquiring what was necessary from every kind of pasturage and agriculture. From these possessions they are said to have made three equal portions: one they gave to the temple, another to the poor, and the third they kept for themselves and their household servants.

As for their character, they were most zealous in observing the law, proved in the fear of the Lord, and perfect in love of God and neighbor. They adorned their marriage bed with the fullest virtues, by which they advanced together, so that they pleased both God and human beings. In concord, innocence, and piety they were of one mind, and rich in every virtue. Both drew their origin from the house of David; both were perfect before the Lord; both awaited the redemption of Israel with longing. Both had been instructed from earliest age in the fear of the Lord; both were dear to God; both were chosen to be the parents of the Mother of God.

Yet Anne is believed, without any injury to her husband, to have been filled with greater grace; for in her venerable womb the most holy Mother of God was formed without any stain. In her heart the love of God found for itself a pleasing dwelling; from her most pure blood the Holy Spirit built a tabernacle for the Son of God. Worthy mother of the worthiest mother, she was the first of all to hasten the coming of our Savior.

Upon her, brothers, direct the eyes of your mind; listen to the pattern of her life, and imprint your conduct upon her footsteps. Do you wish to know what sort of woman she was? Turn over the writings of the earlier fathers. She was dignified in her gait, calm in a face shining with grace, mature in counsel, composed in speech, most holy in worship and in action. She meditated on the law of the Lord day and night, firm in faith, strong in hope, filled with twin charity. She had learned always to devote herself to prayer, and she awaited the coming of the Lord with the greatest longing. She was honorable in deed, sober in habit, humble in mind. No one ever found her running about through streets and squares; no one heard her chattering uselessly in the houses of neighbors; no one saw her going to dances and [unclear].
