And we have found that laypeople too have very often been freed by Anne’s merits from the gravest temptations of flesh and spirit; some, placed in want and poverty, have unexpectedly been abundantly provided for by Anne’s intercessions; others have been healed from serious calamity. Who could recount how many have been cured, through the aid of the most loving mother Anne, of harmful sadness of mind and melancholy? How many also have come through adversity unharmed, fearless among enemies and robbers, safe amid the dangers of the sea?

Anne, the most holy mother, has been accustomed to rescue many devoted to her from various temptations, to free them from the contagion of plague, and to heal them of various infirmities. Who could tell how many she has brought back from the worst despair to hope in mercy; how many she has recalled from the hardest habit of sin to the paths of repentance; how many, living lukewarmly both in religion and outside it, she has inflamed with the fire of divine love? By her merits and intercessions women in childbirth have been freed from dangers otherwise close to death; many also, as we read, have been snatched from the sentence of eternal damnation.

Finally, she is said to have kept some of her worshippers from the disgrace of infamy; others she is remembered to have freed from desperate illness, some from chains and prisons, and certain others also from imminent dangers of death. We read too that by her merits some have been recalled from death to life; others, at the point of death, have been most mercifully visited by her and, before they left the world, made already secure concerning heaven. Often also she is said to have enlightened the dull minds of certain people, so that those who before had been despised as unlearned afterwards became an object of admiration to many. Orators too, often stumbling in speech, she is said, when invoked, to have strengthened so greatly that they found themselves made not only capable, but even readier in speaking to the end. Finally, we read that lost things have been wonderfully found through the invocation of Anne.

But why do I strive after what is boundless? Even if I had an iron voice and a hundred tongues, I could not run through all Anne’s benefits, by which she has known how to come to the aid of her worshippers. There are found as many benefits from her as there are hardships in human life. From whatever tribulation anyone cries to her with devotion, he can obtain help. She extinguishes the flames of fleshly desire; she turns the little sparks of sadness in the mind into saving joy; she grants the necessities of life; she preserves a good and honorable reputation. She frees women in danger in childbirth; she softens pains; she puts demons to flight. When invoked, she helps those laboring in tribulation; she brings travelers back to their homeland; she hears the wretched who cry to her. She knows how to give whatever a Christian person may lawfully ask, provided only that devout faith commend the one who prays.

Concerning all the things that I have merely touched on by mentioning them generally, we have specific examples readily at hand, by which we could show that we are writing not uncertain things, but deeds done in truth. But it is not our present purpose to weave a history, but to set forth the praises of the blessed mother Anne as far as we can.
