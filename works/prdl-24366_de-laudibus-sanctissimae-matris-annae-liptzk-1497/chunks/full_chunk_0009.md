Against those who tear down, despise, and attack the feast and memory of Saint Anne. Chapter 4.

Take heed, hateful man, whoever you are, who disparage the festive memory of the most holy mother Anne; who attack her honor with impious talkativeness; who blaspheme her worshipers with rash presumption. What displeases you in the most holy mother of the Mother of God? Do you doubt her merits? Do you disapprove of her lineage? She is holy; she is beloved of God. Foolish and senseless man, why do you blame what is good in the servants of God, which you ought to have imitated? Why do you withhold the honor due to the saints? Why do you persecute the worshipers of most blessed Anne without cause?

You reproach them and say, “Why do you take up new feasts, you who do not celebrate the old ones?” Listen, profane man. Does the feast of Saint Anne seem new to you, when it is proved from the most ancient records of the elders? If it is new, how could the holy fathers have celebrated it in ancient times? It is not new, in whose honor Saint Augustine is said to have composed an elegant sermon many years ago. It cannot be new, concerning which very ancient songs exist. Or how will it be new, whose golden name is found inserted in the most ancient calendars? But to you it is both new and rare, because you have never celebrated her memory.

For the memory of the most blessed mother Anne cannot be new, since the house in which she was accustomed to live in ancient times is read to have been consecrated as a church under her name not long after the Lord’s passion. Near Lyon there is a monastery of the new order on the Île Barbe, where in a certain very ancient chapel the body of the most blessed mother Anne rests with great reverence from the people; Saint Longinus, the soldier of Christ, is said to have founded it first, and, bringing those most holy relics from Jerusalem, to have placed them there. Very ancient records of this matter are found in the said monastery. Do these things seem new to you?

Cease, therefore, to disparage the feast of the most holy mother; cease to tear down her most worthy worship with impious detraction. Do not spread hateful poison against all; do not be angry at everyone because a few displease you; do not carry an injury done by a man against those above. Why do you call the saints of God into blame, perhaps because of a man? Cease, I beg you, cease wishing to exact vengeance for one man, or for a few, from the most holy mother Anne. I do not want you to overcome her holiness by force; I do not want you to deprive her of her honor; I do not want you to attack her through the devotion of her servants.

If a man sins against a man, God will be able to appease him; but he who sins against the saints of God, against the parents of the Son of God, against the mother of the Mother of God, who will pray for him? How can he have God gracious to him who has offended his saints, and indeed his most holy saints? Saint Anne is worthy of every honor, she who deserved to bear the Mother of God without stain of guilt; whoever lessens her honors does injury to God.

Rather, if you are wise, if you understand, withdraw from this folly of yours; flee from this false madness; turn aside from this most wicked blasphemy; undertake repentance for your former error, and wash away your guilt through tears of compunction and [unclear]. But if you do not do this, if you persist in your obstinacy, you will not hinder the will of God, but you will have a share in the lot of the devil.

God has determined to honor Saint Anne in his Church; who will forbid him? Behold, kings and princes celebrate the memory of Saint Anne with religious devotion; behold, bishops of the churches everywhere consecrate altars in her honor; behold, faithful peoples everywhere invoke her name; and you, in your envy, by which you waste away for the devil, are tormented and rage. Monks and religious of both sexes, the whole clergy of the churches of God, celebrate the feast and memory of the holy mother Anne with singular devotion; and you, envious dog, presume to bark against them all. Why do you bark impiously? Why do you mutter wickedly? Be silent, go away, withdraw: not without grave punishment will this impiety of yours be avenged.

Whoever detracts from the honor and holiness of the most blessed mother Anne, whoever blasphemes her worshipers, will not depart from this life without disgrace. He will be confounded with evil confusion before God and human beings. Reproaches and the worst ignominy will cover his head. He will be wretched and pitiable, and there will be no one to grieve over his calamity, all the days that he shall be upon the earth.
