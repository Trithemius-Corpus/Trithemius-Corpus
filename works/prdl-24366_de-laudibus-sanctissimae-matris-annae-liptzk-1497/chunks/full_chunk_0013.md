For she had been accustomed to avoid wandering excursions; Mary remained in the house, in her usual way. Look to both of them: mothers to the mother, virgins to the virgin. Believe me: it is not fitting for your modesty to take pleasure in public; it is not expedient for virgins to be greeted in the forum, because the reverence due to modesty is lessened in one who frequently goes out into public. Since the case of a virgin is one of modesty, she flees public view. Without modesty there is no sincere virginity, nor can one who is too public remain pure.

That the holy mother Anne conceived her daughter, the most blessed Mother of God, without original stain.

Chapter Seven.

Since it greatly contributes to the declaration of the merits of the sanctity of the most blessed mother Anne that she gave birth to the Mother of God without the stain of original guilt, the prerogative of so great an honor must not be passed over by us in silence. And whatever we know of this most pure conception has been set as a sign which, even to the present day, will be contradicted by many, who, while they wish to defend the custom of nature, do not fear to assail the grace of God.

Why do the foolish bark? Why do the ignorant murmur? Is it not lawful for God to do what he wills? Behold, the Church venerates the conception of the Mother of God as pure and without stain; behold, she celebrates the feast with annual devotion, and captious men labor with rash presumption to stain it. Holy and most learned men, past and present, in various kinds of writing, proclaim in their works that this conception was never subject to original stain, and still the sons of Adam rashly attack it. Men of almost every religious order profess, believe, and preach that it was always most pure; to wish to condemn them as erring is the height of madness.

Behold, secular men, religious, monks, devout and learned men celebrate in verse and prose this conception as always most pure; and lukewarm and unlearned men, to whom the name of philosopher seems enough, strive to defile the holiness of the Mother of God. Many, I say, very learned men have published various treatises in its defense; what truly learned man has written the contrary? But you, by whatever name you are unworthily called, who, setting your mouth against heaven, have impiously blasphemed against the most pure Mother of God, you will not have done this without the grave notice of God, since you were moved to write not by piety but by impiety.

To your foolish rashness, then, we know that more than enough answer has been given by learned men in various styles. Nor by our weapons, but by the power of God, will you depart confounded. I do not condemn your error, but your arrogance, your rashness, and your presumption.
