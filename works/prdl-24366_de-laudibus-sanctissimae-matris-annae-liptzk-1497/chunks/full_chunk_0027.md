So many and such great miracles concerning the blessed mother Anne have become known to me that, if I wished to write them all, I would be compelled to enlarge this treatise excessively. The greatest of miracles is this: if through her merits we may deserve to receive compunction and forgiveness of our sins; if to overcome evil habit; if to come to eternal life. For this end, and no other, she is to be venerated by us.

*On the fraternity of the most blessed mother Anne, namely whether it is useful and necessary, especially in our times. Chapter XV.*

Since in these last days charity is growing cold and iniquity greatly abounds, it is very necessary for us, my brothers, that just as almost daily we are stained by sinning, so also through frequent zeal for holy exercise we may every day be renewed in good will. For there has never been any holy institution so fitted to human conduct that, with time, it has not fallen back from its first fervor. Thus all the orders of religions, failing with the passage of time, have withdrawn from the first form of their institution; and unless they had more often been reformed by greater diligence to the paths of regular life, they would have remained in their deformity. The same also happens among Christians, who today are found to have fallen far away from the first institution of the faith. Where now is the former fervor of our faith? Where the devotion of the people? Where the holy way of life of priests? Charity has grown cold, and all the pursuits of religion, together with it, have for the most part perished from among men. Who would believe how great a loss evil morals have already brought upon the Christian faith? Behold, with God permitting it because of the crimes of men, the infidel Saracens, Turks, Tartars, and the other enemies of the Catholic faith have, after slaughtering Christians, brought Asia, Africa, Greece, the Hellespont, and a great part of Europe under their power, so that the twentieth part of the world which once was Christian is not Christian today. And indeed, unless the mercy of almighty God preserves the remnants left in Europe, it will soon be over with the Christian name. Since, therefore, burdened by our sins, we are not sufficient to obtain the mercy of God, the intercessions of the saints must be implored for us with assiduous prayers, so that, appeased by their merits, the eternal judge may grant us pardon and peace, which we do not deserve. And lest devotion, as is customary, fail with time, one must labor with great care so that, by the mutual exhortation of the brothers, it may attain perseverance. Hence a praiseworthy custom has grown up in the Church of God: that the faithful establish congregations in honor of certain saints, which they call fraternities for the increase of devotion. Thus knights act in honor of Saint George; archers, of Sebastian the martyr; pilgrims from Galicia, of James the apostle.
