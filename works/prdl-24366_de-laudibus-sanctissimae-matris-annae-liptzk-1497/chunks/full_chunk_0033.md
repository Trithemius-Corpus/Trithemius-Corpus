Rejoice, Anne, you who are a refuge for the wretched; we pray, we sing, we bring forth songs. On the holy feast, be present with your offspring; now we speak praises to you.

Rejoice, earth shining, fertile with grass and wet, drenched with deifying dew, from whom was born the Virgin, purer than the ether, innocent of sin.

Rejoice, you who had been of royal seed, already [unclear], given to a husband; you had led a chaste marriage, long without offspring, made a mother by a vow.

Rejoice, you who, at the reproach of the priest, [unclear], were confounded; with tears you call upon God. Having been heard, you receive your husband as he comes to meet you at the gate called Golden.

Rejoice, you who joyfully embraced Joachim, your husband, as he came to meet you; and, taught by a heavenly divine power, thus the Mother is conceived in your womb.

Rejoice, sagacious simplicity, praised by an angelic choir, and divided threefold: goods for the temple, for the needy and the poor, and a part for your servants.

Rejoice; the people of the faithful celebrates the feast. [unclear]; and by your prayers, Anne, may you render Christ propitious. Amen.

*An elegiac poem of Mutgerus, venerable regular canon in Heyna, in the diocese of Worms, on Saint Anne.*

Of the splendor of the fatherland, of the brightness of uncreated light, splendid mother of the Virgin Mother, hail.

You, holy one, bore for the Lord a divine maiden, from whom Jesus, the kindly light of the world, came forth.

You, Anne, steadfastly call the one born your grandson, although he himself made the heavens and the ground.

In the flesh Jesus himself is your grandson; he joined supreme divinity to flesh, for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always existed, yet he chooses and deigns to be called your grandson.

Do not hesitate to assail such a grandson with prayers, Anne; we ask you, who will carry all things asked.

If at first he has not bent ready ears, run more swiftly, Anne, to your daughter.

Let your daughter show her breasts to her son Christ; straightway the kindly parent will grant what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to obtain by prayer, let the daughter seek help with her mother.

My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that presently the Lord will hear both as they pray with a gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers one ought to seek the help of Anne and Mary together.

It is established that if you honor the mother of the Lord's mother, [unclear] you cultivate the very title of the Virgin Mother.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it is clear [unclear] that you prepare praises to the Lord in the mother.

May praise, power, and honor be to the Son born from Anne's child, and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*A Sapphic poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the day of Saint Anne.*

Let us celebrate the feasts of sacred Anne, of the mother excellent in merits, who brought forth to the world the mother of the Son of God the Father.

Her venerable [unclear] life is deservedly praised by us: blessed, distinguished by endowment, bringing relief to wretched minds.

She heals the weak, purges all evils, brightens the hearts of the sorrowful with joy, sets fitting things before us, directs and governs, smooths rough things.

Mighty, residing in the court of the highest King, she wipes away stains by prayer, bestows grace, and leads faithful souls to the stars.

Famous mother, help, preserve, refresh, and govern the brothers here who frequent your feasts, uttering vows and praying to you for good things.

May honor and power be to the Thunderer above, who, God simple and likewise triune, has bestowed the crown of great glory upon Anne forever. Amen.

*This Sapphic hymn is sung to the melody of Ut queant laxis, Vita sanctorum, Virginis proles, Quod chorus vatum, Iste confessor, and similar hymns that are Sapphic in their song. From Sponheim, July 7, 1497.*

*An elegiac poem of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on holy mother Anne.*

How great the rewards that Anne gives to her devotees, no one grasps in mind nor can speak with mouth.

She drives harsh things away, obtains pardon, increases the adornments of honor for her own, and causes them to be in heaven.

By contrast, many exceedingly bitter things accompany those who despise mother Anne; take heed beforehand for yourself.

She [crushes?] the proud, casts the powerful down from honor, and [allows?] them often to endure great scandals.

This is clear and remains fixed: Anne offers joyful things to those who honor her, but, grim, brings back many things to her enemies.

Therefore, men, I advise you: serve so great a patroness; let the female choir also be present together in love.

What we write is clearly established by examples; open ears will receive what helps devout hearts.

From Sponheim, July 17, 1493.
