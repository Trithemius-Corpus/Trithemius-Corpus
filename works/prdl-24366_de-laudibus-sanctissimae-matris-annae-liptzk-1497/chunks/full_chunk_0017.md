That holy mother Anne has the highest honor with God and all the saints. Chapter 9.

We believe, and without any hesitation maintain, that holy Anne, the parent of the God-bearer, is of singular merit and honor with God: honor sufficient not only for her own glory, but also fitting and powerful for bringing help to us. For if we trust that the other saints are fit and powerful with God, we must believe that God’s parents are most powerful.

For Anne, that most holy woman, pleased almighty God so greatly that he willed the mother of his Only-begotten to be born from her. He would never have dedicated a womb to his mother unless he had found in it the merit of great holiness. For Scripture says: “The holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit, and will not dwell in a body subject to sins.” There was nothing deceitful in the most blessed mother Anne, nothing that could have offended God, but all things were upright, pure, holy, and worthy of the dwelling of the Holy Spirit.

Therefore she is rightly believed, next after her daughter, to be nearest to God in glory, since she was able to please him so greatly in this life. Let no sound disputant object to me that the saints of the Old Testament were of less merit than those of the New, since it is not time that grants holiness, but the grace of faith. I except Anne, whom I recognize as a lover not so much of the Old Testament as of the New. For she established the ark of the New Testament, whom she carried for nine months in her womb. Nor does time lessen the holiness that God’s special grace bestows. So great a grace had come beforehand upon most blessed Anne that she had no equal in virtue and holiness in Israel in her time.

It therefore seems fitting that she should avail before God in glory above the rest, she who shone before others in life. For just as the Son of God honors and loves his mother above all, so too, after her, he endowed his beloved grandmother with singular glory. But perhaps someone will say: carnal affection ceases where there is no generation of the flesh. Granted. But love does not cease where virtue abounds; affection does not end where love is continued; the love of kinship does not perish where glory is owed to merits.

I admit that carnal affection ceases in the blessed; the generation of the flesh also ceases. But the love of kinship does not cease, since among them all things are held in common. For no one ever hated his own flesh. Nor is the Son of God so without feeling that he would not love his holy parents, his own flesh, [unclear]. Or how would he not love his own parents, who loves all his saints and servants? For he says: “I love those who love me.” Who could ever love God, according to the flesh, above his parents? From their blood he was incarnate without stain; born of milk, [unclear] nourished. Far be it, therefore, that we should think most blessed Anne [unclear].
