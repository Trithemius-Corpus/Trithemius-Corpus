I who am not worthy to lift my face to heaven because of the multitude of my iniquity, much less to dare touch with impure lips the praises of most blessed Anne. But by your grace I am what I am, and all the good that I will and can do is yours. For you, Lord, enlighten the will by your grace, so that it may will good and be good; you also are he who grants us to work according to a good will. In all things we acknowledge your grace going before our will; in the good things we will and do, we proclaim your gifts without any merits of our own. Nothing comes from us but evil; nothing comes from you but good.

Since, therefore, I can do no good of myself without your grace, I devoutly flee to you; with humble subjection I call upon you, that what my merits hinder, you may mercifully bestow by your grace. Give me, Lord, wisdom, the attendant of your throne, that she may be with me and labor with me at all times, and especially in this work, in which with slender powers I have resolved to praise your name in holy mother Anne.

You also, Anne, most holy mother, whose memory I am treating, deign with your accustomed kindness to be present with me, that I may be made fit to describe your praises and, by your merits, be found a faithful proclaimer. My lady, my hope, my advocate in every tribulation, be graciously present now to me as I long to praise you; and you who by many benefits have stirred my will to write, obtain by your merits ability for my poor talent, so that it may be able to praise you worthily. I seek nothing except the honor of the three; I desire nothing except to please you; I intend to write nothing about your praises except what is right and true. Now, I pray, bring help; give the hand of assistance; obtain grace.

You also, immaculate Mother of God, who taught your servants to honor Anne, your venerable mother, be present with your merits to me as I long to describe her praises, so that the devotion of your servant may obtain in the work the due effect which the grace of God conceived in the mind. You also, blessed spirits of the heavenly fatherland, who rejoice in the most sacred fruit of the mother and the virgin daughter, our Lord Jesus Christ, by your prayers make me worthy to praise Anne, and obtain for me the grace of the Holy Spirit. If you honor mother and daughter in heavenly joy, it is fitting that you assist their praisers in this pilgrimage.

You also, devout worshipers of most blessed Anne, do not detract from my pious studies, but if in anything through weakness I have failed, excuse it through charity. Nothing is written by me through contention or empty glory; nothing of apocryphal uncertainty is alleged; nothing is taken up except what has been proved. I receive nothing that resists the divine Scriptures; I write nothing that stains the Christian faith; I introduce nothing that does not agree with holy things.

Therefore let no one more learned than I detract from me because I have praised you, Anne; let no equal disparage me; let no inferior insult me. What is true prevails, and what proceeds from truth does not fail. Let no one think me contemptible because I write about the most holy woman; let no one disturb my devotion; let no one mutter against the praises of lady mother Anne. I do not fear the barking of dogs; I do not dread the judgments of the unskilled; I do not shudder at the torches of envy. For I write for the devout, not for the wicked; for the studious, not for the ignorant; for the humble, not for the proud. May this present work please those for whom it is written; may it profit those to whom it is offered; may it serve those to whom it is presented. What have I to do with those who are outside? What is it to me if they do not think rightly? What is it to me if they gnaw at holy things beyond reason? I do not fear displeasing those whose rash judgments and censures I know.
