Holy Anne learned from her youth to remain at home; she was accustomed to work at something with her hands; she did not seek to be seen outside by people. She strove to keep concord in goodness and peace with her neighbors; she wronged no one; she disturbed no one by word or deed. She was rarely seen in public; she did not sit in the streets with chattering women; and she did not take part in the conversation of slanderers.

For this most prudent woman knew that sin is not absent from much speaking; therefore she guarded her heart with every care, lest she ever offend in speech. Intent, then, on holy studies and exercises, she deserved to become a mother from barrenness, just as almighty God had ordained from the beginning. Therefore the holy woman conceived from a holy husband; she conceived without original stain; she gave birth without guilt.

O holy and wholly pure conception. She conceived by a man, but this conception was subject to no tongue of accusation. She conceived according to the order of nature, but beyond nature’s custom. O holy conception, to be celebrated with continual praises, which prepares a mother without stain for the Son of God, over which the choir of angels rejoices, and the whole world throughout the earth exults. O truly venerable conception, which was first pleasing to almighty God and then was fulfilled. O truly worthy conception, which, with the Holy Spirit going before it, took its beginning from such purity that she was made ready to conceive and bear God.

Therefore most holy Anne, the parent who conceived the mother of God without any stain, must be honored by us with the greatest devotion. O what an offering worthy of God she made, when she presented her most holy daughter in the temple. Finally, it belongs to the declaration of the holy mother’s merits that the daughter whom she conceived and bore she consecrated to the Lord immediately from infancy. As was the mother’s way of life, so was the daughter’s instruction. No one can teach another what he himself has not learned; no one badly instructed trains his children well. Children often advance according to the habits of their parents, and they more readily embrace what they have seen practiced by them.

A mother worthy of her most holy daughter: from the one from whom the daughter had drawn the matter of her flesh without stain, from her also she drank in the teachings of piety. The grace of God had gone before them both and had filled them both; but it was earlier in the mother, more abundant in the daughter. The holy mother taught her daughter what she herself had first learned from her parents, although the daughter had no need of human instruction, since the Holy Spirit filled her from conception. Nor did she need maternal training, who was full of heavenly grace.

Learn, O mothers, to train your daughters for the honor of God: not to love the pomps of the world, to flee the public gaze of the crowd, to remain at home. The Holy Spirit hates publicity; he loves the secrecy of a pure mind. Why does she run about through lanes and streets, if she burns with virginal modesty? The archangel came to Mary not in public but in secret, not to one speaking in the street but to one silent in her chamber. The apostles received the Holy Spirit not in the marketplace, but shut up in the house. The most blessed Virgin learned this from her mother, or rather from the Holy Spirit, and carefully kept it.
