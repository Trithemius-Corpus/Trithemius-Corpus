In their monasteries let them devoutly celebrate the memory of Anne; let them build chapels and altars; let them pour out pious prayers to her. Let the leaders and brothers of the other religious orders likewise do this, so far as they can, and let no one ever grow lukewarm in the worship of so great a mother.

But you, professors of the Carmelite order, who by the title of the most blessed Mother of God and Virgin Mary are specially distinguished with a most ancient privilege, you ought to honor, venerate, and embrace most holy Anne with singular devotion, for you are called venerable from her daughter. Take care, therefore, that you are never lukewarm in the worship of the mother, you who have obtained from her daughter the name of so great an honor.

And all the rest of you also, chosen in the lot of the Lord, raise and direct your souls to the honor of the most holy mother of the Mother of God. Bestow on her whatever praise you can, and instruct those subject to you to do the same as far as they are able.

You also, men learned and distinguished in letters, in whom the vein of writing abounds, dedicate your talents to the divine mother Anne; compose verses in her honor, and praise her also in prose as much as you can. But simpler people, whose abilities are slower and not sufficiently fitted for writing, should copy out with devotion what has been published by others; let them read it more often, and for the edification of others also let them fasten it to the walls and altars of the holy mother. Let them read prayers fitting for her honor; let them read due praises; let them read hymns, songs, and prose pieces.

Let no one be idle from Anne’s praises; let no one be silent; let no one grow lukewarm. Let all peoples redeemed by the blood of Christ celebrate the memory of Anne, from whose merits great hope of salvation is promised to us. But lest material for prayer be lacking to Anne’s devout worshippers, three years ago we made, in her honor, a brief prayer after the likeness of the Angelic Salutation, which is now both possessed and read by many.

It seems sufficiently fitting to us that, just as in the rosary of the Mother of God we pay five Lord’s Prayers and fifty Angelic Salutations, so also we should do the same in honor of Saint Anne, yet in such a way that in place of “Hail Mary” our prayer which follows is said.

Prayer.

O venerable Anne, mother of the Mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women: blessed are you in your daughter, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, and the virgin-born son of that offspring, Jesus Christ. Amen.

Many have established this prayer in honor of Saint Anne in place of the Angelic Salutation in the rosary, because it is brief and not useless. But if our prayer for the rosary of Saint Anne does not please anyone, let him read the salutation according to the custom of the Angelic Salutations, and at the end, after he has said “Jesus Christ,” let him add this clause: “And blessed be your most honorable mother, Saint Anne, from whom your flesh proceeded without stain and without defilement. Amen.”

And they say that the holy Mother of God revealed this clause also to someone devoutly enclosed [unclear]. If this too does not please anyone, let him, according to his devotion, make another prayer suited to the honors of the most blessed mother Anne, as he thinks better.
