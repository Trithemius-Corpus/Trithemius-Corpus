She is rightly now interpreted as “grace,” because she was to bear the mother of grace. Rightly is she called grace, for through her intercession the grace of God is obtained for the faithful. She is also interpreted as “one who gives,” or “a gift,” because those whom she finds devoted to her honor she makes rich with many gifts of grace. Rightly is she called a gift, through whom remission is granted to the sinner. Therefore whoever is weighed down by the burden of sins, whoever is struck by the darts of temptations, let him flee confidently to the bosom of mother Anne; let him offer prayers and gifts of devotion, and there will be nothing that can prevail against him for evil. For Anne is called grace, and in accordance with her name she does not cease to obtain grace for one who asks, if she finds devout faith. Therefore, all the faithful, observe the feasts of this most holy mother; rejoice in her commemoration; also pour out diligent prayers to her, because she has power to free you from every tribulation. Rejoice in the Lord our God, all you who love Saint Anne; praise the name of the Lord, all faithful peoples, you who love the mother of the Mother of God and of the Virgin. Take Anne as your patroness, and honor her with devoted prayers, and you will always find her a faithful advocate.

By what exercises and devotions we ought to honor the most blessed mother Anne. Chapter 13.

We have heard how much the most blessed mother Anne avails by her merits before the almighty Lord; we know how worthy she is of honor. It remains now that we briefly say how she should be honored by us. And although, because of the varied character of devotion, no general rule about the manner of veneration can be set down by us, nevertheless we shall try, as far as we can, to persuade everyone, so that no one may excuse himself through ignorance from the praise of so great a mother. And since devotion first of all establishes for each person the manner of praising her, the greater that devotion is, the more useful the manner will be. As we are accustomed to venerate the other saints with various ceremonies, so too let us honor her, but more abundantly. You have the manner; apply your desire. If perhaps I seem too brief to you, take a broader manner. Kings, princes, and pontiffs of churches, if they desire to honor blessed Anne, should erect chapels and oratories in her memory, establish prebends, and found monasteries. They should also make diligent prayers to her, celebrate her memory, and feed the poor and needy out of reverence for her. With whatever zeal they venerate the other saints as their special patrons, they should know that the divine mother Anne must be honored not with less, but with more abundant honor. Likewise the prelates of the faithful should have the same manner of veneration, and should decree that the feast of the most holy mother Anne be celebrated by all in their dioceses. They should grant indulgences to the faithful for the cult of Anne, establish confraternities in her honor, and offer the libations of prayers in her name.
