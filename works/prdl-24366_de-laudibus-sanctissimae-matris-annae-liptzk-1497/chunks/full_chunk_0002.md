Here begins the treatise of Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, on the praises of the venerable and most blessed Anne, mother of the Mother of God.

Necessity compels me, and devotion of mind urges me, to write, according to the powers of my ability, for the edification of the faithful, the praises of the most blessed Anne, mother of the Mother of God, and to declare in truthful narration how greatly she has been accustomed in her kindness to aid the wretched. But when I consider the poverty of my understanding, impure as I am, I am afraid to speak of the most holy woman.

For how worthy of praise is she who bore the Mother of God? What speech will explain it? And so I wish to fashion something in honor of so great a mother, and I am afraid, because whatever I say is less than her praises; whatever I write is far beneath the majesty of the matter. Yet when I recall to memory her benefits toward the wretched, I cannot keep silent from her praises, even if I wish. Therefore her very great kindness has deserved the fulfillment of my vow, has stirred up devotion, and requires the display of honor. The devout, and, not to call it importunate, petition of my friends has no less driven me to dare this; from their faith and prayer our weakness has received confidence. Although, then, I know myself to be a sinner, although I am not sufficient to praise worthily the most holy parent of the Queen of heaven, nevertheless I ought by no means to withdraw from the praise I have undertaken, lest I seem to incur the fault of ingratitude.

I shall attempt, according to my devotion, what I can, because no one is required by God to do what he cannot. No one is judged empty of good will before the eternal judge. Therefore I, a man, will praise the lady; a servant, the mistress; a sinner, the most holy one. For he did not restrain sinners from the praise of God who said in the psalm: “Praise the Lord, all nations; praise him, all peoples.” Truly, when he summons the nations to the praise of the Lord, what else does he call but sinners? For they were far from righteousness, because they worshiped not God but idols. Therefore no one is permitted to keep silent from the praises of God, since even idolatrous nations are called to repentance. Let all, then, praise the Lord who receive his mercy; let all who live in the flesh praise him, because he himself is the one who made us all, he himself who gives life. Let the righteous and sinner alike praise: the one because he has been preserved by the mercy of the Lord so that he did not fall, the other because, even after his fall, he has been raised up so that he might not perish forever. And although praise is not beautiful in the mouth of a sinner, still no one is excused from the praises of God by a guilty conscience, since by his mercy all are preserved for repentance. Let the righteous bring praise, the sinner confession. Let the former praise because, by preserving grace, he stood firm; and let the latter confess because, by mercy following after, he rose again. “Praise the Lord, O my soul; I shall praise my God in my life; I shall sing to the Lord as long as I shall be.” I shall open my mouth to confess the Lord; I shall likewise ready my mind and my pen.
