Anne, venerable mother of the mother of the highest Parent,
you who first open the path of salvation to the peoples:
from you flee all diseases, pallor and pain,
and every harmful thing that dwells in soul or body;
whatever there is among human beings that troubles cares and hearts
flees from you, as clouds driven by winds are wont to do.

*Rutgerus [venerable?], regular canon of [Baynen?], to the image of Saint Anne*

Here with deep prayers and with devoted [unclear] I weep,
where Anne, the parent, sits with her blessed offspring.
Do not doubt: you will receive whatever you ask of him,
for powerful Anne continually sends [it] by her prayers.

*A distich by the same on Saint Anne*

Anne, mother of the parent of Jesus Christ the mother of God,
we ask of you: drive every sin away from us.

*The rosary of Saint Anne by Jodocus [Beiffel?], patrician of Aachen, to the reverend father and lord, lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict.*

Whoever wishes to read this rosary in honor of the most blessed mother Anne with devotion should recite the Lord's Prayer together with the salutation of Saint Anne, which he should repeat after every two verses; that salutation is as follows.

Hail, venerable Anne, mother of the Mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women, blessed are you by the Lord, and blessed is the fruit of your womb; and blessed is the blood born from you, Jesus Christ. Amen.

Our Father. Hail, [Mother] of God.

Anne, let me, wretched as I am, set forth your praises;
for, if you permit, I shall bring you garlands and roses.
Anne, you shine, a venerable offspring of the kings of Judah,
renowned for your [unclear], with your pious husband Joachim.
Anne, [unclear] you wept in your husband for chaste loves,
the sole glory of married women and [unclear] honor.
Anne, you seek a pious child, and not for vain uses,
but that with holy posterity she may worship God.
Anne, a swift messenger from the heavenly court announces to you:
"Rejoice," he says, "for, exulting mother, you shall have offspring."
Anne, you will bring forth offspring blessed with such great virtue
that, with virginal honor, she will shine as Mother.

Hail.

Anne, you will bear a daughter whose [unclear], whom the world does not contain,
God himself willingly enters your chaste womb.
Anne, at last from your blood will arise the blood
which, shed for the wretched, hopes [unclear] the guilty.
Anne, so great an expectation befitted your childbirth,
for it is no easy thing to have the mother of the Mother of God.
Anne, the praises of your offspring will fly above the stars;
all ages will proclaim you also blessed.

Our Father. Hail, [Mother] of God.

Anne, you are believed to have conceived Mary pure from evil,
and to have had her holy before God.
Anne, you bear things other than those which figures had signified,
her whom the holy fathers sang would come.
Anne, you bear the enclosed paradise, the tree of life,
blessed and sacred with every honor of the countryside.
Anne, heavily laden you bear the garden which the fiery [unclear]
cherub guards, glowing night and day.
Anne, you bear the flower that surpasses white lilies,
humble violets, and the scarlet rose.
Anne, in you the Mosaic ark is discerned,
and the golden urn of heavenly bread lies there.
Anne, you bear the bride of the true Solomon,
and a house adorned within and without with every jewel.
Anne, you bear the temple whose inner chambers
sweet sounds, music, and every rejoicing have filled.
Anne, you bear the veil, in the beautiful image of the fathers,
holding within itself the things done, which may cover the ark of God.
Anne, you bear the offspring whose head the golden flame
of Phoebus clothes, and the white moon her snowy feet.

Our Father. Hail, [Mother] of God.

Anne, now made possessor of longed-for Mary,
you can scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have arisen.
Anne, all the stars applauded Mary newly born,
and an unaccustomed radiance flowed from the pole.
Anne, it is right to believe that angelic choirs
celebrated the birthday of your child around you.
Anne, the deep shades felt your childbirth;
then the new light itself terrified the Stygian pools.
Anne, as parent, you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands,
she who is brighter than the sun and than the lights of heaven.
Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl?
She smiles at her mother; she does nothing sad.

Hail.
