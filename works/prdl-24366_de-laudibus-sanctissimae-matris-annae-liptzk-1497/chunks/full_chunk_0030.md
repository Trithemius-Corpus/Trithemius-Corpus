A plainly useful fraternity, and always to be honored, which shapes faith by works, raises up hope, stirs charity, and kindles devotion. For through it sluggishness is shaken from the minds of the faithful, and the fervor of holy newness is inspired. Whoever attacks this is mad; whoever speaks against it is more foolish than a fool; whoever strives to destroy it envies Christian salvation. Many things indeed still remained to be said in praise of this most holy fraternity, had not the occupation of domestic affairs claimed me for itself. What has been said by me against its adversaries has been put forward not with the intention of railing, but with the intention of correcting and amending. Yet I trust that there are few who set themselves against the holy mother and her fraternity.

*Conclusion of this work and excuse for the insufficiency of the author, with an exhortation to the fraternity*

*Chapter sixteen*

Although I had proposed at the beginning to describe more things concerning the praises of the most holy mother [Anna], nevertheless from this point onward it has already pleased me to cease, lest, if I extended the treatise to an immense length, I should choke the reader’s devotion with excessive prolixity. For those who desire to persuade others ought to find brief discourse, and one rich in the majesty of thoughts. I certainly do not persuade myself that I have strayed from the first; but what I have accomplished in the second will be judged by those who are to read our poor little night-work, such as it is. I have satisfied my promise; I have also satisfied my friends; but satisfaction has been made neither to my devotion nor to the dignity of the subject. I wished more than I was able, and I did less than I wished. Finally, who could suffice to venerate with worthy praises Anna, the parent of the most blessed Mother of God? I therefore confess myself unworthy to speak of the most holy mother, since I am a sinful man and entangled in offenses from my youth. And yet I could not keep silent from her praise, because necessity lay upon me, and devotion, strongly surrounded by the prayers of friends, was pressing me. But after it has come thus far, the excellence of the subject has oppressed me, impatient of the heat of summer, so that now I ought to honor in silence her whom I do not suffice to praise. But whatever things have been said and written by me in this work, I submit to the holy Roman Church and to all learned Catholic men; from their determination I never intend to dissent. Whatever the most holy Roman Church determines is to be believed will be to me a most firm faith. Finally, we turn our speech to you, friends, devotees, brothers and sisters of the most holy mother Anna, asking that, accepting in good part those things which have been written by us from great devotion for the praise of the holy mother, you beat upon the holy patroness with devout prayers, so that she may assist us with her accustomed loving-kindness, absolve our faults, obtain virtues, and secure eternal life. We also exhort you to take care to persevere in the love and fraternity of the most holy mother, honoring her with continual praises, so that, absolved from faults by her merits, you may live a tranquil life here and, after this life, an eternal one.

*Here ends the book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, on the praises of Saint Anne.*

*There follows a tetrastich on the most holy mother Anne*

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim: a tetrastich on the most holy mother Anne.*

Anne, venerable mother of the nourishing Virgin,
excellent in merits and powerful in piety,
no one who honors and venerates you departs sorrowful;
for you bring to all the gentle help of salvation.

*Distich of the same man, which he placed at the image of Saint Anne.*

Whoever in adversities shall call upon Saint Anne
with devout prayers, he will be well protected.

*Of Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate: a tetrastich on Saint Anne.*

Anne has never dismissed anyone’s prayers empty,
but, radiant, has always brought the hoped-for aid.
Therefore let us heap manifold honor upon the pious mother,
who permits no one’s prayers to be empty.

*Of Theodoricus Gresemundus the Younger of Mainz: a tetrastich to Saint Anne.*

Anne, parent of the Virgin Mary, hail, favorable one,
worthy [unclear] the work of our salvation.
The human redemption which began through you, I pray,
continue, covering our crimes with prayers.

*Of Rodolphus Langius, canon of Münster: a distich on Saint Anne.*

Great parent Anne, hear the vows which pregnant women,
with fruitful womb, bring to you.

*Of Jodocus Badius of Ghent: a tetrastich on Saint Anne.*

Anne, glory of the world, mother of the mother of Jesus,
by the merits of the mother, may your grandson grant the stars.
Saint Anne, bringing forth the first-fruits of our salvation,
help us with your offspring and with your grandson.
