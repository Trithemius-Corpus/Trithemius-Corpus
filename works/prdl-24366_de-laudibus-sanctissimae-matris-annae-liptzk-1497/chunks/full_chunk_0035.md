I have set down beforehand [what concerns] the Mother of God and Virgin; and lest anyone receive it otherwise than my intention holds, I have thought that the readers should be warned. For I know, and it does not escape me, that many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the original stain, men who flourished long before the Council of Basel. Now I pass over their opinion, [unclear]; and I intend to say or write nothing at all against them. Indeed, if anyone sees that some things have been said by me rather harshly, let him refer them not to those who hold opinions otherwise than we do, but only to the points [unclear], which, without learning, the babblers who assert another opinion do not fear to call heretical. And this is what my [writing] indicates; it notes nothing further of the past. For when I say that whatever solid foundation [unclear] which the unskilled murmur, this belongs to what is further, not to what is past; and today it censures obstinate men, not the ancient contrary opinions.

But you ask what learned men have written to the contrary: I am not referring to the ancient Fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the Council of Basel, think otherwise. And so only the obstinacy of the more ignorant persons of this [unclear], not the learned inquiry of those who hold another opinion, is censured; indeed I am not ignorant of the declaration of Sixtus IV, Supreme Pontiff. But it has seemed to me that the most pure conception of [the Virgin] is in no way at variance with the faith of the holy Roman Church, which she believes and venerates, as she shows today by many documents. Finally, with the doctors of the universities of Paris, Cologne, Heidelberg, Erfurt, Vienna, Leipzig, Tübingen, Ingolstadt, Basel, Mainz, and many others at Rome and in nearly all the orders, with the Christian faithful spread throughout the whole Christian world, I am not afraid to venerate the pure conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary; I do not admit obstinacy, if, obedient to the Roman Church, I wish [to remain] in the reverence of that venerable Church, my nurse and mother. This is the pure and sincere intention of this chapter of mine, in which I think that no one’s opinion is in any respect diminished by me, briefly and without obstinacy. Reader, farewell.

This letter of the lord father Johannes, abbot, is missing from certain copies through carelessness and negligence; nevertheless he himself commands that it be appended to all copies, and has decreed that it be considered appended to each one.
