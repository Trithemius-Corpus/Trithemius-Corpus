The election of Anne as mother of his mother before the foundation of the world. Chapter V.

As almighty God disposed before the foundation of the world where he would be born in the flesh, so also from eternity he preordained the parents from whom he would be born. For the Son of God did not simply find his parents; he sent them before, he who appointed them for their time [unclear]. He did not discover them by chance, but chose them beforehand from eternity by a wondrous dispensation.

Therefore most blessed Anne could confidently say what is written of the eternal Wisdom of God: “The Lord possessed me in the beginning of his way, before he made anything from the beginning; from eternity I was ordained, and from ancient times.” She existed before the creation of the world in the divine mind, not made by nature, but foreordained by grace. Truly she is worthy to be honored: grace, going before her, disposed her to be the mother of the Mother of God, and, ever preserving her, made her most holy.

Truly holy and most worthy of every honor is the parent who gave us the pattern of a holier life and brought forth from her own breast the fountain of inexhaustible mercy. She could not have been other than most holy, who carried so precious a treasure in her womb. She stood forth as the most noble Ark of the Covenant, splendid with the adornment of all virtues; in her was stored that urn, the Virgin who was to bear the true manna. O with how great honor must Anne’s womb be thought worthy, which became God’s treasury and carried a treasure of inestimable price.

There is no doubt, dearly beloved brothers, but it must be believed with firm devotion, that blessed Anne was then filled with ineffable joy, when for nine months in her most blessed womb she carried her who was to be the Mother of God, made worthy by the Holy Spirit from her conception and preserved pure from every stain. O who could tell the joys of that most holy mother Anne, which she sensibly received in her heart from the presence of the Holy Spirit? What mouth can express, what tongue recount, what mortal pilgrim’s mind grasp, how great a gladness and exultation filled the inward parts of most blessed Anne, when she deserved to feel the grace of the Holy Spirit in the heart of her daughter not yet born?

If Elizabeth is said to have been filled with the Holy Spirit because of the fullness of her soldier, by many arguments this is proved to have happened more abundantly in venerable Anne. For the former bore in her womb the herald of the supreme King; the latter bore his mother. The former had conceived the soldier, the latter the queen. John was sanctified in the womb after conception; Mary was filled even in conception. Finally, the Holy Spirit, choosing Mary from the hour of conception as his most pure bride and dwelling in her, never afterward abandoned her even for a moment, but, guarding and directing her, enlightened her with a special gift before all mortals.

We believe that the mother of the most blessed Virgin shared in this most sacred illumination, not only when she carried the Virgin still enclosed in her womb, but also afterward, on all the days she lived in the world. For truly the Holy Spirit did not possess without the greatest gifts of his grace the woman whom he had made worthy to be the parent of the Mother of God.

Therefore, dearly beloved brothers, let us choose as our advocate her whom we know to have been always holy and pleasing to God from eternity; and venerating her daily, let us honor her with fitting praises. Let married people honor her, because in a most holy marriage she perfectly served the Lord, to whom she bore a mother without stain. Let widows and the continent praise her, because she prefigured the pattern of their continence. Let virgins love her, because from the height of holiness she bore the Virgin, than whom none ever shone forth more holy. Let the just honor her, because by her most holy way of life she showed them the rule of justice. Let sinners venerate her, because she bore the mother of him who makes the sinful just. Let all honor her with the greatest love, because she brought forth the mother of him who came as Redeemer to all.

Let no one grow sluggish in her praises; let no one fail in love of her; let no one become lukewarm in her worship. Let all love her as their common mother; let all honor her as their public advocate, from whose gift all have received the common Savior. For she will obtain pardon for us sinners, from whose blood the fountain of mercy bodily flowed forth.

Rise up to her praise, O brothers: she does not abandon those who hope in her; she lifts up the oppressed; those she has lifted up she sustains; she protects widows; she mercifully defends orphans and wards; she refreshes the weak; she raises up and heals the feeble; she strengthens in virtue those who have been raised up. It is impossible that she should not be heard when she prays for her worshippers, who built from her own breast so precious a bridal chamber for almighty God.
