Anna, [unclear], behold, your daughter reaches for the milky breasts, and seeks your neck with tender hands.
Anna, you break off delays, baring breasts and bosom; you receive her tender little limbs in your tender lap.
Anna, you cherish your daughter, and from your breast she draws the sweet juices with rosy mouth, sounding gracious murmurs.
Anna, if you knew how great pains your daughter will bear, and how many deaths she will endure, you would now be nothing.
Our Father. Hail, [unclear] of God.
Anna, it is dear to your heart to adorn your daughter with approved morals, and to have ordered her for the highest God.
Anna, you prepare to bring your daughter into the lofty shrine; but the nourishing one of her own accord reached the step of the heavenly ones.
Anna, with slow pace and labor you follow the swift one. The eager little virgin consoles her weary mother.
Anna, why do you marvel at strength in the tender virgin? You do not see human things, but holy signs of God.
Anna, you are accustomed in poverty to give offerings to the sacred temples; now you give that than which no woman ever gave more.
Anna, when you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell, she gladly entered the cloisters and [unclear] virginal Lord.
Anna, about to send away the holy one, you wear away cheeks and eyes with weeping; deep love holds you unmoved.
Anna, return home; leave your daughter trying [unclear]; unless she thinks of the one who is to come, “I am here.”
Anna, may your daughter fly through the ethereal recesses; may love sent from the highest summit possess her.
Anna, two Marys born to you are there to serve you; nor will they honor you with slight devotion.
Our Father. Hail, [unclear] of God.
Anna, by your weeping you have been taught to succor [unclear]. Hence also, when called by the wretched, you readily favor them.
Anna, you are accustomed to overturn the fixed laws of nature, and so you will. Let the barren woman now be a joyful parent.
Anna, you even set in place the gifts of flowing fortune, lest, slipping suddenly, they flee from our laps.
Anna, you drive away disgrace; you give the gifts of fair fame. You relieve sorrows, and love yields to you.
Anna, whether hostile force terrifies someone, or whether the bonds of a dark prison hold him,
Anna, be present as faithful relief to sick mortals; with you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing.
Anna, mother, the sailor calls upon you amid the fearful wave, if wind and water have laid the torn ship low.
Anna, mother, you are always to be feared by the black tyrant; as ice flees fire, so that harmful one flees you.
Anna, mortal hearts cannot ask of you in vain whatever you will, and whatever your daughter and God will.
Anna, therefore blessed by merits and by so great offspring, Anna, great mother, look upon our prayers.
Hail.

*Prayer to be said after the rosary.*
Almighty and most merciful God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as the mother of the mother of your Son: grant, we beseech you, that we who honor her memory with faithful devotion may, through her merits, obtain also the helps of eternal life. Through [etc.]

*Here ends the rosary of Jodocus Beissel, professor of both laws, patrician of Aachen, addressed to the reverend father Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict. From Aachen, on the third day before the Ides of April, in the year 1483.*

*A poem of Adam Werner of Themar on the most holy Anne, addressed to the reverend father lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.*

Let us sing praises to the renowned grandmother. Dear kinsman of Jesus, move the plectra of praise; nor may you, chaste mother, daughter, be absent from the choir as I sing.
Anne, offspring of kings, long endures barren in a second marriage, and grieves that she is without child.
Weeping, she prays for offspring, nor do tears flow in vain.
Heavenly messengers promise a child; pregnant, joined to her husband, then as childbearing mother she is approved by the birth, through whom our salvation came.
Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green; the rod will be fruitful with fertile shoot; refreshed by its flower, we revive from the death pronounced upon us.
By this birth Joachim becomes worthy to stand in the shrines, removing the reproaches of the priest.
Thus, Anne, make us worthy to bear pure prayers to your grandson.
Commend us to the boy whom holy charity bears; we ask it by prayer, O blessed matron. Christ denies you nothing.
To Christ, who as companion of the Spirit reigns with the Father, be glory through all ages; whom your prayers, O Anne, grant as reconciled to us.
Amen.

*This hymn is sung under the melody of that hymn which, on the Nativity of Saint Mary, is used in many churches and begins: “Rejoice, mother, in your inmost bowels.” From the castle of Heidelberg, on the second day before the Kalends of February, 1494.*
