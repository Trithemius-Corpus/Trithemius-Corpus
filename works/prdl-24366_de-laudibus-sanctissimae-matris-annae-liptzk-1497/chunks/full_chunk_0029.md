This confraternity of the most blessed mother Anne is not superstition, as you say, O impious slanderers, but is true, upright, and holy; in it the worship of God is not diminished but increased; obedience is not despised but commended; the Catholic faith also is not [unclear] by this holy confraternity, as you think, but is renewed and defended; [unclear] through the exercises of holy charity, so that, being formed, it shines forth.

As for your lament that parish churches are deserted, that is to be imputed in some measure to your own offenses: you demand milk and wool from the sheep of the Lord, yet you do not feed those very sheep as you ought above all, nor do you guard them. Why do you pretend that offerings are denied to you, when you abound in many riches? Every pastor is a vicar of Christ; if he wishes the faithful to be free in hearing masses, there is no one who can forbid him. You have not seen all the privileges of the monks, nor will you see them. Who does not know that for the sake of your purses you wage war against holy confraternities, and that you assail monks not for love of God, but of gold?

Behold, the supreme pontiff has confirmed this confraternity; behold, the lord archbishop Berthold, than whom there is no other more [unclear], has approved it by ordinary authority; and you foolish men still murmur. Cease, I beseech you, cease from this folly, and do not rebuke what you know so many pontiffs have approved. Believe me: it is not good to stretch out the tongue against the saints of God; it is not good to wish to forbid so great a devotion toward Saint Anne. [Unclear] belongs to Mary, who lets loose her own against these Annes. If you do not believe my words, observe those who are such, and by experience you will know that I have spoken the truth. It is rash to rebuke a good that is seen to be of benefit for the salvation of many.

Many good things pleasing to God come from this most fortunate confraternity, of which those outside it are very often empty. It draws to charity those whom God and his first worshippers [unclear]; by the face of brotherly love [unclear]; it calls them back from vices and sins; it inflames them to the love and desire of supreme happiness. I believe how many masses, how many prayers and alms, how many holy works are done each day in this confraternity, which otherwise perhaps would be omitted. May the almighty Creator illumine the hearts of all Christians for similar exercises of devotion; would that he inflame the minds of all to cultivate the confraternity rightly. It could happen that, by refraining from mutual injuries for Saint Mother Anne, they would be made more ready in the love of God.
