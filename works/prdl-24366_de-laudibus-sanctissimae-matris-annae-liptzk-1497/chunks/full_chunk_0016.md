God had chosen her before the foundation of the world, and what he had arranged without time he fulfilled in this presentation. How great, we think, was the joy then in heaven, because the young Virgin Mary was being joined to the heavenly Bridegroom. The most holy Virgin is led by her mother into the temple: she who was to bear the Lord of the temple, a little child of three years, is now made ready for divine nuptials.

Who, O mother Anna, could suffice to recount your joy then, when you devoutly handed over to service in the temple your daughter, received from the Lord, indeed the one who was to bear the Lord of the temple? No one ever offered God so pleasing a gift; no one ever presented to the temple a treasure of such great worth. Her holy parents marvel at manners beyond her age in their daughter; they marvel at her grace; they marvel at her eagerness. For she was full of grace from the womb, and was never subject to even the slightest stain of any defilement, she who was the temple of God.

Why do you marvel at your daughter, mother? The grace of God is not enclosed under the law of nature; the power of the Holy Spirit does not require age in order to do what it wills; the gift and will of the Most High is not constrained by many years. The Most High sanctified his tabernacle, and the ark in which he had arranged to place the true manna he adorned most beautifully within and without with the purest gold.

How perfect the mother was in the love of God she declared in this offering of her sweetest daughter. For unless she had been steadfast in the fear of the Lord, she would never have permitted her only beloved daughter, tender and noble, to be separated from her. But love of the Creator overcame a mother’s inward affections, and the pledge she held dearest above all created things she offered with a cheerful face to be consecrated to the Lord. Here nothing stronger is done; no long struggle is at work; all things were arranged without time by the Most High; all things were foreordained from eternity by the counsel of God.

O Joachim and Anna, most blessed parents, who were worthy to bring forth her who, without male seed, merited to beget the Son of God. Truly this offering is always to be honored with fitting praises, for it gave to God a mother, to the angels a king, and to the world, long lost, a Savior. And we believe that this offering was adorned with such purity that none purer could exist under God. For she was made queen of the angels, mother of God, lady of the world. The parents too, who begot the mother of the Son of God, are to be believed glorious before God.

Learn, parents, to dedicate your children in the fear of God; learn to form them while young in the love of God, lest, if you neglect them as little children, you later find them defiant. Anna offered her sweetest daughter, three years old, to the Lord; she taught the little child to fear God; she presented her to the temple to be instructed in the fear of the Lord. But now it is not so. You have your daughters instructed for dances and for love of the world’s vanity; you say nothing to them about the fear of the Lord; you urge nothing upon them about eternal life, since you yourselves learned nothing good from your parents.
