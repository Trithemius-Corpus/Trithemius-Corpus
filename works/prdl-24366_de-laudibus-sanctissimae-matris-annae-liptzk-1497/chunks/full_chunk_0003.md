I shall sing the praises of the most blessed mother Anne with what devotion I can.

For if we are commanded to praise God generally in his saints, we are without doubt urged to honor his parents more especially. For he who commanded us to honor our own parents certainly does not exclude his own according to the flesh. Therefore, dearly beloved brothers, it is holy to offer diligent praises of devotion to those from whose blood we believe the flesh of the Son of God was taken. Among them the most blessed Mother of God rightly holds first place, whose merits of holiness are so great that she has been exalted in heaven above the choirs of angels.

After her comes that most holy mother Anne, of whom we have undertaken to speak; just as in the flesh she is nearest to the Son of God, so in life and merits she is believed to be acceptable before the other saints. Nor may we doubt her holiness, since we believe that she gave birth to the Mother of God without the stain of original sin. For she could not have been the mother of so great a Virgin unless she was holy, through whose virginal childbearing we received the remedies of eternal salvation.

Anne is the holy root; Mary the Virgin is the most holy tree; the Son of God is the fruit, the medicine of souls. A good tree, a holy tree, a tree most fruitful without human cultivation, which took its beginning from a holy root and its growth from the richness of the heavenly rain. A good root never brings forth a bad tree, nor does a good tree bear bad fruits. A good tree cannot stand without a root, nor can good fruits be borne unless the tree is good. As a tree is known by its fruit, so what sort the mother is is made clear in the daughter.

In the most holy Mother of God we understand what we ought to think concerning the holiness of her mother. A childbearing without the fault of original stain befitted none but a holy mother, one who would conceive and bear the Mother of God; it suited none but one most pleasing to God and, beyond all then living in Israel, devout. The first instruction of the daughter shows how Anne loved God above all things. The little child is read to have been entrusted to the Lord; she is remembered to have served in the temple of God; she is believed to have been conceived without any fault and always to have lived holy in the world.

What more do you require? Is such an excellent life of the daughter from infancy not enough to declare the mother’s merits? If Anne had not been most holy, she would not have instructed her daughter so diligently and devoutly for the honor of God. We know that daughters are formed according to what their mothers are. But the most blessed mother Anne, just as she surpasses all women in merit and holiness, so she took care to instruct her daughter from childhood in the love of God more diligently than all the saints.

If, then, we love the Son of God, if we honor his most pure Mother, why do we grow lukewarm and fall silent in praise of his most holy grandmother and mother Anne? If we seek the honor of the highest King, why do we not love his parents? Whoever despises the offspring of an emperor does injury to the king.
