Old custom reports that this too must be earnestly promoted: that Anne’s feast be celebrated by faithful and devout Christians with due reverence. For if we honor the feasts of other saints of Christ with yearly devotion, why should we not celebrate the feast of Anne, most beloved by God? For she is not inferior to the other saints in merits and glory, since even while still mortal she was of such dignity that she gave birth to the mother of him who, immortal in spirit, lives in heaven.

And just as we make some daily remembrance of our special patrons, so too, concerning Saint Anne, as right devotion suggests, let us offer something of praise to God every day. And if her devout worshipers ought always to be ready for her honor, then on her birthday especially they ought to be attentive, for her remembrance is observed not only on earth, but is also honored in heaven with the votive praises of the saints.

It also befits devotion and suits piety that, just as Saturday is observed in honor of the most holy Mother of God by clergy and people with masses and prayers, so the commemoration of her mother should be celebrated at least once weekly. For we are quickly drawn away from devotion by concern for household affairs; and it is necessary that we be restored to it at least once in the week, lest, if we put off the honor of venerable Anne too long, we grow lukewarm and lose her patronage.

For celebrating this weekly memory, Tuesday seems quite fitting, since on that day she is read to have been both born and to have died. Indeed, in many places a praiseworthy custom has taken hold, that the remembrance of Saint Anne is celebrated on Tuesday with no less reverence than Saturday is observed in the church of the faithful in memory of the Mother of God. For among the Sicambrians and the Grunenses we have learned that on Tuesday masses are always sung in honor of blessed Anne, lights are lit, offerings are made, and the poor are more abundantly refreshed.

We have also known certain devout worshipers of blessed Anne who, for the honor of Jesus, Mary, and Anne, every Tuesday burned three candles on her altar, gave three special alms, and received as many pardons or disciplines. This custom took its origin from a miracle of Saint Anne that happened in Pannonia, where she advanced a desolate and very poor young man to great honors and riches. If anyone desires knowledge of this matter, let him read the said miracle, which is now so widely known among the worshipers of blessed Anne that it can be hidden from no one.

And we have learned that these things are done by many not only on Tuesday, but every day, according as each person’s devotion directs. Priests especially who desire to be most devoted worshipers of the mother Anne should strive, in her commemoration, to celebrate devoutly at least once in the week. Whoever takes this up for himself as a custom with devotion, we believe, will have Anne as a loving advocate in every need.

Therefore let no one be silent from the praise of so great a patroness; let no one grow sluggish in devotion toward her; let no one neglect her remembrance. For Anne looks for grace without reward. Let us honor so great a parent with fitting praise, for we believe her name was imposed not by chance at her birth, but foreordained by God.
