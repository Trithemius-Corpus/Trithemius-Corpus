Now, therefore, if we believe these and similar confraternities useful for obtaining the mercies of God for the people, we trust that those which are instituted in memory of the most blessed Mother of God and of Anne, her most holy mother, are incomparably better. For by as much as the Mother, together with her venerable parent, surpasses the other saints in heaven in merits and holiness, by so much the confraternity dedicated to her name holds the more eminent place.

The faithful, moved by pious devotion, in order that they might deserve to have her as a ready advocate with God, instituted a confraternity in her honor under the name of the Rosary; and it has now become so widespread through various regions of the world that there is scarcely any province in which they do not have several. But devout worshippers of the most blessed Anne, considering that the devotion of the faithful could also greatly advance through these confraternities, thought to establish, after the example of the Rosary, a praiseworthy congregation in honor of holy mother Anne, so that the name and praises of so great a patroness might come to the knowledge of many.

Thus this holy confraternity began in the diocese of Osnabruck; and, confirmed by Conrad, bishop of the said church, almost ten years before this under Frederick III, and, as is reported, attested by miracles, it has greatly multiplied. Then [Rumoldus de Laubach?], prior of the Carmelite convent of Frankfurt, a very great worshipper and lover of the holy mother Anne, at the urging of certain notable persons, established the same confraternity in his aforesaid convent; Berthold of Henneberg, most reverend archbishop of the church of Mainz, confirmed it, and the supreme pontiff Innocent VIII, in the eighth year of his pontificate, approved the same by apostolic authority.

And indeed it is a worthy institution: that the Carmelite brothers, who are especially distinguished by the title of the most blessed Mother of God, should also be more ready in the praises of her mother. Today there is a great and numerous company of persons of both sexes in this confraternity, who daily do and expend many admirable things for the honor of Saint Anne. But in various places this same venerable confraternity is being erected by the faithful and celebrated with fitting devotion.

Yet detractors of this honorable confraternity are found, as from the beginning there have been detractors of every good thing, who assail it with wicked presumption, tearing it with an impious tooth. What displeases you in this most holy confraternity, O profane detractors? What do you have against them? Bring it forth, that we may hear. You say: These confraternities are superstitious, because they weaken the Catholic faith, withdraw due worship from God, and despise obedience to parish churches. Moreover, they say, it denies the customary and due offerings to their priests, offers abundant gifts to monks and to its own confraternities, and leaves its mother churches empty and ruinous. These are the heads of the opposing party, the charges of an unjust accusation, the objections of wicked detraction. To these we shall briefly respond in order.
