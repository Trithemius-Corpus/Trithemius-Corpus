*A Sapphic poem of Rutger the Sicambrian, for a hymn in praise of Saint Anne.*

We sing your praises, venerable Anne;
favor those who praise you with their measures,
obtaining for all the pardon of salvation,
and granting rewards.

You, Anne, parent of the mother of Christ,
shining in the highest citadel of [heaven?],
obtain whatever you ask of the Begotten
of the almighty one.

You heal the sick, wipe away stains,
make your faithful servants attain their prayer,
and at last grant them to climb
into the heavenly kingdom.

By your approved character, Anne,
you bent the highest Lord with many prayers,
so that he would grant that you become the mother
of so great an offspring in the world.

To the highest Father, to the heavenly Son,
and to the Holy Spirit be honor and power,
brightness, virtue, holiness, and [unclear]
for all [time?].

*Another by the same.*

Anne, mother of the virgin mother,
let us all resound diligent praises
with all the powers of the inmost heart,
with ringing voice.

She brought forth for the wretched race,
stained by the blemish of mother Eve,
the changing offspring, and the radiant beam
which she gave to the world.

This light of the world, as it passed through the darkness,
drove all things away, shining with heavenly
light, as holy John resounds
in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine maiden,
you call the groaning grandson [unclear];
do not refuse the people praying,
so far as the flesh is concerned.

Virgin, on our behalf, favor the prayers
of Anne, your mother, who earnestly entreats;
you, grandson Jesus, easy to entreat,
fulfill her prayer.

To the Son born of Anne’s daughter,
with the highest God, be honor and power;
to the blessed Spirit let equal honor be
for all time.

*A poem by the same, in iambic dimeter, which is sung to the melody of the Ambrosian hymns.*

Bowed down, let us honor
blessed Anne’s feast, singing
due thanks with our voices
and in our inmost hearts.

This mother of the gracious Virgin,
who brought forth the radiance of the world;
this woman who by her virtues
made herself most pleasing to God.

She drives away delays; she confers
grace by her prayers.
She lightens most dreadful illnesses,
of body as well as spirit.

In praise of her, King of heaven,
to you with resounding songs,
and at once to the [unclear] Spirit,
we bring forth pious praises. Amen.

*A poem in alcinamic meter by the same, which is sung to the melody “Crux fidelis” or “Pange lingua.”*

This company, with voice, vows,
and pious hearts,
is eager to utter the feasts of Anne,
so illustrious a mother.
Be gentle, we pray,
to those who praise you.

You, parent of Mary’s mother,
you, most pleasing to God,
preserved the beginnings of the law
which Moses once gave
to the ancient fathers
and to the Hebrew peoples.

Wondrous gifts, gracious one,
you obtain for the faithful
by prayer pleasing
to the Son of the high Father.
You heal the sick, and grant
that hearts be pure.

May the Father, and also the Son
with the Spirit, entreat you;
and may you, rejoicing, give to us
as we sing praises
of the renowned Anne,
foremother of the kindly Virgin. Amen.

*A poem of Rutger Venrai, a German, regular canon of Hein, on the book concerning the praises of Saint Anne.*

Whoever you will be, reader of this excellent book,
put vain fear aside; it contains nothing frivolous.
Now pregnant with wondrous meaning, and sweet with charm,
Abbot Trithemius has produced it by his art:
watching over books, guardian of the Sponheim fold,
who cultivates the sacred writings of threefold language.
Here you will find titles of Anne full of reason and art;
only turn them over with a pious heart.
He does not follow vain and everywhere doubtful little tales;
he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence.
Therefore, I pray, read with love the worthy praises of venerable Anne,
which the learned man has brought forth for you in love.

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, greeting to the reader.*
