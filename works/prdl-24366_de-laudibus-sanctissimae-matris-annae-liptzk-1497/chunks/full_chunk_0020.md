Believe me, there is no tribulation, no distress, no necessity that can stand before Anne’s prayers. Whatever burdens you, whatever troubles you, whatever opposes your good will, bring it to Anne, and for the glory of God she will free you. It is impossible for her not to obtain everything she wishes. The King of heaven is her grandson; he will not be able to deny anything to his most sweet grandmother. The Mother of God is her daughter; she cannot pour out prayers to God in vain. The whole court of the heavenly country loves Anne as a mother and venerates her as a lady. If she asks anything from God, the prayers of all the saints will eagerly join in. Blessed is the person who has Anne as advocate with continual devotion and diligent prayers.

That holy mother Anne, as a most powerful advocate, is to be held by us in the greatest honor. Chapter 11.

With how much honor and how great reverence the most blessed Anne is to be honored by us we have sufficiently considered from what has already been said, since in the heavenly country she is honored by the supreme God himself and by all the saints with such great and, indeed, ineffable praises of proclamation. For if those citizens of the heavenly country, now set in the highest tranquility, venerate and love her as a most powerful lady, how much more should we wretched people, still placed amid many tribulations, invoke and honor her as a loving patroness.

Indeed she is to be cherished by us with great reverence, through whose patronage we may safely escape all the evils of the passing world. And truly the mother is worthy always to be venerated with fitting praises, who bore enclosed beneath her breast the beginning of our redemption. Deservedly, I say, she is to be honored, who bore for us the Mother of God without stain, through whom the Son of God came into the world as Savior. Therefore, just as we venerate and cherish the most blessed parent of God, who bore for us the Redeemer, the immaculate Lamb, so too it is fitting for us to honor Anne, who brought forth for us without fault the mother of the Savior. If she had not had a mother, whence would the daughter have come?

Let no one doubt, let no one waver, let no one burn with wicked thoughts, that unless Anne had been most holy, she would not have been chosen as the mother of the Mother of God. It was fitting that she who was to bear the Mother of God should be adorned with the splendor of every holiness, she who was to become the mother not of just any maiden, but of her than whom, among God’s pure human creatures, no one purer or holier could be found. Therefore the most blessed Anne was worthy of this office by God’s gift; she was worthy by the merit of her life; she was also worthy by the testimony of the law. Worthy, I say, was she to bear the Mother of God, she who knew how to love God above all things, and who had learned to keep his law without offense. There was no holier woman in Israel who is believed to have been honored by God with such great gifts. The barren woman conceived from seed, but without stain of seed.
