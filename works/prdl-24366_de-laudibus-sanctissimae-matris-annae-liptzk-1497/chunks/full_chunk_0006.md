A general exhortation to all the faithful, that the memory and feast of most holy Anne be venerated.

Hear me, you who love what is good and seek the Lord. I act as envoy; I am led by devotion; I am driven by charity. The mother of the Mother of God has sent me to proclaim her most holy merits. And now I come to you not with lofty speech, but as a simple man to the simple, a devout man to the devout. If you are Christians, if you are good, if you love the grace of the Lord’s incarnation, why do you not love his servants, that is, his parents? For they are the ones from whom the Son of God was born according to the flesh; and if their life had not been most holy, it would by no means have been so acceptable to God. But when he willed that his only-begotten Son should be born from them, he plainly showed how holy and dear to him they were. Among these, one shone forth before all others as most pleasing to God: Mary, the ever-virgin, chosen as the mother of that unique birth; and Joachim and most blessed Anne begot her without stain and brought her up most chastely in the fear of the Lord. The Son of this most sacred virgin is God, who redeemed us by his most holy passion. If you love the daughter, why do you not honor her mother? Had there been no mother, you would not have had the daughter.

Therefore my discourse now turns to you who dwell in the valley of tears and bitterness. I address you, sons of Adam, exiled from your homeland; I write to you who have been redeemed by the precious blood of the Son of God. I address you, I admonish and exhort you: if you love the Son of God, devoutly honor his kinsfolk according to the flesh. Among them, after his most holy Mother, divine mother Anne is set before you as the first of all to be honored; her holy merits are shown every day to the human race by very many miracles. Take her as your patron; celebrate her memory with greater devotion.

If you wish to please God, the supreme ruler, believe me, brothers, for I am going to speak the truth: if you love and honor the venerable mother with all your heart, you will learn by experience how much the heavenly emperor grants to her. She has such power with God that whatever she asks of him she is able to obtain without delay. Just as the heavenly King is believed to honor his mother before all others, so after her he venerates mother Anne before all the saints. And very often Anne commands for her faithful servants what her daughter refuses, not because that daughter does not know how to yield to petitions, but so that, in deference to the mother, she may be seen to stir us up to veneration of her. So too the supreme emperor is bent to mercy by the prayers of the saints, not because he begins to will what he did not will before, but so that, in showing regard for his elect, he may instruct us to honor them. For God is not moved in the manner of men, nor is he driven to anger by any disturbance; rather, always unchanging, he wills what he wills. And when he shows mercy at the prayers of the saints, he does not change his counsel, but mitigates his sentence.

Therefore, to obtain the mercy of God, for which our own merits are not sufficient, the suffrages of the saints must be implored for us, so that what we cannot attain by our own merits we may gain by their prayers. Now all the citizens of the heavenly homeland, even if they differ according to the quality of their merits, are nevertheless believed to be of one mind in act and will in helping our wretchedness, according to the variety of those who make supplication, if devotion asks it. For when the faith of the petitioner is aided by the merit of the advocate, it becomes doubled for obtaining what is sought. We can assess this not by conjecture but by experience, when we wonder that one person is heard and another disregarded by the saints. Two blind men come to make supplication to the merit of some saint: one is given sight; the other is left in his blindness. No contempt on the saint’s part is believed to be present; rather, the petitioner’s lack of trust is exposed. Without faith, devotion merits nothing.

I have set these things forth with sure consideration, so that if anyone should feel that most blessed Anne is not present to him with such readiness as we promise, he should not accuse us of falsehood, but recognize his own culpable lack of trust. For the greatest confidence before God and the saints is steadfast faith in their help. Moreover, no Christian doubts that those who are more excellent in merits have greater power with God. Therefore the most blessed Mother of God, the ever-virgin Mary, as she surpasses all the saints in merits and holiness, so by right she is believed to surpass them all in power and majesty, as queen of heaven. At her right hand, if I may speak thus, we believe that most blessed mother Anne stands, whose life we know to have been most holy. There was no other woman holier than she in Israel, who in the conception of the Mother of God prepared a vessel without stain for the Holy Spirit. Finally, if Elizabeth, the mother of the Lord’s forerunner, is believed to have been filled with the Holy Spirit and with ineffable joy, let it be considered with how great exultation, joy, and grace the most holy mother of God’s parent ought to have been filled. As much as a king differs from a soldier, so in her own manner does Anne [unclear].
