So too there are many other practices of holy exercise, which have been left to each person’s judgment and devotion. A devout mind will always find ways to honor most holy Anne with due praises: by fasts and tears, by alms and prayers, by vigils and disciplines, by hymns and readings, the most blessed mother is to be honored by us.

And it offends us not a little that to this day we have found no fitting history concerning holy mother Anne, one suited to her honor, her feast, and everything belonging to it. For the history now in use among many in different forms is varied and, as it seems to many, not sufficiently elegant; I think it less worthy of such great merits, and in very many places it appears apocryphal. Nor is only one found, but several, with people adding, as each saw fit, what was less approved. What shall I say about the office of the Mass, which we see in many places marked by a similar variety? The office sung by very many, which some devout person drew from the Saturday office of blessed Mary by changing the sense and words while the melody remained, seems less conducive to devotion, since hearers, deceived by the likeness of the melody, think that it is being celebrated not for Saint Anne but for the Mother of God. If the material is pleasing, let at least the melody be changed. All this could easily be corrected if the authority of some pontiff intervened, relying on which learned men would compose a history and office regulated in sense and melody and approved by the Scriptures.

That the most blessed mother Anne rewards her devotees in many ways, both in this life and in the life to come.

Chapter Thirteen

If the frequent display of miracles earns reverence for the saints, blessed Anne is most worthy of the highest honor, for she daily bestows so many and such great benefits upon her devout worshippers that there is no one who could recount them all.

We know indeed many miracles of Saint Anne, both new and old, but it is not our purpose here to direct our mind to them, since we have learned that many call them into doubt: not because they contain any falsehood in themselves, but because many modern men, who seem learned to themselves, do not believe miracles but disparage them. Therefore we do not wish to give an occasion for disparagement, nor to expose the treatise, I think, to suspicion, lest they say that we write not correct things but apocrypha. For no one understands, no one believes, no one grasps what daily benefits God shows to the lovers of Saint Anne except the person whom devout experience instructs.

But we know that through the invocation of Saint Anne many powerful, learned, noble, and low-born men, virgins and women, young and old, have often been freed from the greatest tribulations, rescued from dangers, and wonderfully helped in necessities.
