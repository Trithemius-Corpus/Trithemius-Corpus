To attack and rashly define what you do not know is unheard-of arrogance. If we are instructed to interpret for the better those things whose intention we do not know, how much more ought we to receive with piety the hidden works of God, whose reasons we do not know.

None of us was present at God’s counsel; no one knew the manner of the conception. But, led by many reasons, we, from pious belief, believe that she always existed most pure. Our adversaries, drawn away by consideration of nature, defend the contrary opinion with rash presumption. But it is safer to render account for piety toward the Mother of God than for rashness.

If we err, piety, indeed God himself, is the cause; but if you err, adversaries, say for what cause. What consideration moves you to say so boldly that the most pure conception of the Mother of God was subject to original guilt? You will answer, I know: love of truth. But show that your opinion is true, and I shall believe that you are lovers of truth. Yet you will not show it; who does not understand this? You do not understand, because you do not believe. You do not defend the truth, since you do not know it. For no one piously defends what he does not know.

Consideration of human nature deceives you: while you examine its customary course with care, you do not weigh that God is the founder of nature. He who made nature is above nature; God, the founder of the law, is not confined beneath the condition of the human law. But he who willed that a virgin should give birth also preserved her from sin beyond the order of nature. Therefore the cause of the opposing opinion takes its origin from rashness, and for that reason it does not deserve reward from God, but vengeance.

For just as punishment does not make a martyr, but the cause does, so error concerning the conception does not condemn, but obstinacy does. Finally, if examples and records of the ancients are to be believed, we read that many who attacked the purity of the glorious conception perished most wretchedly, but we find no defender of it ever struck down by God. For the surpassing dignity of the Mother of God moves us to the defense of the most holy conception, since she shone forth so great and of such a kind that she was most worthy of every honor.

If we are deceived, we are excused by holy piety. But if you err, who will defend you? Truth? But you do not know it. Justice? But justice condemns errors; it does not excuse them. Mercy? But you do not have the bowels of mercy. Who, then, will deliver you? The Virgin Mary? But you make light of her honor. The Son of God? But you do not love his mother. The prayers of the saints? But you disparage their queen. Therefore you will have no excuse, you who offend God and all his saints. You arm yourselves with scholastic arguments, and you are naked of piety. Whatever you bring forward in defense of your opinion is alien to the fear of God. For you do not seek the honor of God, you who attack the purity of his mother.
