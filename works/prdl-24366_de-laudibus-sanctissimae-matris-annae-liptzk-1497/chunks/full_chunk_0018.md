If the Lord Jesus, the King of heaven, loves those who love him, it must rightly be believed that he loves his parents before all others. Nor can they be in the heavenly homeland without special glory, whom almighty God on earth had filled with such extraordinary grace.

Therefore we believe that Anne, after the Virgin Mother of the Son of God, is first, and a powerful advocate for the wretched; the King of heaven denies her nothing, but in all things for which she asks, he honors her face as that of a most beloved parent. The whole Trinity also loves her; the angelic choir honors her; all the citizens of the heavenly homeland venerate her with the greatest reverence as the honorable mother of their queen.

And not undeservedly. For those who love the king rightly venerate his grandmother. The honor paid to parents is shown to their children. But he proves that he loves the king less who does not venerate his father and mother and all his lineage. We also are usually more grieved by injuries done to our parents than by our own. Why is this, unless because the insult inflicted on them is doubled in us? For we believe that not only our parents, but we ourselves too, are marked by a grave injury. In the same way, just as we believe the insult to our parents to be ours, so we reckon the honor shown to them as shown to us.

Therefore whatever honor we pay to the divine mother Anne, let us believe without doubt that it is shown to God. For the Lord says to us in the Gospel: “Whatever you have done to one of the least of mine, you have done to me.” If he declares that everything bestowed upon the least who believe in Christ is done to himself, how much more must we believe this to be done in those who are not merely the least before him, but the greatest and most powerful. The parents of the daughter of God are not last with God, not least but greatest, not small but most powerful. Therefore let us by no means doubt that whatever veneration we pay them is doubled with God.

For if revelations are to be believed, the most blessed Virgin appeared to a certain devout person and, among other things, said to him: “If you love me, honor my mother; and whatever reverence you pay to her will be doubly pleasing to me.”

Now then, brothers, if we love the Mother of God, if we desire to please the Queen of heaven, let us honor her venerable mother with diligent devotion, and in every tribulation we shall find both of them our pious helpers. Behold, the angels of God and all the saints venerate and love Anne, and we remain mute and silent from honoring her. We, not they who already exult in the homeland, need the intercessions of the saints; and therefore it is fitting for us always to be devoted in venerating them. We, I say, who still live in the flesh and are pilgrims away from the Lord, must honor those citizens of the homeland above with special devotion, so that, helped by their prayers, we may be able to come to the joys in which they dwell.

Among all the saints, however, we believe that the Mother of God is preeminent, and Anne, her venerable parent; and if we are devoted worshippers of them, no adversities will harm us.
