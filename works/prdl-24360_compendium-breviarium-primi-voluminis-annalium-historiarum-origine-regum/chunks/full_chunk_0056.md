¶ After the slaughter of the maidens, the aforesaid two kings, [Guanius?] of the Huns and Melga of the Picts, to please Caesar Gratian, flooded Britain as enemies, stripped as it was of all soldiery by Maximus, as we have said, and with fire and sword wrought a great slaughter and cruelly killed an innumerable multitude of the people. When this had been announced to King Maximus, who was at that time staying at Trier, he sent a certain Gratianus, a citizen, with two legions into Britain, to drive the enemies from there. With the Huns and Picts there were Scots, Dacians, and Norwegians; after an engagement, Gratianus drove them all into Ireland.

¶ In these times Maximus, king of the Britons, usurper of the Roman empire, after all Gaul had now been overcome and Caesar Gratian slain at Lyons, placed the seat of his kingdom in the city of Trier, where he convoked a synod of bishops. At it Saint Martin, bishop of Tours, appeared in person and shone forth with many signs and miracles. Ambrose, bishop of Milan, was present, and also Ausonius, bishop of Bordeaux, who wrote a metrical work in praise of the country and the city of Trier, which he entitled Mosella from the river flowing past. Jerome also, the priest of Bethlehem, copied out anew the book of blessed Hilary, bishop of Poitiers, On Synods, at Trier at the same time.

¶ But Maximus, setting out from Trier with a very great army, appointed his little son Victor king of the Gauls, and appointed Quintinus [and?] Nanneus, chief men of the city of Trier, as his guardians. Then he himself set out with the army into Italy and was slain by Theodosius, in the thirteenth year of Marcomir, king of the Franks. Not long afterward his son Victor also perished by the sword, to gain the grace and favor of Theodosius.

¶ But after the death of Maximus, Marcomir, king of the Franks, gathered troops and sent a great army of his men into Gaul under the leaders Sunno, Genebald, Priam, and Antenor, so that it might not only recover what had been lost, but might also render all Gaul subject to the Franks. And indeed, in a short time, recovering many things that Maximus had taken away, they also added no small part of Gaul to the Frankish kingdom. Nevertheless the kingdom of Armorica, which the Britons inhabited under Conan, as we have said, they could not obtain on that occasion; yet afterward they recovered it.

¶ Meanwhile, while these leaders were devoting themselves to the Gallic war, the Romans, under Caesar Valentinian, who was ruling with Theodosius, sent a great army into Germany against the kingdom of the Franks, intending, as they hoped, to destroy the Franks completely. Against them Marcomir, king of the Franks, advanced to battle with the Germans and Saxons; when an engagement had taken place, he was slain by the enemy and fell with many, while the others [entrusted themselves to the protection of flight?] [unclear], both enemies of the Franks.
