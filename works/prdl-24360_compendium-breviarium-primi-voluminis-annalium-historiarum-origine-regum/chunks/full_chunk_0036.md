The several divisions received their names from particular princes or events. For from Duke Marcomer some were afterwards called Marcomeri; from Brando, his son, who founded the city of Brandenburg, others were named Brandenburgers; and others received other names from other sources.

In the fifth year of King Richimer, Domitian Caesar had previously crushed the Sarmatians and Dacians, who dwelt in the borders of Germany toward the east, and afflicted them exceedingly. And, adding sin to crime, he stirred up the second persecution against the Christians after the most impious Nero, and was the first of all the Caesars to command everyone to call him god and lord.

In the eleventh year of his reign, Richimer, king of the Franks, had a war with the Romans and Gauls not far from the city of Basana, aided by the common support of the Germans; in it many fell on both sides. Meanwhile, when the Roman legions had returned to Cologne Agrippina, Nerva Caesar was announced dead; in his place the soldiers raised Trajan, a man of Spanish nation, to be Caesar. He ruled nineteen years, six months, and fifteen days.

But Richimer, king of the Franks, after many deeds bravely done, at last finally died in the twenty-fourth year of his reign, and in the year 113 of the Lord’s nativity, in the eleventh Roman indiction. Ruthvuigus, bard of the Franks, wrote his life, character, deeds, and worship of the gods in verse.

Odemar, the elder son by birth of the aforesaid Richimer, king of the Franks, reigned after his father for fourteen years. He was a prince of the Franks very beloved by the people, who refrained from wars as much as could be done without harm to the commonwealth. For he made truces with the Gauls and the Romans, which the Franks call treugae, as if “confidences,” because faith was pledged between them; and he showed himself so friendly to them thereafter that he compelled all to lament his death.

Thus, as long as he lived among men, he kept the peace he had promised, and expended all his effort on the honor and adornment of his people. He founded very many castles, cities, and fortifications throughout the kingdom; by the counsel of the priests he both enlarged and renewed the laws and rites of sacrifices. For all the Franks, Saxons, and Germans at that time were still subject to the vanity of idols and far removed from knowledge of the Christian faith. They worshipped Mars as the fierce god of war, from whose divine power they sought victory at all times. To each of their gods they dedicated not only individual altars and temples, but also individual cities and villages; nor was there any number to the insensible gods.

In these times the chief priest, pontiff, elder, and bard of the Franks was named Vechtanus, born from the ancient stock of kings, eloquent in Greek and Latin speech, and highly skilled in astronomy, music, medicine, and the philosophy of the Greeks, since he had devoted much time to study both at Rome and at Athens [unclear]
