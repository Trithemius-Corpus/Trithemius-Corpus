After the killing of King Hilderic, Theodoric, his brother, summoned from the monastery of the holy martyr Dionysius, was restored to the kingdom of the Franks, and thereafter reigned for a second time for fourteen years. His mayor of the palace was Leudesius, son of Erchenvald, appointed for one year by the counsel of Saint Leodegar the bishop, who had then been recalled from the monastery of Luxeuil, and of other leading men.

For in the following year Eberwin, having gone out from the aforesaid monastery, recovered his strength and harried those who had plotted against him with hostile persecution. He fell upon King Theodoric, plundered his treasures and those of the church, slew Leudesius, mayor of the palace, in battle, put the king himself to flight, and, pretending that a certain Clovis was the son of Lothar, formerly king, made him king for himself; and he brought over to obedience to him all whom he could by threats and blandishments, and disturbed the kingdom of the Franks with many upheavals.

In the third year of Theodoric's reign, the holy prelate Leodegar was captured by Eberwin and grievously afflicted together with his brother Gerinus. But Gerinus, not long afterward, was killed at the command of that same tyrant, overwhelmed with stones.

In the sixth year of his reign, Theodoric received Eberwin into favor, and at his persuasion called a synod of bishops. In it, according to Eberwin's judgment, he deprived many of them of their episcopal office, while he condemned others to perpetual exile. Among them Saint Lambert, son of Count Aper, removed from his bishopric of Maastricht, entered the haven of monastic quiet in the monastery of Stavelot, and remained there for seven years.

In the same year Saint Leodegar, bishop of Autun, after being long tortured by Eberwin, after hunger and the squalor of prison, after the tearing out of his eyes, after the cutting of the soles of his feet, after the cutting out of his tongue and lips, by the tyrant's command consummated his martyrdom by the cutting off of his head. Many miracles proved his innocence and merit.

In the eighth year of Theodoric, after the death of Wulfrad, mayor of the palace of the Franks, Pippin, son of Ansegis, held rule in Austrasia together with Duke Martin; Eberwin engaged them and killed Martin.

In the same year Conon was ordained pope at Rome. He ordained Saint Kilian, a Scot by nation and a monk by profession, as bishop, sending him with two monks, Colman the priest and Totnan the deacon, into Germany to preach to the peoples the faith of Christ and the true salvation of souls in him. Coming into eastern Francia, at the city of Wurzburg of the Franks, he for some time devoted himself to the office of holy preaching, where by word and example he called many back to knowledge of the truth. At last, with his aforesaid disciples, he suffered under the secret direction of Geilana, wife of Gosbert, duke of the East Franks, because she feared being separated from her husband when Saint Kilian rebuked him for having her as wife, since his brother had previously had her as wife.
