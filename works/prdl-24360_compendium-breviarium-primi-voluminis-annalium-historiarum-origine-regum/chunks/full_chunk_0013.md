*The number of the whole people of the Franks was 489,360, and Meodarus duke.*

Now the number of the whole people, as Humibaldus testifies, of men, women, and also little children of both sexes, was 489,360, apart from slaves and attendants. But if anyone desires to know fully what great difficulties they had on the journey, what wars they waged with the peoples resisting them, and into what great dangers they either fell or escaped, let him read the aforementioned writer in the book of histories of the race of the Franks. Its beginnings, just as they are remarkable, so they seem to me, saving the peace of those who judge better, to be fabulous in many respects.

At last, after many labors and innumerable difficulties, King Marcomirus, with his dukes and the whole people, came into Germany; and they were received kindly by the Saxons as brothers, and received the dwellings they desired between them and the Rhine, namely that province in which those now dwelling there are called the Western Frisians, and also the Guelders and the Hollanders: around that region, namely, where the Rhine flows into the sea by three mouths. And although those settlements received them at first rather narrowly, they soon made them wider by strength and arms, to the derision of the westerners.

Indeed, in the twentieth year of his reign, King Marcomirus, provoked by an injury from the Gauls across the Rhine, gathered an army, crossed the river, and subjected to his dominion that land which, enclosed by the islands of the Rhine, is nearest the sea, after expelling its inhabitants. He entrusted it to his brother Sunno to guard, and he brought in by ship peoples of his own race who would inhabit and cultivate it.

But when the Gauls were taking ill the incursion of a new and newly arrived people into the borders of the Hollanders, and were harassing it with various attempts, King Marcomirus, in the twenty-fourth year of his reign, gathered a great army, both of his own men and of Saxons, crossed the Rhine by ship, entered western Gaul, and, devastating everything with sword and fire, came as far as the Meuse. Thereafter he fought many battles with the Gauls, victorious and triumphing everywhere; and his name was fearsome to all the Gallic peoples, and all who heard of Marcomirus were afraid. He made a treaty with the Britons inhabiting the island.

From that time, for nearly nine hundred years, the aforementioned kings of the race of the Scythians never ceased from harrying the Romans and Gauls, sometimes defeated, but more often victorious, until at last by arms they forced under their power not only all Gaul, but also all Germany, Swabia, Bavaria, Burgundy, Lombardy, Italy, Slavonia, Thuringia, Austria, Saxony, the Teutons, the Cimbri, the Danes, those on this side of the Rhine, the Mosellans, the Treveri, the people of Metz, the Austrasians, the people of Liege, the Morini, the people of Tournai, the Tungri, the people of the Meuse, the Atrebates, the people of Cologne, the people of Mainz, the Vangiones, the Nemetes, the people of Strasbourg, the Helvetii, the Rauraci, and the Bohemians, [unclear] together with the Spaniards and all the Germans, and almost all Europe.
