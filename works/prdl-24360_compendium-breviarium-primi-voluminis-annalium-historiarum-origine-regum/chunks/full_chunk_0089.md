With Hilperic, the monarchy of the whole kingdom of the Franks reverted to Dagobert, many always tending toward one, and again, by the plurality of sons, confounding unity.

When King Heribert had died, the Vascones refused to obey and be subject to the Franks; King Dagobert attacked them in war and compelled them to submission.

At the same time [Iudicahel?], who held the duchy of the same Armorican Brittany, terrified by the threat of imminent war, subjected himself and all the people of Armorican Brittany to Dagobert, king of the Franks, and to his successors forever. King Dagobert also attacked in war the Slavs, the Sorbs, who are now called Bohemians; he defeated and overcame them, and made them subject and tributary to the Franks.

If this Dagobert, the great king of the Franks, founded that once distinguished, exempt, and royal monastery of our order in the town of Wissembourg, in the diocese of Speyer, concerning which we have heard many expressing doubt, it seems to me that this was done in the third year after the death of his father, [unclear], since he reigned only fourteen years, although certain uncertain authors seem to have thought otherwise. With this reckoning of times agrees the number of the year of the Lord’s Nativity 634, which was the third year of the aforesaid Dagobert, but the sixth of his reign over Austrasia, and not the twentieth, as we read in the copied letters of that Dagobert himself. How shall we be able to know whether they are true or false, unless we see their archetype? Desiring to untie the knot of this doubt, let us set down a copy of the letters.

Dagobert, by the favor of divine clemency king of the Franks, to all the chief men of his kingdom, indeed to all professing the Christian faith, both future and present. A vow by which anyone has bound himself to God or to his saints cannot be made void without the stain of guilt; and the greater and more worthy is he to whom it is vowed, by so much heavier a judgment will the presumption of one who vows and does not pay be struck. Therefore we too ought and wish to render what we have vowed to the Lord, the terrible God, and to him who takes away the spirits of princes, terrible among all the kings of the earth. How he terrified us with the arm of his power, how, striking and healing, he visited us again, we have judged should be made known to all as an example of correction, especially so that every faithful person, having heard of our peril, may restrain himself from the devastation of the churches of God, and [unclear]; distracted, I neglected the duty of royal office, showing no solicitude or diligence toward those entrusted to me, doing or judging nothing from gentleness or zeal for justice. Among my other transgressions, the destruction of the churches of God [unclear].
