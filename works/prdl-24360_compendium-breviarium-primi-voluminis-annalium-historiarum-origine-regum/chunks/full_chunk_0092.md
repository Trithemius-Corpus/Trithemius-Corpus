At the same time Saint Eligius, powerful at the court of Lothar and Dagobert, kings of the Franks, and a marvelous goldsmith, founded, with both royal and his own expenditures, the abbey of the monastery of our order which is called Solignac; there he himself became a monk and innocently discharged the office of abbot. There also Saint Remaclus, coming from Britain, trained himself under him in Christ’s service.

Saint Dado, also called Audoenus, bishop of Rouen and chancellor of King Dagobert, likewise built within the forest of Brie a monastery of our order, which they call Rebais; over it he appointed Saint Agilus as first abbot, a man lovable to Christ and renowned for many virtues.

In the ninth year of his reign King Dagobert appointed his son Sigebert, a boy of seven years, king over the Austrasians, under the guardianship and direction of Saint Cunibert, archbishop of Cologne, and of Pippin, duke of Brabant and count of Namur.

In the same year, which was the 640th year of the Lord’s nativity, in the thirteenth indiction, Saint Arnulf died: Pippin’s grandfather, from mayor of the palace of the Franks bishop of Metz, and from pontiff a solitary and hermit. Clodulf, ordained bishop of Metz after his father, imitated his holiness.

Doda, formerly the lawful wife of Saint Arnulf before his episcopate, and mother of the already mentioned bishop Clodulf, enclosed at Trier for love of Christ, whom she had chosen as her bridegroom, separated from her carnal husband, shone with holiness.

In the eleventh year of his reign, Dagobert, king of the Franks, led an army against the Wends. The Saxons came to his aid with all eagerness, and when battle was joined the Wends were defeated by the Franks and compelled into servitude to the Franks under tribute. But the Saxons, who annually paid five hundred oxen to the Franks as tribute, having gained the opportunity, approached King Dagobert after the victory and asked that he remit the tribute to them. The king, wishing to return favor for their voluntary service, remitted the tribute.

After this Dagobert, king of the Franks, died on the first day of the month of February, and was buried in the church of Saint Dionysius which he himself had built, in the fourteenth year of his reign, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 645, in the third Roman indiction. The son of King Dagobert, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, reigned seventeen years. His mother’s name was Nathildis. And his [unclear], named Erckenwald, was born from the most ancient stock of kings.
