*The Sixth King. Helenus reigned for fourteen years.*

Helenus, the elder son of the aforesaid King Diocles, succeeding his father, reigned for fourteen years: an idle, wanton man, memorable for no exercise of virtue. In his time the Gauls, making repeated incursions, stirred up no small [unclear] among the Sicambri. Against them his brothers, with the nobles of the kingdom, often led out an army; fighting with varying fortune, they came forth sometimes defeated, sometimes victorious. But the king sat idle at home among gatherings of concubines, dissolving in lust, neither useful to the kingdom nor pleasing to the people: foolish in peace and unfit for war.

Therefore, when the fourteenth year of his reign had not yet been completed, the nobles of the kingdom, assembling at Neopagus with the whole soldiery of the army, by the common decree of all deposed him from the kingship as useless and harmful to the people, in the year 284 before the birth of the Lord.

*Helenus reigned for fourteen years.*

*Helenus was deposed from the kingship because of his idleness, in the year 283 before Christ.*

*Basanus reigned for twenty-six years.*

Basanus, the younger son of the above-mentioned King Diocles, after the deposition of his brother Helenus from the kingship, was raised to the kingdom of the Sicambri by the common guidance, and by the election, of the nobles and soldiers, and reigned for thirty-six years: a man most prudent in his generation, strong, bold, and exceedingly warlike.

*The very greatest vanity. Basanus the pontiff.* Because of the greatness of his prudence and the deeds magnificently accomplished by him, they called him Basanus the Great. The Sicambri held him in such reverence that they showed him honors not only royal, but almost divine. No one was ever permitted to contradict his commands with impunity, whether in a small matter or a great one, under the certain penalty of a bitter death. For he was exceedingly desirous of divine esteem; for that reason he both did and said great things.

*Basanus reigned for twenty-six years.*

*Basanus, king of the Franks, philosopher and supreme pontiff.*

*The very greatest vanity. King Basanus wished to be regarded as justice and as a god.*

*King Basanus kills his son for the sake of justice.* Hence, wherever he went, he ordered a [rod?] and a sword to be carried before him, as a manifest sign of justice. He was accustomed to observe it so steadfastly that he spared neither his own son, nor his brother, nor any one of the princes, if anyone knowingly offended even a little against the law of equity which he had established. He had a son named Sedanus, who, accused of adultery, was soon judged by his father to death according to the law that he had established for the Sicambri.
