But after some years, when the queen was dead, the Sicambri enrolled her among the goddesses, in her memory by decree of the kings and nobles. They, who previously were called Novimagi, that is Neumagi, as though “new kinsmen,” by their Saxon friends, were thereafter called Sicambri for about four hundred years, until at last from a certain king of theirs, Francus, as we shall say, they were named Franks, down to the present day, though with an admixture of peoples.

Meanwhile, because they were with their Trojans, they were also called Trojans; with Armenians, Armenians; with Scythians, Scythians; with Germans, Germans; with Gauls, Gauls. They often received a different name from others, and frequently changed customs, language, idiom of speech, and name together with their settlements.

In the twelfth year of Antenor, his paternal uncle Sunno dies, leaving two sons, Marcomir and Hector. The first of these, afterward racing with a horse over the ice, was drowned in the Rhine; the younger governed his father’s duchy in Holland under the authority of the king. He left four other sons, begotten from four other concubines, who are not counted among the dukes by the law of the Sicambri, which forbade, while children of the first wife survived, the sons of later women either to be princes or to be so named. Queen Cambra enacted this law so that the kingdom would not be thrown into confusion.

Priamus, moreover, brother of King Antenor, held the duchy among the Grunes, namely in that region bordering the sea which is now called Frisia, in which the son of Priamus the duke, in the three hundred seventy-seventh year before the birth of Christ, [built?] a new town, which from his own name is called Groningen down to the present day. For the duke’s name was Grunus, from whom the people too, although Franks by origin, were for a time called Grunes.

But Antenor, after many deeds bravely performed, dies in the thirtieth year of his reign, and in the year 382 of the Lord’s birth. The Sicambri, following him with great pomp according to the custom of the gentiles, enrolled him among the gods by order of the queen.

The only son of King Antenor by Queen Cambra reigned after his father over the Sicambri, who were afterward called Franks, for twenty-six years. In his time, entirely from the use of the old speech, there were among them many words which seem to sound more of Greek origin than of the character of the Teutonic or German language, as anyone who has full knowledge of both languages will easily discern. Nor was the change of the ancient speech among the people sudden; what began with time at last passed through usage into nature.
