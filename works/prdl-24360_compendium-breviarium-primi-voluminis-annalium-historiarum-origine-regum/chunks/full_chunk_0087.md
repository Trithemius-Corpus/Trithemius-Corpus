But Pippin the elder begot Charles Martel, and he begot Pippin the younger, mayor of the palace of the Franks, and king after Childeric. Thus from King Marcomir the first down to King Pippin, son of Charles Martel, mayor of the palace, and father of Saint Gertrude, there are counted fifty-one generations over the course of one thousand fifty-nine years.

But King Lothar, in the forty-first year of his reign, which was the six hundred and eighth year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the first indiction of the Romans, appointed Dagobert, his son by Queen Bertrada, king of Austrasia, assigning to him Metz, Trier, Cologne, Liege, Tournai, Arras, Tongeren, Therouanne, the Morini, and whatever lay between the western sea, the English sea to the north also, and the Rhine. He earnestly entrusted him to Saint Arnulf, bishop of Metz, and to Pippin, count of Namur and most noble duke of the Brabantians, to be instructed and guided. Dagobert established the seat of his kingdom, after the custom of the old kings of Austrasia, in the aforesaid city of Metz, and arranged the kingdom of the Franks well, both by himself and through dukes and counts.

In the following year the Saxons, rising in rebellion, refused the customary tribute to the Franks. Therefore King Dagobert, having gathered the forces of his men, moved his army against the rebels and waged war with Berthulf, duke of the Saxons; from the first encounter he was gravely wounded. When his father Lothar, who was then in Ardenne, heard this reported by a swift messenger, he immediately crossed the Rhine at Neuss and hastened to the aid of his son. The battle is renewed, and on both sides the fighting is hard and fierce. At last, however, the Saxons, after their duke Berthulf had been slain by the Franks, were defeated and sought safety in flight.

But King Lothar, pursuing the fugitives with the Franks, laid them low in a pitiable slaughter, sparing no one, but killing with the sword all who exceeded the length of his sword. The people, however, who had remained throughout Saxony he recalled to subjection to the Franks, and imposed on them a tribute of five hundred oxen.
