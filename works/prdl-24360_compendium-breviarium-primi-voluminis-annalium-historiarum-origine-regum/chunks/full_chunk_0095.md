In the first year of Clovis, king of the Franks, Saint Remaclus was ordained bishop of the church of Traiectum, a man of much learning and great holiness.

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 647, in the fifth indiction of the Romans, Pippin the Elder died: count of Namur, duke of Brabant, mayor of the palace, count palatine of Sigebert, king of the Franks, who held the seat of the kingdom at Metz in Austrasia, and father of Saint Gertrude the virgin. He left a son, Grunuald. Grunuald succeeded his father in the rule of the royal household for eleven years; afterward he was killed, as we shall say in its place. He had a son named Hildebert, of whom we shall also tell how he made him king after his own lord had been driven into exile.

In the aforesaid year, Radulf, duke of the Thuringians, raised up in pride, refused to pay the customary tribute to the Franks. Therefore Sigebert, king of the Franks of Austrasia, gathered forces and moved his army into Thuringia; and, when battle was joined, he defeated the duke and compelled him to payment of the tribute.

In the same year Saint Furseus, an abbot from Ireland, a pilgrim for the sake of Christianity, came into Gaul. Received honorably by King Clovis, he founded the monastery of Latiniacum, now of our order, and established monks there. Not long afterward his two brothers, Foillanus and Ultanus, monks who followed him with an equal vow of pilgrimage, illuminated Gaul by the word and example of their life.

Of these, Foillanus, by the gift of Saint Gertrude the virgin, afterward founded the monastery of Fosses, where, crowned also with martyrdom, he rests in the Lord.

In the sixth year of King Clovis, Sigebert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, despairing of posterity from offspring, built here and there twelve monasteries of the order of Saint Benedict in honor of God, with Grunuald, mayor of the palace, and Saint Remaclus, bishop of the people of Traiectum, assisting him in these things. Among them Stavelot and Malmedy were pre-eminent, which are governed under one abbot. This Remaclus, not long afterward, was made a monk in the same monastery of Stavelot, resigning the pontificate, full of years, and at last he happily came to rest.

Also in these times in Gallican Francia, Saint Landelinus, confessor of Christ, who in like manner built four monasteries of the aforesaid order: Lobbes, Aulne, Waslere, and Crespin, where he rests bodily in the Lord with his disciples Adolenus and Domitianus.

At the same time the monastery of Fleury was founded by Abbot Leodebold. To it not long afterward the body of the divine father Benedict, our abbot, was brought, translated by the monk Agilulf from Monte Cassino, after his monastery, as we said above, had been laid waste by the Lombards. The Italians contradict these things.
