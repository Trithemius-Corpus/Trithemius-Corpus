Hence some writers also called these Saxons, or Westphalians, Lombards, because they cast off their customs and rites in Saxony.

In the fifth year of King Chilperic, the king of Austrasia, his brother, remembering the injuries which he had received from him when, in the preceding year, absent from the kingdom, he was fighting with the Huns in Thuringia, gathered a great army and advanced against him to war. He occupied the city of Soissons, the seat of his brother Chilperic’s kingdom. He condemned his son Theodebert, when captured, to exile; he defeated Chilperic himself in battle and put him to flight; and all the things which he had previously taken away he recovered again for himself, and he usurped a great part of his kingdom.

In the same year King Sigebert took as wife Bruna, or Brunhild, daughter of Athanagild, king of the Visigoths, as was said above, who was reigning in Spain.

In the seventh year of Chilperic, king of the Franks, the Huns again devastated Thuringia; by magic arts they accomplished great things. Against them Sigebert, king of the Franks, advanced to battle. By their enchantments he had almost been deceived and defeated, but, prudently invoking the name of Christ, he stood firm, fought manfully, and finally conquered; and when a treaty had been concluded, the Huns returned to their own lands.

In the following year the Winili, who are the Lombards, after the city of Pavia had been taken by surrender after a siege of three years, in a short time gained almost all Italy except Rome and Ravenna, and, with no one resisting them, held dominion powerfully everywhere. In the ninth year of King Chilperic, in the sixth indiction of the Romans, his brother Charibert died, king of the Franks, who reigned in Neustria, in the city of the Parisians, without children.

In the tenth year of Chilperic, king of the Franks, Alboin, king of the Lombards, was killed by a certain armor-bearer of his on the following occasion. In the five hundred and seventh year of the Lord’s nativity, in the tenth indiction of the Romans, in the times of Lothar, great king of the Franks, this same King Alboin had a war with Chunimund, king of the Gepids, in which sixty thousand men fell on both sides. There Alboin killed Chunimund and had a drinking-cup fitted for himself from the shell of his head, which he was especially accustomed to use on the festive days of birthdays. He also took his daughter captive as his wife. Chilperic, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth year of his reign, in the tenth indiction of the Romans, too treacherously presses, harasses, and disturbs Sigebert his brother, king of the Franks in Austrasia, through his son Theodebert.
