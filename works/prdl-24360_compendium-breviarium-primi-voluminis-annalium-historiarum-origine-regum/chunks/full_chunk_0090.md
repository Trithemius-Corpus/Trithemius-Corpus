and I had begun to be the destroyer of those things of which I ought to have been the defender and exalter, fearing neither the eyes of the divine majesty nor the outcry of men. At length the kindness of God, crushing the pride of my obstinate mind with the rod of correction, visited my iniquities, and, when I had been chastened, did not deny me pardon. For, caught up on high and presented before the divine majesty, seated among the choirs of the saints in the guise of one judging, I was gravely and steadfastly accused by the most holy patrons of the churches for ecclesiastical goods seized by robbery by me and consumed, especially by the most holy Mary, mother of God, and by the most blessed Peter and Paul, princes of the apostles.

¶ And when, burdened by conscience, I did not dare to defend my offences, at length my special patron, the martyr Dionysius, came to my aid; by his prayers he obtained for me a period of delay of the punishment that was pressing upon me. He himself, as though setting a manner of penance, placed in this my hope of full pardon: if, to the honour of God and of the holy preachers whom I had more greatly offended, I should build a house suitable for divine services. Gladly making this promise, I vowed it, and, dismissed by those above, I returned. Therefore, in sure hope of remission and pardon of our sins, rendering with eager zeal all that we have done, we have built a church in honour of the Holy Trinity, and of holy Mary ever-virgin, and of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, in the place called Vuyssenburg; and we have determined to exalt it and enrich it with royal munificence. Therefore we give to it, and wish to have been given in perpetuity, from the property of our fisc lying adjacent there, whatever belongs to us, both in villages and in bondsmen, woods, vineyards, fields, meadows, pastures, waters and watercourses; and all these things we transfer by free conveyance to the use of the brethren serving God there under monastic profession. And so that the authority of this our donation may remain stable and firm for all time, we have taken care to note below the boundaries and the names of the surrounding places. Toward the eastern region the march which we have conveyed extends as far as Morchohouen and to Aldenherden, and thence to Goboldefvuege, and thence below Nadumlutra, and thence to Buozdigenhurst, and thence to the boundary which stands on the summit of the valley Iuuenesdal, and thence it leads toward the southern region above Vuarbach, and thence to Bodemloseffaffa, and thence to Sebach, and thence to Kirchdal, and thence to Ingoldesizahe, and thence to the wooded mountains as far as Bedebur. Toward the western region, however, as far as Lutenbach and Bernbach, and thence to Grunenbrunnen, and thence to Otterichescheyt. Toward the northern region as far as Eichenerenberg. But the place itself, which we embrace with singular devotion, now and henceforth we wish to be free from every oppression of servitude, under royal protection, namely in such a way that no public judge, no one from the judicial power, may presume to have any power there, nor to compel the men of that place either to hear cases, or to pay [auffreda?], or taxes.
