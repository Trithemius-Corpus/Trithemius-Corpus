Hunibald records the other things wonderfully said, written, or done by Hildegast. In the twenty-fourth year of Hilderic, king of the Franks, Alexander, the son of Mammaea, Caesar Augustus of the Romans, was killed at Mainz, a city of Germany, in a military mutiny, in the thirteenth year of his reign.

*Alexander Caesar is killed at Mainz.*

Hilderic, moreover, king of the Franks, the first of that name, after many deeds bravely and magnificently performed, died at last in the fortieth year of his reign, and in the two hundred fifty-third year of the Lord's Nativity, in the first Roman indiction.

*The Thirty-First.*

Bartherus, son of King Hilderic, succeeding his father, reigned eighteen years. In his time the Romans waged war with the Goths, who [unclear] the river Ister [unclear] killed [unclear] thousand, under the leadership of Decius Caesar, his son. Many of the Romans also fell, among them his sons. But Decius Caesar, a most wicked persecutor of Christians, while fleeing, was submerged in the whirlpool of a certain marsh; his corpse could never be found by his men.

*Bartherus reigned eighteen years.*

In the tenth year of King Bartherus, when the Romans under the wanton rule of Gallienus were sluggish with idleness, the Franks, thinking that a suitable time was at hand to avenge their insults against them, with the Germans, Saxons, Bavarians, and Thuringians assisting, gathered a great army of warriors, entered the borders of the enemy, and, devastating everything with fire, advanced by hostile incursion with the sword as far as Ravenna; enriched with plunder, they returned to their own lands.

*The Franks launch an expedition against the Romans.*

*Franks, Germans, Saxons, Bavarians, Thuringians.*

In the following year, which was the eleventh of King Bartherus, the Alemanni, after mustering their forces, led a strong army into Gaul; and after the region had been laid waste by sword and fire, they returned to their own lands with spoils and much plunder. In the same year also they launched an expedition into Italy against the Romans, inflicted much damage on them, and avenged the injuries, many in number, that they had received from them.

*The Alemanni laid waste Gaul and Italy.*

In the [thirteenth?] year of King Bartherus, which was the two hundred sixty-sixth year of the Lord's Nativity, in the fourteenth Roman indiction, the Franks, with the aid of the German Saxons, mustered a huge army; after crossing the Rhine and the Meuse, under the leaders Antharius and Luther they entered Gaul, despoiling everything in hostile fashion. Duke Antharius was the son of Bartherus, the aforesaid king of the Franks; Luther was the son of Marbod, king in Saxony.

*The Franks again moved into Gaul.*
