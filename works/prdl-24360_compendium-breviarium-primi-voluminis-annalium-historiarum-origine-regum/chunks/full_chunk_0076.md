Since, therefore, it is not within my powers to reconcile so great a discordance of histories, I follow the judgment of those whom I find to have differed less from one another, which could be observed not uniformly in every case, but in certain cases.

Therefore the third-born son of King Clodoveus, from whom the line of the kings of the Franks descending in direct succession continued down to Hilderic, the last of the stock of Faramund, reigned after his father in Soissons, a city of the Franks, for fifty years; and when his brothers and all their sons had died, he finally recalled into one monarchy the kingdom that had been divided into four parts. Each of his brothers reigned for a time in his own place.

*The Forty-Eighth.*

*Lotharius reigns for fifty years.*

¶ For Theodoricus, the first-born of King Clodoveus, whom he had begotten not by Queen Clotildis, but before his marriage to her by a certain concubine, received in the division of the kingdom of the Franks Austrasia, in which is the city of Metz, together with the other cities belonging to it; and at that time it was called the kingdom of Austrasia, or of the Austrasians, in which he reigned for twenty-three years. After him Theodobertus, his son, reigned over the Austrasians for twenty-two years. When he too had died, his son Theodobaldus reigned over the people of Metz for nine years; and when he had departed this life, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 560, in the eighth Roman indiction, without children, his uncle Lotharius, named above, received the kingdom of the Austrasians, or of the people of Metz.

¶ Clodomirus, however, the third son of Clodoveus, but the first-born by Queen Clotildis, reigned at Orléans in Gaul for seven years; and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 521, after he had killed Sigismund, king of the Burgundians, with his wife and sons, and had again engaged with his brother Godemarus, he was killed by him. He left three young sons, Theodobaldus, Guntherus, and Clodoaldus; after their father’s death, the most pious grandmother Clotildis, living at Tours, raised them with herself like a mother. But a few years later, Lotharius and Hildebertus, kings and brothers of the aforesaid Clodomirus, having summoned to themselves their nephews Theodobaldus and Guntharius, and in their ambition for rule devising wicked things, killed them with their own hands. But the third, changing nobility destined to perish into divine servitude, became a cleric.
