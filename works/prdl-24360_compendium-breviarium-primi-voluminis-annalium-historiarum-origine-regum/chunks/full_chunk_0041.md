*The final acquisition of all Europe by the Franks*

Whatever the Gaul possesses, whatever all Germany possesses,
all will yield to your right, warlike Sicamber,
when it shall have given you this man, the first king by name,
whom the princes, leaders now without a king, will appoint;
then, when he is victorious, the Frank will reign [without end?].

Concerning the empire of the Romans also he added as follows:

Behold, the great God will give brothel-camps to the Frank,
and the lion will break the eagle, dashed against by the serpent.

Hunibald explains this meaning concerning the standards of the Franks which they used in battles, which we call among the emblems of princes.

*What the emblems or arms of the ancient Franks were*

They depicted a lion, with the forepart of its body, on a fleece of blue color, its feet stretched out for seizing and its mouth open in the manner of one roaring; the lower part of its body ended in a serpent’s tail, with which it seemed to press and suffocate the neck of an eagle, depicted beneath on a golden fleece with wings spread, and most strongly bound.

The Romans had the eagle as their standard when fighting. Desiring to suppress and annihilate its proud and arrogant power by strength and prudence together, the Franks took the lion for the one and the serpent for the other. For by the lion the Franks wished mystically to signify their strength, and by the serpent wisdom and prudence, by which they undoubtedly hoped that they would at last overcome [unclear]. All subsequent kings of the Franks used these emblems or arms, though they are improperly so called, down to Clodoneus the Christian, who is read to have taken up, in place of the lion, three lilies sent down from heaven, as will be said in its place.

*Hildegast, a noble teacher of the Franks in all knowledge.*

*The Franks began to be more refined in manners and in building.*

Hildegast also, the wise man of the Franks already mentioned, taught the sons of the nobles to sing in their mother tongue the deeds of their ancestors in songs, with lyres and various other musical instruments. He admonished the people to live by civilized customs, to build houses, and to go about more refined in dress, and he ordered them to put aside savagery. For indeed up to that time the Franks cared little for cultivation or adornment, either in buildings or in clothing. Hildegast established all these things in a more refined form, and most wisely instructed them to crush and subdue the pride of the Romans not only by arms, but also in possessions and manners.
