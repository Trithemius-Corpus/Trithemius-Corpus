*Clodoveus reigned in Neustria.*
*Sigibert, his brother, reigned in Austrasia. Saint Amandus, bishop in Maastricht.*
*Pippin, mayor of the palace in Austrasia.*
*Saint Chunibert, archbishop of Cologne, chancellor of the kingdom of Austrasia.*
*Saint Irmina becomes the first abbess in the Horrea at Trier, of our order.*
*The head of Saint Irmina is kept at Spanheim.*
*Saint Modesta. Saint Anastasia. Saint Basilissa, abbesses there.*
*Saint Modoald, builder of many monasteries.*
*The monastery of Saint Symphorian is founded.*
*Saint Severa, abbess of our order near Trier.*

Clodoveus reigned over the Franks throughout inner Gaul, namely in Neustria, that is, Paris, Amiens, Beauvais, Blois, Reims, Orleans, Tours, also in Aquitaine, and in the whole kingdom that had been his father’s.

Sigibert, however, his brother, as we have said, reigned in Austrasia: over the people of Metz, Trier, Cologne, Liege, and Traiectum, where Saint Amandus was then bishop; also over the Tongri, Morini, Hollanders, Frisians, Saxons, Sorbs, Thuringians, Bavarians, Suevi, Alemanni, Cisrhenani, the people of Strasbourg, Worms, Mainz, [unclear], and the Burgundians. His mayor of the palace, or prince of the palace, was Pippin the Elder, son of Ansegisus and grandson of Arnulf, count of Namur and duke of Brabant, father of [Grimoald?] and of Saint Gertrude, abbess of Nivelles, in the paternal duchy of Brabant.

The chancellor of Sigibert was Saint Chunibert, archbishop of Cologne. In most of the places or provinces already named, under the rule of King Sigibert and of Pippin, mayor of the palace, there were appointed petty kings, dukes, counts, and prefects, by whose administration the people were governed.

Irmina, a virgin of most holy conduct, was one of the daughters of King Dagobert and the sister of the aforesaid kings. For her, her father, while still living, built in the city of Trier, in his very ancient palace which was called the Horrea, a monastery of maidens under the Rule of Saint Benedict, and a church in honor of blessed Mary ever virgin. In it he placed a congregation of nuns, and gave them this same daughter of his as mother and abbess; she was renowned in life and after death for many virtues and miracles, and her venerable head is kept in the abbey of Spanheim. In the governance of the nuns of the aforesaid monastery, Modesta succeeded this Irmina; she was a pupil of the monastery of Romaric and daughter of the sister of blessed Modoald, archbishop of Trier. After her came Anastasia, and in the fourth place Basilissa; all these have been enrolled among the saints.

At the same time the aforesaid bishop of Trier, Modoald, at whose urging the already-mentioned monastery in the Horrea had been built, also constructed another monastery in an ancient palace situated in the suburbs of Trier. In it he established nuns under the same Rule of Saint Benedict, and set over them Adela, another daughter of King Dagobert.

The same bishop Modoald built a third holy congregation of maidens on the bank of the Moselle, beside the walls of the city of Trier, in honor of the holy martyr Symphorian, under the rule of Saint Benedict. There he appointed his sister, named Severa, as mother and abbess over the nuns; living in all purity of mind and body, she in no way fell short of her brother’s holiness.
