Meanwhile, while Sigebert was occupied in Thuringia with the Huns, his brother, King Chilperic, forgetful of brotherly charity, laid waste the borders of his kingdom with a hostile incursion, and besieged the city of Reims, and received it in surrender.

In the same year spears were seen in the air, and battle-lines of soldiers, and at the same time the din of clashes was heard, first portending the incursion of the Lombards into Italy, concerning whom Pope Gregory the Great speaks in a homily. For in the following year, namely the year of the Lord’s nativity 568, in the first Roman indiction, Alboin king of the Lombards entrusted Pannonia, in which he had formerly dwelt with his people, to the Huns, friends of the Lombards, and, taking as allies Saxons, Marcomanni, and other neighboring peoples, occupied with all his people that part of Italy which, from the name of the people, is called Lombardy down to the present.

In the same year Sigebert king of the Franks, who lived at Metz in Austrasia, brother of Chilperic, surveying the land of Saxony, empty of inhabitants who had gone with the Lombards, as we have said, into Italy, lest it be reduced utterly to solitude, handed it over to the Suevi to inhabit. And so the Saxons lived with the Lombards in Italy, and the Suevi in Saxony, for fourteen years. When these had passed, since the Saxons saw themselves among the Lombards in the lowest place, in the condition of slaves, they took counsel to return to Saxony.

Therefore in the year of the Lord’s nativity 582, in the fifteenth Roman indiction, the Saxons who were with the Lombards in Italy, no longer enduring the pride and unjust domination of the Winili, for so the Lombards were formerly called, returned with their children and wives, under the conduct of Sigebert king of the Franks, through southern Gaul to Cologne Agrippina. There, after crossing the Rhine, they sought again their former seats in Saxony, which they had left behind.

But the Suevi, to whom King Sigebert had given them to inhabit, at first denied the Saxons entry; secondly, they agreed to restore to them a third part. But when the Saxons demanded back not a part but the whole, the Suevi offered half the region. The Saxons remained constant in their judgment, demanding back not a part, not half of the land, but the entire whole.

At last the matter was committed to the sword; the fight for possession of the settlements was waged very fiercely by both armies. At length the Suevi were defeated; more than twenty thousand of them perished in that battle by the sword of the Saxons. And thus the Saxons recovered by war the land which, by the persuasion of the Winili, they had left quite imprudently, in the fourteenth year after their departure, some of the Suevi being killed, others expelled.
