In the sixteenth year of Hildebert, king of the Franks, Grimoald, whom Pippin, already weighed down by old age, had appointed mayor of the palace in his place, was killed at Liege, while praying before the altar of St. Lambert, by Ranegarius, an attendant of Radbod, duke of the Frisians.

After his pitiable death, Theudobald, son of Drogo, was made mayor of the palace of the Franks and prince of the court by his grandfather Pippin; in this principate he could scarcely rule for two years, but was cast down by the Franks.

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord's nativity 714, Pippin, son of Ansegisus, mayor of the palace of the kingdom of the Franks, died. He designated as heir of his principate his son named Charles, surnamed Tutides or Martel, born of the concubine Alpaida. But Plectrude, the legitimate wife left by Pippin, seized this Charles, her stepson, assigned him to very close custody at Cologne, and seized the principate with her grandson Theudobald.

Meanwhile Hildebert, king of the Franks, dies in the eighteenth year of his reign, which was the year of the Lord's nativity 704, in the thirteenth indiction. The son of King Hildebert, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, reigned four years, or, as others wish, five. Most writers of histories affirm that this man was not named Clovis but Dagobert; whether this diversity occurred by fault of the writers or on any other occasion, we do not know for certain. The greater part, however, call him Dagobert, and he is cast down from the principate. After him the Franks appoint Ragenfrid mayor of the palace.

The Franks, laboring under hatred of Theudobald, conspire together as one; with forces gathered from here and there they move a sufficiently dangerous war against him, in which, after many had been killed on both sides, Theudobald is at last defeated, put to flight, and cast down from the principate. After him the Franks appoint Ragenfrid mayor of the palace.

Meanwhile Charles, son of Pippin, freed from the custody of his stepmother by divine prompting, soon strives to snatch his grandfather's principate from the hand of Ragenfrid; and first, when Radbod, duke of Frisia, was aiding the enemy, he attacked and suffered loss of his army.

In the third year of Clovis or Dagobert of the Franks, Charles, in the district of Cambrai at Vincy, on Palm Sunday, which was the third day before the Kalends of April, engaged Hilperic and Ragenfrid, turned them both to flight, and pursued them as far as Paris. Hilperic, moreover, was by rank a priest, named Daniel, whom the Franks had established as king for themselves in Neustria.

After this Dagobert, or Clovis, king of the Franks, dies in the fourth year of his reign, and in the year of the Lord's nativity 711, in the second indiction.

Here it is pleasing to insert the false opinion of certain persons concerning the foundation of the monastery of St. Peter the Apostle in Erfurt, who grant to King Dagobert the great honor of this foundation; but neither time nor reason permits this.
