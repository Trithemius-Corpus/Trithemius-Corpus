In the year of the Lord 419 Genebaldus died, interrex of the Franks, son of Dagobert and grandson of King Marcomir, in the twenty-first year of his rule; after his death the Franks, as we shall say, again had kings.

In that same year, immediately after the death of Genebaldus, the nobles and elders of the Franks gathered together and held mature counsel among themselves, judging it useful that, after the custom of the ancients, they should appoint for themselves one king from among the dukes. And the proposal seemed good in the eyes of all; and the following dukes assembled, for the sake of this matter, at another diet: namely Pharamundus, duke of the eastern Franks; Duke Marcomir and Duke Sunno, brothers of the eastern Pharamundus; Duke Clodius, son of Pharamundus; Duke Dagobertus, son of Marcomir; Duke Nicanor; Duke Pharabertus; Duke Richimer; Antenor, duke of the Menipolitani; Duke Priamus, his brother; Bertheri, a Gallican duke; Heribert, an island duke; Duke Sunno and Duke Richer, sons of the late Genebaldus, duke and interrex of the Franks; Duke Diocles; and Duke Meroveus. There were also present some of the priests and wise men of the Franks: Salegasthald, chief priest of Jupiter; Gasthald; Herahd, master of letters; Vuisogasthald, priest of Diana; Rutvuicus; Adelhardus; Richeri; and many others, both from the common people and from the nobles.

All these, after sharing among themselves the counsel of mature deliberation, at last with unanimous consent elected Pharamundus, duke of eastern Francia at Wurzburg, as king of the Franks. He had been the son of Duke Marcomir, who died, as we said above, in the year of the Lord 404; this Marcomir was the son of Clodius, who was the son of Dagobert, who was the son of Genebaldus, first duke of the eastern Franks and brother of King Marcomir; their father was Clogio, king of the Franks, who, as we said above, died in the year of the Lord 478.

Pharamundus, therefore, son of Marcomir, great duke of the eastern Franks, elected king by the common vote of the nobles in the aforesaid year of the Lord’s nativity, 419, in the second Roman indiction, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of April, in the fifty-sixth year of his age, reigned over the Franks for seven years: a man esteemed in his generation, but not a Christian.

He soon entrusted the duchy of eastern Francia to his brother Marcomir to govern; Marcomir ruled it energetically for eighteen years. His lineage and successors in the duchy, continuing in order down to the last duke Hetanus and to Charles the Great, son of Pippin, we have described above, when we set down the genealogy of the first duke.

In the third year of his reign, King Pharamundus of the Franks, in order to restrain by laws a people uncultivated in its customs and fierce in spirit, summoned certain nobles of the kingdom to him and began to hold mature counsel concerning the establishment of new ordinances.
