I would gladly have passed over this babbling of the ignorant, had not the defense of my writings compelled me to bring triflers into the open. For some time ago, at the insistence of the abbots Blasius and Johannes, we wrote two volumes, of no small size, of chronicles or annals of the distinguished monastery of Hirsau of our order, which is situated in the diocese of Speyer; in the first of these we proved sufficiently, by many arguments, that the aforesaid monastery of Saint Peter at Erfurt was founded originally not by Dagobert, king of the Franks, but by Saint Wilhelm, twelfth abbot of Hirsau. And because we have now learned that this assertion of ours is all the more true from the most foolishly fabricated letters which we saw in the aforesaid Erfurt monastery, secured by no seal of anyone, and which we read under the name of Dagobert and transcribed, lest anyone accuse us of falsehood, we have thought it worthwhile to join a copy of the same letters, together with other arguments, to our assertion.

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot, in the first book of the Annals of Hirsau.*

*He proves the Erfurt opinion to be false.*

*The foundation of the monastery of Saint Peter at Erfurt. It is shown to have been made by brothers from Hirsau.*

*Copy of the letters of foundation which are ascribed to Dagobert.*

In the name of the holy and undivided Trinity. I, Dagobert, king of the Franks, make known to clergy as well as people, to Christians as well as pagans, that our city, by name Merovegesburg, which had come to us by hereditary right from our grandfather Meroveus himself, namely the founder of that same city in Thuringia, we have given by royal power to Saint Peter and to his monks serving God there, to be possessed after my death, together with all its services; and the very mountain, after the foundation of the monastery which he built in honor of the aforesaid apostle, took its name, the pagan name of my grandfather having first been abolished, and was called Peter’s Mount. But by what ordinance of God this was done, we shall relate.

There was in the aforesaid city, beside the church of Saint Blasius the martyr, a man of God named Adeodatus, enclosed at my request by Rigebert, bishop of Mainz. This enclosed man, because of peril to his soul, was unwilling to make use of the communion of clerics who used wives and arms, and often asked me to summon for him from some cloister of mine a monk from whom he might hear the divine office and the word of the true faith, and receive the communion of the sacred mystery. Assenting to the request of this man, on account of our mutual familiarity, I sent to the monastery of monks which I likewise built in Selsenburg, summoning from there a venerable elder monk named Truckindus, and directed him to the aforesaid enclosed man Adeodatus as teacher and rector. When he arrived there, gradually, by his teaching and persuasion, the number of believers began to be multiplied, both through the conversion of pagans to baptism and of clerics and laymen to the monastic habit. Seeing this, and understanding the divine favor in it, I handed over my often-mentioned city, with all things belonging to it, to Saint Peter and to his monks, for monastic religion to be established there and an abbey to be made with my aid.

*His grandfather was not, but the fourteenth king before him.*

*At that time Erfurt was a small village, not a city.*

*Rigibertus was bishop of Mainz.*

*It is certain that the kings of the Franks at that time could do nothing. All power was in the hands of the mayor of the palace.*

*Nor did he do this, nor could he, because the mayor of the palace was lord.*
