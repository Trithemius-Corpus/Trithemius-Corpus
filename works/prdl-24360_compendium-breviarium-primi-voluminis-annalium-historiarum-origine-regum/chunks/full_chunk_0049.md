*The Suevi make firm peace with the Franks.*

*Marcomer, duke of the eastern Franks.*

In the same year, in the month of September, Marcomir, son of Duke Genebald, came with the remaining band of the Franks, which by King Clodomir’s command had already earlier been chosen for migration by families, households, and persons, into the southern borders of Thuringia, and around the river Main to receive with his brothers the dwellings promised to them, having on the east the Boii and Bohemians, on the south the Suevi, on the west the Rhenish people, the people of Mainz, and the Apodas, and on the north the Fagophagi and Thuringians.

At the entrance of the Franks, the Thuringians, by order of Heurolus their king, withdrew beyond the forest, leaving all the grain, sufficient for the new guests for the following year. Therefore the Franks, newly arrived, first settled in the borders of the Thuringians between the river Falanus, as it is now called from Falagastus, and the lower parts of the Neckar, around the banks of the river Main. In that district, which was formerly called Meingau and afterwards East Francia, in which over time they built no small number of villages and cities, and in a short while grew in wealth and power.

But Duke Genebald begot Marcomir and Dagobert, who ruled over the eastern Franks for twenty-one years. Dagobert begot Clodius, who ruled over the people for ten years. Clodius begot Marcomir, who ruled for sixteen years. Marcomir begot Faramund and Marcomir. Faramund, both with his father and alone, administered the duchy for fifteen years, and afterwards, having been elected, became king of the Franks, as we shall say in its proper order. He entrusted the duchy of eastern Francia to his brother Marcomir, who ruled it for eighteen years. Duke Marcomir begot Priam, who ruled for twelve years. Priam begot Genebald the second, who ruled for twenty years. Genebald begot Sunno, who ruled for twenty-three years. After him Clodus, brother of Clodoneus king of the Franks, ruled for sixteen years. When he was struck by a boar while hunting and killed, Clodomir, son of the aforesaid Duke Sunno, succeeded in the duchy and ruled for twenty-one years. Clodomir begot Hugbad, who ruled for twenty-six years. Hugbad begot Helenus, who ruled for thirty years. Helenus begot Gotfrid, who ruled for twenty-two years. Gotfrid begot Genebald the third, who ruled for thirty years. Genebald begot Clodomir, who ruled for nineteen years. After him Heribert, his nephew, succeeding in the duchy, ruled for thirty years. When he also died, Clodoneus, his brother’s son, ruled in the duchy for twelve years. Clodoneus begot Gosvuert, who ruled for [unclear] years.
