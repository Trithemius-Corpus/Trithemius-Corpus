From that time the Thuringians did not have a king of their own.

And Radegund, among the other captives, daughter of Bertharius, once king, whom, as we have said, his brother Ermenfrid had caused to be killed through Theodoric king of the Metensians, King Lotharius joined to himself in marriage. She shone with great holiness, and afterward, becoming a nun, was radiant with very many signs and miracles. Concerning this destruction of the Thuringians, Fortunatus the archbishop wrote more fully in verse and in prose.

In the twelfth year of his reign, Lotharius king of the Franks, with his brother Hildebert, besieged Autun, a city of Burgundy, and, after King Godomar had been put to flight, powerfully subjected the whole province of the Burgundians to his rule.

In the year [unclear] of the reign of Lotharius king of the Franks, he powerfully wrested from the hand of King Athalaric whatever his grandfather Theodoric, king of Italy, had possessed in Gaul.

In these days Saint Nicetius flourished, formerly abbot of the monastery of Limoges, archbishop of Trier, a man of many virtues in Christ, whose deeds are extant. Theodoric king of Austrasia, who ruled over the Metensians, the people of Trier, Liege, Cologne, Mainz, and all those dwelling this side of the Rhine, brought many clerics from among the Arvernian citizens, whom he ordered to be enrolled for service to the Lord in the church of Trier.

In the twenty-sixth year of King Lotharius, in the ninth indiction, King Hildebert, with his brother’s son Theodobert, rose against that same Lotharius, his brother. But his mother Clotild, by prayers before God, acting so that there should be no war among her sons, they were restrained by a dreadful storm; and since not even a sign of so great a terror appeared over Lotharius, Hildebert, compelled by manifest necessity, withdrew from his brother in peace.

In the twenty-eighth year, Lotharius and Hildebert, the aforementioned brothers, after besieging the city of Caesaraugusta in Spain, when the citizens prayed to God and carried around the tunic of the holy martyr Vincent, received from them as a gift the stole of that martyr himself, and returned in peace, after the greater part of Spain had been acquired by war. At the same time Saint Maurus and Faustus, disciples of the divine Benedict, flourished in Gallic France, distinguished in conduct, learning, holiness of life, and miracles.

In the fortieth year of Lotharius king of the Franks, and in the year 554 of the Lord’s nativity, in the second Roman indiction, Queen Clotild died, formerly the wife of the great Clovis, king of France, with whom she also was buried at Paris in the church of Saint Peter. In the same year Bucellinus and Amingus, dukes of the Franks, having joined battle with Narses the Roman patrician, were both killed by him, together with not a few others.

For these were the two dukes Bucellinus and Amingus, who seven years earlier had entered Italy with Theodobert, king of the Franks, with a strong force, and did not cease to disturb it with various warlike assaults.
