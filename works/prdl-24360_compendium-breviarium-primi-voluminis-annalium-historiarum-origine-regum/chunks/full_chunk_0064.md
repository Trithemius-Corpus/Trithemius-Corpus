Clodius, son of King Pharamund, succeeding his father in the same year, reigned twenty years, a high-minded, brave, and warlike prince, who by his valor powerfully crushed the Gauls and Romans, and extended the boundaries of the kingdom of the Franks far and wide through Gaul, and because of his power was feared by all the nations throughout all Europe.

He, immediately at the beginning of his reign, commanded all the Franks by edict to grow long hair, so that from the Gauls, whom they were either going to attack or who, already conquered, were dwelling with them, the Franks might be distinguished at first sight. For this reason thereafter King Clodus himself was called “the long-haired” or “the hairy,” for he grew his hair and beard long. But the Gauls conquered by him he ordered to have their hair cut off, as a sign of servitude. From this one may infer that the Frankish Germans subjected the Gauls to servitude, and that neither the Gauls nor any other nation ever overcame the Germans. For it is clear, as Hunibald bears witness, that from the time when the Franks first entered Germany until the death of King Clodoveus, who first received the faith of Christ, as we shall say, through not less than nine hundred forty years, under forty-seven kings and petty kings, they always were and remained entirely free, and never served anyone even for a moment. But after Clodoneus, that all the Franks remained free down to the present day, both those who are in Gaul and those who remained in Germany, since it is known to those skilled in histories without envy, needs no proof.

Clodus, king of the Franks, in the eighth year of his reign, which was the four hundred thirty-fourth year from the Lord’s birth, in the second indiction of the Romans, having gathered forces, moved his army into Thuringia. The Franks, as we said far above, under King Franco, in the thirty-seventh year before the birth of Christ, had made a perpetual treaty with the Thuringians, Saxons, Teutons, and Germans, which was kept inviolate by all for almost 430 years, until the death of King Marcomir. But after Marcomir, king of the Franks, was killed by the emperor Valentinian, as was said before, in the year of the Lord 393, the Thuringians and Saxons, together with the Germans and Teutons, withdrew from the alliance of the Franks, affairs having been conducted not sufficiently prudently under the dukes.

On account of this breach, King Clodus powerfully entered Thuringia, defeated the king and the people, and compelled them to pay annual tribute to the Franks and thereafter to obey the commands of the victors, so that those who had scorned to remain free allies under the law of the ancient treaty were forced to be their servants, subjects, and tributaries. The same later happened to the Germans and Saxons, who, after breaking the treaty of alliance, thereafter no longer found the Franks friends, as before, but kings and lords. Then King Clodius, after crossing both the Rhine and the Meuse, moved an expedition into Gaul, and, devastating and powerfully occupying the whole region of Tongeren, subjected it to the kingdom of the Franks; and for the confirmation of the new possession, which he had formerly lost but now recovered, he settled there for some time. Then, advancing as far as [Tabula?], he recovered everything.

*Forty-Fourth*

Clodus the long-haired reigned twenty years.

The Franks, as free men, grew their hair long.

The Gauls, as slaves, had their hair cut off.

Not the Gauls subjected the Franks to themselves, but the Franks subjected the Gauls.

All the Franks, both Gallic and Germanic, were always entirely free.

King Clodus moved the army of the Franks into Thuringia.

The Thuringians withdraw from the alliance of the Franks.

The Franks subject Thuringia to their kingdom, so that the Thuringians are subjected to the servitude of the Franks.

The Franks subject the Gauls to their servitude.
