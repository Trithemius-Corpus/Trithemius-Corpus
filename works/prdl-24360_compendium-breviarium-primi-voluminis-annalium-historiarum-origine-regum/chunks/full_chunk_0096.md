[unclear] and fifty-second. Sigebert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, adopted Hildebert, son of Grunwald, mayor of the palace, as his son, and the kingdom.

In the following year Saint Judoc, son of the king of the Britons, despising kingdom and world, a pilgrim and hermit in the district of Ponthieu, rested in Christ.

In the eleventh year of King Clovis, Sigebert, king of Austrasia, his brother, died, leaving his son Dagobert still very small and entrusted to the faith of Grunwald, mayor of the palace, so that by his help he might be advanced to the kingdom. In the following year Grunwald had him tonsured by Dido, bishop of Poitiers, and sent him to Scotland; and, against his pledge, he raised up Hildebert his own son as king of the Austrasians. But the Franks, grieving over so great a breach of faith by Grunwald, captured him and presented him at Paris for the judgment of King Clovis. Clovis had him bound and made him waste away in prison by a bitter death; and, after casting down his son, he appointed his own younger son Childeric king of the Austrasians, and avenged the wrong done to his nephew.

King Clovis of the Franks, because of an insufficiently reverent contact with the body of Saint Denis, became mad and senseless for two years and died in the seventeenth year of his reign, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 662, in the fifth indiction.

The eldest son of King Clovis reigned after his father over the Franks for four years. His mayor of the palace was Ebroin, a man of Gallic cunning, deceitful, ready in counsel for crimes, perverse in action.

From this point the kings of the Franks, down to Pippin, father of Charlemagne, for almost ninety years, degenerating from their forefathers in courage and knowledge, reigned in name alone, doing nothing worthy of the royal office; but all power of governing the kingdom was in the hands of the princes of the court, who had been called mayors of the palace. The kings, however, stayed at home in idleness, doing or arranging nothing, but gave themselves sluggishly to feasting and drinking. Only once in the year, at the Kalends of May, did they come forth in public and show themselves to the people to be seen and greeted, and so returned home.

In these times Queen Balthild, mother of Lothar and formerly the wife left by King Clovis, founded two distinguished monasteries of our order in Gallican France, one of which is called Corbie and the other Chelles; in them many most holy and most learned monks were illustrious.
