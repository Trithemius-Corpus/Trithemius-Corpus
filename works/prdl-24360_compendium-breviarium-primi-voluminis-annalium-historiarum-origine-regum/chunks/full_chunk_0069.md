Hilderic, through the diligence and loyalty of his friend Winibald, who was one of the more powerful princes of the kingdom, was recalled from Thuringia to the kingdom of the Franks, restored to his paternal kingdom with the favor of all the nobles and of the nation, and Aegidius, hateful to all, was driven from it. And thereafter Hilderic reigned very moderately for another twenty-two years.

And when Basina, wife of Basanus, king of the Thuringians, had heard that Hilderic had been restored by the Franks to the kingdom, she left her husband and followed him into Gaul, stirred by excessive love for him, which she had contracted from long familiarity in living together among the Thuringians. Hilderic, receiving her as she came with a cheerful countenance, took her as his wife, as one known to him, ill repaying this hospitality to King Basanus, by whom for three years he had been kindly received as a guest.

But as the first night of the marriage approached, Basina advised Hilderic to postpone intercourse for a little while, to watch the doors of the bedchamber, and to report faithfully to her as she prophesied what prodigies he saw in the courtyard on the third occasion. The king, trusting the woman, obeyed as she commanded; and after watching the buildings for the third time, he returned to her, saying that first he had seen in the courtyard of the palace rhinoceroses, that is, unicorns, and also lions and leopards. Secondly, wolves and bears raging and pursuing one another. Thirdly, he said, he saw many dogs, but small ones, which tore one another with their teeth and bit fiercely; but you tell me what they signify.

To these things the woman, a prophetess, spoke thus to the king: Behold, we shall beget offspring, diverse in character in the course of time. For the first generation that will be born from us will be very noble, such that in its nobility it is to be compared to lions and to noble unicorns. From the first generation in time a second will arise, which, after the manner of wolves, with nobility despised, will practice robberies and oppressions of the poor. The third generation after this, which will also be the last from us, will after the manner of dogs tear, bite, and strike one another, until at last, in the final days, the kingdom will fail. That same night the woman conceived and bore a son, named Clodoveus, as we shall say.

In the following year, which was the four hundred sixty-third from the Lord’s nativity, a son was born to Hilderic, named Clodoveus; he afterward succeeded his father in the kingdom of the Franks, and was the first to receive the faith of Christ, as we shall say below.

At about the same time Hilderic, king of the Franks, having gathered a great army of fighters, made war against the people of the Alemanni, who dwelt between Italy and Swabia, where now are the cities of Chur, Sitten, and the Valais. When battle was joined, the Alemanni were defeated and reduced under tribute to the Franks.

In these times also, when Uther Pendragon, king of Britain, which is now called England, had died, his son, named Arthur, was raised up in his kingdom. Concerning him not only the history of the Britons, as Geoffrey of Monmouth relates, but also the voices of the peoples of England proclaim wonders down to the present day, although many things are more fabulous than in accord with the truth.
