This also we have decreed by equal authority: that no bishop of the city of Speyer, nor any of the other bishops, shall have authority there for ruling, or for conducting any cases whatever; but under the defense of our immunity the father of the monastery, with his brothers, shall freely and quietly possess everything belonging to that place. In addition to this, we grant to the same place a coinage having on it images, letters, and the expressed likeness of the coinage of Speyer, and by the equal gift of our authority we wish it to be valid and firm: that the men of the same church may freely attend to their business throughout our whole kingdom, in such a way, namely, that they pay no toll due. And no judge, or any person of whatever rank, shall presume to inflict any violence upon it in a case of this kind.

Moreover, to the brothers serving God there, so that they may guard the order of holy conduct the more devoutly and tranquilly, we have given free power among themselves of choosing an abbot for themselves. To this abbot, and to all who succeed him, we grant this special privilege of dignity: that he may promote into the order and right of ministerials whomever from the household he shall judge worthy of such honor. All the ministerials of the same church shall also receive their benefice from the gift of the abbot, and shall serve the abbot alone; nor shall the advocate dare to exercise any power over them, unless perhaps, because of some disobedience, the abbot invites the advocate to constrain those ministerials as rebels.

But the rest of the household, at the lawful place and time, three times in the year, ought to assemble at the advocate’s court for the doing of justice, and, if the advocate is present, to pay single denarii for the advocate’s service, excepting those who are in the immunity. But if the advocate is absent from these courts, nothing is owed to him from these denarii, unless he is proved to be in the king’s service. And so that this our tradition, the institution of a dignity of granted right, may remain stable and unshaken forever, we have signed this written charter, strengthened by the addition of our seal. Given, as we did it, in that same monastery, in the month of May, on the eleventh day, in the twenty-third year of our reign, in the name of Christ, happily. Amen. In the year of the Lord 624.

I find nothing in these letters that casts doubt for me upon the foundation mentioned; but because the date of the writing of this page does not agree with the computation of the years of the Lord according to Sigebert, the monk of Gembloux, and also Rupert Gaguin, and many other most diligent writers of histories, I do not know whence this has arisen. For if credit is to be given to the said historiographers, it will be plainly established that Lotharius, the father of Dagobert, was still living among men in the year of the Lord 624, although the son himself asserts that he was dead before he began to found the monastery of Wissembourg.
