In the ninth year of King Theodoric, Ebroin, mayor of the palace in Neustria, since he did not cease cruelly to oppress the Franks by his tyranny, was killed by a certain Ermenfrid, a noble man; this Ermenfrid soon fled to Pippin in Austrasia, who held the chief office of mayor of the palace. When the most wicked Ebroin had been slain, a certain Waratto was appointed mayor of the palace.

In the following year a battle between Pippin and Giselmar, son of Waratto, was fought at [Nauinucum?]. Not long afterward, when Giselmar had been punished by divine judgment, and Waratto also had died, his son-in-law Bertharius became mayor of the palace. But the Franks, suffering under Bertharius’s estrangement, sought Pippin’s friendship through hostages, promising aid and faithful service if he would take the chief rule from him in war.

In the twelfth year of his reign King Theodoric and Bertharius, his mayor of the palace, declared war on Pippin, who held himself as mayor of the palace in Austrasia against their will. But Pippin conquered in that battle and bound the captured King Theodoric, as he wished, by his own conditions. Bertharius, however, [unclear].

And so Pippin subjugated Neustria together with Austrasia to himself; under Theodoric alone he ruled the whole kingdom of the Franks for [unclear] years [unclear].

At that time Saint Willibrord, a monk, came from England into Gallican France with twelve companions of the same profession, and lived in great holiness.

In these same times Saint Wandregisel, who [unclear], nephew through his brother Walgisus of Ansegisel, formerly mayor of the palace of the kingdom of the Franks, built two monasteries of monks under the rule of blessed Benedict in Gallican France, namely Fécamp and Fontenelle, which is now on the borders of Normandy; in these, many very holy and very learned men afterward flourished in the course of time.

Theodoric, king of the Franks, died in the fourteenth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 693, in the sixth Roman indiction, leaving two sons, Clovis and Childebert, who succeeded each other in turn.

Clovis, the elder by birth of the aforementioned sons of King Theodoric, succeeded him first and reigned over the Franks for four years, with Pippin being mayor of the palace.
