Whence, in the following year, the fourteenth of King Hilperic, and the year of the Lord’s nativity 578, a cruel war arose between them, in which Theodobert, son of Hilperic, was killed, and many of the Franks were slain on both sides.

After this, the Franks over whom Hilperic ruled, holding their own lord in hatred, resolved by common counsel to set over themselves as king his brother Sigebert, who seemed to be of a gentler disposition. When Fredegund, the wife of Hilperic, learned this, she sent two boys and treacherously caused King Sigebert to be killed in the aforesaid year of the Lord’s nativity.

In the following year Hilperic, king of the Franks, compelled Brunhild, the widow of his brother Sigebert, to go into exile with her sons. Almost all the Franks labored under hatred of this woman; although she made excessive use of much insolence, nevertheless she honored the churches of God. She also built several monasteries, and in various places established from the foundations new buildings of different kinds and of admirable workmanship.

In the seventeenth year of Hilperic, king of the Franks, the dukes of the Lombards began to lay waste the borders of the Gauls. The Franks met them with a great and strong multitude of forces, and fought several battles with them with doubtful fortune, sometimes defeated, and sometimes also victorious. In the end, however, the Franks triumphed.

Hilperic, king of the Franks, in the twenty-third year of his reign after his father, at the contrivance of his wife Fredegund, was killed in the year of the Lord’s nativity 587, in the fifth indiction of the Romans, leaving behind one only son, named Lothar the Younger, an infant of four months, who succeeded in the kingdom. The son of the husband-slaying Fredegund, when he was an infant of four months, was put in his father’s place and reigned forty-four years, beginning under the guardianship and direction of his mother. In the second year of his reign, Antharius, king of the Lombards, after the dukes had been driven out, took as wife Theodelinda, daughter of Garibald, king of Bavaria; to her, as to a woman of singular devotion, blessed Pope Gregory afterward sent the four books of the Dialogues.

In the fifth year of Lothar, king of the Franks, in the tenth indiction of the Romans, Saint Gregory the First, pope, formerly a monk and deacon, was ordained at Rome. He wrote, said, and did many things for the honor and benefit of the holy Church.

In the same year also, when Antharius, king of the Lombards, had died without children, the Lombards, having granted Queen Theodelinda the choice of selecting a husband for herself and a king for Italy, chose Agilulf as king for themselves and at the same time as her husband; thereafter he ruled energetically over the Lombards for twenty-five years.

In the eighth year of Lothar, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth indiction of the Romans, Garibald, king of the Bavarians, died, the father of the aforementioned Theodelinda, queen of the Lombards. After him Hildebert, king of the Franks, appointed as king of Bavaria a certain duke named Thassido, born of the race of the Franks, who overcame the Forabos, who are Slavs but later were called Bohemians, in war.
