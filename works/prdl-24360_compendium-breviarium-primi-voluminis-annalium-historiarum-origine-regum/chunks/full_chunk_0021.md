*Clodomer, son of Basanus, is made king by his father. King Basanus suddenly disappeared in the assembly. King Basanus, most eager for divinity. In the year 248 before the birth of Christ. The eighth. Clodomirus reigned eighteen years. Envoys of the Gauls sent to the Sicambri. The Saxons and Doringi, allies of the Sicambri. The Saxons with the Doringi came to the aid of the Sicambri.*

On one day he designated his son Clodomirus, with the consent of all the nobles, as king over the Sicambri. He foretold many future events to the people in his native song. He commended his sons and the kingdom to the princes. He enjoined justice, and, having entered the chapel of the temple, appeared nowhere again.

I do not know what I should bestow on these little tales: indignation or laughter? Whether he was taken away by trickery or by art remains uncertain. But I think this one thing was the cause: the desire, in a man undoubtedly removed either by his own wit or by the spontaneous will of a demon, of obtaining the estimation of divinity. For, as Amerodagus, philosopher of the Sicambri, writes, King Basanus was most eager for divinity. He cultivated justice. He loved his people. He laid low the enemy. He was most dutiful to good men, always cruel and severe to the wicked.

But he vanished, or, as I should more correctly say, withdrew himself, as Amerodagus is the authority, from the public assembly of the nobles, as we have said, in the thirty-sixth year of his reign, and in the year 248 before the birth of the Lord. The Sicambri, judging that he had been transferred into the fellowship of the gods, worshipped him under the name Basangot.

The elder son of Basanus, succeeding in the kingdom, just as his father had ordained with the consent of the princes, reigned eighteen years. In the first year of his reign, as soon as the Gauls learned that Basanus had vanished, whom they themselves did not doubt was dead, they sent envoys to Clodomirus and demanded back from him all the land between the Meuse and the Rhine, and all around in the circuit, which the Sicambri, at their first entry into the territories on this side of the Rhine, had violently usurped for themselves against justice. If he should scorn to restore it, they threatened that they were prepared to recover everything by the sword, whether he sent back a negative reply through the appointed envoys, or answered nothing at all.

But King Clodomirus, as though he were going to hold counsel with his princes about the urgent matter, prudently and carefully delayed the envoys of the Gauls and kept them with him for a rather long time; and, taking the greatest care that no secret of the design in his mind should come to their knowledge, he ordered his servants to feed them abundantly and arrange for them to have pleasant days. Meanwhile King Clodomirus, sending messengers to the kings of the Saxons and Doringi, very urgently asked that they quickly and secretly send forces in aid to the Sicambri, their friends, against the Gauls, their common adversaries. The Saxons came with the Doringi in support of their friends, as they were asked, bringing forth an army of one hundred thousand fighters. When matters had been arranged very secretly at his nod, King Clodomirus allowed the envoys of the Gauls, given gifts, to depart; and, lest anything on the way about the foregoing matters could come to their knowledge, he assigned escorts for the journey to them as they set out safely, who should accompany them and more cautiously observe them as they went with letters of this sort.
