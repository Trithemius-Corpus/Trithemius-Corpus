Advancing from the city of the people of Metz, this savage man entered the interior of Gaul, and after the desolation of many cities he finally besieged the city of Arles. There he was captured by a certain soldier named Marius, bound in chains, and led around with mockery through the cities which he had devastated; and he ended his excessively impious life by a fitting death.

The Vandals, however, who had remained in Gaul with the Alans, after the death of Carocus set over themselves as king Godgisith, a man notorious for crimes.

Alaric also, king of the Visigoths, devastating Italy with a great army, sent word to Caesar Honorius that he should either engage him in battle or grant the Goths a suitable place in his kingdom where they might remain in peace. But Honorius, not daring to fight with Alaric, after first taking counsel with his men, granted the Gauls, which were otherwise being shaken by the fury of many peoples, to the Goths to inhabit.

The Vandals, however, who had remained in Gaul, hearing of the arrival of the Goths, although they had been born from one Scanzian nation, were nevertheless struck with fear, abandoned Gaul, and with the Alans their allies occupied part of Spain. After remaining there for almost thirty years, they later sailed into Africa and destroyed the city of Hippo.

But Alaric, by the permission of Caesar Honorius, intending to seek Gaul with his men, departed from Italy. Count Stilicho, commander of the Roman army, attacked him on the feast of the Lord’s Resurrection when he suspected no harm, without being ordered, and provoked him unwillingly to battle. In the first assault indeed he was victorious; in the second encounter he was defeated and was compelled to seek safety in flight. King Alaric, who believed that this deed had been done by Honorius’s arrangement, immediately broke the treaty which he had made with him, and, changing his route, returned into Italy and laid waste everything round about belonging to Honorius’s party.

But Caesar Honorius, understanding that Count Stilicho was the cause of such great evils, seized him and beheaded him together with his son Eucharius.

King Alaric then captured the city of Rome, overthrew many buildings, and gravely injured the citizens in their possessions. Yet he restrained himself from bloodshed and the slaughter of men, and spared those who fled to the basilicas of the saints. On the third day he departed from Rome of his own accord; and, intending to sail into Sicily, he suffered shipwreck, and not long afterward, being gravely ill, he suddenly exchanged life for death.

While these things were being done in Italy by the Goths, the Franks, thinking that the time had come for them to subject all Gaul to themselves, gathered forces, assembled a great army, and, under the dukes Faramundus, Marcomirus, Sunno, Priam, and Antenor, entered the Gallic territories. First they engaged the Vandals, who were again raiding Gaul, and killed their king Godgisith, whom some call Godogisilus, together with twenty thousand men.

And unless Respendial, king of the Alans, had come in time from Spain, where he was then reigning, to aid the Vandals, they would all have been killed by the Franks.
