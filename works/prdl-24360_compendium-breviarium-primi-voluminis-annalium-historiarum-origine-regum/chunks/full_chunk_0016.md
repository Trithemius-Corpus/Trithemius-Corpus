The priests, moreover, used the Greek language in sacred rites for a long time, and did not admit any mixing of another language. Until that time they had no temples of the gods, but sacrificed to their divinities under certain leafy trees, which the priests had consecrated after prior judgment.

Therefore in the eighth year of Priamus, king of the Franks, his mother Cambra and the queen, at the command of Theocalus the pontiff, [unclear], of which they called one Neopagus and the other Neomagus, in memory of their newly acquired name. In these two cities, and especially in Neopagus, the residence of the king of the Franks was for a long time.

They also built in the same city of Neomagus a great and very strong temple to Jupiter, in which Theocalus the pontiff, dwelling with the priests, instructed the sons of princes and nobles in morals and knowledge. He pronounced prophecies to the people. He wrote in songs the brave deeds of kings, which the youth, compelled to learn by heart, was accustomed to sing on feast days at [unclear]. At certain times of the year, however, the priests stayed not in cities but in solitude; their food was sparse, and their exercise of mind in learning the courses of the stars, songs, and the histories of the ancients was continual.

King Priamus, after many incursions made against the Gauls, after battles and victories, at last died in the twenty-sixth year of his reign, in the year 356 before the birth of Christ from the Virgin Mary, leaving several sons and daughters from the four wives whom [unclear] had had.

Helenus, the elder-born son of King Priamus, succeeding his father, reigned among the Sicambri for nineteen years, a man severe toward enemies, who also sacrificed infants, as often as he was able to capture them, to Pallas and other demons. Toward his own people, however, he was not cruel but gentle and very humane, burdensome or troublesome to no one and therefore pleasing to all.

In the first year of his reign he had a war with the Transmosan Gauls and the Tungri, in which he laid low more than sixty thousand of the enemy and turned the rest to flight. He also killed with his own hand the firstborn of the king of the Morini, named Guedenus, and subjected to his rule a great part of the land beyond the Meuse, which he entrusted to his dukes to defend. Very often thereafter he fought with the Gauls with varying fortune, now defeated [unclear]. Concerning his battles and deeds, we have written more in the first volume of the Annals.

At last, after many brave deeds performed against the Gauls and other enemies round about, Helenus, king of the Sicambri, died in the nineteenth year of his reign, 338 years before the birth of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.
