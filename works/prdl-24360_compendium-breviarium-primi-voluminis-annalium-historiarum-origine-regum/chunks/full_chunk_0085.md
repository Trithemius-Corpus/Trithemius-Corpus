*King Theodoric enters Cologne after his brother has been killed.*

*The three sons of King Theodobert, who had been slain. One very beautiful daughter of the same man. Brunichild killed her own grandsons.*

*Sigebert, the king’s son, fled to the eastern [lands]. Sigebert becomes duke of the Alemanni and the Helvetians.*

*King Theodoric of Austrasia dies. Lothar becomes monarch of the whole kingdom of the Franks.*

*Brunichild, killer of ten kings, is slain.*

*Lothar entrusted the monarchy of the kingdom to counts. Wernher, mayor of the palace. Harpo is appointed count of Burgundy.*

Thus made victor, in his brother’s blood, the savage King Theodoric entered Cologne in peace, and received the city itself and its citizens, together with his brother’s whole kingdom of the Austrasians, in surrender. He too, at last, died by the poison of his grandmother Brunichild, as we shall say in its place, having been deprived of heirs.

King Theodobert, slain by a miserable fate, left three sons and one, as is read, very beautiful daughter: namely Sigebert, Gunthram, and Lothar. I have found the name of the daughter nowhere. But King Theodoric, after entering Cologne, sent the aforesaid two younger sons of his brother, Gunthram and Lothar, and their sister as captives to the city of Metz. Their cruel grandmother Brunichild, meeting them and inflamed with excessive fury, in hatred of their slain father rushed upon them and killed with her own hand her grandsons, still little children, who [unclear].

But Sigebert, the elder son of Theodobert by birth, lowered by the help of certain citizens secretly over the wall and across the Rhine, fled in the dress of a scholar. Coming into eastern Francia, he lay hidden for some years, unknown, among his kinsmen Godfrid and his son Genebald III, dukes of the Franks, who ruled around the Main and Wurzburg. At length, after the death of King Theodoric, through the intervention of the princes, he received by the gift of King Lothar a duchy in Upper Alemannia, which is now called Helvetia; but he was not permitted either to bear the royal name or to wear the diadem according to the custom of his fathers. And he remained under the authority of the kingdom of the Franks.

He begot a son with his father’s name, Theobert.

In the thirty-first year of King Lothar, and in the six hundred eighteenth year of the Lord’s nativity, in the sixth indiction of the Romans, King Theodoric of Austrasia died at Metz by the poison of his grandmother Brunichild, in the nineteenth year of his reign. Lothar, the already mentioned son of Hilperic, received his kingdom, and so, according to the prophecy that Saint Columbanus had once foretold to him, he attained the monarchy of the whole kingdom of the Franks, and thereafter reigned alone for thirteen years.

And on the other side, when the sons of King Theodoric, at Brunichild’s instigation, raised an army, they were defeated; and Brunichild herself, the inciter of all evils, was captured and presented to Lothar together with Eudolina, daughter of King Theodoric. Condemned by the decree of the nobles, she received the sentence of a dreadful death. For, after being mutilated in one foot and the opposite arm, and tied to the tail of an untamed mare, she was torn apart limb by limb by the violent dragging. They said that she had destroyed ten kings.

Therefore Lothar, raised to monarchy over the whole kingdom of the Franks, which had previously been divided into four, since he could not profitably govern so broad a dominion by himself alone, entrusted the provinces to counts descended from the ancient stock of kings. He appointed as count, or prefect, of the royal palace Guarnerius, a noble and very eminent man.

But Burgundy he committed to a certain Harpo to govern; he too was a count born from the most ancient line of the kings of the Franks.
