In the following year King Agilulf entered, intending to lay waste the borders of the Romans; but through the action of Theodolinda, queen of the Lombards, who was the wife of that same Agilulf, as we have said, not long afterward he made a treaty of friendship with them. Nevertheless, entering the monastery of our holy father Benedict the abbot on Monte Cassino, they plundered and destroyed it, the monks at that same time fleeing to Rome.

In the tenth year of Lothar, king of the Franks, in the fifteenth indiction, Gunthram, king of the Franks, second-born son of Lothar the Great and grandson of King Clovis, died at Orléans in the thirty-third year of his reign, not without a reputation for sanctity. His kingdom passed to his cousin Hildebert by [unclear] of [unclear]. In the following year Richard, king of the Visigoths, entered into a treaty with the kings of the Franks, and took as his wife the sister of the aforesaid King Hildebert. In the same year, on the twenty-first day of the month of November, Saint Columbanus died, abbot, priest, and teacher of the Scots and Picts; he left to his successors many memorials of sanctity, except that, in rustic simplicity, he neither learned nor taught that Easter ought to be celebrated on the Lord’s day, and never on another day.

In the thirteenth year of Lothar, king of the Franks, Hildebert, king of the Franks, died at Orléans. He was the son of King Sigebert, who was the son of Lothar, who was the son of Clovis, the first Christian king. He left two sons, Theobert and Theodoric, who divided their father’s kingdom between themselves; their grandmother Brunichild sowed many kinds of dissensions among them.

Therefore in the year of the Lord’s nativity 605, in the eighth Roman indiction, which was the eighteenth year of the reign of Lothar, Theodobert and Theoderic, the aforesaid brothers, stirred to fury by their grandmother Brunichild, who, as a supporter of Theoderic, labored with incredible hatred against Theodobert, waged a most savage war between themselves, in which more than thirty thousand fighting men fell. Also an angel of the Lord, holding and brandishing a drawn sword in his hand, was seen in the air above the people by many who were present.

At last Theodobert, having been defeated, since he could do nothing else, sought safety in flight. His brother Theoderic pursued him as far as Cologne Agrippina, and along the whole road ceaselessly cut down his soldiers as they fled likewise. But Theodobert entered Cologne and immediately ordered the city gates to be closed.

Theoderic, his brother, pursuing him, as has been said, encircled Cologne with a siege. The citizens, rushing to the walls, asked Theoderic for the terms of peace. He answered them: “You will never have peace from me unless first you cut off my brother’s head and throw it out to me from the wall. But if you refuse to do this, I shall not depart from here until, when the city has been violently taken, I destroy you all with your lord, your little children, and your wives.”

When they heard this, the citizens were exceedingly terrified; and after holding counsel among themselves, at last, lest it happen that all should perish, they cut off the head of King Theodobert, their lord, and threw it out over the wall to the impious Theoderic, as he had ordered, thus purchasing peace disgracefully in the blood of their own king.
