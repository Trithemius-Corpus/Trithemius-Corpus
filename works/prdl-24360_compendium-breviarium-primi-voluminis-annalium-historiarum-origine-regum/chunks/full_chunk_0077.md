With the brothers and their sons and grandsons dead, or cruelly killed, Lothar alone obtained the monarchy of the whole kingdom of the Franks, and thereafter, after all his brothers, reigned still for five years.

In the second year of King Lothar, Sigismund, king of Burgundy, who had built the monastery of the Agaunensian martyrs, killed his son Sigeric, having followed the wicked counsel of that boy’s stepmother. Led to great penitence, he devoted himself wholly to the holy martyrs of Agaunum; and that he was worthy of pardon is attested by frequent miracles after his death, for in his honor health is granted to those suffering from fever.

In the fourth year of King Lothar, Theodoric, king of Austrasia, his brother, having been asked by Ermenfrid, king of the Thuringians, under the promise of half his kingdom, treacherously killed the brothers Bertharius and Balderic on the road, while they were returning toward Thuringia, suspecting no evil, from the city of Metz, to which they had come for the sake of greeting him. But when the crime had been carried out, and Theodoric sought from Ermenfrid the half of the kingdom of Thuringia promised to him, the latter neither gave what he had promised nor sent a fitting reply to the petitioner, deceived by the counsel of his wife, who was the sister of that Theodoric and of Lothar. This matter afterward, as we have said in the first volume of the Annals, brought on the final destruction of all Thuringia and extinguished there the royal name and title. In the same year the holy virgin Brigid, dying in Scotland, ascended to Christ.

In the seventh year of Lothar, king of the Franks, Boethius, the Roman consul, worn down by long imprisonment, was killed by Theodoric, king of the Goths, who, after Odoacer had been slain, was occupying Italy; he wrote many useful works for the faith of Christ. Father Benedict, our holy lawgiver, also flourished in these times in Italy, the first founding abbot of the monastery of Cassino, who was the prince of all western monks. To them he handed down a rule of life outstanding in every discretion, and a lucid teaching of words, made honorable by his deeds, is called by the name of Saint Benedict himself. The Order of Cistercian monks also professes the same rule. The Order of Saint William. The Order of Mount Olivet. The Order of the Slavs. The Order of Vallombrosa. The Order of Saint Paul the First Hermit. The Order of the Humiliati, together with certain others which, for the sake of brevity, we have passed over here. The life of this saint the divine Pope Gregory set forth.

In the tenth year of Lothar, king of the Franks, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 524, the seventh indiction of the Romans, when the king of the people of Metz sought satisfaction from Ermenfrid, king of the Thuringians, for the murder of his brother, as we have said, and did not obtain it, he was roused to anger. Having called to his aid his son Theudebert and his brother Lothar, kings, he moved an expedition into Thuringia, captured King Ermenfrid with his wife and children, and subjected the whole kingdom to himself.
