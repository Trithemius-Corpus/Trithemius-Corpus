For all the territories of the Gauls which the father of the Franks had added to the kingdom, throughout all the days of his reign he not only kept under his own rule, but also, with the Romans wherever they were found either killed or put to flight, he extended the bounds of the possession he had acquired far and wide in a remarkable manner.

In the eighth year of his reign earthquakes were almost continual, and many fearful signs were shown in the sky. For on a certain day, as evening was coming on, the sky appeared in the north red as fire, with certain brighter lines interspersed through the fiery redness, deformed into the likeness of spears. The moon suffered an eclipse; a comet of dreadful magnitude appeared; and many other prodigies were seen, which the evils that followed declare not to have been at all idle.

For in the following year, which was the ninth of King Meroveus and, from the Lord's Nativity, 453, in the sixth indiction of the Romans, Attila king of the Huns, Vualimirus king of the Ostrogoths, Ardaricus king of the Gepids, and other northern peoples, conspiring together, set out from the borders of Pannonia and invaded both the western empire and the kingdom of the Franks in the Gallic regions with five hundred thousand fighting men; they wrought immense slaughter and spared no one.

And first, indeed, throughout all Gaul their fury, God permitting it, raged to such an extent that no strength or firmness of fortification could defend a city, town, or castle from their assault. Against their insolence Meroveus king of the Franks, and Theodoric also, king of the Visigoths, and Aetius, patrician of the Romans, advanced to war with the military forces of various other peoples, by common preparation; with them they fought in the Catalaunian fields from morning until night. And the Franks with their allied companions defeated the enemy, although Attila was not wholly destroyed.

In that battle one hundred eighty thousand fighting men were slain, among whom Theodoric, king of the Visigoths, a most warlike man, also fell. When the Franks, together with the others who had assembled against the Huns, withdrew, King Attila, taking his army, returned into [unclear], so that, after repairing his army, he might be able to continue the fury he had begun and reduce Gaul together with Italy to desolation.

For in the following year King Attila entered Italy and either plundered or utterly overthrew almost all its cities. Pope Leo I came to him dressed in pontifical vestments, and obtained not only safety for the city and people, but also that he should depart, leaving Italy. When he was asked by his men why he had become mild, he replied that he had not revered him, but rather a certain old man, venerable in appearance, standing beside him, who with a drawn sword threatened death to the king if he should either grieve the pontiff or deny anything that he asked. Not long after, King Attila, glutted at his wedding, died from a stroke of apoplexy.
