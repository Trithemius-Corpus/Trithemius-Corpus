The monastery of Gengenbach, in the diocese of Strasbourg, which is one mile distant from Schuttern, the same writer of the Life of Saint Pirmin attests was founded by him.

The same saint founded the monastery which in Latin is called Mauri Monasterium, but in the vernacular Morsmunster, in the diocese of Strasbourg.

Likewise the same saint built the monastery of Neuweiler in the same diocese of Strasbourg; its monks, together with their abbot, in our times withdrew from the order.

There are two monasteries of our order with one name in Bavaria, of which one is called Upper Altha, and the other likewise Altha, but Lower. In the deeds of the dukes of Bavaria we read that Duke Odilo, father of Duke Tassilo, founded Lower Altha. But in the deeds of Saint Pirmin it is said that he built the monastery of Altha in Bavaria, yet it is not there expressed whether Upper or Lower; therefore it is clear that it was Upper.

The blessed Pirmin also built another monastery within the borders of Alsace, if I remember rightly in the diocese of Basel, of the same order of Benedict, which is named Murbach, as is contained in his Life.

He also founded another monastery on an island of the river Rhine, not far from Constance, which is now called Reichenau, in which in the course of time many cloistered men lived, distinguished for learning and holiness. But in these our days it is tossed in great poverty, which once, through extraordinary riches and abundance of goods, was called Rich Reichenau.

He also built from the foundations a new monastery in the town of Amorbach, in the diocese of Würzburg, of our aforesaid order, in which he appointed Saint Amor, his disciple, abbot. And many others which we do not know.

In the fifth year of Theodoric, king of the Franks, died Bede the Venerable, priest and monk in England, a learned and holy man, who wrote many volumes for the usefulness of readers and to the honor of the supreme, undivided deity.

Theodoric, king of the Franks, died in the fifteenth year of his reign, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 741, in the ninth indiction of the Romans. Succeeding him, Childeric, with Charles, surnamed Martel, that is, the Hammer, as mayor of the palace, reigned in name only for nine years. For all power and the resources of the kingdom were in the hands of the mayor [unclear] than that, content with the royal name, with flowing hair and lowered beard, he should sit on a throne and display the appearance of one ruling, hear envoys coming from every quarter, and, as they departed, give them the answers in which he had been instructed.
