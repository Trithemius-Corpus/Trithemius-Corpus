In the year of the Lord’s nativity 746, which was the fifth year of King Childeric and the fourteenth Roman indiction, Adelung, a priest at Würzburg, blind and warned in a dream to seek the bodies of Saint Kilian the bishop and of his companions, martyrs, found the sacred bones and recovered his sight. When Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, learned of this miracle, with the help of Pippin, prince of the palace of the Franks, he obtained from Pope Zacharias that he should establish at Würzburg a new see of the bishop and of his companions, martyrs.

In the seventh year, therefore, of Childeric king of the Franks, and also in the year of the Lord’s nativity 748, in the first indiction, Saint Burghard, English by nation, a monk by profession, was ordained by Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, as first bishop of the church of Würzburg. He presided for forty years. And in the same year little crosses, as if marked with oil, appeared on people’s linen garments.

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 749, in the second indiction, Childeric, king of the Franks, with Zacharias, pope of Rome, also giving counsel, was cast down from the kingdom and tonsured as a monk, in the ninth year of his reign. Therefore, by the common will of all, Pippin, mayor of the palace, was elected king; from him the second volume of our annals takes its beginning. He himself descended from the ancient stock of the kings of the Franks, as we have said in the same volume. Here, then, in the name of Jesus Christ, we end the epitome, or abridgment, of the first volume of our annals, in which, over the course of 1,190 years, sixty-one kings of the Franks are contained, from the first entrance of the people into Germany at the mouths of the Rhine.

The end.

*Here ends the compendium or abridgment of the volume of chronicles, or annals, of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James at Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, concerning the origin and kings of the Franks. On the twentieth day of the month of November, in the year of the Lord 1514, in the fifty-second year of his age.*

*It comprises in sum 1,189 years.*

*The present work of chronicles was printed and completed in the year of the Lord 1515, on the vigil of Margaret the virgin, in the noble and famous city of Mainz, the first inventor of this art of printing, by Johannes Schöffer, grandson of the late honorable man Johannes Fust, citizen of Mainz, the principal originator of the aforesaid art. He at length began to devise and contemplate the art of printing by his own genius in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1450, in the thirteenth indiction, when the most illustrious Frederick III, Roman emperor, was reigning, and when the most reverend father in Christ and lord Theoderic, pincerna of Erbach, prince-elector, was presiding over the holy see of Mainz. But in the year 1452 he perfected it and, with divine grace favoring, brought it into the work of printing, yet with the labor and many necessary inventions of Peter Schöffer of Gernsheim, his assistant and adopted son. To him he also gave his daughter Christina Fust in marriage, as a worthy recompense for his labors and many aids. The two men already named, Johannes Fust and Peter Schöffer, kept this art secret, all their assistants and household members being bound by oath not to reveal it in any way. But when at last, in the year of the Lord 1462, it was spread abroad by those same household members into various lands and provinces, it took no small increase.*

*With the grace and privilege of the imperial majesty, by order and at the expense of the honorable Johannes Haselperg of Aia Maior, of the diocese of Constance.*
