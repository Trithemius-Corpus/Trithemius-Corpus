The aforesaid territories fell to it, namely the whole kingdom of Germany, with the Saxons, Thuringians, Marcomanni, Danes, Frisians, Hessians, Westphalians, Eastern Franks, Bavarians, Swabians, Bohemians, Sorbs, Austrians, Germans, and all the nations, peoples, and races that lie among those named above.

¶ The whole kingdom of Austrasia also, with these cities: of the people of Metz, which is now called all Lotharingia; Trier, with the Mosellani, Eufalii, and [Cynonoti?]; Cologne, Aachen, Liège, and Maastricht, both lower and upper, with Holland, Zeeland, Brabant, Hainaut, the whole region from the river Rhine westward as far as the sea, with the Tongrians, Flemings, Morini, and all the land that either adjoins or lies among the regions and cities mentioned. Finally, the kingdom of Arles, with its eight counties in Gaul, is counted by ancient right as subject under the dominion of the king of the Germans; by reason of this each archbishop of Trier in office for the time being is recognized as having afterwards been appointed archchancellor of the German kingdom throughout Gaul, the aforesaid kingdom of Arles. The kingdom of Burgundy also, with all its counties, was assigned in the same division to the kingdom of the Germans. All Alemannia too, with its cities and peoples, in which are the cities of Chur, Sion, and Valais, fell by lot to the kingdom of the Germans, together with the Helvetians, Alsatians, [Caurici?], Savoyards, the people of Constance, Basel, Strasbourg, Speyer, Worms, Mainz, and all the peoples from the source of the river Rhine as far as the sea, into which it finally flows by three mouths.

¶ But the Roman Empire, together with Italy, Lombardy, and all territories subject to it by ancient right, except Greece, the eastern empire, was then, and remains today, annexed and subject to the kingdom of the Germans. Nevertheless, in the course of time many disputes, invasions, and changes arose between the kings of the Germans and of the Gauls over certain boundaries of the kingdom; these have been written about more fully in the second volume.

¶ The kingdom of the Franks of Gaul also, separated from ours already mentioned, should in like manner, as the constitution states, have its own free king, who is no less free than the king of Germany, subject to absolutely no one. For it is manifest that the Franks were always utterly free from their origin. They never served the Romans, nor any other nation, nor were they subject to them. Rather, they themselves destroyed the empire of the Romans and made it subject to themselves. And therefore both kings, in the division of the kingdom, were to preserve everywhere unharmed for one another the ancient freedom of the Franks.

But the king of Gallic Francia, in the division of the monarchy, received as his portion the kingdom of Neustria, or Paris, the kingdom of Orléans, the kingdom of Aquitaine, the kingdom of Lausanne, the Armorican kingdom, or of New Brittany, with all the provinces, regions, and peoples of inner Gaul as far as Spain, from the river Rhône to the western sea.

After that division of the kingdom of the Franks I compressed the history; and, now omitting thereafter the Gallic Franks, in the second and third volumes of the annals I continue the narrative concerning the German Franks alone. But since, in so long a course of years, first from the entrance of the people into Germany, the history has become rather lengthy, I thought it would be worthwhile if from those three volumes of annals themselves I extracted the same number of shorter books, which would contain in brief the names, successions, and deeds more worthy of remembrance of the kings of the Franks of Germany. For I know that your most reverend lordship delights in ancient histories, and by reason of administering a principality is continually occupied in many affairs. Since, therefore, no prince lives today among mortals to whom, after God, I am by right more indebted, with all my powers, for service and pleasing obedience, nor am I readier with a most submissive will, than to your most excellent and most reverend lordship, I judge no one more worthy in whose name I either ought or wish to dedicate my studies on the origin of the Franks.

*The most reverend lord bishop is a lover of histories.*
