*King Riuacharius. [unclear] Carocus, king of the Morini. Heribertus, king of the Treveri. Godegisilus, king of Metz. Arbogast, king of Mainz. All under one king of the Franks. The Lombards first occupied the Rugi. Childeric, king of the Franks, dies. The forty-fourth. Clovis reigned thirty years, the first Christian. Clovis, king of the Christians, burned churches. Queen Clothild, wife of King Clovis, a Christian. The Franks made war on the Thuringians and overcome them.*

Carocus among the Treveri and Mosellans, Heribertus in the principate of the city of Metz, Godegisilus among the people of Mainz, Arbogast, and others in other cities, they appointed as vicars of the kingdom, according to the custom of the ancient Franks; yet King Clovis later had almost all of them killed because of his ambition for monarchy.

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 479, in the second indiction of the Romans, in the times of the aforesaid Childeric, king of the Franks, after Odoacer’s departure from the Rugi, the Lombards invaded Rugium. At that time a fifth king, named Gudeoch, ruled over them, and they dwelt in that kingdom for some time.

But Childeric, king of the Franks, after he had performed many deeds bravely, died in the completed twenty-third year of his reign, but the twenty-sixth interrupted year, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 484, in the seventh indiction of the Romans. Clovis, son of King Childeric by Basina, when he was twenty-one years of age, succeeded his father and reigned thirty years: fifteen of these he spent as a pagan, like all the kings of the Franks before him, in vanity, and the remaining fifteen, reborn in the faith of Christ, he passed faithfully. He was a prince outstanding in form and distinguished in courage of mind; and soon at the beginning of his reign he drove out the son of the former Roman duke Aegidius, named Syagrius, who held rule over Soissons, and added the city to the kingdom of the Franks.

Proceeding from there, he took the city of Reims, over whose church at that time presided Saint Remigius, a most learned bishop; he plundered all the churches both there and elsewhere, burned many, and divided the booty among the soldiers according to law. Whatever of Roman jurisdiction existed in all Gaul, Clovis in a short time subjected wholly to the dominion of the Franks, just as it remains up to the present day. He took as wife Clothild, daughter of Hilperic, king of the Burgundians, whom Gundebald, the king, his brother, had slain; and his wife, that is, Clothild’s mother, was a faithful Christian woman, who diligently admonished the king to believe in the Lord Jesus and to renounce the vain worship of idols. Although he despised this at first, nevertheless, after fifteen years had passed in his reign, and after obtaining victory over the Alemanni, as we shall say in its place, he afterward believed in Christ.

In the ninth year of his reign, King Clovis made war on Thuringia, because they both disdained to pay the tribute formerly imposed on them, and openly accused his mother Basina not only of adultery but also of theft. And so, coming into the region of the Thuringians, he devastated everything with sword and fire, defeated the king, and compelled them to the payment of tributes.
