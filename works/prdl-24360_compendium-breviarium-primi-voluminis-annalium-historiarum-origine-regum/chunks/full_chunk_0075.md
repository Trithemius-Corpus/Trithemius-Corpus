*Clodoneus, king of the Franks, dies.*

Clodoneus, king of the Franks, after his realm had been extended throughout all Gaul and a great part of Germany, and after many things had been bravely accomplished in war and peace, at last died in the thirtieth year of his reign, the fifty-first of his age, and the year of the Lord’s nativity 514, in the seventh Roman indiction. He was buried at Paris in the church of Saint Peter.

He left four sons: Lotharius, Theodoricus, Clodomirus, and Hildebertus, who divided their father’s kingdom in equal portions. But because several kings were subsequently born from these men, and because at one time the kingdom of the Franks was subject to a single monarch, while at another time it was divided into several parts and ruled by many, we have judged it worth the effort to continue in order, marked chapter by chapter in red, the names of those kings who carried the sequence of the royal stock in a direct line down to Hildericus, the last king. For among the sons of Clodoneus, Lotharius was not the firstborn; rather Theodoricus was the elder by birth. Clodomirus was born second; Lotharius himself, by whom the genealogy of the kings is continued, was born third.

After King Clodoneus died, Hunibald also completed his history of the origin of the Franks, divided into eighteen books, comprising a period of no fewer than nine hundred and fifty years, in which there were forty-seven kings from Marcomirus, son of Antenor, who first led the people from the borders of Scythia to the mouths of the river Rhine in Germany, down to the death of King Clodoneus. What follows in this second volume of our annals we have taken from authors whose names are these.

Einhardus, abbot of Saint-Amand; Flodoardus, priest of the church of Reims; Richerus, monk of Reims; Gregorius, archbishop of Tours; Lampertus, abbot in Hasungen; Regino, abbot of Prum; Eghardus, first abbot in Vraugia; Lampertus, monk of Hersfeld; Freculfus, formerly a monk, bishop of Lisieux; Strabus, monk of Fulda; Vuindekindus, monk in Corvey of Saxony; Rupertus, monk of Saint Alban near Mainz; Ioannes, monk of the same monastery; Adelbertus, monk of the monastery of Saint Vincent at Metz; Meginfridus, monk of Fulda; Golftherus, monk of Saint Matthias, in the deeds of the Treveri; Marianus Scotus, monk of Fulda; Sigebertus, monk of Gembloux; Richardus, monk of Cluny; Rupertus Ganguinus also, formerly minister general of the Order of the Holy Trinity; Ado, bishop of Vienne; Galfridus Brito; Saxo Dacus; Ptolemaeus of Lucca, a Saxon by country, in the Rudimentum novitiorum; Vuernherus, Carthusian monk of Cologne; Gotfridus of Viterbo; Rupertus, Premonstratensian canon; Hartmannus Schedel from Nuremberg; Iacobus of Bergamo, hermit of Saint Augustine; Marcus Antonius Sabellicus.
