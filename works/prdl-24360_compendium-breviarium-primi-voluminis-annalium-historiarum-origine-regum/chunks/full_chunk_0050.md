Therefore there were in eastern Francia, succeeding one another for four hundred and fourteen years, twenty-one dukes in number, who constitute the sacred seven, tripled in order, and not foreign to mystery. But in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 752, in the fifth Roman indiction, Pippin, raised from mayor of the palace to king of the Franks, as was said above, handed over the oft-mentioned duchy of eastern Francia to Saint Burchard, first bishop of the church of Würzburg, and to his successors, as their perpetual property.

From that time, apart from the bishops of the church of Würzburg, down to the present day, no one has been able by right to claim for himself the duchy of the aforesaid Francia, although by the rashness, not the right, of certain kings and emperors, rebels against and excommunicates of the Roman Church, the contrary was sometimes attempted, as if in vengeance for themselves. Among these Conrad, the third of that name and afterward emperor, since he was a Swabian by nation, violently usurped for himself, by royal permission, the duchy of eastern Francia; nevertheless, after he had become Caesar, he restored it to the Church with manifold satisfaction.

Let these things, briefly touched upon by a necessary historical digression concerning the origin, genealogy, and succession of the dukes of the people of eastern Francia, suffice. Let us now return to the order of the kings of the Franks, from which we departed. *The Thirty-Seventh.*

Richimer, the eldest son of King Clodomir, succeeding his father, reigned thirteen years. Throughout all the days of his reign he was always in conflict with the Romans and the Gauls; and with the help of Genebald, his paternal uncle, namely duke of eastern Francia, residing at the city of [peapolitanam], and of the other princes of the Franks, and also of the Thuringians, Saxons, and fierce German friends, he often fought victoriously against them and afflicted them with great slaughter.

In the fourth year of his reign, after gathering the support of his friends, he entered Gaul with a very great multitude of fighters, and laid waste everything with fire and sword. Against him Tiberianus, prefect of the Romans, also moved a great army of his own men to war. When they joined battle, they fought for a long time with uncertain fortune, and many on both sides fell slain. Finally, however, the Gauls were defeated and compelled to seek safety by flight.
