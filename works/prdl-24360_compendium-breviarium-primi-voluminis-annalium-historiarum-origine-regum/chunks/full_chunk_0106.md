In the following year Charles, mayor of the palace of the Franks, launched an expedition into Alemannia and, joining battle with the rebellious Alemanni, emerged victorious. He overcame Lanfrid, their duke, and forced their land into submission.

Lothar, king of the Franks, reigning in name alone, died in the seventh year of his reign, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 726, in the seventh indiction. Theodoric, Lothar’s nephew by his brother, namely the son of the aforesaid King Clovis, whom others called Dagobert, after the death of his uncle and at the same time after the death of Chilperic Daniel the priest, who while Lothar was alive and after his death had acted as king for eight years, was raised by Charles Martel, mayor of the palace, to be king of the Franks, and reigned fifteen years.

In these times there flourished in Gallican Francia Saint Pyrminius, formerly a monk of the monastery of Abbot [Diuinaurus?], bishop of Meaux in the province of Sens, a man admirable for learning and holiness. For the love of Christ, to preach the true faith to the peoples, he came into the regions of Germany, where by word and example he converted many to the worship of truth, and founded several monasteries.

Among these the following are counted as the principal ones and known to us. The monastery of Saint Maurice the Martyr in Dole of Trier, which the great king of the Franks Dagobert, as was said above, once built, he personally visited rather often; for some time he lived there among the brothers, established the conduct of the brothers according to the intention of the Rule of Saint Benedict, and adorned the place.

Likewise the monastery of Schuttern, which is situated three miles above Strasbourg, a city of Alsace, the same Saint Pyrminius founded, just as is found clearly written in the original volume of his Life, which we have in our possession. Nor is it an objection that the brothers of that same monastery, showing no documents of truth, say that Offa, king of England, when he was seeking Rome for the sake of devotion in the times of Saint Gregory the First, pope, was moved by the pleasantness of the place to establish a monastery there; for King Offa and his brother, as we said above under King Hildebert, sought Rome and became monks in the year of the Lord 707, in the fifth Roman indiction. But Saint Pope Gregory, under whom they say that Offa, king of England, came to Rome, had died earlier, in the year of the Lord 605, in the eighth Roman indiction. Sigebert bears witness for our assertion in the chronicles.
