But in that assembly of the Franks there were many men prudent in their generation; from their number four of the more distinguished were chosen, to whom the king, together with the nobles, entrusted full power to frame laws. Their names were said to be these: Salagast, Vuesogast, Vuyndegast, Basogast. The first of these built a village beside the banks of the river Main, which he named Salagastat after himself; Vuyndegast, however, in eastern Francia founded a village of his own name, Vuyndesheim.

Therefore these four, coming together in one place for three assemblies, and carefully considering the origins of the cases, issued a new volume of legal constitutions, striking out a judgment on each matter. From the more principal author, Salagast, who was the chief priest of Jove, it received the name of the Salic law; to it King Clodoveus later added a chapter of interpretation.

In the same year the Vandals, Alans, and Suevi, having divided the region of the Spains among themselves into three parts for habitation, being separated into three kingdoms, clashed with one another in internal war. The Vandals, Alans, Suevi, Swedes, Goths also, Lombards, and Normans are alike in origin as far as concerns the first homeland of their nation, having arisen from the borders of Germany, from the Scandinavian islands, which are now called Gothia under four or five divisions, from the beginning of their migration.

In the third year of Pharamund, king of the Franks, the blessed Jerome, an illustrious priest for fifty-six years, having published his books, worn out by a severe asthmatic ailment and so cast down in his chest that he could not rise from bed unless he grasped with his hands a rope which hung from a beam for this purpose, died at Bethlehem, in the ninety-first year of his age, on the last day of September.

In the sixth year of Pharamund, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 425, in the eighth indiction of the Romans, Saint Theonestus, bishop of Philippi in Greece, driven from his church by the violence of heretics, came into Italy and then into the borders of Germany with Alban the priest, Ursus the deacon, and Abraham the subdeacon. Of these Alban suffered at Mainz for the faith of Christ.

Pharamund, king of the Franks, dies in the seventh year of his reign, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 421, in the ninth indiction of the Romans.
