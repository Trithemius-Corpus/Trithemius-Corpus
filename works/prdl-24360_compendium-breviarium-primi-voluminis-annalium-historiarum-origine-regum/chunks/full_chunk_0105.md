In the aforesaid instrument of letters the ninth Roman indiction is set down, although it was the fourth and by no means the ninth, as a skilled ecclesiastical computist will plainly find.

The third consideration gives me a strong suspicion of fabrication, because the aforesaid letters of foundation, which the brothers at St. Peter’s in Erfurt display as originals, are protected by no one’s seal, although a hole appears in the parchment, as if the seal had fallen off through excessive age.

The fourth consideration is by no means inferior to the foregoing, because it is found plainly written in the life and deeds of blessed Wilhelm, abbot of Hirsau, that he constructed seven new monasteries of our order, among which the often-mentioned monastery of St. Peter in Erfurt is counted fourth in order. I deliberately pass over several other considerations, lest prolixity create tedium for the reader.

Therefore, just as I wrote and long ago showed by many arguments in that very first volume of my Hirsau chronicles, it was necessary for me to think thus and to prefer to fabrication the truth, which I believe has simply perished.

*In the year of the Lord 706 the Roman indiction was the fourth, not the ninth.*

*The letters of foundation which they have were never sealed.*

*It is clear that the man of Hirsau founded the place.*

*The monastery of St. Peter was founded by Hirsau.*

*Which Trithemius proved by many arguments.*

*Fifty-ninth.*

The brother of the aforesaid King Clovis, or Dagobert, after his death, in the year of the Lord, as we said, 719, in the second indiction, at the advancement of Charles Martel, mayor of the palace of the Franks, gathered forces, having them as assistance, and advanced in war against King Lothar and his mayor of the palace, Charles. But, defeated by Charles’s troops, they fled with great difficulty. Eudo, returning in confusion into Aquitaine, took with him Chilperic, who was Daniel.

*Lothar reigned seven years.*

*Daniel the priest-king with his Regenfrid, his mayor of the palace.*

*Daniel, who was Chilperic, is defeated by Charles.*

*Daniel, from priest to king, is put to flight by Charles.*

In the third year of King Lothar, Charles Martel, mayor of the palace, pursuing Regenfrid, besieged Angers; and, after the city had been captured, he received him in surrender. With peace established between them, he granted him the city to live in, and thereafter he himself powerfully held everywhere the principate of the whole kingdom of the Franks.

*Charles moved war against Regenfrid.*

*Regenfrid is besieged by Charles in the city of Orléans.*

In the following year Charles, mayor of the palace, surnamed Martel, launched an expedition into Saxony. After an engagement had taken place, with many killed on both sides, he compelled them to submission, and also forced the rebels to pay tribute to the Franks.

*Charles launched an expedition into Saxony.*

In the fifth year of the aforesaid Lothar, king of the Franks, which was the 724th year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the seventh Roman indiction, the Bavarians scorned to pay tribute to the Franks—

*Charles, mayor of the palace, moved war against the Bavarians.*
