After she had at last most holily completed the course of the present race, she departed to the Lord, and was buried in the same monastery of Saint Symphorian the martyr, whom she had served most devoutly. This Modoaldus, moreover, whom others write as Rodoaldus, was the brother of Itha, wife of Pepin the Elder, and the maternal uncle of the blessed virgin Gertrude. This Itha founded in Brabant the monastery of nuns at Nivelles, in which, after Pepin’s death, she thereafter most holily served almighty God with her daughter.

But the most holy Modoaldus, bishop of Trier, not long after the death of his sister, also himself resting in the Lord, was buried beside her in the church of Saint Symphorian; Numerianus succeeded him in the archbishopric. In their time Dagobert, in the six hundred and twenty-seventh year of the Lord’s nativity, in the fifteenth Roman indiction, began to found the monastery of Doleiense of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, by the counsel and aid of the blessed archbishop Modoaldus; Sigebert, king of Austrasia, his son, completed it. And Saint Pirminius, bishop and monk, afterwards adorned it more beautifully.

In these times men of great holiness shone in the diocese of Trier, one of whose number was Paul, who on Mount Gebenna, opposite the city across the bridge of the river Moselle, led an eremitic life; then, coming to the aforementioned monastery [unclear], he became a monk; finally, ordained bishop of Verdun, he faithfully preserved in all things the rule of his former holiness. The mountain formerly called Gebenna, taking its name from him, is called Mount Paul, in the vernacular Paulsberg, down to the present day.

Wendelin also, first a monk in Tholey, afterwards a hermit in that place where now there stands a town called from his name Saint Wendelin, distant only one mile from that monastery, a man of most holy way of life, who, living and dead, converted many to Christ by word and example; whom I have recorded [unclear], lest two be thought one.

There was another Wendelin, at almost the same time, a monk and priest, disciple of Saint Columbanus, companion of Saint Walaric the monk in pilgrimage, a holy man and excellent preacher, who converted many to Christ by word and example; whom I have recorded [unclear], lest two be thought one.

Ingobert also, monk and priest; Carilephus, monk and priest; Wolffrich; Bantus; his brother Beatus, monk and priest; Wolfiliacus, a layman, men of the greatest holiness, with many others, led a solitary life in the wilderness in the diocese of Trier in these times.

Saint Disibod also, monk and priest, in the diocese of Mainz, in the county of Spanheim, two miles from Kreuznach and one mile from my former monastery of Spanheim, leading an eremitic life, built in that place near Sobernheim, where the Nahe and the Glan meet, a monastery of monks, which today bears his name. Today only a small trace of his monastery is seen.
