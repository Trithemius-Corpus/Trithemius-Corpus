He also entrusted Austrasia to Rado, a most noble count; in it were the most renowned cities: Trier, Metz, Liege, Cologne, the Mosellan [unclear], Luxembourgers, Austrasians, [Euphalians?], Namurians, and many others.

To Genebaldus, the third of that name, duke of the Franks in the east at Marcopolis, which is now called Wurzburg from the adjacent castle of Saint Mary on the mountain, he assigned Mainz, Worms, Speyer, and the peoples of those same regions; he founded Frankfurt beside the Main.

But to Pippin, who was the son of Duke Ansegisus, brother of Saint Arnulf, bishop of Metz, and also count of Namur and duke of Brittany, from whom the counts palatine, the counts of the Rhine, and the dukes of Bavaria who live today, eighteen in number, all descended, as I know, apart from the females, on the mother's side, namely from Gertrude, daughter of Henry, count palatine dwelling at Heidelberg, who, having been married to Otto, duke of Bavaria, in the year of the Lord's nativity 1225, joined the principality of Bavaria to the Palatinate; King Lotharius assigned all Lower or northern Francia, that is, the people of Tournai, Arras, the Morini, Terouanne, Cambrai, and whatever lies between them, besides the county of Namur and the duchy of Brabant, which belonged by right to the aforesaid Ansegisus.

To Duke Sigebertus, son of the late Theodobert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, whose head, as we have said, the people of Cologne cut off, King Lotharius, after he was made duke through the intervention of the nobles, entrusted the cities of Alemannia: Chur, Lausanne, Constance, Basel, and the peoples settled around them, the Alemanni, the [Caurici?], and the Helvetii dwelling at the source of the Rhine.

In the thirty-second year of his reign, in the seventh Roman indiction, Lotharius, king of the Franks, remitted to the Lombards, after a treaty had first been made, the tribute of twelve thousand gold florins which they were obliged to pay each year to the Franks.

In the thirty-eighth year of Lotharius, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth Roman indiction, died Werner, count of the palace, or mayor of the palace of the Franks. After him Ansegil, son of Saint Arnulf, bishop of Metz, monk and hermit, previously a married man, was made mayor of the palace of the Franks; with his son Pippin, and Saint Arnulf himself, faithfully assisting him, he held the principate. Pippin, afterward mayor of the palace, who was called the Elder, was the son of the aforesaid Ansegisus, who was son of Saint Arnulf. He was son of Ansegil, or, as others write, of Arnold the Elder. He was son of Ambertus, surnamed Nicanor, born from Blidehild, daughter of Lotharius, the first king of Clovis. That Ambertus was son of Duke Sigebertus.
