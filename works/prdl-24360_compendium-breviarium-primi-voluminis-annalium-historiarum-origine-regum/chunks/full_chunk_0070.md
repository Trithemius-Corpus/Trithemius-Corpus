*Arthur was a prince magnificent beyond measure.*

Arthur, strong in great uprightness of character, prudence, gentleness, and at the same time humanity, took every care to show himself lovable and venerable to all, because, together with virtue of soul, he also abounded in wondrous liberality toward everyone, and especially toward churchmen, on whom, for the love of Christ, he bestowed many little gifts and also presents.

*King Arthur expelled the Saxons and Picts from Britain.*

He expelled the Saxons and Picts from Britain, and powerfully subjected the Scots, the Irish, and the Orcadians to his kingdom. When the kings of Dacia and Norway heard this, they came of their own accord and submitted themselves to his lordship.

*Merlin.*

Marvelous things concerning this Arthur’s birth, and likewise his future deeds, Merlin, the prophet of the English, foretold to his father Uther Pendragon; we made mention of these in the first volume of the Annals.

*Odoacer the Saxon came into Gaul.*

In these same times Odoacer, by nation a Rugian, or a German Saxon, armed with a multitude of Turcilingi, Heruli, and also Suetii, or those whom we now call Swedes from the island, set out from almost the farthest borders of Saxony and came into Gaul, and entered the city of Orleans as victor. Afterwards he also received Angers into his power by the surrender of its citizens.

*The war of the Franks with Odoacer.*

Against him Childeric, king of the Franks, led out his army on the following day, joined battle, killed Count Paul, obtained the city, and put Odoacer with his army to flight.

*Odoacer, overcome by the Franks, proceeds to Italy.*

Odoacer, making peace with King Childeric, left Gaul and entered Italy intending to conquer it. He compelled Orestes, the patrician of the Romans, who did not dare to meet him in Liguria, to flee back; and when Orestes was hiding in the city of Ticinum, which is now called Pavia, he besieged, captured, and killed him.

*Odoacer reigns in Italy for fourteen years.*

After this he obtained absolutely all the cities and fortifications of Italy, with no one resisting, and possessed them in complete quiet. Placing the diadem of the kingdom on his own head, he reigned in Italy according to his own will for fourteen years; afterwards, however, he was slain by the king of the Goths.

*The Franks, after killing the Romans, subjugated all Gaul to themselves.*

Childeric, king of the Franks, having gathered a very great army of his men, traversed all Gaul with a powerful hand in the year of the Lord’s birth 475, subjected to his kingdom everything that remained, killed those who dared to oppose him, but received graciously those who voluntarily made surrender. In that tumult he killed many of the Romans and freed Gaul from their dominion.

*The Franks, taking Cologne from the Romans, subjected it to themselves.*

At the same time the Franks, seizing Cologne Agrippina from the power of the Romans with a strong hand, also subjected it to their own lordship. Then Mainz, Worms, Speyer, and not long afterward Strasbourg, together with all the towns and fortifications throughout the whole Cisrhenane region from the city of Constance in Alemannia as far as the sea, and thereafter through the Morini all Gaul as far as the Loire, they brought under their power, with no one resisting.

*Here the petty kings of the Franks multiplied. Sigebert, petty king of the Franks at Cologne.*

But in the more important cities they placed dukes from the royal stock of the Franks, who, under the command and power of King Childeric and his successors, were to rule his people and administer justice to individuals according to the laws of the fatherland.
