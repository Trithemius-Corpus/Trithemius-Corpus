Joining weapons with weapons and hands with hands, Antenor, now defeated, now victor, was at last killed by the Goths in battle, together with many of his men, in the aforesaid year, as we have said, 440 before the birth of Christ. His son Marcomer succeeded him in the kingdom.

*Marcomir, the elder son of the slain King Antenor, appointed in his father’s place, reigned for twenty-eight years.* He began almost in the same year in which his father was killed. He was a high-spirited, brave, bold, and warlike man, who avenged his father’s death upon the neighboring Goths by frequent incursions, although he seemed far inferior to the Goths in number of fighters and in strength. Moreover, in the fourth year of his reign he held counsel with the chief men of the kingdom as to how either the Goths might be kept from the kingdom, or, if this could not be done, how he might otherwise provide for the people, lest it perish.

At last it pleased all who were rightly wise, since they could in no way withstand so immense a multitude of Goths, to change their dwelling-places by the will of the gods. Therefore, when the ministers of their gods had been summoned, the elders commanded that they should be stirred up by the divine powers as to which direction they were ordered to go. They prepare rites suited to prophecies; they offer sacrifices, composed by art, for summoning infernal powers. A priest stands in the midst, raving before the altar of Jupiter; he twists his head, utters verses in a murmur, gnashes artfully with his teeth, bent out of the ordinary manner, with his fingers he twists his arms together with his hands, brandishes them, and, flying about, bends himself back in like manner; to one seeing him sane he seems foolish, and more like a madman than a man. At last, when the divine power had been summoned by the chant, it gave back a prophecy to Menolpus as he prayed, and, beginning thus, thundered forth:

“Marcomer, prince of Jupiter, the divine power bids you be calm, and not to fear the chances of the long journey, which stretches toward the west after your brothers to the mouths of the Rhine, where Brutus holds the back, the Trojans the face, [exulrophaea] at rest. Each is your brother by nature, by nation a faithful friend. Go, with swift course take possession of the dwellings prepared, to be extended by the will of the father’s divinity into a third part of the world.”

That same night King Marcomir, in his fatherland’s incantation, called upon the divine power, singing beforehand with a murmur the one whom from her sagacity they named Aliruna as she prophesied. He compared the monster. He confirmed by the authority of the Sibyl the prophecy of the priest, and ordered the people immediately to be led across to the place where, the offspring having multiplied exceedingly, by the foretokening vision it would remain stronger.

Marcomir beheld a three-headed divine power standing before him, of one [unclear] in eternity. He heard the particular prediction of each head, and saw a distinct [unclear] set above each one.
