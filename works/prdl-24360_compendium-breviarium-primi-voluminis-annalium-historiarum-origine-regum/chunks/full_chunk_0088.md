Dagobert, the elder son of King Lotharius, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks in the year of the Lord’s nativity noted above, reigned fourteen years; and earlier in Austrasia, as has been said, three years while his father was still living. Thus the whole time during which he reigned was seventeen years.

Heribert, however, Dagobert’s brother by his father, but born of another mother, as has been said, received in the division of the kingdom Aquitaine, Toulouse, and the other cities contained between the Pyrenees and the Loire, as the possession of his inheritance, on this condition: that in the other kingdoms of the Franks he should claim no right for himself. He established the principal seat of his kingdom at Toulouse. In the fourth year after this, when an occasion for war had been seized, he received the Gascons in surrender.

King Dagobert built the monastery of Saint Dionysius the martyr anew from the foundations, and enriched it with many estates, goods, and possessions, though not without injury to certain churches, which he despoiled by force of many ornaments, as though all things, acquired both lawfully and unlawfully, were owed to the services of blessed Dionysius. He built the church of the same monastery, altogether most beautiful, which was covered with solid silver; into it he transferred the bodies of the said martyr and his companions from the Catulian village and placed them in a golden shrine. He also most beautifully adorned the inner walls of the church with tapestries most fittingly woven with pearls and large pearls. He gave the place to be inhabited by monks of our order.

Dagobert first had a wife named Gertrude, whom he repudiated because of sterility, and he brought in another, whose name was Nanthildis. Finally he kept going to many crowds of concubines, besides those whom he maintained in several places, arrayed and adorned like queens. Yet Pippin, mayor of the palace, or count palatine, who had been duke of Brabant and at the same time count of Namur, nephew of Saint Arnulf and father of Saint Gertrude, a virgin nun at Nivelles in Brabant, resisted his shameful deeds quite considerably, and by no means allowed the king to sink so freely, according to his own will, through so many vices.

In the second year of King Dagobert, which was the six hundred thirty-third year of the Lord’s nativity, his wife Nanthildis bore him a son, who was named Sigebert. This infant, forty days old, when he was baptized by Saint Amandus the bishop, while all were silent, himself answered “Amen” in a clear voice. Heribert, his uncle, who was reigning at Toulouse, lifted this Sigebert from the holy font of baptism. *The fifty-first.*

Dagobert reigned seventeen years.

Heribert, his brother, reigned in Aquitaine.

The monastery of Saint Dionysius is founded in Gaul.

The church of Saint Dionysius was covered with silver.

Queen Gertrude, being sterile, is repudiated. King Dagobert was excessively lustful.

Pippin, mayor of the palace of the Franks.

An infant of forty days, when he was baptized, answered: Amen.

The monarchy of the kingdom of the Franks is transferred to Dagobert.
