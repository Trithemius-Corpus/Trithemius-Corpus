He also wrote certain other smaller works, whose titles he did not indicate to me.

These, dearest brother Nicholas, are the works of Trithemius which, while he was abbot for twenty-three years, he composed at Sponheim for the honor of God amid cares and the constant occupation of household affairs, reading or writing day and night whenever he could steal a little leisure for himself. During the years in which he presided, he brought into the library of Sponheim a very great treasury of books in every field, not only by the art of printing but also of manuscripts. For when I was with him for the space of one year to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, he counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eighty had been properly written with the pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his own mouth that he had spent more than two thousand florins on books; these he had not taken from the substance of the monastery, which was always very scanty, but, since he was both known and dear to many princes, he spent for the most part on books the gifts with which he was honored by them. For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books above all whom I know living in the flesh today. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot in Sponheim, has alienated very many volumes; whether this is true, I certainly do not know at all. In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had arisen between the Realm and Philip, Count Palatine, Trithemius, through fear of the enemy, withdrew with his library and the monastery’s possessions to the nearby town of Kreuznach, and I likewise returned to my own affairs.

But in the year 1506, in the month of October, Trithemius himself, stirred by the envy of certain men, accepted the abbey of St. James at Würzburg and resigned that of Sponheim.
