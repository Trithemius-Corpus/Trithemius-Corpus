Believe me, Caesar, this most mendacious kind of men is very harmful to your sacred empire, and therefore must be utterly exterminated no less than the source of witches. They promise great things, and they lie in everything. For truly, everything promised in their books is vain and false; and they provide those who believe them with nothing except tricks, frauds, and the most subtle deceptions. But if at any time they seem to deliver what they promised, it should be manifest to all that this happens only through the cooperation of demons, from whose company one must flee no less than from the most savage torments of hell. For demons very often attend these necromantic operations, and, in order to keep their invokers obstinate in the treachery they have begun, they grant them what they ask, if they can. At other times, however, when invoked by conjurations, they disdain to appear, and nonetheless they have led astray the minds of those invoking them.

There is also another kind of men, no less foolish than superstitious: namely, those who wish to have no commerce with demons, and yet nonetheless in vain operations use things appropriated to demons, and for that reason utterly unlawful. For they think that great and hidden power resides in characters, and therefore they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But since this is not the time to recount their madness, let us pursue our subject.

Why good and just men do not obtain command over demons must be stated briefly. Just as likeness of wills makes wicked and impious men equal to demons, so unlikeness of wills renders good and just men wholly contrary to them. But between those who are contrary and unlike there can be no familiarity; rather, continual opposition and hatred prevail. Where, however, no familiarity contracted through likeness of wills is found, neither can there be trust of one toward another, nor mutual cooperation in the working of marvels.

Nor do you, glorious Caesar, though you are a man, the great ruler and prince of men, admit as familiar to yourself anyone wholly contrary and unlike you in character, and continually resisting your will. But good and upright men, because they always refuse the most depraved will of demons, can in no way pass into their company; they neither wish nor consent to be familiar with them, but with all their strength flee from and detest meeting them. And therefore they do not obtain over them that fictitious command of which we have spoken. For familiarity is nowhere safe when no likeness of wills has preceded it; nor can there be concord between contraries, nor does friendship stand between those unlike in will. Yet demons do not indiscriminately obey all the wicked and reprobate, but only those with whom they are bound by pacts and services; and when invoked, they willingly attend those whom they cannot otherwise keep faithful, although they falsely claim that they are compelled.

Therefore it is not to be wondered at, excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to obey depraved and perverse men, whom, as we have said, both malevolence has made like to them and a desire for ruin upon the human race has made concordant with them. But far be it from every Christian to wish to command demons through a depraved likeness of will; for if anyone in this life has begun to have the company of malignant spirits, in the life to come he will burn with them without end.

But why can good and just men not command the holy angels of God, into whose likeness of good will they have passed? I think that from what has been said above the solution to this question would easily be given, if the difference of intellects is recalled to mind. For although there are many men established in the Church of God whose justice benefits many unto salvation, nevertheless in our times very few advance so far that, by reason of the merit of holiness, they are either feared by demons or received into the company of familiarity.
