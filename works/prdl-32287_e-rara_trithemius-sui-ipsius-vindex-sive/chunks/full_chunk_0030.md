But let no proud man, presuming upon his own strength, think himself safe from the evil workings of demons; for the man swollen with pride, whom God’s protection abandons, it undoubtedly lays open to the evil workings of demons.

The fourth reason is that there may be a distinction between the good and the wicked. For Scripture says of good men beloved by God in the Acts of the Apostles: “Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.” And Saint Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: “All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution.” The most wise King Solomon also says in Proverbs: “Whom the Lord loves, he corrects, and as a father he takes pleasure in his son.” It is a most glorious thing for a Christian to endure adversities patiently for the love of God, whether they are inflicted by wicked men or by demons. For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and righteous men, not for their ruin, as the Petrones suppose, but rather for the greatest and sure consolation of the soul. Indeed, when a righteous man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeps his commandments with all care, and nevertheless is afflicted by evil workings, whether in body, in possessions, or in both, that man is truly blessed; for when he has been tested, he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the Catholic Church of Christians, and, receiving evils in this world, patiently endures them for the love of God, has a sure sign that he perseveres in the grace of God unto salvation. Therefore we read thus written of the wicked and reprobate: “Why do the impious live? They are lifted up and strengthened with riches. Their houses are secure and at peace, and the rod of God is not upon them. They spend their days in good things, and in a moment they descend to hell.” For it is a manifest sign of damnation if here one lives wickedly, contrary to God in sins and vices, and prospers in the goods of fortune and in bodily health.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and righteous men to be afflicted by the wicked and reprobate is for the complete purification of the mind. For, as Scripture says: “There is no righteous man on earth who does good and does not sin.” But since God lets no sin pass unpunished, he appoints temporal punishments through mercy for righteous men because of their offenses, but eternal punishments through justice for the impious and reprobate. Therefore, out of mercy, he changes eternal punishment for the penitent into temporal punishment, which they must necessarily suffer either in this world or after death in purgatory. But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and most grievous, merciful God is accustomed to purge in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the hardships of diverse tribulations in body and in possessions. For the evil workings of impious men serve good and righteous men as a remedy, but for those who inflict them they serve unto damnation.

The sixth reason is that a man harmed by the assaults of demons may be recalled to penance and may set in his heart the necessary amendment of life. For Almighty God does not at once, when we sin, send vengeance upon us, but waits for our amendment until the appointed time. But if we have neglected to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not expect it; and we who, as sinners, scorned to acknowledge ourselves through repentance in tranquility will at last acknowledge ourselves in tribulation, though late. Hence the holy prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: “If his children forsake my law and do not walk in my judgments, and do not keep my commandments, I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their sins with scourges; but my mercy I will not scatter from them, nor will I do harm in my truth.”
