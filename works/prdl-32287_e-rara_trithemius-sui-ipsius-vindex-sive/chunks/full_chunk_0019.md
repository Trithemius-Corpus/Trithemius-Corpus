Hence no one among mortals is good unless, aided by the counsel of his own will, through the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil unless, of his own accord, he is deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin. By uprightness of will, however, we are made like the good spirits, if we prove by holy and necessary works the piety of the faith which we have in Christ. And just as the consummation of a good will makes good men equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious makes them like demons. Hence, the more evil and perverse men have been corrupted by the malice of their own will, the more like demons they become in disposition.

Through the likeness of friendship, familiarity is introduced; and from it there gradually arises the confidence of the lesser toward the greater, and command, whether it is true or feigned by cunning. Therefore, if wicked men seem to have any command over demons, it arises from the greatness of their depravity, when, that is, their disposition is corrupted to such a degree that by their reprobate will they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons who come together with them.

For demons are not all equal in malice or intelligence; rather, one is much more cunning than another in swiftness of understanding, and far worse in corrupted affection. For those who, having fallen from the high seats, are more closely joined to the earth and inhabit caverns and caves are certainly duller than the rest, even to such a degree that they fear swords and rods, and seem to be moved by the threats of men who terrify them, as may be observed in those whom we call demoniacs. But the demons who dwell farther removed from the earth are of a subtler nature and more rarely meet with human beings. Men full of malice, however, the more they advance in perversity of will, the more like demons they become; and, once admitted by them into familiarity, they thereafter become bold, and receive command over the demons whose familiarity they enjoy, not by violent power, but by feigned pretense.

For demons, in order to keep willingly in their depravity those men whom they have once received into surrender, gladly subject themselves to them as though moved by a simulated dominion; and what they do of their own accord, they pretend to be compelled to do in obedience. For no one attains true command over demons except one whom faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.”

Therefore evil men command demons: malevolence has made them like demons, likeness has made them familiar with them, and familiarity has made them the demons’ own and subject to them. It is as if some servant, born in a humble station, were said to command a great prince, as often happens, because he is joined to him by daily familiarity and obtains from him, without contradiction, everything he has asked, so that he seems rather to rule his lord than to be ruled by him. According to this likeness, evil and reprobate men are admitted by demons into their familiarity, and through more prolonged confidence they become very bold; and they receive over them a simulated, not to say familiar, command, which they have earned by the likeness of a most depraved will.

Hence whatever they have demanded from demons who join with them by an implicit or explicit compact, they receive, except when, being forbidden by divine ordinance, the demons are unable to furnish what is asked. Yet they do not thus join indiscriminately with all reprobate and evil persons, but only with those with whom, through the intervention of some compact, they have entered into covenants, such as the female witches who have offered homage to the devil, and those summoners of demons whom the common people call by the usual word necromancers.
