Concerning this proposed question, I have received from none other than the ancient and Catholic doctors the necessary reasons, which I shall set forth in order as briefly as I can. The first reason is the testing of the faith of Christians and the proving of their integrity, so that, when scourged by the evil arts of demons, they may learn in the sufferings of the flesh what faith and love toward God they have hidden in the mind. In prosperity it is not difficult to be long-suffering; but in adversity, with humble patience, to await help from the Savior of the faithful belongs not to the soft, but to the strong and truly faithful Christians. Most holy Job learned in his tribulation how great a constancy of faith toward God he possessed, he who before, in tranquility, had kept piety without the dart of temptation.

Hence it must also be noted that those whom the evil arts of demons or men have in any way made sick, if for recovering health they approach or consult any sorcerers and enchanters, either personally or through others, act against the precepts of God and the Church, and have neither true faith in God nor love. For they prefer the health of mortal flesh to God, and seek health not from Christ but from the devil and his minister. They prefer to live healthy, but unhappily, in the name of the devil, rather than to suffer a little here for the love of Christ and afterward rejoice with him forever. But whoever does not approach sorcerers because God has forbidden it, preferring to live, with the observance of God’s commandments, in want and sickness rather than against God in riches and bodily health, truly loves God and rightly preserves the integrity of the Christian faith.

O most sacred Caesar, men corrupt in mind and reprobate concerning the faith must not be tolerated among Christians: those who cure the bewitched by sorceries, who reveal thefts and adulteries by the ministry of demons, and who foretell the future by inspection of stones or mirrors. I beg you, do not listen to those who, ignorant of the divine laws, think such ministers of demons useful to your rule, because one skilled in sorcery knows how to undo sorceries; for that pernicious cure of bodies brings death to souls, and makes men withdraw from God, the creator of all things, and cling to impious apostate demons, with whom after death they will be damned forever.

The second reason why Almighty God permits the innocent to be harmed by sorcery is the increase of merits, namely so that, once their patience has been tested in tribulation, they may deserve a worthier crown of glory in heaven. For, as holy Augustine says, a man is unknown to himself as to what he can bear and what he cannot bear. Sometimes he fears that he cannot do what nevertheless he can. When temptation comes, as though it were a kind of interrogator, a man is discovered by himself, who before, being hidden, did not know himself. The divine chief apostle Peter, before temptation came upon him, was hidden both from himself and from others as to how inconsiderate and weak he was. It came, and the weakness that had lain hidden appeared. Therefore temptation is the greatest proof of a man, as Sacred Scripture bears witness: The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men. Patience is easy in prosperity, because no adversities strike the mind. But many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from them all our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the preservation of humility, so that the faithful and righteous man may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or righteousness. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: They are not in the labor of men, and they will not be scourged with men; therefore pride has held them fast; they are covered with their iniquity and impiety; their iniquity has come forth, as it were, from fatness. It is of God’s mercy that he sometimes permits good and righteous men to be shaken by adversities, those whom he knows cannot be humbled in prosperity. For tribulation has made many men humble whom favorable circumstances would have made proud. Finally, the fearful imagining of sorceries makes a good man fear God.
