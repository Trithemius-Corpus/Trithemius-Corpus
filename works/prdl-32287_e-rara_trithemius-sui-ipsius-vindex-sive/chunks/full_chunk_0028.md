Fragment

The sixth reason is the praise owed to the merit of constancy in human virtue, when we praise a just man for this reason: because, although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, aided by the grace of God, remained steadfast in the constancy of a good will.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits demons to commit such great evils through the agency of witches is this: so that those who, having scorned the truth, take delight in falsehood may be seduced by the devil, the inventor of falsehood, and be utterly deceived. For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who despise following the saving teachings of Christ, should all, deceived by demons, be plunged down to hell to be punished forever. They seek help from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have worshiped. For it is just that one who, by his own free will, abandons truth and clings to falsehood should be hurled by the devil into destruction.

The eighth cause is the manifestation of divine power, whose might and strength are above every creature. For when almighty God sometimes permits the devil great things and denies him certain small things, he makes manifest how weak the enemy of the human race is, and how subject in all things to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing of himself; but everything he does, he does by God’s permission. Therefore the first cause of all for which almighty God permits acts of witchcraft to occur is justice, by which he preserves in inviolable order the freedom that he once granted to his creature from the beginning. But since the power of every creature, dependent on its Creator, is restricted and limited, it is clear that the devil cannot do everything he wills, nor can a man, however depraved in will. The will is indeed free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wished, nothing in human affairs would remain safe, but they would bring everything to ruin. The history of holy Job clearly demonstrates by manifest example that the power of demons is bound: Satan did not dare to tempt him any further than had been permitted to him by divine dispensation.

For no one in this present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if they were always able to use freely, at their own discretion, their power to harm. Before the Lord’s Passion they used a greater freedom of power than afterward; but when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, from then on their power was greatly changed. The Savior says in the Gospel: Now the prince of the world will be cast out. For the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints have greatly restrained the boldness of demons, so that they can rightly be called kindlers of evils, but by no means compellers. Nor would the devil grow strong against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture admonishes us, saying: Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. And again: Do not give place to the devil.

Therefore, in concluding the discourse for the understanding of the proposed question, why God permits so many acts of witchcraft to occur, I believe the reasons have been sufficiently made clear. For even if the will of demons is always wicked, their power can nevertheless never be unjust, since what they wickedly will they have from their own choice, but what they are able to do wickedly they have only from God. And these things have been said so far as they concern those who do evil.

But why does almighty God permit demons, through witches, to harm people who have been baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity and faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ? For if they were able to harm only the wicked, the perverse, and unbelievers in body and in possessions, but were restrained from approaching and harming the good and faithful, the permission would rightly seem to be a just judgment. But now, since good and innocent people are more often made ill by the witchcraft of demons, and sometimes are even deprived of life, to pass over in silence the loss of temporal goods which those bewitched often suffer, this has become a source of scruple for many.
