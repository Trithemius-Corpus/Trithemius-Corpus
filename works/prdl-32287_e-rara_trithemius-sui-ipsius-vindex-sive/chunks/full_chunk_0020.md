And this ministry is entrusted to exorcists in the Church of God when they are ordained by the bishop.

Again, there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons, and the most wicked conditions show how unlawfully they presume to do so. The first and most pernicious kind is that of sorceresses, who enter into pacts with demons and, after first making a solemn profession, hand themselves over to some demon as his property and for perpetual service. How great are the evils that this most pernicious kind of human beings commits in your empire, most wise Caesar, no one can express. For first denying God, the faith of Christ, and holy baptism, they offer themselves in mind and body to demons; by their sorceries they make men and beasts sick, harm them, and kill them. There is no disease which, God permitting, they cannot bring on, because, when they present themselves as servants to demons in observance of the covenant they have made, in league with them they dare whatever they wish. Hence they are nowhere to be tolerated, but rather utterly exterminated in every place, as God, the Creator of all, commands: “You shall not suffer sorcerers to live.” Some sorceresses summon demons by charms, others entice them by prayers and sacrifices, and the rest, provoking them by unspeakable things and ceremonies, draw them into the aid of their impiety. It is therefore no wonder that sorceresses seem to command demons, to whom they have made themselves like by depravity of will, and with whom, by mutual intercourse, they are in agreement for evil against the human race.

The second kind of wicked men who wish to command demons is that of those whom the common people call necromancers. They profess arts full of execrations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly visible enclosed in a crystal or other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of falsehood and superstitions, commit evils so great that no good man could imagine them. For, in order to entice demons into their service, they very often use most foul sacrifices, and enter into an express pact with them. They carry about many and various little books full of filth, impiety, and falsehood, falsely inscribed with the names of ancient philosophers and sages; by displaying these they deceive many incautious and curious people, and drag them with themselves into the pit of demons. They promise great and utterly incredible things, by the hearing of which they deceitfully hold men suspended in wonder. By rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations, they say that they can constrain malignant demons so that they appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal or other receptacle, and answer distinctly to everything asked.

All of them indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found to whom malignant spirits, even for evil purposes, are obedient in this way, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it, lest the curiosity of men, prone to novelties, should despise the religion of Christ and in time of need seek aid from demons. But all those to whom malignant spirits, when summoned by the conjurations of necromancers, are obedient have without doubt passed into their fellowship through an explicit or implicit pact. The greater number, however, are of those whom the malignant spirits disdain to obey, although they are never afraid to occupy themselves at all times in the profane arts of necromancers.
