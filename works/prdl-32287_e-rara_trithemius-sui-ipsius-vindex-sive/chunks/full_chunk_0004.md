Apologetic defense.

In his retirement he is writing various works; at my request he recently sent me these titles of them:

To Emperor Maximilian he published a marvelous work, which he entitled *Polygraphia*, in six books.

To the same, the key to the same *Polygraphia*, one book.

Replies to eight questions of the same emperor, one book.

To the same, *On the Seven Intelligences Moving the Spheres*, one book.

To Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg, Prince Elector, *Various Questions, Orations, and Propositions*, twenty books.

To the same, *On Foreknowledges Lawful and Unlawful for a Christian*, two books.

To the same he wrote that marvelous and laborious work of *Steganography*, eight books, which that rash Bovillus, not grasping it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely.

To the same prince, a book of letters, one book.

To the same, *Against Malefactors and All Arts and Superstitions Forbidden by the Church*, five books.

To the same, *On the Causes and Cure of the Falling Sickness and of Malefactors*, three books.

To Jakob Trithemius, his brother, an itinerary of his life, two books.

To Burkhard von Horneck, physician, *On the Property of Nuns*, one book.

He wrote a history, or annals, on the origin, kings, and deeds of the people of the Franks and Germans, three great volumes, beginning the narrative from King Marcomer, who in the four hundred and thirty-third year before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine, and continuing to the thirty-eighth and ninth year of Maximilian Caesar.

To Laurentius, Bishop of Würzburg and Duke of Eastern Franconia, he wrote an epitome or abridgment of those three volumes of histories, three books.

To the same, *On the Dukes and Bishops of the Würzburg Franks*, one book.

*On the Miracles Shown in Memory of Our Lady St. Mary at Dettelbach*, two books.

*On the Miracles of the Same Shown in the Nettle-Grove near Heilbronn*, six books.

An apologetic work against Charles Bovillus, who slanders the things he did not understand, one book.

*On the Origin and Kings of the Franks*, one book.

A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, St. James at Würzburg, one book.

To Albert, of the Margraves of Brandenburg, Archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, *The Life of the Divine Rabanus, Archbishop*, three books.

*On the Translation of the Same from Mainz into Saxony*, one book.

To the same, *The Life of St. Maximius, Bishop*, one book.

To Johann, Count Palatine and Duke of Bavaria, and Count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, *On the Origin, Kings, and Dukes of the Bavarians, together with the Counts Palatine and the Counts of Sponheim*, four books.

He has in hand a laborious work *On Demons and Profane Arts, Sorceries, and Superstitions*, which will have twelve books; in them he rejects, confounds, and overthrows all the wiles of the diabolical arts.

Of Würzburg letters, which he wrote in that abbey, four books.

He also wrote several other smaller works suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as:

A course, rosary, missal, office, and various prayers, together with proses or sequences, on St. Anne, grandmother of our Savior.

He also wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on St. Mary Magdalene.

A rosary on St. Joseph, foster-father of the Lord.

A rosary on St. Peter the Apostle.

A rosary on St. John the Evangelist.

At the request of Johann, Archbishop of Trier, a sequence on St. Anne, which begins, “Jesus of heaven, of the King,” to the melody “Ave praeclara.”

Another, to be sung on her feast, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day,” etc.

A third, for the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, and it begins, “Let us rejoice in honor.”

A fourth, on St. Hildegard, virgin, which begins thus: “Praise be to you, Lord, eternal king,” etc.

A fifth, on St. Rupert, Duke of the people of Bingen, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day.”

A sixth, on St. Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, and it begins thus: “Let us sing, renowned ones.”

A seventh, on St. Rabanus, archbishop.

An eighth, on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus: “Let us adore Jesus Christ,” etc.
