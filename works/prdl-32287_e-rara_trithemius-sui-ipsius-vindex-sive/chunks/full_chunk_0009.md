Certainly I would believe that I could more easily extricate myself from labyrinthine windings, meetings, and returnings than explain steganographic intricacies without the Key. Do you not think that, just as in the earlier books, he also used mysteries in this book? Could it not be that by “motion” he meant the operation itself, and by “the space of twenty-four hours” the twenty-four letters, or the alphabet? Thus the words must be taken not as they sound, but in a mystical sense; nor should one believe that a physical agent is here acting at a distance without an interposed medium, although that is plainly how it appears to those who do not know and do not understand the words.

For I pass over the fact that it is not necessary for an agent always to be present to the patient according to its substance, or by contact of the subject, but that it is enough if it is virtually present to the patient, or by contact of power. I say nothing about sympathies and antipathies. I pass over what some allege against this philosophical saying concerning the remora, or echeneis, and the torpedo-fish: the former, though small in body, nevertheless so stops huge ships that sometimes the sailors are wholly unable to advance, while the water meanwhile is by no means at rest, as Pliny reports in book 31, chapter 1, and others commonly say; the latter makes the hands and arms of fishermen grow numb and torpid, while the net itself does not grow numb, as Aristotle attests in book 9 of the History of Animals, chapter 37.

I am silent about the marvelous force and power of the magnet, which draws iron without drawing the medium placed between, as daily experience teaches. Finally, I pass over the fact that an agent sometimes acts more strongly upon a distant patient than upon one nearby, as is clear from fire, which heats water in the upper part of a vessel more intensely than water at the bottom; indeed, according to Aristotle, section 15, problems 4 and 8, the bottom of the vessel is much colder than the upper water, which nevertheless is farther from the acting fire.

All these things, I say, since they have little bearing on the Magic of which we are speaking, I set aside; and I firmly affirm that Trithemius, a man of proven virtue and highest learning, both in this book and in his other earlier books, used natural means, and therefore did nothing contrary to physical principles, however suspect he may seem to some.

For why did he introduce arithmetical numbers in this book? Why does he so carefully divide motion, that is, operation, into proper, direct, retrograde, mixed, confused, and so on? Why does he wish the operator to use encaustic? Are not all these physical and natural means? But, to say more: do you believe, reader, that it is contrary to physical reason that the thoughts of the mind can be made known without words, without books, and without a messenger to an absent friend? What are those reasons? I myself will show you by experience that, through the art which I have drawn from Trithemius, I can make known to a friend sitting at my table or standing by it whatever he wishes, and that through thirty, fifty, or a hundred words, even words fixed and prescribed by you; yet to that friend, or whoever he may be, I shall speak no word, send no messenger, and, if you wish, not even look at him, and this physically and naturally.

What of the fact that by showing torches at night I can signify the thoughts of my mind even for a whole mile or more, as far as sight can carry, and this not only in the space of twenty-four hours, but even in a single quarter-hour? But if this can be done, why not that also? Against what physical principles does it fight? Surely Trithemius himself explains clearly enough by what means the will can be made known at any distance without a messenger and without letters. For he intends that distance of places to be so established that it is nevertheless regulated by speech. But I can make my conception known without a messenger, that is, without one who knows, but by one who carries the secrets while being wholly ignorant of them, as Trithemius means in the same place; and this is very true and very easy. And understand “without letters” to mean without visible letters, but perhaps with invisible ones, namely either by the dermatic, hyphasmatic, or aleotic kind, as the same Trithemius will teach below, or by some other hieroglyphic sign.
