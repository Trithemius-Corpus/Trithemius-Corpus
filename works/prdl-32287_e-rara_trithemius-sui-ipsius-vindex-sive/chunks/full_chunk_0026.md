Among these demons, too, some are incubones of treasures, which the greed of mortals has hidden in the earth; and lest they should again come into human use, they remove them, steal them, guard them, and sometimes transfer them from place to place. And this kind of demon has no dealings with witch-women.

The sixth kind of demons is called light-fleeing, because they especially dread and detest the light: for they never appear by day, nor can they assume a body in any way except at night. This is the business that walks in darkness, an inscrutable and utterly dark kind of demon, stirred by cold passions, malicious, restless, and disturbed, which violently presses down upon the men it encounters in the hours of the night, and quite often, when God permits, kills them either by a certain breath or by touch. This kind of demon does not associate with witches, nor can it be constrained by charms, because it flees the light, human voices, and likewise every disturbance. The aerial kind is especially accustomed to attend women’s sorceries, since it is subtler than all the others and of the most daring rashness. Some of the terrestrial demons also, as has been said, serve certain witches; yet, being held to be of a lower rank, they can by no means bring about those greater sorceries. It is therefore plainly established that the whole power of witches, by which they do such astonishing things, is granted to demons by the Lord God within limits; and when He does not permit it, they accomplish nothing.

ON DIVINE PERMISSION.

The Seventh Question.

The seventh question of your Most Serene Majesty was this: Why does God, since He is just and the avenger of evils, permit such great sorceries to be done for the destruction of the human race, by which not only sinners but also many innocent people perish, grievously tormented? To this question I remember having answered thus some time ago, when I was placed before your Majesty.

The best God, just and upright, loving and merciful, whatever He permits, permits justly: because He is just, and there is no iniquity with Him, but all things, in true judgment, that are done He either does or permits. Therefore, when it is asked why God permits evils to be done, we answer: He permits them because He is just.

Now let your Majesty see, O Caesar, whether the answer we have given is absurd, since it in no way prejudices divine goodness that He permits evils to be done for a most just reason; rather, it would prejudice it if He did not permit evils to be done in the present world. For indeed, if He did not permit evils to be done, then man could not sin; he would certainly be impeccable, which does not befit his nature, since the Creator of all things gave him free will from the beginning. For man, since he is a creature and not the Creator, is necessarily capable of being turned both to good and to evil, since his being depends on the supreme Maker of all creatures, just as every caused thing depends on the cause of its being. But to create has been left to God alone, who is incorruptible and everlasting; everything corruptible, however, necessarily fails, yet is preserved by the goodness of the Creator so long as it does not lose the influence of its cause.

Hence it neither could nor ought to have been the case that man, by the condition of his nature, was created incapable of sin; for only God, the Maker of all things, naturally possesses this, being immutable and everlasting. But whenever it is granted to man that he seems unable to sin, neither is freedom of will taken away, nor is the possibility simply removed, but the will is confirmed in good. And whenever this gift is granted to a creature, it is a gift of grace, not of nature. For Scripture says: God created man from the beginning, and left him in the hand of his own counsel.
