For indeed, just as almighty God has instituted certain sacraments in his Church which, when worthily administered and with his assistance, signify and produce a saving effect, salvation being worked by the very one who instituted them, so also the devil, who desires nothing more than to be judged equal to God, has instituted certain signs as though they were sacraments. By these, when he is invoked by those with whom the malignant worker is allied by pacts, he is present; and whenever God permits it, he brings about the evil that is requested. If God does not permit it, he does nothing at all.

For the devil is the worker of all necromantic effects and of the works of witches; through the application of the means which he himself first instituted, he produces the desired work upon the subject matter, sometimes really, sometimes only in appearance. The women witches, whose will is already corrupted and who by consent pass through pacts into fellowship with demons, receive from them certain marks and symbolic signs; when the demons are summoned by these, they immediately appear.

In these pernicious operations of the demon nothing is stronger than the foolish credulity of the witches, which is increased by pacts and vows; for the firmer the faith they place either in the demons or in their signs, the more swiftly they obtain the operation through the demons’ cooperation. For the excessively savage corruption of the witches’ will turns them in a certain way into frenzy; when this has seized the mind, the demons at once apply themselves to the raving women, and, invited by pacts and signs, with God permitting, grant to them in their madness the effect of their request.

Plato related something similar in his book on poetic frenzy, saying: Bacchant women, when not of sound mind, draw honey and milk from rivers, but when of sound mind they cannot draw them. Their mind, carried outside itself into fellowship with an evil demon, brings this about. We know by experience that witches have at times drawn butter from stirred-up rivers, which can in no way happen except through the ministry of a demon.

In the same way, when they have been drawn into hatred of some man or beast and long for his destruction, they are driven by a most grievous frenzy, thinking of nothing in their mind except revenge. They resort to the instruments of witchcraft known and familiar to them, invoke demons, display the signs instituted by them, and at the same time the characters; and what they cannot accomplish by their own power, they obtain through the demons’ ministry.

But another person, who is not subject to demons by pact and surrender, even if he knows the things suited to witchcraft and also the method of working, and although he displays the signs, nevertheless rarely obtains the effect, because he is not counted among those familiar to the demon. For the devil, swollen with pride, so far imitates the Church of Christ that, just as one who is not ordained cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he utters the words of consecration, so also the demon, however much someone invokes him and displays signs or characters, either never or only rarely cooperates with one whom he does not know to be subject to him by pact.

For if he were to appear indiscriminately to all who invoked him without pacts being made, he would not possess so many witches dedicated to him by right of ownership. Hence it is that many who are devoted to profane arts labor to summon demons to a circle, to a mirror, or to any other receptacles by dreadful conjurations, and yet obtain no effect, because either God’s command stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man who has not been dedicated to him by homage.

For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is agreed that none is perpetrated: the witch, raging in mind and with corrupted will; the cooperation of a familiar demon; and before all things, divine permission. If one of these does not concur, witchcraft cannot be perpetrated. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not meet the will of the wicked woman, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch is lacking, the devil will by no means complete the witchcraft.
