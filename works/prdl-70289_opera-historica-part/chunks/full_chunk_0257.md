*Johannes Trithemius* not ignorant of secular philosophy, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech, venerable no less for the integrity of his life than for his learning. He wrote several excellent little works, by which, usefully occupying his talent, he commended his memory to posterity. Of these, in the aforesaid gymnasium at Erfurt, he is said to have lectured in ordinary course: On the Epistles of Paul, 14 books. He delivered various orations in praise of Saint Jerome. He is said to have written many sermons and diverse questions, together with certain other things, which have as yet by no means come into our hands. He died in the monastery of Saint Peter at Erfurt, where he was buried, under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1493, indiction 12.

Johannes Frytag of Dufeldorp, a town of the diocese of Cologne, of the order of the brothers of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Mainz and prior of Strasbourg, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, a doctor of Cologne, sharp and forceful in intellect, no less eloquent than learned in delivering sermons to the people, devout in life and conduct, and an outstanding lover of regular discipline. He wrote: On the Observance of Feast Days, 1 book; On the Places of the Holy Land, which he traversed with his own feet and saw with his eyes, 1 book; various sermons on the season and on the saints, 2 books; and many other things. He died under Maximilian, Emperor, in the year of the Lord [1494?], indiction 12, and was buried at Strasbourg.

Sebastian Murrho of Colmar, priest and canon in Colmar, the town of his birth, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a philosopher, orator, poet, and astronomer-cosmographer of very celebrated reputation, distinguished in knowledge of laws and canons, most skilled in the three principal languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, second to none in architecture, music, and the whole Quadrivium, subtle in intellect, rich in eloquence, mature in life and conduct. He wrote to Jakob Wimpfeling of Sélestat a brief indeed, but very fine, epitome on the history and praises of the German princes and peoples, 1 book; also many most elegant poems, 1 book; also many letters to various people, 1 book; and various orations. He began commentaries on Baptista Mantuanus the Carmelite, but, overtaken by death, did not bring them forth. Conrad Leontorius, monk of Maulbronn, undertook to finish them and will shortly permit them to be printed. He died, to the very great loss of scholars, carried off by plague, under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1495, indiction 13.

Rudolf Langius, canon of the church of Münster in Westphalia, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a distinguished orator and poet, subtle in intellect, rich in eloquence. He wrote many excellent little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he made himself useful to those present and memorable to posterity. Of these the following are reported: To the Dean of the Church of Cologne, a book of diverse poems, 1 book; On the Siege of the Town of Neuss by Charles, Duke of Burgundy, 1 book; On the Three Kings, a Sapphic poem; On the Praises of Albert the Great, a poem; many poems; various letters to various people; and certain other things unknown to us. I hear that he is still living, an old man, although there are some who say that he departed this life in this year of the Lord 1495.

Jacob de Stralen, priest of the church of Cologne, a man learned by long study in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, who once for some time held the office of preacher in the church of Basel. He wrote many little works indeed worthy of commendation, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these he is said to have read some publicly in the gymnasium at Erfurt: On the Gospel of John, 1 book; On the Canon of the Mass, 1 book; On the Passion of the Lord, 1 book; Itinerary of the Holy Land, 1 book; various questions disputed in the schools, 1 book; many sermons; and several other things. He is still living in [unclear].

Wilhelm of Aachen, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, excellent in life and conduct, who once for some time held the office of preacher in the church of Basel. He wrote many little works indeed worthy of commendation, by which he commended his memory to posterity.
