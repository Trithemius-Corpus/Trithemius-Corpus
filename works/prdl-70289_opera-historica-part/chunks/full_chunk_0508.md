Guido of Evreux, compiler of excellent sermons, is believed also to have flourished around these times, a brother of the same order; he also, besides the rules of Tyconius, gave certain very apt instructions for grasping the sense of sacred Scripture, showing that through ignorance of four things it often happens that one errs in the exposition of sacred doctrine. These are found collected among the rest of his works in the libraries of certain convents.

Laurentius Gervasius, of the convent of Lisieux of the Order of Preachers in Normandy, in the reign of Louis XI, king of the Franks, was held famous at Paris as a doctor and theologian, a man indeed most renowned for knowledge and doctrine. Because of his outstanding wisdom, since he was especially versed in the doctrine of the most holy doctor Thomas Aquinas, he was sent to reform for the better the study at Cologne. When he had made extraordinary progress there, returning thence, he gave up the spirit at Dijon, a town of Burgundy, and there he was buried with the brothers of his order. He left very useful works, which are called the collected works of Master Laurentius Gervasius, on the whole Summa of Theology of the Angelic Doctor; of that doctrine, indeed, while he lived, he was called the mart.

Silvester of Prierio, of Bologna, of the Order of Friars Preachers, venerable doctor and very famous expositor and likewise most learned defender of Saint Thomas Aquinas, flourished around the times of Innocent VIII, supreme pontiff.

He wrote a defense of the doctrine of Saint Thomas, abridging the writings of John Capreolus, demonstrating more clearly than light the irrefragable doctrine of the most holy doctor, dissolving from his sayings the arguments of all opponents.

He also wrote an Introduction to Logic.

Item, a Summa of Sermons, which is called The Golden Rose.

Item, a Summa on Cases of Conscience.

Item, The Hammer of the Scotists.

Item, on the four books of the Sentences.

He has continued writing even to the present time, dwelling at Bologna, namely up to the year of the Lord 1522.

Dominicus of Flanders, a very famous doctor, of the same Order, of Bologna, at the same times as the preceding man.

He wrote Metaphysical Questions.

Questions on the Soul. Item, on the books of the Posterior Analytics.

He died when Alexander VIII, supreme pontiff, was presiding in the chair of Peter.

Paulus of Soncino, of the same Order, in these times.

He wrote Metaphysical Questions.

Item, on the Predicables and Predicaments.

At the same time, or thereabouts, as the preceding man, he departed this life.
