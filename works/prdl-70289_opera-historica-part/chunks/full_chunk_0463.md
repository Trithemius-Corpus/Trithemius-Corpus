*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...he transmitted his glory, and in the school of Poitiers, both by disputing and by writing, earned great praise for his learning. Of his little works the following are reported: a distinguished and very celebrated work, almost divine, pleasing for its singular usefulness.

On the Sentences, 7 books.
On the little Summa of Peter of Spain, 7 books. Because, as the wise man bears witness.
On mathematical science, 1 book. As the Philosopher says.
On Aristotle's books of the Physics, 8 books. The first part having been completed.
On the book On Heaven and the World, 2 books. Nature; here begins the book.
On the book On Generation and Corruption, 2 books. On generation; here begins...
3 books. On first things. The book begins.
On the books of the Meteors, 1 book. Of good and honorable things.
On the book On the Soul, 12 books. The second part having been completed.
On the books of the Metaphysics, 10 books. Having been treated compendiously.
On the books of the Ethics, 10 books.

And certain other works. He was, moreover, a profound interpreter and most keen defender of the sayings of John Scotus; from whose writings, expounding philosophy, he divided this very work into three parts, mathematical, theological, and ethical, and named it a compendium. He flourished in the times of Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Callixtus III, in the year of the Lord 1455.

Petrus de Unzola, a citizen of Bologna, a very celebrated jurist, well instructed in the disciplines of the liberal arts, outstanding in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, notable for experience in many matters, especially judicial ones. He wrote, for the use of notaries studying in legal science, an excellent volume on the continuation of the fifth, sixth, and seventh chapters of the Summa of Lord Rolandinus, which he entitled:

The Newest Dawn, 1 book. Of scribes and notaries.
Various consultations, 1 book.

He is also said to have written some other works, but they have not come to my knowledge.

Henricus de Herph, of the Order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, German by nation, of the province of Cologne, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of pontifical law, outstanding in intellect, clear in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in declaiming sermons to the people, venerable no less for religious life than for knowledge of the Scriptures. He wrote little works of authority not to be rejected, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of their number the following are reported: a great and distinguished volume.

On the Precepts of the Decalogue, 1 book. I find another law in...
Sermons on the Season, 1 book. Behold, your king comes.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book. Rabbi, where do you dwell?
Sermons throughout Advent, 1 book. Who will be able to think.
On the Three Parts of Penance, 1 book. No one can serve two masters.

And certain other works. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Johannes de Turrecremata, of the Order of Friars Preachers, bishop of Sabina, cardinal priest of the most holy Roman Church, a man studious in the divine Scriptures, an excellent declaimer of sermons, quick in intellect, clear in eloquence. He wrote many little works of his intellect, by which he made himself known. Of these the following are reported. For Arsenius, monk of the monastery of Saint Benedict, which he himself held in commendam, he composed a great work, not to be rejected.

On the Rule of Saint Benedict, 1 book. Since, with the help of our Lord.
On the whole Psalter, 1 book. To the most blessed father [unclear].
On the Sunday Gospels, questions, 1 book. To the most sacred and most revered.
On holy water, 1 book. Recently the reverend father enjoined me.
On the salvation of the soul, 1 book. Divine Scripture teaches us.
Sermons on the Season.

And very many other works. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and the supreme pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1460.
