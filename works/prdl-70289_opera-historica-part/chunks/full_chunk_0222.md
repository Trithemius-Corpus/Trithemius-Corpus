He is said to have written many other things as well, but they have not come to my knowledge. As for the fact that he is commonly accused of necromancy, this is frivolous and fictitious, since it is known that he lived most religiously to the end of his life, and it seems incredible that so good and holy a man devoted himself to evil arts. That he is said to have done certain marvellous things, I do not doubt. For many astonishing things can be done naturally, and the hidden matters of men can be subtly uncovered by certain conjectures, and some future things also predicted; all these are judged by the inexperienced to be above nature. This Albert the Great died under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280, in the eighth indiction, in the eightieth year of his age. He was buried in his convent at Cologne, in the middle of the choir, not without a reputation for sanctity. And after him there did not arise in that order, in all letters, sciences, and subjects, a man so learned, educated, and experienced.

PHILIPP, abbot of the monastery of Otterberg of the Cistercian order, a man in the divine scriptures more studiously skilled through long practice, ready in talent, devout in life and conduct. He wrote for the edification of readers some little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported: *On the Song of Songs of Solomon*, one book. Also various sermons through the cycle of the year, on feasts, to the brothers, two books. He is also said to have written several letters to [various persons?]. He composed some other things as well, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1220.

WALTER, called de Castellione, originally from the town of Lille in Flanders, a man devoted to the divine scriptures and learned, and also excellently instructed in secular letters, a most renowned philosopher, poet, and theologian, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, very ready and skilled in verse. He wrote in elegant verse the deeds of Alexander the Great, a work certainly not to be rejected, which in the schools of the grammarians had for a long time been of such authority that the reading of the ancient poets themselves seemed neglected. But he composed many other things, which have not come to me.

THOMAS of Cantimpre, of the order of Friars Preachers, a Brabantine by country, once, as they report, a disciple of Albert the Great, certainly a most learned man, highly educated both in secular philosophy and in the divine scriptures, outstanding in talent, distinguished in eloquence, venerable no less for honesty of conduct than for knowledge. He wrote some little works not to be despised, by which he commended his name to posterity. Among these there is extant a volume no less useful than delightful to all Christians, on the mystical bees, which he entitled *The Universal Good*, one book. He also composed another volume, *On the Nature of Things*, one book. The life of a certain holy woman in Spain, who was called Christina, one book. He also wrote the life of Saint Lutgard, a nun, one book. He published many other things as well, which have not come to my knowledge. There are some who write that he had skill in the Greek language and translated the books of Aristotle which are now used in the schools. He flourished while living at Louvain, in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

GERARD, monk of Saint-Quentin in Lille, a man devoted to the divine scriptures and skilled through constant practice, not ignorant of secular letters either, ready in talent and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote certain little works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to those then living and to posterity. Among these the following are reported first: *The History of the Translation of the Crown of Thorns and of Part of the Cross of the Lord from Constantinople into France*, one book. Also *On the Miracles of Blessed Elizabeth, Done by Divine Grace after Her Death in Thuringia*, one book. He composed the office of chant for the feast of that same saint. He also published many various sermons. But he is said to have written several other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

ULRICH of Strasbourg, of the order of Friars Preachers, once a hearer and disciple of Albert the Great, a man excellently learned both in canon law and in the divine scriptures, ready in talent, scholastic in speech. He wrote many distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with praise to posterity. Among these the following are reported: *On the Sentences*, four books. A very notable *Summa*, six books. Also *On the Soul*, one book. Various sermons as well. He composed many other things too, which have not come to me.
