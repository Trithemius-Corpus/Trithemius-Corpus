*Theodericus Gresemundus the Younger*, of Mainz, born of his father Theodericus Gresemundus of Meschede, a very distinguished doctor of arts and medicine, a youth of excellent talent, who writes much, in verse and prose, has written remarkable little works, most ornate whatever their subject, displaying Ciceronian eloquence. He offers so great a marvel that, like another Adeodatus, once Augustine’s son, by his native ability and readiness in writing and arguing, he is an object of wonder and astonishment to many, even very learned men. For his talent far outruns his age, since there is in him such learning and eloquence together that he has had few or no equals of his own age. He wrote to me a distinguished dialogue on the seven liberal disciplines, in which he defends, explains, and commends these same arts no less elegantly than learnedly, one book. Also a most ornate oration in the name of Philosophy the orator, to the governors of commonwealths. Likewise he composed an elegant dialogue on the madness of the Germans, by which at carnival time children [unclear], one book. Against a brother [unclear] of the Order of Preachers, stupidly attacking the most pure conception of the Mother of God, he wrote a poem, and many letters. He has also produced many poems of different kinds and epigrams. He has also written most ornate letters, both to me and to others, almost countless, from which the liveliness and vein of his most noble talent are gathered. He is still living at Mainz in his father’s house, being about eighteen years of age, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, in the fourteenth indiction. Let no one be angry with me because I have placed this boy, young in age, among illustrious writers, since by his talent he excels many older men; for from the mouth of infants and sucklings the Most High has perfected praise, and we require not age but talent.

He is said to have written some commendable volumes, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity. But I do not remember that any of them have come into our hands, except that very celebrated work which he composed with great labor: On the year, day, and weekday of the Lord’s Passion, in three volumes. The other things which he wrote and writes are wholly unknown to me. He is still living, as I hear, in the University of Louvain, composing various works, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write this, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

*Theodericus of Osenbruck*, of the Order of Friars Minor of the province of Cologne, a man whose talent is exercised in the divine Scriptures, a very famous declaimer of popular sermons, whose life all who have known him proclaim to be holy and most religious. For in making sermons to the people he is illumined by so great a grace from God, speaks with so great an eloquence, and finally affects his hearers with so great a compunction and sweetness, that he has had no equal among the Germans; hence he was appointed general preacher throughout the whole province. Recently, when he was with me at Cologne in the monastery of Saint Martin, he read to me a beautiful little work, suited to devotion: On the Passion of the Lord, [unclear] for simple devout persons, one book; On daily interior exercise, one book; and various sermons. He wrote certain other things unknown to us. He is still living in the province of Cologne, instructing many by word and example in the discipline of God. Under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

He wrote certain well-constructed little works, by which, exercising his talent, he commended the memory of himself to posterity.
