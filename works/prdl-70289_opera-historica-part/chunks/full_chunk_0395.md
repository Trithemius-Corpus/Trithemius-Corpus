*ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

[Thomas Aquinas, continued:] …Many sermons, 1 book; Against William of Saint-Amour, 1 book. By command of Pope Urban IV he composed the office of the feast of Corpus Christi. He commented on all the books of Aristotle and Porphyry. He published also many other [things], as much in the divine Scriptures as in secular philosophy, which have by no means come into our hands. He dies under the emperor [Rudolf], in the year of the Lord 1274, in the 2nd indiction. And he was canonized by Pope John XXII.

THOMAS of Cantimpré, a Brabanter by nation, of the order of Friars Preachers, once (as they report) a disciple of Albert the Great, a devout man, and excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, prompt in talent, scholastic in eloquence. He is reported to have written certain illustrious volumes, by which he brought his name to the notice of posterity. Of these there is extant a work on the mystical bees, which he entitled:
The Universal Good: 2 books. ["To the reverend in Christ…"]
On the nature of things: 1 book. The Life of Saint Christina: 1 book.
And the rest, which I have not seen. He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270. And there are [those] who write that he had skill in the Greek tongue, [and] translated the books of Aristotle which are now in use in the schools.

ALPHONSO, king of Castile [and] León, the most distinguished philosopher [and] astronomer among all of his time, [flourished] in the time of the vacancy of the empire, in the year of the Lord 1170.

[A Dominican,] of the order of Friars Preachers, a Gaul by nation, provincial prior of the same religion in France, a man learned by long study in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, acute in talent, prompt in eloquence, in declaiming sermons to the people [outstanding]; [who], when the brother of the king of England had been elected king of the Romans, [and] was a good [and] prudent man—Richard being dead—in the fifteenth year of his election, for the sake of peace voluntarily ceded before Pope Gregory X, [and] spontaneously renounced all his right which he had had in his election. He wrote very useful [and] elegant [works]:
In Astronomy, Tables: 1 book; Of letters to various [persons]: 1 book.
Sermons on the [seasons of the] year: 1 book; also Sermons on the saints: 1 book.
But he is said to have written some others, which have not come to my knowledge.

PETER of Tarentaise, of the order of Preachers, after Gregory X, pope—canonically consecrated under the name of Innocent V—sat in the supreme pontificate only five months, a man certainly most erudite in the divine Scriptures, nobly learned in Aristotelian philosophy, who taught theology excellently for many years, [and] acquired great praise of his erudition. He wrote many illustrious little works, by which he made his name known to those then present, [and] afterward to those to come. Of these there are extant:
On the Sentences: [—] books; On the Song of Songs: 1 book.
On all the Epistles of Paul: 1 book; And certain others.
He wrote also other lectures on the Bible, [and], as is said, several, which I have not yet seen.

PETER of Russia, of the order of Friars Minor, after Saint Bonaventure, sent to Constantinople by Pope Gregory X, brought back the Greeks to the obedience of the Roman church by his exhortations, [and] persuaded [them] to come with him to the Council of Lyon. He wrote, for the edification of the faithful, [works] not to be despised:
Several letters to various [persons]: 1 book; Sermons on the Saints: 1 book.
He flourished, the empire being vacant, in the year of the Lord 1270. And certain others.

JOHN Esculanus of Ascoli, of the order of Friars Minor, minister general, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures, a distinguished declaimer of sermons, not ignorant of secular literature, since indeed he taught theology excellently for many years, [and] left after him many distinguished disciples. He wrote some illustrious little works, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant, to Haymo the Englishman, minister general of his order, an equally most learned man, a Declaration:
On the Rule of Saint Francis: 1 book; also On the Sentences: [—] books.
Various sermons: 1 book; And certain others.
He flourished, the empire being vacant, in the year of the Lord 1170. [VDAL—]
