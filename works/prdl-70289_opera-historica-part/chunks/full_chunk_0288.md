*Of the Most Commended Man and Reverend Father in Christ, Lord Johannes de Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim. Book on Ecclesiastical Writers.*

Clement, fourth bishop of the holy Roman Church from blessed Peter, as some hold, a Roman by nation, a holy man and beloved of God, adorned also with comeliness of morals, whose life and passion are found in ecclesiastical writings, sat in the pontificate for nine years and ten days, banished into exile for the faith of Christ with steadfast spirit. He died in the third year of Trajan Caesar, and in the one hundred third year of the Lord. This most blessed martyr is said to have written, in the person of the Roman Church, some letters full of faith and learning, of which the principal ones are extant:

To the Church of the Corinthians.
Also to James the Apostle.
Moreover, of the itinerary of his life.
Likewise, of the disputation of Peter.

These last two volumes, however, are counted among the apocrypha, as is clear in distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*. Other letters of his are also reported, but they are apocryphal.

Dionysius the Areopagite, an Athenian, a distinguished philosopher, converted to the faith by blessed Paul the Apostle, was ordained the first bishop of Athens, as some hold, a man of outstanding holiness and incomparable learning. After the death of the Apostles, blessed Clement, bishop of the city of Rome, sent him with Rusticus the priest and Eleutherius the deacon into France to preach the word of God to the nations, another having been ordained bishop in [unclear] place. He wrote many excellent little works in the Greek language. Of these I have found among the Latins only the following:

On the Celestial Hierarchy, one book.
On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy, one book.
On the Divine Names, one book.
On Mystical Theology, one book.
On Symbolic Theology, one book.
To Gaius, four letters.
To Dorotheus the minister, one letter.
To Sosipater the priest, one letter.
To Polycarp, one letter.
To Demophilus, one letter.
To Saint John the Evangelist, one letter.
To Apollophanes the philosopher, one letter.
On the death of Paul, to Timothy, one letter.

Every best gift, etc.
Our priest indeed.
Now already the man of God after.
Of the supersubstantial Trinity.
Saint Timothy indeed.
Darknesses are hidden.
The divine darkness is in.
Do not suppose here [unclear].
I indeed did not know to.
Of the history of the Hebrews.
I greet you, holy soul.
To Apollophanes [unclear].
Farewell, divine disciple.

These are the things that I find among the Latins concerning the writings of blessed Dionysius; if he published anything further, it has remained among the Greeks. But since the aforesaid works were covered with great obscurity, they were made clear by the explanation of the following fathers.
