For this reason he himself devised a book which he calls the Key of Polygraphy, in order to open the door to it and disclose the secrets, which could not be communicated, seen, or recognized except with inestimable labor.

It would be superfluous to recite here the catalogue of his other books; I will only say that he is to be reproved in this: that, being too curious about the black and occult sciences of magic, he wrote in his book entitled Steganography several superstitious things unworthy of an ecclesiastical man. By this means he furnished many, who were seeking no better bread, with matter for mocking monks, saying that the state of a religious man did not consist in recounting such superstitions and astrological movements; adding that he is thought to have penetrated farther and to have had communication with familiar spirits, which I would not wish to approve.

It is quite true that there have been countless students of this science, which is practiced in many parts of the world, among others in Cambaluh, Malacca, Goa, and even in China, a country of the East Indies. Not that I wish to approve the fable received by Regius in the book he made on the vicissitudes of things, where he says that it is not permitted to those of those countries to attain offices and honors in the commonwealth unless they are learned in this science of magic. This was misunderstood by him, since those who use these sciences are only the sacrificers and priests of their idols, and some other beggarly rogues, most of whom are slaves.

For the rest, I will not occupy myself here in giving you to understand [unclear]; I may call him a beacon illuminating his age, in which letters remained buried, and he is one of those who first revived and clarified them. After having had the occupations I have just recounted, having crossed the forest of this life under the harshness and inclemency of the time, with countless labors and false and wholly unjust calumnies by which he was assailed, he died in the year one thousand four hundred ninety-nine, under Pope Alexander VI and while the Emperor Maximilian was reigning. As for others, it would be too tedious to specify them here; I shall be content, among the others, to choose Josse Badius and Jacques Fèvre, who together produced great fruits throughout all Europe, and especially in our France, and who were for the most part companions in labors, affections, and undertakings.
