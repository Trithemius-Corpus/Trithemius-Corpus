Coming into eastern Francia, to Würzburg, a city of the Franks, he devoted himself for some time to the office of holy preaching, and by word and example brought many back to knowledge of the truth. At last, with his aforesaid disciples, he suffered martyrdom through the secret instigation of Gailana, wife of Gosbert, duke of the East Franks, because she feared being separated from her husband, since Saint Kilian reproved him for having as wife her whom his brother had previously had as wife.

In the ninth year of King Theodoric, Ebroin, mayor of the palace in Neustria, since he did not cease cruelly to oppress the Franks by his tyranny, was killed by a certain Ermenfrid, a noble man; this Ermenfrid soon fled to Pippin, who held the principate of the mayoralty of the palace in Austrasia. After the most impious Ebroin had been slain, a certain Waratho was appointed mayor of the palace.

In the following year a battle was fought near Namur between Pippin and Gislemar, son of Waratho. And not long after, Gislemar having been punished by divine agency, and Waratho also having died, his son-in-law Berthar was mayor of the palace. But the Franks, suffering under Berthar’s hostility, sought Pippin’s friendship through hostages, promising aid and loyalty if by war he should wrest the principate from him.

In the twelfth year of his reign King Theodoric and Berthar, his mayor of the palace, declared war on Pippin, who had acted as mayor of the palace in Neustria against their will. But Pippin won in that battle, and having captured King Theodoric, bound him, as he wished, by his own conditions. Berthar, however, was killed by his own men in favor of Pippin. And so Pippin subjected Neustria together with Austrasia to himself; under Theodoric he alone ruled and governed the whole kingdom of the Franks for twenty-three years. He restored Saint Lambert, brought out from the monastery of Stavelot, to the pontificate of the church of Maastricht upon the Meuse, and accomplished many useful things in the kingdom of the Franks.

At that time Saint Willibrord, a monk, came from England into Gallican Francia with twelve companions of the same profession, and lived most holily.

In these times also Saint Wandregisel, who was Wando, grandson through Walgis, his brother, of Ansegis, formerly mayor of the palace of the kingdom of the Franks, built two monasteries of monks under the rule of divine Benedict in Gallican Francia, namely Fécamp and Fontenelle, which is now in the borders of Normandy; in these, many most holy and most learned men afterward became illustrious in the course of time.

Theodoric, king of the Franks, died in the fourteenth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord’s birth 693, in the sixth Roman indiction, leaving two sons, Clovis and Hildebert, who succeeded one another.

*The Fifty-Sixth.*

Clovis, the elder son of the aforementioned King Theodoric, succeeding his father, reigned over the Franks for four years, with Pippin existing as mayor of the palace. In that year the Ewalds, the White and the Black, monks and priests, coming from England into Saxony for love of Christ, were martyred, and were buried at Cologne by order of Prince Pippin.

In the first year of King Clovis, Pippin, mayor of the palace of the Franks, in whose hands all power of the whole kingdom had been placed, made war on the Frisians and defeated their duke Radbod. He also appointed Saint Willibrord, English by nation, of whom we have spoken, as teacher and apostle of the Frisian people.

In these days there flourished in Gallican Francia, from the disciples of the blessed bishop Kilian, previously martyred at Würzburg, Arnuald and Adelan, abbots; Adelmus and Wilfrid, bishops from among the monks, who gave the souls of many to salvation.

Clovis, king of the Franks, died in the fourth year of his reign, which was the year of the Lord’s birth 697, in the tenth Roman indiction.

*The Fifty-Seventh.*

Hildebert, brother of the aforementioned King Clovis and son of the late King Theodoric, reigned alone after his brother for eighteen years. His mayor of the palace was Pippin, son of the younger Ansegis: count in Namur, duke in Brabant, a most noble and energetic man, sprung from the ancient lineage of the kings of the Franks.

In the same year Saint Willibrord the monk was sent by Pippin, mayor of the palace, to Rome with letters; on the feast of Saint Clement he was ordained bishop of the Frisians by Pope Sergius. From the gift of the aforementioned Prince Pippin he established an episcopal seat [unclear]. [The bishopric?] was sent to eastern Francia.
