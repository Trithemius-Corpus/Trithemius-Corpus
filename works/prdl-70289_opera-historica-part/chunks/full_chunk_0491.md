He also compiled, with wonderful art and talent, though in the common and vernacular tongue, a certain little book which he called the Ship of Narragonia. In it he so elegantly expressed the cause and root of all follies, censures the morals of men, and offers certain salutary remedies to each, that he would not rightly have called that work a book of fools, but rather a divine satire. For I do not know whether anything more wholesome or more pleasant could be read for the uses of our age. They say that he is striving greatly so that the same work may come out in Latin as soon as possible, alike in verse and in prose. He is still living at Basel, being 36 years of age, and printers make very great use of his labor and industry in adorning their books. For he writes various things under Maximilian, king of the Romans, whom he loves in a singular way, and has composed many very sweet poems about him and to him, even secret ones, as they say. In the year in which we write these things, 1494.

Jacob Wimpeling of Schlettstadt, a German by nation, priest of the church of Speyer, a man most learned in divine matters and excellently taught in secular letters, a very celebrated theologian, philosopher, orator, and poet, most outstanding among all, first at Erfurt and then in the Heidelberg academy publicly explaining philosophy and sacred scripture, acquired great glory for his learning. A man, I say, most learned from every side, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, excellent both in meter and in prose, he wrote in both kinds certain distinguished little works, by which he spread his name far and wide. Of these the following are extant. To Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, he composed a very celebrated work in hexameter and pentameter verse, most elegantly, which he entitled On the Threefold Whiteness.

He composed many elegant letters, both to me and to others, of which the number is uncertain. At the urging of Ludwig, bishop of Speyer, he composed an office on the compassion of the blessed Mary ever virgin, which is solemnly performed annually throughout that same diocese. To this Jacob we have already long since dedicated the book On the Illustrious Men of Germany. He is living in manly age, being about forty-three years old, in the renowned city of Speyer, and writes various things under Maximilian, king of the Romans. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Baptista de Salis, of the order of Friars Minor of the province of Genoa, a man devoted to and learned in the divine scriptures, and excellently taught both in both laws and in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, devout in life and conduct, and distinguished in observance of the rules of discipline, published certain little works necessary for the instruction of simple priests, by which he spread his name far and wide. Of these there is extant a summa on cases in the forum of conscience, very useful, which, according to the author’s name, is called

The Baptista Summa, book 1. He completed this same summa under Frederick III, emperor. And he is still living while his son Maximilian holds the kingdom. In the year of the Lord 1494.
