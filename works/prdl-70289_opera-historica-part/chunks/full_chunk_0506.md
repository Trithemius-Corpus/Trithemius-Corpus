CHARLES Bovillus, [of Vermandois?], of Amiens, a man polished with rhetorical fluency, poetic variety, and the most glittering splendor of all philosophy, and an eminent geometer. Nature is believed to have brought him forth to investigate the divine secrets of things and the hidden essences of all beings, rather than that, confined in a mortal body, he should lead a human life. For he was endowed with so refined a sharpness of intellect that he not only brings into the light the natures of earthly beings, previously covered in blind darkness, but, with philosophy as his guide, penetrates most deeply the star-bearing spheres and the open structure of the heavens; by the philosophical proportion of worldly things he shows beforehand the natures of supernatural beings, and by philosophical demonstration and the most unified scrutiny of earthly things exactly prefigures the [ineffable?] Trinity, the number, of the heavenly court. He was altogether so devoted to the contemplation of the highest philosophy and to geometrical abstractions that very often, for nine hours, leaning against the trunk of a tree, with unmoving eyes and motionless body, forgetful of himself, his own, and human sustenance, the mass of his body as it were dissolved, he seemed to dwell in the heavens rather than, bound by human ties, to draw vital breaths in this sensible world. By the keenness of his intellect he discovered many things unknown to former ages. For he worked out the quadrature of the circle by his own geometrical devices, which no philosopher before had discovered with sufficient exactness. With great admiration from all, he wrote a book *On Nothing* with wondrous ingenuity, and he left to posterity very many other philosophical works which no mortal in past ages had foreseen.

Six introductory books on Geometry. One book *On the Quadrature of the Circle*. An *Introduction to the Science of Perspective*. A book *On the Divine, Angelic, and Human Intellect*. A book *On Sense*. A book *On Nothing*. *The Art of Opposites*. A book *On Generation*. A book *On the Wise Man*. A book *On the Twelve Numbers*. Several philosophical letters.

GUILHELMUS de Quercu, an eminent doctor of theology, in this our age most distinguished among all other mortals in counsel, morals, and sacred eloquence. When that majesty of the French was pressed by the dreadful assault of the supreme pontiff, the English, and the Spaniards, with the cruel apparatus of wars, he alone, by his counsel and the sound judgment of his mind, most kindly came to meet the straitened necessity of the French. Crossing the Alps, he indeed reached the city of [Pisa?], where, in a very great assembly of cardinals and bishops concerning the French affair, disturbed by so great a prodigy of wars, he strengthened it by the sagacity of his mind alone and claimed for himself the more eminent parts of so great a council. Nor does he use lesser eloquence among the people for the undertaking of divine offices. For he is cultivated with such weight of judgments and knowledge of divine counsels that he easily composes the untamed manners of mortals and barbarous peoples with fluent eloquence, and easily leads them back to the bright recognition of the highest Creator by his kindly voice, pleasing eyes, moderated gesture, and delightful comparisons.

RENATUS Garnerius of Luçon, doctor in both laws, most learned in our age, who, because of his brilliance in every kind of discipline, and especially in the distinguished faculty of laws, has spread his fame and distinction through various regions of the earth, and has deservedly and easily claimed for himself everywhere the roles of Lycurgus and Socrates in establishing and interpreting laws. For by a rather polished eloquence of speech and a tested profession he increased the sagacity of his mind, so that he has adorned the [Pictavian?] territories with perpetual honor and daily ennobles all France by his eminence.

CHRISTOPHER Longolius, a Parisian, a most ingenious philosopher and the most distinguished orator of our century. Just as mother Nature adorned him with the greatest gifts of mind, so she most abundantly endowed him with [unclear] by a most pleasing dexterity. He increased [unclear] nature by labor and exercise, so that he emerged as the most distinguished philosopher and most abundant orator of our age.
