*CHRONICLE OF THE DUKES OF BAVARIA.*

…when, on the vigil of Saint Andrew the Apostle, he had been merry with [his] wife and household, he died a sudden death.

Otto, duke of Bavaria, being dead, his sons Ludwig [and] Heinrich divide their father's princedom between themselves. To Ludwig fell the county Palatine of the Rhine, with the others in upper Bavaria; but to Heinrich fortune gave Landshut, with lower Bavaria. In the year of the Lord 1256.

LUDWIG, count Palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, son of Otto duke of Bavaria, brother of the aforesaid prince Heinrich, first married Maria, daughter of the duke of Brabant, whom in the year of the Lord 1247 he himself killed. Afterward he took Anna, daughter of the king of Poland. He begot three sons: Ludwig; Rudolf the count Palatine; [and] another Ludwig, afterward king of the Romans—of whom we shall speak below in order.

In the year of the Lord 1247, duke Ludwig, for the defense of Bavaria, built the fortress which is called Friedberg, against the bishop of Augsburg, [as] a city. Ludwig, after many [things] excellently accomplished, dies; he is buried in Fürstenfeld, which he himself had built before; after whom his sons rose up, as we shall say in [its] place.

HEINRICH, duke of Bavaria, son of Otto duke of Bavaria, count Palatine of the Rhine, brother of the said Ludwig, had as wife Elizabeth, daughter of Béla king of Hungary, who was brother of Saint Elizabeth the widow; [she] bore him three sons: Otto king of Hungary, Ludwig, [and] Stephen, dukes of Bavaria, of whom we shall make mention in what follows.

In the year of the Lord 1231, Albert, the last count of Bogen, dies, whose county is turned over to the dukes of Bavaria. The aforesaid Heinrich, duke of Bavaria, dies in the year of the Lord 1291; he is buried at Landshut. Others say that he was buried at Burghausen. But he left three sons, of whom we shall speak afterward.

RUDOLF, count Palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, son of Ludwig, whose brother was [Bogen]. Ludwig [afterward] king of the Romans [had a wife] who bore him three sons—Rudolf, Rupert, [and] Adolf—of whom it will be spoken afterward. From this Rudolf descended all the counts Palatine of the Rhine who live today, as will clearly appear from their genealogy in what follows.

When Ludwig, brother of the aforesaid Rudolf, count Palatine, had been elected king of the Romans, Rudolf, bearing this ill, elected Frederick, duke of Austria, as king of the Romans. Which deed the emperor Ludwig took so ill that he expelled him, despoiled of all paternal possession, from [his] country; [and] he, fleeing into England, died there. His sons, however, were admitted by [their] uncle the emperor to the paternal inheritance, as we shall say.

LUDWIG, count Palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, emperor of the Romans, son of the aforesaid prince Ludwig, brother of Rudolf of whom we have already made mention, was made emperor of the Romans in the year of the Lord 1315. He had as wife Margaret, granddaughter—through a daughter—of Philip king of France. For the sister of king Philip had married William, count of Hainaut, who bore William duke of Holland [and] Margaret, wife of this emperor Ludwig, who bore him six sons—namely, Ludwig margrave of Brandenburg; Stephen at Landshut; Albert at Straubing; William duke of Holland, Zeeland, [and] Friesland, of Breda; Ludwig who was called the Roman (because he was born at Rome); [and] Otto of Brandenburg. From this Margaret, therefore, wife of the emperor Ludwig, the duchies of Holland, Zeeland, Friesland, [and] Hainaut, with the other lands pertaining to her, came to the dukes of Bavaria by hereditary right, in the year of the Lord 1324.

Hereafter I shall bring in the sons of Heinrich, duke of Bavaria dwelling at Landshut, son of Otto the duke and brother of Ludwig; I shall unfold the order of their generation. They were three: Otto king of Hungary, Ludwig, [and] Stephen, dukes of Bavaria, as is shown above.

OTTO, duke of Bavaria, king of Hungary, son of Heinrich the duke dwelling at Landshut, brother of Ludwig [and] Stephen, dukes of Bavaria, had as wife a daughter of the duke of Poland, who bore [him] a son by name Heinrich, fourteen days before the death of Otto himself, in the year of the Lord 1309.
