Hermann, who was called Contractus, a monk of the monastery of St. Gall, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, son of the count of Veringen in Swabia, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, most thoroughly instructed in secular letters, a philosopher, poet, astronomer, rhetorician, musician, second to none in his time, and highly skilled in the three languages Greek, Latin, and Arabic. He wrote many distinguished volumes in verse and prose, of which I have found only the following, namely:

*On Music*, one book.
*On the Monochord*, one book. “In considering the mono-.”
*On the Six Ages of the World*, one book. “The first is of this world.”
*On the Construction of the Astrolabe*, one book. “Therefore in measuring under-.”
*On the Usefulness of the Astrolabe*, one book. “Whoever of astronomical-.”
*On Eclipses*, one book.
*On Computation*, one book.
*On the Squaring of the Circle*, one book.
*On the Conflict of Rhythmomachy*, one book. “He who is skilled in arithmetic-.”
*On Physiognomy*, one book. “Of a distinguished prudent man.”
*Lives of Several Saints*, one book.
Various hymns and sequences. He also composed, in sweet and regular melody, most beautiful chants and sequences. Among these is that very sweet one concerning the Blessed Virgin Mary, “Salve regina,” and “Alma redemptoris mater,” and others, of which the number is abundant. He also translated into Latin some volumes of Greek philosophers and Arab astrologers. In his limbs he was, in accordance with his name, contracted, but always persevering in thanksgiving; hence he deserved to be inwardly wonderfully consoled and enlightened by the most blessed Virgin, Mother of God.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Henry II, in the year of the Lord [1040?].

Alphanus, archbishop of Salerno, formerly a monk of the monastery of Monte Cassino, of the order of St. Benedict, a man nobly learned in the divine Scriptures and perfected in secular letters, and also of very celebrated reputation among secular writers. He wrote certain little works [unclear], by which he made known to posterity the name also of profound understanding and his talent [unclear]. Of these there survive:

*On the Union of the Word of God and Man*, one book.

He also composed other volumes in every branch of literature, which have by no means come down to us.
