He is said also to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Heraclonas, son of Heraclius, in the year of the Lord 630, in the times of Sisebut, king of the Goths in Spain; and on account of his most holy life and conduct he was inserted in the catalogue of the saints. His feast is celebrated on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of February.

Caesarius, from a monk of the monastery of Lerins bishop of Arles, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, gentle in mind, sweet and adorned in speech, and distinguished by a wondrous brevity of sentences, most holy in life and conduct, since from his youth he had been trained in monastic disciplines and was for a long time abbot of Lerins, wrote certain excellent little works of no contemptible reading, which are very necessary and useful for monastic and religious persons dedicated to God. Of these there are extant:

On Grace and Free Will, 1 book.
Of Sermons to Monks, 1 book.
The Decree of the Three Councils of Arles, 3 books. I have found nothing concerning the rest.

In the third sermon, which is on the monk's battle against the vices, he acknowledges both himself and those to whom he speaks as professed men and monks of the order and rule of our most holy father Benedict.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Constantine, son of Heraclius, and of his sons, in the year of the Lord 670; under them he gloriously presided over the Synod of Arles.

Audoenus, who is Dado, bishop of Rouen, a Gaul by nation, brother of the monk Saint Ado, a learned and most holy man, founder of the monastery of Rebais, since he was instructed both in the holy Scriptures and also in secular letters, wrote in a most elegant style to Rupert, bishop of Paris:

The Life of Saint Eligius, bishop of Noyon, 3 books. And certain other things.

He was renowned for great holiness, virtues, and signs, in the times of the above-named emperor Constantine, in the year of the Lord 660.

Theodore, seventh archbishop of Canterbury, consecrated by Pope Vitalian, a Greek by nation, from Tarsus of Cilicia, a monk of our order in the city of Rome, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in his time in secular letters, highly skilled in Greek and Latin speech, excellent also in verse and prose, no less renowned for knowledge, after holding a synod in England made many provisions for the benefit of the English Church. Among the other monuments of his talent he wrote an excellent work on the quality of penance to be imposed on those who had sinned in many and enormous ways. The title of this work is:

The Penitential of Theodore, 1 book. Some letters to various persons.

He died, not without a reputation for holiness, under Justinian the Younger, in the year of the Lord 690, in the third indiction, in the seventy-eighth year of his age and the twenty-second of his episcopate.
