*The Abbot of Sponheim*

On the Rule of Saint Benedict, one book. On the Mirror of Monks, one book. On the Signs of Speech, one book. Of the rest I have seen nothing.

Since he was a very celebrated doctor of the decretals, he commented on the Rule of the holy father Benedict in a new and unusual kind of exposition, comparing with pontifical law not only all its chapters, judgments, and teachings, but also, what is the work of greater diligence, its words, counsels, and precepts; with admirable subtlety he confirmed them by the canons of the fathers. He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

JACOBUS of Altavilla, a German by nation, by homeland of [Rinaugia?], of the diocese of Mainz, a most learned professor of sacred letters, nobly educated in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech. He published some notable little works on divine writings, by which he destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have thus far been able to see only an excellent volume.

On the Sentences, one book. Various Questions also, one book.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

JOHANNES of Imenhufen, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, ready in intellect, cautious in knowledge, scholastic in speech, an outstanding teacher no less by example than by word. He wrote certain excellent volumes, of reading not to be despised, by which, magnificently exercising his talent, he also made known to posterity the memory of his name. Of these I have found only:

On the Sentences, one book. Elegant Sermons.

Of other things I have seen nothing. He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year 1360.

PETRUS Berchorius, a Frenchman by nation, from Poitiers by homeland, prior of the monastery of Saint Eligius at Paris, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man most zealous and very learned in divine writings, and excellently learned in secular letters, of outstanding talent in delivering sermons to the people, a philosopher, historian, and distinguished cosmographer. He wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported. To Johannes de Pratis, bishop of Palestrina and cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, he wrote a great and distinguished work, which has now been printed in three volumes, not unuseful to simpler preachers, which they call the Dictionarium, etc.; but he himself wished it to be entitled:

Moral Repertory, one book. To the Reverend [Christopa.?], one book.

Moral Reductory also, one book. “See, since not only in.”

Moral [Inductorium?], one book.

Breviary of the Histories of the Bible, one book. “Considering the history.”

He wrote a Cosmography, one book. Various Sermons.

And many other things. Finally, at the urging of John, king of France, he translated Titus Livy into the French language. And it should be noted that of the three moral works which we assigned the first place, one, which consists of three large volumes, of which one part of the Moral Reductory, embracing morally all the historical books of the Bible, has been printed, begins from Genesis thus: “God in the creation of the world.” There follows his Letters to various persons, small but many treatises. He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1360.

JOHANNES of Tambach, a German by nation, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a man much practiced and learned in divine writings, and also not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect, composed with the brilliance of scholastic eloquence, published certain volumes not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these I have found only the following: a large work not to be rejected against the darts of fortune favoring adverse things, no less useful than pleasant, which he wished to be entitled:

Consolation of Theology, one book. “Whatever things have been written,” one book.

On the Sensible Delights of Paradise, one book. “In the delights of the paradise of God.”

He published Sermons, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under Emperor Charles, in the year of the Lord 1350.
