*248. ABBOT OF SPONHEIM.*

…passing the time of [his] life in the dwelling of the same monastery, he gave all [his] effort to meditating on the Scriptures; and, amid the observance of regular discipline and the daily care of singing in church, he always held [it] sweet either to learn, or to teach, or to write. In the nineteenth year of his age he received the diaconate, in the thirtieth the grade of the presbyterate, each through the ministry of bishop John, Ceolfrid the abbot commanding [it]. [There follows his works-list—On Genesis up to Isaac, 4 books; On Exodus; On Numbers; On Leviticus; On Deuteronomy; On the tabernacle and vessels, 3 books; On Kings; On the building of the temple; On Judges; On Joshua, 2 books; On Paralipomenon, 3 books; thirty questions on Kings, 6 books; On Proverbs; On Ecclesiastes, 3 books; On Jeremiah; On the Song of Songs; On Daniel; On Ezra and Nehemiah, 2 books; On Tobias, 4 books; On Isaiah; On Ezekiel, 12 books; On the twelve minor prophets, 7 books; On Luke, 6 books; On the canonical Epistles, 2 books; On the Acts of the Apostles; the Chronicle of his own monastery, 2 books; On the nature of things and [its] reckoning; Hymns in diverse meter, 2 books; On the holy places, 1 book; Epigrams in heroic meter, 1 book; Lessons of the New Testament; Lessons on the Old Testament; Distinctions on Job; Distinctions on Jeremiah, 2 books; On the canticle of Habakkuk; the Life of Saint Albert the bishop; a Martyrology; On the art of meter; Of letters to various [persons]; On figures; On orthography—rendered as printed]. He published, besides, many others, which have not come to the notice of our reading. His little works, even while he yet lived and was always writing new [ones], were of such great authority that, by the ordinance of the prelates of England, they were publicly read in the churches. And since, in the heading of [his] homilies, they wrote by custom the name of "the venerable author Bede the presbyter"—not being able, while he lived, to call him otherwise—the title attributed once from the beginning has prevailed up to the present day, that he is called Bede the Venerable rather than Saint. For while [he was] living it was not permitted to name [him] otherwise (even if he were a saint). There are [those] who invent other reasons for this title "Venerable"—some from the inscription of [his] epitaph, others dreaming that Bede was blind. But they err, since Bede was neither blind, nor do we know such an epitaph inscribed on his tomb. And indeed, were I not consulting brevity, I would most easily refute these ravings. He dies under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 732, in the 15th indiction, in the 72nd year of his age, the day before the Kalends of June.
