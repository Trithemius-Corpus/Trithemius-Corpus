TERTULLIAN, priest, by homeland a Carthaginian of the province of Africa, his father a proconsular centurion, a man of sharp and vehement talent, most learned both in divine and in secular writings, since indeed he taught rhetoric gloriously at Carthage for many years. He wrote in the Latin language almost countless little works, in which he vigorously crushed the errors of the heretics. The most blessed martyr Cyprian held this man’s writings in such veneration that he let no day pass without reading them. For he was accustomed to say to his secretary, “Give me the master,” meaning Tertullian. At last he is said to have slipped into the error of Montanus; on account of this his little works are counted among the apocrypha. 15, distinction, *Sancta Romana*. ORIGEN, also called Adamantius, priest, an Alexandrian by nationality, son of the martyr Leonides, once a disciple of Clement of Alexandria, was a man of great and excellent talent, so that in zeal and learning he never had an equal. In the tenth year of Severus Pertinax, when a persecution was stirred up against the Christians, and when his father Leonides had been crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ, he was left poor with his widowed mother and six brothers, being about seventeen years old. The treasury had seized all their substance because of confession of Christ, so that they had nothing more from which they might live. And although the number of years was reckoned small in the young man, nevertheless his learning was already wonderful, and the constancy of his Christian faith admirable. For he had determined to fall bravely with his father Leonides for the faith of Christ, if, through his mother’s stratagem, it had not happened that he, destined to be a great teacher for the usefulness of the Church, was preserved. For when early in the morning he had resolved to go to the place of contest to his father, his clothing having been taken away by his mother during the night and the doors of the chamber locked, he was compelled to remain at home. But when he had grown into manhood, he resolved to lead a more ample life, and, announcing the word of God round about him, he walked barefoot for many years. Finally, he never slept on a bed spread with feathers; he entirely abstained from wine and meats, except in infirmity; and he was intent on serving God devotedly with fasts, vigils, prayers, and continual studies and expositions of the divine Scriptures.
