*To the most reverend and most illustrious prince Julius, bishop of Würzburg, duke of Eastern Franconia.*

He to whom the splendid fame of a renowned name befell while he lived and saw the light, him still, after death and a hundred harvests, bright honor follows with undying praise. I speak of great Trithemius, born on the wine-bearing bank of the Moselle, a kind of marvel, because of the hidden arts of his higher intellect, and the godlike knowledge of a thousand matters, which Apollo granted no other mortal to know so equally, or to penetrate to the lofty spirits, with the deity privy to secret things favoring him. But I do not touch the perplexities and winding courses of the Polygraphy, nor the mazes of the Steganography, scarcely to be explained to the sophistical Sphinx if the key is absent. Yet we ourselves have seen this key, and have read the wondrous characters in Hebrew figures.

But let these things be gone. I would commend, duke of Franconia, the plain histories of the very celebrated man; from their recollected books, brought to exactness, behold, today a new edition comes forth into the bright air, under Freher’s guidance. It does not displease you, Julius, that what once pleased the polished intellect of Bibraeus, to whom Trithemius himself dedicated his labors, afterward buried in the church of Saint James as one of heaven’s own. And then would that ancient Mainz, foster-child of the Rhine, or sacred Green Verruca of Pyrcus, born of the Main, would bring forth from papers hidden in shadows that great work on the Frankish people and their renowned kings, whom Hunibald described with truthful pen; even if he is accused of having recorded, in fabulous deeds, some things. Yet it is rash where we suspect that something must be judged of slight credibility; and the smoke of adverse opinion often blinds the eyes.

Let it be recalled, let the ancient enclosed mass of Hunibald’s volumes be brought back from Orcus; what do I wish for more? And, Trithemius, O how great a favor you will afterward enter into among all learned men, and likewise among all Franks throughout the recesses of the eastern boundary and the western, greatest priest of the Rhine, and no lesser priest of the Main, if you readily communicate aid to one another with the graphic promise of Freher, eager thereafter to publish many more things, so that the glory of each learned age may be abundantly enriched, and the storehouse of history increased. For your lecterns in the twofold cave abound in writings, just as Bamberg too is filled with excellent treasures, and with very many a codex throughout its shelves.

Paulus Melissus Francus,
Count Palatine and golden knight,
Roman citizen.
