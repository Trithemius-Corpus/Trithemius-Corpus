*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Sermons on the saints, one book. In the beginning God created.

Letters and various questions. The rest that he composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

Jacobus de Gruytrode, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of All the Apostles near Liege, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, outstanding in talent, sweet in speech. He wrote certain distinguished, devout little works, the reading of which is said not to be unprofitable for religious persons, lovers of their own salvation. Of these the following are reported.

On the True Virtues, three books. On the Seven Deadly Sins, one book.

On the Four Last Things, one book.

On the Life of the Lord Jesus, one book. A bundle of myrrh, beloved.

On Preparation before Mass, one book. On the Definition of the Name Monk, one book.

Meditations on the Passion of the Lord, one book.

A Lectionary by Month, one book. Eat, friends, drink.

Mirror of Prelates, one book. Mirror of Subjects, one book.

Mirror of Priests, one book. Mirror of Secular Men, one book.

Mirror of Old Age, one book. Colloquy of Jesus and the Monk, one book.

Colloquy of Jesus and the Prelate, one book. Deal with me, sweetest Jesus.

Colloquy of Jesus and the Old Man, one book. Do not be angry, I pray.

Colloquy of Jesus and the Hermit, one book. Colloquy of Jesus and the Disciple, one book.

Contemplation of Saint Mary, one book. You have wounded my heart.

Little Crown of Saint Mary.

Dialogue of the Crucified One and the Sinner, one book. Forgive, I pray, most gracious one.

Dialogue of Mary and the Sinner, one book. I have a word for you.

Another Dialogue of Mary and the Sinner.

On the Name of Jesus and Mary, one book. Meditations on the Compassion of Mary, one book.

Sermons on the Season, one book. Sermons on the Pontiffs.

Sermons on the Saints, one book. Letters to Various Persons, one book.

And many other things. He died most devoutly under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1472, in the fifth indiction, on the day before the Ides of February.

Jacobus Zenus, a Venetian patrician, bishop of the church of Padua, a man zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent, eloquent, ready and circumspect in counsel, and distinguished by experience of many things. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory. Of these there is extant a most elegant work:

On the Deeds of the Supreme Pontiffs, one book. And certain other things.

He died, however, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1477, and in the ninth indiction.

Guilhelmus Forleon, a Breton by nation, of the order of Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently taught in secular philosophy, renowned in talent, scholastic in eloquence, who in the Paris gymnasium, by teaching, disputing, and writing, earned great praise for his learning, published certain distinguished little works on the Scriptures. Of these there is extant a very celebrated work:

On the Sentences, one book. Various Questions.

And several other things, one book.

His former hearer and disciple was the most learned man, easily the foremost of the theologians of our age, Master Stephanus, called Brulefer, a Frenchman by nation, of the same order, who taught theology in the Paris convent; afterward, betaking himself to the observance of the order at Mainz in Germany, he began to lecture there on the Sentences, which he continued in the convent of Metz with great praise. This Guilhelmus, however, came to an end under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.
