Under the name of Peregrinus he published many excellent volumes, in which the adorned arrangement of the thoughts and the pleasing elegance of the language show that their author was inferior to none of the ancients. Among these the following are reported: a notable work of dialogues under the names of Peregrinus the priest and Theodota, a virgin consecrated to God, which he entitled *The Mirror of Virgins*, 8 books; on the Gospels through the cycle of the year, a large volume, 1 book; *On the Life of the Spirit, the Fruit of Death*, 1 book; and another which is entitled *The Matricularius*, 1 book; another, *Didascalon*, 1 book; *On Music, the Difference of Tones*, 1 book; *On the Praises of Saint Benedict*, in verse, 1 book; various sermons, homilies, 1 book. He is also said to have written some other works, but they have not come into our hands. Whatever he wrote in prose, however, apart from sermons and letters, he arranged for the most part in the manner of a dialogue. There was another preacher who, according to the custom of the moderns, composed sermons to the people under the name of Peregrinus, not to be despised, but much inferior to our Peregrinus in learning and eloquence. For that man, in the manner of the declaimers of this time, content with simple speech, introduces frequent quotations from the Scriptures; but our author, echoing Ciceronian eloquence in the manner of the ancients, was not accustomed to make his discourse scarred with superfluous citations. He flourished under the emperor Conrad, the third of that name, in the year of the Lord 1140.

*Dudechinus*, priest of the church in Lobenstein, a studious and learned man, for the hope of eternal life went with many others to Jerusalem under the emperor Conrad III. Often fighting with the Saracens in naval battle, he shone forth with great devotion and steadfastness for the faith of Christ. And he published some things on various matters to different persons, and certain other things unknown to us. He flourished under the emperor Conrad, in the year of the Lord 1140.

*Christian*, archchancellor of the emperor Frederick I, afterward the thirtieth archbishop of Mainz, appointed by him, sat for eighteen years. He was a man most learned in secular literature, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, brilliant in talent, eloquent in speech; even Italy was amazed at him when he spoke. Among other monuments of his ability he wrote: *The Deeds of the Emperor Frederick I*, 1 book; also very elegant letters to various persons, and certain other things still unknown to us. Leo Tuscus, notary of Emmanuel, emperor of the Greeks, and a contemporary of this Christian, mentions him with praise in the book *On the Prevarications and Errors of the Greeks*. He flourished in the times of the same Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1180.

*Eckebert*, second abbot of the monastery of Saint Florin at Schoenau, of the order of the divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, was the brother of the blessed Elisabeth, abbess and virgin of Christ. He wrote: on the *Magnificat*, 1 book; on the life and death of his holy sister, 1 book; *Meditations on Jesus and Mary*, 1 book; *On the Praise of Our Savior*, 1 book; also sermons, in the manner of the ancients, uncorrupted by citations of chapters, 2 books; letters to various persons, 1 book. He also published some other works which have not come into our hands. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1160.

*Elisabeth*, a nun, sister of the aforesaid abbot Eckebert, abbess of the convent of virgins of Christ which likewise is called Schoenau, is seen to be almost adjacent to the aforesaid monastery of men. A virgin of most holy manner of life, consecrated to the Lord from her infancy, she deserved to be divinely illuminated by many approved revelations, which, for the edification of the faithful, she duly committed to writing. Of these the following are read: she wrote a very beautiful work, namely, on martyrs, confessors, and virgins, 1 book; on the eleven thousand virgins crowned with holy martyrdom at Cologne Agrippina, 1 book; of her revelations, 2 books; to her brother Eckebert, still a canon at Bonn, 1 book; of letters to various persons, 1 book; to Saint Hildegard, virgin of Christ, one letter.
