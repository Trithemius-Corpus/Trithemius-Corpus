Antonius Cornazanus, a native of Ferrara, a most brilliant orator and poet, so polished, copious, and pleasing, especially in poetry, that many suppose he received poetry from the mouths of angels. For among other works he sang of the life and death of the Virgin Mother of God so devoutly, piously, copiously, brilliantly, and holily that all proclaim his poetry not mortal but altogether divine. He inscribed and dedicated that work to Hippolita, queen of the Neapolitans, while she was still a maiden.

He published certain elegies and various poems.

He wrote in prose the life of Bartholomaeus Coloneus, the greatest commander of the Venetians.

Commentaries on Juvenal. On Statius and many others.

He published certain centuries. Many books of letters.

He died at Milan, in the year of the Lord 1493.

Georgius Merula, a native of Alessandria, an orator and philosopher most learned in both languages, always served literature well by teaching and writing. He wrote, in many volumes, the deeds of Ludovicus Sporcia and of his ancestors.

Commentaries on Juvenal, book 1.

Bernardinus Gadolu, a Brescian, Camaldolese abbot, a man especially learned in good letters, philosophy, and canon law, renowned for holiness of life and religion, left us many monuments of his learning, in which he shows himself most learned.

He wrote commentaries on all the books of the Bible.

Rogerus Sanutus, a Venetian patrician, was not only a man learned in good letters, but also skilled in the administration of the commonwealth; he excelled greatly in eloquence, as can easily be recognized from his little works. For he wrote the following.

On the Magistrates of Venice, book 1. On the Accents of the Latins, book 1.

On the Gallic War, certain books, in Latin and in the vernacular.

He wrote certain other things.

Many epigrams to various persons.

Very many letters, both in prose and in verse.
