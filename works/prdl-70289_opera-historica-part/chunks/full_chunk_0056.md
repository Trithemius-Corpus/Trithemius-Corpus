*The Thirty-Second.*

*Clodius reigned 27 years. War occurs between the Romans and the Alemanni.*

Clodius, the elder son of King Barther, succeeding his father, reigned over the Franks for twenty-seven years. In his third year the Romans, sending legions and mindful of the earlier injury, attacked Alemannia in war. Against their incursion the Alemanni advanced armed, having in their army thirty thousand fighters. A very hard battle was joined not far from Lake Benacus, in which almost fifteen thousand of the Alemanni were killed.

*The Thirty-Third.*

*Great disturbances in Britain. The tenth persecution of the Christians occurred. Walther reigned 8 years. Severus. Churches and divine books they burn with fire, and the Christians in the tenth persecution after Nero...*

Walther, son of King Clodius, after his father reigned over the Franks for eight years; a magnanimous, brave, and warlike prince. In the time of his reign, when persecution had been proclaimed everywhere, Diocletian in the east and Maximian in the west overthrow the churches of Christ, burn the divine books with fire, and miserably afflict the Christians in the tenth persecution after Nero; and Diocletian, in the first year of the aforesaid King Walther, commanded himself to be worshipped as God; he also at the same time subjected Alexandria with all Egypt to the Romans.

In the first, second, and third year of Walther king of the Franks, there were great disturbances in Britain, which is now called England. For, some years before, King Lucius had died, who first received the faith of Christ with his people from Pope Eleutherius, as we said above.

*The Thirty-Third.*

In the one hundred eighty-fifth year, in the times of Farabert king of the Franks, the Romans sent Severus the senator into Britain to take up the kingdom. When he too had been killed at York by Fulgenius, Bassianus, the son of this same Severus, was admitted as king. When he also was killed, Carausius seized the kingdom, and despised the commands of the Romans.
