*Chronicle of the Dukes of Bavaria.*

... Rudolf, duke of Bavaria, having been reduced to a count, he himself took up the duchy of Bavaria. And thus the duchy of Bavaria remained with the Saxons until Henry the Third, emperor; under him, when the stock of this Henry failed, the duchy reverted to the nephews of the aforesaid count Rudolf, as we shall say in its place. This duke Henry had as wife Lucardis, daughter of Arnold, duke of Bavaria, sister of the aforesaid count Rudolf; she bore him two sons, Bruno, margrave of Saxony, and Henry, later duke of Bavaria, and one daughter named Gerburg, abbess in Gandersheim.

And since we have said that all the modern dukes of Bavaria descended from the stock of Charlemagne, we must continue the line of descent from Count Rudolf, though the dukes of the Saxons were interposed for some years, until the duchy of Bavaria returned to the nephews of that Rudolf, so that the truth of what has been said may shine more clearly for all.

Rudolf, from duke become count of Bavaria, together with his brothers, pursued Duke Henry, the husband of his sister, as an invader of his paternal inheritance; he besieged and took Regensburg, and drove out Henry’s wife with her sons. Note that, when Arnold, duke of Bavaria, had died, Emperor Otto I had entrusted it to Berthold, his brother; and when he too had died, he appointed his own brother Henry duke, as has been said.

Welf, count of Bavaria, son of the aforesaid count Rudolf, had as wife Adelaide, daughter of the count of lower Baden; she bore him a son named Conrad, whose son Welf again obtained the duchy of Bavaria, as we shall say later.

*Henry.* Henry, duke of Bavaria, son of the aforesaid Duke Henry the Saxon, brother of Otto I, succeeding his father in the duchy of Bavaria, had as wife Bertha, daughter of the duke of Lotharingia; she bore him three sons and two daughters: two Henrys, of whom the elder was later emperor; Bruno, bishop of Augsburg; Gisela, wife of Saint Stephen, king of Hungary, who was converted to the faith of Christ; and Hedwig, whom Lothar, burgrave of Magdeburg, took as wife.

Saint Henry, emperor and duke of Bavaria, son of the aforesaid Henry, duke of Bavaria, after Otto III had died without children, was made emperor of the Romans by election of the princes: a vigorous, devout, and most holy man, who did many good things in the commonwealth. He had as wife Cunegund, daughter of Sigfrid, count palatine of the Rhine, most holy in conduct and life; both remained in holiness until death through love of Christ, and both were also enrolled in the catalogue of the saints.

*Trube. Valey.* When at length all the counts of Trube-Valey had died, their county [unclear]. Dachau, Andechs, Wittelsbach, Bogen, Kastl, Amertal, Scheyern. In the course of succession there were many counts in these places; to recount them all by name would be too lengthy and difficult.

When the count of Dachau had died, those three counties devolved upon the counts of Scheyern. The counts palatine of Wittelsbach were few; and when they too at length failed, the county in Wittelsbach also devolved upon Scheyern.

Henry, emperor from duke of Bavaria, among the many good things that he did, established the bishopric of Hamburg, and constructed the monastery of Monks’ Mountain of the Order of Saint Benedict [unclear]; Saint Cunegund, his most chaste wife, with help from her own patrimony, [unclear] with an army returns to her father.

In the year of the Lord 1005, Henry, the Bavarian emperor, entered Italy; in the siege of a certain castle he was captured, but, with the Lord’s help, was freed, lowering himself down through the wall.

Since the region of Bavaria is very broad, it was subject, at intervals of time, to different dukes at different times: sometimes to one, sometimes to two, and at times even to several.
