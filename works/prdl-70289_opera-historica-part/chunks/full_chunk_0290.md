These are the writings of Seneca that I have been able to find up to the present. He is said, however, to have written some other little works, which have not yet come into our hands. He was indeed a most ardent lover of wisdom, a zealous investigator of it, and of so tenacious a memory that he immediately recited from memory two thousand names read in his presence. Moreover, when a certain poet had produced two hundred verses and was reciting them in order before Nero, gathered together on a sheet, Seneca at once, using his memory in place of a volume, repeated those same verses one by one in reverse order, beginning from the last, back to the first. He is also read to have held in memory the names of all his fellow citizens. He flourished at Rome in the times of the apostle Paul under the emperor Nero, in the year of the Lord 50.

Marcellus, son of Marcus, prefect of the city of Rome, once a disciple of Simon Magus, sufficiently instructed in secular letters, converted to the faith of Jesus Christ by the preaching of blessed Peter the Apostle, composed many things for the usefulness of the Church of God. For he wrote, at the request of Christians:

On the conflict of Peter and Simon, one book.
To the saints Nereus and Achilleus, one book.
On the acts of Peter and Paul, one book.
On the arts of Simon, one book.
On the death of Saint Petronella, one book.
On the passion of Saint Felicula, one book.
On the passion of Saint Nicomedes, one book.

He also published other works concerning the deeds of the saints, which have not come to our knowledge. He flourished at Rome in the times of Saint Peter the Apostle, in the year of the Lord 50.

Linus I, after blessed Peter bishop of the city of Rome, a Tuscan by nationality, a man of most holy conduct, shining with many miracles in life and after death, sat in the pontificate eleven years, three months, twelve days. Since he was a most learned man, beloved of God, he wrote in the Greek language concerning the holy apostles Peter and Paul, who suffered on one day:

The martyrdom, one book.
And certain letters.

At length he was punished with capital sentence by the prefect Saturnius, whose daughter he had freed from a demon, and was made a martyr, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of October. He flourished at Rome under the emperor Galba, in the year of the Lord 70.
