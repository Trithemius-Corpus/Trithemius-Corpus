Jodocus Gallus Rubiacensis, a man zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures, and excellently instructed in secular letters, a theologian and orator, distinguished in talent, eloquent in speech, excelling in verse and prose, diligently teaching the youth of the Heidelberg academy in humane letters. He is said to have composed many treatises still unknown to us, by which he commended his memory to posterity. There are extant several of his most ornate orations to the university and clergy, of which I have found only: an oration on Saint Catherine; an oration on the Purification of the holy Virgin Mother; an oration on the Nativity of the Lord; an oration on the Annunciation of the Lord; two orations to the Synod of Speyer; an oration on Saint John the Baptist; two orations on the Holy Spirit; an oration on Epiphany; a dialogue, in the manner of an oration, on the Purification; an oration to the clergy of Worms, delivered by another; two dialogues on those to be promoted to the mastership; several orations for bachelors; and many other most ornate works. He is still living in the Heidelberg academy, having passed 35 years of age, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Eitelwolf von Stein, a Swabian by country, a noble man and most learned in humane letters, especially in philosophy and oratory, subtle in talent, and a very great lover and emulator of Roman eloquence, who, because of his learning and natural wisdom, is held in no small esteem by the Margrave of Brandenburg. He wrote to Georg von Stein, his maternal uncle, a lover of letters as much as he himself, *On the Praises of Heroes and Illustrious Men*, 1 book. Also most ornate letters to various persons, 1 book. And many other things unknown to us. He was once a pupil of Master Craftho, the excellent and most watchful instructor of the youth of Schlettstadt, who is himself also said to have composed some works that are unknown to us. He is still living, steadfastly persisting in the study he has begun, and writes various things. Under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Johannes, from the town of Brussels in Brabant, of the order of the regular canons of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of Saint Agnes near Zwolle, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not unacquainted with secular literature, distinguished in talent and polished in speech. He wrote a large and not unuseful volume for the instruction of all Christians, which he entitled *Rose-Garden of Spiritual Exercises and Sacred Meditations*, in 2 books. Likewise *On the Illustrious Men of His Order*, 1 book. And certain other things unknown to us. He is still living, as I understand, a young man in the town [unclear], companion of the confessor of the nuns, and writes various things, under Maximilian, most excellent King of the Romans. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Johannes Tinctoris, a Brabanter by country, from the city of Nivelles, and a canon in the church of that same city, doctor of both laws, formerly arch-chaplain and singer of King Ferdinand of Naples, most learned in every respect, a very great mathematician, an outstanding musician, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, wrote and writes many excellent little works, by which he renders himself useful to those now living and memorable to posterity. Of these I have found only: in music, *On the Art of Counterpoint*, 3 books. Likewise *On the Tones*, 1 book. Also *On the Origin of Music*, 1 book. He gave several most ornate letters to various persons; he depicted one figure in which he included all the most ancient musicians, and called Jesus Christ the supreme singer. He is still living in Italy, writing various things, being about 60 years of age. Under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 15.
