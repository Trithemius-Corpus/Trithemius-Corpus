Moving from there to Wesfaram he pitched camp. There the Angrarians and Eastphalians gave hostages and swore oaths; when these things had been done, the king returned to Worms.

In the year of the Lord 780, King Charles now for the sixth time moved into Saxony. As he came, all the Saxons of the eastern parts met him, and many of the people were baptized. Coming from there to the place where the Ohre and the Elbe flow together, the king set in order both the Saxons and the Slavs; and, having returned into Francia and held counsel, he set out for Rome for the sake of prayer with his wife Hildegard and his children, and celebrated the Nativity of the year at Pavia. 800.

In this year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the ninth indiction, Charles, king of the Franks, son of King Pippin, after he had reigned thirty-three years, in the fifty-ninth year of his age, was made emperor of the Romans and ruled as emperor for thirteen years. For when he was at Rome because of the charge made too wrongfully against Pope Leo III, and was celebrating the Lord’s Nativity there, on that most sacred day itself, coming to the solemnities of the Mass, while before the confession of the blessed Apostle Peter, after prayer, he was thinking of, not to say hoping for, nothing less than the empire, Leo, adorned with the papal insignia, placed a crown upon his head and proclaimed him emperor of the Romans.

At once it was shouted by the whole Roman people: “To Charles Augustus, crowned by God, great and peace-bringing emperor of the Romans, life and victory.” After these acclamations, he was anointed by the apostolic hand and, in the manner of the ancient princes, adored by the pope; and, the title of patrician, with which he had previously been distinguished, having been removed, he was called Emperor and Augustus by the favor of many nations.

In the year of the Lord 804, Emperor Charles wintered at Aachen. But in summer, having led an army into Saxony, he transferred into Francia all the Saxons who lived beyond the river Elbe, and granted the districts beyond the Elbe to be inhabited and cultivated by the Obodrite peoples under an annual tribute.

In the year of the Lord 805, the emperor sent his son Charles with an army into Bohemia; after laying everything waste, he killed the duke of that land, named Lecho, and made his duchy subject to the kingdom of the Franks.

In the year of the Lord 811, the elder son of Emperor Charles, himself also called by his father’s name Charles, died on the day before the Nones of December.

In the year of the Lord 813, Emperor Charles, after holding a general assembly of the princes at Aachen, called to himself his son Louis, king of Aquitaine, and, placing the crown upon him, made him his partner in the imperial name. But Bernard, his grandson, the son of his son Pippin, he appointed king of Italy.

In the same year the bridge over the Rhine at Mainz, which the emperor had constructed of wood over ten years with immense labor and marvelous workmanship, so that it seemed able to last forever, in the month of May was so consumed in three hours by an accidental fire that, apart from what was covered by the water, not even one little splinter of it remained. Many councils of bishops were held in the same year by order of the emperor.

In the year of the Lord’s Nativity 814, in the eighth indiction of the Romans, on the twenty-eighth day of the month of January, Charles, surnamed the Great, king of the Franks and ever-august emperor of the Romans, died at Aachen, and there in the church of blessed Mary ever virgin, which he himself had built, was honorably buried, in the seventy-second year of his age, the forty-sixth of his reign over the Franks, and the thirteenth of his empire; he added no fewer provinces to the kingdom of the Franks than it had had before.

*Louis king and emperor, son of Charles, 26 years.*

Louis, according to the custom of the Sicambrians, who change L into V, a count in East Francia, with the consent of Saint Burchard, bishop of Würzburg, built the monastery of Schwarzach, not far from the Main, with the help of his wife Imma; he caused its church to be consecrated in honor of our Lord Jesus Christ, and also in memory of the holy Mother of God and of the holy martyrs Sebastian and Felicity, and established monks of the order of the divine father Benedict there.
