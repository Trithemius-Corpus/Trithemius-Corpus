*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Many homilies to the people, one book.
To Bishop Flavian against Eutyches, one book.
To Emperor Leo against Eutyches, one book.
To Bishop Turribius against Priscillian, one book.
To Rusticus, bishop of Narbonne, one book.
To Anastasius, bishop of Thessalonica, one book.
Many letters to various persons, one book.

Of the rest I have found nothing. At last he died, after many labors for the benefit of the Church, under Emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 461, in the fourteenth indiction, on the fourth day before the Kalends of July.

Peter, bishop of Ravenna, a learned and most holy man, shining in life with many miracles, was of outstanding ability in delivering homilies to the people, and converted many, both by word and by example, to the path of truth. He wrote many things for the edification of the faithful. Of these there are current:

Several sermons and homilies, one book.
To Eutyches the presbyter, one letter: “Sad, I read sad letters.”
He also wrote several other letters.

He flourished under Emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Flavian, bishop of Constantinople, to whom blessed Pope Leo wrote many letters, was indeed a learned man and an especially devoted lover of Catholic unity. He deposed and excommunicated Eutyches, presbyter and archimandrite, who held impious views concerning Christ’s humanity, as he testifies in a certain letter sent to the aforementioned Pope Leo. He accomplished and endured much for the extirpation of heresies. He wrote in Greek certain works not to be despised. Of these there are current:

Proceedings with Eutyches the presbyter, one book.
Against the errors of the same man, one book.
To Pope Leo concerning the same matter, one letter: “No thing of the devil [unclear].”
Letters to various persons, one book.
And some other things which we do not know.

He flourished under Theodosius the Younger and Marcian, in the year 454.

Hilary, pope, a Sardinian by nation, sat in the pontificate after Leo the Great for six years and as many months. He was a man most learned in the divine scriptures and distinguished by the adornment of many virtues; being continually devoted to almsgiving and holy works, he shone with no small reputation for sanctity. At Rome, after holding a synod of fifty bishops, he established certain measures for ecclesiastical propriety. He also wrote certain necessary works. Of these I have found only the following:

A synodal decree, one book: “Since the more religious Holy Spirit.”
To the bishops of the province of Tarragona, one letter: “After your letters.”
To Ascanius, bishop of Tarragona, one letter: “The grace of God toward us.”
Concerning the Council of Nicaea, one letter.
Concerning the Council of Ephesus, one letter.
Concerning the Council of Chalcedon, one letter.
Letters to various persons, one book.

He died under Emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 468, in the sixth indiction, on the ninth day before the Kalends of March; Simplicius succeeded him.

Eusebius, bishop of Milan, very learned in the holy scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, was both familiar and dear to the most blessed pontiff Leo. Fighting and writing bravely for the holy Catholic faith against heretics, he is said to have published some works for the edification of the faithful, but they have not come to my knowledge. For I have seen only one letter of his, which he is said to have written in matters of faith:

To Saint Pope Leo, one letter: “Having returned, with the Lord granting it.”

He flourished under Emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Asclepius the African, bishop of the territory of Vaga, a studious and learned man:

Innumerable homilies to the people, one book.
Many homilies unknown to us.
And many other things unknown to us.

He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Prosper, bishop of Reggio after Faventius, an Aquitanian by nation, a man in [unclear], one book.
