Rudolphus Langius, German by nation, canon of the church of Münster in Westphalia, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and, in secular letters, a most learned philosopher, orator, and very celebrated poet, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote many distinguished little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he spread his name far and wide with immense glory. Of these the following are extant. To Hermann Langius, dean of the same church: *On the City of Jerusalem*, 2 books, “A certain most splendid [unclear].” To the dean of Cologne, *Poems*, 1 book, “Clear virtue, whom [unclear].” *On the Siege of the City of Neuss*, 1 book. *A Poem on the Three Magi*, 1 book. Several letters to various persons. Various poems also. And many other works.

He is still living, very old, in the city of Münster, composing many works under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Sebastianus Murrho of Colmar, German by nation, priest and canon in Colmar, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and most learned in secular letters, an outstanding philosopher, orator, poet, and cosmographer, very skilled in three languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, celebrated for his knowledge of laws and canons, second to none in architecture, music, and the whole quadrivium, wrote, among other things, to Jacob Wimpfeling of Sélestat, whom we mentioned above, a brief but very beautiful work: *Epitome on the Praises of the Germans*, 1 book. *On the Works of Baptista Mantuanus*, 1 book. And certain other works.

He is living today in Colmar, where he is a canon, and is composing various works which we hope will be published day by day, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Carolus Phernandus, German by nation, born at Bruges, a town of Flanders, blind from earliest childhood, and for that reason also wholly ignorant of the elements, presents so great a marvel of himself that, like another Homer, he became a very celebrated poet, musician, philosopher, and orator, and also a most sagacious interpreter of the divine Scriptures, excellent in verse and prose, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, and most upright in life and conduct. Having been granted a public and perpetual stipend by the king of the French, he taught humane letters with distinction for a long time in the celebrated college of Paris, and explained many authors most beautifully. At length, despising everything, he abandoned the world together with his stipends, and about two years ago entered the order of our holy father Benedict, in a well-reformed monastery called Casale Benedictum, situated in a very dense wood about ten miles distant from the city of Bourges. There he perseveres to this day, burning with great zeal for souls, so much so that, ordained a Levite by apostolic dispensation, he publicly preaches the word of God with the greatest profit and admiration of all. He wrote many very distinguished volumes, both in verse and in prose, by which he commended his name to posterity. Against the metrical contestation of Robert Gaguin on the most pure conception of the holy Mother of God and Virgin Mary, against Vincentius de Castronovo of the Order of Preachers, a most elegant work: *Commentaries*, 1 book, “We do not doubt from the Apostle.” *Praises of the Order of Carmelites*, 1 book, “While the Parthian turns his weapons.” *Odes in Praise of Christ*, 4 books. *On the Conception against Vincentius*, 1 book. *Iambic Poem on the Same*, 1 book, “How much he surpasses created things.” *Oration on the Conception to the Carthusians*, 1 book. *Oration on Saint Catherine*, 1 book. *Many Letters*, 2 books. Almost innumerable poems, 1 book. *Elegies on Contempt of the World*, 1 book. And many other works.

He is living today, blind but most learned, in the aforesaid monastery, composing many and varied works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494, indiction 12.
