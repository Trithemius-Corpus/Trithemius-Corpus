Of these I have found only the following: a great volume, a distinguished work, on the life and teaching of our Lord Jesus Christ, which, drawing from the Gospel, he entitled *The Life of Jesus*, in two books; likewise, on the whole Psalter, a very celebrated work, one book. He is also said to have written several other things, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Simon of Speyer, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior of Germany, of a certain [unclear] church, a man most learned in divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, who taught theology with distinction for a long time in the school of Cologne, acquired great glory for his learning. He wrote *On the Sentences*, four books; *Against the Jews*, one book; also various postils on the Scriptures, which he read at Cologne; also sermons not to be rejected, one book. He also published several other things which I have not yet been able to see. He died at Cologne, buried in his own convent, in the year of the Lord 1340, in the eighth indiction, on 6 January, under Pope Benedict XII.

John of Saxony, of the order of Friars Minor, studiously learned in holy Scriptures, excellently instructed both in canon law and in Peripatetic philosophy, and a man of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote several distinguished volumes, by which he commended his name to the memory and knowledge of posterity. Of these there is extant a notable summa on cases of conscience, not useless to hearers of confessions, one book; also sermons for the seasons and for the saints, two books; but certain other works are unknown to us. He flourished under Louis the Bavarian in the year of the Lord 1340.

John Eligerus of Gondersleuen, formerly a hearer of lord John of Saxony, the very distinguished astronomer, a man learned in holy Scriptures, the most celebrated among all astronomers in his time, a philosopher and astronomer [unclear] among all, and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote volumes in astronomy not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported: *On the Construction of the Astrolabe*, one book; *On the Usefulness of the Astrolabe*, one book; *On the Usefulness of the Quadrant*, one book; also *On the Magnet*, two books; *On the Astrogemeter*, one book; and certain other works unknown to us. He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John de Beka, cleric of the Church of Utrecht, a man learned in holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters. He published certain little works of his talent not to be despised; of these I have seen only [unclear], a volume bringing [unclear] to the knowledge of John, bishop of the Church of Utrecht, and William, duke of Holland, concerning the deeds of the bishops of Utrecht and of the counts and dukes of Holland, beginning from the time of Willibrord, the first bishop, down to the year of the Lord 1346. Concerning the rest I have seen nothing; they also have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1348.

Thomas of Argentina, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of [unclear], [unclear] of Rimini, prior general of the whole order, a learned and erudite man, a preacher and a distinguished interpreter of secular philosophy, notable in life and conduct, since he vigorously governed his order by word and example. He wrote certain excellent volumes in divine Scriptures, by which he transmitted his memory with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is extant a very celebrated work on the *Sentences* of Peter Lombard, four books; *Constitutions of the Order*, one book; also various sermons to the people, two books. But he is said to have written certain other things also, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1366, in the third indiction, on 10 February, under Charles IV, emperor.
