*Ecclesiastical Description*

Peter of Blois, archdeacon of Bath, the close friend of the above-mentioned bishop John of Chartres, to whom he dictated some letters, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally instructed in secular letters, acute and forceful in talent, who knew not how to flatter the vices of princes and prelates but to rebuke them, held in esteem among kings, princes, and bishops for his learning and the uprightness of his life, wrote many little works. Of these the following are reported:

*On Christian Friendship*, 1 book. “For they have been diminished.”

*On Salutary Penitence*, 1 book. “You asked me that for you [unclear].”

1 book. “Who will give to my head...”

*On Hastening the Passage*, 1 book. “Your excessive [austerity?] of severity...”

1 book. “You are divided [unclear].”

*On the Quality of Penitence*, 1 book. “Your little exhortation.”

1 book. *Against the Depraver*, 2 books. “You have been chosen by your own.”

*Exhortation to an Abbot*, 2 books.

*On the Office of a Bishop*, 1 book. “You ask more urgently, reverend father.”

1 book. “Whenever I recall the words.”

1 book. “I give thanks to your grace.”

*Letters*, 194 in number, 1 book. “Asked by you, [unclear].”

2 books. He wrote many other works.

*On the Danger of Prelates*, 2 books.

*On the Life of Court Clerics*.

*On the Deceptions of Fortune*.

He flourished under Frederick the First, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1160.

Alexander, the third pope, a Sienese by homeland, after Adrian the Fourth sat in the chair of Peter for twenty-one years; a learned and vigorous man, who endured many injuries from Emperor Frederick, while he stoutly crushed the three antipopes raised up by him against himself, and at last, overcoming the emperor himself, trampled his neck with his foot as a sign of his subjection, saying: “It is written: You shall walk upon the asp and the basilisk; you shall trample the lion and the dragon.” He wrote elegant works:

*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book; and *On Ecclesiastical Power*, 1 book.

And certain other works. He died in the time of Frederick the First, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1181, in the fourteenth indiction.

Crisolanus, archbishop of the church of Milan, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, distinguished in Greek and Latin eloquence, published certain little works of great authority, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity; none of these has come into our hands. His work is reported:

*On the Holy Spirit, Against the Greeks*, 1 book. *On the Holy Trinity*, 1 book.

Letters, sermons, and various other treatises, which are unknown to me.

Hugh Eterianus, a Tuscan by nation, easily the most learned in the holy Scriptures of all the learned men of his time, exceptionally instructed in secular letters, a philosopher and most eloquent rhetorician, perfectly equipped in Greek and Latin speech, whom all Greece admired as he wrote, most subtle in talent, a disputant second to none. He wrote many distinguished volumes, both in Greek and in Latin, by which he transmits his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have found only one work, brief indeed, but not to be despised, on the heresies which the Greeks lay upon the Latins:

*To Cardinal Ardoinus*, 1 book. “Careful prudence.”

*On the Immortal God*, 1 book. I have found nothing about the rest.

He flourished in the times of Manuel, emperor of Constantinople, with whom he was of great authority, under Frederick the First, emperor of the Romans, and Pope Alexander III, in the year of the Lord 1160.

Christian, archchancellor of Frederick the First, emperor, a Teuton by nation, a man most learned in secular literature, eloquent and industrious, whom the Italians in his time admired when he spoke, not ignorant also of the divine Scriptures, whom Leo the Tuscan, notary of Manuel, emperor of the Greeks, extols with praises. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, of which there are extant:

*The Deeds of the Life of Frederick the First, Emperor*, 1 book. Various letters, or speeches, and certain other works.

He flourished under the same Frederick the First, in the year of the Lord 1170.
