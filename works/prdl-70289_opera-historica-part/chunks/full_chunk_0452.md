[unclear] sufficiently qualified, distinguished no less in conduct than in learning, and also an excellent declaimer of sermons, and formerly confessor-secretary of Henry V, king of the English.

Among other monuments of his talent he wrote a notable work, divided into three large volumes, against the mad Wycliffites and Hussite heretics; Martin V, pope, after it had been examined, confirmed it by apostolic authority. This work is entitled:

*The Doctrinal of the Church*, 3 books. *Let God arise, let him scatter*.
Also, *On the Sentences*, 4 books.
*On the Books of the Physics*, 8 books.
*On Generation and Corruption*, 2 books.
*On the Books on the Soul*, 4 books.
*On the Books of the Ethics*, 10 books.
*On the Book On the Heavens and the World*, 4 books.
*On the Books of the Meteorology*, 4 books.

He is said to have written many other works besides, which have not come to my knowledge. He died at Rouen, honorably buried in his convent, under Emperor Sigismund, in the last year of Pope Martin V, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1430, indiction 8, on the third day of the month of November, that is, 3 Nones.

Paulus of Castro, the most celebrated jurist of all his time, a man of great learning and eloquence, who for many years, hired at a public salary, taught civil law with distinction at Siena, Florence, and Padua, composed many little works on the laws, known and dear to the professors of this faculty, by which he left to posterity the memory of his very celebrated name. Among these the following are reported:

*On the Code*, 9 books.
*On the New Digest*, 12 books.
*On the Institutes*, 4 books.
*On the Old Digest*, 24 books.
*On the Infortiatum*, 14 books.
Most elegant *Counsels*, 1 book.
And many other works.

He died in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1437, indiction 15.

Ambrosius the Florentine, general abbot of the Camaldolese order, once a pupil of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher teaching Greek letters at Venice, a man most learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular studies, a most brilliant philosopher, rhetorician, and theologian, left to posterity many distinguished monuments of his talent. He was the nuncio and orator of Pope Eugenius to the Council of Basel, where he delivered a solemn oration on his behalf to the fathers. He wrote a notable volume:

*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.
Various *Orations*.
And many other works.

He also translated from Greek into Latin the four books of blessed Dionysius the Areopagite, in a rather elegant and easy style. Also the original *Life of Blessed John Chrysostom*, to Emperor Sigismund. Likewise Basil *On Virginity*, to Cardinal Gabriel, afterward Pope Eugenius IV. Likewise Laertius *On the Life of the Philosophers*, to Cosimo de’ Medici. But he translated the three books of divine John Chrysostom to the monk Stagirius [Arrepticius?] in clear and ornate eloquence. And many other works still known to us. He flourished in the times of the Councils of Constance and Basel, and adorned both by his presence, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Caietanus, by homeland a Vicentine, a Paduan canon, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and physician, a very celebrated theologian, who taught sacred scholastic letters publicly in the Paduan gymnasium, acquired great praise for his learning. He wrote, as they report, several little works in both literatures, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

*On the Books of the Physics*, 8 books.
*On the Book On the Heavens and the World*, 4 books.
*On the Books on the Soul*, 4 books.
