*Catalogue of Illustrious Men*

Adelmann the grammarian, formerly a cleric of the church of Liege, bishop of Brixen, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, not moderately learned in secular letters, a philosopher, poet, and dialectician famous in his time, excellent in verse and prose. He wrote, in a twofold style, certain little works not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there survives a not unpleasant little book on the body and blood of the Lord, addressed to Berengar of Tours, at one time his fellow pupil under Fulbert of Chartres, who was already then beginning to teach errors on this matter; in it he strives against him with many testimonies of the Scriptures. On the same matter he issued a letter to Paulinus, primicerius of Metz. He wrote many letters to various persons, and certain other things. He flourished under Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1040.

Ratbertus Paschasius, abbot of Corbie of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, a distinguished translator of Greek and Latin discourse, venerable no less for his life than for his knowledge. He wrote to a certain Placidus, an abbot of our order, a notable work on the sacrament of the altar, one book; also the life of Saint Adalhard the bishop, one book; on the Lamentations of Jeremiah the prophet, one book; and several other works unknown to me. He translated some things from Greek into the Latin tongue. He flourished in the time of the ruler Charles the Fat, in the year of the Lord 880.

Theoderic, monk of the monastery of Saint Matthias the Apostle near Trier, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned in secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence. Among other monuments of his study he wrote on the discovery of Saint Celsus, one book; on the miracles of the same saint, one book; many sermons in honor of the saints, one book. He also published some other works which have by no means come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished under the emperor Otto III, in the year of the Lord 1000.

Theoderic, archbishop of Trier, the seventy-second after Saint Eucharius, formerly provost of the greater church of Mainz, a zealous and learned man, of outstanding industry in conducting ecclesiastical business. He wrote On the Praises of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of God, one book; also the life of Saint Liuttrudis, one book; and certain other things. Under the pontificate of this Theoderic, the canons of the greater church of Trier ceased to be regulars. He flourished in the times of the emperors Otto II and III, in the year of the Lord 967. He was buried at Mainz in the church of Saint Gangolf, which he himself had built and endowed for twelve brethren.

Remigius, monk and abbot of the monastery of Saint Luthwin at Mettlach, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, a man most zealous and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, devout in life and conduct, a philosopher, poet, and musician by far the most distinguished of all in his time. He was of such knowledge and learning that from all Germany and Gaul he had most distinguished pupils placed under his instruction to be taught; of these many are read to have been taken up into ecclesiastical rule in diverse regions of the world, some consecrated as abbots, others as bishops. He wrote many excellent little works, by which his name is made known to posterity: on the five books of Moses; not careless commentaries on the Gospel; also Rules on the Apostle; On the Vision of Habakkuk, one book; also an Excerpt from Priscian, one book; a commentary on Donatus, one book.
