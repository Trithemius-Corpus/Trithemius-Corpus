Cinus of Pistoia, born from the noble family of the Symbaldi, formerly a hearer and disciple of Dinus Mugellanus, a very distinguished man, and a close friend of Johannes Andreae, was certainly the most learned of all men of his time in civil law, subtle in intellect, ready and very abundant in knowledge. He published many little works in that faculty, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported.

On the Code, nine books.
On the first part of the Old Digest, one book.
On the second part, one book.
Additions on the remaining parts, one book.
And many other things. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Monaldus, of the order of Friars Minor, a most learned theologian and jurist, subtle in intellect, cautious in knowledge, scholastic in speech, a man of excellent industry in delivering sermons to the people. He published, for the benefit of the simple, little works not to be scorned even by the learned, by which he provided both for those then present and for those who would live long afterward. Of these the following necessary and very beautiful works are reported.

A Summa of canon law, one book. Since one who is ignorant [unclear].
Questions on the Sentences, four books.
Various sermons, one book.
And several other works, which have by no means come to our knowledge.

Oldradus de Laude, an Italian by nation, formerly a hearer of Dinus Mugellanus, a most learned man, and the very close friend of Johannes Andreae, a man most skilled in both laws, also a student of philosophy and excellently imbued with the disciplines, who for many years was a consistorial advocate and acquired great praise and glory for his erudition. He wrote several distinguished little works in both laws, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have seen only:

A volume of Counsels, one book. In the name of the Lord, Amen.

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He is said to have left many selections in civil law afterward, which I have not yet seen. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Johannes de Blomendal, a German by nation, of the order of Friars Minor, a man studious and learned in the divine writings and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, was an excellent teacher in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, for the edification of the faithful, volumes not to be scorned, by which he made his name known to posterity; of these I have so far found only:

Sermons on the season, one book. Take up your arms, quiver [unclear].
Sermons on the saints, one book.
Sermons for Lent, one book.

He flourished under Louis the Bavarian, fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Guido de Monte Rocherii, a man very studious in the sacred writings and rich in reading of the ancients, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech. He wrote little works of his intellect not to be despised, by which he made the memory of his name known even among posterity.
