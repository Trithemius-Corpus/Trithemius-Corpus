[Johannes?] Benedicti, learned in the sacred Scriptures and rich in the reading of the ancients, subtle in intellect, endowed with singular charm in speech, and also an outstanding disputant of scholastic questions. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity; among their number the following are reported. *Questions on the Sentences*, one book. *Various Questions*, one book. *Speeches*, one book. *On the Power of the Virtues*, one book. *On Abstinence from Meat*, one book. *On the Use of Flocks in God*, one book. *On Progress*, one book, under which are contained the following. *The Soliloquy of a Man to God*, one book. *Letters to Various Persons*, one book. *Various Sermons and Speeches*, one book. *Various Poems or Epigrams*, one book, and certain other brief works, but not unpleasing. He died in the year [unclear].
