He published many letters to Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal, and to various others. But he is also said to have written many other things which have not come to my knowledge. He recast the little works of John Cassian into an easier style, also for the sake of the simpler brothers. He died under the emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1471, in the fourth indiction, on the day of blessed Gregory, which is the twelfth of the month of March, and was buried with his brothers in the common cemetery of the aforesaid house of Roermond, not without a reputation for holiness.

Johannes de Hagen, otherwise de Indagine, a monk of the Carthusian order of the house of Mount Saint Savior at Erfurt, prior in Eisenach and in Stettin, was a man most devoted to the study of the divine Scriptures and very learned, and excellently taught in canon law; subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, ready and provident in counsel, devout in life and conduct. He wrote many distinguished volumes to kings, princes, bishops, and other prelates of churches, when he was asked about various and diverse matters and questions.
