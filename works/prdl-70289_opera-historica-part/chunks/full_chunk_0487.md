*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...today at Ferrara, where, succeeding his father, for thirty-three years now he has taught both languages most flourishingly and composes various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI. In the year of the Lord in which we are writing these things, 1494, indiction 12. He himself has a son learned in the Greek and Latin languages, following his ancestral discipline.

Angelus Politianus, a man skilled in every respect and a very learned, celebrated grammarian, an outstanding philosopher, orator, and poet; perfectly instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, excellent in talent, who for a long time illuminated Italy by his learning and doctrine. He wrote various distinguished volumes, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Among these the following are reported:

One book, *On the Errors of the Latin Language*, one book.
Various letters to different persons, one book.

And he is said to have written certain other works which have not come to my knowledge. Besides these he dared to present Homer’s *Iliad* for reading in Latin verses. He is still flourishing in Italy, as I have heard, composing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff. In the year of the Lord 1494.

Baptista Mantuanus, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in knowledge of secular letters, doctor of sacred theology, distinguished philosopher, most celebrated poet and orator, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, outstanding in life and conduct, an ornament of the Latin language, a renowned interpreter of Greek, excellent in verse and prose. Johannes Bertram of Nuremberg, a German by nation, once a hearer of Georgius Molitor of Nuremberg, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures; not ignorant of secular philosophy, renowned in talent, scholastic in speech, first at Erfurt, then in the gymnasium of Mainz publicly teaching theology and instructing many. He wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known. Among them there exists a large work.

One book. He flourishes in mature age in the gymnasium of Mainz, teaching theology in the ordinary course and composing various works, under the most renowned Maximilian, king of the Romans, and the supreme pontiff Alexander VI. In the year of the Lord in which we are writing these things, 1494.
