And many other works. Near Cusa, the village of his birth, on the banks of the Moselle opposite Bernkastel, he built a very famous hospital in honor of Saint Nicholas the bishop, which he richly endowed with estates and revenues, and adorned with many books in every science, Greek, Latin, and others. He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1464, in the twelfth indiction, on the eleventh day of the month of August, in the sixty-third year of his age. He was buried at Rome; his heart was sent to the aforesaid hospital, and there buried beneath marble in the middle of the choir.

Johannes Rode, abbot of the monastery of Saint Matthias the Apostle outside the walls of Trier, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, from Trier by homeland, a man devoted to and learned in the divine scriptures, and outstandingly learned in canon law, devout in morals and manner of life, and a very famous restorer of regular observance, from whose labor and foundation the most recent Bursfeld reform of our order in Germany takes its beginning. He formerly devoted himself to letters in the school of Heidelberg, where, becoming a bachelor of sacred theology and a most learned licentiate of canon law, he already seemed to despise the world in mind and body. Afterwards made canon of the church of Metz, dean of Saint Simeon, and official of the Trier curia, he despised all things for love of Christ, and entered the Carthusian order outside Trier, to the amazement of all; and for some years before, though refusing and unwilling, he had taken up the office of prior. But Otto, archbishop of Trier, perceiving that this man was learned, experienced, and zealous, drew him out of the Charterhouse by authority of the supreme pontiff and appointed him abbot in the aforesaid monastery; through him he reformed many monasteries in his diocese. He himself also, by authority of the Council of Basel, magnificently extended the reform. He wrote, for the reform of monks:

*Useful Constitutions*, 1 book. “When the divine office which...”

*On the Quality of an Abbot*, 1 book. “In what way from on high proceeds every...”

And certain other things. He died under Emperor Albert and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1439, the twentieth of his abbacy, in the second indiction, on the first day of the month of December. He was buried in the said monastery of Saint Matthias the Apostle at Trier, before the altar of Stephen the protomartyr, in the right apse of the choir, beneath a stone inscribed with his name.

Guarino of Verona, once a hearer of Chrysoloras the Byzantine philosopher, in secular writings easily the most learned of all in his time, and not ignorant of divine letters, most skilled in Greek and Latin speech; by his labor the studies of the good arts, which seemed weakened and already almost extinct, were stirred up and recalled into the light. He taught first at Venice, afterwards at Ferrara, and in both places had very famous pupils. He wrote to Prince Leonello a work entitled:

*On the Difference between a Flatterer and a Friend*, 1 book.

Also a brief *Grammar*, 1 book.

*A Comparison of Scipio and Gaius*, 1 book.

*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.

Many *Orations*, 1 book.

Many poems.

And not a few other things. By order of Pope Nicholas V he translated into Latin the *Geography* of Strabo, a work no less [unclear] than delightful. From Plutarch he made Latin versions of the lives of many illustrious men. But he also translated Plutarch himself *On the Education of Children*, with many other works, most elegantly; these are partly extant. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460, in the eighth indiction, on the day before the Nones of December. He was buried at Ferrara in the convent of the Carmelites, honorably granted a marble tomb.

Henricus Kaltysen, archbishop of the churches of Nydrosia and Caesarea, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a German by nation, from Koblenz by homeland, where also he entered the order; first made lector of the sacred palace, preacher of the Holy Cross, and inquisitor of heretical depravity, and afterwards, as we have said, consecrated arch-prelate of two provinces by apostolic authority, he became renowned throughout the whole world for his learning and preaching.
