On the miracles of the same, shown at Urticetum near Helpruma, six books. An Apology against Charles Bovillus, who slanders things he did not understand, one book. On the Origin and Kings of the Franks, one book. A Chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, of Saint James at Wurzburg, one book. To Albert, of the Margraves of Brandenburg, Archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, the Life of Saint Rabanus, arch-prelate, three books. On the translation of the same from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To the same, the Life of Saint Maximus, bishop, one book. To John, Count Palatine, Duke of Bavaria, and Count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, On the Origin, Kings, and Dukes of the Bavarians, and likewise of the Counts Palatine and of Sponheim, four books. On Demons and Profane, Maleficent, and Superstitious Arts, a laborious work he has in hand, which will have twelve books, in which he blasts, confounds, and overthrows all the tricks of diabolical arts. Of Wurzburg Letters, which he wrote in that abbey, four books. He also wrote many other smaller works suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as a Cursus, a Rosary, an Office of the Mass, and various prayers, together with proses or sequences on Saint Anne, grandmother of our Savior. He also wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene; a rosary on Saint John the Evangelist. At the request of John, Archbishop of Trier, a sequence on Anne, which begins, "Jesus, king of the heavens," to the melody Ave praeclara. Another, to be sung on her feast, which begins: "Let them rejoice on this day." A third to the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, and it begins, "Let us rejoice in honor." A fourth on Saint Hildegard, virgin, which begins thus: "Praise be to you, Lord, king of eternal glory." A fifth on Saint Rupert, leader of the people of Bingen, which begins: "Let them rejoice on this day." A sixth on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, and it begins thus: "[Let us sing?], illustrious ones." A seventh on Saint Rabanus, archbishop. An eighth on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus: "Let us adore Jesus Christ; let us lament with the crucified." A ninth on the compassion of Blessed Mary ever virgin, and it begins thus: "Ah, let us weep with devout mind, sharing the suffering of the Blessed Virgin Mary," to the melody, namely, Ave praeclara. A tenth on Saint Joachim, father of our Lady, which begins thus: "Let us adore the Trinity; let us praise together the unity of Father, Son, and Spirit." An eleventh on the good proper angel of each human being. A twelfth for the proper Mass of each priest. A thirteenth on Saint Maximus, bishop of Mainz. A fourteenth on Saint Martha, virgin, host of Christ. A fifteenth on Saint Mary of Egypt. A sixteenth on Saint Mary of Egypt. A sixteenth on Saint James the Greater, apostle. A nineteenth on Saint Benedict, abbot. A twentieth on All Saints. And for each of the named commemorations and saints he composed individual new offices of the Mass, conformable to the divine Scriptures. He wrote besides a little work of meditations and prayers on the Lord's incarnation, nativity, life, passion, and death, suited to devotion, two books. The Mirror of Heavenly Doctrine, serving not a little for compunction of mind, one book, which begins thus: "[The beginning?] of human salvation." He also composed, in beautiful variety, many most devout meditations and prayers on our Lord Jesus Christ, on his most holy mother the Virgin Mary, and on various saints, together with many other great works, which all devout posterity will see and admire. These, my dearest Nicolaus, are the volumes of our Abbot Trithemius, which for almost thirty-two years he has worked out in unremitting study, apart from those which, not yet finished, are in hand under the hammer and unknown to me. Farewell from my Duraclusian house, on the fifth day of October, in the year of our salvation 1515.

Johannes Duraclusius.
