*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

PRISCIAN the grammarian, a native of Caesarea, a man most learned in secular letters, skilled in Greek and Latin eloquence, was held in great esteem with Chosroes, king of the Persians. He wrote certain distinguished volumes on the art of grammar, which among the ancients were held in great authority. Of these the following are reported: To Julian the patrician. *On the Parts of Speech*, 2 books. *On Construction*, 1 book. *On Accents*, 1 book. *On Cosmography*, 1 book. *On Natural Questions*, to [him?], 1 book.

He is also said to have written several other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished, as some think, in the times of Chosroes king of the Persians and of the emperor Heraclius, in the year of the Lord 620.

MARCUS, a monk, disciple of the holy father Benedict, most affectionately loved by him, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most skilled in secular letters, a distinguished orator and poet, outstanding in character and manner of life. After the blessed Pope Gregory he wrote, adding certain things in a heroic verse running most elegantly: *The Life of Saint Benedict*, 1 book, and certain other works.

He flourished down to the reign of Heraclius Augustus, in the year of the Lord 610.

FORTUNATUS, bishop of Poitiers, an Italian by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most erudite in secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent and polished in speech, excelling in verse and prose, shone brightly among all the teachers of his age, renowned no less for holiness of life than for knowledge and learning. Freed from pain in his eyes by the power of Saint Martin, he came to Tours to his tomb to give thanks; then going on to Poitiers, he was ordained there first priest and then bishop, and was renowned for great learning and religion. He wrote in elegant verse many useful and devout little works. Of these I have seen only the following. To Saint Radegund, daughter of the last king of the Thuringians, captive of the king of the Franks, afterwards abbess of the monastery of Poitiers, in hexameter verse: *The Destruction of the Kingdom of the Thuringians*, 1 book. “Conditio belli tristis.”

To Jovinus, *On Radegund*, 1 book. “Aonias avido qui [unclear].”
To Justin the Younger, 1 book. “Gloria summi patris natique.”
To Sophia Augusta, 1 book. “Cui meritis compar [unclear].”
In the person of Radegund, 1 book. “Post patriae cineres.”
*In Praise of Martin the Bishop*, 1 book. “Lumen Apostolicum cum spar[unclear].”
*On Leontius, the Venerable Bishop*, 1 book. “Inter quos genuit radi[unclear].”
On the same, a praise, 1 book. “Agnoscat omne saeculum.”
*Seventy-seven Hymns*, 1 book. “Agnoscat omne saeculum.”
*The Life of Saint Hilary of Poitiers*, 1 book. *To Oporicus on His Life*, 1 book.
*The Life of Saint Maurilius the Bishop*, 1 book. *Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book.
*Poems to Friends*, 1 book. “Rex regionis apex.”

He also composed many other works, which, since they are short, I did not wish to record under the title of books; for he wrote many epitaphs and short verses, which I have seen. He flourished under Justin the Younger Augustus, in the year of the Lord 570.

GREGORY, bishop of Tours, formerly a monk and once a disciple of Saint Avitus, bishop of the Auvergne, a learned, noble, gentle, and humble man, was of such holiness and purity that even in life he shone with miracles. Visiting the thresholds of the apostles Peter and Paul, he was received by the blessed Pope Gregory with the greatest reverence and love, as a truly holy man, a singular friend uniquely beloved by him. He wrote several volumes in a simple and clear style. Of these there survive:

*On the Praise of the Holy Martyrs*, 1 book. “Hieronymus presbyter.”
*On the Life of Certain Confessors*, 1 book. “Statueram illa tantum seri[unclear].”
*On the Miracles of Saint Julian the Martyr*, 1 book. “Magnum in nobis quo.”
*On the Miracles of Saint Martin*, 1 book. “Omnibus sanctis in Christi.”
*History of the Franks*, 10 books. “Scripturus bella regum.”
*Epitome of the Same*, 1 book. *Chronicle of Ecclesiastical History*, 1 book.

The other works which he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished in the times of the emperor Maurice, in the year of the Lord 600.
