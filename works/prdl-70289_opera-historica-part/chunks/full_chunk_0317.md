In the church of Constantinople, one homily. On the coming of the Lord and [unclear], one homily. “Zeal, whither it tends,” [unclear]. On the death of the Innocents.

There are also extant among us certain other homilies of his, brief indeed, but very useful. Concerning his other little works, although many are reported, I have so far found nothing. He flourished under Arcadius and Honorius, at the same time as John Chrysostom. In the year of the Lord 400.

Vitellius, an African, defending the schism of the Donatists, was indeed a man practiced in the divine Scriptures, and, if he had remained in ecclesiastical unity, he would have composed sufficiently useful doctrine. But because he accused the true Catholics as betrayers and persecutors, he was deservedly held in contempt, together with his volumes. The following volumes are extant from his writings.

Against the nations, one book. That the servants of God are hated by the world, one book.

John Cassian, a Scythian by birth, formerly a hearer and disciple of the blessed John Chrysostom, bishop of Constantinople, and ordained deacon by him, after some years was expelled by the same man, for what cause I do not know. Consecrated presbyter at Marseilles, he founded two monasteries, one of men and one of women, in which great fervor of regular discipline remained for many years.
