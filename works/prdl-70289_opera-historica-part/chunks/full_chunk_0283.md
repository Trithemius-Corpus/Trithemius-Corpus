*Catalogue of Ecclesiastical Writers, or Illustrious Men, with an appendix of those who have also flourished as very learned men in our own age.*

*Most judiciously compiled by the venerable man, Lord Johannes, Abbot of Spanheim.*

*In praise of this most noble work, an epigram by D. Sebastian Brant.*

Whoever you are who wish to know the approved writers of the Church, and whatever distinction each one has, read this epitome of Johannes, Abbot of Spanheim, whom his learned life commends.

As he restores the ancient teachings of the fathers of the order, and renews the sacred laws of Benedict, so he takes care to gather together the scattered names of the ancient writers and collect them into one book.

He sets forth a catalogue in which he reckons the times, and what that venerable company of authors wrote. He rescues from decay so many names of worthy men, whom devouring time now tramples underfoot.

And it is wonderful that one man could, in order, either read or recall such many worthy men. Indeed, I should hardly doubt that, if perhaps he did not read them all, still a large part of all of them was seen by him.

Hence it comes about that I rightly place this magnanimous and distinguished man also among the number of the learned.
