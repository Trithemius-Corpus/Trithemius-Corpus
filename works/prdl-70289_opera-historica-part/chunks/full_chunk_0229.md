Bertrammus, bishop of [unclear], of the order of the Friars Preachers of the convent of Koblenz, and vicar in pontificals of the bishop of Metz, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most famous among the doctors of his time, outstanding in talent and renowned in eloquence, also an excellent declaimer of sermons. He wrote *On the Sentences*, 4 books. Likewise, to Cuno, archbishop of Trier, *On the Illusions of Demons*, 1 book. To the same man, *On the Schism*, 1 book. He also issued various sermons to the people and to the clergy, and besides many other things, which have not come into our hands. He died under the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1387, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of February, in the tenth indiction. He was buried at Koblenz in the diocese of Trier, in his own convent of Preachers, where, after having once received the order, he had been prior.

Heinrich of Kalkar, monk of the Carthusian order and prior of the house of Saint Barbara in Cologne, a man whose understanding was illumined in the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent, sweet in eloquence, and devout also in life and conduct. He issued little works of reading not to be despised, the use of which is judged not unprofitable for devout brothers. Among these the following are reported: *Monastic Exercise*, 1 book; *Speech on Rhetoric*, 1 book; *Song on Music*, 1 book; *On the Origin and Progress of the Carthusian Order*, 1 book. He also composed sermons to the brothers not to be despised, and certain other things especially pertaining to the cleansing of consciences. He flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1390.

John Ruusbroec, of the order of the regular canons, prior of the monastery of Green Valley in Brabant, a studious and devout man. He wrote, among the other works of his talent: *On the Adornment of Spiritual Marriage*, 3 books; *On the Progress of Religious*, 2 books; also many sermons to the brothers; also several letters to various persons, 1 book. He also issued some other works, which have not come into our hands. This man’s writings in the third book *On the Adornment of Spiritual Marriage* are to be read cautiously, as John Gerson, chancellor of Paris, warns us in the book issued by him on this matter. He flourished under the emperor Wenceslaus, not without a reputation for sanctity, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Conrad, monk of the monastery of Fons Salutis in Swabia, of the Cistercian order, a man most studious in the divine writings, not moderately learned in secular letters, renowned in talent and plain in eloquence. He wrote *On the Sentences*, 4 books; also sermons on the season, 2 books; sermons on the saints, 2 books. But he issued some other works which have not come into our hands. This man’s sermons are commonly called “sermons of juice,” because they were extracted with sagacious study from the juicy marrow of the Scriptures. Yet there are some who pretend that the reason for this word took its origin from footwear, because he used to carry with him to the choir or elsewhere slips enclosed in his boots for composing sermons. He is said to have flourished in the times of the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1390.

John of Schoonhoven, of the order of the Brothers of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of the convent of Mechelen, a man studiously learned in the holy writings, not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people. He wrote, for the usefulness of simple preachers, volumes not to be rejected, by which he commended his memory to posterity: a distinguished work *On Vices, Virtues, and Every Preachable Matter*, according to the order of the alphabet, in two volumes, which he entitled with the Greek word *Polypodium*, 2 books. He also wrote sermons, not inelegant, through the circle of the year on the season and on the saints, 2 books. But he is said to have written several other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Conrad of Zutphen, by homeland a Gelderlander, of the order of the regular canons of the monastery of Windesheim in the diocese of Utrecht, a man more studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, venerable no less in conduct than in knowledge, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence. He wrote many excellent volumes for the instruction of simple brothers; yet only a few of these have come to the knowledge of our reading, for I have found only the following: *On Spiritual Ascents*, a very useful treatise on notable men, 1 book; also *On the Reformation of the Inner Man*, a work not to be rejected, 1 book.
