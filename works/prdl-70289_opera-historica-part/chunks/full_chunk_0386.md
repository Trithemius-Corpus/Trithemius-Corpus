*Abbot of Sponheim*

[unclear], since before entering the order he taught theology gloriously for a long time at Toulouse, Bologna, and Padua, and left distinguished disciples after him. He was of such holiness that, living and dead, he shone with many miracles. He was moreover a most excellent preacher, so much so that the supreme pontiff was accustomed to call him the ark of the covenant. And St. Francis used to call him his bishop. Among other little works of his talent he wrote very elegant and most ardent works:

Sermons on the season, 2 books. “There shall be signs [unclear].”
Also sermons on the saints, 1 book.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1230. And because of the frequency of miracles after his death he was at once enrolled in the catalogue of saints by Pope Gregory IX, together with Saint Dominic and Elizabeth, landgravine of the Thuringians.

Jordan of the Order of Preachers, after Saint Dominic, as they say, master general, a German by nation, a man sufficiently trained and learned in the divine Scriptures, an outstanding preacher of the word of God, venerable no less for holiness than for knowledge, by his own example and honey-flowing teaching instructed many in piety. He is said to have written some little works, the reading of which is judged not unprofitable for declaimers and devout brothers. Of these, from the office of the priorship, there are said to be, to the brothers of his order:

Several exhortatory letters, 4 books.
Sermons to the people.
On the institution of the order.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, at the same time as blessed Dominic, in the year of the Lord 1220.

Hubert of Bobbio, professor of both laws, a most sagacious interpreter, subtle in intellect, clear in expression after the scholastic manner, who among the old professors of this science was regarded as one of the foremost doctors, handed down his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity by teaching and writing, giving to others both an example of learning and an aid to advancement. For he wrote a notable summa, which he entitled:

On the order of judges, 3 books. “Because paternal piety.”
Questions and determinations.

And not a few other things, which nevertheless have by no means come into our hands.

Bernard Dorna, a Provençal by nation, formerly a hearer and disciple of the very famous Azo of Bologna, professor and most learned interpreter of both laws, stirred by the example of his most learned teacher, applying himself wholly to letters, wrote various little works, by which he consecrated his name to immortality. Of these there survives a distinguished volume:

On the conceptions of libels, 1 book.
And other things which I have not seen.

Accursius, an Italian by nation, born in the city of Florence, formerly a hearer and disciple of Azo, a man most skilled in both laws, and not among the least learned in the other disciplines of secular letters, subtle in intellect and famous in eloquence. How great his authority is at this time among professors of the laws, no one even moderately learned is unaware. He wrote many distinguished little works; he was the first to gloss the whole civil law:

On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest.
On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Code.
On the Institutes, 4 books. “Since by imperial command.”

He published besides not a few other things, which are very well known to students of the laws. He flourished under Emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1240.

Hubert of Bonacursus, an Italian by nation, a man skilled in both laws and not ignorant of secular philosophy, ready in intellect and clear in speech, who among the old expounders of law was of no obscure reputation. He composed in that faculty certain distinguished little works, of which I do not recall that any has come into our hands. There is said to be his:

On the preludes of cases, 1 book.
Other useful and distinguished works.

We believe, however, that both these and his other works are not unknown to skilled jurists.

John of Abbeville, archbishop of Besançon, cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, a Frenchman by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, a very famous interpreter of those same sacred letters, who in the Parisian studium taught theology excellently for many years, most excellent in declaiming sermons to the people; for this reason especially, from a master at Paris he was made archbishop of Besançon. He wrote:

Sermons on the season, 2 books.
Sermons on the saints.
Other things.
