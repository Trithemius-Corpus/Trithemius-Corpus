*Johannes Trithemius*

Matthew Herbenus, a native of Maastricht, a man studious in every respect and very learned, an excellent philosopher and a musician of singular erudition, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, of which the following are extant: *On the Nature of the Voice and the Theory of Music*, a very beautiful work, 5 books; *On Scholastic Institutions*, 2 books; *Diasynthetica*, 1 book; *On the Capture of Negroponte*, 1 book; *On the Restoration of His Native Soil, namely the town of Maastricht*, 1 book; *Against Wigand [Cauponis?], of the Order of Preachers, on the Most Pure Conception of Blessed Mary Ever Virgin*, 1 book, which he entitled *Antilogion*. Also orations of various kinds and poems. He also wrote many very ornate letters, both to me and to others, and certain other things. He is still living in Maastricht, writing various works, being 44 years of age, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Gerard of Marbais, born in the town of Maastricht, canon there in the church of Blessed Mary Ever Virgin, a man excellently learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, skilled in both laws, a philosopher and eloquent rhetorician, who greatly adorns the nobility of his family by his erudition and by the study of the good arts, a great lover of books and cultivator of discipline. He wrote, in a very fine dialogue, *On Prime Matter*, 1 book. He also composed another dialogue, *On the Capture of the City of Tongeren Made Last Winter*, 1 book, where he neatly mocks the people of Tongeren, who, by sleeping, so wretchedly lost their city. In these he introduces as speakers Andrew Kanharus the Frisian, Matthew Herbenus of Maastricht mentioned above, and Aegidius de Holy of Tongeren. But he is said to have written some other things which have not come into our hands. He is still living in the city of Maastricht, firmly persisting in the study he began above, about 28 years old, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Matthias Mynnecomius, of the order of those whom people commonly call Crosiers, a native of upper Maastricht, once a hearer and disciple of Matthew Herbenus, a man studious and learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, skilled in mathematics, very learned in the art of music, subtle in talent, clear in speech. With remarkable zeal he abbreviated the music of Boethius in one volume. Likewise, with similar industrious compression, he reduced the *Natural Theology* of Raymond Sebeidi into one volume. He is reported to be writing some other works, soon to be made public. He still lives [unclear] of the monastery of Saint Jerome in Ghent, a man studious and learned in divine writing and rich in reading of the ancients, also not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in talent, clear in speech. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote a treatise *On the Titles of the Psalms according to the Ancients*, furnished with eloquence and erudition; several letters, both to me and to others, of nectar-like eloquence. He is still living in Ghent, a young man who gives us hope of a larger work of study, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Christian Masseen, priest of the order of the monastery of Saint Jerome in Ghent, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures and rich in reading of the ancients, also not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in talent, clear in speech. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote [unclear], subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, strong in verse and prose. He is said to be writing many things, of which I have read only a long Sapphic poem and various epigrams. The rest of what he wrote has not come to my knowledge. He is still living in Gyßlingen, a village of Swabia, writing various things, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Johannes Trithemius, abbot, although unworthy, of the monastery of the holy bishop Martin in Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Mainz, a Mosellan by homeland, native of the village of Trittenheim in the diocese of Trier. I do not think myself worthy to occupy a place among the illustrious writers, men of Germany, because of the poverty of my talent. But lest there befall me what I have read has happened to many, who omitted the titles of their works. But in the *Apology* to which we write: in these matters let the testimony of conscience suffice for each person; let no one seek more outside himself. Let others, out of humility, conceal the little works which they have published; the things that I have written I have resolved, rather out of humility and charity, to bring to light. For I prefer, if error appears anywhere, to be corrected while living rather than when dead, since, just as confession ceases from the dead, so correction benefits the living.
