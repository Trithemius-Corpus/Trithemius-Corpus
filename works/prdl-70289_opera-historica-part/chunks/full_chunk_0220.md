Heinrich of Ghent, archdeacon of Tournai, easily the most learned of all men of his time, and very well instructed both in secular philosophy and in divine scripture, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, a most acute disputant of written questions, who, because of the great glory of his learning, deserved to be called the Solemn Doctor. Among the many small works of his intellect he wrote: an outstanding Summa of Theology, 1 book; On the Sentences, 4 books; Quodlibets, 2 books; On holy virginity, 1 book; On penance, 1 book; also various sermons, and diverse lectures on sacred scripture; On Aristotle’s Metaphysics, 14 books; also on the books of the Physics, 8 books; and on many other books of Aristotle. He wrote many other things besides, which have not come into our hands. He died under the emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1293, in the third indiction, on the day of the apostles Peter and Paul. He was buried at Tournai in the church of the Mother of God.

Emicho, abbot of the monastery of Schönau, of the order of the divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, once a hearer and disciple of the above-mentioned abbot Eckbert, a man studious and learned in divine scripture, and not ignorant of secular literature, ready in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, notable no less for life than for knowledge. He wrote many excellent small works, both in verse and in prose, though few of them have come to my knowledge. I have read his little work On the Praises of the blessed virgin Elisabeth of Schönau. Also many and various sermons, and certain other smaller works, not without usefulness. The rest that he composed have not come into our hands. He flourished under Abbot Eckbert, in the year of the Lord 1200, or thereabouts.

Conrad of Media Civitas, commonly Halberstadt, of the Order of Preachers, a man learned to no low degree both in divine writings and in secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and an excellent disputant. He wrote On the Sentences, 4 books; also a Summa for students, 3 books; On the trivium, 1 book; a noteworthy lecture on the books of the holy abbot, 1 book; also many and various sermons, 1 book. He is said also to have written many other things, which have not come to me. For he is said to have made lectures and explanations on many books of the Bible. Finally, he is reported to have been the first to arrange the concordances of the New and Old Testament in alphabetical order.

Nicolaus of Bibra, master of the gymnasium of Erfurt, nobly learned in secular writings and not ignorant of divine scripture, ready in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote volumes in both styles that are not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I find only: a work mixed of verse and prose, On Avoiding Evil, which he entitled The Hidden, 1 book; also Letters to various persons, 1 book; and diverse poems. The rest that he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Hugo of Strasbourg, of the Order of Preachers, a doctor of Paris, studious and learned in holy writings, and excellently learned in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. Among other exercises of his intellect he wrote: On the Sentences, 4 books; also a Compendium of Theological Truth, a work not to be rejected, 6 books; also many sermons; quodlibets, questions, disputations, and various explanations in the divine books; and he is said to have written not a few other things, which have by no means come to our knowledge.

Gerard, of the same Order of Preachers, lector of the convent of Liège, a man studious and learned in divine scripture, an excellent declaimer in making sermons to the people, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech. Among other things he wrote a very useful treatise On God and the Persons, which he entitled On the Doctrine of the Heart, teaching the seven dispositions to be made concerning the heart, and sagaciously gathering here and there from divine scripture many things pertaining to edification. Also sermons, as they say, on the season and on the saints, very noteworthy, 2 books. But he is said to have written several other things, which have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading.

Alan of Lille, master of Paris, a man most learned in divine writings, second to none in his time in secular letters, an outstanding theologian, a distinguished philosopher, and a most celebrated poet, who for a long time presided over the ecclesiastical school at Paris, where he shone brightly in every science, divine and likewise human, so that he deserved to obtain the name Universal Doctor. He wrote almost countless small works in verse and prose; not all, but only a few of these have come to my knowledge: On the Sentences, 4 books; Quodlibet, 1 book; To the abbot of Saint-Gilles, on the words of sacred scripture, a work of no small size, which he entitled The Eye of Sacred Scripture, 1 book; On the Doctrine of Moses, the same number of books; On the Song of Songs, 1 book; On Penance, to the patriarch of Bourges, 4 books; On Mystical Equivocations
