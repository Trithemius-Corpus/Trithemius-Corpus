*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Albert, bishop of Ratisbon, a friar of the Order of Preachers, German by nation, Swabian by homeland, originally from Lauingen, a man very devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular philosophy second to none in his time, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, devout and most holy in life and conduct, who because of his doctrine and learning was called “the Great,” taught sacred letters and Aristotelian philosophy for many years: first at Paris, afterward at Cologne, where among others he had Thomas Aquinas as an outstanding disciple. Then, having been taken up to the bishopric of the church of Ratisbon, after he had governed it for some years he left it, and out of love for salutary study returned to his convent at Cologne, where he persevered in teaching and writing until the end of his life.
