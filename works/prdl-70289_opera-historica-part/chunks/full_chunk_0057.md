*ANNALS OF TRITHEMIUS. 23.*

…namely in the year of the Lord's nativity 181, in the fourth Roman indiction, whose manifold deeds Hunibald sufficiently sets forth.

THE THIRTY-SECOND [king]. CLODIUS, the elder-born son of king Barther, succeeding [his] father, reigned over the Franks twenty-seven years; in whose third year the Romans, sending legions, attacked Alemannia in war, mindful of the former injury. Against whose incursion the Alemanni advanced armed, having in their army thirty thousand warriors. A battle hard enough is joined not far from Lake Benacus [Garda], in which nearly fifteen thousand of the Alemanni perish.

In the twelfth year of king Clodius, the Franks under [their] dukes invaded Gaul, and slew many thousands both of the Roman guards and of the Gauls, and, occupying a great part of interior Gaul, inhabited the north.

But Clodius, king of the Franks, dies after these [things], in the twenty-seventh year of his reign, [and] of the Lord's nativity 317, in the first Roman indiction.

THE THIRTY-THIRD. [Margin: a tumult in Britain; the tenth persecution of Christians.] WALTHER, son of king Clodius, after [his] begetter reigned over the Franks eight years, a magnanimous prince, brave and warlike, in whose reign—the persecution being everywhere published—Diocletian in the East and Maximian in the West overturn [the churches of Christ], burn the divine books with fire, and miserably afflict the Christians with the tenth persecution after Nero; and Diocletian, in the first year of the said king Walther, commanded [himself] to be adored as God, who also at the same time subjected Alexandria with all Egypt to the Romans.

In the first, second, and third year of Walther, king of the Franks, there were great tumults in Britain (which is now called England). For king Lucius—who first with his people received the faith of Christ from Pope Eleutherius (as we said above)—having died some years before, in the year 185, in the times of Farabert, king of the Franks, the Romans sent the senator Severus into Britain to undertake the regency. He too being killed at York by Fulgenius, Bassianus, son of the same Severus, was admitted as king. He too being slain, Carausius invaded the kingdom, who despised the commands of the Romans.

THE THIRTY-FOURTH. [Margin: Asclepiodotus, powerful king of Britain. Dagobert reigned 11 years.] DAGOBERT, the elder-born son of Walther, king of the Franks, succeeding [his] father, reigned eleven years, a man [kind] to [his] subjects, gentle, very humane, whom they loved with singular love. Against him Asclepiodotus, a warlike, brave, and bold man, rising up, killed [the usurper] with two Roman legions, and placed the diadem of the kingdom upon himself, and, against the will of the Caesars and the senate, reigned ten years, cultivated justice, [and] shut out the [Roman] province.

WALTHER, king of the Franks, in these times rested from wars, and at last finally dies, in the eighth year of his reign, [and] of the Lord's nativity 306, in the 9th Roman indiction; before which there was an earthquake.
