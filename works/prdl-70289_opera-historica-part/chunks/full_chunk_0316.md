At last, after many labors and holy exercises, through the deceit of Theophilus of Alexandria and Eudoxia Augusta, he died in exile in Pontus, on the sixth day before the Kalends of February, in the year of the Lord 411, in the ninth indiction. 50.

Severianus, bishop of Gabala, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and an admirable teacher in delivering homilies to the people, was of incredible eloquence; for this reason he was summoned to Constantinople for a sermon. Saint John entrusted his people to him when he was being driven into exile, venerating not only his doctrine but also the merit of his life. But Severianus, recommending himself to the people more than he ought, supplied kindling for discord between himself and John. But when the patriarch returned from exile, they returned to the covenant of fraternal love, as the homily delivered on this matter in the church of Constantinople testifies.
