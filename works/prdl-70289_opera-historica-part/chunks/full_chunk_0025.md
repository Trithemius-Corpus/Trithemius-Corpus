The kingdom of the Franks, Germany, is to have perpetually by itself its own king, subject to no mortal whatsoever, not even to the Roman Empire, but, according to the ancient custom of the Franks, most free. To this kingdom, in the aforesaid division, fell the subject nations, cities, peoples, and races: namely the whole kingdom of Germany, with the Saxons, Thuringians, Marcomanni, Danes, Frisians, Hessians, [Westphalians?], East Franks, Bavarians, Swabians, Bohemians, Sorbs, southern Germans, and all the nations, peoples, and races that lie among those named above.

Likewise the whole kingdom of Austrasia, with these cities: Metz, the whole of what is now called Lorraine, Trier, with the Mosellans, the Eifel people, and the [Cinonotes?], Cologne, Aachen, Liege, and both lower and upper Utrecht, with Holland, Zeeland, Brabant, Hainaut, the entire region from the river Rhine westward as far as the sea; with the Tongrians, Flemings, Morini, and the whole land that either adjoins or lies between the aforesaid regions and cities.

Finally, the kingdom of Arles, with its eight counties in Gaul, is considered from ancient times subject to the rule of the king of the Germans. For this reason the archbishop of Trier, whoever he may be at the time, is afterwards known to have been appointed archchancellor of the German kingdom throughout Gaul, for the aforesaid kingdom of Arles. The kingdom of Burgundy also, with all its counties, was assigned in the same division to the kingdom of the Germans. All Alemannia as well, with its cities and peoples, in which are the cities of Chur, Sion, and [Valais?], fell by lot to the kingdom of the Germans, together with the Swiss, Alsatians, [Caurici?], Savoyards, people of Constance, Basel, Strasbourg, Speyer, Worms, Mainz, and all peoples from the rising of the river Rhine as far as the sea, into which it flows by three mouths.

But the Roman Empire, together with Italy, Lombardy, and all those subject to it by ancient right, except Greece, the Eastern Empire, was, and today remains, annexed and subject to the kingdom of the Germans. Nevertheless, over time many disputes, invasions, and changes arose between the kings of the Germans and the Gauls concerning certain boundaries of the kingdom; of these matters it has been written more fully in the second volume.

The kingdom of the Franks of Gaul also, now divided from ours already mentioned, is in like manner, as the constitution declares, to have its own free king, who is no less free than the king of Germany and is subject to no one at all.
