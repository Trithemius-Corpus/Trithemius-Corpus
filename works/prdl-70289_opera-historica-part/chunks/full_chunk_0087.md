To this the woman, a seer, said thus to the king: “Behold, we shall beget offspring, differing in various habits with time. For the first that is born from us will be very noble, to be compared in its nobility with lions and noble unicorns. From the first generation, with time, a second will arise, which, after the manner of wolves, despising nobility, will practice plunder and oppression of the poor. The generation after this, the third and last from us, will, after the manner of dogs, tear, bite, and strike one another, until at last in the final days the kingdom will fail.” That same night the woman conceived, and bore a son, named Clodoveus, as we shall say.

In the following year, which was the 463rd from the Lord’s nativity, a son named Clodoveus was born to Hilderic. He afterwards succeeded his father in the kingdom of the Franks and was the first to receive the faith of Christ, as we shall say below. At almost the same time Hilderic, king of the Franks, having gathered a great army of fighters, made war against the people of the Alemanni, who lived between Italy and Suevia, where the cities of Chur and Sitten and Valais now are. When battle was joined, the Alemanni were defeated and brought under the tribute of the Franks. In these times also, when Vtherpendragon, king of Britain, which is now called England, had died, his son, named Arthur, was raised to his kingdom. Concerning him not only the history of the Britons, as Geoffrey of Monmouth relates, but also the voices of the peoples of England sound forth wonderful things down to the present day, although many of them are more fabulous than in accord with truth. This Arthur, endowed with great uprightness of conduct, prudence, gentleness, and at the same time kindness, strove with all zeal to make himself beloved and venerable to all, because, along with virtue of soul, he also abounded in marvelous generosity toward everyone, and especially toward ecclesiastics, on whom for the love of Christ he bestowed many small gifts as well as offerings.

He expelled the Saxons and Picts from Britain; he powerfully subjected to his kingdom the Scots, the Irish, and the Orkneys. When the kings of Dacia, Norway, and [unclear] heard this, they came of their own accord and submitted themselves to his dominion. Merlin, the seer of the English, foretold to his father Vtherpendragon wonderful things about this Arthur’s birth as well as his future deeds; we made mention of these in the first volume of the annals.

In these times also Odacar, by nation a Rugian, or a Saxon Teuton, armed with a multitude of Turcilingi, Heruli, and also Sueti, or those whom we now call Swedes from the island, having come out from nearly the farthest boundaries of Saxony, came into Gaul and entered the city of Orléans as victor. Afterwards he also received Angers into his power by the surrender of its citizens.

Against him Hilderic, king of the Franks, led out his army on the following day, joined battle, killed Count Paul, and took the city. Odoacar, making peace with King Hilderic, left Gaul and entered Italy to conquer it. He compelled Orestes, patrician of the Romans, who did not dare to meet him in Liguria, to flee back; and when he was hiding in the city of Ticinum, which is now called Pavia, he besieged, captured, and killed him. After this he obtained all the cities and fortifications of Italy, with no one resisting at all, and possessed them very peacefully; and, having placed the diadem of the kingdom on his own head, he reigned in Italy for fourteen years according to his own pleasure. Afterwards, however, he was killed by the king of the Goths.

Hilderic, king of the Franks, having gathered a very great army of his own men, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 475 ranged with a powerful hand through all Gaul, subjected to his kingdom everything that remained, and killed those who dared to resist; but those who made surrender voluntarily he received with favor. In that disturbance he killed many of the Romans and freed Gaul from their dominion.

At the same time the Franks, seizing Cologne Agrippina from the power of the Romans with a strong hand, also subjected it to their own dominion. Then Mainz, Worms, Speyer, and not long afterwards Strasbourg, together with all the towns and fortifications throughout the whole region [Cisrhenana?], from the city of Constance in Alemannia as far as the sea; and thereafter among the Treveri and Mosellani they set Heribert, among the vicars of the city of Metz, after the custom of the ancient kings of France. Clodoveus the king, however, afterwards had nearly all of them killed because of his ambition for sole rule.
