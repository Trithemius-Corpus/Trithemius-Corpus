THEODORETUS, bishop of the city which from its founder, Cyrus, king of the Persians, was called Cyrus, a man very learned in the divine scriptures and sufficiently instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, is said to have composed some little works on the sacred volumes, but they have not come into our hands. His notable work, published after Jerome, is reported to be:

Ecclesiastical History, one book. On the Incarnation of the Lord against Eutyches, one book.

He flourished in the times of the most sacred prince Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390. His history is now inserted in the Tripartite History.

EVODIUS, bishop in Africa, disciple of blessed Augustine, bishop of Hippo, to whom Augustine wrote that same treatise On the Soul of Christ among the Dead, and many letters, was a man learned in the holy scriptures and most upright in the conduct of his life, who deservedly should be placed in the catalogue of the saints. He is reported to have written certain devout little works. Of these there is extant a volume concerning miracles performed in Africa through the relics brought by the priest Orosius:

Of St. Stephen the Martyr, one book. And several letters to various persons, one book.

And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Saint Augustine, in the year of the Lord 420.

PALLADIUS, the first bishop ordained by Pope Celestine for the Britons or Scots of the island of Ireland, Greek by nation, a learned and holy man, by his most sweet preaching converted the people committed to him to the faith of Christ. After him Saint Patrick, a Briton by birth, consecrated by Saint Pope Celestine as archbishop of Ireland, shone with signs and miracles for forty-nine years and converted the whole island to the faith. Palladius wrote:

The Life of Saint John Chrysostom, one book.

He was sent into Scotland by Pope Celestine I in the year of the Lord 432, in the fifteenth indiction, according to Marianus Scotus.

CYRIL, bishop of Alexandria, Greek by nation, once the ornament and notable inhabitant of Mount Carmel, a learned and holy man, by order of Pope Celestine I presided in the council of two hundred fathers at Ephesus. There, condemning Nestorius and Pelagius with their errors, he uttered many elegant judgments. Since he was most learned in the holy scriptures, he wrote many little works. Of these, the following are reported:

On Faith against the Heretics, one book. Against Nestorius, one book.

On the Failing of the Synagogue, one book. On the Origin and Praise of Saint Mary, one book.

On the Paschal Cycle of 95 Years, one book. Commentaries on the Gospel, four books.

In addition he composed several letters, homilies, and sermons, and various other treatises, which have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished while presiding at the Council of Ephesus under the emperor Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 432, in the fifth indiction.

THEODORE, bishop of Ancyra in Galatia, a man devoted to the divine scriptures and very learned in secular letters, especially strong in dialectic, eloquent in speech and an acute disputant, being quite eminent in the Council of Ephesus, wrote there in a dialectical manner, very obscurely:

Against Nestorius, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under the prince Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 430.

SOZOMEN, Greek by nation, an outstanding cultivator of secular letters and sufficiently learned in the divine scriptures, is reported to have written some little works for the edification of the churches. Of these there is extant a notable work addressed to Theodosius the Younger, prince, from the third consulship of Crispus and Constantine Caesars down to the seventeenth year of that same Theodosius:

Ecclesiastical History, nine books. “Thoughts sometimes to me.”

The rest of what he composed has not come to my knowledge.

He flourished under the aforesaid emperor Theodosius, in the year 430.

SOCRATES, a certain Greek by nation, a learned and eloquent man, a notable historiographer and renowned for experience of great affairs, wrote from the time of the first Constantine the Great, emperor, down to the reign of Theodosius the Younger, a volume:

Of Ecclesiastical History, one book.
