Ruggandus, bishop of Metz, nephew by his sister of King Pippin, ennobled the nobility of his lineage by holy morals and by the study of the Scriptures; he was an outstanding venerator of the bodies of the holy martyrs, desiring to instruct in justice, by word and example, the clergy and people committed to him. For the clergy of the church of Metz he wrote a sufficiently instructive and useful

Rule of canons, one book. And certain other things.

He transferred from Rome into Francia the bodies of the holy martyrs Gorgonius, Nabor, and Nazarius. The body of Saint Nazarius was laid up in the monastery of Lorsch, then of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Worms.

He flourished under King Pippin, father of Emperor Charles, in the year of the Lord 760.

Leo, the third pope, a Roman by nation, sat after Pope Adrian for twenty years; he was a man very learned in both divine and secular writings, and also most renowned for the holiness of his morals and life. He bound Charles, great both in name and deed, to the empire of the Romans, and transferred this dignity from the Greeks to the Franks. Among other things he wrote:

Letters to various persons, one book.

He died under Charles on the Ides of June, in the year of the Lord 816, indiction 9.

Haymo, third bishop of the church of Halberstadt in Saxony, formerly abbot of the monastery of Hersfeld of the order of Saint Benedict, a German by nation, once a hearer of the monk Alcuin, to whom Rabanus, then abbot of Fulda, composed twenty-two books On the Universe, was certainly a most learned man in every respect, a most acute interpreter of the divine Scriptures, and of excellent talent in delivering homilies to the people. He was first a monk of the monastery of Fulda with Rabanus; afterward he was made the third abbot of Hersfeld; finally he was consecrated the third bishop of Halberstadt. He governed that church vigorously by word and example for thirteen years. He wrote almost innumerable little works; among them the following which we have set below are reported.
