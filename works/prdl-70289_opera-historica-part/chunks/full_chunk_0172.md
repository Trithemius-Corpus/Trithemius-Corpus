*Chronicle of the Succession of the Dukes of Bavaria and of the Counts Palatine, by Johannes Trithemius, Abbot: to Philip, Count Palatine, Prince-Elector.*

Bavaria, as certain writers say, took its name and origin from a certain Duke Boiarus, whom they report to have come from Armenia into this region before the Trojan War with a great people. After the inhabitants had been driven out, being very rude and living like beasts on acorns, he committed the excellent land to cultivation by oxen.

Boiarus, the first duke of Bavaria, from whom the people and the region received their name, reigned in Bavaria for many years, and after him his sons down to the destruction of Troy; their names and deeds, because of their excessive antiquity, we do not know.

Norix, king of Bavaria, son of the most valiant Hercules, from whom Bavaria was afterward called Norica, built a notable city upon the Danube, which today is called Regensburg, and which Tiberius Caesar, a long time afterward, called Tiberina. Many dukes succeeded this Duke Norix in Bavaria at intervals of time down to Tiberius Caesar; their names and deeds antiquity has brought into oblivion.

Boamundus, duke of Bavaria, a noble prince, driven from Armenia by Tiberius Caesar, came into Bavaria with Ingramus, a man equally noble, and there reigned over the Bavarians for many years.

Adelgerus, duke of Bavaria, a brave and vigorous man, after Boamundus and Ingramus ruled the Bavarians for many years.

Theodo, king of Bavaria, after the aforesaid Duke Adelgerus, reigned over the Bavarians; his deeds are found scattered here and there.

Gerobaldus, king of Bavaria, in the time of the Emperor Maurice, was a most Christian man. Among other children he had a most devout daughter named Theodelinda, whom he gave as wife to Otharius, king of the Lombards; through her many good things were done in the Church of God. To this holy queen blessed Gregory wrote the four books of the Dialogues and many letters, in the year of the Lord 600.

Gondoaldus, king of Bavaria, son of the aforesaid Gerobaldus and brother of Queen Theodelinda, reigned after his father in Bavaria in the time of Childebert, king of the Franks, having under him Bavaria and Austria and the province of Noricum.

When Otharius, king of the Lombards, had died, Agilulf was elected king after him; he married his widow Theodelinda. Blessed Gregory asked her by letter to induce her husband to accept peace with the Romans, and she did so.

Childebert, king of the Franks, being at war with the Lombards, expelled Gondoaldus, the queen’s brother, from Bavaria, because he was bringing aid to the Lombards, and gave his duchy to Tassilo, son of Othilo, king of Bavaria. For Bavaria, being extensive, was at times subject to several princes.

Othilo, king of Bavaria, and afterward duke, was the father of Tassilo. He had as wife Hiltrud, sister of King Pippin, that is, the father of Charlemagne; by her he begot this same Tassilo.

Tassilo, duke of Bavaria, son of King Othilo, after his kinsman Gondoaldus had been expelled by Childebert, received his duchy from this same Childebert, king of the Franks. He had as wife Luitburg, daughter of Desiderius, the last king of the Lombards, who bore him a son Theodo, whom Saint Rupert, bishop of Worms, baptized at Regensburg in the year of the Lord 561. This man begot a son Theobert.

Duke Tassilo defeated the Slavs, built the monastery of Lorsch, and many other things. Othilo, his father, among other things also founded the monastery of Altaha, of the Order of Saint Benedict in Bavaria, which still exists today.
