HENRICUS of Gulpen, abbot of the monastery of Saint Giles at Nuremberg, of the order of our holy father Benedict, doctor of decrees, studious and learned in both divine and secular writings, and venerable no less for his religious manner of life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, whose readiness of mind and foresight in counsel Johannes Nider, having once experienced it, commends in the book On Ants, wrote elegantly on [unclear] concerning penance, 1 book; likewise on the distinction On Consecration, 1 book; On the Passion of the Lord, 1 book; and certain other things. He was personally prominent at the Council of Constance, when he was still held to be a secular master of arts and a student of law; and at the Council of Basel, already doctor and abbot, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1435.

RUDOLPHUS of Bruxella, from a village in Swabia, in the diocese of Speyer, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently taught in the secular philosophy which they call Aristotelian, a distinguished professor of sacred letters, who in the Heidelberg gymnasium acquired great praise for his learning by teaching and writing, wrote in that faculty certain excellent little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Among them these are reported: Questions on the Sentences, 1 book; many very useful sermons; various questions disputed in the schools; many orations to the fathers of the same gymnasium. The rest that he composed have not come into our hands. He flourished at the Council of Basel in the year 1435.

HENRICUS of Gauda, a man skilled in the holy Scriptures through long study, subtle in intellect and a very keen disputant of scholastic questions, earned the chair of mastership in the Heidelberg gymnasium both by teaching and by writing. He wrote certain excellent monuments of his intellect, by which he showed himself useful to those present and memorable to those to come. Of these I have found only: on the third book of the Sentences, 1 book; also On the Celebration of Masses, 1 book; various orations delivered in the university; also many questions excellently disputed in the schools. But of the rest that he composed, nothing has reached our hands. He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1435.

NICOLAUS of Susato, by birth a Westphalian, the ornament and pillar of the Heidelberg gymnasium, a distinguished doctor of sacred theology, held in esteem by Ludwig, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, and at the Council of Constance joined, from the German nation, with the remaining cardinals for electing the supreme pontiff, wrote certain excellent monuments of his intellect, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have found only: notable questions on the Sentences, 1 book; several elegant sermons and orations; and various questions on diverse subjects disputed in the schools. The rest that he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the aforesaid Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1417 and thereafter.

JOHANNES Gritsch, of the order of Friars Preachers, by birth from Basel, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and skilled through long exercise both in canon law and in the other letters of secular philosophy, an excellent declaimer of popular sermons, outstanding in intellect and clear in eloquence, wrote for preachers of the divine word little works not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these there are extant: sermons on the temporal cycle and on the saints, 2 books; sermons through Lent, 1 book; also On the Passion of the Lord, 1 book; and many other things. He flourished, as his own people report, in the times of Emperor Sigismund and of the Council of Basel, in the year of the Lord 1435.
