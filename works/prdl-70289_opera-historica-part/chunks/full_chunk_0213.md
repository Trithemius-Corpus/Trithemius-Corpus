Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Lympurg, of the order of the holy father Benedict, in the diocese of Speyer, a man excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech, devout in life and conduct. He applied himself to philosophy for not a few years in the gymnasium of Paris, while he was still a secular man, and became learned in every scholastic science. At length, having returned to his homeland, namely to the city of the Vangiones, whence he had drawn the origin of his life, he despised philosophy, useless to the soul, together with the things of the world, for the love of God, and put on the habit of holy conversion in the aforesaid monastery; and finally, as his merits increased, by the consent of all the brethren he was elected abbot. There he established, by word and example, the greatest observance of regular discipline, and he deserved often to be refreshed by divine revelations. At the command, seen in a vision, of the most holy Mother of God, he wrote a very beautiful work in praise of the same Virgin: on the Song of Songs, 1 book; on the contempt and vanity of this world, 2 books; on Saint John the Baptist, 1 book; also of his own revelations, 1 book. He also composed many sermons in honor of the saints whose relics or patronages are held in his monastery, and letters to various persons, and various treatises on the holy Scriptures. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord [1120?].

Rudolf, abbot of the monastery of Saint Trudo, of the order of the holy father Benedict, in the diocese of Liege, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and skilled through long practice, and sufficiently learned in the knowledge of secular letters, distinguished in talent and life, excellent also in verse and prose. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Among these there is an excellent work against the Simoniacs, 7 books; also a chronicle of deeds of past time, 1 book. He also published many poems of different kinds. He also composed chants in praise of the saints with sweet and regular modulation. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1125.

Franco, second abbot of the monastery of Hasligem, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and well instructed in the knowledge of secular literature, strong in talent and distinguished in speech. He wrote certain commendable little works, both in meter and in prose, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. While he was still a monk, at the command of Fulgentius his abbot, he began an excellent work on the grace of God, which afterwards, when made abbot, he completed. He also wrote metrically on the state of future glory, 1 book; also letters to various persons and various sermons; and certain other things. He flourished under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1110.

Eckhard, first abbot of the monastery of Saint Lawrence the Martyr, which is called Vraugia, near the Hercynian forest in the diocese of Wurzburg, of the order of the holy father Benedict, a man devoted to and very learned in the divine Scriptures, and not moderately learned in secular letters, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading, in which he magnificently exercised his talent; among them the following are reported. In imitation of Boethius he wrote, in both kinds, that is, in an alternating style, an excellent volume which he entitled The Lantern of Monks, 1 book; also various and many sermons to the brethren; also many poems and epigrams of different kinds; and certain other things. He flourished under the Emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 1130.

Engelbert, abbot of the monastery of Admont, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man distinguished for learning in the Scriptures and venerable no less for integrity of morals than for knowledge, made his name known by writing and expounding various things, and both by word and example did the lowest things for the edification of his brethren. Among the other works of his talent he wrote: on vices and virtues, 1 book; also many useful sermons to the brethren, 1 book; also several letters. But he also published many other things which have not come into our hands.
