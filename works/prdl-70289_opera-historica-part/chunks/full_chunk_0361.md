Flodoardus, a monk of Reims of the Order of St. Benedict, learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech. He wrote little works of reading not to be despised, by study of which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among these there is said to be a distinguished work from the founding of the city of Reims, which, they record, the soldiers of Remus, [after Remus had been killed by?] his brother Romulus and [they had been] driven out, built in memory of their lord and so named, down to his own times; in it, interweaving many things about the holy martyrs and other matters and deeds, he chose to give the volume this title:

*The Deeds of the Bishops of Reims*, one book. Also *Lives of Many Saints*, one book.
Other [lives] of several saints, one book. Songs in honor of the saints.
And certain other things. He flourished in much religious discipline in the times of the emperor Otto [unclear], in the year of the Lord [unclear].

Aribo, archbishop of Mainz, the nineteenth after St. Boniface the martyr, for whom Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, wrote a treatise *On the Advent of the Lord*, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures: after holding a synod at Seligenstadt with Burchard, bishop of Worms, he established many useful things, which are contained in authentic writings. He also wrote, among other works of his talent:

*On the Fifteen Gradual Psalms*, one book. *To Abbot Berno*, one letter.
And certain letters to others. He died under the emperor Conrad the Younger, in the year of the Lord 1023.

Burchardus, from a monk of the monastery of Lobbes of the Order of St. Benedict [became] bishop of Worms, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very learned in secular letters. While he was still a monk, he kept a public school of monks at Lobbes, in which he instructed [unclear], together with others, in both literatures. He wrote, from the opinions of the holy Fathers, of general councils, and of various pontiffs, a great and notable work after Isidore, namely:

*Decrees*, which he divided into twenty books. “In the New Testament first...”
*Letters to Various Persons*, one book. He flourished under the holy emperor Henry, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau of the Order of St. Benedict, in the diocese of Constance, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, very nobly instructed in secular letters, a musician and poet second to none in his time, distinguished in life and conduct. He wrote certain excellent volumes both in verse and prose; of these I have found only the following. For Pilgrim, archbishop of Cologne, he composed an excellent and useful volume:

*On Music, or on Tones*, one book. “Every regular [unclear]...”
*On the Instruments of Music*, one book. “The musician is not to be content...”
*On the Advent of the Lord, to Aribo*, one book. “Of the highest dignity...”
Also *On the Office of the Mass*, one book. *On the Fast of the Four Seasons*, one book.
*On the Saturday Fast*, one book. *On the Measurement of the Monochord*, one book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

He published many other things besides, which have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of St. Henry, the first emperor but the second king, because the first did not receive the crown of empire, in the year of the Lord 1020.

Adelboldus, bishop of Utrecht, formerly, as they say, a monk of Lobbes of the Order of St. Benedict, under whose person Abbot Heriger composed the dialogue *On the Advent of the Lord*, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, held in great esteem at the court of St. Henry the emperor; no less by arms than by learning he [unclear] the unbelievers.
