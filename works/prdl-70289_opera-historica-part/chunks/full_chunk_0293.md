He also composed many other short treatises pertaining to the edification of the Catholic Church, which among the Latins are seldom found together. He was ordained bishop in the year of the Lord 173, in the eleventh indiction.

APOLLINARIS of Hierapolis, bishop in Asia, a learned man, an outstanding defender of the Christian faith, brilliant in intellect, eloquent in speech, wrote little works of no contemptible reading, in which he showed himself a man not so much learned as zealous. Of these the following are reported:

Apology for the Christians, book 1. Against the Nations, 5 books.
On Truth, 3 books. Against the Cataphrygians, 2 books.

But he published some other works, knowledge of which antiquity has taken from us. He flourished under Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 165.

DIONYSIUS, bishop of the church of the Corinthians, a most eloquent man, very learned in the divine Scriptures, a most watchful shepherd of Christ’s sheepfold, was of such solicitude and diligence that he instructed not only the people of his own city with saving teachings, but also urged the congregations of foreign cities, through letters, to constancy in the faith. These epistles are said to be so useful and lengthy that they may not improperly be called books. Of these, the following are reckoned:

To the Lacedaemonians, epistle 1. To the Athenians, epistle 1.
To the Nicomedians, epistle 1. To the Cretans, epistle 1.
To the [Gnossians?], epistle 1. To the Roman pope, epistle 1.
To the [Amastrians?], epistle 1. To Chrysophora, a woman, epistle 1.

He flourished under the rulers Marcus Antoninus Verus and Lucius Aurelius, in the year of the Lord 170. Not without a reputation for sanctity, he wrote various things.

TATIAN, a heresiarch, was a hearer and follower of Justin Martyr so long as he did not depart from his side. But afterward, when Saint Justin had been crowned, puffed up with the swelling of eloquence, he founded a new heresy, which is called that of the Encratites; Severus later increased it, from whom the heretics of the same party were called Severians. They abominate meats, do not drink wine, do not receive the Old Testament, and do not believe in the resurrection. Tatian wrote volumes, which nevertheless have not come to my knowledge. For because of [unclear], countless volumes, indeed [unclear] volumes, perished. There survives of his, according to Jerome, an outstanding [volume?], which he entitled:

Against the Nations, book 1.

He flourished under Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 170.

PHILIP, bishop of Crete, that is, of the city of [Gortyna?], whom Dionysius, bishop of the Corinthians set above, mentions in his letter which he had written to the church of the same city, was a noble and learned man. For the benefit of believers he published works sufficiently learned, as they say, which by no means came into our hands. There survives of his:

Against Marcion, book 1. The rest is unknown to us.

He also wrote certain letters to various persons and various treatises, which to our [unclear]; in them the abundant vein of his talent is visible. Of these there survives a notable volume:

Against Marcion.

He flourished in the year of the Lord 170, in the times of Marcus Antoninus Verus, emperor.

MODESTUS, indeed a man of flourishing talent, although what dignity or rank he held is unknown to us, wrote, as blessed Jerome bears witness, little works of no contemptible reading, in which the abundant vein of his talent is visible. Of these there survive volumes; of these the following are reported:

Against the Nations, book 1. On the Monarchy of God, book 1.
On Discipline, book 1. On the Ogdoad, book 1.
To Blastus on Schism, book 1. On Easter, to Pope Victor, book 2.
Against Heresies, 5 books. On the Apostolic Preaching, book 1.
