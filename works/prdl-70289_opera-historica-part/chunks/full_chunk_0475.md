Ludovicus Donatus, a Venetian patrician and bishop of Bergamo, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of scholastic philosophy, also distinguished for piety, religion, clemency, and gentleness, subtle in talent and renowned in speech, wrote certain lucid small works by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these he deserved to see nothing anywhere. His distinguished work to Paul II is reported:

*On the Sentences*, four books. Also *Various Orations*, one book.

He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1484, in the second indiction. He was buried in the church of Saint Vincent the martyr.

Conradus of Rodenberg, German by nation, abbot of the monastery of Saint John the Baptist of Rheingau, in the diocese of Mainz, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a devout and learned man, and an outstanding guardian and lover of regular observance, who previously for many years held the office of prior at Saint Matthias the Apostle in Trier and Saint Martin in Cologne. Since he was a most ardent lover of the blessed Mary ever virgin, he wrote in her honor, in plain discourse, a very celebrated volume, taking the material of praise from each of her members, under the figure of a cluster of grapes, to which he gave the most fitting title:

*On the Vineyard of the Lord Sabaoth*, one book. Its foundations are in the mountains.
*The Exercise of Novices*, one book. Consider your calling, [unclear].
*On Preparation before Mass*, one book. Our Lord Jesus Christ.
*On the Ruin of the Order*, an oration, one book. The crown of the head has fallen.
Catch for us the little foxes.
*On [unclear]*, an oration, one book. Blessed is the servant whom [unclear].
*On Pastoral Care*, an oration, one book. To the thresholds of blessed Peter.
And various conferences in chapters.

He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Innocent VIII, pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1486, in the fourth indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of January. He was buried in the right apse of the choir, before the altar of Saint Benedict, toward the ambulatory.

Stephanus of Gaeta, a Neapolitan, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, professor of both laws, a most learned interpreter, acute in talent, well suited for disputing and resolving questions of the Scriptures, renowned and orderly in speech, wrote to Giovanni Battista de Bentevogli of Sassoferrato, counsellor of Ferdinand, king of Sicily, a very celebrated work:

*On the Sacraments*, two books. To the thresholds of blessed Peter.

And certain other things. He flourished in the time of Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Georgius Molitoris of Nuremberg, German by nation, a man long exercised in the divine Scriptures by study, an outstanding doctor, and not moderately learned in scholastic philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, keen in talent, distinguished in life, publicly taught theology for many years in the gymnasium at Erfurt and won great praise for his learning. He wrote certain distinguished small works of his talent, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Sentences*, two books. *On the Passion of the Lord*, one book.
Many *Sermons*, one book. *Various Questions*.

And many other things. He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484, in the second indiction.

Bartholomaeus, surnamed Platina, by homeland a Cremonese, formerly an apostolic abbreviator, a man most learned in every respect, a most celebrated philosopher and rhetor, subtle and forceful in talent, eloquent in speech, pleasing, constant and strong in spirit, suffered many calamities from Pope Paul II, so that, stripped of all goods and dignity, after being suspended on the rack he was most cruelly thrust into prison and detained until the death of that Paul.
