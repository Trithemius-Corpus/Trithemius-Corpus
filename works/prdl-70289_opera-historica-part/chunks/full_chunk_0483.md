On the art of dissolving sophistical arguments, one treatise.
Explanation of all the books of Aristotle's Logic, one book.
Explanation of the books of the same author's Physics, one book.
Explanation of the six books of the same author's Metaphysics, one book.
On the things required for a worthy priest, one treatise.
A resolution of doubts occurring concerning the Mass, one treatise.
A summary of the Passion of Jesus Christ, one treatise.
On the Ascension of the Lord, among the sermons of Meffret, one sermon.
On Meffret's sermons concerning the Immaculate Conception of the Virgin, one treatise.
Very many sermons, both to the clergy and to the people, one book.
Various orations and letters, one book.
Many other works also, of which no mention is made here.

Moreover he divided many books of various authors into treatises and chapters, prefixing arguments to each, by which what the writer's intention is in them can clearly be known. Among these are Cicero's books On Duties, On Friendship, On Old Age, and On the Dream of Scipio. Likewise Aristotle's books of Logic; the same author's books On the Soul; the books of the Metaphysics. Likewise the books of Ephrem the deacon. The books of John Chrysostom On Compunction of Heart. The books of Saint Augustine On Contrition of Heart. The book of letters of the same author. And nearly all the books of Saint Ambrose the bishop. He is still living in the Charterhouse of Basel, composing various works, and by his own example and writings he greatly summons men to holy studies, under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, Alexander VI being supreme pontiff. In the year of the Lord 1494.

Angelus de Clavasio, an Italian by nation, of the Order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated jurist, outstanding in ability, renowned in speech, devout in life and conduct. He is reported to have fashioned certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known far and wide. Among these there is extant a notable summa on cases, very useful for simple confessors, and nevertheless for learned men who are occupied, necessary and now scattered everywhere, which from the author's name is designated:

The Angelic Summa, one book. It begins: "Angelic spirit."
Additions to the same, one book. And certain other works.

He completed this summa under Emperor Frederick III, Sixtus IV being supreme pontiff. And I hear that he is still alive, while Maximilian governs the kingdom of the Roman Germans. In the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494, the twelfth indiction.

Jason de Mayno, by homeland a Milanese, a ducal senator, a man most learned in both laws, most thoroughly instructed both in the divine Scriptures and in the other secular disciplines, a renowned philosopher, a very celebrated orator, subtle in ability, eloquent in speech, distinguished in counsel and action. He is reported to have written certain excellent little works, in which, exercising his talent, he also commended his memory to posterity. But apart from the oration which he is said to have delivered at Rome before Pope Alexander VI, and which circulates in print, I do not recall that anything [unclear] has come into our hands. But, as I have learned for certain, he wrote elegantly:

On the title De accidentibus in the Institutes, ten books. Also on the Code, one book.
On the Digest he wrote various things, fifty books. Orations, letters.

And certain other works. He is renowned today among the princes of Milan, in the highest esteem, under Maximilian, most illustrious king of the Romans, Alexander VI being supreme pontiff. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1493, the eleventh indiction.

Laurentius Butellus, of Dijon, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine writers, excellently taught in secular letters, outstanding in ability, renowned in speech, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote in both genres certain excellent volumes, by which he made his name known. Among these the following are reported: a very celebrated work in praise of Helias, patriarch of the Carmelites, which he entitled:

The Heliad, one book. On the Illustrious Men of His Order, one book.

And many other works. He is still living in great esteem among his own, writing and composing many things under Maximilian, most unconquered king of the Romans, Alexander VI being supreme pontiff. In the year of the Lord 1494.
