His kingdom Lotharius, the already mentioned son of Hilperic, received; and so, according to the prophecy which Saint Columbanus had once foretold to him, he became monarch of the whole kingdom of the Franks, and thereafter reigned alone for thirteen years. Against him the sons of King Theodoric, at the instigation of Brunichildis, raised an army; they were defeated, and Brunichildis herself, instigator of all evils, was captured and, together with Eudolina, daughter of King Theodoric, presented to Lotharius. Condemned by decree of the nobles, she received the sentence of a dreadful death. For, deprived of one foot and the other arm, and tied to the tail of an untamed mare, she was torn apart limb by limb by violent dragging; they said that she had destroyed ten kings.

Lotharius, therefore, having been raised to monarchy over the whole kingdom of the Franks, which previously had been divided into four parts, since he could not govern so broad a dominion effectively by himself, entrusted the provinces to counts descended from the ancient stock of kings. He appointed Guarnerius, a noble and very eminent man, count or prefect of the royal palace. Burgundy, however, he entrusted to a certain Harpo to govern; he too was a count sprung from the most ancient race of the kings of the Franks.

To Rado also, a most noble count, he entrusted Austrasia, in which are the very famous cities Trier, Metz, Liege, Cologne, and the peoples of the Moselle region, the Luxembourgers, the Austrasians, the [Euphalij], the people of Namur, and many others.

To Genebaldus, third duke of the Franks in the east of that name, at Marcopolis, which is now called Wirtzburg from the castle of Saint Mary adjoining it on the mountain, he entrusted Mainz, Worms, Speyer, and the people of those regions; he built the town of Frankfurt beside the Main.

But to Pippin, who was the son of Duke Ansegisus and brother of Saint Arnulph, bishop of Metz, and also count of Namur and duke of Britain, from whom the Palatine counts of the Rhine and the dukes of Bavaria, who today live to the number of eighteen whom I have known, apart from the women, all descended on the mother’s side, namely from Gertrudis, daughter of Henry, count palatine, residing at Heidelberg, who, married to Otto, duke of Bavaria, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 1225, joined together the principality of Bavaria and the Palatinate, King Lotharius entrusted all lower or northern Francia, that is, the people of Tournai, Arras, the Morini, the Taravani, the people of Cambrai, and whatever lies between them, except the county of Namur and the duchy of Brabant, which belonged by right to the aforesaid Ansegisus. To Duke Sigebert, son once of Theodobert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, whose head, as we have said, the people of Cologne had cut off, King Lotharius, when he had been made duke through the intervention of the nobles, entrusted the cities of Germany: Chur, Lausanne, Sion, Constance, Basel, and the surrounding peoples, the Alemanni, Rauraci, Helvetii, and those dwelling at the source of the Rhine.

Lotharius, king of the Franks, in the thirty-second year of his reign, in the seventh Roman indiction, after making an alliance, remitted to the Lombards the tribute of twelve thousand gold florins which they were bound to pay yearly to the Franks.

In the thirty-eighth year of Lotharius, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth Roman indiction, Guarnerius, count of the palace or mayor of the house of the Franks, died. After him Ansegisus, son of Saint Arnulph, bishop of Metz, monk, hermit, formerly married, was made mayor of the house of the Franks; with his son Pippin, and at the same time Saint Arnulph himself, faithfully helping him, he held the principate. Pippin, later mayor of the house, who was also called the Elder, was the son of the aforesaid Ansegisus, who was son of Saint Arnulph. He was son of Ansegisus, or, as others write, of Arnold the Elder. He was son of Ambertus, surnamed Nicanor, born from Blidehildis, daughter of Lotharius, the first king of Clovis. He was son of Duke Priam. He was son of Duke Diocles.
