REGINO, abbot of the monastery of Prum, of the order of the holy father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, exceptionally instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in speech, devout in life and conduct, easily held the first place among the teachers of his time. He wrote for Adelbero, bishop of Metz, a history of the deeds of the Frankish Germans from the birth of Christ down to his own times, 10 books. At the command of Ratbod, archbishop of Trier, he compiled from the decrees and decisions of the fathers and of general councils a distinguished canonical work, which he entitled *On Ecclesiastical Discipline and the Christian Religion*, in two volumes, 2 books. He also composed many elegant sermons, 1 book; also letters to various persons, 1 book; and many other things which have not yet come at all to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished in the times of Emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 910.

WIDUKIND, a monk of Corvey in Saxony, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, ready in intellect, distinguished in speech, excellent in verse and prose, wrote in both styles many excellent volumes, by which he consecrated his name to posterity. Of these I have found only the following: to Mechtild, daughter of Otto I, *On the Deeds of the Saxons*, 3 books; to the same, *The Deeds of Otto I*, 1 book; sermons to the brothers, 1 book; letters to various persons, 1 book; the life of Saint Paul the First Hermit, in prose and verse, 2 books; also the passion of the virgin Thecla, likewise in verse, 1 book; epigrams, 1 book. He is said besides to have written many other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Otto I, in the year of the Lord 950.

ROSWITHA, a consecrated virgin of the monastery of Gandersheim in Saxony, noble, of the order of the holy father Benedict, devotedly learned in the divine Scriptures, and well imbued with the disciplines of secular letters, outstanding in intellect, distinguished in speech, and no less notable for conduct than for learning, wrote, both in verse and in prose, many excellent little works, by which, surpassing her sex in talent, she transmitted her name to posterity with great glory. Of these I have read the following: to the abbess Gerberga, in heroic verse, on the life and conduct of the unstained Mother of God, the Virgin Mary, 1 book; on the Passion of the Lord, in the same verse, 1 book; likewise on Saint Gangolf, 1 book; on the passion of Saint Pelagius, martyr, who suffered in these times at Cordoba, 1 book; on the fall of Theophilus the vicedominus and his repentance, 1 book; on the fall of a certain young man, converted through Saint Basil, 1 book; on the passion of Saint Dionysius, bishop and martyr, 1 book; on the passion of Saint Agnes, virgin, 1 book. She composed all the aforesaid in very elegant verse. She wrote besides six comedies, imitating Terence in style, of which the first is entitled *The Conversion of the Prince Gallicanus*, including the passion of the martyrs John and Paul. The second is on the passion of Agape, Chionia, and Irene, virgins. The third is on the resurrection of Calimachus and Drusiana through Saint John. The fourth is on the fall and conversion of Mary, niece of Abraham the Hermit. The fifth is entitled *The Conversion of Thais the Prostitute*. The sixth recounts the passion of the holy virgins Faith, Hope, and [Charity?]. She also wrote in verse the deeds of Emperor Otto I, 1 book; epigrams of various kinds, 1 book; and certain letters not without elegance. The rest that she composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading. She flourished in the times of Otto I and II, most illustrious emperors, in the year of the Lord 970.

SMARAGDUS, abbot of the monastery of Saint Michael in Saxony, of the order of the holy father Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, acute in intellect, composed in speech, devout in life and conduct, a very celebrated guide of regular discipline, made his name known far and wide in expounding the holy Scriptures and obtained a great reputation for learning. For he wrote many excellent volumes, of which the following are extant: on the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, 1 book; on the whole Psalter, 1 book; on the Epistles and Gospels through the cycle of the year, 2 books; sermons to the brothers and others, 1 book; also a distinguished work on the virtues, which he entitled *The Diadem of Monks*, 1 book. He made a commentary on the greater Donatus that is not to be rejected. He is said also to have written many other volumes, both on divine and on secular writings, which have not come into our hands.
