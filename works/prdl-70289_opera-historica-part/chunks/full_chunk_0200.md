He flourished in the times of the emperors Louis and Lothair, in the year of the Lord 870.

Franco, bishop of Liege, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in the study of secular letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, poet, and excellent musician, keen in intellect, eloquent in speech, devout and most holy in life and conduct, presided for many years over the public school and trained many very learned pupils in every branch of knowledge. Among other little works of his talent he wrote many very useful sermons for the edification of the faithful, one book; also to Hermann, archbishop of Cologne, On the Squaring of the Circle, one book; On the Ecclesiastical Computus, one book; also letters to various persons, one book. Finally, he composed many chants, antiphons, legends, and responsories in honor of the saints, in sweet and regular melody. He is also said to have written some other things, which have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

Werenbert, a monk at Saint Gall of the order of the holy father Benedict, formerly a hearer and pupil of Hrabanus Maurus of Fulda, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, an outstanding poet, not ignorant of the Greek language, wrote for the edification of readers many splendid volumes, by which he brought back with great profit the talent entrusted to him by the Lord. He composed many elegant sermons, one book; also letters to various persons, one book; epigrams in different kinds of meter, one book; also on the Gospels, four books. He also composed various hymns and chants in honor of our Lord Jesus Christ and of the saints. The rest which he is said to have written, although they are many, have by no means come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Louis II, in the year of the Lord 870.

Hartmud, a monk of the same monastery of Saint Gall, of the order of our blessed father Benedict, formerly a hearer and pupil of Hrabanus Maurus, to whom Otfrid, the monk of Weissenburg, exercised his talent in many letters, a man learned on every side, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, devout in life and conduct, skilled in Greek, Latin, and Hebrew, and furthermore not ignorant of Arabic, wrote many splendid little works both in meter and in prose; but I do not remember that any of them has come to the knowledge of our reading. There are said to exist commentaries of his, not to be rejected, on certain books of the Bible; likewise letters to various persons, one book; also sermons, chants, hymns, and various treatises on the sacred Scriptures, which every studious person should seek out. He flourished in the times of the emperors Lothair and Louis his son, in the year of the Lord 870.

Stephen, bishop of Liege after Franco, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and magnificently skilled in the disciplines of secular letters, prompt in intellect, orderly in speech, devout in life and conduct. I have found certain things of no contemptible reading that he wrote: The Life of Saint Lambert, bishop and martyr, one book; also to the bishop of Metz, one book. He also composed many elegant sermons, and several letters to various persons. The office concerning the Holy Trinity, which we sing every year on the octave of Pentecost, this Stephen composed from the volume of Albinus and set to sweet melody. But he also arranged, with beautiful grace, works on the Holy Cross, on Saint Lambert the martyr, and on many others.

Ratbod, the fourteenth bishop of the church of Utrecht, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, most holy in life and conduct, shone forth with many miracles both while alive and after death. Nanno, a very learned philosopher, instructed him in good letters; from him he advanced in every branch of knowledge, and finally, ordained bishop of the aforesaid city, he presided for seventeen years under the grave persecution of the Danes.
