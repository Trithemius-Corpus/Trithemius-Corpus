David, of the Order of Friars Minor, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and venerable no less in life and conduct than in knowledge, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, for the edification of simple brothers, little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported: On the Instruction of Novices, 2 books; also Various Sermons to the People, and certain similar things, the reading of which is judged very useful for devout brothers. He was buried in the convent at Augsburg.

Walter, bishop of [Maguelonne?], born from Lille, a town of Flanders, a man exercised by long study in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, who in his time was held in great esteem because of his learning and erudition. At the request of the provost and clerics of the church of Lille, he wrote a work, elegant in polished speech, On the Psalter, 1 book; and, as they report, certain not inelegant letters to the rich. He also published some other works, which have by no means come to the knowledge of our reading.

Albert, surnamed the Great, of the Order of Friars Preachers, bishop of the church of Regensburg in Bavaria, born from Lauingen, a village of Swabia, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and, in secular philosophy, second to none in his time; subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse, devout and most holy in life and conduct. He publicly taught the divine letters and Aristotelian philosophy for many years, first at Paris, then at Cologne Agrippina, where among others he had Saint Thomas Aquinas as a pupil. Afterwards assumed to the episcopate of the church of Regensburg, after he had governed it for some time he left it, and out of love for saving study returned to his convent at Cologne, where he remained teaching and writing until the end of his life.
