CLEMENT, the fifth pope, originally from Gascony, previously called Bernard, bishop of Bordeaux, sat after Benedict XI in the chair of Peter for eight years and ten months; a learned and vigorous man, at whose command the order of Templars was destroyed on one day throughout the whole world. He celebrated the Council of Vienne, in which he established many things for the utility of the Church. He composed the seventh book of the Decretals, which John XXII, his successor, published, as is contained at the beginning of the same. It is commonly called:

The Code of the Clementines, 1 book. The foundations of the Catholic faith.
Also Letters to Various Persons, 1 book. Clement, bishop, servant...

He died while Henry VII governed the Roman Empire, in the year of the Lord 1314, indiction 11.

PETER of Perpignan, called Rimi, a Catalan by birth, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the holy scriptures and a subtle expounder of scholastic philosophy, ready in intellect and an outstanding declaimer. He published many little works of reading not to be despised, by which, exercising the abundant vein of his intellect, he made his name known also to posterity. Among them are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, 1 book.
On the Whole Psalter.
Various Sermons, 1 book.

But concerning the other things that he composed, nothing has ever come to my knowledge.

ODERIC, of the order of Friars Minor, a holy and learned man, an outstanding preacher of the divine word, on account of his most fervent love for which he undertook a pilgrimage and penetrated Asia and India, where, by preaching and teaching, evangelizing the faith of Christ, he converted many from iniquity to the path of justice. His most holy life was confirmed by many signs both in the flesh and after death. Among the other little works of his intellect he wrote:

The History of His Pilgrimage, 1 book.
Also Various Sermons, 1 book.

And certain letters. He flourished in the times of Louis, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

FRANCIS of Meyronnes, of the order of Friars Minor, formerly a hearer of John Scotus, a man most learned in divine writings and nobly taught in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, composed in style, constant in study, sufficiently acute and very fit for debating and resolving questions of the scriptures. He wrote not a few distinguished volumes of his intellect, in which he showed himself most learned and transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books. Concerning the proemium of the first.
On Formalities, 1 book.
Various Questions, 1 book.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.

It is also reported that he composed certain commentaries on the divine volumes and other various treatises, which have not yet come at all to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1320.

BONAVENTURA of Padua, of the order of Hermits of St. Augustine, formerly prior general, and afterward priest-cardinal of the holy Church of the title of St. Cecilia, a man learned in divine writings, not ignorant of secular letters, ready in intellect and clear in eloquence, reverend no less for his manner of life than for his learning, of great reputation in delivering sermons to the people. When he was defending the liberty of the Church most vigorously against tyrants, he was at last pierced by an arrow by the lord of Carrara and crowned with martyrdom. He wrote very useful and elegant works:

Sermons on the Season, 1 book. The desired one will come with...
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book. But far be it from me to glory...

And certain others. He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.
