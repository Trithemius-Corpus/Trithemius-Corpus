*The Abbot of Sponheim* Most celebrated among the Germans, subtle in talent, distinguished in eloquence, excellent in verse and prose. In both styles he wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known. Of these there survives a volume, in verses with rhymes, painted with beautiful variety, on the most holy and most pure Mary, Mother of God, entitled On the Redemption of the Human Race. A figured work, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. Poems also, one book. And many other things. He flourished around the times of the Emperor Charles IV. In the year of the Lord 1370.

Stephen [Petringon?], English by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, a most acute disputant, and very distinguished in delivering sermons to the people, published little works not to be despised, by which he brought his name to the knowledge of many. Among these are: Against the Wycliffites, one book. A Repertory of Arguments, one book. A Certain Notable Lecture, one book. Sermons on the Season, one book. He flourished in the University of Oxford at the time when John Wyclif was burned.

Bertramus, bishop of [Thefelicensis?], of the Order of the Brothers Preachers, German by nation, vicar in pontificals of the bishop of Metz, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, very famous among the doctors of his time, excellent in talent, distinguished in eloquence, and also an outstanding preacher of sermons. He wrote to Cuno, archbishop of Trier, a distinguished work entitled On the Illusions of Demons, one book. “Most reverend in Christ.” To the same, On the Schism, one book. “The wise [unclear] glory.” Various Sermons, one book. And several other things. He died under the Emperor Wenceslas and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1387, on 20 January, in the tenth indiction. He was buried in his convent at Koblenz.

Thomas Lombe, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, a man learned in the sacred Scriptures through long exercise and continual study, well instructed in the precepts of the philosophers, subtle and keen in talent, who, having obtained the magisterial chair in the Oxford school, earned praise for his learning. He wrote an outstanding work: On the Sentences, four books. Against the Lollards, one book. Sermons also, one book. On the Incarnation of the Lord. And various Questions. And certain other things. He flourished in the University of Oxford especially at the time when John Wyclif in England was exhumed and publicly burned.

Philip Riboti, Catalan by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior of Catalonia, a man studious and learned in the sacred Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, venerable also in life and conduct, and an outstanding lover and guardian of the Carmelite Order. For the edification of his brothers he wrote little works not to be scorned, in which, showing the fervor of spiritual zeal toward his religion, he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survives a volume which he entitled On the Particular Deeds of the Carmelites, ten books. “When the people of Israel.” On the Illustrious Men of Carmel, one book. Sermons. Letters. He composed several other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge.

Nicholas Ritzonis, from Toulouse by homeland, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior in the kingdom of Sicily, a learned man, distinguished in life and conduct, an outstanding preacher of sermons, subtle in talent and lucid in speech, published some little works in writing, the reading of which will not be contemptible for preachers of the word. Among these are reported: Sermons on the Season, one book. Sermons on the Saints, one book. A Double Lenten Collection.
