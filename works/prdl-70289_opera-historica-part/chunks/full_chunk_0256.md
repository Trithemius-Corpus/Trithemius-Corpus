JOHANNES of the little village of Lutra, called de Lutrea, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures, learned and excellently taught in scholastic philosophy, subtle in intellect, composed in speech, honorable in life and conduct, singularly devout, a lover of virtues, an enemy of vices, a most steadfast despiser of greed, who abhorred plurality of benefices and scorned the title of a bishopric offered to him, first taught theology gloriously for many years in the school of Erfurt, and afterward vigorously performed the office of preacher in the church of Mainz. He wrote On the Sentences, four books; also various Questions; Sermons on the season and on the saints, two books; and certain other things unknown to us. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1479, in the twelfth indiction.

ALANUS de Rupe, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, of most excellent reputation in declaiming sermons to the people, devoted to God in life and conduct, a special lover of blessed Mary ever virgin, and a venerable [unclear] promoter of her Rosary, deserved to be refreshed by a very singular gift of heavenly consolation, and, as they say, to be honored with frequent revelations and miracles. He wrote, among other little works of his talent: A Compendium of the Psalter of Blessed Mary Ever Virgin, one book; also On the Miracles of the Rosary of the same glorious Virgin, one book; many various Sermons, one book; On the Fraternity of the Rosary, one book; and many other things. He died in his convent of Zwolle, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1484, in the seventh indiction, on the vigil of the Assumption of Mary.

CONRADUS de Zabernia, zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, an excellent musician, an outstanding preacher of the divine word, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, honorable in life and conduct. He wrote in plain speech certain little works not to be rejected, by which, exercising his talent usefully and fruitfully, he also left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have found only the following: On the Monochord, one book; On the Manner of Singing Well, one book; On the Conclusions of the Collects, one book; many various Sermons, one book. The rest that he composed have not at all come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1475, under whom he died.

JOHANNES de Dorfftein, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, of the convent of Erfurt, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, sufficiently suited to disputing and resolving difficult questions of the Scriptures, and also of excellent eloquence in declaiming sermons to the people. In the school of Erfurt he excelled in teaching and preaching. He wrote little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported: Questions on the Sentences, one book; On the Vice of the Tongue, one book; Sermons on the season and on the saints, two books; and certain other things. He flourished at Erfurt under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1475.

THEODERICUS de Herxen, visitor and rector of the congregation of the Brethren Clerics Living in Common, a devout man, zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech. He wrote for the edification of his brethren many excellent little works, by which, exercising his talent usefully enough, he commended the memory of himself to posterity. Of these the following are extant: On the Whole Psalter, three books; On the Lord’s Prayer, one book; On the Angelic Salutation, one book; On the Passion of the Lord, one book; also On the Desire of Dying, one book; several Sermons to the brothers, and certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year 1481, in the fourteenth indiction.

ANGELUS de Brunsuco, a Saxon, who was commonly called Master Engelinus, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently taught in secular philosophy, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, eloquent in declaiming sermons to the people, who shone out very brightly in teaching and lecturing in the school of Erfurt. He wrote a notable work On the Canon of the Mass, which he is said to have read at Mainz before the clergy; many various Sermons, one book. He also disputed diverse Questions in the schools, and certain other things. He died at Strasbourg and was buried in the monastery of the penitents, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1481, in the thirteenth indiction.
