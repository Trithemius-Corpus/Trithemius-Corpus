*The Abbot of Spanheim*

Against Eutyches, 9 books.
Against Pelagius, 3 books.
Against all heresies, 8 books.
On Illustrious Men, 1 book. “James, by surname.”
On the Heresy of the Thousand Years, 1 book.
He wrote these and certain other works, but they have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 490.

Gelasius, Pope the First, an African by nation, sat after Felix in the pontificate for four years. He was a holy man and very learned in the divine scriptures. Soon at the beginning of his pontifical office he sent into exile the Manichaeans whom he found in the city, and publicly burned their books at the basilica of Saint Mary Major. He established many things for ecclesiastical utility and wrote very many works against heretics. Among his works the following are cited:

Synodal Decree, 1 book. “A necessary distinction of things.”
Against the Arians, 2 books.
Against Eutyches and Nestorius, 5 books.
Hymns in the manner of Ambrose, 1 book.
Letters to various persons, 1 book.
Several weighty prayers.
And he arranged certain other graduals, alleluias, and tracts, with prefaces suitable for the Mass, and separated the authentic books of the holy fathers from the apocryphal, as is found in Distinction 13, “Sancta Rom.” He died while Anastasius Augustus was emperor, in the year of the Lord 496.

Faustus, formerly abbot of the monastery of Lerins, bishop of Riez in Gaul, was indeed a learned and eloquent man, but stained by the Pelagian error; hence his little works are counted among the apocrypha, as is clear in Distinction 15, “Sancta Rom.” At length, corrected and amended by blessed Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe, he abandoned the error and returned to the truth of the catholic faith. As proof of this matter he published an excellent volume against his former errors, which he entitled On the Grace of God. Of his works the following are cited. The same volume:

On the Grace of God, 1 book.
Against the Arians, 1 book.

On the Holy Spirit, [unclear].
To Felix the prefect, 1 book.
To Gratus the deacon, against Nesto[rius?].
Several homilies to the people.
He flourished under the emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 480.

Fulgentius, bishop of Ruspe in Africa, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, was subtle in intellect, deep in understanding, sweet in speech, eloquent and polished, very ready for teaching, sharp for arguing, fit and most gentle for persuading. He shone forth most brightly no less by holiness of life than by knowledge of the scriptures. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which the following are cited:
