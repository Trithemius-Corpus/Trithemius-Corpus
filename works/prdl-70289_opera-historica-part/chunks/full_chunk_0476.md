Nicolaus of Wachenheim, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned in secular philosophy, outstanding in ability and distinguished in eloquence, who in the Heidelberg school for almost fifty years, by teaching, lecturing, and disputing, was held in the highest esteem as a prince among theologians, wrote some works of reading not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of his name even to posterity. Of these few have come to me: *Questions on the Sentences*, one book; many sermons, one book; several conferences and orations, one book; *Quia approbante sacri* [unclear]; and certain other works. He flourished at Heidelberg under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1470. Already very old, he died under Pope Sixtus IV, about seven years before this.

Michael of Milan, of the Order of Friars Minor, studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, distinguished in life and conduct, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, wrote, for the use of those who devote themselves to preaching, works not to be despised, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these there are extant: sermons on the season, one book; sermons on the saints, one book; sermons through Advent, one book; sermons on penance, beginning *Per quod* [unclear], one book; sermons on sins, one book; sermons on the Ten Commandments, one book; and certain other works. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1480, and died under Pope Innocent VIII.

Sixtus IV, pope, Ligurian by nation, born in the maritime city of Savona, previously called Franciscus, minister general of the Order of Friars, sat after Paul II in the chair of Peter for thirteen years. He was a man learned in the divine Scriptures and an excellent disputant, a lover of the clergy, a promoter of religious, and singular in beneficence, especially toward those whom a life of observance commended. When Pope Paul once heard him disputing, he admired him so greatly that he made him worthy of the honor of the cardinalate. Before his pontificate he wrote certain excellent little works. Among these the following are reported: *On Future Contingents*, one book; *On the Power of God*, one book; *On the Blood of Christ*, one book; and certain other works. He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1474, indiction 2.

Johannes Consobrinus, Portuguese by nation, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine writings and not ignorant of secular literature and canon law, outstanding in ability, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, first in England and then in Portugal gave great and effective barkings against the enemies of the faith. He rejected and refuted very many heresies and errors by disputing, preaching, and writing. At the request of the provost of the royal college of Saint Mary of Amhten [unclear] in England, he wrote a distinguished tripartite work on contracts and exchanges, which he entitled *On Commutative Justice*, two books; various sermons, one book; and certain other works. He shortened the *Conflatum* of Franciscus Maro into a pleasing compendium. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1475, and under Pope Sixtus IV died at Lisbon, poisoned for the faith.

Henricus, surnamed Prudens, of the Order of Carthusians, prior of the house of the Valley near Bruges in Flanders, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in ability, devout in life and conduct and singularly devoted to God, wrote for the instruction of religious certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there is extant a very celebrated work, most suited to devotion, divided into three parts according to the differences of states, of beginners and of the perfect, in the manner of a dialogue, in which speakers are introduced: an angel and a monk, Jesus and the heavenly Father, Mary, the loving mother-in-law [unclear] of holy souls.
