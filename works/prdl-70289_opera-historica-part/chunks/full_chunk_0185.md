Therefore, when he had entered Pannonia with a strong force, after some days the Hungarians gathered and promised him complete fidelity; he sent his soldiers back into Bavaria, and after his coronation, when he had penetrated farther into Hungary, he was captured by a certain prince of the region. Since he would not take that prince’s daughter as his wife, with the help of a certain servant he was let down over the wall, changed his clothing, escaped alone, and in rustic guise, after traversing Prussia and many lands, came to Wrocław, where, being recognized and betrayed, he was again captured by the duke of Poland. After taking his daughter in marriage, he was received with great honor in Bavaria; and not long afterward he died.

*Otto.*

OTTO, king of Hungary and duke of Bavaria, when he saw that the time of his death was drawing near, entrusted his son Henry, a boy of fourteen days, who afterward was called prince of [Natternberg?], and the two sons of his brother Stephen, Henry, who was eight years of age, and Otto, who had not yet completed his seventh year, to the citizens in Landshut and in Straubing, not to be handed over under oath to anyone except Louis, duke of Upper Bavaria; and he died in the year of the Lord 1312. After his death, the nobles of the land demanded that the aforesaid three boys, entrusted by Otto himself to the people of Landshut and Straubing, be given to them, wishing to place them under the guardianship of the duke of Austria and to transfer this dominion to him.

And so, in the year of the Lord 1314, on the day of Saint Theodore the martyr, through this occasion a great battle took place between Duke Louis, afterward emperor, and the duke of Austria, near a certain village called Gammelsdorf. And Duke Louis emerged victorious and obtained for himself the lordship over the boys, as their legitimate guardian.

*Stephen.*

STEPHEN, duke of Bavaria, brother of the aforesaid Otto, king of Hungary, namely the son of Duke Henry in Landshut, left two sons: Henry, surnamed the Good, and Otto of Burghausen. Henry had as wife the daughter of John, king of Bohemia, as we shall say later. These two princes, together with his own son Henry of [Natternberg?], Otto, king of Hungary and duke of Bavaria, now on the point of death, entrusted to the citizens in Landshut and Straubing, to be conveyed to Louis, afterward emperor, their legitimate guardian, as was said above.

OTTO, son of Stephen, duke of Bavaria, brother of the aforesaid Henry, who lived at Burghausen, died without children in the year of the Lord 1335.

HENRY, son of Otto, king of Hungary, called the Younger, or of [Natternberg?], duke of Bavaria, whose mother was the daughter of the duke of Poland, died without children when he was twenty years old, in the year of the Lord 1333. The aforesaid three dukes, Henry the Good, his brother Otto of Burghausen, and Henry the Younger, or of [Natternberg?], son of Otto, king of Hungary, divided Lower Bavaria among themselves, as follows.

*Division of Lower Bavaria among the aforesaid dukes.*

In the year of the Lord 1331 there was a great disagreement between Duke Henry, surnamed the Good, on one side, and Otto his brother and Henry the Younger, son of Otto, king of Hungary, on the other side, concerning the duchy of Lower Bavaria. Therefore John, king of Bohemia, father-in-law of the elder Henry, held a meeting with other princes in Regensburg, with a sure hope of bringing the aforesaid dukes to concord. For Louis the emperor had already called the Hungarians to aid Otto and Henry the Younger. At last, after many negotiations, they agreed that the duchy should be divided among them; and this was done around the feast of Saint Bartholomew the Apostle, in the year as above. And so the lot fell: the elder Henry obtained Landshut, Straubing, Schärding, Pfarrkirchen, and the other surrounding castles. Otto his brother received Burghausen, Öttingen, Traunstein, Hall, and the remaining castles as far as the Salzburg Alps. Further, the lot gave to Henry the Younger Dingolfing, Landau, Vilshofen, Deggendorf, Cham, Bogen, Kelheim, and other towns and castles lying round about.
