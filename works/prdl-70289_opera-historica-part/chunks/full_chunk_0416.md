Johannes Danck of Saxony, a German by nationality, a contemporary of the aforesaid Johannes de Ligneriis and companion in his studies, a man most learned in every respect, a philosopher and astronomer, very celebrated among all men of his time, subtle in intellect and eminent. He produced many distinguished and admirable little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. But thus far I have found very few:

Eclipse Canons, one book: The quantity of an eclipse of the sun.
Canons of the Tables, one book: The time of the fourth aspect.
On the Astrolabe, one book. On Incensions, one book.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Petrus de Palude, a Frenchman by nationality, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy and canon law, an excellent disputant and declaimer. He produced certain distinguished volumes on the holy scriptures, by which he bestowed his name upon immortality. Among these are reported:

On the Sentences, four books. Various Questions, one book.
And various sermons, one book. And many other things.

He is also said to have adorned and expounded many books of the Bible with his commentaries.

Johannes Calderini, an Italian by nationality, from Bologna, once a hearer and disciple of Johannes Andreae, a professor and very celebrated interpreter of canon law, not ignorant of secular philosophy and the divine scriptures, prompt in intellect and clear in speech. He wrote not a few volumes in his faculty, by which, proving his ability through diligent exercise, he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity. Among these I found a great and distinguished work, divided into three not small volumes, which he entitled:

Repertory of Law, one book: A. This word sometimes from...
On the Decretals, one book.
On Ecclesiastical Interdict, one book: Although there are several doubts concerning...
Many Counsels, one book. Of the others I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Louis IV the Bavarian, emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Paulus de Liazariis, a hearer of Johannes Andreae of Bologna and fellow student of Johannes Calderini, a man most skilled in both laws and nobly instructed in the precepts of the philosophers, distinguished in intellect and speech, who was by no means the least among the doctors of law in his time. Among the other little works of his ability he wrote a distinguished volume, which is called:

On the Clementines, one book.
And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Louis IV the Bavarian, emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Lupoldus of Bebenburg, bishop of Bamberg, a German by nationality, once, as they report, a hearer of Johannes Andreae of Bologna, a very celebrated jurist, and nobly learned both in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, subtle in intellect and clear in speech. He produced little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with great glory. Among these there is extant a distinguished work on the zeal for the Christian religion of the ancient princes:

Of the Germans, one book. On the Rights of the Roman Empire, one book.
And certain other volumes on diverse subjects, which have come to me.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Louis IV and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Nicolaus of Modena, a man nobly learned in both laws and especially in the laws, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, cautious in knowledge and counsel, so that he might leave memorials of his ability to posterity. He wrote certain distinguished volumes in civil law, by which he had made himself useful to those then living and known to those to come. Among these are reported:

On the Code, nine books. On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.
On the New Digest, eleven books. On the Infortiatum, three books.

He flourished in the times of Louis IV the Bavarian, emperor, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1348.
