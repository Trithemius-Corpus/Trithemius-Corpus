*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Sermons for Lent, one book. Certain letters.

He was present in person at the Council of Constance in the year of the Lord 1418. After his death, because of frequent miracles, he was canonized by Pope Calixtus III in the year of the Lord 1455, in the third indiction. His feast is in April.

Boniface Ferrer of Valencia, a Catalan by nation, of the Carthusian order, the twenty-fifth prior, brother of the aforesaid blessed Vincent the preacher, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and an outstanding doctor of canon law, not falling short of his holy brother in life and conduct, governed the Grande Chartreuse energetically by word and example. Among other things he wrote a notable treatise on why few in the Carthusian order have been canonized and why they do not perform miracles publicly.

To Boniface of the same order, one book. On the approbation of the order, one book. Certain letters. And many other things. He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Lupus of Oliveto, a Spaniard by nation, of the order of the hermit-monks of Saint Jerome, prior general, a man devoted to and learned in the divine writings, and an outstanding restorer of his order, wrote several excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these are reported, from the sayings of Saint Jerome:

The Rule of his order, one book: “Dearest brothers, not...” Sermons for the season, one book. Sermons on the Saints, one book. And certain other things. Martin V confirmed this newly compiled rule in thirty-two chapters. He flourished at the time of the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1426.

Dominic of San Gimignano, a most distinguished legal consultant, learned both in the divine Scriptures and in the other letters of the secular disciplines, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, wrote certain excellent volumes in canon law, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is an eminent and very celebrated work, divided into two volumes, two books: “Because the first gloss without...”

On the Sixth Book of the Decretals. And various declarations. The other works which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

Paul of Saint Mary, Jewish by birth, Christian in faith, first bishop of Cartagena and afterwards bishop of Burgos in Spain, arch-chancellor of the king of Castile and Leon, a man most devoted to and very learned in the divine writings, and master of sacred theology, having been converted in his mature age to the Catholic faith, advanced so far in life and doctrine that among all the doctors of Spain he had no equal in his time. He wrote for his son Alfonso, dean of Compostela and doctor of laws, on the Postils of Nicholas of Lyra:

Notable Additions, one book: “What do you wish for yourself, that living...” Scrutiny of the Scriptures, two books: “Search the Scriptures...” And certain other things.

He flourished and completed this Scrutiny of his under Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugene IV, in the year of the Lord 1434, in the twelfth indiction, in the eighty-first year of his age.

Petrus Paulus Vergerius, by homeland a Justinopolitan, a man devoted to the divine writings and most learned in secular letters, and a very celebrated rhetorician, perfectly trained in Greek and Latin speech, formerly a hearer of Chrysoloras, the Byzantine philosopher who taught Greek letters at Venice, wrote certain excellent little works of excellent talent, which he left to posterity. Among these there is an eminent work containing the deeds of the Carrara lords, one book: “A very beautiful history...”

In Praise of Saint Jerome, one book. And many other things. On Liberal Manners, one book. He flourished at the Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Maffeo Vegio, by homeland of Lodi, datary of Pope Martin V, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, outstandingly learned, and in secular letters most learned, a very celebrated orator and poet, distinguished no less in conduct than in knowledge: through his care and expense the relics of Saint Monica, mother of Saint Augustine, were translated from Ostia Tiberina to Rome and deposited in a new chapel, expensively built by him, in which he himself is buried.
