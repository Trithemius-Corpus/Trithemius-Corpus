Johannes Langer of Bolkenhain, priest of Wroclaw, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, excellent in verse and prose, in life and conduct worthy of a priest. He wrote many excellent little works in both kinds of style, by which he made his name known. Of these there exists a distinguished work addressed to Georg von Stein, lord of Steynaw, which he entitled:

On [unclear] and [unclear], one book. “Today I am oppressed by a great [unclear].”
Letters to various persons, one book. Many poems.
And many other things.

He is still living in Wroclaw and writing various works, under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Alexander VI, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Werner Rolevinck, of Laer, a German by nation, by homeland a Westphalian, of the Carthusian order, of the house of Saint Barbara in Cologne, a man most devoted to and very learned in the divine Scriptures, excellent in talent, devout in life and conduct, published many excellent little works, by which he made himself useful to the present and memorable to the future. Of these the following survive: a large work on all the epistles of Paul, which he entitled:

The Teaching of Paul, two books. “The teaching of Paul the apostle.”
On the Life of Paul, one book. “Just as I was with Moses, so.”
On the Value of Masses, one book. On the Rule of Princes, one book.
On the Study of Theology, one book.
The Bundle of Times, one book. “Generation and genera.”
The Paradise of Conscience, one book. “The Lord God took the man.”
Many sermons, one book. And many other things.

I once saw in his cell treatises, small indeed, but numerous and useful, whose titles oblivion has now taken from my memory. He is still living in the Cologne Charterhouse, of advanced age, and writing various works under Maximilian, king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494, the twelfth indiction.

Arnold Bostius, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Ghent, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and exceptionally learned in secular letters, a distinguished theologian, philosopher, orator, and poet, acute in talent, eloquent in speech, devout in life and conduct, of whom several letters to me survive. He wrote many excellent little works in verse and prose. Of these the following survive: a distinguished volume written in verse and prose, in which he clearly shows the prerogative origin and progress of his order in view of Marian protection; for that reason it was pleasing that it be entitled:

On the Patronage of Mary, one book. “You asked, most distinguished man.”
On the Illustrious Men of the Carthusians, one book. “To the venerable and among [unclear].”
On the Illustrious Men of His Own Order, one book. “Brother Arnold Bostius.”
Against Vincent in the New Castle, one book. “How long always [unclear].”
Letters to various persons, one book. On the Four Last Things, one book.
Various poems. And many other things.

He is still living at Ghent, in vigorous age, and composing various works, and by his example and writings he summons many to sacred studies. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

Heinrich Euticus, an East Franconian, an excellent professor of both kinds of medicine, has published many things which he prepares for posterity. Also, in a varied and pleasing character of poems, a very beautiful little work which he entitled:

Jests, or Urbane and Witty Jokes of Young Men.
In Praise of the Blessed Virgin. And many other things.
A Satire against Sophists and Dialecticians and Enemies of the Study of the Humanities.

He is still living, when we wrote these things, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.
