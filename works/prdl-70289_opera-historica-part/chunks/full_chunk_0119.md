He left three sons: Pippin, Carloman, and Grifo. Of these, Pippin and Carloman divided their father’s principate between themselves, excluding Grifo, and in this way ruled over the Franks for four years. After four years, however, Carloman, moved by the divine spirit, resigned the whole principate to his brother Pippin, went to Rome, and became a monk on Monte Cassino. Pippin then ruled the principate alone for six years, and, after King Hilderic had been deposed, was made king by common vote.

*Grifo is captured and consigned to custody. Carloman became a monk. Pippin holds the principate in the [unclear] year.*

Grifo, grieving that he had been scorned by his brothers, on the advice of his mother seized the city of Laon in advance and declared war on his brothers. His mother was the stepmother of Pippin and Carloman. They quickly gathered their army, surrounded Laon with a siege, took the city, and held their brother captive for four years.

*Saint Winfrid, who is Boniface, archbishop of Mainz. The monasteries of Hersfeld and Fulda are founded. Convents of nuns at Kitzingen and Bischofsheim.*

In these times there flourished in the kingdom of the Franks Saint Boniface, English by nation, a monk by profession, archbishop of the church of Mainz by rank, an apostolic man, who by preaching converted the Thuringians and many others to Christ, for whom he afterward suffered martyrdom while preaching in Frisia. He [founded?] two [monasteries?] in the forest of Buchonia, [unclear] diocese: one of them is called Hersfeld, in the diocese of Mainz; the other, from the river Fulda flowing past it, in which he was buried, is in the diocese of Würzburg.

He also built several convents of nuns; among them there still stands out the monastery in the town of Kitzingen beside the Main, in the diocese of Würzburg. Over it he appointed a holy virgin named Hadeloga; [the reading concerning] Thecla, whom he named, born from England, [is unclear]. Another was near the town of Bischofsheim, beside the Tauber, in the diocese of Mainz; over it he appointed Saint Lioba, a virgin beloved by God, likewise English by homeland. Of that convent nothing survives today.

*Odilo, duke of Bavaria, [took Pippin’s sister as wife?]. Adelung the priest, blind, receives sight. The bishopric of Würzburg is established.*

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 746, which was the fifth year of King Hilderic, in the fourteenth Roman indiction, Adelung, a priest at Würzburg, blind and warned in a dream to seek the bodies of Saint Kilian the bishop and his companion martyrs, recovered his sight when the [unclear] bones had been found. When Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, learned of this miracle, with the help of Pippin, prince of the court of the Franks, he obtained from Pope Zacharias that he should establish a new episcopal seat at Würzburg, to the honor of almighty God and of Saint Kilian the bishop and his companion martyrs.

Therefore, in the seventh year of Hilderic, king of the Franks, and in the year of the Lord’s nativity 748, in the first indiction, Saint Burghard, English by nation and a monk by profession, was ordained by Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, as the first bishop of the church of Würzburg, and presided for forty years. And in the same year little crosses, marked as if with oil, appeared on people’s linen garments.
