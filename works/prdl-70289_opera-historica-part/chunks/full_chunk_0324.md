*224 The Abbot of Sponheim*

SIMPLICIANUS of Milan, bishop after Saint Ambrose, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, a lover of poverty, urged Augustine, while he was still a presbyter, in many letters to exercise his talent and devote himself to expositions of the Scriptures, so that he might seem, like another Ambrose, an instigator of Origen’s work. Hence Augustine resolved many questions in letters addressed to him. But he himself wrote useful letters to Augustine and various others, which are found partly here and there. He flourished under the rulers Theodosius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

ISIDORE the elder, bishop of Cordoba, a man very skilled in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, quick in talent, sufficiently eloquent in speech, distinguished no less by the conduct of his life than by knowledge of the Scriptures, whose learning was held in great esteem among the ancients, wrote not a few small works in various volumes. Of these, however, I have found an excellent work which he published for Saint Orosius, the Spanish presbyter:

On the Four Books of Kings, 4 books.

Of the rest I have found nothing. He flourished under the rulers Theodosius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 420.

OROSIUS, presbyter, Spanish by nationality, once a hearer of blessed Augustine, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures, most skilled in secular letters, and very renowned in life and conduct. Blessed Augustine sent him, when he came to him from Spain to learn the question concerning the nature of the soul, to Saint Jerome at Bethlehem of Judea; there, after staying for some time, he clearly learned the question for which he had come, and, returning to Hippo, carried with him relics of Stephen the protomartyr. To this most studious man there are extant several works of blessed Augustine, varied resolutions of questions of the Scriptures. He himself wrote the following volumes:

Apologetic, 7 books.
Against the Pelagians, 1 book.
Chronicle on the Hormesta of the world, 1 book.
On the Reason of the Soul, 1 book.
Several Letters to Augustine, 1 book.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book.

He flourished, illustrious in life and doctrine, with Saint Augustine, in the year of the Lord 420.

JOHN, bishop and patriarch of Jerusalem, devoted and learned in the holy Scriptures, but too intent on the teachings of Origen, stirred up writings of the fathers against himself, namely of the most holy presbyter Jerome and of Bishop Epiphanius, as is found in the letters sent among their acts. But John himself, giving an account of his own study, says that he imitated not the faith of Origen but his talent. For this reason especially he wrote a noteworthy work:

Against the Detractors of His Study, 1 book.

And certain letters to various persons, and certain other things. He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 400.

MAXIMUS, bishop of Turin, most learned in the holy Scriptures, second to none in his time in delivering homilies to the people, was distinguished not only in doctrine but also shone most brightly in holiness of life. He wrote many excellent small works, which holy mother Church has received to be read with great authority. Among these the following are reported:

On Chapters of the Gospels, 1 book.
On the Acts of the Apostles, 1 book.
In Praise of the Apostles, 1 book. Incipit: “The most glorious solemnities of Christ to us.”
In Praise of John the Baptist, 1 book. Incipit: “Of the holy fathers my.”
On the Life of Eusebius of Vercelli, 1 book. Incipit: “Concerning the words of Isaiah.”
On Saint Cyprian the Martyr, 1 book. Incipit: “The feast of Saint Cyprian.”
On the Grace of Baptism, 1 book. Incipit: “On avarice. The waning of the moon.”
On Hospitality, 1 book. Incipit: “On the tradition of the judge.”
On Alms, 1 book.
On the Lord’s Sepulcher, 3 homilies.
On the Office of the Mass, 1 book.
On the Accusation of the Lord, 1 homily.
On the Resurrection of the Lord, 4 homilies. Incipit: “Not undeservedly, brothers.”
On Saint Lawrence, 1 homily. Incipit: “It is holy, brothers, and God.”
