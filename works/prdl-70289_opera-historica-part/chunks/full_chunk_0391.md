*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

[of the Friars Minor?] general minister, and thereafter bishop of Albano, and cardinal of the most holy Roman Church; formerly a hearer and disciple of Alexander of Hales; a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, devout and most holy in life and conduct. His teacher Alexander, considering the gift of his intellect and the purity of his life, used to say: "In Brother Bonaventure Adam does not seem to have sinned." He wrote many profound and most devout little works, in which, putting forth burning words, he kindles the reader's affection in the love of Christ no less than he illumines the intellect with holy teachings. For he easily surpasses all the doctors of his time in the usefulness of his works, if you attend to the spirit of divine love and Christian devotion speaking in him. He is profound, not wordy; subtle, not curious; eloquent, not vain; putting forth flaming, not inflated, words. Hence he is read more safely, understood more easily by one who loves, frequented more usefully, retained more sweetly and fruitfully. Many put forth doctrine; many preach devotion; few by writing books have taught both. But Bonaventure surpassed many few, while his devotion instructs doctrine and his doctrine devotion. Therefore, if you wish to be learned and devout, be intent upon his little works. These are:

He also published some other works which have not come to my knowledge. He died while the Empire was vacant, under Pope Gregory X, in the Council of Lyons, in the year of the Lord 1283, indiction 1. He was buried in the cathedral church of Lyons. At length, on account of his holiness and miracles, in our days he was canonized by Pope Sixtus IV, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1482, indiction 15.

Philip, chancellor of Paris, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in secular philosophy, since he was a famous master of sacred letters, presided gloriously for a long time over the theological school at Paris, where he magnificently instructed disciples by lecturing and peoples by preaching. He wrote two or three sermons on each of the Psalms.
