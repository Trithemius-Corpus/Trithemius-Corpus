STEPHAN HOEST of Ladenburg, formerly a canon of the church of Speyer, German by nation, a man learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, keen in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in declaiming sermons to the people, since for some years he held the office of preacher in the castle of Heidelberg. He wrote works not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there is extant:

On the books of the Ethics, 10 books. Sermons of collation, 1 book.
Certain other poems.

He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1461, in the fourth indiction.

BERNARD JUSTINIAN, a Venetian by homeland, a very celebrated philosopher and orator, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, who, on account of his singular grace in speaking, was held in esteem not only before his own Venetian senate, but also before the Roman pontiffs, cardinals, the other princes of Italy, and the senates of cities. Among other monuments of his talent he wrote of Lorenzo Giustiniani, patriarch of Venice, his maternal uncle:

His life and most holy manner of life, 1 book. Also Histories, 1 book.
Several elegant speeches, 1 book. Letters and certain other things, 1 book.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1470.

HENRY DE PIRO, German by nation, born from the city of Cologne, of the Carthusian order, professed of the house of Saint Barbara in the same city; before his entry into the order a subtle and famous doctor of both laws, and afterward also practiced in the divine Scriptures. He wrote certain excellent volumes, of which the following are reported:

On the Institutes, 4 books. Annua te mihi [unclear].
Also To Novices, 1 book. Counsels in various cases, 1 book.
Sermons on the season, 1 book. Sermons on the saints, 1 book.

And certain other things. He flourished in the times of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1490.

NICHOLAS DONIS, German by nation, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man studious and learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a distinguished philosopher and mathematician, a most vigilant finder and very sagacious restorer of Ptolemy’s Cosmography, outstanding in talent and clear in speech. He wrote volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant a remarkable work, with new illustrations and tables most elegantly arranged and most diligently corrected:

On the Cosmography of Ptolemy,
To Pope Paul II, 7 books. It does not escape me, blessed [father].
Also On Marvelous Places, 1 book. Since, as Augustine says.
Various letters to different persons.

And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1490.

GEORGE OF PEUERBACH, German by nation, a man most skilled in the knowledge of secular letters, not ignorant of the holy Scriptures, easily the most brilliant philosopher and astronomer of his time, who in the university of Vienna publicly taught philosophy and the science of the stars for a long time; there, among countless other disciples, he instructed Johannes of Regiomontanus, afterward a most learned man. He wrote certain excellent works of a very penetrating talent. Of their number I have seen only:

New Theories of the Planets, 1 book. The sun has three spheres [unclear].

I have as yet seen nothing of the others. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and the Roman pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1470.
