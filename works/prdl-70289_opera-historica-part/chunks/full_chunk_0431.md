Richard Maidstone, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, a man very learned in the divine scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian; distinguished in ability, rather scholastic in speech, and an eminent declaimer. He wrote many commendable minor works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found:

*Against the Lollards of Wyclif*, book I. *Sermons on the Season*, book I.
*Sermons on the Saints*, book I. The rest are unknown to me.

He flourished at the school of Oxford, especially at that time when the heresy of John Wyclif began to sprout in England, and he wrote various works.

Marsilius of Inghen, English by nation, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and most nobly instructed in secular philosophy, the first planter of the school of Heidelberg, which Rupert, duke of Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine, established and magnificently endowed, and which Pope Urban VI confirmed on the model of Paris; also, as Wimpheling testifies, canon and treasurer of the church of St. Andrew at Cologne. He published minor works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these, in the library of Heidelberg today, written in his own hand, are held:

*Questions on the Sentences*, book II. *Notable Dialectic*, book I.
*Commentaries on Aristotle*, several books. And certain other things.

He died under the Emperor Wenceslas and Pope Boniface [unclear], in the year of the Lord 1384, on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of September, in the second indiction. He was buried at Heidelberg in the church of St. Peter. There are those who write that he was German; to them I freely concede their judgment. But it is established that he was called from the school of Paris to Heidelberg for the beginning of the university.

John, monk of the monastery of Castell, in the diocese of Eichstatt, of the order of St. Benedict, German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters; excellent in ability and eloquent in speech, an eminent theologian, philosopher, and rhetorician. He wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only a great and very distinguished work, divided into two volumes, which he published with great and commendable study.

Conrad of Monte Puellarum, German by nation, canon of the church of Regensburg, a man learned in the divine scriptures and very learned in secular philosophy, subtle in ability and distinguished in eloquence. First in the school of Erfurt [unclear] the rudiments of the liberal arts; then [unclear] to the university of Paris, where, drawing in philosophy and sacred letters, he then betook himself to the university of Paris, where, publicly reading philosophy and sacred letters to students for eight years, he attained the insignia of the doctorate. He wrote for Lord Lupold, bishop of Bamberg, a great and noble work, which he entitled according to the Greeks:

*Economics*, book II. All of moral philosophy.
*Monastics*, to the duke of Austria, book I. *Politics* also, book I.
*Various Questions*, book I.

And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the Emperor Wenceslas and Pope Urban VI, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Henry Boyc, Breton by nation, from the diocese of Leon, as he testifies at the beginning of his work on the Decretals; a man most learned in canon law, nobly instructed in scholastic philosophy, outstanding in ability, distinguished in eloquence, eloquent and abundant in judgment. He wrote in that faculty volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Decretals*, book II. *Venerabilibus et dif.*
*On the Sixth Book of the Decretals*, book I. *On the Clementines*, book I.

It is also reported that he published commentaries on the Decrees, and various other treatises on different subjects, which have by no means come into our hands.
