*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Antimachus wrote many poems, and [unclear]; among individual [unclear] scarcely a single Plato is found and remains. But we are now speaking of these things out of season. Arator flourished under the emperor Justinian, in the year of the Lord 540.

Victor, bishop of the [Tunnunensian?] church in Africa, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly trained in secular letters, for his defense of the Three Chapters against the emperor Justinian and Eutychius, bishop of the city of Constantinople, when he resisted the emperor and bishop as detractors of those same chapters, was sent to a monastery of that same city to be kept under guard, and, as they say, died remaining under the same condemnation. Among other little works of his talent he wrote, from the beginning of the world down to the first year of Justin the Younger’s empire, a very useful history On Deeds and Ecclesiastical Wars, one book; For the Defense of the Three Chapters, one book; and certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Justinian, in the year of the Lord 510.

Gregory, the first pope from the monastic order of St. Benedict, a Roman by birth, sat after Pope Pelagius in the chair of Peter for 13 years and 6 months. He was a man most learned in divine Scripture and likewise most learned in secular letters, the prince of theologians, the splendor of philosophers, and the light of rhetoricians, upright and most holy in life and conduct. As he was expounding the divine Scriptures, the Holy Spirit sometimes appeared to him in visible form, and is reported to have revealed to him the hidden mysteries of those same four bishops. At Rome, in a synod of twenty-four bishops held at St. Peter’s, he established many things for the benefit of the Church and promulgated a decree concerning the immunity of monasteries. He wrote many necessary volumes, of which the following are given:

To Leander, bishop of Seville.
Moralia on the Book of Job, 35 books. “Among many, often...”
On Pastoral Care, 2 books. “The care of the pastoral office...”
Dialogues on the Miracles of the Saints, 4 books. “One day, when exceedingly...”
On the Song of Songs, 4 books. “After from paradise...”
Homilies on the Gospels, 40 homilies, 2 books. “By the inspiration of almighty God...”
A brief Explanation of the Four Gospels, 1 book. “Our Lord and Redeemer...”
On the Seven Penitential Psalms, 4 books. “Matthew, as in...”
Letters to various persons, 10 books. “Gregory, bishop, servant...” or otherwise “Very necessary...”
Rationale of Divine Things, 1 book.
Proems to the New and Old Testament, 1 book.
On the Sacraments, 1 book.

When he was still a deacon, in the person of Pope Pelagius, he composed a very notable letter to Elias, bishop of Aquileia, who was unwilling to accept the Three Chapters of the Council of Chalcedon; it is not found among his letters. He is also said to have written many other works, namely on the prophets, on the books of Kings, also on the books of Moses, and on other holy Scriptures, which after his death, before they had yet been multiplied, his most wicked rivals, envying his glory and holiness, burned with fire, as John the Deacon complains in the book of his life. The most blessed Pope Gregory died in the second year of the emperor Phocas, which was the year of the Lord 605, in the eighth indiction, on the fourth day before the Ides of March, shining with many miracles.

Leander, bishop of Seville, formerly a monk, a Carthaginian by birth, from the province of Spain, born of his father Severianus, was a most studious man, nobly learned both in divine Scripture and in secular letters, sweet in eloquence, most outstanding in talent, and also holy in life and conduct and beloved by God. By his preaching and effort Hermenegild, son of Leovigild, the Arian king of the Goths, was brought back to the confession of the orthodox faith and, killed by his heretical father on the vigil of Easter, received the crown of martyrdom. His brother Reccared, as soon as he had received the kingdom after his father’s death, at Leander’s urging and preaching, renounced heresy and converted the whole people of the Visigoths subject to him to the Catholic faith. To this Leander blessed Gregory dedicated the volumes of his commentaries on Job. He himself also wrote several little works, of which the following are given. When he had been driven into exile by the Arians because of the Catholic faith, he wrote:

Against the Heretics, one book.
Against the Institutions of the Arians, one book.
On Virginity, to Florentina, one book.
Prayers, in a double edition, on the Psalms, one book.
Letters to His Brother, one book.
On the Praise of Sacrifices, one book.

He also published several other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the year of the Lord 590, under the emperor Maurice. He died under Reccared, the most devout prince of the Goths, shining with miracles.
