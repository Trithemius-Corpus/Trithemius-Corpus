*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Theophrastus, *On Plants*, one book. Aristotle, *Problems*, one book. The same, *On Animals*, one book. And certain other things. He died at Rome under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1478, in the eleventh indiction.

John de Lutria, German by nation, a man devoted to and learned in sacred Scripture, and excellently trained in scholastic philosophy, keen in intellect, illustrious in life, and a very celebrated preacher of sermons, first taught theology gloriously in the university of Erfurt, and afterward energetically carried out the office of preacher in the church of Mainz. He wrote certain little works of no contemptible reading, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported: *On the Sentences*, four books; various sermons, one book; *Disputed Questions*, one book; and certain works in philosophy, one book. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1479, in the twelfth indiction.

[Alanus?] de Rupe, German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in sacred Scripture, of the highest reputation in delivering sermons to the people, devoted to God in life and conduct, and a special lover of the blessed Mary ever virgin and a venerable promoter of her rosary and service; by her also he several times merited to be refreshed with the singular gift of heavenly consolation, and now to be sustained in eternal felicity. Among other works of his talent he wrote: *Compendium of the Marian Psalter*, one book, beginning, 'Because Catholic truth ...'; *On the Miracles of the Rosary*, one book, beginning, 'Although beautiful praise [unclear]'; likewise various sermons, one book; and certain other things. He died in his convent at Zwolle under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1474, in the seventh indiction, on the vigil of the Assumption of the blessed Virgin Mary. He recalled to the minds and practices of men her rosary and the psalter commemorative of Christ's Passion, which had long since been neglected; in whose honor [unclear]. By fruitfully practicing these, he also commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of their number the following are extant: *On the Monochord*, one book, beginning, 'Since, as a certain wise man ...'; *On the Method of Singing Well*, one book, beginning, 'For the benefit of the whole clergy ...'; *On the Ending of the Collects*, one book, beginning, 'Although many concerning ...'; many and various sermons, one book; and certain other works. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1470, under Pope Sixtus IV, under whom he also died.

Conradus de Zabernia, German by nation, a man devoted to and practiced in sacred Scripture, and not ignorant of secular literature, especially music, teachable in intellect and exemplary in life, clear and devout in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, in plain speech, certain little works and volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity; of these the following are reported: *On the Seven Deadly Sins*, one book.

Laurentius Calcaneus of Brescia, a noble man, a knight with the golden spur, and a very celebrated doctor of laws, a philosopher and most lucid historian, distinguished in intellect and eloquence, and a most acute disputant of questions. He wrote certain distinguished works by which he transmitted his name to posterity; among them the following are reported: *On the Holy Conception of Mary*, one book; also many legal opinions, one book; and certain other small works. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1478, in the eleventh indiction.

John of Dorsten, German by nation, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man devoted to and learned in sacred Scripture, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, and sufficiently fit for disputing and disentangling questions of Scripture; also of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people, who was held in esteem for teaching and preaching in the university of Erfurt. He wrote little works by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these are reported: sermons for the temporal cycle, one book; sermons on the saints, one book.

Angelus Saxo of Brunswick, German by nation, a man learned in sacred Scripture [unclear], under Pope [Sixtus] IV, in the year of the Lord 1475.
