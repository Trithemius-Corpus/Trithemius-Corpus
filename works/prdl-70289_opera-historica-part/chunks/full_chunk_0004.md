*The Life and Writings of Johannes Trithemius, first Abbot of Sponheim, and afterward of St. James at Würzburg, from the Letter of Johannes Duraclusius, disciple of the same Trithemius, to Nicolaus Hamerius Emelanus.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot, was born in the village of Trittenheim, in the diocese of Trier, on the banks of the river Moselle, on the first day of February, at the eleventh hour and thirtieth minute after noon, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1462, in the tenth Roman indiction: his father was Johannes of Heidenberg, and his mother was called Elisabeth of Longuich. This was, namely, the year in which the city of Mainz, in the dissension of two pontiffs, was captured, lost its liberty, and found servitude, many of its citizens having been driven into exile; in the fourth year then running of Pope Pius, the second of that name, and the twenty-second of Emperor Frederick III.

In the year of the Lord 1482, however, in the monastery of Sponheim in the diocese of Mainz, on the first day of February, that is, the day on which he had formerly been born, he laid aside the secular habit and there took up the monastic one under the rule of the blessed father Benedict, according to the observance newly introduced. In the following year, which was 1483, after Johannes of Colenhussen, the abbot, had been transferred by canonical postulation to Seligenstadt, Johannes Trithemius, by the common election of the brothers, was advanced and adorned as abbot of the aforesaid monastery of Sponheim on the fourth day before the Kalends of July, and presided for twenty-three years, one month, and nineteen days. When these had passed, not without many labors, provoked by the rashness of certain rivals, he accepted the abbey of St. James at Würzburg, which was being offered to him, and of his own free will resigned Sponheim, full of useless cares.

In both places he wrote several little works, from which he recently set down for me with his own hand in a note the items written below. Since you, my friend, ask me for their titles, I gladly agree to your wishes, so that, from consideration of so many of his labors, you may have the memory of our common friend and teacher [unclear] useless presumptions.

He wrote first the Constitutions of his order, which were made in provincial chapters by command of the presidents, one book. On the manner of holding a provincial chapter, one book. On the Rule of Saint Benedict, two books. Homilies and exhortatory sermons to his monks, two books, which he dedicated to his predecessor. On the temptations of cloistered persons and their remedies, two books. To Nicolaus, priest of Münstermaifeld, on the priestly life, one book. Against the private property of monks, one book. A penthic work, that is, a mournful one, on the ruin of his order, one book. On the illustrious men of his order, four books. Praises of the order of the Carmelite brothers, two books. To Gerlacus, abbot of Deutz, praises of manuscript writers, one book. To Johannes Dalburgius, bishop of Worms, on ecclesiastical writers, one book. On the praises of Saint Anne, grandmother of the Lord, one book. To the questions of Autgerus Sicamber, canon of Deventer, one book. A chronicle of his monastery of Sponheim, no small volume, one book. A chronicle or annals of the monastery of Hirsau, two large volumes. [unclear] the prince elector, a single book. The life of Saint Irmina, nun and first abbess in the Horrea at Trier, one book. On the misery of cloistered prelates, two books. Praises of Saint Andrew the Apostle, one book.
