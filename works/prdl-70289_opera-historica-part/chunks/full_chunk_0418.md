Hermann of Schildis, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man most devoted to and very skilled in the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent, and distinguished no less in life than in learning and knowledge, published many excellent volumes, in which he showed the abundant vein of his talent and transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with great praise and glory. Of these the following are reported: *On the First Book of the Sentences*, 1 book; *A Lecture on Genesis*, 1 book; *A Double Hexaemeron*, 1 book; *On the Song of Songs*, 1 book; *On the Lord’s Prayer*, in two treatments, 2 books; *On the Matter of the Song of Songs*, 1 book; *Brevilogue*, 1 book; *An Exposition of the Mass*, 1 book; *On the Four Senses of Scripture*, 1 book; *On the Capital Vices*, 1 book; *A Manual for Priests*, 1 book; *On the Conception of Saint Mary*, 1 book; *The Cloister of the Soul*, 1 book; *Introduction to Law*, 1 book; *On True and False Friendship*, 1 book; *On the Canonical Hours*, 1 book; *An Exposition of the Mass*, 1 book; *Against the Flagellants*, 1 book; *On the Forty-Two Stations*, 1 book; *On the Comparison of the Mass*, 1 book; *The Division of Philosophy*, in verse, 1 book; *On the chapter Omnis utriusque*, 1 book; various sermons, 1 book; preachable conferences, 1 book; *Various Questions*, 1 book; *On Aristotle’s Rhetoric*, 1 book. He also published many other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Louis IV and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Guilhelmus of Kayothus, of the Order of Preachers, provincial prior in France, a man learned in the divine writings, and nobly instructed both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, subtle in talent and clear in eloquence, of excellent ability in declaiming sermons to the people, wrote certain works not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity with praise. He abridged the *Summa* of Johannes Teutonicus, which is called *The Confessors’ Summa*, 1 book, “What is simony ...”; various sermons, 1 book. The rest which he composed have by no means come to my knowledge.

Peter of Casa, a Limousin, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the fourteenth prior general, then bishop of [Basionia?], and finally, as they report, patriarch of Jerusalem, a man most learned in the divine writings, ready in talent and clear in eloquence, learned and eloquent in declaiming sermons to the people, published certain excellent volumes by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are reported: *On the Sentences*, 4 books; sermons for the season, 1 book; sermons on the saints, 1 book; and certain other works. He flourished, and was elected prior general of his order from the convent of Limoges, under Emperor Louis IV and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1350, indiction 13. He ruled his order energetically for twelve years.

Paulus of Perugia, of the Order of the Friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man devoted to and learned in the divine writings, and sufficiently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in talent and scholastic in speech, published several works of great authority, by which, exercising a brilliant talent, he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity with great glory. Of these there survives a very celebrated and almost divine work, which he recited in the University of Paris: *On the Sentences*, 1 book, “Concerning the first book of the Sentences ...”; also *Quodlibets*, 1 book; and certain other works. He flourished at Paris and completed his lecture under Louis the Bavarian, Emperor IV, and Pope Clement VI, in the year 1344, indiction 12.
