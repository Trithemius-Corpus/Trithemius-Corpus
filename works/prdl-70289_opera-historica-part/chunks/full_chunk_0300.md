Pamphilus, priest of Eusebius of Caesarea, out of love for the Scriptures gathered so many books at Caesarea that at that time no library in all the earth was more celebrated. Indeed, he copied out with his own hand a great part of the books of Origen and placed them in that same Caesarean library. From him Eusebius obtained the surname Pamphili. But before Eusebius began to write, he wrote some little works of no contemptible authority. Of these I have found only:

Against the Mathematicians, one book.
An Apology for Origen, one book.

He is also said to have written certain letters full of faith and learning. At last, after blessed studies and many labors for the Church of God, he was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ at Caesarea in Palestine, under the persecution of Maximian, who ruled around the year of the Lord 240.

Anatolius of Alexandria, bishop of Laodicea in Syria, a man skilled in the divine Scriptures and second to none in secular letters in his time, a distinguished rhetorician, astronomer, and geometer, left many splendid little works of his genius; yet very few of them have come to our knowledge. There survives his notable volume entitled:

On Easter, one book.
On Arithmetical Instruction, ten books.

He flourished under the emperors Probus and Carus, in the year 285.

Archelaus, bishop of Mesopotamia in Syria, a learned man and acute disputant, wrote some little works in the Syriac language, which are rarely found among the Latins. He vigorously disputed against Mani, the heresiarch coming out of Persia, and confounded all his arguments, although he was unable to recall him from error. He wrote that same disputation:

Against Mani, one book.

Other works are unknown to us. He flourished under the emperors Aurelian and Probus, in the year of the Lord 280.

Lucian, priest of the church of Antioch, a man of such learning and eloquence that, learned and eloquent, he was of excellent ability in delivering homilies to the people, and venerable no less for his life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ under the persecution of Maximian. He was buried at Helenopolis in Bithynia. Among other things he wrote:

On Our Faith, one book.

And various other treatises, sermons, homilies, and letters. He flourished under the emperor Maximian, in the year of the Lord 240.

Pierius, priest of the church of Alexandria, a noble and most learned man, was called the younger Origen; he was most excellent in delivering homilies to the people. Under Theonas, bishop of the Alexandrians, shining forth as most renowned in life and doctrine, he was a distinguished cultivator of voluntary poverty for love of Christ and an admirable teacher of the people. He wrote countless treatises, which nevertheless have not come to our knowledge. There survives his very long discourse delivered to the people on the vigil of Easter:

On the Prophet Hosea, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Diocletian, in the year of the Lord 290.

Phileas, bishop of the city of Thebaid in Egypt, a noble and most learned man, distinguished no less in character than in knowledge of the Scriptures, after many labors and various treatises published for the benefit of the faithful, at last under the emperor Maximian flew away to heaven as a martyr for the faith of Christ. Of his writings there survives a disputation held:

Against an Unjust Judge, one book.
On the Praise of Martyrs.

The other things he wrote are unknown to us, although many are reported by the fathers. He flourished under the emperor Maximian, in the year of the Lord 240.

Arnobius, priest, philosopher, and distinguished rhetorician, while he was still a pagan teaching rhetoric, was called by divine mercy and began to profess himself a Christian. And when, going to the bishops, he sought to be baptized, they did not believe him, fearing that a man then swollen with secular eloquence was attempting to mock the sacraments of the faith; and therefore they deferred their judgment. But Arnobius, seeing that no trust was being placed in him, as proof of his sincerity wrote and offered to the bishops a notable volume:

Against the Nations, seven books.
Also On the Psalter, one book.
