*Johannes Trithemius [unclear]*

... excellently learned in [secular?] letters, a philosopher, jurist, theologian, and very celebrated chronicler, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence. He wrote little works of authority not to be despised, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity; among them the following are reported: A notable work of histories concerning memorable deeds, one book. Also a noteworthy Summa on cases of conscience, one book. Many sermons, and various other treatises; from these we conjecture that he was a religious, although we do not know the distinction of his order. He flourished under Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Johannes of Erfurt, of the order of Friars Minor, an enlightened doctor in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, a most learned interpreter of philosophy and of both laws, subtle in intellect, ready in eloquence. He wrote several volumes of great usefulness, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these, however, few have come into our hands: Table of Law, one book. Also a Summa on cases of conscience, not useless for hearers of confessions, two books. Popular sermons, not inelegant, on the season and on the saints, three books. The other things which he composed have not come into our hands.

Thilmannus of Aachen, of the order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, after Heinrich of Dolendorp provincial prior of Lower Germany, a distinguished doctor of the divine scriptures, presiding over the theological school first at Paris and then at Cologne, acquired great glory for his learning both by teaching and by writing. Among the other works of his talent he wrote: On the Sentences, four books. Also a noteworthy reading on the Gospel of Matthew, which he read publicly at Cologne in his convent, one book. He also disputed various questions. He likewise composed many elegant sermons, and certain other things unknown to us. He flourished under Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Jacobus of Altavilla, by homeland of the Rheingau, fifteenth abbot of the monastery of Eberbach of the Cistercian order, which is situated in the Rheingau almost two miles from Mainz, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, an ornament of the Parisian school, a distinguished doctor, wrote a noteworthy work on the Sentences, four books. But also various questions disputed at Paris, one book; likewise many sermons to the brothers, which are partly extant, but partly have been lost through negligence. He also published elegant letters to various persons, and other things unknown to us. He flourished under Charles, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Johannes of Ymenhusen, doctor of Paris, studious and learned in the holy scriptures, and excellently learned in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, an acute disputant. Among other things he wrote a distinguished volume on the Sentences, four books; also sermons, and various questions of the scriptures. Finally, he is said to have made elegant commentaries on certain books of the Bible, and to have written many other things, but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands. He flourished under Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Johannes of Tambach, of the order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin, [unclear], much practiced and learned, not ignorant also of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect, composed with the polish of scholastic speech. He published certain volumes not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity; among these they report the following: A large work, not to be rejected, against the darts of flattering fortune, no less useful than pleasant, which he entitled Consolation of Theology, four books. On the sensible delights of paradise, one book. Also sermons on the season and on the saints, two books. Certain other things. He flourished under Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

Johannes of Hildesheim, of the order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, bachelor of sacred theology, lector, prior of Kassel, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, in secular letters by everyone’s opinion the most learned of his time; a theologian, rhetorician, philosopher, and very celebrated poet. In declaiming homilies to the people he was of excellent talent. He wrote many outstanding volumes, by which he commended his name to posterity; among them the following are extant: On the Sentences, three books. To Florentius, bishop of Münster, On the Translation of the Three Kings, one book. Also a Chronicle of the Times, one book. A Defense of the Mendicant Orders against Richard, archbishop of Armagh, one book. A metrical work On Monsters in the Church, one book. The work entitled Fountain of Life, one book. On Antichrist, one book. Against the Jews, one book. Against a certain man painting shameful things, one book. Sermons on the season and on the saints, one book. Of letters to various persons, number 80, one book.
