*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Antonius, of the Order of Friars Preachers, archbishop of Florence, an Italian by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, famous in preaching sermons to the people and of celebrated reputation, and no less venerable for his life and conduct than for his learning. He wrote many excellent volumes, in all of which he labored magnificently for the edification of the Church militant. Of these there is extant a great and very celebrated work, divided into four not small volumes, which is entitled:

*Summa of cases of conscience*, 4 books. “How magnified are...”

*A great and noteworthy Chronicle*, 3 books. “I shall speak propositions from...”

*Sermons on the season*, 1 book. And certain other things.

*Sermons on the saints*, 1 book.

He died, after many labors in sacred study, not without a reputation for holiness, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1459, indiction 7, on the sixth day before the Nones of May. He was buried at Saint Mark’s, the convent of his order.
