*Abbot of Sponheim*

On the joys of the heavenly kingdom, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book. Poems of various kinds, one book.
Lost through the negligence of copyists, and erased and torn by the ignorance of monks, they have not come down to me.
He flourished under the emperors Louis, Lothair, and Charles, succeeding one another, in the year of the Lord 870.

Adrevald, also called Albert, a monk of the monastery of Fleury, of the Order of St. Benedict, a man distinguished on every side and most learned, made his name known by writing, when for the edification of simple brothers he composed many volumes on the holy Scriptures. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote, from the time of the translation of the body of St. Benedict from the monastery of Cassino to the monastery of Fleury down to the time of Odo, king of the Franks, a history containing the matter in lucid speech.
The Miracles of St. Benedict, one book.
And many other things, which, however, I have not seen. He flourished in the times of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 890.

Odo, first abbot of Cluny, of the Order of St. Benedict, once a pupil of Remigius, monk of Auxerre, a man diligently trained and learned in the holy Scriptures, an outstanding restorer of the fallen order of the father Benedict, shone forth no less renowned for holiness than for erudition.
He was a notable musician, arch-cantor of the church of Tours, who, after hearing the fame of Berno, the most holy abbot of Gigny, entered the order under him; afterwards appointed abbot of Cluny, he adorned the order with great holiness. Since he was an outstanding preacher of homilies, he composed many sermons for the brothers. He also wrote, among the other works of his talent:
The Life of Saint Gerard, one book. Praises of St. Martin, one book.
A brief chronicle, one book.
He also composed various hymns and chants in honor of the saints in a sweet, regulated melody. At the request of the brothers, too, he abridged the books of blessed Pope Gregory on Job and brought them together into one volume. He flourished in the times of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 920.

Rathbod, the fourteenth bishop of Utrecht, a German by nation, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in speech, and most holy in life and conduct, shone with many miracles both while alive and after death. Nanno, a most learned philosopher, had instructed him in all humane letters; he was advocate in the palace of King Charles the Fat, afterwards entered a monastery, and finally, as we have said, was consecrated bishop. He governed the church of Utrecht energetically for seventeen years during the heavy persecution by the Danes. He wrote several excellent little works, of which these are reported:
Praises of Saint Martin, one book. Praises of St. Boniface the bishop, one book.
On Saint Amalberga, one book. Homiletic sermons, one book.
Flowers on St. Willibrord, one book.
He also composed a complete office on the translation of St. Martin, and various chants in honor of the saints. He died under Louis, son of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 917, in the fifth indiction, on the third day before the Kalends of December, in the eighteenth year of his episcopate. He was buried at Deventer, where previously he had been accustomed to reside in his episcopate.

Aurelian, a cleric of the church of Reims, very diligent in the holy Scriptures, nobly learned in secular letters, an excellent and notable musician in his time. He wrote to Bernard, arch-cantor and later bishop, an outstanding volume on the rules of modulations, which they call tones or tenors, and on the names of [unclear], whose title is:
The Regular Tonary, one book. He wrote certain other things.
He flourished around the times of the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 900.

Regino, abbot of the monastery of Prum in the diocese of Trier, of the Order of St. Benedict, a German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in secular letters, easily held the first place among the teachers of Germany and Gaul in his time. He wrote little works of great authority, of which I have found only the following. To Albero, first bishop of Metz, containing especially the deeds of the Franks and Germans from the nativity of Christ down to the year 98 of the same. He wrote a History or Chronicle, [unclear] books.
On Ecclesiastical Discipline, [unclear] books. “First one must inquire.”
