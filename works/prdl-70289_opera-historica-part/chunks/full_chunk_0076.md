14 *Compendium, Book I.*

To Alaric, after prior deliberation with his own men, he granted the Gauls, otherwise agitated by the fury of shifting peoples, to be inhabited by the Goths.

The Vandals, however, who had remained in the Gauls, hearing of the coming of the Goths, although they had been produced from one Scanzian nation, nevertheless, terrified by fear, abandoned Gaul; and with the Alans and Suebi they occupied part of the Spains, where, after remaining for almost thirty years, they afterwards sailed into Africa and destroyed the city of Hippo.

But Alaric, by permission of Honorius Caesar, intending to seek Gaul, departed from Italy with his men. Against him Count Stilicho, commander of the Roman army, without orders rushed upon him on the feast of the Lord’s Resurrection, when he suspected no evil, and provoked him, unwilling, to battle. And in the first assault he indeed proved victor; in the second encounter, defeated, he was compelled to seek safety in flight. King Alaric, who believed that this deed had been contrived by Honorius, immediately broke the treaty which he had made with him, and, changing his route, returned into Italy and cut down everything round about.

Stilicho the count is killed by Spain.

Rome is captured and overthrown by Alaric, king of the Goths.

King Alaric captured the city of Rome, overthrew many buildings, and gravely harmed the citizens in their goods. Yet he refrained from bloodshed and the slaughter of men, and spared those fleeing to the basilicas of the saints. On the third day he departed from Rome of his own accord, and, as he was about to sail to Sicily, he endured [unclear]; and not long afterward, having fallen gravely ill, he suddenly exchanged life for death.

While these things were being done in Italy by the Goths, the Franks, thinking that the time had come when they might subject all Gaul to themselves, gathered forces and assembled a great army; and under the leaders Pharamund, Marcomir, Sunno, Priam, and Antenor, they entered the Gallican borders. First they engaged with the Vandals, who were again raiding the Gauls, and killed their king Godgisith, whom some call Godogisil, with twenty thousand men. And unless Respendial, king of the Alans, had come in timely aid to the Vandals from Spain, where he was then reigning, they would all have been killed by the Franks.

In the year of the Lord 419, Genebald, inter-king of the Franks, son of Dagobert and grandson of King Marcomir, died in the twenty-first year of his rule and beyond; after his death the Franks, as we shall say, again had kings.

For in the same year, immediately after the death of Genebald, the nobles and elders of the Franks, coming together as one, held mature counsel among themselves, judging it useful that, after the custom of the ancients, they should appoint for themselves as king one of the dukes. And the proposal seemed good in the eyes of all, and the following dukes came together for the purpose of this business at another assembly: namely Pharamund, duke of the Eastern Franks; Marcomir, duke, and Sunno, duke, brothers of Pharamund the Easterner; Clodius, duke, son of Pharamund; Dagobert, duke, son of Marcomir; Nicanor, duke; Pharabert, duke; Richimer, duke; Antenor, duke of the Menapians; Priam, duke, his brother; Berther, Gallican duke; Hieribert, duke of the Islanders; Sunno, duke, and Richer, duke, sons of Genebald, the deceased duke and inter-king of the Franks; Diocles, duke, and Meroveus, duke; Adelhard, Richer, and very many others, both from the common people and from the nobles.

All these, after sharing among themselves the counsel of mature deliberation, at last by unanimous consent elected Pharamund, duke of Eastern Francia, at Würzburg as king of the Franks; he had been the son of Duke Marcomir, who, as we said above, died in the year of the Lord 404. This Marcomir was the son of Clodius, who was the son of Dagobert, who was the son of Genebald, the first duke of the Franks and brother of King Marcomir, whose father was Clogio, king of the Franks, who, as we said above, died in the year of the Lord 478.
