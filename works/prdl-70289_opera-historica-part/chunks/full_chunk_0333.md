He flourished, as I have found, under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.

Julian, bishop of the church of Toledo in Spain, a Moor by nation, to whom Saint Prosper is said to have written three books *On Contemplation*, was a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular studies, scholastic in speech, clear in understanding. He wrote many notable volumes, of which the following are reported:

Eight books.

*On Holy Virginity*, one book. *On the Nature of the Soul*, one book.

*Questions on the Resurrection*, one book. *On Contempt of the World*, one book.

*Prognosticon of the Future Age*, three books. “The faith of the nascent Church.”

*On Vices and Virtues*, one book. *Letters to Various Persons*, one book.

He is also said to have composed some other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 450.

Victorianus Buconius, bishop of the city of Castelliana in Mauretania, a man exercised by constant study in the divine Scriptures and not moderately learned in the disciplines of secular letters, a most powerful hammer of heretics and an outstanding [unclear] of the faithful peoples, is said to have written many treatises in defense of the Catholic faith. Of these the principal ones are reported as follows:

One book. *Against the Arians*, two books.

*Against All Heretics*, three books. Also *On the Sacraments*, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 460.

Musaeus, priest of the church of Marseilles, being most learned in the divine Scriptures and refined by most subtle exercise in their meanings, scholastic and polished in language, at the exhortation of Saint Venerius the bishop excerpted from the holy Scriptures readings and responsories suited to the individual feast days of the whole year, and arranged psalms suited to each season to be sung in the Church. He also wrote an excellent little work to Eustasius the bishop.

Seven books. Several homilies to the people.

Vincentius, a priest, a Gaul by nation, much exercised in the divine writings, made [unclear] by love, a man very learned in secular letters, distinguished in skill of speaking. In his writings, using syllogisms more than the authorities of the divine readings, while he inveighs too much against Nestorius, he is said to favor the dogma of Timothy, and by his ambiguities he suspends the mind from agreement with the Council of Chalcedon; nor does he think one should acquiesce in the view that the Son of God, after the incarnation, consists of two natures. Of his writings, however, there is reported:

One book. *Against Heretics*, one book.

Commentaries on the Psalms, and certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 510.
