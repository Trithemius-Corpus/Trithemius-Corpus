*The Abbot of Sponheim, book I.*

On the book of Ruth. On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. And many other things. He flourished in Gaul under Charlemagne, in the year of the Lord 800. Seven books.

Freculphus, bishop of Lisieux, in the province of Rouen, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, toward whom Rabanus, while still abbot, exercised his talent in many volumes, a man most devoted to and very skilled in the divine Scriptures, and quite fit for teaching the people, distinguished no less by his conduct than by his learning, is said to have written certain excellent little works. Of these I have read only a large and distinguished volume, containing from the beginning of the world down to the birth of our Lord Jesus Christ.

A history of deeds done, “While some [unclear] of time.”

Of the others I have seen nothing. He wrote the aforementioned work to Helisachar; in it he also unraveled the more difficult questions of the Scriptures, and in lucid speech wrote down the deeds of both the Jews and the Gentiles from the beginning of the world down to the Virgin’s giving birth. One book. He flourished under Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Theodulphus, bishop of Orléans, of a monastery of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and instructed from youth in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose. He is said to have composed several little works in both styles, but they have not yet come into our hands. He, having been innocently sent into prison by the Emperor Louis on account of I know not what suspicion, composed the hymn “Gloria laus”; when, on Palm Sunday, as the emperor was passing by, he had sung it in a loud voice, it so pleased the emperor that he immediately allowed him to go free. He wrote, among other things, in various meters:

Hymns. Letters, one book.

He flourished under the empire of Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Einhard, chancellor of the Emperor Charlemagne, a German by nation, and afterward a monk of the Order of the holy father Benedict, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures. He wrote little works not to be despised in reading; of these the following are reported. Imitating Bede, who abridged the Hebrew Psalter by extracting from it all the verses containing words of prayer, he himself abridged the Gallican Psalter, extracting from it all the verses containing words of prayer. He also wrote in his own person [unclear] of the emperor.

Letters to various persons. The Life of the Emperor Charles, one book. A history of the times. And certain other things.

He flourished under Charles and his son Louis, in the year of the Lord 820.

John Scotus, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, formerly hearer and disciple of the monk and most famous doctor Bede, colleague of Albinus, coming with the same [unclear] into Gaul, was held in great esteem by the Emperor Charles. Since he was a man most learned in all the Scriptures, he published very famous little works in clear speech, of which these are reported:

On the Gospel of Matthew. On human duties, one book. On the division of nature. He also composed many other things, which have not come to my notice.

He flourished in the times of the Emperor Charlemagne, in the year of the Lord 800.

Hilduin, abbot of the monastery of Saint Denis at Paris, of the Order of the holy father Benedict, archchaplain of the sacred palace, to whom Rabanus, abbot of Fulda, published commentaries on the four books of Kings, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and very skilled in secular letters, excellent both in verse and in prose. He wrote to the Emperor Louis in both styles:

The Life of Saint Dionysius. And certain other things.

He flourished under Louis, son of the Emperor Charles, in the year of the Lord 840.

Hincmar, archbishop of Reims, formerly a monk of Saint Denis at Paris, a man singularly learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in secular letters, conspicuous for wisdom and uprightness of morals, and most eloquent. He wrote several little works of authority not to be despised, of which I have found only the following. In the person of the Emperor Charlemagne he wrote very instructively:

To the church of Ravenna. The Life of Saint Remigius, bishop, one book. To Walo, bishop of Metz, one book. Of many letters, one book.

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished under the emperors Charlemagne and Louis his son. Year [unclear]. Year [unclear].
