Otto, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, surnamed Mosbach, which, together with its dependencies, he possessed by inheritance among the brothers, the youngest son of Rupert, king of the Romans, had as wife Joanna, daughter of Henry of Landshut, duke of Bavaria; by her he begot Otto of Mosbach, Rupert, bishop of [unclear], and John and Albert, canons. This Otto at length died and was buried at Reichenbach. For the sake of brevity I am compelled to omit many things about these princes, which at another time could be explained more fully. Now we shall set forth in order the line of the dukes of Bavaria descended from the line of the emperor Louis.

Louis, duke of Bavaria, called Hogel, son of Louis, duke of Bavaria, count in [unclear], born of his mother Anna, sister of the king of Cyprus, had as wife Margaret, daughter of Frederick, burgrave of Nuremberg and afterward margrave; by her he had no children. After Louis Hogel had died, she is said secretly to have taken Martin Valenrode in marriage; she possessed Friburg, which afterward she sold to Louis, duke in Landshut, surnamed the Rich.

Albert of Munich, duke of Bavaria, son of Duke Ernest, had as wife the daughter of the duke of Brunswick; by her he begot five sons: John, Sigismund, Albert, Christopher, and Wolfgang. We shall speak of them in their proper place. This Albert in his youth had a certain concubine, the daughter of a certain bathkeeper in Straubing, exceedingly beautiful, whom he loved with a too-blind passion. His father, Duke Ernest, wishing to consult his son’s advantage rather than his pleasure, had her drowned; nevertheless he later grieved over this, since from his son’s violent grief many evils followed.

Adolphus, duke of Bavaria, [son of?] the emperor, died without children.

Louis, duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Rich, son of Henry the Rich, prince in Landshut, took as wife the daughter of the duke of Saxony, who bore him one son named George, who today possesses his father’s duchy, and a daughter, Margaret, a most devout woman, whom Philip, count palatine of the Rhine, prince-elector, took as wife. Mention of them will be made in its proper place.

*The line of the generation of the counts palatine of the Rhine and dukes of Bavaria must consequently be continued in order.*

Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, firstborn of Duke Louis with the Beard, born of his first wife Blanca, called Engellant, because his mother was English, died without children in the year of the Lord 1426.

Louis, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, prince-elector, second-born of Louis the Bearded, namely born of his second wife Mechtilde, took as wife Margaret, daughter of the duke of Savoy; by her he begot one son named Philip, a distinguished prince of his father’s virtue, who, adorned with noble offspring, today possesses his parent’s rights. This Louis, the father of Philip, was a good, just, pious, and gentle man, very devout toward God in all fervor of spirit, as the account of those who knew him closely bears witness, his life and conduct having in part been described.

When he was about to die, this most Christian prince commended to his brother Frederick his only-begotten son Philip, who was only one year old, his very dear wife, and all his land, on the condition that, when Philip reached his eighteenth year, he should restore to him his paternal principality and dominion, as to the legitimate heir, without opposition. And so Louis, a most merciful prince, died in the year of the Lord 1449, on the day of Saint Hippolytus the martyr, and was buried in the church of the Holy Spirit at Heidelberg.

Frederick, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, the third son of Louis with the Beard, brother of the aforesaid Prince Louis, had no wife and left no legitimate heirs. Yet he begot two sons by a certain Clara [unclear]; the first of them, Frederick, was a canon in the church of Speyer, and the second, Louis, made count in Lowenstein, is still living today. There are those who say that he contracted marriage with the aforesaid woman clandestinely in the face of the Church. And because the wars waged by this Frederick are known to all, I shall pass them over in silence.
