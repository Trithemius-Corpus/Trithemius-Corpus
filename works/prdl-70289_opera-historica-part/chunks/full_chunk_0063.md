*Compendium, Book I.*

*Here begins the duchy of the Eastern Franks. 32,580 Franks will come to Herbipolis.*

Therefore, in the seventh year of Clodomir, king of the Franks, and in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 426, in the thirteenth Roman indiction, on the ninth day of the month of April, by command of the aforesaid king, there went out from the mouths of the Rhine, where the Sicambri had once had their first settlements in Germany, with their wives, children, household goods [unclear], and pack animals, thirty-six armed men [unclear], farmers and craftsmen of various trades, two thousand six hundred eighty-six, setting out for the new settlements promised to the Franks by the Thuringians. They did not all go out together at one time, but successively by bands, beginning on the prescribed day, until at last in the month of September they completed the number previously designated, as Hunibald, the historian of the Franks, attests.

King Clodomir appointed as duke of the people of Eastern Francia his brother Genebald, to whom, according to the custom of the Franks, he entrusted both the army and the administration of the whole duchy on these conditions: namely, that both he himself and his sons, heirs, and successors should remain forever under the authority of the kings of the Franks, should faithfully obey their commands in all things, should go forth with them to war against any adversaries whatever, and, according to the custom of the other dukes of the kingdom of the Franks, should everywhere render to those kings the service and honor owed to them.

When these conditions had been given and accepted, Genebald, duke of Eastern Francia, set out with the people assigned to him for Menigantium, and ruled the principality offered to him for almost thirty years. For he was a prudent, bold, and warlike man, who not only governed and distributed the land of the duchy of Francia prudently, but also greatly enlarged the boundaries of its possession.

When the Suebi heard of the arrival of the Eastern Franks at their borders, they bore it rather ill, fearing that what did happen would come to pass: that, multiplied in number and strength, they would also extend the boundaries of the possession they had received to the Suebi’s ruin. But when they saw that they could in no way resist the undertakings of the Franks, pretending a welcoming gathering for their arrival and sending envoys, they asked for peace, more from necessity, as it seemed to many, than from intention. They also [unclear] every cause of dissension that they alleged against the Thuringians.

*Peace is established between the Suebus and Francia. Marcomir, duke of the Eastern Franks. All the Thuringians [unclear].*

In the same year, in the month of September, Marcomir, son of Duke Genebald, with the remaining band of Franks which had already earlier been chosen by King Clodomir’s command for migration, by families, households, and persons, came into the southern borders of Thuringia, and around the river Main, with his brothers, to receive the dwellings promised to them: having to the east the Boii and Bohemians; to the south, the Suebi; to the west, the Rhenish people, the people of Mainz, and the Apodae; and to the north, the Fagophagi and Thuringians.

*At the entrance of the Franks all the Thuringians fall [unclear].*

At the entry of the Franks, the Thuringians, by command of their king Heurolus, withdrew beyond the forest, leaving behind for the new guests all the grain sufficient for the following year. Therefore the newly arrived Franks first settled in the borders of the Thuringians, between the river Saale, as it is now called from Salagaast, and the lower parts of the Neckar, around the banks of the river Main, in that district which was formerly Meningau and was later called Eastern Francia. In it, in the course of time, they built not a few villages and cities in number, and grew in [unclear] and power.

*Genealogy of the dukes of Eastern Francia. Faramund, duke [unclear] of the Eastern Franks. Marcomer, son of Faramund, duke.*

Duke Genebald begot Marcomir and Dagobert, who ruled over Eastern Francia for twenty-one years. Dagobert begot Clodius, who ruled over the people for ten years. Clodius begot Marcomir. Faramund, however, governed the duchy with his father and alone for fifteen years, and afterward was chosen king of the Franks, as we shall say in its proper order. He entrusted the duchy of Eastern Francia to his brother Marcomir, who held it for eighteen years. Duke Marcomir begot Priamus, who ruled for twelve years. Duke Priamus begot Genebald the second, who ruled for twenty-one years. When he was struck and killed by a boar while hunting, Clodomir, son of the aforesaid Duke Sunno, succeeded in the duchy and ruled for thirty-one years. Clodomir begot Hugbad, who ruled for twenty-six years. Hugbad begot Helenus, who ruled for thirty years. Helenus begot Gotfrid, who ruled for twenty-four years. Godfrid begot Genebald the third, who ruled for twenty years. Genebald begot Clodomir, who ruled for twenty-four years.
