*Johannes Trithemius*

under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1165, indiction 14, in the thirty-sixth year of her age, the twenty-third of her conversion, and the thirteenth of the visitation of the heavenly light, on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of July. She was buried in the church of the monks of Saint Florinus the martyr, in the middle of the choir, not without the very greatest reputation for sanctity.

Hildegard, a nun, abbess of the monastery of Saint Rupert the Confessor opposite the town of Bingen, on the other side of the river Nahe, in the diocese of Mainz, of the order of our lord father Benedict, born in the county of Sponheim, once a disciple of the blessed virgin Jutta, sister of Megenhard, count of Sponheim, our founder, a virgin of most holy way of life and from her childhood enlightened by divine revelations, was renowned in her life for many miracles, and spread the odor of sanctity into distant regions, so that Eugenius III, Anastasius IV, Adrian IV, and Alexander III, Roman pontiffs, often visiting her by their writings, commended both themselves and the Roman Church to her prayers. Eugenius III also, in a council held at Trier, with Saint Bernard and many cardinals, bishops, abbots, and clerics present, approved the books of this virgin, read publicly before all, as divinely inspired and consonant with the Catholic faith, and admonished her by apostolic letters to persevere in the undertaking she had begun.

She wrote many little works from angelic revelation, of which the following are extant: a brief explanation on the Rule of Saint Benedict, one book; to Wibert the monk, on thirty questions proposed to her by him, one book; the life of Saint Rupert, duke and confessor, one book; also the life of Saint Disibod, an Irish bishop, who, coming as a pilgrim for Christ into Germany, rests in the county of Sponheim, famous for many miracles, one book. On the Sunday Gospels she composed fifty-eight homilies, very obscure and intelligible only to the devout and learned. Likewise, on the Sacrament of the altar against certain heretics of this time, one book. Also a great volume which she entitled Scivias, that is, Know the Ways, namely of God and of the saints, concerning which she disputes very subtly and religiously. Another, which she inscribed Life of Merits, a work not to be despised, three books. Likewise another entitled Simple Medicine, one book, and another called Compound Medicine, one book. In these two she relates the marvelous and secret things of nature by subtle exposition to a mystical sense, so that, unless from the Holy Spirit, a woman could by no means know such things.

She wrote another large volume whose title is Divine Works, one book; to the clergy of the city of Trier, on the future calamities of the Church, one book; also to the people of Cologne on the same subject, one book. In these she foretells wonderful things, yet without determining the time. She also wrote, at the request of certain priests, an exhortatory book for secular persons, one book; to her sisters, a book of exhortation, one book; to the gray monks, one book. Various songs composed with sweet melody. To Saint Bernard of Clairvaux, one letter. Also a book of letters to various persons, of which I have read one hundred thirty-five; in all of them Catholic doctrine shines forth, by which she either confirms faith or instructs discipline. [unclear] in the same volume, stirred by the fame of her sanctity and divine revelations from regions of the world [unclear], commended themselves to her prayers through messengers and letters, and sought from her the solution of various questions.

She died under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1180, indiction 14, on the fifteenth day before the Kalends of October, in the ninety-first year of her age, and was buried in the middle of the choir of the aforementioned monastery of Saint Rupert, before the high altar.

Werner, abbot of the monastery of Saint Blaise.

Theodoric, an abbot of the order of Saint Benedict, most chaste, a man practiced and learned both in sacred Scriptures and in secular letters, ready in talent, scholastic in speech, wrote for the edification of many the life of the same Hildegard, bride of Christ, one book; also various letters to diverse persons, and certain other things unknown to us. He flourished in the times of Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1200.
