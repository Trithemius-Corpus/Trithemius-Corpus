*Eugenius obtains the aid of the Franks.*

In the following year Eugenius, king of Gaul, who was seeking the Roman empire, when he learned that Theodosius, emperor of the East, was going to come from Constantinople into Gaul against him, grew afraid; and, coming to the Rhine, he called upon the aid of both the eastern and western Franks, together with the Alemanni, against Caesar. In the same year he was killed by Theodosius, because he did not fear the aid of the Franks. Arbogast, chief of the evil men, killed himself with his own sword. Theodosius died at Milan; his corpse was taken back to Constantinople and buried, and his two sons succeeded in the empire: Arcadius in the East, and Honorius among the Romans in the West.

*Pharamundus succeeds Marcomer.*

In the eighteenth year of Genebaldus, great duke of the western Franks, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 403, Marcomer, duke of the eastern Franks, died on the mountain called Franckenberg, and was buried according to the custom of the gentiles. After him his son Pharamundus succeeded in the duchy; after two years he was elected king of all the Franks.

In the same year Marcomer and Sunno, dukes and brothers of Genebaldus of Lower Francia, died without male children and were buried at Neopagus.

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 404, Genebaldus, great duke of the western Franks, died in the nineteenth year of his principate, leaving two sons, Sunno and Richer; the elder of them was lame in his feet, while the younger was not sufficiently fit for the kingdom.

At that time the Franks greatly extended their borders, and were subject to many dukes, all of whom nevertheless obeyed one greater man as though a king. It seemed to them, however, that this division of rule was not good for the people of the Franks; but, judging that it would be better, in contempt of the Romans, to appoint for themselves their own king, as they had had thirty years before, they took counsel.

*Assembly of the nobles of the Franks for the election and institution of a new king.*

Therefore, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 406, in the third Roman indiction, all the dukes, magnates, and nobles of the Franks came together in the month of March at Neopagus for the election of a new king. Among them these men were more eminent than the rest: Duke Pharamundus of the eastern Franks, and his two brothers Marcomer and Sunno with their sons, Clodio, Dagabert, Nicanor, Farabert, and Richimer; Duke Anthenor, prince of the Franks among the Hogii and the inhabitants of Frankfurt on the Main, with his brothers Priamus and Dagabert; Duke Bertherus the Gallican; Duke Heribertus the Islander; Duke Sunno and Richer, brothers and sons of Duke Genebaldus. Among many magnates were Salagast Philippus, Gasthald the secretary, Herhald, Wisogasthald, Rutwich, Adelhardet, and Rieherus. All these, with the rest, by one consent unanimously elected as king Pharamundus, born of royal stock and duke at Marcopolis among the eastern Franks.
