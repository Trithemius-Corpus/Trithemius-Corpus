Peter of Rosenheim, a monk of the monastery of Melk in the regions of Austria, of the order of the holy father Benedict, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and magnificently instructed in secular letters, an excellent philosopher and poet, ready in intellect and renowned in eloquence. He wrote, in verse and prose, little works not to be rejected, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Among these I have read a metrical volume which, at the persuasion and request of Nicholas Dinkelsbuehl, he composed for lord Branda, cardinal of San Clemente, apostolic legate in Austria, on all the chapters of the Bible, which he entitled *Roseate Memorial of the Divine Utterances*, book 1. Also a *History*, or *Chronicle of the Times*, book 1; many various sermons, book 2; letters to various persons, book 1. He also published many other works which have not yet come at all to the notice of my reading. He flourished in the times of the Council of Constance and of the Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

*Nicholas, monk of Holy Cross.*

Nicholas, a monk of the monastery of Holy Cross in Austria, of the Cistercian order, a man learned in the holy divine Scriptures and rich in the reading of the ancients, excellent in intellect and renowned in eloquence. He wrote certain little works of no contemptible usefulness, by which he showed to posterity the liveliness of his intellect. Among these the following are extant: a work in praise of blessed Mary, mother of God, not useless to preachers of the divine word, which he entitled *Image of Holy Mary*, book 1; various sermons, book 2; also many letters to various persons. The rest which he composed [unclear] did not come into our hands. He flourished in the times of the Council of Constance and of the most clement Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420. He composed various treatises on the holy Scriptures.

*Henry of Hesse the Younger.*

...of the monks of Saint Mary, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect and renowned in eloquence. He published many outstanding volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name. Many of them he read publicly, before his entry into religion, in the universal schools. Among these the following are reported: on the *Sentences*, book 1; on the Apocalypse of John, book [unclear]; also book 2; on the Proverbs of Solomon, book 3; on the Apocalypse, book 4; various sermons [unclear]. He died under the Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1428, in the sixth indiction.

John called Plaeth, doctor of Heidelberg, most learned in the holy Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, and venerable no less for the integrity of his conduct than for his knowledge of letters; also sufficiently apt and eloquent in delivering sermons to the people. Among the other works of his intellect he wrote [unclear] a great and distinguished work on the Catholic faith, on the chapter *Firmly we believe*, which he read publicly in ordinary course in the aforesaid school of Heidelberg. Likewise he wrote various questions on the *Sentences*, book 1; against the errors of the Bohemians, book 2; sermons to the people [unclear]. Devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, renowned in intellect, clear in speech, an acute disputant, a declaimer of popular sermons, renowned in intellect, most eloquent in speech because of his singular learning; chaplain and secretary to Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, because of his singular learning and prudence, and in many difficult affairs [sent] to kings [unclear]. He flourished under the Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

*John of Dieburg.*

John of Dieburg, called of Frankfurt, doctor of Heidelberg, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, renowned in intellect, clear in speech, an acute disputant. A declaimer of popular sermons, renowned in intellect, most eloquent in speech because of his singular learning; chaplain and secretary to Louis, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, because of his singular learning and prudence, and in many difficult affairs [sent] to kings [unclear]. He flourished under the Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.
