Arnold Bostius, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin, of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Ghent, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, composed in speech, devout in life and conduct, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote several little works of reading not to be despised, of which the following survive: a prose volume, with poems sometimes inserted, on the fact that the Blessed Virgin Mary is the special patroness of the Carmelite Order, whence he wished to entitle it *On Patronage*, one book; *On the Illustrious Men of His Own [Order]*, one book; *On the Illustrious Men of the Carthusian Order*, one book; against Vincent of Newcastle, of the Order of Preachers, who attacked the most pure conception of the Mother of God, one book; *On the Four Last Things*, one book; many poems and epigrams. He wrote almost innumerable letters both to me and to others. And certain other things unknown to me. He is still living in the new convent of nuns near Bruges, having passed forty-five years of age, under King Maximilian, in the year in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Jodocus Beysselius, the unique ornament among the patricians of the royal city of Aachen, a man most learned both in the holy Scriptures and in secular letters, a philosopher, orator, distinguished poet, most celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech. Although he is necessarily occupied with the affairs of his commonwealth, nevertheless with incredible fervor of love for the Scriptures he writes much, forges much, and composes much in verse and in prose, by which his name is spread abroad with great glory. Of these the following have become known to us: a very fine dialogue, *On the Best Kind of Musicians*, one book; *On Christian Ambition*, one book; an epitome of *The Goad of Love*, one book; *On the Rosaceous Crown of Mary*, one book; a metrical rosary on the Blessed Virgin; a poem on the Sacrament of the altar. He wrote to me an elegiac rosary on the most holy mother Anna. He also began the deeds of the Flemings, but, hindered by various occupations, has so far been unable to finish them. He is also said to have written various most elegant orations. He also wrote many epigrams and a considerable number of poems of different kinds. He gave almost innumerable, most ornate letters both to me and to others. And other things. He is still living at Aachen, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 13.

Jodocus Badius Ascensius, originally from Ghent, a man most learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a most learned philosopher, rhetorician, and poet, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech, who once at Ferrara heard Baptista Guarino teaching Greek letters. He also became skilled in that language. He wrote in verse on the Blessed Virgin, one book; also very many epigrams, almost innumerable, on vices and virtues, which, lucidly explaining them with his own commentaries, he entitled *Moral Woods*, twelve books. Jacob Sprenger, of the Order of the Brothers Preachers, provincial of Cologne, professor and learned interpreter of the divine Scriptures, and excellently learned in Aristotelian philosophy, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, when formerly he had been appointed inquisitor of heretical depravity by Pope Innocent VIII, together with Heinrich Institoris, theologian of the same order. He wrote, for the precaution and instruction of the simple, against little women who are witches, one book. If he wrote anything further, it has not come to my knowledge.
