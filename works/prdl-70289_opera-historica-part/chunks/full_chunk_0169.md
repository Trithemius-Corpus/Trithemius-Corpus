*On the Origin of the Franks.*

In the following year King Charles, having again assembled at Worms a general council of the princes, with the common consent of all declared war on the Saxons. Having gathered a considerable army of Franks, he entered Saxony and laid everything waste with sword and fire. When he had taken a certain fortress, Eresburg, he destroyed the idol called in the Saxon language Ermensul. Departing thence he advanced to the river Weser, where the Saxons, coming together, sought the king’s favor and promised fidelity. But the king, after receiving twelve hostages, granted peace.

*The first expedition of King Charles the Great against the Saxons.*

In the year of the Lord 773, when Pope Adrian could no longer endure the insolence of Desiderius, king of the Lombards, and the oppression of the Church, he sent an embassy to Charles and implored his aid. Charles, having gathered an army, entered Lombardy and besieged Desiderius in the city of Ticinum, which is now Pavia. At length, after a long siege, he compelled the city to surrender; the remaining cities followed it and subjected themselves to King Charles’s power. Therefore the king, after Italy had been subdued and set in order as time permitted, returned to Francia, bringing King Desiderius with him as a captive. Adelgisus, however, Desiderius’s son, fled to Greece, to the emperor Constantine, and there ended his life.

*King Charles, in favor of the Roman Church, defeats the Lombards.*

Meanwhile, because King Charles was engaged in war in Lombardy, the Saxons, taking opportunity from his absence, began to devastate the neighboring territories of the Hessians. And coming to the place called Fritzlar, they attempted to burn the church which the holy archbishop Boniface had once consecrated. For the Saxons at that time were still worshippers of idols. And when they were trying with great force to burn it, there appeared to certain persons, both pagans and Christians, two young men in white garments preventing the whole fire; terrified by their presence, the Saxons all turned to flight, though no one pursued them.

In the year of the Lord 774, Charles, having returned home from Lombardy, before the Saxons perceived that he had come, sent a three-part army into their land; devastating everything with plundering and fires, and killing very many of those who attempted to resist, they returned with immense booty. And this was the second expedition of King Charles into Saxony.

In the year of the Lord 775, King Charles the Great resolved to enter Saxony and to remain in it so long until either they should receive the faith of Christ or all be exterminated. Therefore, making for Saxony with the whole force of the Franks, in the first assault he took Sigiburg, a very strongly fortified castle opposite the Rhine, two miles from Bonn, in which there was a Saxon garrison; restoring Eresburg, which had been destroyed by the Saxons, he fortified it and placed a Frankish garrison in it. After causing great slaughter, and after receiving hostages from them and from the Westphalians, he granted peace to those capable of giving it and withdrew.

*The third expedition of King Charles made against the Saxons.*

In the year of the Lord 777, King Charles the Great, after holding council with the princes of the kingdom of the Franks at the place Paderborn, on the borders of Saxony, where there is now an episcopal city, now for the fourth time led an army into Saxony and found all of them deceitfully devoted to him. For all came to him except Widukind, one of the foremost men of the Westphalians, who, conscious of many crimes, feared the king and fled with a few men to Sigefrid, king of the Danes. The rest, however, who had assembled submitted themselves so far to the king’s power that they then merited pardon on this condition: that if they should hereafter violate his statutes, they would be deprived of homeland and liberty. Many were baptized at the king’s insistence, yet few of them proved true Christians.

*The fourth expedition of King Charles against the Saxons.*

In the year of the Lord 778, King Charles moved into Spain, and the cities which he brought under his authority, and received hostages from the Saracens. Meanwhile the Saxons, discovering that the king was far away, took up arms and advanced as far as the Rhine. But since they could not cross the river, whatever there was of hamlets and estates from the village of Deutz, which is opposite Cologne, as far as Koblenz, where the Moselle flows into the Rhine, they laid waste with fire and sword, making no distinction of sex or age, but killed infants together with their mothers.

*Saxons devastate parts of the Franks.*

When the king, stationed at [Altfiodoro], heard this, he immediately sent the eastern Franks and the Alemanni to drive them back. Hearing of their arrival, the Saxons at once withdrew. But those men, following them on their trail, found them in the regions of the Hessians beside the river Eder, and immediately attacked them in the very ford of the river and laid them low with such slaughter that very few remained.
