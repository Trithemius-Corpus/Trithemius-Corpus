Boniface, also called Winfrid, first archbishop of the church of Mainz, a monk of the Order of Saint Benedict, English by nation but German in his way of life and dignity: a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, upright and most holy in life and morals. By his learned preaching he converted many to the Christian faith, so that he was deservedly held to be the teacher and apostle of the Germans. He wrote several distinguished little works, of which I have seen the following: To Pope Gregory II, On the Faith of the Holy Trinity, one book; The Life of Saint Lebuin, one book; Letters to Various Persons, one book; On Various Deeds of the Saints, one book; and certain other things. At last, while preaching the word of God in Frisia, he was crowned with martyrdom under Pippin, king of the Franks, in the year of the Lord 755, in the eighth indiction, in the thirty-seventh year of his episcopate, on the Nones of June. He was buried in the monastery of Fulda, which he himself built.

Walafrid, abbot of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and remarkably learned in secular letters, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, excelling in verse and prose, a monarch of regular discipline, father of many monks, wrote, among other little works of his talent: The Life of Saint Gall in prose, one book; the same afterwards in verse, one book; On the Divine Offices also, one book; and various letters to various persons, and certain other things. He flourished about the times of the emperor Tiberius, in the year of the Lord 700.

Willibald, priest of Saint Boniface, a monk of the divine father Benedict, brother of Saint Walpurga, virgin abbess of Heidenheim, and afterwards bishop of Eichstatt, a learned and most holy man, ready in intellect and sweet in speech, wrote, at the command of Saint Lullus of Mainz, archbishop after Boniface, The Life of the same most holy martyr Boniface, two books, and, as is reported, certain letters to devout servants of God. He flourished under Pippin, father of the emperor Charlemagne, in the year of the Lord 760.

Ryggandus, bishop of Metz, grandson of King Pippin by his sister, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters as well, venerable no less in life and conduct than in dignity. Among other monuments of his talent he wrote The Rule of the Canons of the Church of Metz, one book, and various letters to various persons. He transferred from Rome the bodies of the holy martyrs Gorgonius, Nabor, and Nazarius; of these, he deposited the body of Saint Nazarius in the monastery of Lorsch, then of our order in Germany, in the diocese of Worms. He flourished in the times of Pippin, his uncle, king of Charlemagne, in the year of the Lord 870.

Haymo, third bishop of the church of Halberstadt in Saxony, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in secular letters, acute in intellect, eloquent in speech, devout in life and conduct, was of most celebrated industry in delivering homilies to the people; from him he became most learned in every discipline of the good arts. Then, as his merits advanced, he was appointed third abbot of the monastery of Hersfeld. Finally, likewise, he was chosen third bishop of Halberstadt. He wrote many, almost innumerable volumes, of which the following are extant: On the Five Books of Moses, five books; On Joshua, one book; On Tobit, one book; On Ezra and Nehemiah, three books; On the Book of Judith, one book; On Esther also, one book; On Job, one book; On the Song of Songs, one book; On the Proverbs of Solomon, one book; On Ecclesiastes, one book; On Isaiah the Prophet, four books; On Jeremiah the Prophet, one book; On Ezekiel also, four books; On Daniel, one book; On the Twelve Minor Prophets, twelve books; On the Psalms, one book; On the Gospels, homilies through the cycle of the year, two large volumes, two books; On All the Epistles of Paul, fourteen books; On the Canonical Epistles, seven books; On the Apocalypse of John, one book; On the Holy Trinity, one book; On the Memory of Christian Things, ten books; On the Variety of Books, four books; On the Acts of the Apostles, one book. He composed sermons, homilies, letters, and various other treatises, but they have not yet come into our hands. He died under Louis, the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 934, in the twelfth indiction, in the thirteenth year of his episcopate, and was buried in the church of Saint Stephen at Halberstadt.
