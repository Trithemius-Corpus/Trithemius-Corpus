42. Bruno, bishop and duke of the Franks, presided for eleven years, one month, and fourteen days; he built the greater church. He died on the third day before the Kalends of June, 1065, under Henry III.

*Bruno.*

43. Adelbero, bishop and duke, presided for forty years. In that dissension which occurred between Henry III and his son, the fourth Henry, he was driven from his see by the elder emperor, in the year of the Lord 1085. Meginhard was substituted for him by that Caesar himself. Adelbero, however, having been expelled, died in Bavaria on the day before the Nones of October, in the fifth year of his exile, and in the year of the Lord 1090, in the monastery of Lambach, which his father had built; he was buried there.

44. Megenhard II presided for three years and twenty-five days. He died on the twelfth day before the Kalends of July, in the year 1098, under Henry IV.

*Megenhard.*

45. Emehard, bishop and duke of the Franks, was ordained while Adelbero was still living in exile at Lambach in Bavaria. He presided for fifteen years, eight months, and three days. He died on the fourth day before the Kalends of March, in the year of the Lord 1103, under Henry IV.

*Emehard.*

46. Erlung, bishop and duke of the Franks, appointed by the emperor, presided for two years. In the year of the Lord 1105, the emperor's son, Henry V, deposed him again because he adhered to his father Henry IV. He caused Rupert to be ordained in his place. The father, soon following, restored Erlung of Würzburg and drove out Rupert. But not long afterward Erlung was deposed by Caesar's son, and Rupert was restored to the bishopric, in the year of the Lord 1105.

47. Rupert, bishop and duke of the Franks, presided for not fully two years. While he was making his way to a general council in Lombardy in the month of September, he died on the seventh day before the Kalends of October, in the year of the Lord 1106, under Henry IV [or V?].

*Rupert.*

48. Erlung, again bishop and duke, was taken up by the consent of all, and thereafter presided for sixteen years and eleven days. For four continuous years before his death he was afflicted with elephantine disease, and at last died on the second day before the Kalends of January, in the year of the Lord 1122, under Henry V. Because the schism forbade it, he was buried not in the city but in the monastery of Schwarzach.

*Gebehard the intruder.*

49. Gebehard was appointed as bishop of the Franks against the will of all by the emperor, while he was still a layman, less suitable because he was young. But the clergy and the procurators of the people elected Ruger the deacon as pontiff for themselves; Gebehard drove him from the city in the year of the Lord 1032.

50. Ruger, bishop-elect and duke of the Franks, lawfully prevailed against the intruder Gebehard, and was consecrated in the monastery of Schwarzach by the hands of the archbishop of Mainz, in the presence of the legates of the Apostolic See and of certain princes. He held for his own use that part of the bishopric which lies around the river Neckar. Gebehard, however, securely possessed the city of Würzburg and the places adjacent to it. Ruger presided for four years and three months, and died in the year of the Lord 1126.

*Ruger.*

51. Hetzelinus, bishop and duke of the Franks, presided for eleven years and four months; he was buried at Bamberg, in the year of the Lord 1138, under Lothar II.
