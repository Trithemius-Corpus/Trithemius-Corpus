*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

On the Song of Songs, one book: "Divine prophecy [unclear]." On the whole Psalter, one book. On the canon of the Mass, one book. On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. On the festivals of the saints, one book. On the divine offices, one book. Also several sermons, one book. To the Bishop of the Aedui, one book. He also composed other works, both on sacred letters and on secular writings, expounding Donatus and the old authors, which are held by many. He flourished under the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 880.

Constantinus, a monk of the monastery of Monte Cassino, of the order of Saint Benedict, an African by nation, a man very zealous in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, especially in philosophy and medicine, fully instructed in Greek, Latin, and Arabic expression. He wrote many small works in medicine, which are valued among physicians. He also translated many volumes of the ancient physicians from Greek and Arabic into Latin, which he prefaced with his own prefaces and letters. Of his small works I have seen these:

Universal diets, one book: "What first compelled." Particular diets, one book: "More fully in the first book." On medicinal weights, one book: "Medicinal weights." Also on urines, one book. As for the rest of his volumes, which he both wrote and translated in great number, I do not recall that any has come into our hands. They are better known to physicians.

Ambrosius Ansbertus, monk and priest of the order of Saint Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, gentle in disposition and sweet in speech, published many commentaries on the sacred volumes, by which he transmitted the fame of his name to posterity. Yet few have come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following:

On the Song of Songs, one book: "The gift of wisdom with Sa[unclear]." Also on the Psalter, one book. On the Apocalypse of John, ten books. On cupidity, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. He flourished under the emperor Arnulf, in the year of the Lord 890.

Paschasius Ratbertus, abbot of Corbie, of the order of Saint Benedict, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, nobly instructed in Greek and Latin expression, acute in intellect, composed in speech. He wrote not a few small works in verse and prose, of which I have found only the following. To Placidus, abbot of our order, a distinguished work:

On the sacrament of the altar, one book: "Whoever among Catholics." The life of Saint Adalhard the bishop, one book. On the Lamentations of the prophet Jeremiah, one book. He also translated some things from Greek into Latin, and composed many others.

Ericus, monk of the order of Saint Benedict, of the monastery of the city of Auxerre, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and most learned in the disciplines of secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, was an outstanding teacher in delivering homilies to the people. He wrote clearly in verse:

Homilies to the people, one book.

Several sermons and letters. He also composed many other works in both styles, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished around the times of the emperor Charles the Fat, in the year of the Lord 880.

Otfridus, a monk of the monastery of Weissenburg, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, once a hearer and disciple of Rabanus Maurus, abbot of Fulda, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned in secular letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, and distinguished poet, excellent in intellect and eloquent in speech. He wrote many splendid small works both in verse and prose, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. After the example also of the great emperor Charles he attempted to reduce the barbarousness of the German tongue to grammatical rules, which he partly accomplished. Hence those things which he wrote in his native language cannot easily be read and understood in our age, even by a man however skilled in the German tongue. Of his small works the following survive. To Luitbert, the eighth archbishop of the church of Mainz, a great and distinguished work on the Gospel, which he entitled:

The grace of Theotista, one book: "By the height of dignity, grace." To King Louis, one book: "To Louis of the easterners." To Bishop Solomon, one book: "To Solomon the bishop, Otfrid."
