*Annals of Trithemius, 47.* In the ninth year of King Hilperic, in the sixth Roman indiction, his brother Heribertus, king of the Franks, who was reigning in Neustria in the city of Paris, died without children. *Heribertus, king of Neustria, dies at Paris.* In the tenth year of Hilperic, king of the Franks, Alboin, king of the Lombards, was killed by one of his own armor-bearers on the following occasion. *Alboin, king of the Lombards, is killed.* In the 547th year of the Lord's nativity, in the tenth Roman indiction, in the time of Lotharius the Great, king of the Franks, this same King Alboin had a war with Chunimundus, king of the Gepids, in which sixty thousand men fell on each side. *The king of the Gepids, Chunimundus, is killed.* There Alboin slew Chunimundus and had a drinking-cup fitted for himself from the bone of his head, which he was especially accustomed to use on days of rejoicing and feasting. He also took his daughter captive as his wife. And when, in the aforesaid year of Hilperic, on a certain solemn day, he had become rather merry over his cups and offered his wife her father's [cup?], saying, 'Drink with your father,' she, kindled with womanly fury, had the king killed at night while he was asleep by his own armor-bearer. *Alboin, king of the Lombards, is killed.* After his [death?] Cleph was again set in the kingdom and reigned for two years; after he died at the end of the two-year period, the Lombards were under dukes for ten years.

Hilperic, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth year of his reign, in the tenth Roman indiction, pressed, harassed, and disturbed his brother Sigebert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, very greatly, both in person and through his son Theodobert. Hence in the following year, the fourteenth of King Hilperic, and the 578th of the Lord's nativity, a cruel war arose between them, in which Theodobert, son of Hilperic, was slain, and many Franks on both sides [were killed]. *Sigebert, king of the Franks among the Austrasians [unclear].* *Conspiracy of the Franks against King Hilperic.* After this the Franks over whom Hilperic held dominion, hating their lord the king himself, [unclear] Sigebert, brother of [unclear], over themselves [unclear]; with hatred of [the woman?] almost all the Franks were troubled; and although she abused very much with insolence, nevertheless she honored the churches of God. She established many new [buildings?] from the foundations. *King Sigebert [is killed] by the treachery of Fredegund.* [unclear] had King Sigebert treacherously killed, in the year of the Lord's nativity [unclear]. *[Brunhild: unclear].*

*War of the Franks [unclear].* In the following year, the seventeenth of Hilperic, king of the Franks, the dukes of the Lombards began to devastate the territories of Gaul. The Franks met them with a great and strong multitude of troops and fought many battles with them at the risk of death, sometimes defeated and sometimes also victorious. Finally, however, the Franks triumphed. *Gregory, from a monk [became?] pope.* [unclear], in the [unclear] Roman indiction, leaving a single son named Lotharius the Younger, an infant of four months, who succeeded in the kingdom. *Lotharius, son of Fredegund the husband-slayer, reigns as an infant.* Lotharius, when he was an infant of four months, was placed in his father's stead and reigned forty-four years, beginning under the guardianship and governance of his mother. In the second year of his reign Antharius, king of the Lombards after the dukes had been driven out, [unclear], in the 58[unclear] year of the Lord's nativity, took as wife Theodolinda, daughter of Garibald, king of Bavaria; to her the blessed Pope Gregory afterwards sent the four books of the Dialogues, as to a woman of singular devotion. *Theodelinda, queen of the Lombards.* *King Hilperic is slain by the treachery of his wife.*

In the same year also, when Antharius, king of the Lombards, had died without children, the Lombards, having granted Queen Theodolina the choice of selecting for herself a husband and king of Italy, [she] wanted Agilulf to be king for them and at the same time her husband; he thereafter reigned vigorously over the Lombards for twenty-five years. *Agilulf is appointed over the Lombards for twenty-five years.* *Thassilo, king of Bavaria under [unclear] of the Franks.* In the fifth year of Lotharius, king of the Franks, in the thirteenth Roman indiction, Garibald, king of the Bavarians, father of the aforementioned Theodolina, queen of the Lombards, died. After him Hildebert, king of the Franks, appointed as king in Bavaria a certain duke named Thassilo, born from the race of the Franks, who defeated in war the Sorbs, who are Slavs but afterwards were called Bohemians.
