Raphael Fulgosius, by nation a man of Piacenza, very learned in both laws and well instructed in the precepts of secular philosophy, excellent in talent, cautious and steady in counsel, published certain distinguished volumes on civil law, by which he showed the force and liveliness of his talent also to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books. On the Old Digest, one book.
On the New Digest, twelve books. Many legal opinions.
And certain other works. He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1416.

Johannes de Palomar, archdeacon of the church of Barcelona, a distinguished doctor of decretals, and nobly learned both in sacred scripture and in secular letters, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, excellent in life and conduct, published little works not to be despised in reading, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is extant a distinguished treatise addressed to Johannes, abbot of the Scottish monastery of the order of Saint Benedict at Vienna.

“The sons will rise in judgment.” Sermons and questions. On abstinence from meats, one book. And several others. He flourished under Sigismund. The rest of what he composed have by no means come to the knowledge of our reading. Famous in the university of Vienna in Austria, he composed many works in the time of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Raphael Cumanus, a distinguished professor of civil law and a very celebrated interpreter, excellent in talent and outstanding in counsel, whose authority among jurists is by no means slight, wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, nine books. On the Old Digest, twenty-four books.
Also one book of legal opinions. And several others. He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1416.

Johannes de Teneramunda, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Sillimacum in Savoy, a man devoted to sacred scripture and very learned, devout in life and conduct, famous in talent, composed in speech, published works very useful for the edification of religious brothers:

On the Christian faith, one book. On the nature and fall of man, one book.
On the conception of Saint Mary, one book. On the love of God, one book.
He also elegantly composed many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

Gasparinus, a very celebrated grammarian, by homeland a man of Bergamo, born of Barziza Rusculus, a man most learned in secular letters, a philosopher and most outstanding rhetorician, subtle in talent, eloquent and articulate, who raised from the dead the elegance of Latin speech when it was half-dead. He wrote many distinguished little works in that faculty, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there are extant:

On orthography, one book. “I rejoice very greatly and [unclear].”
Letters to various persons, one book. On the letters of Cicero, one book.
Various orations, one book. On the same author’s On the Orator, one book.
On the same author’s On Duties, one book.
And certain other works. He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.
