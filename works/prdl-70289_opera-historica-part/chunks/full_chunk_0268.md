If only he could have leisure for letters as much as he is compelled to give himself to affairs of the commonwealth, our Mosellan land would have no one equal to him in learning and in the publication of books. He is still living with his archbishop of Trier, whose illustrious name he makes more illustrious by his talent and prudence, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 13.

*Heinrich [Euticus?]*, an easterner from Mount Noricus, otherwise Nuremberg, a most skilled doctor of the arts, philosophy, and medicine, who for a long time practised his art in Augsburg and now practises it in Frankfurt, a man certainly most learned in every respect, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, excellent in verse and prose. He wrote, in a varied kind of meter, a polished and very bright work which he entitled *Scommata, or the Urbane and Witty Jests of Young Men*, one book; also a satire against the Sophist Dialecticians, enemies of the study of humanity, one book; *In Praise of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin*, one book; also various epigrams, and many most ornate letters to various people, and several other things unknown to us. He is still living in the imperial [unclear], also a philosopher and a very celebrated rhetorician, ready in talent, eloquent in speech, mature in life and conduct. He wrote certain distinguished treatises, with which he profitably occupies his talent and commends his memory to posterity. Yet he conceals the things he writes, either to avoid the [unclear] of vain glory, or so that he may correct what he has written with the file of mature examination. For few of his works have become known to us: *On the Method of Single Combat*, one book; also *On War Machines*, [unclear]; learned and studious in [unclear], and outstandingly learned in secular letters, professor of both laws; *On the Care of Soldiers' Horses*, one book; elegant orations delivered on other occasions in synods. [unclear] he adorns in [unclear] fashion. He wrote many distinguished little works both in verse and in prose, and in addition published many letters, both to me and to others, full of every charm; from these it is easily apparent to readers how much he is worth in learning, in eloquence, and in experience of many things. He is still living in the church of Speyer, thirty-five years of age, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we have written these things, 1495, indiction 13.

*Sebastian Titio, otherwise Brant*, citizen of Strasbourg, distinguished doctor of both laws, and very learned both in the divine scriptures and in other humane letters, no mean master of poetry, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, outstanding in counsel and action, who has spread his name far and wide. Among his works the following are extant: *On the Titles of Both [Laws?]* and [unclear] to be recovered, one book; to the same, an epilogue, [The Kingdom?] of Jerusalem, in elegiac verse, one book; *In Praise of the Glorious Virgin Mary* and of many saints, many poems of various kinds, one book; *Of Various Epigrams*, one book; a Sapphic poem in our book *On the Misery of Prelates*; *Against Slanderous Advocates*; *On the Captivity of King Maximilian*; commendatory poems for various printings. Furthermore, with wondrous skill and industry, though in the common vernacular tongue, he compiled a certain little book which he called the *Ship of Narragonia*, in which he so elegantly expressed the cause and root of all follies, censures the morals of fools, and gives certain remedies for each, that it is [unclear] called not a book of fools, but [unclear]. For he weaves together various things under Emperor Maximilian, whom he singularly loves, and composed about him and to him several very charming poems, even secret ones, as they say, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.
