*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

His authority among jurists is to be considered by no means slight. In his own faculty he wrote several little works, of which there survives an excellent work:

Of his allegations, one book. On the Clementines, one book.

And many other things. He flourished in the time of the emperor Louis IV and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Guerricus, abbot of Igny, a man by no means poorly learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in talent, sweet and well-ordered in speech, and quite fit for persuasion; distinguished no less for his manner of life and love of regular observance than for knowledge of the Scriptures. For the edification of simple and devout brothers he wrote little works not to be despised, in which, showing himself a useful and faithful shepherd of Christ’s sheep, he made his name known also to posterity. Of these I have found only a work of sermons on the season and on the saints throughout the cycle of the year, which he delivered:

To his brothers, one book. We await the Savior.

He also wrote letters.

But concerning the rest which he is said to have composed, I have nowhere been able to find anything.

Henry of Urimaria, of the order of the Hermits of St. Augustine, by nation, as they say, a German; a man from boyhood most zealous and very learned in the holy Scriptures, second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy. For many years he was an outstanding doctor in the school of Paris and shone forth as distinguished for celebrated learning. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which the following are said to exist:

On the Sentences, four books. Quodlibets, one book.

Flowers from the conference of the fathers, one book. Hear, O Israel, the commandments.

On the perfection of man, two books. Because many [unclear].

On the fourfold impulse, one book. On the incarnation of the Word, one book.

On exemption, one book. Behold, other seed fell in.

Sermons on the season, ten books. Exposition of the decree On the celebration of Mass, one book.

On the books of the Ethics, sixteen books. Sermons on the saints, one book.

And many other things. He flourished in the time of the emperor Louis IV, surnamed the Bavarian, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Bartholomaeus de Osa, a native of Bergamo, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and excellently learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and historiographer, [unclear] in the eloquence of preachers. He is said to have fashioned some volumes of excellent learning, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following work excels:

A notable Histories, sixteen books.

He flourished under the emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Bartholomaeus of Pisa, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a man learned in the holy Scriptures, very skilled in both laws, and nobly instructed in philosophical knowledge; distinguished in talent, clear in speech, cautious in knowledge and counsel. He is said to have written certain excellent little works, of which I have seen only the summa which they call the Pisan, truly a work not to be rejected. A few years ago it was in use among confessors of the common people. The author entitled it:

A magisterial summa, one book. Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under the emperor Louis IV, and completed his summa, as he himself testifies, in the year of the Lord 1338.

Albertus of Brescia, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a man zealous and learned in the holy Scriptures, and by no means poorly learned in both laws; also an outstanding declaimer of sermons, and no less holy than learned, since he is said to have shone with miracles even in life. Among the other monuments of his talent he wrote a notable work:

Various sermons, one book.

A summa on cases, [unclear].

And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Ludolph, of the Carthusian order in the house of Strasbourg, by nation a German, born from the land of Saxony; a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters; gentle in talent, sweet and well-ordered in speech, and distinguished also in life and conduct.
