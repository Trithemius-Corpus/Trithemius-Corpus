*296 The Abbot of Sponheim*

ULRICH of Strasbourg, a German by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, once a hearer and disciple of Albert the Great, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, composed little works of reading not to be despised, through which he became known to those present and to those to come. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, 4 books. A Summa of Theology.
Also On the Soul, 1 book. And many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

HAYMO, an Englishman by nation, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, a man trained by long study in the holy Scriptures, since, having attained the master's chair of the faculty of theology, he instructed many pupils in it; at the command of Pope Alexander IV he corrected and rubricated the Roman Breviary. He is said, among other things, to have written compendiously:

On the Sentences, 4 books. And certain sermons, 1 book.
And certain other things. He flourished when the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord [1270?].

OTTO, bishop of Freising in Bavaria in Germany, as some write a nephew of Emperor Frederick I, a German by nation, whom Pope Pius II cites and commends in a certain oration, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not meanly instructed in secular letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there is extant an outstanding work, to be praised with every commendation:

Histories, 1 book. And other things which I have not seen.

He flourished when the empire was vacant, in the year of the Lord [unclear].

PETER the Spaniard, a Portuguese, bishop of Tusculum, cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, and finally supreme pontiff after Hadrian V, a man very learned in medicine, magnificently instructed in secular philosophy, sat in the pontificate for only eight months. As a physician he wrote little works not to be despised, of which the following are reported:

Treasury of the Poor, 1 book. On Problems, 1 book.
Canons of Medicine.

And certain other things. He flourished in the pontificate under the name John XXI, in the times of Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord [1275?].

WILLIAM, a citizen of Piacenza, a learned doctor of the art of medicine, not ignorant of other humane letters, who, hired on a public salary at Verona, held a school in that faculty for many years and instructed several excellent pupils. He wrote little works not to be despised for professors of this science, by which he made his name known both to those present and to those to come. Of these there is extant an outstanding volume which he wished to be entitled:

On All Medicine, 4 books. Also On Surgery, 1 book.

Concerning the rest I have found nothing. He flourished under Emperor [Arnolph?], in the year of the Lord [1270?].

WILLIAM, bishop of Paris, by native land an Auvergnat, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, clear in eloquence, and venerable no less in manner of life than in learning, composed not a few works of his erudition, by which, showing himself a learned and devout master, he made the memory of his name immortal. Of these the following are reported: an outstanding volume entitled:

On the Holy Trinity, 1 book. On the First Principle, 1 book.
On the Sacraments, 1 book. I shall add still four.
Why God Became Man. Summa of Vices, 1 book.
Summa of Virtues. In the order of divine things, holy, 1 book.
On Faith and Laws. On Good and Evil, 1 book.
On Human Souls. On the Profession of Novices, 1 book.
On the Thirty Remedies for Temptations. On the Works of the Virtues, 1 book.
The Cloister of the Soul. Against Plurality of Benefices, 1 book. [unclear]
On Divine Rhetoric. In sacred things and [their] signification.
On Penance. The second plank after [shipwreck], 1 book.
On Demons. After which, by the help of God, [unclear], 1 book.
On the Psalter. On the Proverbs of Solomon, 1 book.
On Ecclesiastes. On the Song of Songs, 1 book.
Letters to various persons. He also published some other works, which have come to my knowledge, 1 book.
