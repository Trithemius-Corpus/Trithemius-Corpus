*[Of] Johannes Trithemius*

[unclear] studious and learned, an outstanding lover of regular discipline, devout in life and conduct. He wrote, in a clear and plain style, certain little works not to be despised for the edification of his brethren, with which he usefully occupied his talent. Of these he once showed me: On the Spiritual Vineyard, a work not to be rejected, one book; likewise On the Fuller Instruction of Novices, one book. He also wrote certain other small but useful exercises for religious, which have not yet come to the knowledge of my reading. He died old and full of days and good works, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1485.

Heinrich Arnoldi, a Saxon by nation, by profession a monk of the Carthusian order, of the house of Basel, a man devout in religion, distinguished in talent, sincere in affection, grave in character, sweet in speech, affable in conduct, pious, humble, gentle, bright in example, very much exercised in sacred letters, and not ignorant of pontifical law. When he held the office of notary for many years in the holy Council of Basel, he was approved by the testimony of all as faithful, just, and prudent. At length, seeing that all things are vanity, having chosen all things for the love of Christ, he governed the monks subject to him for many years in great peace and tranquillity. He wrote many very devout and wholesome little works, among which the following are reported: Many Prayers on Various Saints, one book. Also Many Sermons, one book. Histories of Many Saints, one book. The Passion of the Lord Harmonized from the Four, one book. Meditations on the Life of Christ, one book. On the Immaculate Conception of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin, one book. On the Instruction of Novices, one book. Dialogue between Jesus and Mary, one book. Dialogue between Saint Margaret and Her Little Servant on Spiritual Exercises, one book. Another Dialogue between the Same on the Cross, one book. On the Commendation of Charity, one book. On Holy Humility in the Form of a Prayer, one book. Again, On Humility, one book. Chronicle of His Carthusian House in Basel, one book; and certain other things. He died under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1487, in the fifth indiction.

Jodocus Eichman of Calw, born in a village of Swabia, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and also not moderately instructed in secular philosophy, ready in talent, scholastic in speech, was held in esteem for many years in the Heidelberg gymnasium by reading, teaching, and preaching. There also, according to custom, he held for some time the office of preacher in the church of the Holy Spirit. He wrote certain treatises, as they say, not to be rejected, but they have not come to the knowledge of my reading. There are extant many learned sermons of his, two books. Also various disputed questions. Also some orations to the fathers. He also wrote A Vocabulary for Preachers, one book, and certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1485, and died under Pope Innocent.

Heinrich of Gelnhausen, a monk of our monastery in Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, bachelor of the Erfurt gymnasium, and once rector of scholars in his native town, a man learned in secular philosophy, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, with which he occupied his talent usefully enough. To the novices of the monastery of Porta Mariae of the Order of Saint William he wrote a long and learned letter On Spiritual Progress and Instruction, one book. The Life of Saint Benedict the Abbot in elegiac verse, one book. Sermons on the Temporale and on the Saints, one book. Against the Plurality of Benefices, one book. And certain letters to various persons. He died under Emperor Frederick III, at the age of fifty, in the year of the Lord 1488, in the sixth indiction, on the seventh day before the Kalends of April, buried in the southern part of the cloister.

Peter le Kyrslaken, of the order of the Friars Preachers, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin languages, a famous declaimer of sermons. He wrote to the bishop of the church of Regensburg in Bavaria a treatise against the faithless Jews On the Conditions of the True Messiah, that is, Christ. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord [unclear].

Philipp Kersbeele, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Ghent, philosopher and theologian, [wrote?] against Vincent of Newcastle, of the order of Preachers, who was barking against the most pure conception of the divine Virgin Mary, which Carolus Fernandus, a monk of the divine order of Benedict in France, reshaped into a more elegant style. He died while studying at Paris almost three years ago,
