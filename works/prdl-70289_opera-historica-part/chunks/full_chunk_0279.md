Adam Werner of Themar, a man excellently learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine scriptures, a student and interpreter of imperial laws, no less learned than eloquent, subtle in talent and distinguished in speech, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote little works not to be despised, by which he entrusted his name to posterity and memory. Among these there is extant in my possession a volume of various poems and epigrams, one book. He wrote many poems of different kinds to me concerning Saint Anne. Besides this he gave many letters, both to me and to several others, of singular learning and eloquence. He also wrote some other things, to be brought to light in their own time. His pupil is Johannes Herbst of Luterburg, chaplain in the castle at Heidelberg, a man equally learned and erudite, who himself also wrote to me many poems about Saint Anne and most ornate letters. But also certain other things are shortly to be published. Both are still living in the gymnasium of the city of Heidelberg, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year in which we write these things, 1495.

Johannes of Lamsheim, born in a village situated not far from Worms, of the order of the regular canons of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of the Garden of Blessed Mary, which is otherwise called Kyrsgarten, outside the walls of the city of the Wangiones, a studious man and practiced by continual reading of the scriptures, gifted in talent and open in speech; worthy indeed that he should devote himself only to letters and not also to the cares of temporal affairs, for he is steward, he would compose many things worthy of reading. He wrote *On the Institution and Utility of the Fraternity of the Rosary of the Virgin Mary*, one book; *The Mirror of the Mass*, one book; *The Mirror of the Last Things*, one book; another is entitled *The [unclear] of Eternal Salvation*, one book; and certain other things. He is still living in the aforesaid monastery near Worms, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495, in the fourteenth indiction.

Franciscus Wyler, of the order of the Friars Minor of the Observance, a man studious and learned in the divine scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, ready in talent and distinguished in speech, an excellent declaimer of sermons, when about three years ago he was living in the convent of the town of Kreuznach, wrote at the instance of Albert Latro, professor of the same order, an outstanding *Repertory of the Letters of Saint Jerome the Priest*, one book; to the same man, *On Remedies for Scruples of Conscience*, one book; against a certain man attacking the most pure conception of the Mother of God, a lengthy letter in the manner of a little book; also many sermons to the people. There are extant many letters of his, both to me and to several others. Likewise, on the Passion of the Lord, on vices and virtues, on music and its praises, a varied and not-to-be-rejected compilation. And certain other things. He is still living under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, in the fourteenth indiction.

Thomas of Strasbourg, of the order of the Friars Preachers, first a secular priest, parish priest of Saint Thomas at Strasbourg, then curate of Saint Andrew in Andlau; soon, after taking up the religion of the Friars Preachers, he was made confessor of the nuns of his order in the upper Steiga and in Rentyngen, who had been expelled from Klingental at Basel. For the love of God and of devout sisters he has sustained many labors with a brave spirit, and sustains them up to the present. A man of most elegant sermons, so suited to devotion and compunction that he never seems to me to speak superficially, but from the heart, so learnedly, so eloquently, so devoutly that he seems able to move hearts of stone. For he does not spend time on superfluous citations, but, after the example of the ancients, proceeds in rhetorical fashion. He wrote in the same spirit and fervor most beautiful *Meditations*, one book; letters to various persons, both many and ornate. Questions of the scriptures, profound ones, put to him by friends, he unraveled so appropriately, so catholically, so eloquently, that nothing more apt could have been answered to the matter proposed. He is still living in Steiga, a monastery of nuns in the diocese of Strasbourg, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, in the eighteenth indiction.
