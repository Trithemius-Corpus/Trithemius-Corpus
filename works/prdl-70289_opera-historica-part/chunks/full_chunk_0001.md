*The Historical Works of Johannes Trithemius of Sponheim, first Abbot of Sponheim, then of St. James the Greater at Würzburg, a man most learned in his age: all the works of the first part that have so far been able to be found; partly recalled from old editions now disappearing and corrected according to the authority of the archetypes, partly now first published from manuscripts. A catalogue of them is shown on the reverse page. From the library of Marquard Freher, Palatine councillor. With a very full index. Frankfurt, printed by the Wechel press, at the shop of Claudius Marnius and the heirs of Johannes Aubrius. 1601.*

*Historical Opuscules Contained in This Volume*

1. *Mystical Chronology*, on the secondary beings or intelligences that, after God, move the spheres; a book written to Maximilian, Roman Emperor.

2. *Compendium* or *Breviary* of the first volume of the *Chronicles* or *Annals*, on the origin of the people and kings of the Franks, over 1189 years, from Marcomirus down to King Pippin; addressed to Laurentius, Bishop of Würzburg and Duke of Eastern Francia.

3. A compendium on the origin of the people of the Franks, excerpted from Hunibald; in which there is also an enumeration of the bishops of Würzburg.

4. *Chronicle* of the succession of the Dukes of Bavaria and Counts Palatine; addressed to Philip, Count Palatine, Prince Elector.

5. *Catalogue* of illustrious men who have adorned Germany in every way by their talent and writings; addressed to Jacob Wimpheling of Schlettstadt, theologian, with his prothesis or addition.

6. *Catalogue of Ecclesiastical Writers*, addressed to Johann Dalberg, Bishop of Worms, with additions by certain persons.

The Annals of the same author will be published separately in a separate volume.

Since I was about to bring back into the light, for the sake of posterity, the extremely laborious writings of a most diligent writer on German affairs, writings that were almost tending toward oblivion, and was looking around for a worthy patron and defender for them, I did not have to deliberate long, as though faced with a doubtful choice, if I did not wish to change the custom I have carefully observed up to now in publishing and inscribing various monuments of both languages: namely, that I should either dedicate or restore them only to those to whom they seemed especially to belong, and even to be owed. For by how many titles these Trithemian works, belonging especially to the illumination of the Frankish people and of your Church, are most deservedly owed to your name, illustrious prelate, the works themselves declare even if I am silent: since they were both born on your soil and originally inscribed by the author to your predecessors.
