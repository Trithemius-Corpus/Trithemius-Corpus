But now, after this necessary digression, let us return to the above-mentioned Pharamund, king of the Franks, for the continuation of the history which we have begun.

In the fourth year of King Pharamund, on the last day of the month of December, the Vandals and Alans, under the leader Modigiscus, crossed the frozen Rhine and led an army into Gaul with their children and wives, and afterward passed on into Spain.

In the eighth year of Pharamund, the Goths under King Alaric captured and overthrew Rome. In the same year King Pharamund, under the command of Clodius his son, sent a great army of the Franks into Gaul against the Romans. When the Franks entered Gaul, some of the Romans sought safety in flight, others were killed by the Franks. And thus the Franks obtained a great part of Gaul, the Romans having been utterly wiped out.

Hearing this, the king of the Vandals [unclear], and fearing that he might one day be driven from Gaul by the Franks, undertook war against them. Hence a fierce battle was joined, in which the victorious Franks killed more than twenty thousand Vandals. And unless Respendial, king of the Alans, who was already then ruling in Spain, had intervened in time, all the Vandals would have been slain by the Franks.

The Vandals, like the Goths, originated from the island of Scandia, and after the defeat received from the Franks they remained in Spain for 22 years; after this they sailed to Africa. The Franks, however, greatly increased in number and power. And King Pharamund died in the twenty-sixth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord 431.

Clodius the Long-Haired, son of Pharamund, succeeding his father in the same year, reigned for 20 years. In his first year, Aetius, after hearing of the death of King Pharamund, led the Roman army into Gaul, and, after many Franks had been slain, recovered [unclear].

In these times Salagastus flourished, a wise man and philosopher of the Franks, and from the ancient line of kings, drawing his origin from dukes. Since he was most learned in every literature, by order of King Clodius he wrote down the laws of the Franks, which he named Salic laws after his own name. Later King Clodoneus, converted to the faith, had these laws enlarged.

The same Salagastus, by permission of King Clodius, founded a new city on the bank of the Main not far from Frankfurt, which he named Salagast after his own name, and which ignorant authors today call, by a corrupted term, Seligenstadt. In this same city of his, Salagastus died and, after being burned, was placed in an urn, in the sixteenth year of Clodius.

In the eleventh year of his reign, King Clodius, [unclear] the power of the Franks to be greatly diminished, sent scouts into Gaul, who were to investigate secretly and carefully the situation of the land, the strength of the people, and the guard of the cities. At that time the Burgundians lived in the region of Lyons, and the Goths in Aquitaine.
