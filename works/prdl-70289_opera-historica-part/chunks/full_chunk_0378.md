*The Abbot of Sponheim*

On Jeremiah the prophet, one book.
On the Gospel of John, two books. On the Apocalypse of John, three books.
To Henry the Sixth, three books. “You piously ask something from ...”
On the seven seals, four books. “The first time ...”
The Psalter of ten strings, five books. “Of concord ...”
On the fifteen pontiffs, one book. “Go up, bald man, that [unclear] ...”
On future times, one book. “Since the general [unclear] of the world ...”
Letters to various persons, one book. “Among all historians ...”
He also published many other works, especially against the Jews and other adversaries of the Catholic faith, which have not come into our hands. But the treatise which he wrote against Peter Lombard, bishop of Paris, was condemned in a general council, as is clear at the beginning of the Decretals, “We condemn.”
He flourished under Henry the Sixth, in the year of the Lord 1100.

Rupert, monk of the monastery of St. Remigius in the diocese of Reims, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a Frenchman by nation, a man trained by constant study in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular disciplines, distinguished in talent and clear in speech, wrote several small works of his intellect, by which his name became known to posterity. Of these I have seen only the deeds done by the Christians in the Holy Land in the time of Urban the Second against the infidels.
A distinguished history, ten books. “Among all historians ...”
Of the others I have seen nothing. He flourished under Henry the Fifth, in the year of the Lord 1120.

Roswitha, a noble woman born in Saxony, a nun in the monastery of Gandersheim, which is not far distant from Hildesheim, was distinguished by marvelous talent and learning, and admirable in both kinds of writing. For, exhorting consecrated virgins to chastity and continence, she wrote six comedies in the Latin language. On the deeds of the Ottos, a panegyric in verse, one book. “The sole hope ...” Also the passion of St. Dionysius in elegiac verse. Also several works on St. Gangolphus and other saints. She also had knowledge of the Greek language. She was a contemporary of John the Englishman, who by his learning deserved the papacy.

Dudechinus, priest of the church in Logenstein, a German by nation, a man learned through long practice in the holy Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in the disciplines of secular letters, went to Jerusalem, in hope of eternal recompense, with many others under the emperor Conrad the Third; when they fought with the Saracens in a naval battle, [unclear]. Great in subtlety and distinguished in speech, he transmitted his name with glory to posterity by writing many and varied works. Of his small works few have come into our hands. These, however, are reported:
A Summa of Theology, one book. “On the books of Dionysius ...”
The Summa which is called Numerical, one book. “Those who rule well, pref. ...”
On the celestial sphere, one book. “Our intention in this treatise ...”
On ecclesiastical computation, one book. “Computation is the science of nume...”
A beautiful calendar, one book.
He furthermore published many other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

John, bishop of Chartres, by native place of Salisbury, formerly clerk and disciple of blessed Thomas, archbishop of Canterbury, a man learned in the divine writings and nobly instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, and composed with beautiful grace, excellent in verse and prose, and venerable no less for religion than for knowledge, wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity; but few of them have come into our hands. I have read his distinguished work on the trifles of courtiers and philosophers, which he wished to title:
Polycraticon, eight books. “Most delightful then among many ...”
A Penitential, one book. “Since the mercies of the Lord ...”
The Life of St. Thomas the archbishop, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
He flourished under Frederick, the first emperor, in the year of the Lord 1140.

Peter
