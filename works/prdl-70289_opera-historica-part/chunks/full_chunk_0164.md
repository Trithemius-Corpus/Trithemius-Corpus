*Dagobert reigns [unclear] years.*

[Two?] kings of the Franks of slight authority and importance existed in these times, but everything was done through the mayors of the palace, among whom various disturbances and seditions also frequently arose. For after Ebroin, under Theodoric, had died, the Franks made Garaco count of the palace; not long afterward Gillemarus, his son, removed him from office. After wars against Pippin, duke of Brabant, whom the Franks had driven out of the mayoralty of the palace, this man died suddenly, and Garaco his father recovered the dignity. When he too had died shortly afterward, Bertharius, a useless man, was appointed mayor of the palace.

*Pippin wages war against Bertharius.*

Pippin, duke of Brabant, perceiving the dissension of the Franks, gathered troops and led an army against Theodoric and Bertharius. The battle was fiercely fought; Theodoric, his army routed, took flight. Bertharius was killed. At last, when peace had been confirmed with King Theodoric, Pippin recovered the office, and died in the year of the Lord 715, in the thirteenth Roman indiction, leaving a son named Charles Martel, surnamed the Strong. Plectrude, his stepmother, Pippin’s last wife, placed him in custody at Cologne, and [made?] Theobald mayor of the palace.

*Pippin recovers the mayoralty of the palace.*

Not long afterward the Franks removed Theobald from office; war was undertaken; the party of King Dagobert was weakened. For after Theobald had been driven from office, the Frankish nobles made Raganfrid mayor of the palace. Meanwhile Charles Martel, Pippin’s son, escaped from the custody of his stepmother.

But Dagobert, the younger king of the Franks, at last died in the year of the Lord 715, in the thirteenth indiction, leaving a son named Theodoric.

After the death of King Dagobert, the Franks appointed a certain priest named Daniel mayor of the palace; afterward, raising him to be king, they gave him the name Chilperic. He often fought against Charles Martel. But Charles, having miraculously slipped away from the custody of his stepmother, labored with the greatest zeal to be able to recover his father’s principate. Therefore, since he was powerful, he set up a certain Lotharius, of royal stock, as king of the Franks against Daniel, by whom he himself was also made mayor of the palace. Charles fought many battles with Daniel, sometimes defeated, yet more often victorious.

Ragenfrid, duke of the Frisians, and Eudo, prince of the Wascons, had conspired with Daniel against Lotharius and Charles; but all were defeated, and Lotharius, with Charles’s aid, powerfully held the kingdom for eight years.

This Lotharius, the interrex, died in the year of the Lord’s nativity 723, in the eighth year of his reign, in the sixth Roman indiction. When he had died, Daniel, who was also Chilperic the priest, was restored to the kingdom. He did not survive long, but died in the following year, that is, 724.

*Theodoric reigns.*

Theodoric, son of King Dagobert the last, brought up, as they say, for fear of enemies in a certain convent of nuns, after the death of the two interreges Lotharius and Daniel, was taken up as king by the Franks with the counsel of the mayor of the palace Charles Martel, who was now very powerful; and he reigned fourteen years. But all power in the whole kingdom was in the hands of the mayor of the palace, and the king was compelled to be content with the name alone.

This Charles, mayor of the palace of the Franks, count of Namur and duke of Brabant, restored the kingdom of the Franks, almost desolated, to wholeness, enlarged it, and made it glorious. He fought many battles with the peoples, and always came out victorious. First he led a great army beyond the Rhine and the Danube through the subdued Suevi and Bavarians to the Bulgars, the Rhaetians, and those peoples whom today we call Austrians beyond the Danube; powerfully subjecting them to the kingdom of the Franks, he returned to Gaul with much spoil.

When the Suevi defected from their pledged subjection to the Franks, Charles declared war and, having gathered forces of soldiers, fought with them. The battle was fiercely fought, and many fell on both sides, yet more of the Suevi than of the Franks. Hence in the end the victory fell to Charles, and, after Leutfred, duke of the Suevi, had been killed, he subjected that people, together with its region, to the Franks.

In the ninth year of Theodoric king of the Franks, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 733, Charles, mayor of the palace, waged war with the Frisians, who were trying to vindicate themselves into freedom from subjection to the Franks. A savage battle was joined, and the Frisians, after Radbod, their leader, had been slain, were overcome.

In the following year Charles moved his army into Wascony. For Eudo, prince of the Wascons, with Abdiramus, king of Spain, as his ally, when he had an infinite multitude of fighters, broke into the territories of the Franks and, having set up camp at Bordeaux, took the city by storm, after all the churches had been plundered and burned.
