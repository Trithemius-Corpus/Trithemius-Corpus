*The Abbot of Sponheim*

...a philosopher, rhetorician, and very celebrated poet, who, placed by Pope Eugenius in charge of the public school at Rome, taught rhetoric and poetics for many years, and left after him many pupils most skilled in every science. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these are extant:

*A Very Beautiful Rhetoric*, one book.
*On the Orations of Cicero*, one book.
And certain other things, one book.
*A Singular Dialectic*, one book.
*Various Orations and Letters*, one book.

He flourished at Rome in the time of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1435.

Florianus of San Pietro, an Italian by nation, from Bologna by homeland, a man easily the most learned of all his time in civil law, not ignorant of either the divine Scriptures or secular philosophy, acute in intellect, clear in speech, a subtle disputant, a cautious adviser. He wrote in that faculty certain distinguished little works, by which he commended to posterity the memory of his very celebrated name. Of these there are reported:

*On the Code*, two books.
*On the New Digest*, three books.
*On the Infortiatum*, four books.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1435.

Nicolaus Lackman, a German by nation, of the Order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, minister of Saxony, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of Aristotelian philosophy, outstanding in intellect, fit for disputation, and of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote little works not to be rejected in reading, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number the following are reported as the more important:

*On the Sentences*, nine books.
*Sermons on the Season*, ten books.
And very many other things, one book.
*Various Questions*, two books.
*Sermons on the Saints*, three books.

He flourished in the time of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Johannes of Schonhouen, a German by nation, of the Order of the Regular Canons of Saint Augustine, of the monastery in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, which in the vernacular is called Groenendael and in Latin Viridis Vallis, in which Johannes Rusbrugh once held the office of prior; a man studious and learned in the divine writings, not ignorant of secular philosophy, cautious in intellect, composed in speech, and no less distinguished in manner of life than in knowledge. He wrote to Simon, his nephew:

*A Spiritual Exhortatory*, one book. “Great joy to me...”
*On the Course of a Monk*, one book. “That to you, dearest...”
*A Defense of Johannes Rusbrog*, one book. “Fight the good fight...”
*Various Sermons*, one book. “Since a certain doctor...”
one book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, two books.
And very many other things.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Constance, under Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Petrus de Colle, of the Order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, exceptionally taught in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, eloquent and clear in delivering sermons to the people, published for the edification of readers several volumes, by which he transmitted the memory of his distinguished name to posterity. Among these there is extant an instructed work which he recited to the fathers at the Council of Basel:

*On the Authority of a Council*, one book. “The Church fought many...”
*Questions on the Sentences*, one book.
*Various Sermons*, two books.
And very many other things.

He flourished at the time of the same Council of Basel, at the beginning of the reign of Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1440.
