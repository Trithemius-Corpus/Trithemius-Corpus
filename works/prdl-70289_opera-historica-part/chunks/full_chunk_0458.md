*The Abbot of Sponheim*

...a philosopher and a most eloquent orator, who in the Council of Florence, which Pope Eugenius IV held against the Synod of Basel, was numbered among the distinguished men. He wrote a book full of error on the angels; defending it obstinately, he was condemned as a heretic and burned at the city of [unclear]. Carefully examining the Chronicle of Eusebius of Caesarea, he corrected it and, completing it down to the year of the Lord 1448, gave it a title.

To Peter, [son] of Cosimo de' Medici, one book: *Innate in our minds*.
Letters to various persons, one book. Also various orations.
And certain other works.
He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1440.

Johannes Ernesti, a German by nation, once the ornament and rector of the Heidelberg school, learned in the Holy Scriptures and rich in the reading of the ancients, subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech with singular charm, an outstanding disputant of questions. He wrote, as they say, certain distinguished little works, by which the memory of his name is transmitted with glory to posterity. Of their number the following are reported.

Questions on the Sentences, one book. Disputed questions, one book.
Many sermons, one book: *Although of speaking concerning the greatest...* 26.
An oration on the Nativity of the Lord, one book: *Most sacred desires...*.
On fasting, to the university, one book. On the authority of a council, one book.
Many and various orations, one book. And very many other works.
He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, at the beginning of the reign of Emperor Frederick, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Bartholomaeus, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Bethlehem in the town of Roermond in Guelders, a German by nation; an acute, devout, and celebrated interpreter of sacred letters. He wrote for the edification of his brothers certain distinguished little works, in which he magnificently exercised his talent and left the memory of his name to posterity. Of their number the following are reported.

Exercise of devotion, one book. Summary of vices, one book.
On abstinence from meats, one book. On the proclamations of the brothers, one book.
On rash judgment, one book. On an oath, one book.
Also on a vow, one book. He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord [unclear], on the fourth day before the Ides of July.
On the Sentences, one book. And very many other works. 36.
Also various sermons, one book: *Having perhaps gone out from the cubit...*.
He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1490.

Henricus de Werlis, a German by nation, of the order of Friars Minor, of the province of Cologne, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, an excellent scholastic in talent and speech, an acute disputant and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, composed certain lucid volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these there survives a notable work.

...the Rhine and the Hercynian forest, which, called the Saonus, extends toward Trier to the west; certainly a studious man, learned in the divine writings and not ignorant also of secular letters, outstanding in talent and clear in speech. He wrote, both in verse and in prose, little works not to be despised, of which the following are extant.

Soliloquies of man to God, one book: *When once by the divine calling...*.
On the progress of virtues, one book: *Of your fraternity...*.
On the use of flocks, one book: *Reverend father, recently from...*.
Letters to various persons, one book: *Although among all who sinisterly...*.
Various conferences, one book: *Although there is nothing in me that...*.
Poems and rhythms. And certain other brief works.
He flourished in the aforesaid monastery of Sponheim, before the introduction of the most recent Bursfelde reform, in the year of the Lord 145[unclear].
