*The Abbot of Spanheim*

He flourished under the princes Theodosius and Arcadius in the year 400.

Petronius, bishop of Bologna, a monk in his manner of life, very learned and eloquent in the divine Scriptures, and no less worthy of reverence for holiness than for knowledge, shone with many miracles in life and after death, as is read in his history. Among other things he wrote:

On the Life of the Holy Fathers, book I. On the Ordination of a Bishop, book I.

And other things unknown to us.

He died under Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 385, in the thirteenth indiction.

Pachomius, abbot, a most magnificent founder of the monks of his time, founder of many monasteries, and a man of most holy life, shining with many miracles in life and after death, was a learned man, instructed in the divine Scriptures. For the edification of the brothers he wrote many letters, of which the following are reported:

To Abbot Cornelius, letter I. To the provosts of the monasteries, letter I.

To Abbot Syrus, letter I. To the working brothers, letter I.

Also a Rule of Monks, book I.

He flourished under the princes Valentinian and Theodosius in the year 390.

Aurelius Clemens Prudentius, a Christian poet, a man most learned in secular letters, sufficiently skilled in the divine Scriptures, excellent in meter and prose, was held fast for fifty-seven years by the loves of the world; he read and expounded many little works of the poets, and from his own vein wrote many profound poems. At last, however, in old age, having rejected the vanity of the world, he betook himself to the sacred Scriptures, and in verse and prose expounded many hidden things of both Testaments. He also published some other works which have not come into our hands.

He flourished under the emperor Gratian in the year of the Lord 380.

John, monk and priest of Damascus, a learned and holy man, of whom wondrous things are told on account of the excellence of his teaching and the sincerity of his life, held in high esteem at Constantinople and appointed prelate of the monks, turned many away from iniquity. In the Greek language he wrote little works of profound meaning, but few of them have come into our hands. I have read only that famous work:

On the Tradition of the Orthodox Faith, book I. “No one has ever seen God.”

The History of Barlaam and Josaphat, book I. I have seen nothing of the rest.

He flourished under the most devout prince Theodosius in the year of the Lord 390.
