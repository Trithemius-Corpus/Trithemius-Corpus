*306 | The Abbot of Sponheim | I*

Dinus de Garbo, by country a Florentine, a most expert doctor of the art of medicine, and nobly learned in the rudiments of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. In his own faculty he published for students some works not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these are reported:

Commentaries on Galen. *On the Malignity of Various Complexion*, 1 book.
On Hippocrates, *On the Nature of the Fetus*. *Various Questions*, 1 book.
Also on Avicenna. Also various works in philosophy, 1 book.
And certain other things. His father Brunus was a most expert physician in the art of surgery; he handed this Dinus over to Thadaeus, the Florentine physician, to be instructed in that science. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Guilhelmus of Cumae, a Gaul by nation, a most learned interpreter of civil law, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence; in that faculty in his own time he had few more learned than himself, and by teaching and writing he magnificently adorned the school of Orleans. He published for professors of law some works not to be despised, by which he also made his name known to posterity:

On the Old Digest, 24 books. Also on the Code, 9 books.
And various other things.
He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Jacobus of Viterbo, archbishop of Naples, of the order of the hermit brothers of Saint Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and no less venerable in conduct than in knowledge. He wrote, for the edification of readers, some works not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity with glory. Of these I have not yet deserved to see any. There is, however, said to be his work addressed to Pope Clement V:

*On the Government of Christians*, 1 book. On the Sentences, 4 books.
Also quodlibets. And some other things, 1 book.
He flourished in the times of Henry of Luxembourg, the seventh emperor, and of Pope Clement V, in the year of the Lord 1310.

Henricus of Brussels, a monk of the monastery of Affligem of the order of the divine father Benedict, a man devoted to the divine writings, skilled through continual exercise, an outstanding philosopher and computist, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote some commendable works, by which in his time he became known far and wide. Among these are reported:

*On the Method of Computus*, 1 book. *Calendar on Incensions*, 1 book.
And certain other things, which have not yet at all come to my knowledge.

Alexander of Alexandria, sixteenth minister general of the order of the Friars Minor, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in his time in philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, profound in sense and understanding. He composed certain works in both branches of knowledge, by which, exercising his intellect, he brought his name down to the knowledge of posterity. Among these are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books. Various questions, 1 book. 40.
On Aristotle’s Metaphysics. On the books *On the Soul*, 3 books.
He also commented on other books of Aristotle and wrote various things. He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord 1319.

Eckardus, a Teuton by nation, of the order, as some will have it, of Preachers, a man learned in the divine writings, in Aristotelian philosophy the most learned of all in his time, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence; but, pursuing philosophy too much, he introduced [unclear] novelty, among which there survive seventeen articles condemned by the supreme pontiff and retracted at the end of his life by the same Master Eckardus, many of which are contained in his exposition on the Gospel of John. Finally, the masters of the theological faculty of the school of Heidelberg, in the year of the Lord [1430?], publicly declared the same errors, together with certain others, to have been condemned. Eleven articles, however, were left as suspect; [unclear] he wrote in Catholic fashion and gave sufficiently profound and useful doctrines. Of his books I have found:

On the Sentences, 1 book.
On Genesis,
