In the year of the Lord 840, in the third indiction of the Romans, Louis, king of the Franks and emperor of the Romans, died and was buried in the church at Metz. After his death Lothar, under the name of emperor, held Italy; Pippin obtained Aquitaine; Charles, Gaul; and Louis, Germany.

*The division of the kingdom of the Franks into two kingdoms, the German and the Gallican.*

In the year of the Lord's nativity 861, from the entry of the Sicambri, or Franks, into the kingdom in Germany and the seats accepted at the mouths of the Rhine, the year 1280, in the fourth indiction of the Romans; in the 2030th year from the destruction of Troy, the three sons of King and Emperor Louis divided among themselves the very broad kingdom of the Franks, together with the empire of the Romans. In this division Lothar, as has been said, obtained Italy with the title of the Empire; Louis obtained Germany; Charles, Gaul; and Pippin their nephew, Aquitaine.

From that time the division of the kingdom of the Franks, begun as a twofold division, has remained, now for 673 years down to the present day, and will remain henceforth perpetual. This division, confirmed by the writings and law of the ancestors, made from one kingdom of the Franks two kingdoms, of which one is that of Germany, the other only of Celtica and Aquitaine. For the greater part of Belgic Gaul, in the later division, was adjudged to the kingdom of Germany; in that kingdom are these provinces, with their cities.

All Alemannia, or Swabia, with its cities: Constance, Basel, [Biriensibus?], the Helvetians, Lausanne, the people of Sion, who are also the Valaisians; also the Burgundy of Besancon; Lotharingia, Metz, Liege, Cambrai, Arras, Flanders, Holland, Zeeland, Brabant, Utrecht, Cologne, Trier, with the Mosellans, Mainz, Worms, and Speyer, with the [Cynonoti?] and [Westrasi?] and the Vosges people, Strasbourg with Alsace, and all the rest adjacent to these, belong to the kingdom of Germany, and are called cities of the German kingdom, not of the Roman Empire.

Next, all Germany is subject to this kingdom, in which are: the Frisians, Saxons, Hessians, Westphalians, [Hogij?], who are also the Wetterau people, Thuringians, Meisseners, the margraves of Brandenburg, Pomeranians, Rugi, people of Stettin, Holsteiners, Danes, Swedes, Norwegians, Prussians, Poles, Silesians, Sorbs, who also are the Bohemians of old and are called Slavs, Austrians, Carinthians, [Siturij?], Tyroleans, Bavarians, Rhaetians, and whatever lies among these. But it is also clear that all Italy, apart from the goods of the Church, by ancient right belonged to the kingdom of the Franks of Germany, under the rule of Charles the Great.

Therefore the king of Germany, whoever he may be for the time, has two things: the kingdom and the empire. To the kingdom he is elected by the princes and needs confirmation by no one; but to the empire, which is joined to the kingdom by right, he must be confirmed and anointed by the Roman pontiff. The kingdom is to be called by the name of the Germans, but the empire by the name of the Romans. Those who call Maximilian king of the Romans speak confusedly, since they ought rather to have said king of the Germans. Likewise those who say that Cologne, Trier, Liege, Metz, or other cities of Germany are imperial cities do not speak correctly, since they are royal cities. For if it should ever happen that the empire were taken away from the Germans, the kingdom of Germany would nevertheless remain, free as it now is, having subject to itself all the provinces and cities throughout all the places written above, as it has them subject today.

Louis, therefore, brother of the emperor, son of the elder Louis and grandson of Charles the Great, having obtained the kingdom of Germany in the division, reigned thirty years. After him his son, named Charles the Younger, was king of Germany and emperor of the Romans for eleven years; under him the kingdom was divided among his brothers. For he himself, with Germany, had the empire; Louis had East Francia; Charles, Alemannia; and Carloman, Bavaria, with his son Arnulf afterward obtaining the monarchy of the whole German kingdom and also of the empire. From this point the name of the Franks remained with Gaul to the kings of Gaul, and gradually began to depart from the Germans. We are German Franks; they, however, are Gauls.

1514.

*End of the Compendium of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James at Wurzburg, on the origin of the people of the Franks, and also on their kingdom, name, and kings.*
