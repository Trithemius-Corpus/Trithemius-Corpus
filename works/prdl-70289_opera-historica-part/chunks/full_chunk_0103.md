*Compendium, Book I*

...the church, in honor of the Holy Trinity, and of holy Mary, ever-virgin, and of the holy Apostles Peter and Paul, in the place called Wyssenburg, which we have built, and which we have resolved to exalt and enrich with royal munificence. Therefore we give to it, and wish it to have been given forever, from the possessions of our fisc lying adjacent there, whatever pertains to us, as much in villages as in bondsmen, woods, vineyards, fields, meadows, pastures, waters, and all their courses; and all these things we bring by free conveyance to the use of the brethren there serving God under monastic profession. And so that the authority of this our donation might remain stable and firm for all time, we have taken care to note below the boundaries and the names of the surrounding places. Toward the eastern region the march which we have conveyed extends to Aldenburg, and thence to Geboldesvege, and thence below the ford of the Lutra, and thence to Buozdingenhorste, and thence to the boundary which stands on the summit of the valley Inuenesdal, and thence it leads toward the southern region above Warspach, and thence to Bodemlose stompha, and thence to Sebach, and thence to Ingeldeshaere, and thence to the wooded mountains as far as Beedebur. But toward the western region, as far as Lautenbach and Bernbach, and thence to Belebach, and thence to Gruneburnen, and thence to Otterichesheyt. Toward the northern region, as far as Eichenberg. And the place itself, which we embrace with singular affection, now and henceforth under royal will free from every oppression of servitude, namely in such a way that no public judge, no one by judicial license, may presume to exercise or have [authority] there; nor to constrain the men of that place, or [summon them] for hearing cases, or paying fines or tributes; finally, to exact absolutely nothing from them...

*He wishes the monastery to be exempt.*

...of the other bishops, should have any power of ruling there, or of conducting any causes whatever; but under the protection of our immunity, let the father of the monastery with his brethren possess freely and quietly everything belonging there. In addition, we grant to the same place a coinage having on it images and letters expressed in the likeness of the coinage of Nemetum, and we wish it to be valid and firm by the gift of our authority, so that the men of the same church may freely attend to their business throughout all the bounds of our kingdom, namely in such a way that no toll is owed by them. And no judge, or any person of whatever order, may presume to inflict any violence on them in a matter of this kind. But to the brethren there serving God, so that they may keep the order of holy conduct the more devoutly and peacefully, we grant free power of choosing an abbot for themselves from among themselves. To this abbot, and to all who succeed him, we grant this special privilege of dignity: that he may advance into the order and right of ministerials whomever from the household he shall judge worthy of such honor. Also all the ministerials of the same church by gift of the abbot...

*Coinage in the manner of Speyer is granted to the monastery.*

*The men of the monastery are to be free. Freedom of the monks in choosing an abbot for themselves.*

...from the lord abbot.

*The ministerials are to obey the abbot alone. The right of advocacy over the household is expressed from the first [unclear].*

*In the year of the Lord 714, in the twelfth indiction.*

*Discordance of the time gives rise to doubt.*

*Lotharius was still living in the year 624.*

*Works of Trithemius kept for use by our [unclear].*

*St. Eligius, goldsmith, formerly a bishop. St. Remaclius, from a monk, a bishop of our order.*

I find nothing in these letters that introduces doubt for me concerning the aforesaid foundation; but because, except those who are in the immunity. But if the advocate has been absent in these pleas, nothing of the denarii from these is owed, unless he is proved to be in the king’s service. And so that this our conveyance and the institution of the dignity granted may remain stable and unshaken forever, we have signed this written charter, strengthened by the affixing of our seal. Given, as we did, in the monastery itself, in the month of May, on the eleventh day, in the twenty-third year of our reign, in the name of Christ, happily, Amen.

You will find several foundations and donations of this kind made by this King Dagobert in Franciscus de Rosieres, in the *Stemmatum Lotharingiae*, among the prefixed diplomas of kings and princes; and also in Brusch’s work on monasteries.

At the same time Saint Eligius, powerful in the court of Lotharius and Dagobert, kings of the Franks, and from being an admirable goldsmith, founded, with expenses both royal and his own, the abbey of our order which is called Solignac; in it he himself became a monk and innocently carried out the office of abbot, where also Saint Remaclus, coming from Britain, trained himself under him in the holy soldiery of Christ.
