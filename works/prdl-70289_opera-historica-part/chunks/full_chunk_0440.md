Reinhardus of Fronthoven, a German by nation, of the order, as they say, of the Friars Preachers, a man studious in divine Scripture, sought out by long practice, not ignorant of secular literature. He wrote works not to be rejected by preachers of the word of God, by which he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have seen only:

Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book. Sermons on the Saints, one book.

I have seen nothing of the others. He flourished, as his own people say, in the convent at Koblenz, where he died.

Johannes Capreolus, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a Toulousain by homeland, a man learned in the Holy Scriptures, excellently instructed in the disciplines of Aristotelian philosophy, acute in intellect, quite suited for disputation, a defender and most vigorous champion of the sayings of Saint Thomas Aquinas. He wrote very pleasing monuments of his intellect, by which he made his name known. Of these many volumes are extant:

On the Sentences, four books. And Various Sermons, one book.

And certain other things. He flourished under the Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1415.

Henricus of Coesfeld, a German by nation, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Saint Mary near the mount of Saint Gertrude in Holland, a man very learned in divine Scripture, devout in life and conduct, of great fame, subtle in intellect, clear in eloquence, and also a very celebrated declaimer of sermons. He wrote many excellent volumes, the frequent use of which is judged useful for devout brothers who love their own salvation. Of these the following are reported:

On Exodus Mystically, one book. On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, one book.
On the Three Substantial Vows, two books. Do not love the world.
Against the Vice of Property, one book. On the Three Monastic Custodies, one book.
On the Instruction of Novices, one book. The Mystical Circumcisorium, one book.
On the Sacrament of the Altar, one book. On the Annunciation of the Lord, one book.
Eulogy of Paul the Hermit, one book. Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book.
Sermons on the Saints, one book. Letters to Various Persons, one book, 30.

And many other things. With remarkable industry he examined the difficult work of Guilhelmus, bishop of Paris, On the Whole Corporeal and Incorporeal or Spiritual Universe, and beautifully brought it to a close in a brief compendium. He died in the house of the Valley of Grace near Bruges, while being healthfully occupied in the act of visitation, in the times of the Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410, in the seventh indiction, on the Ides of July.

Henricus of Hachenburg, of the order of the Friars Preachers, a German by nation, a man magnificently exercised and well instructed in the divine writings, of excellent intellect and very celebrated reputation in declaiming sermons to the people. He wrote volumes not to be rejected for the direction of simple priests. Of these there are extant:

Jordanus, of the order of the Friars of Saint Augustine of the Hermitage, a German by nation, a devout man, an excellent expounder and interpreter of the divine Scriptures, of most excellent intellect in declaiming sermons. He wrote several elegant treatises useful to devout brothers, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Yet only a few of these have come into our hands. These, however, are reported:

On the Apocalypse of John, one book. Apology for His Order, one book.

Lenten Sermons, one book. Various Questions, one book.

And many other things. He flourished under King Rupert, in the year of the Lord 1410.
