*Grifo.* This Grifo, entering Bavaria, wishing to claim the duchy for himself by hereditary right, drove out Duke Tassilo. But King Pippin, his brother, restored the duchy to Tassilo and led Grifo into Francia, giving him twelve cities in the manner of counts. He, by no means content, stirred up many wars against his brothers; hence, with little fortune, he was killed in the fifth year thereafter.

Therefore, after Grifo had been expelled from Bavaria by his brother Pippin, Tassilo obtained the duchy; him at last Charlemagne, son of Pippin, as has been said, deposed, and entrusted the province to counts or prefects.

Pippin, therefore, the brother of Grifo, begot Charlemagne. After the surrender of Duke Tassilo, he caused Bavaria for some time to be governed not by dukes but by counts. Charlemagne begot Louis, surnamed the Pious, who succeeded him in the empire; his second wife was Judith, daughter of Welf, count of Bavaria; hence also he established him as duke or king of Bavaria after the counts had been driven out. He also begot Charles, whom he established as king of Germany, and Pippin, whom he made king of Aquitaine and Spain. When the emperor Charlemagne had died and been buried at Aachen, Louis, duke of Bavaria, his son, succeeded him.

*Louis,* surnamed the Pious, son of Charlemagne and afterward Roman emperor, had a wife named Margaret, by whom he received three sons: Lothar, Pippin, and Louis. The first of these, Lothar, he placed over Bavaria, and he also was emperor after him. When his wife Margaret had died, he married another, named Judith, daughter of Welf, count of Bavaria. *Louis Prust,* surnamed the Pious, emperor, after many distinguished deeds, died and was buried at Metz; Lothar his son, king of Bavaria, succeeded him. Against him his brothers waged a very grave war near Auxerre, in which antiquity testifies that many thousands of men fell.

*Lothar.* This emperor had as wife Egilla, daughter of the king of England; she bore him two sons, Lothar and Louis, who succeeded him in the empire. They say that from this Lothar Lotharingia was so named.

*Louis,* son of the emperor Louis the Pious: when his brother Lothar had been raised to the empire, he himself obtained the kingdom of Bavaria and Germany. He had three sons: Charles, afterward the third emperor of this name; Louis, king of the Franks; and Carloman, the last king of Bavaria.

When Carloman, king of Bavaria, had died and been buried at Ötting, his brother Louis, king of the eastern Franks, subjected Bavaria to himself and gave to Arnulf, his brother's son, the duchy of Carinthia and Moosburg. And thus kings ceased in Bavaria.

*Louis,* brother of Carloman, king of the eastern Franks, after a war had been fought with the Northmen, died at Frankfurt on the Main and was buried in the monastery of Lorsch; and Arnulf returned to the duchy of Bavaria.

In the year of the Lord 855, Lothar the emperor abandoned the empire, when he had already ruled for fifteen years, and, having assumed the garb of a pilgrim, became a monk in the monastery of Prum, of the order of Saint Benedict. Louis's son succeeded him in the empire for twenty-one years.

*Louis,* the second of this name, son of the emperor Lothar, succeeding his father in the empire, ruled for forty-one years. His father Lothar, before the empire, was king of Bavaria. Theutberga, daughter of Charles the Bald, his father's brother, bore him one son, named Charles, and one daughter, named Gilla, whom he gave to a certain nobleman Boso, and made him duke of Burgundy. Afterward he gave up the empire to his son Charles the Bald, and, entering monasteries, died.

*Charles the Bald,* son of the emperor Louis the Pious and of Judith, daughter of Welf, count of Bavaria, after Louis his nephew, ruled for almost four years. He had as wife Adeltrude, sister of Boso, duke of Burgundy; she bore him one son named Louis and two daughters, Theoberga, whom he gave as wife to King Louis, his nephew, and Judith, wife of Baldwin, count of Flanders. This Charles built many monasteries.

When the emperor Charles the Bald had died, Charles, the third of this name, brother of Ca[unclear], [unclear] twelve, had as wife Adelburga, daughter of the king of Granaria. He at last, terrified by a horrible vision of hell, ordered many of his relatives, because of their bad administration of the people and the kingdom, to be tormented with very severe punishments; and, having given up the empire, he is said to have set out abroad. He left no heir, and his surname was Charles the Fat.
