*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

Dominic, a monk of the Carthusian order, of the house of Saint Alban near Trier, by nation indeed a Prussian, a learned and most devout man, lived laudably in his order for more than fifty years. He wrote certain volumes, not to be despised for devotion, in which he usefully exercised his talent. Of these I have found the following: a volume entitled *The Sound of One Feasting*, because he produced it in the solitude of his cell while eating and almost ruminating. Another, which he wished to entitle *The Crown of Blessed Mary Ever Virgin*, in which he compares her life to precious stones. Likewise a third work which he wrote, he entitled *The Book of Experience*, in which he inserts various revelations divinely made to him. I once saw also certain little works of his, whose titles oblivion has taken from my memory. He died, not without a reputation for holiness, in the year of the Lord 1360, in the eighth indiction, on 31 December, and was buried at Trier in the common cemetery of his brethren, as he had requested.

*Bartholomew, Prior of Bethlehem, Carthusian monk.*

Bartholomew, a monk of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of Bethlehem in the town of Roermond in Guelders, a man zealous and learned in the Holy Scriptures, and an acute, devout, and highly reputed interpreter of them, wrote for the edification of his brethren certain excellent little works, of which the following are current: *Exercise of Devotion*, one book; *Summary of Vices, or the Destruction of Vices*, a notable work, one book; *On Abstinence from the Eating of Meat*, one book; *On the Proclamations of the Brethren in Chapter*, one book; *On Rash Judgment*, one book; *On the Oath*, one book; and *On the Vow*, one book. The rest that he wrote have not come to my knowledge.

At length he died, after many holy exercises, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord’s incarnation 1446, in the ninth indiction, on 12 July.

John Rode, abbot of the monastery of Saint Matthew the Apostle near Trier, of the order of the divine father Benedict, born from the city of Trier, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally learned in canon law, devout in life and conduct, who was a most celebrated restorer of regular observance; from his labor and foundation the latest Bursfelde reform of our order began in Germany. He devoted some effort to letters at the University of Heidelberg, where, becoming a bachelor of sacred theology and a most learned licentiate of canon law, he already seemed in mind and body to despise the world. For although he was a canon of the church of Metz, dean of Saint Simeon’s at Trier, and official of the court of Trier, he despised all things for the love of Christ and, having entered the order of Carthusian monks near Trier, was not long afterward unanimously elected prior. Finally, at the instance of lord Otto, archbishop of Trier, he was drawn out of the Charterhouse by apostolic mandate and appointed abbot of the aforesaid monastery; by his diligence many monasteries were reformed. He wrote, for the restoration of regular discipline, *Notable Constitutions*, one book; *On the Governance of an Abbot*, one book; and certain other things. He died in the year of the Lord 1439, in the second indiction, on the first day of September, in the twentieth year of his abbatial office, under Emperor Albert.

*Nicholas of Cusa, bishop of Brixen.*

Nicholas of Cusa, bishop of Brixen and cardinal priest of the most holy Roman Church, of the title of Saint Peter in Chains, born in the diocese of Trier in a village by the Moselle called Cusa, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and easily the foremost theologian of his time; also exceptionally learned in secular letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, and most celebrated mathematician of all; subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech, skilled in the three principal languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin; wholly upright in life and conduct and singularly devout; a sincere and fervent lover of the salvation of souls; a most fluent preacher of sermons; a most bitter enemy of all vices; unchangeable in constancy of mind; most patient, even in old age, of honorable labors; eloquent beyond expectation. He retained all histories in memory; in the mathematical disciplines no one in his time was more learned than he. He recited from memory civil and pontifical law and the decrees of the fathers; he was a most acute disputant in Aristotelian philosophy. Indeed he was the highest interpreter and master of Christian theology, as Johannes Aleriensis, secretary of the Apostolic Library, testifies at length in a certain place. This Nicholas, sent into his Germany as apostolic legate, did and established many useful things for the reform of the Church and of monastic religion.
