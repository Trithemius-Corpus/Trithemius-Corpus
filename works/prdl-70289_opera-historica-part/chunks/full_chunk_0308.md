[Hesychius?], priest and monk, formerly hearer and disciple of blessed Gregory Nazianzen, bishop, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, admonished by the example of his teacher, is said to have written many little works of explanations on the divine books, and to have labored over various volumes on both the New and the Old Testament, which have not yet come into our hands. I have read only that notable and very large work which he wrote to Eutychianus, the venerable deacon.

On Leviticus, 1 book. To the venerable deacon.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, in the year of the Lord 400.

Ambrose, bishop of Milan, a Roman by country, a man most celebrated for faith and holiness, easily the most learned of all in the divine scriptures and in secular letters, perfectly instructed in the Greek and Latin tongue, whose merit the whole world proclaims, whose works Holy Mother Church confirms as books with a great commendation of authority. In expounding and explaining the divine scriptures he surpassed all doctors, so that, although many bishops were banished into exile for the faith, no one ever dared to touch Ambrose. For he was a most steadfast hammer of heretics, whose tongue, speaking in the faith of Christ, no human authority could ever impose silence upon. Always ready to undergo any dangers for the faith, with the Lord protecting him he escaped everywhere free and unharmed.
