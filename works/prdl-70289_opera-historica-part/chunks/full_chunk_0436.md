RICHARD LAVINGHAM, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, a man exceptionally learned in the sacred Scriptures and an outstanding interpreter of secular philosophy, and also of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He also wrote some volumes, among which these are reported:

Against the Lollards, one book. On the foundation of his order.
For the book of Bridget, one book. Various sermons.
Also on logic, one book. And various questions.

He flourished in the University of Oxford, especially at the time when the heresy of John Wyclif and of the Lollards began in Bohemia.

JOHN OF WERDEN, of the order, as they say, of the Friars Minor, German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the sacred Scriptures, rich from reading the ancients, and sufficiently learned in natural philosophy; of excellent talent, composed in scholastic simplicity of speech, and in his time a very celebrated preacher of sermons to the people. Among the other monuments of his talent he wrote little works very necessary for preachers of the word of God, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant:

Double sermons on the season, two books. “Let us put on,” etc.; see.
Double sermons on the saints, two books. Also sermons throughout Lent, [unclear].

But the other things that he composed have by no means come to our knowledge.

JAMES, who is called the Great, of Toledo by homeland, of the order of the Hermits of St. Augustine, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished for knowledge of natural philosophy, and well supplied with reading of the ancients. He is reported to have published various treatises and commentaries on the divine books, which have not yet come into our hands. I have found one excellent, notable volume of his, entitled On Speech, Inquiry, and Wisdom.

Sophologium, three books. “Of the most illustrious prince.”

Of the others I have seen nothing. He flourished in the times of the Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1400.

JOHN CAMPSEN, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, English by nation, long exercised in the sacred Scriptures by study and rich in talent through continual reading of the ancients, an excellent preacher of sermons. He is reported to have published certain distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of persons. Of these number the following very noteworthy works are said to be extant:

Sermons on the season, two books. Sermons on the saints, [unclear].

But of the other things that he composed I have not been able to find the titles of the works.

On the whole Psalter, one book. “Blessed is he who [unclear].”
On the Proverbs of Solomon, three books. “In hidden things I shall prepare.”
On Augustine’s City of God, ten books. “The force of the river [unclear].”
On the Song of Songs, one book.

He flourished in the University of Oxford, where he is said to have composed many volumes, especially various lectures on several books of the Bible. A distinguished and notable work.

PHILIP, abbot of the monastery of Otterberg, in the diocese of Worms, of the Cistercian order, German by nation, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures and learned through long practice, not ignorant of secular literature, published volumes not to be despised for the edification of readers; by these, properly exercising his talent, he also left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these number the following are reported. If he published anything further, it has by no means yet come to my knowledge.
