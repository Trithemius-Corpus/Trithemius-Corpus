*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Alanus of Insulis, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in secular letters, a most celebrated theologian, philosopher, and poet, who for a long time gloriously presided over the ecclesiastical school at Paris, where, shining clearly in every divine and human science, he deserved to be called universal. He wrote, in verse and prose, almost countless little works, by which the memory of his name has been made immortal among posterity. But few of them have come to my knowledge. For I have found only the following under their titles.

On the Sentences, 4 books. Quodlibeta also, 1 book.
The Eye of Sacred Scripture, 1 book. “To the most reverend father and [unclear],” 5 books. “Every science rests upon its own [unclear].”
On Doctrine also, 1 book. “Since many, for adorning...”
On the Five Books of Moses, 4 books. “By the grace of God, [to the] Bituricensian father...”
On the Song of Songs, 1 book. “Concerning the six wings of the Cherubim,” 1 book.
On Penance, to the Bituricensian, [unclear].
On Mystical Equivocal Terms.
On the Lament of Nature.
On Parables.
On the Art of Preaching.
On the Natures of Animals.
Letters to various persons.

Finally, he is said to have published distinguished commentaries and lectures on many books of the Bible, especially on the Prophets, on the Gospel, and on the Apostle. Concerning these I have so far been able to find nothing. Certain marvellous things are told about him; I neither affirm their credibility nor rashly undermine it.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Sermons, not without usefulness, 1 book. Visions of a Certain Nun, 1 book.
On the Life of Saint Lutgardis, 1 book.

But other works which he composed have not yet at all come to my knowledge.

Guilhelmus, a monk of the monastery of Affligem, of the Order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, and for some time prior in the same monastery, a man devoted to studies of divine writings, learned by long practice, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, strong in verse and prose, published in both kinds of writing certain little works not to be despised, by which he perpetuated the memory of his name.

Glosses on the Laws, 1 book. Disputations also, 1 book.
And several other things. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

Franciscus of Fano, professor and interpreter of both laws, most celebrated among all in his time, teacher of Johannes Andreae of Bologna, subtle in intellect and distinguished in speech, most acute in pleading judicial cases. He wrote excellent volumes in his faculty, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these I have found only:

On Actions, 1 book. “I indeed, Martinus...”
On the Judgment of Cases, 1 book. “Since many principal...”

I have seen nothing of the others. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

On Leviticus, 1 book. On Numbers, 1 book.
On Deuteronomy, 4 books. On the Gospel, 1 book.
On the Epistles of Paul, 14 books. Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book. Sermons Through Lent, 1 book.
