*The Abbot of Sponheim*

[He was] subtle and very acute in scholastic disputations, as his distinguished works attest: [unclear], 1 book; On the Sentences, 4 books; On Virginity, 1 book, “For holy virgins [unclear]”; Quodlibets, 2 books; On Penance; On the Metaphysics, 14 books; On the Books of the Physics, 8 books. He composed many other things indeed, but they have not come to my knowledge. He died under the Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1293, in the sixth indiction, on the feast of the apostles Peter and Paul. He was buried at Tournai in the church of Mary, the holy Mother of God.

Hugolinus, patriarch of Constantinople, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently learned, of [subtle?] intellect. He composed many other things indeed, but they have not come to my knowledge. He died under the Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1290, in the sixth indiction, on the feast of the apostles Peter and Paul. He was buried at Tournai in the church of Mary, the holy Mother of God. Among these the following are reported, among others, as the more important: On the Sentences, 4 books; Quodlibets, 1 book; Sermons on the Season, 1 book; Various Sermons, 2 books.

Arlotus, minister general of the Order of Friars Minor, Tuscan by nation, a man excellently learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular philosophy, a subtle dialectician and very acute disputant, who by writing various things attained great [unclear] of learning and praise. In declaiming sermons to the people he was of excellent talent. He is said to have composed the New and Old Testament into one. He flourished under the Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1290. Concordances, 1 book; Various Sermons; and certain other things. He flourished in the times of the Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Jacobus, bishop of Genoa, of the Order of Preachers, a man trained in the divine Scriptures, who because of his frequent citations of them is called Vorago, ready in intellect and clear in speech, was sufficiently suited to declaiming sermons to the people. For the use of readers he wrote not a few little works, by which he made his name known. Of these a few have come to my knowledge, and they are set down below. A volume of great size on the life and deeds of many saints, which he entitled The Golden Legend or Mirror of Saints, but it is now commonly called the Legend or Lombard History, 1 book, “The whole [unclear] of the present time.” A Mariale on St. Mary, 1 book, “As I was considering, [unclear] day [unclear].” A Chronicle of the Genoese, 1 book, “Holy Scripture of the divine [unclear].” On the Little Works of St. Augustine, 1 book. Lenten Sermons, 1 book, “He followed in his footsteps.” He also published several other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of the Emperor Adolf, in the year of the Lord 1290.

Lucas, of the Order of Friars Minor, Italian by nation, born at Padua, a man trained by constant study in the holy writings, an outstanding preacher of the divine word, clearer in intellect and open in speech, taught the peoples the way of God in truth no less by deed than by word, and, with the Holy Spirit working together, converted the hearts of many to the love of Christ. He wrote certain little works, not to be despised, in both branches of knowledge, by which his name was brought down to the memory of posterity. But few of them have become known to me.
