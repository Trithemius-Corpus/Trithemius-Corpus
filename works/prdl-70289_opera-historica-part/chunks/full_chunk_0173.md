Pope Adrian, having sent legates to Tassilo, duke of Bavaria, warned him to submit himself to Charles. Persuaded by the entreaties of the legates, he came to Worms to the emperor, with the legates accompanying him, and there swore fealty to Charles; but he did not remain in fealty. Therefore he was excommunicated by Pope Adrian, together with all who adhered to him.

Charles, having returned from Rome to Worms after his coronation, summoned Tassilo, on the counsel of the princes, to an appointed day, and demanded obedience and fidelity from him; but Tassilo neither came to the assembly of the princes nor wished to obey the emperor.

The emperor, moved to anger, attacked Bavaria and Tassilo in war and surrounded them with three armies. He himself, with one army, set up camp near Augsburg, and between Bavaria and Alemannia, in the place called the Lech. Pepin his son arrived with the Italians in the valley of Trent, and the Saxons, coming with the Franks to the Danube, surrounded Bavaria with a threefold battle-line. At last Duke Tassilo, seeing that he was unequal to such great armies, sought pardon, and obtained it, giving his son Theodo, with twelve others, as hostages into the hands of the emperor.

Charles celebrated Easter at Ingelheim; there he ordered Duke Tassilo to be present. When he had come, he was gravely accused by the Bavarians and others of infidelity shown to the king. Therefore, although by the judgment of the princes he ought to have undergone a capital sentence, by the indulgence of the king, whose kinsman he was, he was thrust into the monastery of Lorsch for penance together with Theodo his son, and afterwards lived in great holiness.

This Tassilo founded very many monasteries in Bavaria, namely Wessobrunn, Kremsmünster, Lorsch, Weltenburg, and Pfaffenmünster. He renewed the walls of the city of Passau, and at that time transferred the episcopal see from Lauriacum.

There are those who say that he was blinded in prison by Charles the great emperor, by the continuous application of two heated plates. However that may be, it is agreed that in his own monastery of Lorsch, which he had previously built in [Straramontensi], not far from Worms, at great expense, he ended his life under the rule of Saint Benedict; and they say that he was buried there not without a reputation for holiness.

After Tassilo had been deposed from the duchy and had entered the monastery, the emperor entrusted the duchy of Bavaria to Count Gerold, who was killed by the Huns, who are the Hungarians, in the year of the Lord 1299, as is clear in the Cologne Chronicle.

At the time when Saint Boniface governed the church of Mainz, Occarius and Albertus, brothers and most illustrious dukes of Noricum, strong, noble, and most devout men, went out of love to see Rome. Pope Zacharias told them of the calamity that he was suffering from the unbelievers; wherefore the most Christian dukes, moved by zeal for the faith, armed themselves together with the Romans, bravely attacked the enemies, seized the spoils, and compelled them to leave the boundaries of the Church. But they left the spoils to the Romans, since they declared that they had come and fought not for bodily goods, but for the honor of God. Among the many relics which he gave them, the pope also granted them the body of Saint Quirinus. These two most holy dukes founded many churches in the province of Noricum.

Naynus, duke of Bavaria, in the time of the emperor Charles the Great, did many brave deeds against the unbelievers, by whom at last he was killed, a man altogether most excellent in zeal for the Christian faith. See the rest of what he did in the twenty-fifth book of Vincentius, chapter twenty-one, of the *Speculum*.

At almost the same time Lanfridus, Wairamus, and Hellandus, most Christian princes of Bavaria, bestowed many benefits on the church of God, fighting the wars of the Lord with a great fervor of piety. Among other things they built four monasteries, namely Burtochlarum, Schlechdorf, and Scheyern.

It should be known that the father of the two brothers named above, the dukes of Noricum Albertus and Occarius, was Burgundian, whose wife, their mother, was Bavarian. Of these, Occarius remained in Burgundy, in his paternal principality; but Albertus, younger by twelve years, held counties in Bavaria.
