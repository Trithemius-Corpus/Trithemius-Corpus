*Abbot of Spanheim* ... renowned for [unclear] and eloquence, and venerable no less for honorable conduct than for learning, he published several little works in law, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

On the Decretals, 1 book. On the Clementines, 1 book.
Repertory of Canon Law, 2 books. Repertory of Civil Law, 1 book.
Most elegant Consilia, 1 book. And certain others.
He flourished in the time of Emperor Sigismund and of the general Council of Constance, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Gabriel of Spoleto, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man learned in divine Scripture and not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent and an outstanding declaimer of sermons. He wrote some commendable little works, by which he showed to posterity too the knowledge of his lively talent. But I have not deserved to see any of them up to this point. Nevertheless, these are reported as his principal works:

Against the Heretics, 1 book. Against the Fraticelli, 1 book.
And certain others. He flourished in the time of the Council of Constance and of Emperor Sigismund, in the year 1417.

Franciscus de Zabarellis, a Paduan by nation, cardinal priest of the Roman Church, a man most learned in canon law, and nobly taught both in divine Scripture and in secular letters, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence. He wrote little works in law not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, the following are reported:

On the Decretals, 1 book. On the ten books of sermons [unclear]. [unclear]
On the Clementines, 1 book. That there is nothing new [unclear].
On the Canonical Hours, 1 book. About to repeat the decretal.
Various Repetitions. And many other things. He flourished in the Council of Constance under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418. There he also died, and was buried among the Friars Minor. Poggio delivered an oration in his praise.

Petrus Maurocenus, a Venetian patrician, bishop-cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, a man most learned in divine Scripture and in canon law, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent, composed in speech, notable in life and conduct. He is reported to have fashioned many excellent little works, in which the liveliness of his talent is known to shine forth. But I, up to the present, have not deserved to see any of them. There is reported as his an outstanding volume:

On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, 1 book. And various determinations.
He flourished in person at the Council of Constance, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1418.
