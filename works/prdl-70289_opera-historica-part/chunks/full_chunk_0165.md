He afflicted the Poitevins and burned the church of Saint Hilary. Then he moved his army against the city of Tours, which he attacked by a like stratagem and wished to storm.

But Charles, meeting the enemy with his men, stood in the way, so that he should not obtain what he wished. Battle was joined and the fighting was extremely fierce; but God granted victory to Charles. On that day he laid low 385,000 of the enemy, although only five hundred from the army of the Franks had fallen in that battle. Not long afterward, when Edulo, also called Eudo, had died, Charles moved into Wasconia, subjected it to his rule, and checked the Burgundians as they were attempting new disturbances.

*Charles subjected Wasconia to his rule.*

In the eleventh year of King Theodoric, the Vandals, from their remaining settlements, after they had greatly troubled the peoples neighboring them, at last burst into Francia with a cruel multitude and came as far as Sens. But when an attack was made upon them, Eppo, bishop of the city, the siege having been lifted, put them to flight.

*The Vandals who entered Francia are put to flight.*

The Goths, bursting from the borders of the Spains into Francia, Charles received bravely in battle, laid them low, and compelled them to flee with the greatest disgrace. Finally, he also completely subdued the peoples on this side of the Rhine, the Mosellans too, the Cynonoti, the Westrasii, the Mainz people, the Vangiones, and the Neometes, ancient enemies of the Gauls.

*Charles Martel overcame the Goths.*

Theodoric, king of the Franks, died in the thirteenth year of his reign, which was the year of the Lord’s Nativity 737, in the fifth indiction.
