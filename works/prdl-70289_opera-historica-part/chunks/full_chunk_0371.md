He died under Conrad, the third emperor, in the year of the Lord 1140.

Anselm of Laon, scholasticus, very devoted to the divine Scriptures, skilled through long practice, and in the disciplines of secular letters a notably acute teacher, scholastic in speech, distinguished no less in life than in knowledge, explained the Scriptures of both Testaments with an interlinear and marginal gloss, from the writings of the Fathers, in a new kind of exposition. He also wrote certain other little works which have come to me. It is reported.
Commentaries on the Psalter, one book. And certain letters.
He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1110.

Hugh, priest and monk of Saint-Victor at Paris, of the order of the regular canons of Augustine, abbot there, as they say, a Saxon by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, inferior to none of the ancients in secular philosophy, who, like a second Augustine, was regarded as a most celebrated teacher in his time, subtle in intellect, adorned in eloquence, and venerable no less in conduct than in learning. He wrote many, almost countless, little works, of which few have so far come into our hands.
