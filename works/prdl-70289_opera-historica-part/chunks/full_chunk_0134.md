*Johannes Trithemius. Hilderic reigned 40 years.*

Hilderic, the elder son of Suimo, succeeding his father, reigned forty years, a man powerful in arms and of great prudence. On an island of the Rhine he built a very strong castle, not far from Neoco, which he called Hildeburg from his own name. In his time flourished Hildegast, the wisest philosopher of the Franks, counsellor and seer, or prophet, born of royal seed, who succeeded Odemar in the highest pontificate. For forty-six years he was of such opinion and reputation among the Franks that he was heard by all as though he were some divine power, and gave commands both to kings and dukes and to the whole people of the Franks as if he were God and Lord.

*The prophecy of Hildegast concerning [arvosa Friderum], that is, the lion and the serpent.*

On the birthday of King Hilderic, standing in the temple of Jove at Neopagus before the image of that very god, after prayers poured forth for prophecy, he began to rave, as is the custom with those prophesying; and with head shaken and hands curved back, he prophesied thus: “Victory comes to the Franks from the western god, whom the ancient Dardanian fathers worshipped. Whatever the Gaul possesses, whatever all Germany possesses, all will yield to your right, warlike Sicambrian. When he has given you the first king from the name, whom the princes and dukes without a king shall establish, while he reigns the Frank will reign without end.” Concerning the Romans too he added these words: “Behold, the great god will give the Frank the brothel-camps, and the lion will break the eagle, the serpent [unclear].”

For they had been more accustomed to live in fields than in cities or in beautiful or elaborate dwellings. Often, indeed, by command of their kings they were compelled to change their seats and homes, and therefore they did not care for beautiful buildings. But Hildegast taught them more cultivated speech and manners, and to build houses divided into different apartments. For whatever they had constructed before, through excessive simplicity, was contemptible. Hence the Germans, taking up a proverb, when they see anything made or painted with ancient simplicity, are accustomed to say down to this day: “This is an old work of the Franks.” Das ist ein alt Fränckisch Werck. The rest concerning Hilderic is in Hunibald. He died in the fortieth year of his reign, and in the 253rd year of the Lord’s birth.

Bertherius, son of King Hilderic, reigned after his father for seventeen years. In his time the Romans, who were staying in legions in Gaul on this side of the Rhine, waged war with the Suevic Alemanni, leading their army to Augusta, the city of the Raetians, which is now in Swabia, in the seventh year of Berther, king of the Franks, where Valerian was created Augustus by the soldiers in the army, and Gallienus was called Caesar by the senate at Rome, Gallus and Volusianus having been killed by Aemilianus.

*The Franks devastated Italy, Gaul, and Spain.*

In Berther’s tenth year, Germans, with the aid of the Franks and Saxons, entered the territories of the Romans as enemies, and, devastating everything, came as far as Ravenna.

*Clodius reigned 27 years.*

In Berther’s eleventh year, the Alemanni crossed the Rhine, entered Gaul with a great army, and, coming as far as Trier, cruelly devastated everything with fire and sword. Proceeding from there, they reached as far as Italy and inflicted many losses on the Romans. In the following year the Franks, having the Germans and Saxons as allies, by order of King Berther, under the dukes Anther, his son, and Luther, son of the king of the Saxons, ranged through Gaul with a great army and devastated it, beginning from the region of the Tungri, through the [Belucaci?] as far as the Seine, and farther, devastating everything, they reached as far as Spain and overthrew the city of Tarragona. King Berther died in the eighteenth year of his reign, in the year of the Lord 261.

Clodius, son of Berther, reigned after his father for twenty-eight years. In his eighth year the Romans expelled the Franks from the territories of Spain and inner Gaul, many being slain on both sides. For the Franks withdrew as far as their old seats on the Meuse.

Thereafter, in the twelfth year of his reign, King Clodius, desiring to avenge himself on the Romans, again invaded Gaul through his dukes, and many thousands of the Romans were killed by the Franks. And so

*The Franks held a great part of inner Gaul for seven years.*

In the nineteenth year of King Clodius, the Romans came again under the commander Probus, and again expelled the Franks from Gaul, with not a few of their own men killed and destroyed in war on both sides.
