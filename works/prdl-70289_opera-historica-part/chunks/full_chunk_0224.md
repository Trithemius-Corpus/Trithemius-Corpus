*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

On mystical [matters], one book; On the Six Wings of the Cherubim, one book; The Complaint of Nature, one book; a Summa of Virtues and Vices, one book; On Parables, one book; Anticlaudianus, one book; On the Maxims of Theology, one book; On the Natures of Animals, one book. He is also said to have written many sermons and various letters to various [recipients]; whether all these can be found I do not know. Finally, he is reported to have composed commentaries and elegant lectures on many books of the Bible, especially on the prophets, the Gospel, and the Apostle, but I do not remember that any of them has come into our hands. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in A.D. 1300.

William, monk of Affligem of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, skilled in verse and prose, published in both genres, that is, in alternating style, little works not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity: Sermons, not useless, to the people and the brothers, one book; also the Life of Saint Lutgard, one book; also Visions of a Certain Nun, one book. He also published many other things which have not come to my notice.

Henry of Brussels, monk of the same monastery of Affligem, of the Order of our holy father Benedict, skilled in the holy Scriptures through long practice, not ignorant of secular literature, an excellent philosopher and computist, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote On the Method of Computus, one book; likewise a Calendar on [indictions?], one book; also On the Astrolabe, one book. Moreover he is said to have written many other things, which have not yet come to the notice of our reading.

Eckardus, of the Order, as some maintain, of Preachers, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, second to none in his time in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and a most diligent preacher of the divine word. He wrote many subtle volumes by which he commended his memory to posterity; among them I have found the following: On the Sentences, three books; On Genesis, one book; On Exodus, one book; On the Book of Wisdom, one book; Of His Positions, one book; The Gospel of John, one book; On the Lord's Prayer, one book; Positions in Defense, one book; Sermons on the Saints, one book; various Collations delivered at other times in chapters of the Preachers. The rest that he composed have not come to the notice of our reading. We made mention, in the book On Ecclesiastical Writers, of certain of his sayings that were condemned. He flourished in his time especially in Austria, where by preaching, teaching, and writing he was held in great esteem by the people.

Peter of Saxony, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, a man of excellent industry in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote a summa On Cases, not useless for preachers and confessors, which in its time was in the greatest esteem. Likewise, sermons on the temporal cycle [unclear]. Finally, [unclear] on many books of the Bible; Sermons for Lent, one book; Sermons on the Saints, one book; the Rule of his order, one book. Also many various sermons. He corrected the ordinal of his religious order with discerning attention. But he is said to have written several other things, which have not come to my notice. He flourished in the convent of Cologne in the times of Louis IV of Bavaria, emperor, and Pope John XXII, in A.D. 1320.

John of Blomendale, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, was excellent in delivering sermons to the people. Among other things he wrote elegant Sermons on the Temporal Cycle, one book; also Sermons for Lent, one book; also Sermons on the Saints through the cycle of the year, one book.
