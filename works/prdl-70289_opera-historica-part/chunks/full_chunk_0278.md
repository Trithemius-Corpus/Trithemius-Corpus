*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

[unclear], following the example of the sainted fathers, I shall append the titles of my works. First, at the command of our General Chapter, I made an abridgement of the statutes of the order, one book. Then I wrote The Manner of Celebrating the Provincial Chapter itself, one book; also On the Visitation of Monks, one book. These three little works, by command of that General Chapter, have recently been printed at Nuremberg in one volume.

I also wrote On the Rule of our holy father Benedict, two books; Exhortations to Monks, two books; On the Temptations of Religious, two books; To Nicholas, priest of [Mernic?], On Priestly Life and Formation, one book; On the Vice of Private Possession among Monks, one book; On the Vanity and Misery of This Life, one book; A Lament on the Ruin of Our Order, one book; On the Illustrious Men of Our Order, four books; Praises of the Order of Carmelites, two books; to Lord Johann Kämmerer Dalberg, bishop of Worms, On Ecclesiastical Writers from the time of the apostles down to the last year of Emperor Frederick III, one book; to Rumold, prior of the Carmelites of Frankfurt, On the Praises of Saint Anne, one book; to Rutger the Sicamber, canon of [Dumetum?], On Greek Questions in the Gospel of John, one book; On the Misery of Claustral Prelates, one book; A Chronicle of our monastery of Spanheim, one book.

At the request of Lord Blasius, abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, I wrote the Chronicle of that monastery of his, Hirsau, one book; the History of the Virgin Daughter of Dagobert, whose head is among us, one book; On the Praises of Saint Andrew the Apostle, one book; Praises of Saint Joseph, Spouse of Mary, one book; Praises of Saint Benedict the Abbot, one book; Praises of the Coenobitic Life, one book; On the Praise of Penitence, one book; A Man’s Soliloquy to the Soul, one book; On the Praise, Love, and Study of Scripture, one book; On the Miracles of Saint Anne, one book; also various sermons, delivered both to the brothers and sometimes to the people on the highest feasts, one book; Letters to Various Persons, one book. Orations delivered in chapters or elsewhere are without a fixed number.

From Greek I translated the dialogue of Maximus the Abbot, On the Incarnation of the Lord; the Anathemas of Saint Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, against the heretics, promulgated in the third synod of Ephesus; and certain letters of the same synod. These are the things that at present I recall having written.

Under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495, indiction 13, on the last day of the month of July, I corrected, enlarged, and completed for the second time, after the first edition, this Catalogue of Illustrious Germans. To the honor of almighty God, the benefit of those who do not despise our works, and the adornment and praise of our German nation, to which we owe whatever we can do.

Here, most beloved Jakob Wimpfeling of Sélestat, you have from me the work long requested, in which, according to your desire, I have arranged in order the illustrious men of Germany and those excellent in talent, and have indicated, as far as I was able, what so venerable a cohort wrote. Yet you should not think that I have written up all who by their talent and writings have made our nation illustrious, but rather that I have included a few from among many, and those who readily occurred to me. I have also inserted into this little work not a few, both from among the living and from those departed from life, of whom no mention is made in the larger catalogue, because their works were formerly unknown to me, but in the meantime they have been brought to light, not without labor.

Nor indeed am I unaware how many today illuminate our Germany by writing various things, who have obtained no remembrance either in this catalogue or in the larger one; not because I judged them unworthy, but because nothing at all about their little works became known to me. Indeed I call God as witness, who knows the hidden things of the heart, that I have never knowingly omitted anyone whose works, even a few, I had known. Therefore, if any of the living who write anything have been passed over by me, let them charge it not to my will but to necessity and the shortness of time. For no opportunity was given to search out those who are lacking.

Meanwhile, sweetest Wimpfeling, see that you fare well, and accept as commended to you the little work destined for you, together with its author and friend. From our monastery of Spanheim, on the last day of the month of July, in the year of the Lord 1495.

*Here ends the book of Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, On the Illustrious Men of Germany.*

*The End.*
