Gabriel Biel, of the order of clerics living in common, a man exercised by long study in the divine Scriptures, excellently learned in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, distinguished in talent, composed in speech, devout in life and conduct, presided for many years over the school of Tübingen from the beginning of its foundation, where he trained notable pupils both in philosophy and in sacred letters. He wrote certain works not to be despised, by which he made his name known. Among these, in the aforesaid academy, he ordinarily lectured on the Canon of the Mass, a large and very beautiful volume, one book. He also wrote On the Passion of the Lord, ten books; also many sermons, [unclear] book[s]. He is now said to be writing on the Sentences, a singular and useful work, which he now lectures to his pupils. He is also said to have written several other things, which have not come to our knowledge. He is still living, nearly seventy years old, and persists vigorously in the study he has undertaken, under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1495, indiction XIII.

Johannes de Lapide, a monk of the Carthusian order, of the Basel house, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, outstanding in life and conduct, who formerly, in the school of Paris, obtained the mastership in the faculty of arts, governed there for several years, and, diligently pursuing the study of sacred letters by teaching, lecturing, and disputing, deserved to be adorned with the dignity of doctor in the school of theologians. He first transferred to the university of Basel the teaching of those Parisians who are called Realists, and there planted, strengthened, and enlarged it. He was also one of the principal founders of the Tübingen school. Finally, after the free resignation of several benefices, he was called to the canonry and office of preaching of the distinguished church of Basel; and at length, after a few years had passed, leaving all things for the love of Christ, he assumed the habit of the sacred Carthusian order. He wrote many excellent volumes, by which he made himself memorable as one who would be useful to those now living and to those to come; yet only a few of these have come into our possession. He also wrote several sermons both to the clergy and to the people, one book; also various prayers and letters, one book; and many other things unknown to us. Moreover, he divided many books of various authors into treatises and chapters, prefixing arguments to each, by which their intentions may be clearly recognized. Among these are Cicero’s books On Duties, On Friendship, On Old Age, and On the Dream of Scipio; likewise Aristotle’s books of Logic, the same author’s books On the Soul, and the books of the Metaphysics; likewise the books of blessed Ephrem the deacon, of John Chrysostom, and of Augustine, the same author’s book of letters, almost all the books of Saint Ambrose the bishop, and many others. He is still living in good old age in the Basel Charterhouse, composes various works, and by his writings and example summons many to holy studies. Under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we have written this, 1495, indiction XIII.

Conrad, called Hensel, by country a Hessian, pastor and rector of the church of Frankfurt, doctor of sacred theology and canon law, a very celebrated interpreter, most subtle in talent, composed in speech, who first, after absorbing letters in the school of Erfurt, publicly taught philosophy for some time, then in the school of Greifswald, and, having obtained the church of the imperial city of Frankfurt, vigorously governs to this day, and has for many years governed, by word and example the common people committed to him. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which the following are extant. On the Sentences, [unclear] book[s]. Also On the Canonical Epistles, seven books. On the Song of Songs, one book. On Penance, Fasting, and Prayer, one book. On Faith, Hope, and Charity, one book. On the Four Virtues Which They Call Cardinal, one book. On the Sanctification of the Sabbath, one book. On the Gospels and Sunday Epistles through the cycle of the year, one book. Many various sermons, one book. Also On the Precepts of the Church and Evangelical Truth, one book. On the Interdict, one book. On Ecclesiastical Liberty, one book. Against Heretical Malefactors, one book. Against the Bohemian Hussites, one book.
