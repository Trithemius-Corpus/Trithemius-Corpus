*[page 107]. 76. JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS.*

The Franks immediately recovered their former borders, and compelled the Roman garrison to flee. At last, however, the emperor Constantine, son of Constantius and Helena, slew Richimer in battle, in the 13th year of his reign, [and] of the Lord 340.

[Margin: Theodomerus reigned 10 years.] Theodomerus, son of Richimer, after [his] father reigned ten years, who also himself waged many battles with the Romans. At that time the dwelling of the kings of the Franks was in the region of the Tungri, not far from the river Meuse, in a fort which was called Dispargum. But from the border of the Franks up to the river Loire the Gauls dwelt, and beyond the Loire the Goths. At the same time the duke of the eastern Franks, Dagobert, the Rhine being crossed, devastated Gaul, and burned with fire the despoiled cities of the Treveri. But king Theodomerus, together with Astila [his] mother, was captured by the Romans occupying Gaul, and slain by the sword, in the 10th year of his reign, [and] of the Lord's nativity 350.

[Margin: Clogio reigned over the Franks 18 years.] Clogio, son of Theodomerus, succeeding [his] father in the kingdom of the Franks, reigned eighteen years, in whose time Constans, son of Constantine the Great, Caesar, joined many battles with the Franks, more often vanquished than victor. For king Clogio, a great army of the Franks having been gathered, in the first year of his reign stormed the city of Cambrai, and cruelly killed many of the Romans in vengeance for [his] father's death; thence advancing, [he subjected] Gaul up to the river Somme to the kingdom of the Franks.

Meanwhile, while these [things] were being done in Gaul, Dagobert, duke in eastern Francia, crossing the Rhine by a bridge built [for it], at the village of Altrip between Speyer and Worms, devastates the Gauls and Romans in hostile fashion. The Goths also, at the same time, invading Spain, were tearing [it] apart. Constans, therefore, seeing that, set in the midst of danger, he could not resist all, made peace with the Franks. And Constans, without delay, leads an army into Spain against the Goths, whom he compelled to retreat; and not long after, in the third year of Clogio, he was slain, in the 30th year of his age, [and] the 13th of [his] reign, the Roman commonwealth being left in disturbance. Clogio, king of the Franks, dies in the 18th year of his reign, [and] of the Lord's nativity 367.

Marcomerus, son of Clogio, after [his] father reigned four years. Against him the Romans moved war in his first year, which lasted four years; for they bore [it] very ill that the Franks inhabited Gaul. But the Franks stood firm. At last, however, king Marcomerus, in the fourth year of his reign, was killed by the Romans in battle, in the year of the Lord's nativity 381.

But the Franks, their king having been slain, considering that they could not on that occasion resist the Romans, sought peace from them; which [the Romans] gave on such a condition, that the Franks should thereafter not have a king, but, the kingdom being reduced to a province, should be governed by dukes, and should pay tribute yearly to the Romans, [and] keep faith. And although it seemed too grievous to the Franks—who had hitherto always been free—to give tribute, yet, compelled by the malice of the times, they did otherwise than they would have wished.

In the year, therefore, 711 from the first coming of the Franks into Germany, that nation, hitherto always unconquered and feared by all nations—[its] kings being lost and [it] committed to dukes—is compelled to pay tribute to the Romans for 20 years.

[Margin: Dagobert, duke or viceroy of the Franks, 13 years. Valentinian emperor.] In the eighth year of the same duke Dagobert, the Romans, under [their] duke Gratian, joining battle with the Alemanni not far from Argentaria [Colmar], slew thirty thousand of them. But duke Dagobert had three sons—Genebald, Marcomerus, and Suno—to whom, while he yet lived healthy, with all the people favoring, he divided the princedom. In the eleventh year of duke Dagobert, Clodius, duke of the eastern Franks, died, to whom Marcomer his son, succeeding, governs the upper Franks for 20 years.
