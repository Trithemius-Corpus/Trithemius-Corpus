In these Conferences of the saints there is useful and necessary instruction for monks; but because in certain matters he is criticized by blessed Prosper concerning freedom of choice, his little works are reckoned among the apocrypha. At length he died, after the publication of many books, at Marseilles, not without a reputation for holiness, when Theodosius and Valentinian were reigning. In the year of the Lord 435.

LUCIAN, priest of the Church of Jerusalem, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, most holy in life and conduct, to whom, in the times of Honorius and Theodosius, almighty God revealed the place of the tomb and relics of the most holy protomartyr Stephen. He is said to have written some little works.

Epistle.
Lucian the priest.
On the discovery of Saint Stephen. 1 book.
Of the other things I have seen nothing.

He wrote this revelation in the Greek language; Avitus, a priest of Spanish birth, translated it into Latin speech, and, with his own letter added, sent it through Orosius to be read by Easterners. He flourished under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, around the years of the Lord 410.

SEVERUS the priest, surnamed Sulpicius, born from the province of Aquitaine, a man noble in birth and letters, and conspicuous for his love of poverty and humility. Dear also to the holy men Martin of Tours and Paulinus of Nola, bishops, in his old age he was deceived by the Pelagians; and, recognizing the fault of loquacity, he kept silence until death, so that the sin which he had contracted by speaking he might healthfully amend by keeping silent. He wrote little works not unworthy of reading, of which the following are reported.

“Most mortals.”
On the life of Saint Martin the bishop. 3 books.
“When I into one place.”
Conference of Postumianus and Gallus. 3 books.
Letters to his sister. 1 book.
Letters to various persons. 1 book.
To Paulinus, bishop of Nola. 2 books.
He is also said to have written some other things, which have not come to my knowledge.
He flourished with Saint Martin, archbishop of Tours, under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius. In the year of the Lord 420.

AUSONIUS, bishop of Bordeaux, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular letters second to none in his own time, excelling in verse and prose, shone forth no less famous for content [unclear] than for learning. At Trier, in a council under the emperor Maximus, together with the saints Martin, Ambrose of Milan, and Jerome the priest, he did many splendid things. While there, among other things, he wrote in verse the distinguished little work that is entitled:

On the praise of the same homeland. 1 book.
Mosella. 1 book.

But he is also said to have written many other little works which have in no way come down to us. He flourished under Maximus. In the year of the Lord 410.

AUGUSTINE, bishop of Hippo, by nation a Carthaginian in Africa, a man most learned of all the doctors both in the divine writings and in secular letters, eloquent in speech, renowned in expression, profound in understanding, most holy in life and conduct. He wrote so many little works that I would believe no one could either read or possess them all. Hence without doubt he is convicted of lying who professes that he has thus found the whole of Augustine. For he surpasses all writers both in the number of books and in usefulness, since he composed more than a thousand distinguished treatises. No one explained sacred Scripture so profoundly as Augustine; no one so usefully instructed Christian morals; nor did anyone defend the catholic faith more ardently than he. The king brought him into the wine-cellar, and clothed him with such strength that with the sword of his mouth he powerfully laid low the obstinate adversaries of the faith. If you had him as teacher, even if the others were lacking, he alone would suffice for you. Now therefore, from the many books which he published, let us record at least a few.
