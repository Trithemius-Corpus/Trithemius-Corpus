*Chronicle of the Dukes of Bavaria*

After Charles III had been deposed from the empire, Arnulph, duke of Bavaria, son of Carloman, the last king of Bavaria, was made emperor by the consent of all. He ruled for twelve years, a powerful, wealthy, and fortunate man, who accomplished many outstanding things in the commonwealth. He had as wife Agnes, daughter of the emperor of the Greeks, by whom he had two sons, Arnold and Bernher. Arnold married Agnes, daughter of the king of Hungary; and his brother Bernher married her sister, likewise a daughter of the king of Hungary, named Beatrice. These two sisters, daughters of the king of Hungary, were baptized in the castle which the emperor Arnolph built in Bavaria, a very strong one, named Scheyern. Arnold obtained the duchy of the Noricans, and his brother Bernher received the county of Scheyern and the county of the Palatine of the Rhine. Arnold himself gave his daughter, named Adelheid, as wife to Otto, duke of Saxony; she bore a son named Henry, whom Arnold made duke of Bavaria, against the will of Bernhert, his count of Scheyern. And thus the duchy of Bavaria came for some time to the Saxons; but afterward it was again restored to the Bavarians.

When the empress Agnes, mother of the aforesaid Arnold and Bernher, had died, Arnolph took another wife, Lucard, daughter of Vulp, duke of Bavaria, by whom he had two sons: Louis, who succeeded him in the empire, and Conrad, father of Conrad, the first emperor, who was made duke of Lotharingia; and one daughter, named Lucard, whom the duke of the Saxons took as wife. He also had a son by a concubine, named Rindebald, whom his father afterward made king of Lotharingia, in the year of the Lord 896.

And so Arnold, son of the emperor Arnolph, duke of the Noricans, begot his daughter Adelheid for Otto, duke of the Saxons; she bore Henry, the above-named duke, and a daughter Adelheid, who later became abbess in Quedlinburg, a virgin of holy conduct.

Bernhard, his brother, count in Scheyern, begot by his wife Beatrice, daughter of the king of Hungary, Otto of Wittelsbach, count Palatine of the Rhine, and Echard, later count of Scheyern in Bavaria. Echard begot Arnold, Otto, and Conrad. Further, Arnold begot Conrad, called the Monk of Scheyern; and this Arnold was lord in Dachau, his brother Otto in Scheyern, and Conrad in Valley. From these three brothers, sons of Count Echard, many counts and princes of Scheyern took their beginning, so that at one time in a certain court of Vulp, duke of Bavaria, at a tournament, there were thirty-four counts, all descended from Scheyern, although not all of them dwelt there; for each one was established in his own lordship. And thus the castle of Scheyern, deprived of inhabitants and at length ruinous from age, was changed into a monastery in the year of the Lord 1304, as we shall say later. But all those thirty-four died within three years afterward, except two, Arnold of Dachau and Otto of Scheyern. This Arnold, lord of Dachau, begot Conrad; Conrad begot Arnold and Conrad; Arnold begot Conrad, duke of the Ruthenians, who was killed near Milan and buried in Scheyern.

The emperor Arnulph, powerful and an outstanding defender of the Christian faith, overcame the Normans in a fierce war, killed innumerable numbers of them, and drove them from the territories of the Christians after they had afflicted Germany for forty years. In the same war he killed Sigifrid, king of the Normans, and Gotfrid, king of the Danes. Returning from Gaul to Bavaria after the slaughter of the Normans, the emperor brought with him the body of blessed Dionysius the Areopagite, which he placed at Regensburg in the monastery of Saint Hemeranus the martyr, where it is still honorably preserved today.

Conrad, by Lucard, the second wife of the emperor Arnulph, duke of Lotharingia, begot two sons, Eberhard, who was killed, and Conrad, afterward emperor of the Romans; Arnold, the aforesaid brother of his father, opposed him, as we shall say more fully later in its time.

After the capture of Rome, the defeat of the Northmen, and many other glorious deeds, the emperor Arnulph died. There are those who write that he was consumed by lice, which, growing from the excessively frequent eating of garlic, no one could ward off by any skill.

Louis, son of Arnulph, was a Bavarian emperor. After the emperor Arnulph had died,

*Louis III, emperor:*

Louis his son, the third of this name in the empire, succeeded him, born of Lucard, daughter of Vulp, duke of Bavaria; and thus by both parents he was Bavarian. He was the brother of Arnold, duke of the Noricans, and Bernher, count of Scheyern. He had as wife Lucard, daughter of Ludolph, duke of Saxony, sister of Otto, and died without children.
