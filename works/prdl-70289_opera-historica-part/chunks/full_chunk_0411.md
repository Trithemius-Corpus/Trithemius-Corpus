Guido of Perpignan, a Catalan by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, the twelfth prior general, and afterward bishop of the churches of Majorca and Elne, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and pleasing in eloquence, an acute disputant, and distinguished no less in manner of life than in knowledge. He wrote many excellent little works, by which he commended his name to posterity, and among them the following are reported: a polished and distinguished work in three parts.

Peter of Abano, an Italian by nation, a Paduan by homeland, a man devoted to and learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher and a physician of most celebrated reputation, subtle in talent and renowned for knowledge of many languages, is said to have worked through many little works both in philosophy and in medicine, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. There are some who write that he was a magician and displayed many marvels by that superstitious art; whether this is true, I would not dare affirm, since indeed I know nothing certain about it. For I do not recall either having read anything of his little works or having seen them anywhere. Yet among them the following are reported: a distinguished work divided into one hundred and ten distinctions, which he entitled:

On Medicine of Every Kind, book 1.
On John Mesue, book 2.
And certain other works. He translated Galen on black bile and on the regimen of health, together with many other works. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year 1320.
