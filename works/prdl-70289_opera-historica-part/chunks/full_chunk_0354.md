He also published many other things besides, which have not come to my knowledge. He died under the princes Louis and Lothar, in the year of the Lord 855, in the third indiction, in the ninth year of his episcopate. He was buried in the monastery of Saint Alban near Mainz, not without a reputation for holiness; I have read his epitaph in nearly twenty ornate verses.

Bellator, a priest of a certain church, studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, rich in the reading of the ancients, distinguished in talent and speech, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters. He is said to have expounded many volumes on the holy Scriptures, in which he splendidly displayed his talent. Among the rest there is extant an outstanding volume of his, which he published at the request of friends:

On the Book of Wisdom, 7 books. I have found nothing concerning the others.

He is said to have flourished in the times of the emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Strabus, a monk of Fulda, a German by nation, formerly the pupil and scribe of Abbot Rabanus, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in the studies of secular letters, subtle in talent and distinguished in speech. He wrote not a few little works in exposition of the sacred volumes, in which, showing himself a learned man, he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Therefore, imitating his master, Abbot Rabanus, he wrote:

On Genesis, 1 book.
On Exodus, 1 book.
On Leviticus, 1 book.
A very brief work on the Book of Leviticus.

And many other things. Finally, this Strabus is remembered as the first to have compiled, from the sayings of the holy fathers, the gloss on the whole Bible which is now called the Ordinary Gloss; others afterward enlarged it by adding many sayings of the fathers.

On Three Questions, 1 book. And certain letters, as well as other things.

He flourished under the emperor Louis, in the year of the Lord 840.

Lupus Servatus, priest, to whom Rabanus, abbot of Fulda, is read to have written eight books on the epistle of Paul the Apostle to the Romans, was a man studious and learned in the divine writings, and, according to the age of his time, abundantly skilled in secular letters. He wrote some little works of his talent, of which very few have come to my knowledge. There is extant a volume of his:

The Hierarchy, the books of Dionysius, 4 books. He wrote on the same, 4 books.

And certain other things. He flourished under the emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 830.
