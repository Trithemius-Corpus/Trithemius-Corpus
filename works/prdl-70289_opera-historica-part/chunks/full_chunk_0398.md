William de la Mare, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man learned in the divine Scriptures by long use and practice, and sufficiently skilled in secular philosophy according to the custom of that time, distinguished in intellect, scholastic in speech, composed various little works on the Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them these are reported:

*On the Sentences*, four books.

*Additions to Bonaventure*, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280.

Richard, archbishop of Reims in France, of the Order of Friars Minor, excellently learned in the holy Scriptures, inferior to none of the doctors of his time in delivering sermons to the people, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, is said to have written many excellent volumes, by which he made himself useful to those then present and known to those to come; but I do not recall that any of them have come into our hands. These are reported as his:

*Sermons on the Season*, one book.

He flourished under Emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1270.

William of Samuco, of the Order of the Brothers of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, French by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, distinguished in intellect and eloquence, and venerable no less in conduct than in doctrine. He published several little works of great usefulness, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to the knowledge of posterity. Giles of Rome, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St. Augustine, prior general, and afterwards archbishop of Bourges, formerly a student of blessed Thomas Aquinas, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, devout in life and conduct.
