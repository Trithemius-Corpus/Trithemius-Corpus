This Clodoveus, son of Dagobert, had a wife named Bathildis, an illustrious woman of the Saxon nation, who bore him Lotharius, Hilderic, and Theodoric; concerning these, in their proper places, something must briefly be said in order.

Dagobert, great king of the Franks, died in the thirty-fourth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord’s nativity 665, in the eighth Roman indiction.

Clodoveus, son of the great Dagobert, succeeded his father and reigned sixteen years. His mother’s name was Nantchildis. Under him the mayor of the household, or count of the palace, was Ansegisus, son of Saint Arnulf, bishop of Metz, a vigorous and magnificent man, who was count of Namur; his son Pippin was duke of Brabant.

Clodoveus reigned, as has been said, in Neustria and Burgundy, that is, at Paris, Beauvais, Amiens, Blois, Reims, Lyon, Toulouse, and in the whole Gallican kingdom as far as the sea and the Spains through Alemannia. In eastern Francia, Bavaria, Alsace, Thuringia, and other provinces subject to the kingdom of the Franks, dukes and counts of the noble blood of the ancient kings of France had been appointed, who administered them under the kings.

Sigebert, however, the brother of Clodoveus, as we have said, reigned in Austrasia, which is joined to lower Champagne, among the people of Metz, Cologne, Liège, Trier, the Mosellans, Toul, Mainz, [Neometenses], and Worms.

King Clodoveus died in the sixteenth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord’s nativity 681, in the ninth Roman indiction, leaving three sons, as has been said, by his wife Bathildis: Lotharius, Hilderic, and Theodoric.

Lotharius, the elder son of Clodoveus, succeeded his father and reigned only four years. His mayor of the household was Ebervinus, an impious man, who tore out the eyes of Saint Leodegarius the bishop and made him a martyr of Christ. King Lotharius died in the year of the Lord 675, in the fourteenth indiction.

Theodoric, brother of Lotharius and son of Clodoveus, succeeded his dead brother and reigned almost seven years. For he was made king twice. First, succeeding his brother and given over to leisure and sloth, he began to arrange all the affairs of the kingdom through the count of the palace, whom they called the mayor of the household, showing himself once a year, on the Kalends of May, for the people to see and greet; all the rest of the time he spent in idleness. Hence the commonwealth of the Franks suffered no small loss. For everything was carried out at the discretion of the mayors of the household. His mayor of the household was Ebervinus, whom others wrongly write as Ebroinus, because it is a Germanic name, a wicked and impious man who did many evils; moved by these, the nobles of the Franks cast Theodoric down from the kingdom and confined both him and Ebervinus, separated from one another, in two monasteries.

Hilderic, the middle brother of the two preceding kings and son of Clodoveus, after his younger brother Theodoric had been cast down from the kingdom of the Franks, was called from Austrasia to the kingdom by election of the nobles and reigned a few years. Since he was harsh in character and hateful to many nobles of the Franks, he was killed while hunting.

Theodoric, after his brother had been killed while hunting, together with his pregnant wife Ulida, was restored to the kingdom by the Franks, and thereafter reigned three years. He died in the year of the Lord’s nativity 692, leaving by Queen Clothildis two sons, Clodoveus and Hildebert, who reigned after him.

Clodoveus, the elder son of Theodoric, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks in the above-noted year of the Lord 692, reigned three years with his brother Hildebert. The mayor of the household of the kingdom of the Franks at that time was Pippin, son of Ansegisus, count and renowned duke of Brabant. In the last year of this King Clodoveus, Saint Willibrord, who was also called Clement by Pope Sergius at Rome, was ordained bishop on the day of Saint Clement and sent into Belgic Gaul, Austrasia, and also Frisia, to preach.

This King Clodoveus of the Franks died in the year of the Lord’s nativity 696, in the ninth Roman indiction; his brother succeeded him.

Hildebert, succeeding his dead brother Clodoveus, afterwards reigned fourteen years. In his fifth year Saint Lambert, bishop of Liège, was killed.

King Hildebert died in the year of the Lord’s nativity 610, in the eighth Roman indiction, leaving his son Dagobert.
