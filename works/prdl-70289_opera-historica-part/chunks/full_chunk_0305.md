Achacius, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, a man indeed well exercised in the divine Scriptures, but corrupted by Arian impiety, was held in such reverence by the emperor Constantius that he obtained the appointment of Felix the presbyter as bishop at Rome in the place of Pope Liberius. This Achacius, because he was one-eyed, was called Monoculus. He wrote many and various treatises, which would truly have been useful if they had had a catholic author. Of these the better are said to be those which we have set below.

Seventeen books of [Zethematon?]. One book of [Simmicton?].

He was distinguished under the emperor Constantius, in the year of the Lord 350.

Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Aquitaine, a man very venerable in morals, life, and doctrine, suffered many injuries from the Arians for the faith of Christ. For, through the faction of Saturninus, bishop of Arles, after being banished from the synod of Beziers to Phrygia, for four years he bore hard exile with a constant spirit, where by his prayers he is read to have called a dead man back to life. Since he was a man most learned in every respect, he composed many short works. Of their number the following are reported.

Against Dioscorus, one book. Against Valens and Ursacius, one book.

One book, “God who everywhere...”

He also published some other works, which have not come into our hands. At length, having returned to his city from his exile, he died under the emperor Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 371, in the fourteenth indiction; his feast is celebrated on the Ides of January.

Victorinus the African, a man of consular rank, the most celebrated rhetorician of all in his time, taught rhetoric for a long time in the city of Rome under the emperor Constantine; there, because of the excellence of his learning, he earned a bronze statue in the Forum of Trajan. Converted to the faith of Christ in his old age, he published many treatises on the holy Scriptures in a dialectical manner, obscure enough and scarcely accessible even to the most learned men. Of these the following are extant.

Against the Arians, two books, [unclear]. “In the first sermon...”

On the Holy Trinity, one book, [unclear]. “Homoiotypos...”

Against the heretics, one book, [unclear]. “He lives and [unclear]...”

On the homoousion also, one book, [unclear]. “Be present, true light...”

On the letters of the apostle Paul, four books, [unclear].

And certain other things.

He was distinguished at Rome under the emperor Constantine, in the second year, 360.

Titus, bishop of Bostra, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, sharp and vehement in talent, is said to have fashioned many short works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. Of their number the following are reported.

Two books. Commentaries on the Gospel, one book.

Against the Manichaeans. And many other works besides.

He was distinguished in the year of the Lord 349, and died under the emperor Valens.
