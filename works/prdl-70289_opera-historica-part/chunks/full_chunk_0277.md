Engelhard Funck, otherwise Scintilla, an East Franconian, native of Suobach, a learned man and skilled in every respect, a philosopher and distinguished poet, not ignorant also of the divine Scriptures and pontifical law, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech. He is said to have fashioned certain little works in verse and prose, by which he made his name known far and wide. Among these is: an elegiac poem in praise of his homeland, Franconia; a poem on the ring; a poem on the death of the Cardinal of San Marco; also one book of epigrams in various kinds of meter. He also composed very ornate letters to various persons, and certain other things unknown to us. He still lives, as I hear, in the city of Rome, an advocate in legal cases, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Johannes Langer of Bolekenhan, priest of the Church of Wroclaw, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, excellent in talent, composed in speech, practiced in verse and prose. He wrote, in a double style, certain little works worthy of commendation, by which he made his name known. To Georgius de Lapide, lord of Steynaura, he wrote a distinguished volume on rents and redemptions, one book; also many letters to various persons; many poems; and certain other things. He still lives in Wroclaw and will compose various things, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Paulus Niavis, commonly Schneevogel, a man learned in humane letters, especially in rhetoric, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, who by teaching and writing in the gymnasium of Leipzig acquired great fame for erudition for his name. He wrote for religious novices on the implanting of Latin speech, one book; a life of a certain hermit, one book; on the colors of rhetoric, one book; formal letters for the instruction of young men, [unclear]; [unclear] and a learned Gymnosophist, one book; [unclear] letters to various persons, three books; also familiar letters to various persons, and certain other things unknown to us. I believe the things that came into our hands are not all. He still lives in the margraviate of Meissen and composes various things, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Heinrich, native of the town of Northeim in Saxony, a man studious and learned in secular letters, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in talent and clear in speech, magnificently practiced in verse and prose. He wrote to Frederick, margrave of Meissen, duke of Saxony, the Dymetromachia on the conflict of virtues and vices, one book; also many poems and various letters to various persons; and certain other things unknown to us. I believe that he is still living, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, in the fourteenth indiction.

Nicolaus of Alsenz, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin, of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Kreuznach in the county of Sponheim, lector of sacred theology; studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy; ready in talent, scholastic in speech, and practiced by long habit as a preacher of popular sermons. He wrote on Exodus, one book; also on the Apocalypse of John, one book; on the office of the Mass and the canon, a long and extensive volume, one book; sermons for the season and on the saints, two books; and certain things still hidden with him. He lives to this day in his convent of Kreuznach, about forty years old, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, in the fourteenth indiction.

Bartholomaeus of Cologne, a man studious in secular letters and [unclear] of Carmel, clear in talent and eloquent in speech, a distinguished philosopher and poet, who in the school at Deventer shone with no small distinction by teaching and writing. He wrote in verse and prose several little works worthy of commendation, by which he spread abroad his name. A poem on the praises of philosophy, which he entitled Sylva of Poems, one book; a poem on the feast of Diogenes the philosopher, one book. The rest that he composed have by no means come into our hands. He still lives at Deventer, a town of the diocese of Utrecht, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495.
