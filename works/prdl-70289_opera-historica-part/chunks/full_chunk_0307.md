Gregory, bishop of Emesa, brother of blessed Basil, bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia, a man very devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, stirred also by his brother’s zeal, himself wrote several little works in Greek. Of these, among our writers, I have found only:

On the Image and Condition of Man, book 1.
Against Eunomius, 2 books.

He flourished, younger than his brother, under the princes Valentinian and Valens; he composed various works. In the year of the Lord 380.

Gregory, formerly a monk, bishop of Nazianzus, a man of such learning, integrity, and eloquence that not even his adversaries dared in any way to contradict what was said by him, so that, for the confirmation of authority, it sufficed merely to say that Gregory had said something. He was a cultivator and lover of the monastic life; when, because of the excellence of his teaching and the holiness of his life, he was called to the episcopate, he fled and hid himself, preferring to be abject in the house of the Lord than to be exalted in a crowd by fleeting honors. But almighty God did not wish so bright a radiance to be hidden under the bushel of the aforesaid way of life, since it could be extended to the salvation of many. Found therefore by divine indication, he was led by the people to the episcopal see, and immediately, in excuse for his flight, a solemn homily was delivered, in which the government of souls is shown to be the art of arts. On account of his distinguished learning and holiness of life he was called Gregory the Great before the blessed pope of the same name. He wrote many little works in Greek. He published several sermons and homilies, and various treatises on diverse subjects, which have not come to my knowledge. At length, worn out by old age, when he saw that he could no longer be equal to the government of the people, after another bishop had been ordained in his place, he returned to the pursuit of the solitude he had left behind.

He flourished under Valentinian and Theodosius. In the year of the Lord 390.
