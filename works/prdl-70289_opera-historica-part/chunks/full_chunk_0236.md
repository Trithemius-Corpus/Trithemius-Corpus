*Heimericus de Campo, vice-chancellor of the Cologne gymnasium.* Heimericus de Campo, vice-chancellor of the Cologne gymnasium, a man most learned in divine Scripture, second to none in his time in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, and a most acute disputant of difficult questions. He wrote many distinguished volumes by which he commended his memory to posterity, though only a few of them have come into our hands. On the *Sentences*, 4 books. To Nicholas of Cusa, later cardinal, he wrote a *Compendium of Questions*, 1 book; *On Being and Essence*, 1 book; [unclear].

*Johannes de Schonhouia.* Johannes de Schonhouia, of the Order of the Canons Regular of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of Groenendaal in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, a man learned in divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, subtle in intellect, composed in speech, devout in life and conduct. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, whose reading is judged very useful for devout brothers: a spiritual exhortation to his nephew, 1 book; *On the Course of a Monk*, 1 book; also [several corrupt repetitions of the same titles]; he published various sermons, and several letters to various persons, and many other things.
