*Johannes Trithemius*

He composed very many scholastic [works], beyond number. In addition, for our common use he translated many little works from Greek into Latin: Xenophon’s Apology for Socrates; likewise Nestorius the heretic, whom Saint Cyril the bishop refuted at the Council of Ephesus; also the confession of a certain Cyprian the Greek; Proclus on the praises of blessed Mary ever virgin; some of Lucian’s Dialogues of the Dead; a dialogue by the same author on the council of the gods. He also translated metrically into the German language the single combat from Homer’s Iliad, on the duel of Paris and Menelaus. Likewise he rendered into Latin Ephanius on the life and death of the prophets, and also Maximus Tyrius, the Platonic philosopher, in the oration: If God made good things, whence do evils come? Besides these he translated some other things, which forgetfulness has now taken from our memory. He lives near Stuttgart, bound by the bond of marriage, yet no less applying himself to letters and writing various things, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

*Wilhelmus de Velde*

Wilhelmus de Velde, by homeland a Gueldrian, canon of the order of the regulars of Saint Augustine in Frankenthal in the diocese of Worms, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, a philosopher and astronomer, and a cosmographer of celebrated reputation, deep in intellect, acute, and very subtle; composed in speech, devout in life and conduct. He wrote and is writing certain very beautiful and wondrous volumes, in which he clearly shows to readers the sharpness of his intellect. Of these he recently showed me the following: Statutes of the sisters of his order, one book; a most subtle work on the whole universe, that is, on the beginning, middle, and end of the world, which he entitled The Greater Empyrean, 24 books; likewise a compendium of this work, which he entitled [unclear]; also many tables on various subjects, no less useful than subtle; many sermons to the people; epigrams and various poems; also some things not to be rejected in mathematics; also many letters to me and to others. Finally he composed some other things, which will come to light in their own time. He is still living, fifty years old, in Darmstein, a village of the territory of Worms, serving as parish priest, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 8.

*Petrus Bo-Landan Lautenburger; Petrus Bolandanus of Ladenburg; Petrus*

Petrus Bolandus of Ladenburg, parish priest in the town of Schriesheim in the diocese of Worms, a man most learned in every respect, a distinguished philosopher, mathematician, theologian, and very celebrated poet, ready in intellect, eloquent in expression, excellent in verse, and also an outstanding declaimer of sermons in prose. He wrote some splendid volumes, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Yet few of these have come to my knowledge: a Sapphic poem for Emperor Frederick III; a Sapphic poem on the death of Rudolf Agricola; innumerable epigrams from the sayings of Seneca and Plato; a long Sapphic poem on the divine Mary; a heroic poem on the work On the Threefold Whiteness; a heroic poem on our work On the Misery of Prelates; elegiac poems, Sapphic poems, and letters to Johann Camerarius Dalburg, his bishop of Worms. He wrote a poem to the students of Heidelberg on account of the plague; of epigrams and elegies almost innumerable, one book; besides these many sermons and elegant orations, and very ornate letters to various persons. He is also said to be writing many other things, but in secret. He is still living in Schriesheim, not far from Heidelberg, being 50 years of age, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction 13.

*Johannes Bertram of Neuenburg; Johannes*

Johannes Bertram Nuenburghen wrote to Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, a long letter in the form of a little book, On the Value of Masses, one book. He also explained various questions disputed in the schools. He is now writing and lecturing ordinarily on the epistles of Paul. Almost ten years before he had lectured on Genesis, a great and pleasing work. He is still living in the academy of Mainz, having passed his forty-fifth year of age, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction 14.
