*Abbot of Spanheim*

The Sequentiary, however, thus, one book.
On music and symphony, one book.
On the expositors of sacred Scripture, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
Of the other works which he composed, nothing has come to the notice of our reading. Pope Nicholas I approved the Sequences of this most learned man and ordered them to be sung at Masses. Because of the distinction of his learning and life he was first made abbot, and then, in his old age, consecrated bishop; tenth in order, he presided over the government of the church of Liège, under the emperor Lothar, son of Louis, in the year of the Lord 850. Under him he also died, not without a reputation for sanctity.

CHRISTIANUS, also called Druthmarus, monk and priest of Corbie, of the order of Benedict of holy memory of Cassino, an Aquitanian by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and imbued with Greek and Latin speech, coming from Aquitaine into Gaul, made his name known by writing. Of his little works I have thus far merited only a great and distinguished work, written in plain language to the monks of the monasteries of Stavelot and Malmedy, standing under one abbot in the diocese of the church of Liège:

On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. He did not make this title: “Euan.”
Of the others I have seen nothing.

In the prologue to the exposition of the Gospel according to Matthew he also promised that he would write on the other Evangelists, and this he is said to have done, but it is unknown to us.

WANDELBERT, deacon and monk of the monastery of Prüm, of the order of St. Benedict, of the diocese of Trier, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and magnificently instructed in secular letters, ready in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote not a few volumes both in metre and in prose, of which I have seen only a certain one addressed to Abbot Marcuard:

The Life of St. Goar the priest, one book.
The Miracles of the same, one book. A Martyrology in metre, one book.
He flourished under the emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 860.

ALMANNUS, monk of the monastery of Hautvillers, of the order of St. Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote various little works both in metre and in prose, by which he made his name known also to posterity. And because in his time Francia was being laid waste by the Normans, after the example of the prophecy of Jeremiah he lamented the desolation of that same land and of his monastery in a fourfold alphabetic composition, one book.
The Life of St. Vinardus the bishop, one book.
The Life of St. Helena Augusta, one book.
The Translation of the same.
On Sobriety, in metre, one book. The Life of St. Amandus, in metre, one book.
He is also said to have written some other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Charles III, in the year of the Lord 890.

MILORD, monk of the monastery of St. Amand, of the order of the divine Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned in every respect, an orator, poet, and distinguished musician, and no less venerable for honorable conduct than for knowledge of the Scriptures. He wrote not a few volumes both in verse and in unbound discourse. Of these the following especially are current, addressed to the emperor Charles the Bald:

Letters to various persons, one book. The Life of St. Amandus, in metre, one book.
He flourished in the times of Charles the Second, emperor, surnamed the Bald, in the year of the Lord 870, and composed many excellent works.

HUGOBALD, monk of the monastery of Elno, of the order of St. Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned both in divine writings and in secular letters, in music, poetry, philosophy, and the other arts of humanity second to none in his time, as the many works written by him in both styles testify. Of these I have so far found only the following: addressed to the emperor Charles the Bald, a remarkable work of 136 verses, in which all the words begin with one letter, C: “Carmina clarisone Calvis cantate camenae,” as it is entitled.

On the Praise of Bald Men, one book. Carmina clarisone cal., one book.
On the Art of Music, one book. The Deeds of St. Lebuinus.
Letters to various persons, one book.

He also described the lives of many saints in ornate eloquence, and composed songs in their honor with sweet and regular melody. He flourished in the times of the emperor Charles the Bald, 880.
