*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

NOTGER, bishop of Liège, formerly abbot of the monastery of Saint Gall, once very close to Rabanus, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and easily the most learned of all men of his time in the disciplines of secular letters, a philosopher, poet, musician, astronomer, and very celebrated theologian, venerable no less for his way of life than for his knowledge, wrote many excellent and necessary little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of many. Of these, a few have come to the notice of my reading. I have seen the original work of proses or sequences for the cycle of the year concerning the feasts, which he wrote to Lutward, bishop of Vercelli, archchaplain of the great emperor, book I; to Salomon, afterward bishop of the church of Constance, On the Principal Expositors of Sacred Scripture, book I; On Music, the Modes of Harmonies, book I; Letters to Various Persons, book I. He composed sermons and various homilies. And he elegantly expounded certain books of Sacred Scripture; he wrote many other things. While this Notger was still a monk at Saint Gall, Pope Nicholas I confirmed the sequences and allowed them to be sung in the Mass. He wrote many various hymns, chants, antiphons, and responsories, which down to the present day are used frequently in the Church of God in praises. He flourished in the episcopate under Emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 850.

SALOMON, bishop of Constance, formerly a monk of the monastery of Saint Gall, of the order of the divine father Benedict, once hearer and disciple of the above-named Notger, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and exceptionally taught in secular letters; practiced in verse and prose, subtle in talent, eloquent in speech, and venerable no less for his way of life than for his knowledge. He wrote one book On the Seven Arts which they call liberal. Also one book, a Vocabulary, not to be despised. One book of Letters to Various Persons. He is also said to have composed many sermons and various treatises, which have not come into my hands. He flourished under Emperor Lothar in the year of the Lord 860.

WANDELBERT, deacon and monk of the monastery of Prüm, of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, a man most learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, sharp in talent, eloquent in speech, a philosopher, rhetorician, and most learned poet, wrote, among other little works of his talent, to Abbot Marquard, the Life of Saint Goar the priest, one book; On the Miracles of the same most holy man, one book. He composed a very beautiful Martyrology in verse. He also published several other works, which have not come into my hands. He flourished in the times of the emperors Lothar and Louis, in the year of the Lord 849.
