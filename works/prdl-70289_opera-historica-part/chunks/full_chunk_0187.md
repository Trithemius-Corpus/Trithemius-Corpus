Margaretha, daughter of Rudolphus and wife of Carolus, king of Hungary, bore three sons and two daughters: Wenceslaus, king of the Romans after his father; Ioannes, king of Bohemia; Sigismundus, king of Hungary and afterwards emperor; Helena, who married the son of the king of England; and Margaretha, wife of the margrave of the Old March.

*Adolphus.* Adolphus, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, son of Prince Rudolphus and brother of the above-mentioned Rudolphus, had as wife the daughter of the count of [Ortingen?], who bore him a son named the younger Rupertus, who was also very often called Adolphus after his father's name; of him we shall speak later.

Rupertus, son of Rudolphus, prince and brother of the aforesaid princes Rudolphus and Adolphus, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, had two wives. The first was Mechtildis, duchess of Brabant, who died without children; the second was Beatrix, daughter of the duke of [Mons?], by whom he had no children. This Rupertus founded the University of Heidelberg, which Pope Benedict XII confirmed. He also built the collegiate church in Neustadt with fine stone paneling. He died in the year of the Lord 1412, on the tenth day of the month of February, and was buried in the choir of the church of Neustadt. Next I shall record the sons of Ludovicus the Bavarian, emperor, and their successions, through whom Lower Bavaria was possessed.

Ludovicus, margrave of Brandenburg, son of Ludovicus the Bavarian, emperor, a pious, just, and warlike man, had as wife the daughter of the count of Tyrol, who bore him a son named Meynhardus, who afterwards was count in Tyrol. He married the sister of Adolphus, duke of Austria, and died without heirs; his wife made the county of Tyrol subject to her brother Rudolphus, duke of Austria, whence no small dissension arose between the dukes of Bavaria and Austria.

*Stephanus.* Stephanus, duke of Bavaria, son of Ludovicus, emperor and duke of Bavaria, and brother of the aforesaid Ludovicus of Brandenburg, dwelt in Landshut. He had three sons: Stephanus, Ioannes, and Fredericus, who after their father's death divided his duchy among themselves in the year of the Lord 1413. Ioannes received Upper Bavaria, namely Munich, with its appurtenances; Stephanus received Ingolstadt; and Fredericus accepted Landshut as his lot. Of these we shall speak in their proper place.

Albertus, duke of Bavaria of Straubing, son of Ludovicus the emperor and brother of the two aforesaid dukes, Ludovicus and Stephanus, received, as his share of his patrimony, Straubing with the places belonging to it; he was a good, just, and pious prince. He had as wife the daughter of Ludovicus, duke of Poland, who bore him three sons: Wilhelmus, Albertus, and Ioannes. Of these Albertus possessed [part] of Lower Bavaria, namely Straubing with the adjacent places. He had no wife and died without children. Ioannes, however, the elect of Liege, after the death of his brother gave up the bishopric which for many years he had possessed as elect, and married a wife, the holy Council of Constance disputing with him about this, as we say in its proper place.

His title was this: Albertus, by the grace of God count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, count of Hainaut, Zeeland, Holland, and lord of Frisia. After Duke Albertus died, his son Wilhelmus governed after him for a few years the counties and lordship of the lower land of Hainaut, Holland, Zeeland, and Frisia; he died [unclear] in the year of the Lord 1409. His daughter, named Jacoba, wishing to claim and possess her paternal inheritance for herself, styled herself daughter of the aforesaid counties. Against her Ioannes, her father's brother, abandoning the bishopric of Liege, as we have said, brought those lands and counties into his power by force in many wars. In hope of succession he married the daughter of Ioannes, duke of Gorlitz; but nevertheless he died without children, poisoned by the master of his household, in the year of the Lord 1415.
