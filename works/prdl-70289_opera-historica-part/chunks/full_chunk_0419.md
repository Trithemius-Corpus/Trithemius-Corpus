*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

BERNARDUS Parentinus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures, skilled through long practice, and not moderately learned in scholastic disciplines, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote, for the instruction of simple priests, certain volumes not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these there is extant:

An exposition of the Mass, 1 book: “Since the Wise Man cries out.”
Various sermons, 2 books.
And many other things.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

OSBERTUS, English by nation, of the Order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man certainly very learned in the holy Scriptures and excellently taught in secular philosophy, of sharp and forceful intellect, of scholastic eloquence, subtle in disputing and resolving questions of the Scriptures. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity; of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, 4 books: “One conclusion having been proposed.”
Various determinations, 5 books: “It is asked whether [unclear].”
Also various sermons, 6 books.

The rest that he composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

JOHANNES Estuidi, English by nation, a man easily the most learned of all in secular letters in his time, a philosopher and astronomer second to none, outstanding in intellect and sweet in eloquence. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his intellect, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there is extant an outstanding work of judicial astrology, on the accidents of the world, which is commonly called:

The English Summa, 2 books: “My intention in this book.”

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished and completed the same summa, as is stated at its beginning, under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1347, the fifteenth indiction, on the eighteenth day before the Kalends of January.

BERNARDUS, abbot of the monastery of Cassino, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in intellect and distinguished in life, and a notable cultivator and propagator of regular observance. He wrote, for the edification of monks, certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are extant:

Mirror of Monks, 1 book.
On the Rule of Saint Benedict, 1 book: “It is read in Proverbs.”
On regular precepts, 2 books.
Sermons to the brothers, 1 book.

He is also said to have composed many other things, which have by no means reached us.

SIMON of Cremona, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of the liberal arts, outstanding in intellect and clear in eloquence, quite excellent in delivering sermons to the people, since for a long time among the Venetians he labored at this office, where by word and example he instructed very many. He wrote, for the instruction of simple preachers, little works not to be despised, of which there are extant:

On the Gospels, 1 book: “Lift up your heads, because.”
Also on the Epistles, 1 book: “Let us cast off the works of darkness.”

The rest that he composed have not yet come to the knowledge of our reading.

PETRUS Raymundi of Insula Grassa, French by nation, of the Order [unclear], who after Petrus of [unclear], of Mount Carmel, the fifteenth prior general, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, vigorously governed his order for fifteen years. He wrote elegantly:

On the Sentences, 4 books.
And certain other things.

He flourished and was elected general from the convent of Carcassonne in the province of Narbonne, under Emperor Louis IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1343, the eleventh indiction.

REINHERUS Foroliviensis, the most celebrated jurist of all in his time, teacher of Bartolus of Sassoferrato, a man distinguished in intellect and eloquence. He is said to have composed various little works in civil law, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with glory to posterity. But I have not been able to find any of them anywhere. He died under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1358, the third indiction.
