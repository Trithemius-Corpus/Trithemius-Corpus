*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

BARTHOLOMAEUS of Cologne, German by nation, a man excellently learned in secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, a distinguished philosopher and poet, whose poems even the most learned poets admire and extol with praises. He wrote in both styles certain little works worthy of notice, by which he made his name known. Of these I have so far seen only a little work on the praises of philosophy, composed in meter, which he entitled:

A *Woodland of Poems*, book I. “Why [unclear] gold to Pluto.” *On the Sect of Diogenes*, book I. “Whoever desires [unclear].” A mythological letter.

There is, however, as is said, what he wrote on this book, which bore the sign of the cross, and through him [unclear]. Of the others I have seen nothing. He is still living today at Deventer, composing various works, under King Maximilian and Pope Alexander VI. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

JOHANNES Thurotus, a Hungarian, a man studiously trained in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine scriptures, excellent in intellect, renowned in eloquence. He is said to have fashioned some little works, by which he made his name known. Of these there exists a volume not to be rejected, which he assembled with much industry, containing:

A *Chronicle of Hungary*, book II. “In constant leisure in these years [unclear].”

And certain other things. He is said still to be living and composing various works, under King Maximilian. In the year of the Lord 1494.

HIERONYMUS of Padua, Italian by nation, a man studious in the divine scriptures, excellently learned in secular letters, a distinguished philosopher, orator, and poet, excellent, eloquent in speech. He wrote certain little works not of contemptible reading, by which he commended the memory of himself to posterity. Of these there exists a very beautiful little book, entitled *Jesuita*, addressed in meter to Peter Donatus, bishop of the city of Padua:

*On the Passion of the Lord*, book I. “Greatest of the heaven-dwellers [unclear].”

Other works of his exist. He is said still to be living and composing various works, under King Maximilian. In the year of the Lord 1494.

FRANCISCUS Mataracius of Perugia, a man educated in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher, orator, and poet, highly skilled in both languages, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech. He wrote certain distinguished works, book I [unclear]. Of these there exists a very celebrated little work addressed to the young man Petrus Paulus Cornelius:

*On Composing Verses*, book II. “The ancients [unclear] the poetic art.” Letters to various persons, book II. Many epigrams.

And many other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Sixtus IV.

DOMINICUS Mancinus, a man very learned in the divine scriptures and excellently learned in secular letters, excellent in meter and prose, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence. He wrote certain approved little works, by which he spread abroad his name. Of these I have read only that treatise which he composed in elegant meter:

*On the Passion of the Lord*, book I. “Not the praises of men, nor [unclear].”

He wrote other things. He is said still to be living and writing various works, under Maximilian, most noble king of the Romans. In the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1494.

LUDOVICUS Brunus, Italian by nation, from the marquisate of Montferrat, a man most learned in every respect, a very celebrated professor and interpreter of both laws, a poet laureate, philosopher, distinguished orator, and not ignorant of the divine writings, excellent in intellect, eloquent in speech. He is said to have fashioned many distinguished little works in both kinds of writing, but none of them has come into our hands except that treatise which he described in elegant verse:

*On the Coronation of King Maximilian*, book II. “Holy through eternity [unclear].”

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He is still living, and is said to be writing various works, under the same King Maximilian. In the year of the Lord 1494.
