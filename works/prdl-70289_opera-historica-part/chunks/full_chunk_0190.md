*Chronicle of the Dukes of Bavaria.*

...brought Duke George, surnamed the Rich, with him from France into Bavaria.

John, duke of Bavaria, bishop of Regensburg, son of Duke Stephen in Ingolstadt, and brother of the above-mentioned Louis and of Elizabeth, queen of the French, presided over the Church of Regensburg for twenty-six years.

Ernest, duke of Bavaria, son of Duke John in Munich, and brother of the following William and of Sophia, the second wife of Wenceslaus, king of Bohemia and of the Romans, dwelling in Munich, begot a son named Albert of Munich, of whom we shall speak afterward in his own place.

Henry, duke of Bavaria, son of Duke Frederick in Landshut, had as wife Elizabeth, daughter of the duke of Austria. She bore him a son named Louis, whom he called the Rich, and two daughters, one of whom he gave to Duke Otto of Mosbach, the other to Count Ulrich of Württemberg. He also had two sisters, as we said above, one of whom, named Elizabeth, was the wife of Frederick, burgrave of Nuremberg; from her were born Albert, John, Frederick, another Frederick, margraves of Brandenburg; and Margaret, whom Louis Hogel, duke of Bavaria, took as wife. This Duke Henry of Landshut was at the beginning frugal and poor, but at the end was found to be very rich. His wife, moreover, was an ingenious woman, and loved all honorable arts and sciences.

*He returns to the Palatines descended from King Rupert.*

Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, first-born son of Rupert, king of the Romans, who was called Pipan, armed with zeal for the faith, set himself against the Turks, not without great expense. For in the year of the Lord 1396, John, duke of Burgundy, son of the sister of Albert, duke of Straubing, came with a great army into Bavaria to fight against the unbelievers; this Duke Rupert joined himself to him, with many others. And when, having crossed Hungary, they had come to a certain city of the Turks, which they call Nicopolis, but our people call Schiltau, through the discord of the princes they were defeated by the unbelievers. The duke of Burgundy was captured with many others; innumerable men were killed; Rupert himself, scarcely escaping, returned poor into Bavaria. And not long afterward he died without children and was buried in Amberg.

Frederick, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, second-born son of Rupert, king of the Romans and count palatine, died before his father without children.

Louis, palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, elector, third son of Rupert, king of the Romans, was an upright, just, holy man, who for love of Christ visited his holy sepulcher in Jerusalem. Having become gravely ill at sea, blind and lame, after making the pilgrimage, in the month of May he took as his second wife Mechthild, daughter of the duke of Savoy, a devout and most holy woman. By her he begot three sons, Louis, Frederick, Rupert, and certain daughters, of whom two became nuns; the third the count of Württemberg took as wife, and afterward Albert, duke of Austria, brother of Emperor Frederick III.

Many praises and proclamations of honor ought to have been spoken concerning this prince, which require leisure and another volume. This Duke Louis was elected protector, defender, and advocate of the most universal Council of Constance. John XXIII, pope, handed over to him by the council, he kept in custody for almost three years in his castle of Mannheim.

In the year of the Lord 1438, on the twelfth day of the month of May, Mechthild, wife of this most serene prince, died. At last this most Christian prince Louis, because of blindness and various infirmities, was removed from his administration; not long afterward he died, in the year of the Lord 1449, on the last day of December.

John, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, fourth son of King Rupert, the Neunburger, was a good and just man. He had two wives: Catherine, daughter of the duke of Pomerania, who bore him six sons, Christopher, king of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, Otto, John, another John, Frederick, and Adolf. The last five all died in childhood. His second wife was the daughter of Ernest, duke of Bavaria in Munich; by her he had no children. This Duke John did much against the schismatic Bohemians, and suffered much from them for the Catholic faith. [He died?] in Neunburg, a man beloved by God.

Stephen, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, fifth son of King Rupert, called Zweibrücker, because he received this place as an inheritance, was a just, upright, devout man. He had as wife Anna, daughter of the last count of Veldenz, who bore him five sons: Frederick of Simmern, who obtained the county of Sponheim; Louis, surnamed the Black, who obtained the county of Veldenz; Rupert, bishop of Strasbourg...
