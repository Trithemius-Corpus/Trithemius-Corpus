The same Emperor Otto gave him a part of the forest in the province of the Norici, namely in that place where the Castellense monastery of the order of Saint Benedict now stands; after rooting out the woods, he made the land fit for ploughs. From this duke Ernest many notable lords of Bavaria descended in the Norican province, especially the counts of Castello-Sultzbach.

This Ernest, duke of the Norici, had as wife Adelheid, daughter of the duke of Burgundy, who bore him a son named Ernest, of whom marvelous things are told. When Duke Ernest the elder died, Emperor Otto I joined his wife Adelheid, mother of the younger Ernest, to himself in marriage; she bore him Otto II, emperor, and a daughter Mechthild, who was abbess in Quedlinburg.

Ernest the younger, duke of the Norici, son of the aforesaid Duke Ernest, after his father’s death, gave himself to the service of masters, so that he might learn Latin, Greek, and Italian speech; afterwards he did marvelous things.

Henry, count palatine of the Rhine, a friend of Emperor Otto, hating this duke Ernest, was accustomed to accuse him gravely before the emperor his stepfather, though unjustly, as if he were plotting against the life of the empire itself; this was by no means hidden from Ernest. Hence, when the emperor one day in the city of Speyer was secretly engaged with the son of Count Henry, with the doors closed, Ernest, with Count Wetzel his uncle accompanying him, burst in, broke down the doors, killed Henry the palatine as he was sitting at the emperor’s side, and then, having taken up the cross, went with Wetzel his uncle and many others to the Holy Land. But after a delay at sea in a storm, all except himself [unclear]; he accomplished many outstanding deeds, on land and sea, in many distant regions, which are preserved in writings. Then, returning with Count Wetzel to his homeland, he brought back several men of diverse nations and forms. The emperor, when he had learned of his innocence, received his son back into his favor, appointing him ruling prince of the whole imperial court.

In the year of the Lord 1008, Saint Henry the emperor, duke of Bavaria, defeated Baldwin, count of Flanders, who was rebelling against him, and subjected him to himself; he took Ghent, and, after an engagement, overcame Boleslaus, duke of Poland, who was repeatedly plundering the Saxons, and converted him with his people to the faith of Christ, having in his army Saint Stephen, king of Hungary, his sister’s husband.

Saint Henry the emperor died in the year of the Lord 1025, and was buried at Bamberg in the greater church which he himself had built; his deeds are preserved in writings. Conrad, duke of Franconia, succeeded to the empire; after him Henry III, and after him the fourth emperor.

Henry, duke of Bavaria, brother of Conrad, duke of Swabia, after Saint Henry, from duke became emperor and obtained the duchy of Bavaria. He built a monastery in Osterhofen, in the diocese of Passau; Bernard and Eckhard at length obtained the kingdom, as we said above.

Cuno, duke of the Norici, son of the aforesaid Duke Eckhard, took up the duchy of Bavaria, which his father had held, after his death. He entered into a treaty with the Hungarians, whom he called to his aid against Emperor Henry III, in the year of the Lord 1054.

Cuno, duke of the Norici, at length driven out of Bavaria by Emperor Henry, fled to the Hungarians in the year of the Lord 1055. There, as long as he lived, he remained in exile. The emperor gave his duchy to his brother Conrad, who died a little later.

Conrad, duke of Bavaria, brother of Emperor Henry IV, after Cuno had been expelled, received his duchy from the emperor; he died not long afterward, namely in the year of the Lord 1056.

Agnes the empress, mother of Henry IV, after the death of the aforesaid Conrad, received the duchy of Bavaria from her son; after some years she gave it to a certain noble Otto, living among the Saxons, of whom we shall speak later. In the year of the Lord 1061, Agnes the empress, mother of Emperor Henry IV, conferred the duchy of Bavaria on Otto, count of Northeim; she herself went into Italy.

Otto, duke of Bavaria, a Saxon by race, count in Northeim, received the duchy of Bavaria from the empress, as has been said: a noble, vigorous, and just man, powerful and very acceptable in the court of King Henry. The aforesaid Otto, duke of Bavaria, founded in Northeim a monastery of the aforesaid order of Saint Benedict, giving it many estates, gifts from his county.
