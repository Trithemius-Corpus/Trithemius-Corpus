William, duke of Bavaria, count of Holland, son of the emperor Louis, and brother of the aforesaid princes, who was called Holland, was a good and vigorous man. Poisoned by impious and perverse men, he lost the free use of reason and died without children. His dominion Albert, his brother, received.

Louis the Roman, duke of Bavaria, son of the emperor Louis, and brother [unclear]. Since he was established at Rome with his wife, he was born there; he himself died without children.

Otto, called of Brandenburg, duke of Bavaria, son of the emperor Louis, brother of the aforesaid princes, and margrave, also died in the year of the Lord 1379, without children.

In the year of the Lord 1448, lord Conrad von Deck was killed in his own house in the city of Munich by [Suickerus?] von Gundelfingen. Albert, duke of Bavaria, son of the emperor Louis, fortified the castle of Notterberg at Straubing against fear of the emperor Charles, of whom there was a rumor that he wished to attack him in war. But, agreement intervening, the dissension was settled.

Rupert, count palatine of the Rhine, duke of Bavaria, son of Adolf, prince palatine, of whom we made mention above, also called the Younger to distinguish him from the elder Rupert, his father’s brother, had as wife Beatrice, daughter of the king of Sicily. She bore him Rupert the Little, afterward king of the Romans, concerning whom in his own place [unclear] of Bavaria divided, of whom it has been spoken. Done at Regensburg on the day of Saint Oswald.

In the year of the Lord 1348, lord Conrad von Deck in his house [unclear], a vigorous man, very ready in the art of warfare, in the time of the emperor Charles entered Saxony with an armed force, and on one day triumphed twice over the Saxons; but in the third encounter, defeated by them, he was captured.

Now let us pursue the line of generation of the dukes of Bavaria descended from the sons of the emperor Louis, because from the stock of Rudolf, count palatine, apart from the aforesaid Rupert, there was no male seed.

Meinhard, count in Tyrol, son of Louis of Brandenburg, who was son of Louis the Bavarian, emperor: he had as wife [the daughter?] of Adolf, duke of Austria; he died without children. His wife handed over his duchy to her brother Rudolf, duke of Austria, not without prejudice to the dukes of Bavaria. Besides this Meinhard, Louis left no sons.

Stephen, duke of Bavaria, son of Duke Stephen, who had been the son of the emperor Louis, who possessed Ingolstadt together with what adhered to it, begot two sons: Louis, duke of Bavaria in Ingolstadt, and John, bishop of Regensburg; and one daughter named Elizabeth, whom the king of the Franks took as wife.

John, duke of Upper Bavaria, son of Duke Stephen, son of the emperor Louis, and brother of the above-mentioned Stephen, had as wife the daughter of the duke of [Görlitz?]. By her he begot sons, Ernest and William, and one daughter, Sophia, whom he gave as wife to Wenceslaus, king of the Romans. This Duke John, in the division of the paternal inheritance, obtained Munich with the other surrounding castles.

Frederick, duke of Bavaria, son of Stephen, son of the emperor Louis, and brother of the aforesaid princes Stephen and John, in the division of the paternal inheritance obtained Landshut with what belonged to it. He had as wife the sister of the duke of Milan, who bore him one son, Henry in Landshut, and two daughters, namely Margaret and Elizabeth, whom he gave as wife to Frederick, burgrave of Nuremberg, whom the emperor made margrave of Brandenburg. Stephen also, mentioned above, brother of this Frederick, took as wife another sister of the duke of Milan, the sister of this man’s wife.

The division of the paternal inheritance among the aforesaid three princes of Bavaria was made in the year of the Lord 1412.

Now let us speak of the line of generation of the sons of Albert, son of the emperor Louis and brother of the aforesaid Louis and Stephen. They were three: William, Albert, John.
