*The chronicle of Wasthald ends here.*

Down to this year Wasthald brought and completed his twelve books, on the origin of the Sicambri from the destruction of Troy, continuing them year by year for 768 years. From here Hunibald, in six books, continues the history of the kings and deeds of the Sicambri for 930 years, down to the death of Clovis the great king.

Antenor, succeeding his father in the kingdom, reigned for thirty years. He was held in great honor among his Sicambri, and obtained the very strong friendship of the Saxons; with their auxiliary help he undertook many wars against the Gauls and Romans. For his brothers Priam and Nicanor faithfully assisted him, and they bravely carried out several incursions against the Gauls after crossing the Rhine. But Suimo, his uncle, was always his most faithful helper on his side.

In the eighth year of his reign, when the Sicambri complained of the narrowness of their settlements, especially because pastures were lacking for their cattle, he ordered them to wait a little until the time was suitable for the matter. When a very cold winter set in and all the rivers were frozen, he assembled 80,000 armed Sicambri and invaded the land of a neighboring people, which lay between the northern sea and their territory. All the males, even infants, were slaughtered; only the women he spared. He distributed the region to his people; it abounded in excellent pastures. This land was later called Frisia from a certain Sicambrian prince who was called Friso.

*Suino, duke, brother of Marcomar.*

*Antenor reigns over the Sicambri for thirty years.*

In the twelfth year of Antenor, Suimo his uncle died. He left two sons, Marcomirus and Hector; the first of these perished in the Rhine, and the second administered the county beyond the river Rhine in his father’s place under King Antenor. Priam was appointed by his brother King Antenor as prince of the northern land which they had newly acquired, that is, around the Germanic Sea. From these many princes were born in the course of time, of whom Hunibald records several, some dukes, some counts, but all remained under the command of one king. King Antenor died when the twenty-seventh year of his reign was completed, which was 380 before the Nativity of the Lord, leaving one son named Priam.

Priam, son of Antenor, succeeding his father in the kingdom, reigned for twenty-four years. In his time the Sicambri, already having a great familiarity with the Saxons, began to forget their old language and fatherland, and gradually to use Germanic, until at length they all knew no other language except Theutonic alone. Many words remained among them which seem to have a Greek rather than a Germanic origin, as one who understands both Greek and Theutonic well knows; so it remains now.

*Theocalus, pontiff of the Sicambri, priest of Jove.*

In the sixth year of Priam, Marcomirus died, the duke of King Priam, a kinsman from Suimo. He left a son named Theocalus, whom the Sicambri appointed high priest of Jove; he was for them a prelate, seer, and father of kings. Up to that time the Sicambri had no temples of the gods, but stood and took counsel under oaks. In the eighth year of Priam, among many buildings the Sicambri constructed one very strong city, which they named Neopagus, near the Rhine, where the kings sat for a long time. They made a temple to Jove, whom they worshiped above the others. Theocalus was the priest of this shrine. Priam, after excellently executed invasions against those beyond the Rhine and the Gauls, died in the twenty-fourth year of his reign, 356 before the Nativity of Christ.

*Helenus reigned nineteen years.*

Helenus, son of Priam, succeeding his father, reigned for nineteen years. He venerated Pallas, for whom he built a sanctuary; he sacrificed the boys of captives. He had a bloody war with the Gauls in the region of the Tungri, in which he killed more than 16,000 of them and turned the rest to flight. Having occupied the region, he placed Sicambri there to inhabit it; he appointed princes with many fighters throughout the circuit, so that they might defend the people. King Helenus died in the nineteenth year of his reign, 337 before the Nativity of the Lord.

*King Diocles, reigning thirty-nine years.*

*King Diocles.*

*Saxons. Thuringians against the incursions of the Goths.*

In the tenth year of his reign, a multitude of peoples came out from the Scandinavian islands, which are now called Gothic. Pouring into the borders of Saxony, it carried off everything and devastated the region with sword and iron. Against this people the king of the Saxons, the king of Thuringia, and Diocles himself, king of the Sicambri, marched out to war, having in their army 210,000 brave men. A fierce battle was joined, in which more than 100,000 fighters fell on the side of the Goths. On the side of the Saxons, among the foremost fell Heriman, king of Thuringia, with 20,600 men, and the Saxons [unclear] all the plunder.
