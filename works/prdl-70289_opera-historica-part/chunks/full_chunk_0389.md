Jacobus Baldewini, once a hearer of Odofredus Beneventanus, a most distinguished man, a professor of civil law and an eminent interpreter, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, outstanding in counsel and action, wrote in that faculty certain excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, twelve books. On the old Digest, twenty-four books. Book 13. On the Infortiatum, fourteen books.

And certain other works. He flourished under Frederick, the second emperor: 1240.

David, of the Order, as they say, of Minors, German by nation, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures, notable in life and conduct, was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain little works in plain speech, whose reading is not to be despised by devout brothers; of these the following are extant. To novices.

Humbert, the fifth master general of the Order of Preachers, Viennese by homeland, once a hearer of the theologian Hugh of Barcelona, a man learned in the divine writings and an outstanding preacher, evangelical in life, pure in conscience, outstanding in action, sweet in eloquence, and fit for persuasion, who once drew Hugh, his own teacher in sacred theology, into the order, wrote certain devout little works, by which his name came to the knowledge of posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the rule of St. Augustine, Every man is to be exhorted, one book. A Summa for preachers. On the offices of his order, one book. To the sons of grace and coheirs. Vincent, a Gaul by Burgundian nation, of the Order of Preachers, called of Beauvais, a man studious and practiced in the divine writings, and rich from reading the ancients, subtle in talent and composed in speech, applied so much zeal to letters that he could never be called away from their cultivation by any labors, vigils, or occupations, without always either reading, writing, or preaching. Therefore he was so great that after him he has not yet had an equal, if we weigh in an even balance with the others the lofty little works which he wrote, laboriously collected from many sources. For at the request of the king of the Franks, and with him providing the expenses, he brought together, with very great labors, the great and admirable work of the Mirrors, concerning everything knowable, so to speak, in many volumes, which he prefaced and divided in this way:
