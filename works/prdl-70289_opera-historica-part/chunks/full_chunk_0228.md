*CATALOGUE OF ILLUSTRIOUS MEN.*

This John was once hearer and familiar friend of blessed Peter Thomas, patriarch of Constantinople, formerly master general, [then] residing at Avignon. He flourished under Charles IV. In the year of the Lord 1360.

Albert of Rickmersdorp, a Saxon by country, thirty-first bishop of Halberstadt in order, a man studious and erudite in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, a doctor of Paris. He wrote many illustrious volumes, by which he commended his memory to posterity, of which these are reported: On the Sentences, 4 books; he published commentaries on many books of Aristotle; and he unfolded various questions in the holy Scriptures. But he composed also many others, which have not come to my knowledge. He received the bishopric of the aforesaid church of Halberstadt from Pope Urban V, in the year of the Lord 1365, in the third indiction, and presided twenty-four years. He was buried in his greater church of Halberstadt.

Matthew of Cracow, doctor of Prague, bishop of Worms, a man most erudite in the divine Scriptures, and excellently learned in secular philosophy, subtle in talent and renowned in eloquence, who, on account of [his] inestimable learning, [was] constituted president of the theological school first at Prague [and] then at Paris, [and] obtained great glory among the doctors by reading and writing. He wrote many illustrious little works, of which, however, I recall few to have come into our hands: On the Sentences, 4 books; to Heinrich, bishop of Worms, a beautiful volume on predestination, and that God has done all things well, whose interlocutors are the Father [and] the Son, which he entitled the Rationale of the divine works, 7 books; On contracts, 1 book; On the frequenting or intermitting of [the] celebration [of Mass]; a Dialogue of the reasoning of conscience, 1 book; Of letters to various [persons], 1 book; also various sermons. The rest which he composed have not come to my knowledge. He dies in the year of the Lord [1410?], the fifth day of March; he is buried at Worms.

John [Klenkok?], of the order of the hermit-brothers of Saint Augustine, sprung from Saxony, doctor of Prague, most learned in the holy Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, subtle in talent [and] eloquent in speech. He wrote certain illustrious little works, by which he commended his name to posterity, of which the following are reported: On the Sentences, 4 books; Questions of various positions disputed at Prague, 1 book; also sermons on the [seasons of the] year [and] on the saints, 2 books; Of letters to various [persons], 1 book. The rest which he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1360.

John Fust, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, first lector of the convent of Kreuznach in the county of Sponheim, and then Prior of Strasbourg, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in eloquence, devout in life [and] conversation, a most wonderful declaimer of popular sermons. He wrote, among the other monuments of his talent: Questions of the Sentences, 1 book; he published also some sermons, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in his convent of Kreuznach, where he first put on the habit of [his order], under the emperor Charles IV. In the year of the Lord 1370.

Gerard Groot, called also Magnus, of Deventer, a man most learned both in the divine and in the human writings, especially in canon law, subtle in talent, scholastic in eloquence, devout in life [and] conversation. He wrote several little works of no contemptible reading, by which he transmitted his name to posterity with glory, of which these are reported: On contracts [and] usuries, 1 book; On the instruction of novices, 1 book; On the taking-up of the pastoral care, 1 book; On the inner conversation, 1 book; On the governance of nuns, 1 book; On the inconveniences of matrimony, 1 book; also Of letters to various [persons], 1 book; he published, besides, various sermons [and] many [things], which have not come to the notice of our reading. He dies under the emperor Wenceslaus, in the year of the Lord 1384, in the seventh indiction.

Conrad of Alzey—a town of the duke Palatine [and] count, in the diocese of Mainz, situated three miles from Worms—a man erudite in the divine Scriptures, excellently learned in secular letters, a most celebrated philosopher [and] poet, subtle in talent, renowned in eloquence, exercised in meter [and] prose.
