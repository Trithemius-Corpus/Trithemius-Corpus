We have seen a Bible and the Decretum, which he wrote with his own hand and which today are extant at Molsheim. He died in the year of the Lord 1328.

Johannes Buridanus, a very great philosopher, first taught philosophy gloriously for many years in the school at Paris, and afterward instructed many in the same faculty at the University of Vienna in Austria, not without the admiration of all. Among many little works of his talent he wrote Questions on Aristotle's Ethics, solid, [unclear], profound, as Johannes de Gerson bears witness; he composed many other things, which do not now come to our notice.

Apollonius, a most eminent and very penetrating physician of Erfurt, who in [unclear] established perpetual stipends for learned men in philosophy, which he endowed magnificently. In his profession he wrote some little works, which, however, have as yet by no means come into our hands.

Sigismund, Emperor of the Romans, a man distinguished both for learning and for knowledge of many languages, delivered a Latin oration at the great Council of Constance, full of all religious piety and true Christian religion, which Johannes Gerson relates that he heard with his own ears, not without pious compunction. Nor did he hesitate to compare him with most holy David.

Johannes, called Sonnemannus, that is, Man of the Sun, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, Doctor of Sacred Theology, of the convent of Kassel in Hesse, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, an excellent interpreter of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, a most eloquent declaimer of popular sermons. He wrote On the Ten Commandments, one book; On the Apostles' Creed, one book; On the Preparation of the Heart, one book; On Superstitions, one book; On Contracts, one book; Cases against the redemption of rents, one book; Sermons for the season and on the saints, two books. He also published some other things that have not come to my notice. He flourished in his convent at Kassel, under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Gerardus de Castris, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of the convent of Mainz, a learned and erudite man, outstanding in intellect, scholastic in speech, devout also in life and conduct, and a very celebrated cultivator of regular observance, an excellent declaimer of sermons. He wrote On the Passion of the Lord, one book; Sermons for the season and on the saints, two books; and other things not to be scorned. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Gregorius Heimburgius, no ordinary jurist and orator, did not depart from Italy, especially from the Venetians, without praise for his speeches. Although his admirable talent, trained not merely in the empty windiness of words but also well instructed in the most sacred laws, procured for him the envy of others. For envy seeks the highest things; the winds blow upon the loftiest. He wrote various speeches and letters in defense of Sigismund, duke of Austria, and an appeal from the sentence of the supreme pontiff, and other things.

Johannes Simlerus of Strasbourg, a very celebrated jurist, outstanding in intellect, eloquent in speech, supreme in prudence, wrote many very elegant letters, both to me and to others, and certain other little works not to be despised, in which he exercised his lively intellect. He died under Pope Innocent VIII.

You now have from me, candid reader, the *Prosthesis* which I promised, that is, an addition of certain men outstanding in talent to the famous Catalogue of the reverend father Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, in which he described the illustrious men of our Germany with the greatest eagerness. Nor do we doubt that there remain almost countless men of whom we have made no mention, because their names and little works are wholly unknown to us. But let each man after us add those whom he finds, and the glory of the Germans, ever increased, will become known to all foreigners. For we, as much as was permitted us, have labored with a most ready mind for the praise of our nation.
