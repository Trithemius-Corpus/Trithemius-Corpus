*Johannes de Beetz.*

Johannes de Beetz, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Tienen, a man most learned in the divine scriptures and most skilled in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, and devout also in life and conduct, teaches as a declaimer of popular sermons no less than as an eloquent man. He wrote On Paul’s Epistle to the Romans, one book; also On the Precepts of the Divine Law, ten books; On the Sacrament of the Altar, one book; Various Questions Disputed in the Schools, one book; On the Passion of the Lord, one book; Sermons on the Season and on the Saints, two books. Finally he published many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. *Arnoldus de Busco.*

Arnoldus de Busco, canon regular of Saint Augustine, of the monastery of Windesheim in the diocese of Utrecht, for some time prior in Sulta in Saxony, in the diocese of Hildesheim, a man studious in the holy scriptures, of honorable morals and devout conduct. He wrote, in a plain style, little works not to be despised, the reading of which is judged useful for devout brothers who love their salvation. Of these, however, I recall that few have come into our hands. I have read that volume which he wrote On the Lives of the Illustrious Men of his Order, of the Monastery of Windesheim, one book; also On the Origin of the Modern Devotion and the Reform of his Order, [unclear] book; a lengthy letter On the Manner of Making Progress in Religion. The other things which he wrote have not come into our hands. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1470. And he died a few years ago.

*Rudolphus de Rudesheim.*

Rudolphus de Rudesheim, a Rhenish countryman from the diocese of [unclear], first dean of Worms and afterward bishop of Wroclaw, doctor of sacred letters, subtle in intellect, distinguished in eloquence, honorable in life and conduct, flourished gloriously for many years in the academy of Heidelberg, both by reading and by teaching. He wrote many distinguished volumes, of which the following are extant: On Genesis, one book; On Exodus, one book; on Leviticus, one book; also on Numbers, one book; on Deuteronomy, one book; on Joseph, one book; on Judges, one book; on the prophet Isaiah, [unclear] book; on the Sentences, four books; many various sermons on the Season and on the Saints, one book. He also delivered several orations, which it would be too long to describe one by one. But he published some other things, still quite unknown to us. He flourished under Emperor Frederick, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Johannes de Regiomonte, once a disciple of the astronomer Georgius Burbachius, a man most learned in every respect, no less than Anaximander of Miletus or Archimedes of Syracuse, most learned in Greek and Latin eloquence, the glory of the Germans, on account of astronomy and the other mathematical disciplines endowed by Matthias, king of Pannonia, with a consulship and perpetual stipend among the citizens of Nuremberg, attained great praise for his learning not only among our Germans but also among the Italians and the French. For, summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV to correct the Paschal computation of Dionysius, while he was preparing many things for posterity, he died of disease or plague, not without the very great loss of students. He wrote a very fine Calendar with [unclear], [unclear] book; likewise Ephemerides on the disposition of the heavens for individual days over certain years, one book; Against Gerardus Cremonensis, one book; various orations; many letters to different persons; for Lord Johannes, archbishop of Esztergom, Tables of Directions and Profections, useful in nativities, one book; also Tables of the Right Sine, one book; On the Composition of the Astrolabe, one book.
