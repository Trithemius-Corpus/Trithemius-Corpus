Henry, the younger duke of Bavaria, son of Otto, the aforementioned king of Hungary, died in the year of the Lord 1333, without children, in the twenty-first year of his age. Otto, duke of Bavaria, son of the often-mentioned Duke Stephen, died in the year of the Lord 1335, without children. Henry the elder, brother of Otto, duke of Bavaria, died in the year of the Lord 1349, leaving a son named John, who, living for a short time, succeeded his father in the duchy.

John, duke of Bavaria, son of the aforesaid Duke Henry the elder, succeeded his dead father in the duchy of Lower Bavaria for a few years. He had as wife the daughter of Duke Louis, and not long after his father he died without children. Therefore, when the aforesaid Duke John had died without children, Louis the emperor and duke of Bavaria received his inheritance; from him descend all the dukes of Lower Bavaria who live today, as we shall say in its place. And thus the stock of Henry, duke of Landshut, fails here.

In the year of the Lord 1325, Louis, duke of Bavaria, was elected king of the Romans, as we have said; against him Frederick, duke of Austria, through Rudolf, Louis’s brother, and certain others, was elected king, whence many evils and wars arose. Therefore on the day which precedes the feast of Saint Michael, each proceeded to war with a multitude of armed men, between Mühldorf and Oettingen, in a meadow beside the road. In this battle, when they fought fiercely, Louis came out victor and captured Frederick, duke of Austria, whom he committed to custody in the castle called Trausnitz, not far from Nabburg; but his brother Rudolf, as we have said sufficiently, he despoiled of all his goods and drove into exile. At length Emperor Louis, through the deceits and false letters of the master of Augsburg, his chancellor, incurred the indignation of the pope; and how it happened I shall explain more fully when there is need. To this Emperor Louis, William of Ockham of the Order of Minors, a most learned man, with certain other brothers of his order, having been embittered by Pope John XXII, fled; he remained defended with him, and wrote against the pope and declared him a heretic. Concerning these matters there is a fuller account elsewhere.

Emperor Louis did and suffered many wondrous things, which I can in no way include in this supplement. If it is necessary, I shall write at an opportune time.

Now I shall continue the line of descent from the sons of Rudolf, count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, brother of the aforesaid Emperor Louis, from whom all the counts palatine of the Rhine of this time have descended. Afterward I shall add the sons of that Emperor Louis himself, from whom the dukes of Lower Bavaria arose. Since the generation of princes, having multiplied, thereafter passed into various successions, I shall write only of the principal dukes of Bavaria and counts palatine of the Rhine.

Therefore, after Rudolf, the aforesaid count palatine of the Rhine and duke of Bavaria, had been driven into England by his brother, as we remember that we have said, and had died, Emperor Louis, when he saw his sons, said: “The father, and not the sons, sinned against me; hence it is not just that they should undergo punishment for another’s crime.” Accordingly he gave them the power of choosing among Bavaria, Swabia, Austria, and the Rhine whichever of these provinces they wished. Having taken counsel beforehand, they chose their paternal inheritance, namely the county palatine of the Rhine with its properties and the other cities in Bavaria, namely Amberg and Nabburg, with others adhering to them, in the year of the Lord 1439, on the sixth feria before the day of Saint Oswald the martyr. The sons of Rudolf were three: namely Rudolf, Adolf, and Rupert. We shall speak of them in order.
