Johannes Gauwer, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, lector of sacred Scripture in the convent of Mainz, was a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and quite fit for delivering sermons to the people. He wrote some not-to-be-despised little works of his practiced intellect, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there are extant:

*On the Sentences*, four books. “To the praise of almighty God.”
*On Exodus*, one book. “The law for the common utility.”
*Harmony of the Evangelists*, one book. “Because God sent his Son.”
*Sermons on the Season*, one book.
*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.
*A Threefold Lenten Work*, three books. “When you fast,” etc., “knowing...”
And many others.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1440.

Bernardinus, of the order of the Friars Minor, an Italian by nation, from Siena as his homeland, was a man learned in the divine Scriptures, the most celebrated preacher of all his time, and most holy in life and conduct, since both alive and dead he showed by countless miracles the force of his practiced intellect also to posterity. Of these there are extant:

*On the Apocalypse of John*, one book. “Blessed is he who reads,” etc., “threefold...”
*On the Eternal Gospel*, one book. “For I came not to destroy the law.”
*On the Christian Religion*, one book. “Now remain faith, hope.”
*On the Conception of Saint Mary*, one book. “The depths were not yet,” and...
*On the Passion of the Lord*, one book.
*Sermons on the Season*, one book.
*Sermons on the Saints*, one book.
And certain other works.

He died at Aquila, a city of Abruzzo, in the reign of Emperor Frederick III, under Pope Eugenius IV, in the year of the Lord 1443, in the sixth indiction, in the sixty-third year of his age. Because of his frequent miracles he was inserted by Pope Nicholas V into the catalogue of the saints, in the year 50, and the seventh after his death. His feast is observed on the twentieth day of the month of May.

Antonius Gayerus, from Pavia by nation, a very celebrated doctor of the art of medicine, nobly learned in the precepts of the philosophers, excellent in intellect, and by no means slightly renowned for skill in his profession, wrote in that discipline certain lucid little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On Disease of the Stomach*, one book.
*On Fever*, one book.
*On Diseases in General*, one book.
*On Pleurisy*, one book.
*On the Womb*, one book.
*On Fluxes*, one book.
*On Arthritis in the Joints*, one book.
*On the Ailments of Stone*, one book.
*On the Third Book of Avicenna*, one book.
And many others.

He flourished at the time of the Council of Basel, in the year of the Lord 1440.
