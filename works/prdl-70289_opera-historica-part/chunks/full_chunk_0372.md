He also composed many sermons and various other treatises. He flourished under Emperor Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1130.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Deutz near Cologne, of the Order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, enlightened by the Holy Spirit through vision, not ignorant of secular letters, a monk of Saint Lawrence of [Traiectum], was taken from there by Frederick, archbishop of Cologne, on account of his incredible learning, and raised to be abbot of the monastery of Deutz. He composed many distinguished little works. For from the time when in his youth he entered the monastery, he had exercised himself so much in the holy Scriptures that not even when sleeping did he seem able to rest from those meditations, but his tongue and lips were moved as if in reading. Of his works I have found only the following at present. To Cuno, abbot of Siegburg and afterwards bishop of Regensburg.

Finally, he is said to have written many other works, and to have explained the greater part of the New and Old Testament in many books; of these, up to the present, we have seen nothing except those which we have marked. He flourished in great holiness, gleaming with doctrine and miracles, under Henry V, in the year of the Lord 1124. And he died under Lothar III.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Limburg in the diocese of Speyer, of the Order of Saint Benedict, German by nation, magnificently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, distinguished in life and conduct, so much so that he deserved to be visited by frequent divine revelations. For many years he applied himself to secular philosophy in the school of Paris and became a noble philosopher. But, considering that the philosophy of the present life was empty, he abandoned the study of vanity; returning to his homeland, he became a monk in the said monastery, and finally abbot.
