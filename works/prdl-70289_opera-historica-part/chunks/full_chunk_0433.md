From his writings it appears that he was a devout, religious man, of some order, although it is unknown to us. There are indeed those who write that he was a canon regular, which we pass over as probable. He flourished in the school of Cologne. He wrote various works.

John of Hesdin, of the order of the brothers of the Hospital of Saint John of Jerusalem, a man learned in the divine writings, not ignorant of secular philosophy, an eminent doctor of the sacred page, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, keen in talent and orderly in speech, published many distinguished volumes; at the University of Paris he delivered many elegant lectures. He is also said to have written commentaries and lectures on many books of the Bible, which have as yet by no means come into our hands. He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Philip of Monte Calerio, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man practiced in the sacred writings, a great and excellent preacher of the word of God. For the use of those who devote themselves to declamations he wrote small works not to be despised, by which, nobly exercising his talent, he also destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only the following:

Sermons on the Temporale, 1 book. There will be signs in the sun, [and in the moon?].
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book. Lenten Sermons, 1 book.

He is said besides to have composed various other treatises and to have explained several books of the divine writings, but none of them has come to me.

John, surnamed Grossus, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, nineteenth prior general, others have it twenty-first, a Toulousain by country, a studious and learned man. In clear speech he wrote volumes not to be scorned, by which, having splendidly exercised his talent, he also made his name known to posterity. Of these I have seen only the distinguished work which, containing the foundation and progress of the Carmelite order, is entitled:

Viridarium, 3 books. By what things [unclear].
His Sermons, 1 book, are reported. And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1406.

Nicholas of Gorran, of the order of the Preachers of Saint Dominic, a man most devoted to the holy Scriptures, nobly learned, outstanding in talent, published in scholastic and clear speech many volumes, the reading of which will not be to be rejected by those who desire to be initiated in the holy Scriptures; of these I remember that few have come into our hands. Among the many, the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 4 books. On the five books of Moses, 5 books.
On Isaiah the prophet, 1 book. On Jeremiah also.
On Ecclesiasticus, a large work, 1 book. He will give a blessing.
On the Song of Songs, 1 book. On the Gospel of Mark, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book. On the Gospel of John, 1 book.
