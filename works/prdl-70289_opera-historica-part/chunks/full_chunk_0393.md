He died under the emperor Rudolf, in the year of the Lord 1280, in the eighth indiction, in the eightieth year of his age. He was buried in the middle of the choir of his convent at Cologne. And after him there did not arise a man like him, who was so learned, erudite, and experienced in all letters, sciences, and matters. As for his being accused of necromancy, the man beloved by God suffers an injustice.

SIMON of Tournai, priest, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and subtly trained in secular philosophy, a very acute dialectician and highly skilled in all the liberal arts, became master of the theological school and taught for many years in the Parisian gymnasium, where he instructed very many hearers. But while he follows Aristotle too much, he is found to have erred in certain matters. He wrote certain little works of his own talent, of which the following are reported.

His Sentences, one book. Various Questions, one book.
On the Creed of Athanasius, one book. He also published some other works, which have not come to my knowledge.

THOMAS of Aquino, of the order of Friars Preachers, hearer and disciple of Albert the Great in the convent at Cologne, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly trained in secular letters, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, notable in life and conduct, easily obtained first place among all the doctors of his time. Like another Augustine, he gave himself wholly to the study of the Scriptures, spending day and night in love and practice of it; he wrote so many and such great volumes that among the moderns he has few equals either in number or in usefulness.
