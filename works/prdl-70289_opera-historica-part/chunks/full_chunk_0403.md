*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

On the Sixth of the Decretals, book 1. On the Code, 10 books. On the Old Digest, 24 books. On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books. Many counsels, book 1.

Besides these he published not a few other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

John Duns, a Scot by nation, of the Order of Friars Minor, once a pupil at Paris of Alexander of Hales the Englishman, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and most learned in Aristotelian philosophy, and so profound that his writings are penetrable by few, and for that reason also less commonly used. He published certain well-ordered volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences, 4 books, “Desiring concerning the prologue.” Book 1, “All things are difficult.” Book 1, “Because the highest thing for us.”

Quodlibets also, book 1, “There shall be signs,” etc. “In this.”

On the knowledge of God.

Questions on the Metaphysics.

Sermons on the season, book 1.

Sermons on the saints, book 1.

He is said to have written commentaries on the Gospel and the Apostle, and to have composed various other treatises, which have not come to my knowledge. He died in the time of Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1308, indiction 6. He was buried at Cologne among the Minorites.

Simon, monk of Hasungen, of the Order of Saint Benedict, a man most studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, so turned into practice the counsel of our divine lawgiver Benedict, by which the monk is commanded to love the divine Scriptures, that nothing in this life was sweeter to him than to read sacred literature and to turn over with diligent study the little works of the holy fathers. He abridged:

The Moralia of Pope Gregory, book 1. He wrote sermons on the Song of Songs, book 1.

The vision of a certain convert, book 1. Several sermons, book 1.

Letters also to various persons, book 1. And many other things.

He also collected excerpts from the sermons of blessed Pope Gregory on Ezekiel; likewise from the Conferences of the holy fathers, from Richard of Saint Victor on the twelve patriarchs and contemplation, and various things from various sources, not to be despised.

Peter de Bella Parthica, a Burgundian by nation, a man most skilled in both civil and canon law, subtle and sharp in intellect, and most ready in disputing and resolving doubts and questions of law. He wrote many distinguished volumes on the laws, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Among them these are reported:

On the Code, 9 books. On the Institutes, book 1. On the Old Digest, book 1. One hundred disputations, book 1.

24 books. And certain other things.

On the Infortiatum, 14 books. Pope Octavus. In the year of the Lord 1500.

He flourished at the same time as Saint Mugelanus, under Emperor Albert and Boniface [unclear].

Absolution. Concerning absolution.

Reiner of Pisa, an Italian by nation, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures and sufficiently skilled in canon law, prompt in intellect, scholastic in eloquence, cautious in counsel, venerable no less for religion than for knowledge, and certain other things.

Gerard of the Order, On the Teaching of the Heart, book 1, “Prepare your hearts.”

Sermons on the season, book 1. Sermons on the saints.

Besides these he published certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge.
