*Johannes Trithemius*

A sect was founded in these times, in the year 40 of Samael, or thereabouts. Hence, for the rest, it is that all things happen by providence. The Saracens waged many battles against the Christians in Asia and Africa. Constantinople was taken by the Germans, and Baldwin, Count of Flanders, was made emperor. From Germany more than 26,000 children were drowned at sea by pirates; seduced by a vain spirit, they said that they were going to recover the Holy Land. From Spain, herdsmen gathered together, admitting no one to Paris, seized the goods of the clergy, while the people rejoiced. But when they stretched out their hands to the goods of the laymen, they were killed. In the year of the Christians 1212 Frederick II was elected; he ruled for 33 years, and did much against the Church.

In the year 1238 an eclipse and earthquakes continually crushed many thousands of people. Frisia too was almost submerged by sea-floods, and more than 100,000 people perished. The Tartars devastated Hungary and Poland, after first overcoming Greater Armenia and many regions. In the year of the Christians 1244, a certain Jew at Toledo, while digging, found a book in which it was written: “In the third world Christ will be born of the Virgin Mary and will suffer for the salvation of men.” Believing at once, he was baptized. The third world was the third revolution of the spirit of Saturn, of which mention was made above; at its beginning Christ was born of the Virgin. The Roman pontiffs, removing Frederick from the empire, declared that it had been vacant for 28 years, until the election of Rudolf, Count of Habsburg, whom they set among the kings. By election of the princes, first Henry, Count of Schwarzburg, a Thuringian; William, Count of Holland; [Conrad?], son of Frederick; Alfonso, King of Castile; and Richard, Earl of Cornwall, brother of the King of England; and evils were multiplied on the earth. At this time, around the year of the Lord 1260, the confederation of the Swiss first began: a people small in number, which grew with time and killed many of the nobles, driving others from their borders; warlike men, whose commonwealth is known to all the peoples of Germany. In the year of the Christians 1273 Rudolf of Habsburg was made emperor by election of the princes, and ruled for 18 years, a man in all respects prudent and excellent, from whom all the later dukes of Austria descended. The Tartars, invading the lands of the Christians, Constantinople, and Greece, inflicted much damage on the Christians. The Saracens occupied many cities in Asia and killed more than four hundred thousand Christians. When Rudolf died, Adolf of Nassau was elected king and ruled for 6 years; afterward Albert, son of Rudolf, defeated him in battle near Worms, killed him, and was elected emperor in the year 1298; he ruled for 10 years and was killed by his brother’s son. The order of the Templars was destroyed by Pope Clement V. The island of Rhodes was taken by Christians from the hands of the Saracens in the fourth year of the siege and of continuous war against them. After Albert was killed by his nephew, Henry VIII, Count of Luxembourg, was made emperor, and ruled for five years. When he died, Louis IV of Bavaria ruled for [12?] years, beginning in the year [1315?]; the Roman pontiffs gave him the crown. Frederick, Duke [unclear]. Charles IV, King of Bohemia, emperor. Earthquake. Tolls. [Charles’s?] son Wenceslaus, emperor. [The Duke] of Austria opposed him, but was defeated by him. After Louis, Charles IV, King of Bohemia, was made emperor; he raised the bishopric of Prague to an archbishopric and ruled for 31 years. There were very great earthquakes. This Charles newly instituted many things concerning tolls in favor of the prince-electors. Günther, Count of Schwarzburg, calling himself king, opposed Emperor Charles, but prevailed in nothing against him. After Charles, his son Wenceslaus ruled for [112?] years; after him Jodocus, Margrave of Moravia, succeeded Sigismund, brother of Wenceslaus. Leopold, Duke of Austria, with 8 counts and more than 4,000 men, was killed fighting in battle by the Swiss. Under Wenceslaus, king and emperor of the Bohemians, the teaching of the Hussites took its beginning. Wenceslaus was deposed; after his deposition, Rupert, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, was elected, and ruled for 10 years. In the year of the Lord [1369?], the Christians brought war upon the Saracens, but it went badly because of the arrogance of the French, since they killed more than 100,000 of our men, besides the captives, among whom was John, Duke of the Burgundians. There were many wars in these times. In the year [1407?] Sigismund became emperor for 27 years. He tried to lay waste Bohemia for the extirpation of heresies, but made little progress. The kingdom of France was cruelly laid waste and afflicted by the English and the Burgundians. When Sigismund departed this life, Albert, Duke of Austria, his son-in-law, succeeded him in the year of the Christians 1438; and he ruled for [56?] years, a man divine in intellect and peaceful in spirit. In the year of the Lord 1440 he began to reign. In the year of the Christians 1453 Constantinople was taken by the Turks through the betrayal of a certain Genoese, and gradually all Greece fell away from the Christian faith.
