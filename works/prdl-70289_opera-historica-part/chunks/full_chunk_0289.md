TERTULLIAN, a priest, by origin a Carthaginian of the African province, his father a centurion and a man of proconsular rank, was a man of keen and vehement intellect, most learned in both divine and secular writings, since at Carthage he taught rhetoric with distinction for many years. He wrote in Latin almost innumerable small works, in which he powerfully crushed the errors of the heretics. The most blessed martyr Cyprian held this man’s writings in such veneration that he let no day pass without reading them. For he was accustomed to say to his secretary, “Give me the master,” meaning Tertullian. At last he is said to have slipped into the error of Montanus; for this reason his works are counted among the apocrypha, Distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*. ORIGEN, who is also Adamantius, a priest, by nation an Alexandrian, son of the martyr Leonides, once a disciple of Clement of Alexandria, was a man of great and excellent intellect, so that he never had an equal in zeal and learning. In the tenth year of Severus Pertinax, when a persecution was stirred up against the Christians and his father Leonides was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ, he was left poor with his widowed mother and six brothers, being about seventeen years old. For the treasury had seized all their substance because of their confession of Christ, so that they no longer had anything from which they might live. And although the number of years reckoned in the young man was rather small, nevertheless his learning was already wonderful, and the constancy of his Christian faith admirable. For he had resolved to fall bravely with his father Leonides for the faith of Christ, had it not happened that, through his mother’s stratagem, he was preserved for the advantage of the Church, since he was to become a great teacher. For when he had decided early in the morning to hasten to his father at the place of contest on the day before, his clothes were taken away by his mother during the night and the doors of the lodging were locked, and he was forced to remain at home. But when he grew into a man, he resolved to lead a most ample life, and, proclaiming the word of God round about him, he went barefoot for many years. Finally, he never slept on a bed spread with feathers; he abstained altogether from wine and meat, except in illness; and he was intent on serving God devoutly with fasts, vigils, prayers, and continual studies and expositions of the divine Scriptures.
