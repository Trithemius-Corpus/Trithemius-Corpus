VICTORINUS, a rhetorician of Marseilles, a man most learned in secular letters and very devoted to the Holy Scriptures, attempted to expound the Holy Scriptures; but, occupied with secular literature, and having entered the field of the divine utterances without a teacher, he did not accomplish what he wished. Hence the little works that he wrote are indeed excellent in Christian sense and pious zeal, but are shaped by a lighter weight of judgment. He published, addressed to the person of his son Etherius, commentaries from the beginning down to the death of the patriarch Abraham:

On Genesis, four books. And certain other things which are hidden from us.

He flourished under Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year 430.

PHILIPPUS, monk and priest, once a hearer and disciple of blessed Jerome, a studious and learned man, a notable lover of voluntary poverty, who by word and example instructed many toward piety, after the death of his master published certain commentaries on the divine Scriptures. Of these there is extant, as they say, an outstanding work:

On Job, one book. Letters to various persons, two books.

He flourished under the emperors Theodosius and Valentinian, in the year of the Lord 430. And he died under Marcian.

VINCENTIUS, monk and priest of the island of Lérins, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, distinguished in life and morals. There is extant his notable work, which he composed under the name Peregrinus, very celebrated and praised by the ancient doctors, on avoiding the errors [unclear] of heretics:

Against all heretics, two books.

He is also read to have written some other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished while Theodosius and Valentinian were reigning, in the year of the Lord 430.

PAULUS, priest, a Pannonian by nation, which is now called Hungary, a man devoted to sacred letters, sufficiently learned, venerable no less for the practice of religion than for knowledge of the Scriptures, a zealous defender of the Christian faith, is said to have written not a few volumes, the reading of which is held to be quite suited to confuting heretics. There are extant his works addressed to Constantia, a virgin dedicated to Christ:

On virginity and contempt of the world, two books.

Concerning the others I have as yet found nothing. He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 430.

ISAAC, from being a monk a priest of the church of Antioch, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, of very celebrated reputation for delivering homilies to the people, beloved by God in life and conduct, venerable to men, wrote in the Syriac tongue, in imitation of Saint Ephrem, several devout little works, the reading of which is judged very useful for religious men.

Of these I have found among our holdings the following:

Against the Nestorians and Euty[ches?], two books.

An exhortation to the spiritual life, one book.

On the battle against vices, one book.

On access to God, one book.

On the difficulty of virtues, one book.

A dialogue on spiritual progress, one book.

On the order of the monastic life, one book.

On humility, one book.

On the three orders of those making progress, one book.

On the solitude of monks, one book.

On diverse temptations, one book.

On the instruction of novices, one book.

On penitence, one book.

A poem on the desolation of Antioch, one book.

He delivered many homilies to the people, but they have by no means come into our hands. He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 440.
