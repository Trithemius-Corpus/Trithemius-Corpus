JOHANNES, archdeacon of a certain church of Horit., a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote certain little works, not to be despised, for the edification of the faithful, by which he made his name known. Of these there is an excellent and distinguished work, addressed to a certain royal notary Stephanus, against the Greeks.

Also various letters, 1 book. “[unclear] Greek.”
*On the Procession of the Holy Spirit*.

But he is also said to have written certain other works, which have not come into our hands.

GODEFRIDUS of Viterbo, priest, a man zealous and learned in the divine writings, and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding and distinguished in talent, practiced in verse and prose, wrote little works of reading not to be despised, by which he left his memory to posterity. Of these there is a large volume addressed to Urban III, mixed in verse and prose, on the beginning of the world, on the whole Old and New Testament, and on the ages of the world, the deeds of the times, and all the histories of kings, which he entitled:

*Pantheon*, 15 books. “On the beginning of the world.”

Of the others I have seen nothing.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick I and his son Henry, in the year of the Lord 1185.

ECKEBERTUS, second abbot of the monastery of Saint Florinus, of the order of the divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, a German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and no less venerable in conduct than in knowledge, was the brother of the blessed virgin Helizabet, abbess of Schonau. He had previously been a canon of the church of Bonn; summoned by his holy sister to Schonau, he was first made a monk, and then abbot. He wrote little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I found the following:

*Against Heresies*, 1 book. “Prophesied long ago [unclear].”
*On the Beginning of the Gospel of John*, 1 book. “The mysteries of divine [unclear].”
*On the Magnificat*, 1 book. “The Spirit of the Lord upon you [unclear].”
*On ‘The Angel Was Sent’*, 1 book. “The Gospel sermon [unclear].”
*On the Death of His Sister*, 1 book. “To virgins consecrated to God.”
*Meditations*, 1 book. “I have a word for you.”
*Sermons through the Year*, 15 books. “There will be signs,” etc., “of Advent.”
*Praises of the Savior*, 1 book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book. “It happened in the beginning.”

He also published some other works which have not come to my knowledge. For he was an excellent, learned, and eloquent declaimer, so much so that by a single disputation he overcame a certain heretic whom no one was able to refute. He flourished in the aforesaid monastery of Saint Florinus, which is called Schonau, in the diocese of Trier, under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1160, and was held in esteem because of his religious life and learning.

HELIZABET, nun and abbess of the sisters’ monastery of Schonau, of the order of Saint Benedict (for there are two monasteries, one of monks and the other of nuns, situated at the end of the diocese of Trier not far from the Rhine in the Hercynian forest), sister of the aforesaid abbot Eckebertus, a virgin of most holy conduct, received many revelations from God, which she committed to writing for the edification of posterity. Of these I have read the following:

*The Ways of God*, a useful work, 1 book. “It happened in the beginning.”
*On the 11,000 Virgins of Cologne*, 15 books. “To us who [unclear] pious [unclear].”
*Of Various Revelations*, 1 book. “All who are going to read.”
*To Her Brother Eckebertus*, 15 books. “You ask of me, brother.”
*Letters to Various Persons*, 1 book. “From the bishopric of Metz [unclear].”
*To Saint Hildegard*, 15 books. And certain other works.

She died under Emperor Frederick I, in the year of the Lord 1165, in the thirteenth indiction, in the thirty-sixth year of her age.
