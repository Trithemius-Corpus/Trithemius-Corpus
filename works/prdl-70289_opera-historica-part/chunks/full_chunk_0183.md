*OTTO, duke of Bavaria.* This Otto was count in Scheyern, from the stock of the great duke Cuno, of whom mention was made above, from the line of Charlemagne the emperor. Frederick the emperor conferred the duchy of Bavaria upon him, as the lawful and nearest heir, after Henry had been removed; from him all the dukes of Bavaria and counts palatine of the Rhine who exist today descended, as we shall say in order in what follows.

Thus in the year of the Lord 1180 the aforesaid Otto, count of Scheyern, was made duke of Bavaria, a strong and just man, and most renowned in the Christian religion. He had a very devout wife named Agnes, by whom he begot Louis, his successor in the duchy of Bavaria, and Elizabeth, previously queen of the Jerusalemites.

In the year of the Lord 1124 Otto, count of Scheyern, afterward duke, made the castle of Scheyern a monastery in honor of great blessed Mary ever virgin. There were at that time many counts in Bavaria bearing the title “of Scheyern,” but none of them lived in that castle. Since it had nearly fallen into ruin through extreme age, Otto [unclear] summoned all those having a share in the place, namely Conrad, Count Otto, count of Dachau, Otto count palatine in Wittelsbach, another Otto of Scheyern, Count Arnold, with seven others; all of them unanimously offered to blessed Mary ever virgin, for the building of a monastery, the right which they had in the castle of Scheyern. And they chose that place as the perpetual burial-place of themselves and of their family. Count Arnold, however, offering his part not to God but to the Devil, threw his glove into the air and said: “You, Lord Devil, receive my part into your right.” Demons carried off the glove. The others, however, notwithstanding the treachery of one man, completed the work they had begun with a cheerful spirit.

In the year of the Lord 1181 Gebhard of Burghausen, duke in Bavaria, died, a vigorous and just man, and one who feared God. In the same year Conrad, duke of Bavaria, Dalmatia, and Croatia, died in Scheyern; by his magnificent deeds he won for himself eternal remembrance among posterity.

*HENRY,* son of Duke Henry of Saxony called the Lion, brother of Otto the fourth emperor and of William duke of Brunswick, having been made count palatine of the Rhine, took as wife Agnes, daughter of Conrad count palatine, of whom we shall speak later; Gertrude, whom the duke of Bavaria took as wife; and Adelaide, whom Eric count of Baden took. When Agnes, his first wife, had died, Henry took another Agnes, daughter of the most noble count of Landsberg, by whom he had no children.

In the year of the Lord 1198 Otto, most noble duke of Bavaria, died; he was buried in the monastery of Scheyern next to his brother Bernher, leaving a son named Louis.

*LOUIS, duke of Bavaria:* son of the above-mentioned Otto, he succeeded his father as prince after his death. He had as wife Ludmilla, daughter of the king of Bohemia, who bore him a son named Otto, duke of Bavaria and count palatine, of whom we shall speak later. This Ludmilla before Louis had married Albert, margrave of Bogen, to whom she bore two sons, namely Albert and Berthold, margraves after their father.

*LUDMILLA,* duchess of Bavaria, wife of Louis, built a monastery in Bavaria named Stedental, in which she lies buried.

*LOUIS,* duke of Bavaria, went to the Holy Land with Emperor Frederick the second, Pope Honorius the third, and Leopold duke of Austria. He was later killed through the plots of Emperor Frederick at Kelheim, in the year of the Lord 1231.

*OTTO,* duke of Bavaria, count palatine of the Rhine, son of the above-mentioned Louis duke of Bavaria, succeeding his father, had as wife Gertrude, daughter of Henry count palatine of the Rhine; she bore him two sons, Henry and Louis.

This Otto, son of Duke Louis, obtained the duchy of Bavaria after his father as lawful heir, and with his wife took possession of the Palatine county of the Rhine, Heidelberg, together with what belonged to it. She gave Braubach as a fief to the count of Katzenelnbogen.

Thus Otto, duke of Bavaria and count palatine of the Rhine, at the beginning of his rule was a pious, vigorous, and just prince; but afterward, because of the agreement which he had with Emperor Frederick and his son Conrad, he was excommunicated by Pope Innocent.
