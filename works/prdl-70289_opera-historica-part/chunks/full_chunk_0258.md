Since he was an outstanding lover of blessed Mary ever virgin, he wrote in her honor, in plain discourse, a very celebrated volume, taking the material of praise from each of her members under the figure of a cluster of grapes; he entitled it *On the Vineyard of the Lord Sabaoth*, one book. *The Exercise of Novices*, one book. *On the Preparation of the Priest for Mass*, one book. He delivered various addresses in our chapters, one of which begins: “The crown of our head has fallen”; another: “Catch us the little foxes”; a third: “Blessed is the servant,” etc. He also composed various sermons for the brothers, which are partly extant and partly have been lost through negligence. He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Innocent VIII, pope, in the year of the Lord 1486, in the fourth indiction, on the eighth day before the Kalends of January. He was buried in the right apse of the choir toward the south side, before the altar of the holy father Benedict.

Georgius Molitoris of Neuenburg, a man trained by long study in the divine Scriptures, excellently learned, and not moderately instructed in scholastic philosophy, which they call Aristotelian; subtle and acute in intellect, honorable in life and conduct. In the school of Erfurt he taught theological science gloriously for not a few years. He wrote *On the Sentences*, three books; *On the Passion of the Lord*, one book; also many sermons and various questions. He died under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, pope, in the year of the Lord 1490, in the second indiction.

Nicolaus of Wachenheim, doctor of Heidelberg, skilled in the holy Scriptures through long and sustained practice, an acute interpreter of Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, mature in life and conduct. In the school of Heidelberg, for almost fifty years, in teaching, lecturing, and writing, he was held in the highest esteem as a prince among theologians. He wrote works not to be despised in reading, by which he commended his memory to posterity; of these, however, very few have come to my knowledge. He flourished at Heidelberg under Frederick III, emperor. *Questions on the Sentences*, one book. Several sermons; speeches not useless to the clergy, [unclear]. He died at Heidelberg under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1480, a very old man, under Sixtus IV, pope; he died almost [seven years?] before this.

Heinrich, surnamed Prudent, a monk of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of the Valley near Bruges in Flanders, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished in intellect, devout in life and conduct, singularly devoted to God. For the instruction of religious he wrote several devout little works, by which he commended his name to posterity. Among these there is extant a very celebrated work, especially suited to devotion, which he entitled *Tetralogue of Devotion*, three books. He also wrote many devout letters to various persons, and many other things not yet seen by me. He died in the new house of Saint Sophia of Constantinople near ’s-Hertogenbosch, in which he held the office of prior for some years, under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1483, in the second indiction.

Rudolphus Agricola of Groningen, a Frisian by birth, a man the most learned of all in secular letters in his time, not ignorant of divine letters, a philosopher, rhetorician, and most celebrated poet, skilled in the three principal languages, Hebrew, Greek, and Latin; subtle in intellect, eloquent in speech; formerly the teacher of Johann Dalberg, bishop of Worms, a man equally most learned. In the school of Heidelberg, by teaching and writing, he easily held first place in Latin. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have found the following: a heroic poem *On Saint Anne*, one book; *Life of Francis the Patriarch*, one book; also a very subtle *Dialectic*, three books; also many letters to various persons, several poems and epigrams, and various treatises. He composed an epitaph of this kind: “Envious fates have shut beneath this marble Rudolph Agricola, the hope and glory of Frisian soil; in this one man, namely, Germany earned whatever praise Latium has, whatever Greece has.” He was buried at Heidelberg in the convent of the Friars Minor.
