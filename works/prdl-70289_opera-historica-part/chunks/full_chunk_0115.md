*Schuttern.* The monastery of Schuttern also, which stands three miles above Strasbourg, a city of Alsace, was founded by the same Saint Pirminius, as is found clearly written in the original volume of his Life, which we have in our possession. Nor is it an objection that the brothers of that same monastery, showing no documents of truth, say that Offo, king of England, when he was making for Rome out of devotion, in the times of Saint Gregory the First, pope, was moved by the pleasantness of the place and founded a monastery there; since King Offo and his brother, as we said above under King Hildebert, made for Rome and became monks in the year of the Lord 707, in the fifth Roman indiction. But Saint Pope Gregory, under whom they say that Offo, king of England, came to Rome, had died earlier, in the year of the Lord 605, in the eighth Roman indiction. Sigebert bears witness for our assertion in the Chronicles. *A false opinion is seriously refuted.*

The monastery of Schwarzach, situated in the diocese of Strasbourg, the same saint likewise founded, as the writer of his Life testifies in the original.

The monastery of Gengenbach in the diocese of Strasbourg, which is one mile distant from Schuttern, the same writer of the Life of Saint Pirminius testifies was founded by him.

The monastery which the Latins call Mauri monasterium, but in the vernacular Morsmunster, in the diocese of Strasbourg, the same saint founded.

The monastery of Neuwiller also, in the same diocese of Strasbourg, the same saint built; its monks, with their abbot, in our times withdrew from the order.

*There are two monasteries in Bavaria, Altach Upper and [unclear].* There are two monasteries of our order of one name in Bavaria, of which one is called Altach Upper, but [the other] Lower. In the Deeds of the Dukes of Bavaria we read that Duke Odilo, father of Duke Tassilo, founded Lower Altach. But in the deeds of Saint Pirminius [unclear] is expressed, nor Lower; therefore it is clear that Pirminius built Upper there. The monastery of the same Benedictine order, which is called Murbach, [he also built], as is contained in his Life.

The monastery also in the town of Amorbach, in the diocese of Wurzburg, of our aforementioned order, in which the saint appointed his disciple Amor as abbot, he built anew from the foundations. And many other things which we do not know.

In the fifth year of Theodoric, king of the Franks, Bede the Venerable died, priest and monk in England, a learned and holy man, who wrote many volumes for the benefit of readers, to the honor of the supreme and undivided Godhead. Theodoric, king of the Franks, died in the fifteenth year of his reign, but in the year of the Lord's nativity 741, in the ninth Roman indiction.

*The Sixty-First.*

Hilderic, son of King Theodoric, succeeding his father, reigned in name only for nine years. For all the power and wealth of the kingdom were held by the mayor of the palace, Charles, surnamed Martel, that is, the Hammer, to whom the sum of affairs belonged. Nor was anything else at all permitted to the king except that, content with the royal name, with flowing hair, [unclear], he should hear those coming [unclear] and give to them as they departed the answers which he had been taught or ordered, as if from his own power; he possessed nothing else except what the mayor of the palace furnished at his own discretion.

In the same year Charles Martel died, mayor of the palace of the Franks, duke of Brabant and count of Namur; since he was very warlike and fortunate in all things, he greatly enlarged the borders of the kingdom. He conquered in war and overcame with the sword the Saxons, Bavarians, Burgundians, Sorbs, the Bulgarians dwelling beyond the Danube, also the Gascons, Vandals, Goths and Saracens in Spain, the Frisians, and many other nations round about.
