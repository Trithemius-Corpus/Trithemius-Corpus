*Johannes Trithemius*

...while they were attacking fiercely, the Bavarians again make ready for battle, and, when the army of the emperor Louis comes up, they kill many of the Hungarians and turn the rest to flight. Conrad, duke of Lotharingia, brother of this Louis and son of Arnolph, a Bavarian by birth, was killed by the treachery of Adalbert, margrave of Bamberg.

Louis III, emperor of the Romans, son of the Bavarian emperor Arnolph, dies without children in the year of the Lord 813; after him Conrad, the son of his brother, is elected.

Conrad the emperor, born of a Bavarian father, the duke: when the emperor Louis, son of Arnolph, had died, Conrad, son of his brother Conrad, duke of Lotharingia, whom Adalbert, margrave of Bamberg, had killed, from the line of Charlemagne, was made the last emperor in the empire. He did not have a wife. Eberhard, of whom we shall speak later, [unclear].

Arnolph, duke of Bavaria, brother of Conrad, father of this Conrad, duke of the Romans, that is, son of the emperor Arnolph, opposed this emperor Conrad; defeated at length by him, he fled with his wife and children into Hungary, until the death of the aforesaid Conrad.

King Conrad, after Duke Arnolph had been driven out of Bavaria, planned in like manner to attack Henry, duke of the Saxons, and so he besieged Merseburg. There on the king’s side fell Eberhard his brother and Hatto, archbishop of Mainz, and he himself scarcely escaped. Thereafter Henry, duke of the Saxons, devastated all the king’s land. When King Conrad was about to die, he sent the crown of the kingdom to the above-mentioned Henry, duke of the Saxons, who reigned after him; and he died in Eastern Francia, buried at Würzburg.

Arnolph, duke of Bavaria, having heard of the death of King Conrad, returns from Hungary to Bavaria and is received by all with great honor, all asking that he deign to take up the kingdom of the Romans. Since Henry, duke of the Saxons, was succeeding in the kingdom, and all the princes except the aforesaid Arnolph, duke of Bavaria, obeyed him, Henry gathered his army and advanced against him in war, and besieged Regensburg. And Duke Arnolph, seeing that he could not resist so great a multitude, opened the gates, sought favor, and obtained it. Accordingly a very firm peace is made between them under these conditions: that the king confer upon Duke Arnolph the full fief or lordship over all the bishoprics in Bavaria, leave him to possess his duchy peacefully, and hold him chief among his friends; and so it was done. And so Henry remained in the kingdom. When he died, Otto I, his son, succeeded; after him Otto II, son of the first; then Otto III, son of the second, who died without children. And Henry, duke of Bavaria, succeeded the aforesaid in the empire by election, as we shall say later in its place.

Under Otto I the emperor there was a very grave war between the Christians and the Hungarians. At that time they were still unbelievers at Augsburg, where the emperor himself triumphed with the Christians. There fell Conrad, duke of Bavaria, and Count Theobald, brother of Saint Ulrich, bishop of Augsburg.

The cause of the war [unclear] just: Count Bernher was claiming the duchy of Bavaria as the legitimate heir; the sons of his brother, one of whom was emperor, opposed him. Moved by this, he summoned the Hungarians, asking for aid. After Count Bernher of Scheyern was killed by the Hungarians, Eberhard and Bernher, his sons, succeeding in the place of their father, were claiming the duchy of Bavaria, which Eberhard at last [unclear], as we shall say.

Arnolph, duke of the Noricans, dies, leaving, among other children, Duke Rudolph after him, and a daughter named Lucarda, whom Henry, duke of Saxony and later also of Bavaria, son of Henry I the emperor and brother of Otto I, took as wife, as we shall say later.

Rudolph, duke of Bavaria after the death of the aforesaid Henry I, rebels against Emperor Otto, his son; hence from duke of Bavaria he was made count in Scheyern.
