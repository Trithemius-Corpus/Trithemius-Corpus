Franciscus Diedus, a noble Venetian patrician, a man excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, a philosopher and rhetorician, distinguished for learning and eloquence, a very celebrated interpreter of pontifical and civil law, and an indefatigable lover and guardian of the Venetian republic, wrote certain excellent little works both in verse and prose, by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these the following are reported: The History of Saint Roch the Confessor, one book; Orations and various letters. And certain other things. He died at Verona under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, in the year of the Lord 1483, in the first indiction.

Rudolphus Agricola of Groningen, by nation a Frisian, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, rhetorician, and very celebrated poet, skilled in Greek and Latin speech, and not ignorant of the Hebrew language, excellent in ability, eloquent in speech, formerly teacher of Johannes Camerarius of Dalberg, bishop of Worms and a most learned man, easily obtained the first place among all the doctors, even of whatever faculty, by teaching and writing in the Heidelberg gymnasium. He wrote certain most excellent little works of his genius, by which he consecrated his name to immortality and left it to posterity.
