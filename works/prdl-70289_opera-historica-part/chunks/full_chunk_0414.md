*The Abbot of Spanheim*

of Mount Carmel, a man in every respect most learned, an eminent professor of sacred Scripture, of keen and vigorous intellect, quite suited to resolving questions of the Scriptures. He is said to have written some excellent little works, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is found an outstanding work, as his own report:

On the Sentences, 4 books. And various questions.

But he is also said to have composed certain other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Rupertus, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures by long practice, and a subtle and profound interpreter of scholastic philosophy, an acute disputant and outstanding declaimer, keen in intellect, scholastic in speech. He published certain works not to be despised, by which, exercising his talent fruitfully and usefully, he made his name known not only to those then living but also to those to come. Of these I have seen only the necessary one:

On the Sentences, 4 books. Sermons on the season, 1 book.

Sermons on the saints, 1 book.

Moreover, he adorned many books of Aristotle with very elegant commentaries. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Astesanus of Asti, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man nobly learned in divine writings, a philosopher and distinguished jurist, renowned in knowledge, profound in intellect, cautious in counsel, firm in judgment, composed in speech. He is said to have fashioned various little works of his excellent talent, by which he made his name known both to those then living and to those to come. Of these I have seen only the necessary one:

A Summa on cases, 8 books. “Since, therefore, we are about to speak in this first...”

Concerning the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1330.

Johannes de Alerio, by nation a Toulousain, of the Order of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, the thirteenth prior general, a man studious and learned in the divine writings, and venerable no less for the fervor of religion than for learning, governed the Carmelite order energetically for nine years. He published several excellent volumes, in which he showed the abundant vein of his talent. Of these there survive:

On the Sentences, [4?] books. A Reading on Ecclesiasticus, [1?] book.

And many others. He was elected general of his order at Montpellier, in the general chapter, on the day of Pentecost, under Louis IV, emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1321, indiction 4.

Johannes de Regno, by nation a Frenchman, of the Order of the Friars of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, from the convent of Toulouse, a man learned in the sacred Scriptures and not ignorant of secular learning, ready in intellect, scholastic in speech, outstanding in delivering sermons to the people. He published many excellent volumes, by which he made his name known even to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Sentences, 1 book. Sermons on the season, 1 book.

On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book. Lenten sermons, 1 book.
