320 *The Abbot of Sponheim*

Simon of Cascia, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, and venerable no less for holiness of life than for knowledge. Indeed, he is said to have been illumined by the spirit of prophecy in the Florentine convent. He published excellent volumes of great usefulness, in which he shows himself a learned and devout man. Among these I have found a notable work on the whole Gospel, which is commonly entitled:

The Deeds of the Savior, 15 books. “From the good, concerning the good.”
On Christian Doctrine, 1 book. On Patience, 1 book.
An Exposition of the Creed, 1 book. On the Mirror of the Cross, 1 book.
On the Discipline of Spiritual Persons, 1 book. And certain others.
Letters to Various Persons, 1 book. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.
On the Sentences, 4 books. Also Against the Jews, 1 book.
A Postil read at Cologne, 1 book.
In addition he published several other works, but they have by no means come to our knowledge.

Sertorius, whom some call Fortanerius, of the Order of Friars Minor, the nineteenth minister general, English by nation, Welsh by homeland, afterward Archbishop of Ravenna, and finally Patriarch of Grado, cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, a man [unclear] in divine Scripture, won from all men great praise for learning. He wrote a notable volume:

On Augustine’s City of God, 22 books. I have found nothing about the rest.

Finally, he is also said to have composed various treatises on the books of the Bible. He flourished when he was elected minister general of his order in the chapter at Marseilles, under Emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1356, indiction 9.

Albericus of Rosate, from Bergamo by homeland, a distinguished professor of both laws, a most renowned interpreter, excellently learned also in the other secular letters, ready in intellect and polished in speech. He published many well-ordered volumes on the laws, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the Code, 12 books. On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books.
On the Institutes, 1 book. On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, 1 book.
On Dante’s Comedy, 1 book.

He also composed several other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

John of Saxony, of the Order of Friars Minor, German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, sufficiently learned both in canon law and in scholastic philosophy, of excellent reputation in delivering sermons to the people, published certain excellent volumes of his talent, by which he made his name known also to posterity. Of these there is extant:

A Summa on Cases, 1 book. And other works which I have not seen.

He flourished under Emperor Louis IV, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Gerard of Siena, Italian by nation, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man not moderately learned in the holy Scriptures, skilled through long practice in both laws, subtle and sharp in intellect; John Andrea, his contemporary, often commends and cites his learning. He wrote several excellent little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported as the more important:

On the First Book of the Sentences, 1 book. On Contracts of Usury, 1 book.
On Prescriptions, 1 book. I have found nothing about the rest.

He flourished with John Andrea, under Louis the Bavarian, Emperor IV, and Pope Benedict XII, in the year of the Lord 1340.
