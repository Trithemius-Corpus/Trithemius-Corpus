*On the Origin of the Franks*

But the holy abbot Columbanus warned Lotharius not to pollute himself with the murder of his nephews, for in the end it would come to pass that he would become their heir and monarch of the whole kingdom.

But Brunhild, the grandmother, was inciting those same grandsons without interruption into mutual war. The grandmother pursued Theodobert with particular hatred, and for that reason diligently provoked his brother against him. Hence at last Theodoric moved an army against his brother Theodobert, and a fierce battle was joined, in which many thousands of the Franks fell on both sides. At length Theodobert, having been defeated, fled and withdrew within the walls of the city of Cologne Agrippina. His brother pursued him and surrounded Cologne with a siege. The citizens asked Theodoric for peace; he answered them: “You will have no peace from me unless you throw down to me from the wall the head of my brother Theodobert.” The citizens therefore cut off the head of Theodobert, their lord, and threw it through the wall to Theodoric his brother, as he had ordered. Thus, made victor in his brother’s blood, he entered Cologne peacefully, an impious warrior, and subjected to himself all Austrasia, which his brother had possessed.

Theodobert had three sons, Sigebert, Gunthram, and Lotharius, and one very beautiful daughter. Sigebert, the eldest, escaped by flight and lay hidden very secretly until the death of Theodoric with his uncle Genebald, duke of the eastern Franks; after the death of Theodoric, through the intervention of the nobles, he received the kingdom of the Helvetians, as will be said below hereafter, yet he was not permitted to put on the diadem or to possess royal dignity. From this Sigebert was born Odobert, through whom the counts in Habsburg, as we said a little earlier, together with many others, arose in Upper Alemannia of Helvetia.

Theodoric the king, their uncle, led away the two brothers and the sister of the aforesaid Sigebert, who were still little children, to Metz. Brunhild, meeting him, as soon as she saw the little grandchildren, rushed upon them like a raging lioness and cruelly killed them with her own hand. Thus Theodoric obtained Austrasia.

Not much time elapsed after this, namely in the year of the Lord 618, which was the thirty-second of King Lotharius, and King Theodoric perished by Brunhild’s poison, in the twenty-first year of his reign. After his death, Lotharius, according to the prophecy of the holy abbot Columbanus, was made monarch of the whole kingdom and thereafter reigned alone over the Franks and Gauls for thirteen years. He insisted that Brunhild, the murderess of ten kings, captured at Worms, undergo cruel [unclear] by judgment of the nobles.

Lotharius, therefore, now entrusted the provinces of the kingdom to four counts. He appointed Garnerius, a noble man of the ancient stock of the kings of the Franks, prefect of his palace; from this his descendants were called counts palatine of his house, succeeding afterward in the same office. Burgundy he entrusted to a certain Harpo, who was himself a count sprung from the most ancient line of the kings of the Franks, and who performed many brave deeds. To Rado, a most noble count of the Franks, he entrusted Austrasia, that is, Metz, Liège, Cologne, Trier, and whatever lies between them. Mainz, Worms, Speyer, and all Westria, together with the adjoining regions, he entrusted to Clodomir, who was duke of the eastern Franks at Würzburg, a high-spirited man descended from the lineage of kings. Lower Francia, or northern Gaul, he entrusted to Pippin; in it are these more important cities: Thérouanne, the Morini, Tournai, Arras, Cambrai, Maastricht, and whatever lies between them. Alsace and Alemannia on this side of the Rhine, that is, Constance, Basel, Strasbourg, whatever right the Franks had between the Burgundians, the Rhine, and the Lombards, and the Helvetians themselves, he committed to Sigebert, son of the late Theodobert, king of the Austrasians, whom his brother Theodoric killed, as has been said. This Sigebert, as long as his uncle survived after the killing of his father, brothers, and sister, together with Brunhild, lay hidden among the dukes of eastern Francia.

By Lotharius, however, through the intervention of the nobles, he was made count in the aforesaid regions, and he begot Odobert, Gunthram, and Theodobert by Engeltrude, daughter of Heribert, duke of the eastern Franks. He presided over the province committed to him for thirty-eight years. Odobert, after his father died, received four counties in the land of the Suebi, Habsburg, Kyburg, and two others whose names are not now [unclear] to me.

Gunthram was count in Homburg and begot Udalric, father of Saint Othilia, virgin of Christ and nun. Theodobert was count of the Rauraci. From these many counts descend.
