Rodericus, bishop of Zamora, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular letters, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, wrote some little works of no contemptible authority, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have so far seen only one, which he entitled:

*The Mirror of Human Life*, two books: “Above all human...”

The rest I have not seen. He flourished in the times of Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Ambrosius Choriolanus, of the Order of the Hermit Friars of St Augustine, prior general, Roman by country, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and distinguished in knowledge of secular letters, excellent in talent, fluent in eloquence, learned and eloquent, wrote certain notable little works, by which he commended his name to immortality. Of these, however, only a few have come to my knowledge by reading; they follow below:

On the Rule of St Augustine, one book: “From the learned parent of all...”
A Commendation of the Same Rule, one book: “After blessed Augustine...”
On the Second Rule, one book: “Keen in talent, foremost in life...”
On the Life of St Augustine, one book: “Very often, when near...”
On the Praises of the Same, one book: “Most blessed father, they have been accustomed...”
Various Orations, one book. He wrote also many other things.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Benedictus Stendel of Halle, a town of Saxony, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in talent and clear in speech, taught theology gloriously for many years in the school of Erfurt, and shone forth brightly, renowned both in life and in doctrine. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, from which he regularly lectured in the said university on the following:

On Genesis, one book.
On Exodus, one book.
On Leviticus, one book.
On Numbers, one book.
On Deuteronomy, one book.
Various Sermons, one book.
Different Collations, one book.

And certain other things in logic, philosophy, and the other scholastic studies and exercises. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1490.

Sifridus, titled bishop of Cyrene, of the Order of Preachers, German by nation, vicar in pontifical matters of Theodericus, archbishop of Mainz, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin languages, subtle in talent and clear in speech, an acute disputant and an outstanding preacher of the divine word, ready in counsel, distinguished in life, wrote certain little works worthy of reading, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are extant, addressed to the aforesaid Archbishop Theodericus:

On the Usury of the Jews, one book: “It is asked in the forum of conscience...”
On Rents and Redemption, one book: “It was asked first whether...”
Sermons and Collations, one book.
Various Questions, one book.

And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Frederick and the Roman pontiffs Pius and Paul, in the year of the Lord 1476.

Godescalcus of Meschede, German by nation, Westphalian by country, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, keen in talent, distinguished in life, a profound doctor of most sacred theology, who for many years taught that science gloriously in the school of Erfurt and deserved great praise from all for his learning, wrote and lectured in the same university on certain notable little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, four books.
Sermons and Collations, one book.
Various Disputed Questions, one book.
And many other things.

His brother Theodericus Gresmunt of Meschede, doctor of arts and medicine, a man most learned in every respect, still living, resides at Mainz; he too, among other things, composed an elegant treatise, *On the Regimen of Health in Time of Pestilence*. He has a son called by the name Theodericus, scarcely completing three lustra of age, whose admirable talent, and singular learning as well as eloquence, far surpass many of advanced age, even doctors.
