232 *The Abbot of Sponheim* was most learned in the divine Scriptures, and trained in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, eloquent in speech, deep in understanding, subtle in intellect, vigorous in assertion, and most holy in life and conduct. He was once notary of blessed Pope Leo the Great, and composed many letters in his person. He also wrote many distinguished little works from his own vein. Of these I have found only the following.

From the Sentences of blessed Augustine the bishop, in hexameter and pentameter verses, mixed with prose, a very beautiful work which he wished to entitle:

Epigrams, one book. “While it pleases [unclear].”
On the Contemplative Life, three books. “The contemplative life in …”
On Free Choice, one book. “To the lord brother, most beloved, Ru…”
Responses to the Chapters of the Gauls, one book. “The doctrine which holy …”
To the Objections of the Vincentians, one book. “Certain Christian and …”
To the Excerpts of the Genoese, one book. “In the books of blessed memory …”
A large Sum of 330 propositions, one book. “True knowledge is that which …”
Against Cassian on Free Choice, one book.
A Chronicle of Histories, one book.
Letters to Various Persons, one book.
On Illustrious Men, one book.
On the Captivity of Rome, one book.

He is said besides to have written some other works, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished in the time of the great emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460. His feast is celebrated on the seventh day before the Kalends of July.

VICTOR, bishop of the Mauretanian city of Cartenna, learned in the holy Scriptures and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, a notable persecutor of heretics, is said to have composed many volumes, in which he both instructed the devout people and vigorously crushed those erring about the faith. Of these, the following are reported:

Against the Arians, one book.
Against the Donatists, one book.
A Consolatory Work to Basil, one book.
On Penance also, one book.
Homilies to the People, one book.

He also published not a few other works, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Marcian Augustus, in the year of the Lord 450.

VICTORINUS, an Aquitanian by nation, a man very studious in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, is said to have left certain little works, which have not yet come to my knowledge at all. At the command of Pope Hilary he wrote, for finding the date of Easter:

The Great Cycle of 532 Years, one book.

This is called the great cycle of 532 years; when they have elapsed, all the calculations of the luminaries return to the same point. Dionysius the abbot later corrected it, and it has endured down to our own age, although in some points it has failed. That Victorinus also wrote certain letters. He flourished under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460, and wrote his cycle under Pope Hilary, in the third year of his pontificate.

PETER, priest of the church of Edessa, learned and practiced in the holy Scriptures, obtained an altogether excellent power and talent for declaiming homilies to the people, so much so that in his times he had no equal. He wrote many distinguished volumes in the Syrian language. Of these, only a very few titles have come to my knowledge:

Commentaries on the Psalms, one book.
He also played in meter on the Psalms, one book.

He composed and delivered almost infinite homilies to the people, which are rarely found among us. Nor is the number of his treatises certain to us. He flourished under the emperor Marcian, in the year of the Lord 459.

SABATIUS, bishop of a Gallic province, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and sufficiently trained in secular letters, subtle and keen in intellect, often fought against the poison of heretics, both by writing and by disputing. Hence, asked by a certain Secunda, a virgin dedicated to Christ, he wrote a notable and memorable volume:

Against Marcion and Valentinus, one book.
Against Eunomius and Aetius, one book.

He also published some other works, but they have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, around the year of the Lord 440.
