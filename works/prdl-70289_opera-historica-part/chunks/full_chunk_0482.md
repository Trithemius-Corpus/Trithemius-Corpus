He has published very many and diverse epigrams, which we hope are to be made public daily. He also translated from Greek into Latin several sermons of blessed John Chrysostom, and many other things unknown to us. He is still living at Ferraria and is writing various works under Maximilian, most merciful king of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1494.

Petrus Brutus, a Venetian by nation, bishop of Cathara, a man certainly most learned in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular letters, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, skilled in the Hebrew and Latin languages, who for the confirmation of the Christian faith carried through many contests of disputation against the faithless Jews, always as victor. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote, to the senate of Vicenza, a very celebrated and notable work, abundantly furnished with the authorities of the holy Scriptures.

Against the Jews, one book, beginning: “As I, reading many [unclear].”

And certain other works. He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1485. And I do not know whether he is still living.

Hubertinus, cleric of Crescentino, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a philosopher, rhetorician, and distinguished poet, and most skilled in both languages, outstanding in talent and eloquent in speech, who in the very celebrated academy of Pavia for seven years read publicly, with the attention and profit of his hearers, on a stipend. He wrote many distinguished little works, both in verse and in prose, of which the following are extant.

On the Letters of Cicero, sixteen books, beginning: “The letters of this volume...” On the Offices of the same, three books. On the Metamorphoses of Naso, one book. Also one book of letters. Many poems, one book.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1480. And I do not know whether he is still living.

Guilhelmus of Aachen, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, scholastic in eloquence, distinguished in life and conduct, who formerly held the office of preacher in the church of Basel. He wrote many commendable little works, by which he commends his memory to posterity. Of these he read certain works publicly in the academy of Erfurt, with the attention and praise of all. He wrote:

On the Gospel of John, one book. On the Canon of the Mass, one book. On the Passion of the Lord, one book, beginning: “Jesus handed over willingly...” Disputed Questions, one book. Itinerary of the Holy Land, one book. Various sermons, one book. And several other works.

He flourished under Frederick III, emperor, and Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff, in the year of the Lord [1490?] 1485. And I believe that he is still living.

Johannes de Lapide, a German by nation, a monk of the Carthusian order of the house of Basel, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, distinguished in life and conduct. Formerly, having obtained the mastership in the faculty of arts in the academy of Paris, he ruled in the same for several years, and, diligently pursuing the study of sacred letters by teaching, reading, and disputing, he deserved to be adorned with the doctoral dignity in the school of theologians. He first brought to the university of Basel the doctrine of those Parisians who are called Realists, and there planted, strengthened, and enlarged it. He was also one of the chief initiators and authors of the study at Tübingen. Finally, after freely resigning several benefices, he was called to a canonry and to the office of preaching in the notable church of Basel, and at last, after a few years had passed, leaving everything for the love of Christ, he put on the habit of the holy Carthusian order. He wrote many distinguished little works, by which he made himself useful to the present and memorable to the future. A copy of only a few of these has come to us.
