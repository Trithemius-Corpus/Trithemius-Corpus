Ludovicus Pontanus of Rome, an Italian by nation, a most distinguished jurist, and no less zealous in the sacred Scriptures than in secular philosophy, excellent in talent, concise and weighty in judgment, published volumes not to be despised for those wishing to make progress in the faculty of law, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number I [have seen] only the work entitled:

Notable singular points, one book: “A word is wholly understood.”

Johannes de Ragulio, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, published certain excellent little works of his talent, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these I have read a work addressed to the fathers of the Council of Basel:

Against the Hussites, one book: “Most reverend, venerable...”

He flourished in person at the Council of Basel, where he publicly pronounced the said treatise against the heretics before the fathers, under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1438.

Blondus Flavius of Forli, secretary of Pope Eugene IV, a man skilled in every discipline of the good arts, a most celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, and a distinguished historiographer, excellent in talent, eloquent in speech, and second to none in grace of speaking, wrote many excellent volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Heymericus de Campo, a German by nation, vice-chancellor of the University of Cologne, a man learned in the divine writings and second to none in his time in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in talent and excellent in disputing difficult questions, wrote many excellent little works, of which, however, few have so far come into our hands. He was present in person at the Council of Basel, sent by the University of Cologne, where at the urging of master Nicolaus of Cusa, later cardinal, he published:
