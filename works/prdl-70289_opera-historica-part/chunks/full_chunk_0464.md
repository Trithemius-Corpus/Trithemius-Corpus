*The Abbot of Sponheim*

On the Sentences, 4 books. On the Psalter, 1 book. Various Questions, 1 book. Sermons and Conferences, 1 book. And many other things. He flourished in the time of Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Dionysius Rykel, otherwise de Leeuwis, German by nation, of the Carthusian order, of the house of Bethlehem in Roermond, a man most devoted to the divine Scriptures and skilled through continual practice, not ignorant also of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in discourse, and singularly devout in life and conduct, so much so that he was even considered worthy of divine revelations; to him there are extant several letters of Nicholas of Cusa, a most learned cardinal. He wrote so many and such great works that, in the number of his treatises, among the Latins he had no equal except Augustine. He applied himself to contemplation and prayer with such fervor that you would have thought he could never have written anything. Again, in writing and reading he was so diligent that you would have judged that he could nowhere pray or contemplate. He slept very little, was admirable in abstinence from food and drink, and, after the example of blessed Jerome, judged it sweet to meditate day and night on the law of the Lord, always either writing something useful or reading, so that prayer often interrupted reading, and sacred reading frequently followed prayer.
