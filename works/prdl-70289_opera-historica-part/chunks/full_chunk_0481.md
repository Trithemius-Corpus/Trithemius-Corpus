Marsilius Ficinus, an Italian by nation, a Florentine by native land, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very celebrated philosopher and rhetorician, highly skilled in both languages, the unique glory, ornament, and crown of Platonic theology, wrote many distinguished little works in prose, in which he showed to posterity the vein of his excellent talent. Of these the following are reported:

A Notable Theology, one book, beginning [unclear].
On Intellect and Intelligence, one book.
On Plato’s Symposium, one book, beginning “Plato, chief of philosophers...”
Letters, several books.

And many others. From Greek, moreover, he translated many things into Latin. For Pietro de’ Medici of Florence he translated fifty-six treatises of Plato. Also Hermes Trismegistus, On the Power and Wisdom of God. The book of Xenocrates the Platonist On Death, for the same Pietro de’ Medici. For Giovanni Cavalcanti he translated the little works of Speusippus, Alcinous, and Pythagoras. For the same man, the book of Alcinous the Platonist On the Doctrine of Plato. In addition he translated and wrote very many other things which have by no means come to our knowledge through reading. He flourished at Florence under Emperor Frederick III and under Sixtus IV and Innocent VIII, Roman pontiffs, in the year of the Lord 1486. And I believe him still to be among the living.

Rupertus of Liccio, bishop of Aquila, a city of the Marsi or Abruzzi, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, excellent in talent, sweet and orderly in speech, distinguished in life and conduct, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons and famous throughout the whole Christian world, who by word and example converted many from iniquity, wrote for the edification of readers, and especially of preachers of the divine word, certain distinguished volumes, of which the following are reported:

Sermons on the Season, one book, beginning “The night has passed,” etc., words.
On the Praises of the Saints, one book, beginning “Let not the wise man glory in...”
On Penance through Lent, one book, beginning “There are many...” etc., “on account of...”
On Sin also, one book.
And certain others.

He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent, in the year of the Lord 1486. I do not know whether he is still alive.

Johannes Pfeffer of Widenberg, a German by nation, an East Franconian by native land, a man learned by long study in the divine Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in talent, devout, gentle, and distinguished in life and conduct, who, sent from the University of Heidelberg, adorned the beginning of the Freiburg academy by his learning, where now for very many years he has gloriously taught theology and has properly instructed many of his hearers, wrote certain distinguished little works, of which there is extant a notable work on the epistles of Paul, which he marked out after it had been read publicly:

Priestly Directory, one book, beginning “Concerning the reading of the epistles...”

And many others. He was still flourishing among the living under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1490. He may still be alive.

Johannes Britannicus, born at Palazzolo, a town in the territory of Brescia, a man most learned in secular letters, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a famous philosopher, distinguished poet, and clear rhetorician, who among his own Brescian people is today held in esteem, and whom he renders daily more illustrious by inestimable learning, doctrine, and writings, wrote many and various works, by which he made his name known to posterity and venerable to those now living. Of these the following are reported: a very celebrated work of commentaries:

On Persius, one book. On Statius also, one book.
On Ovid’s Metamorphoses, one book. Various letters also.

And many others. He flourishes in the city of Brescia and composes various little works under Maximilian, king of the Romans, and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493.
