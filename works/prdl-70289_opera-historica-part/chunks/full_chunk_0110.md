*Compendium, Book I*

*The monasteries of Celles, Hautmont, Soignies, Maubeuge, and Hasbania are founded. St. Gertrude dies in the year 663. Lothar, king of the Franks, dies in the year 666. Theodoric reigned 2 years. Ebroin, mayor of the palace, a perverse man of the same name. Theodoric is deposed from the kingship because he was most insolent. Hilderic reigned 12 years. Wolfrad, mayor of the palace of the Franks. St. Leodegar, bishop. King Hilderic is killed with his wife. Theodoric reigns a second time for 49 years. Leudesius, mayor of the palace in Neustria. Ebroin declares war on Theodoric. Clovis is set up as king by Ebroin. St. Leodegar is captured. Ebroin is received back into favor by Theodoric. St. Lambert is driven out by Ebroin.*

The monastery of Celles, over which Saint Gislenus presided as abbot. The monastery of Hautmont, over which Saint Madelgarius presided, also called Vincentius, a monk and abbot after having been a duke. The same Madelgarius founded the monastery of Soignies. The same man also built the monastery of Maubeuge. Saint Trudo founded the monastery of Hasbania, where he was also buried.

In the second year of the reign of Lothar, king of the Franks, Saint Gertrude died, abbess of the monastery of Nivelles in Brabant, of the order of Saint Benedict, daughter of the elder Pippin, mayor of the palace of the kingdom of the Franks throughout Austrasia.

But the aforesaid Lothar, king of the Franks, died in the fourth year of his reign, which was the six hundred and sixth year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the ninth indiction.

*The Fifty-Fourth*

Theodoric, brother of Lothar and son of the late King Clovis, after Lothar’s death reigned over the Franks scarcely one year. Because of the insolence of Ebroin, mayor of the palace, he was cast down from the kingship by sentence of the nobles, was shorn, and was sent to the monastery of Saint Dionysius at Paris, and was compelled to live as a monk among the monks. But Ebroin, shorn by decree of the magnates, was thrust into the monastery of Luxeuil.

Theodoric was deposed from the kingdom of the Franks in the first year of his reign, which was 667 from the Lord’s Nativity, in the tenth indiction of the Romans; and his brother Hilderic, who was reigning in Austrasia, was chosen by common consent.

*The Fifty-Fifth*

Hilderic, brother of the two preceding kings, who was reigning in Austrasia, after the deposition of Theodoric was, by the common election of the nobles, taken up as king and monarch of the whole kingdom of the Franks. He reigned twelve years. His mayor of the palace was Wolfrad, a noble man, powerful in deed and speech.

In the ninth year of his reign, King Hilderic, by the levity of his own conduct, kindled against himself the hatred of many nobles. Saint Leodegar, from a monk of our order made bishop of Autun, was driven from his see and thrust into Luxeuil.

In the twelfth year of his reign, moreover, and the 679th year from the Lord’s Nativity, in the seventh indiction, a certain Frank named Bodilo, whom King Hilderic himself had ordered to be bound to a post and flogged, killed him while he was hunting, together with his pregnant wife Blithild, by a cruel blow.

After the killing of King Hilderic, his brother Theodoric was called out from the monastery of the holy martyr Dionysius and restored to the kingdom of the Franks; and thereafter he reigned a second time for fourteen years. His mayor of the palace was Leudesius, son of Erchenwald, appointed for one year by the counsel of Saint Leodegar the bishop, then recalled from the monastery of Luxeuil, and of the other princes.

For in the following year Ebroin came out from the aforementioned monastery, recovered his strength, and harried his ambushers with hostile pursuit. He rushed upon King Theodoric, plundered his treasures and those of the church, killed Leudesius, the mayor of the palace, in battle, put the king himself to flight, and, pretending that a certain Clovis was the king’s son, made him king for himself. By threats and blandishments he induced to obedience to him whomever he could, and with many disturbances he troubled the kingdom of the Franks.

In the third year of Theodoric’s reign, the holy bishop Leodegar was captured by Ebroin and was gravely afflicted together with his brother Gerinus. Gerinus, however, not long afterward, by order of that tyrant, was buried under stones and killed.

In the sixth year of his reign, Theodoric received Ebroin back into favor, and at his persuasion summoned a synod of bishops. In it, at Ebroin’s sentence, he deprived many of them of their episcopal office, while others he condemned to perpetual exile. Among them Saint Lambert, son of Count Aper, removed from his own bishopric of Maastricht, entered at the monastery of Stavelot into the harbor of monastic quiet, and remained in it for seven years.

In the same year Saint Leodegar, bishop of Autun, long tortured by Ebroin, after hunger and bodily filth, after the tearing out of his eyes, after the cutting of the soles of his feet, after the cutting out of his tongue and lips, completed his martyrdom, at the tyrant’s command, by the cutting off of his head. Many miracles proved his innocence and merit.

In the eighth year of Theodoric, after the death of Wolfrad, mayor of the palace of the Franks, Pippin, son of Ansegis, together with Duke Martin, held the chief power in Austrasia. Ebroin, engaging with them, killed Martin.
