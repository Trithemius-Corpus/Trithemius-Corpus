*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

...a German by nation, a man zealous and learned in divine studies, rich in his reading of the ancients, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote certain small works of no contemptible usefulness, by which he transmitted the memory of his talent to posterity. Of these the following are reported: a work not to be rejected, in praise of the most blessed mother of God and virgin Mary, useful to preachers, which he entitled:

The Image of Saint Mary, 3 books. The name of the Virgin Mary.
Various Sermons, 1 book.
The rest, although many works are reported which he composed, have by no means come down to us.

BARTHOLOMAEUS of Urbino, of the order of the hermit brothers of Saint Augustine, a man zealous in divine writings, not ignorant of secular letters, excellent in talent, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, is said to have composed many distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity; of these he brought together [unclear] little works into one alphabetical order, to which he gave the title:

Milleloquium of Augustine, 1 book. Milleloquium of Ambrose, 1 book.
Also On Spiritual Warfare, 1 book. And very many others.
He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and Pope Gregory XII, in the year of the Lord 1410.

FRANCISCUS de Plataea, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, a man learned in the divine scriptures, instructed both in law and in secular philosophy, excellent in talent, an outstanding declaimer of sermons, is said to have composed many distinguished little works, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to posterity; of which [unclear] he brought together into one alphabetical order, to which he gave the title:

A distinguished and excellent work, which he entitled:
On Restitutions, 1 book. Whether restitution is to be made concerning the man.
On [censuitis?], 1 book. Various Sermons, 1 book.
The other works, however, which he composed have not come to the knowledge of our reading.

Petrus de Candia, a Cretan by nation, of the order of the Friars Minor, first bishop of Novara, then archbishop of Milan, afterwards cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, finally supreme pontiff named Alexander V, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, magnificently instructed in scholastic philosophy, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, who once presided over a public school in the university of Paris, acquired great and immense praises by teaching and writing. Among his most elegant little works there survive clear and useful commentaries:

On the Sentences, 1 book. Also On the Sentences, 1 book.
And certain other works. He died in the last year of Rupert, king of the Romans, and was buried in the convent of the Minorites at Bologna, in the year of the Lord 1410, indiction 3.

FRANCISCUS de Bachone, a Catalan by country, of the order of the brothers of the blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Girona, a man learned in the divine scriptures, nobly instructed in secular philosophy, had such power in teaching and disputing in the university of Paris that he was surnamed the Sublime Doctor. Among other monuments of his talent he wrote a great and distinguished work from the writings of the holy fathers, sufficiently useful for declaimers of sermons to the people, which we can title:

Repertory of Preachers, 1 book. Also Repertory of Preachers, 1 book.
He is said besides to have written certain other works, but they have not come to our hands.

Michael Herbrant of Duren, of the order of the brothers of the blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a Teuton by nation, lector of sacred theology, prior of the convent of Creuznach in the diocese of Mainz, a learned man and so distinguished a declaimer that he was summoned not only nearby but also to distant places and cathedral churches, and preached while being held in admiration by all. He was of great familiarity and authority with Johann, the last count of Spanheim, and obtained from him many goods for his convent. Among other things he wrote elegant works:

Sermons on the Season, 1 book. Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
Synodal Collations, 1 book. And certain other works.
He flourished in the times of Rupert, king of the Romans, and the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410.
