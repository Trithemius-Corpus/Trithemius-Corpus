In the year of the Lord’s Nativity 745 they entered Saxony again, and they caused the captured Theodoric, so often a transgressor of faith, to be beheaded. And again the Saxons, compelled to submit to the Franks, began to pay tribute.

*Again the Saxons are overcome by the Franks.*

In the ninth year of Childeric, named the idle king of the Franks, but in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 747, Carloman, the most noble prince of the Franks, mayor of the palace, count of Namur, duke of Brabant by birth, and vice-king of Germany, also principal prefect, sole and only lord, powerful and to be feared, of Trier, Metz, Liege, Cologne, Mainz, Speyer, Strasbourg, Basel, Constance, the Alemanni, Suevi, Bavarians, Helvetians, Bohemians, Austrians, eastern Franks, Thuringians, Frisians, and Saxons, [unclear] the principate, which once at one time sufficed for twelve kings; and, having despised the riches of the world and all honors, he set out for Rome and offered himself to Zacharias, the supreme pontiff. By him he was soon made a cleric; and, his dress changed, he had himself clothed in monastic garments, and, made a monk on Mount Soracte, built the church of Saint Silvester, in which he served the Lord. But after some years, when many nobles from Francia came to Rome on pilgrimage out of devotion, [unclear] he withdrew to [unclear], remaining as a monk among monks and serving God until death.

*Carloman surrendered the principate of the Franks to his brother.*

Pippin alone thereafter administered the whole kingdom of the Franks for five years still; when these had elapsed he was raised to kingship and reigned sixteen years. And the whole time of his principate after his father was twenty-six years.

*Pippin obtained the monarchy of that kingdom.*

*Saint Burghard, first bishop of Wurzburg.*

In the thirteenth year of Childeric, king of the Franks, but in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 751, in the fourth Roman indiction, Saint Burghard, English by nation, a monk by profession, was made the first bishop of the church of Wurzburg; he presided in great sanctity for forty years.

*The nobles of the Franks choose Duke Pippin for the kingdom.*

In the same year the nobles of the whole kingdom, assembling together, began to take counsel concerning the removal of the useless king Childeric. It pleased all in common that they should depose King Childeric, who had neither skill nor power for ruling, and should raise to kingship Pippin, in whose hands the supreme authority of the whole kingdom remained. But Pippin would not consent to this matter unless the counsel of the Roman pontiff Zacharias were first sought, alleging the reasons that moved him. Therefore, after counsel had been shared, Saint Burghard, bishop of Wurzburg, and Abbot Fulrad were sent to Rome to Pope Zacharias, to consult him concerning the aforesaid business of the kings: namely, what seemed to him better, more just, and more right; whether the royal name with the diadem ought to remain with him who was always idle and did nothing worthy of the royal name, or rather should be conferred on another, that is, on Pippin, who disposed all the affairs of the kingdom and alone performed the labor. The pope answered the envoys that it seemed just to him that he who bore the labors of the kingdom and disposed all affairs should bear the royal name and diadem, rather than Childeric, who, always idle, did nothing for the advantage of the kingdom. He therefore wrote to the bishops and nobles of the Franks and advised them that, after Childeric had been removed and thrust into a monastery, they should make Pippin king. He also commanded Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, by apostolic letters, to anoint the same Pippin as king of the Franks.

Pippin, by order of Pope Zacharias, was anointed and crowned king of the Franks in the city of Soissons by the hands of Saint Boniface, archbishop of Mainz, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 751, in the fifth Roman indiction; he reigned sixteen years. Pippin was the son of Ansegisus, who was the son of Arnulf, who was the son of Ansegisus, who was the son of Nicanor, who was the son of Sigebert, who was the son of Priam, who was the son of Heribert, who was the son of Leontius, who was the son of Merovech, who was the son of Diocles, who was the son of Simon, who was the son of Genebald, who was the son of Dagobert, who was the son of Marcomer, who was the son of Clodio, king of the Franks reigning in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 301. Moreover King Childeric, deposed according to the pope’s judgment, was made a monk, and in him the line of the kings of the Franks descending from Pharamond came to an end.
