*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

Nicolaus of Crutzenach, from a town of the county of Spanheim, in the diocese of Mainz, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, distinguished in life, an acute disputant and an excellent preacher of sermons, taught theology publicly for many years in the university of Vienna. Both among the scholars there and before Emperor Frederick III he won great [unclear] and profit from his learning. He wrote certain distinguished volumes of his talent, of which the following are reported: On the Sentences, four books; On the Most Pure Conception of the Blessed Mary Ever Virgin, one book; Sermons on the Season and on the Saints, two books; many orations to [unclear]; various questions disputed in the schools; and certain other works unknown to us. He died at Vienna in Austria, under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Innocent VIII, in the year of the Lord 1491, in the ninth indiction.

Petrus Schot of Strasbourg, priest and canon of Saint Peter in Strasbourg, a man whose intellect was practiced in the divine Scriptures, most learned in both laws, and very learned in the other arts of humanity, a philosopher, rhetorician, and very celebrated poet, excellent in intellect, mature, honorable, and distinguished in life and conduct. He wrote certain beautiful little works, both in verse and in prose, by which he commended his memory to posterity. On Saints John the Baptist, the Evangelist, and Chrysostom, he wrote a learned and delightful work in elegiac verse, one book; Diverse Poems, two books; Letters to Various Persons, one book; Brief praises of Andrew the Apostle in verse; Praises of John Gerson. Before the first [unclear] in the year of the Lord 1491, in the ninth indiction.

Johannes Pheffer of Widenberg, by nation an East Frank, a man learned in the divine Scriptures through long study, excellently taught in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect, devout, gentle, and distinguished in life and conduct. Sent from the University of Heidelberg, he illuminated the beginning of the university of Freiburg by his learning; there he taught sacred letters for many years with great glory, and thoroughly instructed many of his hearers. He wrote an outstanding volume on the Epistles of Paul, which, when read publicly, he entitled; Priestly Directory, thirteen books; also Questions on the Sentences, one book; sermons; and various treatises on the Scriptures, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III in the year of the Lord 1490. And he died almost two years before this.
