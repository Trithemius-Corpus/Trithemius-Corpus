*The Abbot of Sponheim*

But he also published some other works, both in verse and in prose, which have not come to my knowledge. He died under Conrad the younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1027, in the tenth indiction, in the eighteenth year of his episcopate, on the sixth day before the Kalends of December.

Osbert, a monk of Canterbury, of the Order of St. Benedict, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and very skilled in the art of music, subtle in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, published various treatises in both kinds of literature, by which he transmitted the fame of his name to posterity. He wrote, among other things: The Life of St. Dunstan, archbishop, one book. Also On Music, one book. He flourished in the times of St. Henry the emperor, in the year of the Lord 1026.

Odilo, fourth abbot of Cluny, of the Order of St. Benedict, a holy and learned man, presiding over the monastery for fifty-six years, led the souls of many to Christ by word and example; Peter Damian, bishop and cardinal of Ostia, described his life, full of virtues and miracles, in a lucid discourse. He composed for the edification of his brothers: Elegant Sermons, one book. He died under Henry II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1048, in the first indiction, in the eighty-seventh year of his age, on the first day of the month of January.

Fulbert, bishop of Chartres, most learned in the divine Scriptures and, in the disciplines of secular letters, the most learned of teachers in all his time, a distinguished poet and dialectician, presiding for many years over the public school, produced very many highly learned hearers. In life also most holy, he is read to have shone with many miracles. Among other things, he is said to have written: Letters to various persons, one book. In Praises of St. Mary, one book. Various hymns and prayers. And certain other devotional works. He flourished in the times of Henry, the first king, in the year of the Lord 1016.

Bruno, bishop of Wurzburg, paternal cousin of Conrad II, emperor, German by nation, a man zealous in the divine Scriptures, most holy in life and conduct, who, because of the excellence of his merits, was inscribed in the catalogue of the saints and is held to be the special patron of East Francia. He gathered from the writings of the ancient fathers a fine exposition: On the Whole Psalter, one book, "Blessed is the man who has not gone away." Whether he wrote anything else I do not know. He died under Henry II, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1045, in the thirteenth indiction, on the sixth day before the Kalends of June.

Rupert, abbot of the monastery of Cassino, of the Order of St. Benedict, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and exceptionally instructed in secular letters, zealous for regular discipline and an active governor, so that he could be considered a useful shepherd of the sheep committed to him by word and example. He wrote for the edification of his brothers fine and elegant Sermons, one book. He also published some other works, which have not yet come to our knowledge.

Guido, monk and abbot of the monastery of the Cross of St. Leufredus, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, by homeland an Aretine, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular disciplines, an outstanding dialectician and musician. In this too he is to be preferred to the rest: that even boys and girls may learn unknown songs more easily by themselves through his rule on their hand than through the voice of a master or by any instrument. He wrote, among the other little works of his intellect, against Berengar, cleric of the church of Tours: On the Body and Blood of the Lord, one book. Also On Music, two books, "Therefore those who [unclear] our discipline." And other works which I have not seen. He flourished under Conrad the younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1030.

Egelnoth, archbishop of the church of Canterbury in England, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, most holy in life and conduct, a most merciful advocate of the poor. At his exhortation Cnut, king of England, made many alms, especially to the church of Chartres, over which the most learned man Fulbert, bishop, then presided, of whom we have made mention above. He is said to have written, to the honor of almighty God, a fine work: In Praise of St. Mary, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. And certain other things. He flourished in the times of Conrad the younger, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1039.
