*The Abbot of Spanheim*

He wrote many excellent volumes. Of these the following are reported:

On the Divine Institutes, one book. It begins: “Great and excellent.”
On the Origin of Error, one book. It begins: “Although in the first book.”
On False Wisdom, one book. It begins: “I should wish it to be declared to me.”
On True Wisdom, one book. It begins: “As I was thinking, and with my mind.”
On Justice, to Constantine, one book. It begins: “There is no doubt with me.”
On True Worship, one book. It begins: “That was its duty.”
On the Blessed Life, one book. It begins: “It is well.”
On the Anger of God, to Donatus, one book. It begins: “I have often observed, Do[natus].”
On the Workmanship of God, one book. It begins: “How very little at rest I am.”
To Asclepiades, one book.
Letters to Probus, four books.
On Persecution, one book.
Symposium, in hexameter verse, one book.
Letters to Severus, two books.
Entitled The Grammarian, one book.
An Epitome against the Gentiles, one book. It begins: “There is a happy place in the first.”
On Paradise, in hexameter, one book.

He is said to have written not a few other works besides, but they have not come into our hands. He flourished under the emperor Constantine, to whom he was very dear, in the year 320.

Eusebius, bishop of the church of Caesarea in Palestine, surnamed Pamphilus, formerly a disciple of the eunuch Dorotheus, a most learned man, very devoted to the divine Scriptures, and, together with the martyr Pamphilus, a most diligent restorer of the ecclesiastical library: although he was an Arian, when he came to the first council of the fathers held at Nicaea, he put aside his error and, following the opinion of the fathers, at their command composed the creed which is called Nicene, and offered it to them to be read and confirmed as testimony of his own most pure faith. In it he powerfully crushed the errors of the Arians, and nothing has been either added to or taken from his edition, but it is sung publicly in the Church of God down to the present day. After this, persevering in the orthodox faith, he was most holily brought to his end. He wrote many excellent volumes in Greek. Of these the following are reported among the Latins, namely:

Against Porphyry, thirty books.
Explanations of the Gospel, twenty books.
On Isaiah the Prophet, [fourteen?] books. It begins: “Since what is for Christians.”
On the Preparation of the Gospel, ten books.
Successions of the Saints.
Ecclesiastical History, one book. It begins: “Moses of the Hebrew people.”
Epitome of the Chronicle, one book.
On the Disagreement of the Evangelists, one book.
Theophany, one book.
Apology for Origen, six books.
Topics, three books.
On the Whole Psalter, one book.
On the Life of Pamphilus, one book.
Martyrology, one book.

He also composed many other works which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under the great emperor Constantine, to whom he was very dear because of his distinguished learning and the holiness of his life, in the year of the Lord 320.

Rheticus, bishop of the Aedui at Autun, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures and, in his time, a teacher of renowned reputation. He is said to have written not a few little works, and especially commentaries on the divine books, but I recall that few have come to our knowledge, namely:

On the Song of Songs, one book.
Against Novatian, one book.

Saint Jerome censures his exposition on the Song of Songs as foolish. He flourished in Gaul under Constantine the Great, in the year of the Lord 320.

Jacob, surnamed the Wise, bishop of the city of Nisibis of the Persians, a man of great learning and incomparable holiness, one of the number of the confessors who were victorious under the persecutor Maximian, was present at the Council of Nicaea and condemned the most impious Arius by the addition of homoousion. This man of most holy conduct often freed the city entrusted to him from many dangers by his prayers. For the edification of the faithful he wrote not a few little works in the Syrian tongue, of which the following are reported:

On Faith, one book.
On Love of Neighbor, one book.
On General Charity, one book.
On Prayer, one book.
On Fasting, one book.
On Life after Death, one book.
On the Resurrection, one book.
On Patience, one book.
On Humility, one book.
On Satisfaction, one book.
On Penance, one book.
