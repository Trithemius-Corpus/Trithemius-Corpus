JACOBUS de Theramo, an Aprutian canon and Archdeacon of Aversa, a very celebrated jurist, a distinguished doctor of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, clear and ordered in speech, whose authority among professors of this faculty was great in his own time, published in that discipline certain excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only the dialogue on the redemption of the human race, commonly called Belial, which the author entitled:

The Consolation of Sinners, one book. After through the tree of knowledge.
On the Clementines he is said to have written one book. And certain other things.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

GUILIELMUS de Oppenbach, a German by nation, an eminent doctor learned in the Holy Scriptures and nobly practiced in scholastic philosophy, keen and forceful in intellect, sufficiently suited to disputing and unraveling questions of the Scriptures. He wrote in the theological faculty some little works not to be rejected, by which he made his name known in the Parisian school; of these I have found:

On the Sentences, four books. Disputed questions.
He is also said to have written certain sermons and other treatises, and to have labored over ten lectures in the schools, which have not come to my knowledge.

HENRICUS de Langenstein, called de Hassia, a German by nation, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular philosophy, excellent in intellect, abundant and expansive, since after spending many years in lecturing on Genesis and scarcely reaching the fourth chapter of the book, he completed as many volumes. For he was ready for everything, and copious in speaking and writing, so that for him it was a matter of greater difficulty to repel things worthy of mention that came to him of their own accord than to find new things to say. As I have found from a certain letter of his, I would believe that he was a canon of the church of Worms. He flourished first in the University of Paris, where he earned the laurel of the doctorate; then, for the honor of the new planting of the University of Vienna, he betook himself there, and by both teaching and writing acquired great glory for his learning. Various questions. He also published many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Rupert the Palatine, king of the Romans, and Pope Boniface IX, in the year 1400.
