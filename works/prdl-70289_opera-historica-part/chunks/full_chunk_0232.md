*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

Thomas de Kempis, of the order of the regular canons of Saint Augustine, of the Mount of the holy martyr and virgin Agnes near Zwolle in the diocese of Utrecht, a man zealous and learned in the Scriptures, devout in life and conduct, and an outstanding venerator of blessed Mary, Mother of God, formerly, as they say, a disciple of Gerard the Great of Deventer, about whom marvelous things are told. He wrote certain devout volumes, of which the following are reported: *On Contempt of the World and the Imitation of Christ*, 4 books; sermons to novices, 3 books; *Dialogue of Novices*, 1 book; sermons to the brothers, 1 book; *On the Solitary Life*, 1 book; *[On] Tabernacles*, 1 book; *Alphabet of True Compunction*, 1 book; *Little Garden of Spiritual Roses*, 1 book; *Valley of Lilies*, 1 book; *Alphabet of the Monk*, 1 book; *Consolation of the Poor*, 1 book; *Epitaph of the Monk*, 1 book; *What Sort of Man a Monk Ought to Be*, 1 book; *Manual for Little Ones*, 1 book; *Doctrinal for Youths*, 1 book; *On the Vanity of the World and Contempt of It*, 1 book; letters to various persons, 1 book; and others. He flourished in the times of Rupert of Bavaria, the most renowned emperor, in the year of the Lord 1410. And it should be noted that two men are reported to have had this name: both from Kempen, both regulars on the Mount of Saint Agnes, both outstanding in talent, and both composing various little works. The first of these, converted to religious life in the times of Master Gerard the Great and deemed worthy of divine revelations, is said to have written the things which we have listed above. The second, however, still lived almost in our own times among men, and composed various things which have not come into our hands. And perhaps some things have been ascribed to the first which the second is thought to have made. The little book *On the Imitation of Christ*, however, is reported to be by the first author; our elders say that, many years before, their elders read it, although I know that some think the contrary in this matter. This elder Thomas flourished under Emperor Rupert of Bavaria, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Michael Herbrant of Düren, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of the convent of Kreuznach in the diocese of Mainz, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished by experience in many matters, ready in talent, lucid in speech, and also an excellent preacher or declaimer of the divine word. He wrote certain little works of reading not to be despised, by which he commended his memory to posterity; among these there are many elegant sermons, 1 book, also conferences and many [unclear]. And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Rupert, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Peter of Speyer, of the order of the hermits of Saint Augustine, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, eloquent in speech, an excellent declaimer of popular sermons. He wrote things not to be rejected [unclear] zealous in the divine Scriptures and skilled through long practice. I have found the following: a mystical exposition on Exodus, 1 book; on the epistle of Saint Paul the Apostle to the Romans, 1 book; *On the Three Monastic Custodies*, 1 book; *On the Annunciation of the Lord*, 1 book; *Eulogy of Paul the First Hermit*, 1 book; sermons on the season and on the saints, 1 book; also several letters to various persons. Surveying with remarkable industry the difficult work of William of Paris on the whole corporeal and incorporeal universe, he beautifully reduced it into a brief compendium. He wrote also some other things which have by no means come to our knowledge. He died in the house of the Valley of Grace near Bruges, while being beneficially occupied in the act of visitation, on the seventh Ides of July, in the year of the Lord 1410, in the third indiction.
