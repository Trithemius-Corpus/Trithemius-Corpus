*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

PAUL of Perugia, professor of both laws and a very celebrated interpreter, and also excellently learned in other humane letters, perfectly trained in Greek and Latin eloquence, was for a long time keeper and master of the library of Rupert, king of Sicily, a most learned and zealous prince. He wrote certain distinguished little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there survives a notable volume, in which there are many things both pleasant and useful concerning the gods of the gentiles and others, which he entitled *Collections*, 1 book. And certain other things. He flourished in the time of the emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

BONOMINUS, [Bergomensis?] by nation, born in the town of Clusone, a learned physician and easily the most skilled of all in his time, and in the other disciplines of philosophy not moderately learned, most expert in healing human bodies. He wrote in that faculty little works not to be despised, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. But I do not remember having seen any of them. The following, however, is reported as his: *On Poisons*, 1 book; *Diverse Questions*, 1 book. He is also said to have composed works *On the Sentences* and various commentaries on the Bible. Concerning these and certain other things. He flourished in the times of the emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

TILMANNUS of Aachen, a German by nation, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a distinguished professor of sacred letters, outstanding in intellect and an excellent preacher, and also quite sufficiently learned in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, wrote works not to be despised for the use of readers [unclear]. Of these there is reported one especially notable lecture, which he delivered publicly at Cologne: *On the Gospel of Matthew*, 24 books. He is also said to have composed sermons and various commentaries on the Bible. Since I am uncertain about these, I can judge nothing certain.

BARTOLUS de Saxoferrato, formerly a hearer and disciple of the learned Cino and Jacobus Butrigarius, a very celebrated jurist, one like whom earlier ages had not had in that faculty, subtle and keen in intellect, elegant and eloquent in speech. He published certain distinguished volumes, especially on the laws; how great their authority is today among jurists no one even slightly learned and experienced is unaware. Among these are reported the following notable commentaries of civil law: *On the Code*, 9 books; *On the Old Digest*, 1 book; [unclear], 2 books; *On the Infortiatum*, 4 books; *On the Institutes*, 4 books; also *Tiberiadis*, 1 book; also *Counsels*, 7 books; *On the Guelphs and Ghibellines*, 1 book. And many other things. He flourished under the emperor Charles IV and Pope Innocent VI, in the year of the Lord 1355, in the eighth indiction, and in the fifty-sixth year of his age.

PETRUS Thomas, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Car-[unclear], patriarch of Constantinople, a man most learned in divine writings, in life and conduct [unclear], in the year of the Lord 1350. And buried in Cyprus.

BARTHOLOMAEUS, of the order of the Friars Minor of Saint Francis, English by nation, [unclear], was of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain distinguished volumes, of which the following are reported: *On the Sentences*, 19 books; [unclear] *The Properties of Things*, 1 book; [unclear]; *On the Properties of Things*; various sermons. He is also said to have written many other things, but they have by no means come into our hands.

Petrus Boherius, abbot of Saint Avianus, of the order of our lord father Benedict, a jurist and an excellent cultivator of the sacred Scriptures, subtle in intellect, renowned in eloquence, a very celebrated director of monastic discipline. He published for the edification of his brothers a number of little works, in which he transmitted his name to posterity. Of these I have found only [unclear].
