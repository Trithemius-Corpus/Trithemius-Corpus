The second consideration, which made me doubtful, is this: that in the aforesaid written instrument of the letters the ninth Roman indiction is given, although it was the fourth, and by no means the ninth, as a skilled ecclesiastical computist will clearly find.

The third consideration gives rise in me to a strong suspicion of fabrication, because the aforementioned letters of foundation, which the brothers at Saint Peter’s in Erfurt show as originals, are protected by no seal, although a hole appears in the parchment, as if the seal had fallen off because of excessive antiquity.

The fourth consideration is in no way inferior to those set out above, because it would be found plainly written in the life and deeds of the blessed William, abbot of Hirsau, that he built seven new monasteries of our order, among which the often-mentioned monastery of Saint Peter in Erfurt is counted fourth in order. I deliberately pass over many other considerations, lest prolixity create tedium for the reader.

Therefore, just as I wrote and long ago showed by many arguments in the very first volume of my Chronicles of Hirsau, so it has been necessary for me to think, and to set before the fabrication which I believe I have discovered, the truth.

*The Fifty-Ninth.*

Lotharius, brother of the aforesaid King Clodoveus, or Dagobert, after his brother’s death, in the year of the Lord, as we have said, 719, in the second indiction, by the advancement of Charles Martel, mayor of the palace of the Franks, was raised to be king and reigned seven years. Against him the priest Daniel, whom the Franks in Neustria had appointed king for themselves, together with his mayor of the palace Regenfrid, contended in dissension. Daniel, however, had changed his name, calling himself Helperic.

In the year of the Lord 720, Hilperic the anti-king, Regenfrid the mayor of the palace, having Eudo, duke of Aquitaine, as their ally, gathered forces and advanced to war against King Lotharius and his mayor of the palace Charles. But, defeated by Charles’s troops, they fled with great difficulty. Eudo, returning in confusion to Aquitaine, took Helperic, who was Daniel, away with him.

In the third year of King Lotharius, Charles Martel, mayor of the palace, pursuing Regenfrid, besieged him at Angers; and, when the city had been taken, he accepted him in surrender. After peace had been established between them, he granted him the city to live in; thereafter he himself powerfully held everywhere the principate of the whole kingdom of the Franks.

In the fifth year of the aforementioned Lotharius, king of the Franks, which was the seven hundred and fourth year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the seventh Roman indiction, the Bavarians, refusing to pay tribute to the Franks, prepared themselves for open rebellion. When this matter had become known to Charles, mayor of the palace, he assembled a great army of the Franks, set out into Bavaria, and attacked the disobedient in war; he powerfully brought them back to complete subjection.

In the following year, Charles, mayor of the palace of the Franks, moved an expedition into Alemannia; joining battle with the rebellious Alemanni, he emerged victorious. He overcame Lanfrid, their duke, and forced their land into subjection.

Lotharius, king of the Franks, reigning in name alone, died in the seventh year of his reign, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 726, in the seventh indiction.

*The Sixtieth.*

Theodoric, Lotharius’s nephew through his brother, namely the son of the aforesaid King Clodoveus, whom others called Dagobert, after his uncle had died and Helperic Daniel the priest had likewise departed this life, who while Lotharius was alive and after his death had acted as king for eight years, was raised to be king of the Franks by the mayor of the palace, Charles Martel, and reigned fifteen years.

In these times there flourished in Gallic Francia Saint Pirminius, formerly a monk of the monastery of Saint Maurus the abbot, bishop of Meaux in the province of Sens, a man admirable for learning and holiness. For love of Christ, to preach the right faith to peoples, he came into the regions of Germany, where by word and example he converted many to the worship of truth and founded several monasteries.
