*Annals of Trithemius.*

In the same year Saint Furseus, abbot, came from Ireland into Gaul as a pilgrim for the love of Christ, and was honorably received by King Clovis. He founded the monastery of Latiniacum, now of our order, and established monks there. Not long afterward two brothers of his, Foillanus and Vitanus, monks, following him with an equal vow of pilgrimage, enlightened Gaul by the word and by the example of their life.

Of these, Foillanus, by the gift of Saint Gertrude the virgin, later founded the monastery of Fosses, where also, crowned with martyrdom, he rests in the Lord.

In the sixth year of King Clovis, Sigebert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, despairing of offspring in posterity, built here and there twelve monasteries of the order of Saint Benedict in honor of God, with Grunwald, mayor of the palace, and Saint Remaclus, bishop of the people of Maastricht, working with him in these matters. Among them Stavelot and Malmedy were preeminent, which are governed under one abbot. This Remaclus, not long afterward, became a monk in the same monastery of Stavelot, resigning the episcopal office, full of cares, and at last happily rested.

In these times also, in Gallic France, Saint Landelinus, confessor of Christ, and Morotius built four monasteries of the aforesaid order: Lobbes, [Alciense], [Wafariense], and Crespin, where he rests bodily in the Lord with his disciples Adolenus and Domitianus.

At the same time the monastery of Fleury was founded by Abbot Leodeboldus; to it, not much later, the body of our divine father Benedict the abbot is said to have been transferred from Monte Cassino by the monk Agilulf, after his monastery, as we said above, had been laid waste by the Lombards. The Italians contradict this.

In the seventh year of Clovis, which was the six hundred fifty-second year of the Lord’s Nativity, Sigebert, king of the Franks in Austrasia, adopted Hildebert, son of Grunwald the mayor of the palace, as his son, [unclear].

In the following year Saint Judocus, son of the king of the Britons, despising the kingdom and the world, as a pilgrim and hermit in the district of Ponthieu, rested in Christ.

In the eleventh year of King Clovis, Sigebert, king of Austrasia, his brother, died, after commending his son Dagobert, still very small, to the trust of Grunwald, mayor of the palace, so that by his help he might be advanced to the kingdom. In the following year Grunwald had him tonsured by Dido, bishop of Poitiers, and sent him into Scotland; against good faith he raised up his own son Hildebert as king of the Austrasians. But the Franks, grieving over such great faithlessness on Grunwald’s part, captured him and presented him for the judgment of King Clovis at Paris. Clovis caused him, bound in chains, to be consumed by a bitter death in prison; and, after casting down his son, he appointed his own younger son Hilderic king of the Austrasians and avenged his nephew’s injury.

Clovis, king of the Franks, because of his insufficiently reverent contact with the body of Saint Dionysius, died after two years insane and out of his mind, in the seventeenth year of his reign, in the year of the Lord’s Nativity 662, in the fifth indiction.

*The fifty-third.*

Lothar, the elder-born son of King Clovis, reigned after his father over the Franks for four years. His mayor of the palace was Eberwinus, whom the French, not understanding the property of the name, since it is Teutonic, wrongly write as Ebroin: an impious and wicked man, a destroyer of the Church, a persecutor of the clergy, a most abominable murderer of bishops, [unclear] and they returned home.

In these times Queen Balthildis, mother of Lothar, formerly the surviving wife of King Clovis, founded two distinguished monasteries of our order in Gallic France, of which one is named Corbie and the other Chelles; in them many most holy and most learned monks flourished. At the same time also many other monasteries of the aforesaid order of our divine father Benedict were built in the Gallic kingdom, among the number of which these are recorded: the monastery of Chelles, [unclear].
