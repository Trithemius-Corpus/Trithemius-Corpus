*Welf, also called Welff, duke of Bavaria*, son of Conrad, count of Bavaria, received from the emperor, as the legitimate heir, the duchy of Bavaria after Otto; his forefathers had possessed it before. For his great-grandfather Rudolph, son of Arnolph, duke of Bavaria, had been duke; rebelling against Otto, the first emperor of that name, and defeated by him, he was made count of Scheyern from duke, as was said above.

Thus, after the duchy of Bavaria had been restored to the line of the emperor Arnolph, let us continue the sequence of generations of the dukes descending from Charlemagne, begun earlier. I am not unaware that some among the writers have dared to maintain that this duke Welf was a Swabian by nation, while others say that he was an Italian, the son of a certain marquis. But it is established that he was the son of Conrad, count of Bavaria, son of Arnolph, duke and emperor of the Romans. Nor does it matter where he was born, provided that the sequence of descent is established to have come down to the lawful dukes of Bavaria.

Therefore this duke Welff betrothed to himself as wife Ethelina, daughter of the aforesaid duke Otto, count of Northeim. But when Otto was driven from the duchy, he sent the daughter back to him, fearing King Henry, from whom he had received the duchy and who was Otto's enemy. Count Hermann afterward took her as his wife, left as a widow by the king of England. She bore him three sons and one daughter: Henry, surnamed the Black, who was duke of Bavaria after him; Welf, who went into Italy; Sigebert; and Adelheid, whom Conrad, burgrave of Magdeburg, took as wife.

*Otto, formerly duke of Bavaria and count in Northeim*, had many battles against the emperor in Saxony; in one of them Ernest, marquis of Bavaria, son of Duke Ernest [unclear], who had often been victorious in Hungary and in various other regions, fell, in the year of the Lord 1075.

The other things that the aforesaid Otto did with the Saxons against the emperor, and suffered from him, and how at last he was received back into his favor, are written very fully by Lampert, the monk of Hersfeld, in the deeds of that Emperor Henry; it would be too tedious to set them out here.

*Welf, duke of Bavaria*, learning that the Saxons were being unjustly oppressed by King Henry, refused to give him aid against them; whence he too was judged an enemy of the commonwealth and was also deprived by him of the duchy. But afterward, received again into the emperor's favor, he deserved not only to recover the duchy but also to have his son confirmed as duke of Bavaria. Then, having taken the cross in the time of Pope Urban II, in the year of the Lord 1106 he set out for the Holy Land, and died in Cyprus; and almost all his men, with many other Christians, were destroyed and captured through the treachery of Alexius, emperor of the Greeks.

This duke Welff, among other things that he did, built monasteries, namely Steingaden, Altdorf, Weingarten, Altenmunster, and Nietenbach. When he died and was buried in the monastery of [Artesheym?], Henry, surnamed the Black, succeeded him in the duchy of Bavaria. Some write that this duke Welff, after the death of his wife Jutta, took as wife Agnes, the mother of Emperor Henry IV.

*Ecchard, duke of Bavaria*, brother of Werner of Scheyern, who for the recovery of his father's duchy brought the Hungarians out for the third time against the emperor, of whom mention was made above, in the time of Pope Urban II, took the cross with Godfrey, duke of Lorraine, and set out for the Holy Land, and Jerusalem was recovered by the Christians. There was also in these times Otto, bishop of Bamberg, and Frederick, count in Ammerthal, who lies buried in the monastery of Kastl. He had a daughter named Sophia, whom Otto, duke of Bavaria in Ammerthal, son, as some think, of Ernest, took as wife; he did many good things for the monastery of Kastl and was buried there.

*Henry, duke of Bavaria, surnamed the Black*, son of the aforesaid duke Welff, had as wife Wulfhild, daughter of Magnus, duke of Luneburg. She bore him two sons and four daughters: Henry, Welf, Sophia, whom he gave to Berthold, duke of Thuringia; after he was killed, she took Leopold II, marquis of Steyr, as husband.
