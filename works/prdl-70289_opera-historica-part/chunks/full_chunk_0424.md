324 *Abbot of Sponheim*

The Constitutions of his order, 1 book.

He flourished under Emperor Charles IV, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John Brãmart of Aachen, German by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, provincial prior of Lower Germany, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures and, according to the custom of the scholastics of this time, a most celebrated expounder of secular philosophy, distinguished in talent and eloquence, an excellent teacher in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote for the no small profit of the faithful some minor works, by which, exercising his talent, he transmitted the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there survives:

On the Sentences, 4 books. Various sermons, [unclear].

He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Henry of Erfurt, German by nation, exercised by long study in the divine Scriptures and not moderately learned, and excellently instructed in secular letters, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence. He wrote for the edification of the Church a number of volumes, by which he made his name and fame known to many. Among these is reported a work of histories:

On memorable deeds, 1 book.

And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Emperor Charles IV and Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John of Erfurt, German by nation, a Thuringian by homeland, of the Order of Friars Minor, an enlightened and skilled doctor in the divine Scriptures, and nobly and excellently learned both in each law and in secular philosophy. He published several works of great authority, in which he showed also to posterity the power of his outstanding talent. Of these I have seen only a large volume which he entitled:

Table of Law, 1 book. Since, as it is written, 12.

Summary on cases, 1 book. Asked by the brothers that.

Of the others I have seen nothing. He is said, however, to have composed elegant sermons, but they have not come to me.

John Tacephale, English by nation, of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a learned doctor of the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, very subtle in talent, composed in speech, the most celebrated preacher of sermons of all in his time. He published works of no contemptible reading, by which, exercising his talent [unclear], he transmitted his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Among these the following are reported:

On the Sentences. On the Apocalypse of John.

Sermons on the saints, 4 books.

And certain other things.
