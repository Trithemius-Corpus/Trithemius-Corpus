Hegesippus, a Jew by nation, converted to the faith of Christ, shone forth in the Church of God as admirable in learning and holiness; his feast, with those of the other saints, obtained a place in the martyrology. He wrote several little works of excellent talent, of which I have found only: an outstanding work, from the time of the Lord’s Passion down to his own age; A History of Ecclesiastical Acts, five books, “In the Parthian war which between...” I have seen no other of his works.

He flourished near the apostolic times under Antoninus Pius, in the year of the Lord 160.

Justin, converted from being a philosopher to the catholic faith, an eminent Christian and worshipper, when in the city of Rome he was refuting Crescens the Cynic, who was blaspheming Christians, as gluttonous, fearful of death, and a follower of luxury and lusts, was accused before the senate through that man’s plots because he was a Christian, and was crowned with martyrdom for Christ. He wrote many distinguished little works in defense of our faith, of which these are reported:

Against the Gentiles, to Antoninus, one book. On the Soul, one book.
To Aurelius, Against the Gentiles, one book. [The Psalmist?] so entitled, one book.
Likewise Against the Gentiles, one book. Against Trypho the Jew, one book.
Corrections Against the Gentiles, one book. Against Marcion, one book.
On the Monarchy of God, one book. Against All Heresies, one book.

He also composed many other works which have not come to our knowledge.

He flourished under Antoninus Pius, in the year of the Lord 150.

Quadratus the Athenian, bishop after Publius the martyr, formerly a hearer and disciple of the holy apostles, a man steadfast and of incomparable learning, labored and advanced much for the advantage of the Christian faith, and gathered into the unity of faith the Church that had been dispersed by fear of persecution. Since he was an eminent teacher, he wrote, among other things, for the Christians to the emperor Hadrian an Apologetic full of instruction, one book, and other things unknown to us. At last he suffered for the defense of the catholic faith on the seventh day before the Kalends of June. He flourished under the emperor Hadrian, in the year of the Lord 120.

Aristides the Athenian philosopher, a man most learned in secular letters and very devoted to the divine Scriptures, shone forth distinguished by such eloquence that he presented a marvel of himself to the gentiles; under the garb of a philosopher he bore Christian faith and conduct, and shone forth famous, not without a reputation for holiness. He wrote an Apologetic for the Christians, to the emperor Hadrian, one book, and certain other things. He flourished under the emperor Hadrian, in the year of the Lord 120.

Melito the Asian, bishop of Sardis, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and venerable no less for holiness than for doctrine, shone forth famous among catholic preachers. He wrote many excellent volumes for the advantage of the Church of the faithful. Of these, the following are listed:

On Easter, one book. On the Life of the Prophets.
On the Fallen, one book. [On the Fallen?]
On the Body and the Soul, one book. On the Senses.
On Baptism, one book. On Faith.
On Truth, one book. Philoxenia.
one book. The work that is entitled [The Key?].
On the Generation of Christ, one book. On the Devil.
Eclogues, one book. On the Apocalypse of John,
one book. “Melito, servant of Christ.”
On the Passing of Saint Mary, one book.

He also published other little works which antiquity has not permitted to come to our memory. Tertullian, mentioning in a certain place his elegant and declamatory talent, asserts that he was regarded by our people as a prophet. It should indeed be noted that the book On the Passing of Saint Mary written by him is reckoned among the apocrypha, 15 dist., Sancta Romana.

He flourished under the emperor Marcus Antoninus Verus, in the year of the Lord 160.
