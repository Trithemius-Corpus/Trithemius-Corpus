Rolandinus de Romantiis, a professor of both laws and a very celebrated interpreter, and nobly learned in secular philosophy, was not among the least of the old jurists. In that faculty he wrote certain excellent opuscula, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Of these I have found only a summa, one book. It begins: “A little book begins: On the order of crimes”; Determinations and Questions.

The rest which he is said to have composed have not come to my knowledge.

Bernardus, priest of Compostella, familiar and chaplain of Pope Innocent IV, a man most learned in law, prompted by the zeal of the same pontiff, wrote certain excellent opuscula in law, which in these times are held in great authority among jurists. Of these I was able to find a few, namely an outstanding apparatus:

On the Decretals, five books. It begins: “At the beginning of this book.”

Cases of the Decretals, five books. It begins: “The peace-making king, by pious [mercy?]...”

Of the rest I have seen nothing. He flourished under the emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1250.

Guido de Suzaria, a most skilled doctor in law, and sufficiently instructed in the other disciplines of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, ready and cautious in counsel, had great authority among the old experts in these matters. He is said to have composed certain excellent opuscula in law. Among them there exists a notable summa which he entitled:

On the Actions of Cases, one book. It begins: “On the ordering of cases.”

Also Various Declarations, [unclear] books. It begins: “Since, with [unclear] supporting...”

And many other works, which have not yet come into our hands at all.

Bartholomaeus Brixianus, a professor and very learned interpreter of canon law, and excellently learned in the other disciplines of secular literature, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, wrote various excellent opuscula both in law and on other matters, by which he gave his name to immortality. Among these the following are reported:

A Repertory of the Decretum, three books.

A Chronicle of Italy, one book.

Disputations on the Decretals, five books.

And certain other works.

Letters to Various Persons, one book.

He flourished under the emperor Frederick II, in the year of the Lord 1240.

Johannes de Deo, a professor and outstanding expounder of both laws, and sufficiently learned in the disciplines of philosophy, subtle in intellect and scholastic in speech, by the memory of his name came to the knowledge of many. Of his works only a few have come to my knowledge; for I found only the following:

A Summa of Cavillations, seven books. It begins: “To the honor of the highest [Trinity?].”

Another, entitled Judgment, [unclear] books. It begins: “May he be present at our beginning.”

A Table of the Decretum, [unclear] books. It begins: “The book of decrees, distin[ction]...”

A Concordance of the Decretum and the Decretals, [unclear] books. It begins: “For those wishing readily...”

Various Questions. A tractate on constitutions.

He taught in the city of Bologna, where he composed the aforesaid works and others.

Hugo Barchinonensis, of the Order of Preachers, made cardinal priest of the holy Church of the title of St Sabina, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, who before entering the order had been a master of sacred theology and had many distinguished disciples. He wrote several outstanding opuscula, by which he consecrated his name to immortality.
