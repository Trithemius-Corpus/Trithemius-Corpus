*Abbot of Spanheim.*

...devout and most holy in life, a very great lover of the blessed priest Jerome. He wrote various little works in both kinds. Of these the following are extant.

The deeds of the supreme pontiffs, 1 book. “The blessed apostle Peter...”
On the faith, against heretics, 1 book. “Since after the Nicene council...”
Letters to various persons, 1 book. “Bishop Damasus...”
Various poems. And other works.

He flourished under Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 370.

Apollinaris, bishop of Laodicea in Syria, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and by far most learned in grammatical training, sharp and forceful in talent, distinguished in speech, wrote certain notable volumes in defense of the Catholic faith; among them there is extant the very celebrated work:

Against the mad Porphyry, 30 books. And certain other works.

He flourished under the emperor Gratian, in the year of the Lord 380.

Didymus of Alexandria, deprived of his eyes from early childhood, and for this reason wholly ignorant even of the elements, displayed so great a miracle of himself that he perfectly learned dialectic and geometry, which especially require sight. In the divine Scriptures he was very learned, the teacher of the blessed Jerome and a very close friend of Saint Antony, sharp in talent, distinguished in life. He wrote the following works in Greek:

Against the Arians, 2 books.
Also On Doctrines, 1 book.
On the canonical epistles, 7 books. “James, etc. [unclear]...”
On the Holy Spirit, 1 book. “All indeed which are divine...”
On the books of Saint Job, 1 book.
On Isaiah the prophet, 10 books.
[unclear], 3 books.
On Zechariah, to the same, 5 books.
On Hosea, to Jerome, 1 book.
On the Gospel of John, 1 book.
Also On the Psalter, 1 book.
And certain other works.

He flourished as an octogenarian under the emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 395.

Optatus, bishop of Milevis, an African by nation, a man learned in the study of the divine writings, outstanding in talent, devout in life, a Catholic, is said to have written certain notable little works in defense of our faith, in which he magnificently exercised his abundant talent. Among these there is said to be extant a great and distinguished volume which he wrote:

Against the Donatists, 6 books.
And several letters. And many other works.

He flourished under the rulers Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 370.

Basil, bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia, a learned and most holy man, had such authority among the Greeks that no one, however slightly learned, ever dared to contradict his teaching or writings in any matter. He wrote many notable little works in Greek, but few of them have come into our hands.

On the Holy Spirit, 1 book.
Against Eunomius, 9 books. “In the beginning, etc. [unclear]...”
A rule for monks, 1 book. “The human race...”
An exhortation for monks, 1 book. “Hear, my son, the admonition...”
On the education of boys, 2 books. “Indeed of God, without [unclear]...”
On faith, 1 book. “Every scripture [unclear] by divine...”
Several homilies, 4 books. “It is time to cry out...”

In addition he composed other treatises, sermons, and letters to various persons.

He flourished under Valentinian and Valens, in the year 380.

Ephrem the monk, a distinguished inhabitant of the desert, deacon of the church of Edessa, ordained priest by the blessed Basil, because of humility never approached to perform the divine mysteries, although before God and men he was considered worthy. Nevertheless, so far as belonged to his office, in admonishing the people he delivered innumerable homilies. This man, as is read in the Lives of the Holy Fathers, was seen by an angel to have received a book written in golden letters; from this, soon on the next day and thereafter, with the Holy Spirit speaking in him, he had so great a grace of declaiming that he found no one like himself in his time. He wrote in the Syrian language
