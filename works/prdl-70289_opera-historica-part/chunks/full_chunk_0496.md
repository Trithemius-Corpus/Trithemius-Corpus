Bohuslaus of Hassenstein, a German by nation, a noble man and learned on every side, a distinguished orator and poet, who, contrary to the custom of the other nobles of this age, both handles arms and with remarkable zeal cultivates, fosters, and celebrates letters, so that, like a second Pliny, even while serving as a soldier he composes books and poems, wrote certain excellent little works both in verse and in prose, by which he made his name known. Of these the following are extant:

*A distinguished work on the proper inventions of the Germans and Italians*, one book.
*Various letters*.
*Poems also*, one book.
*Various letters*.
And many other works.

He lives to this day in his castle Hassenstein and writes various works under Maximilian, most excellent King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494, indiction 12.

Henricus of Northeim, a Teuton by nation, a man devoted to and learned in secular letters, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, subtle in talent and fluent in speech, excellent in verse and prose, wrote certain little works not to be despised in reading, by which he commended his name with glory to posterity. Of these I have seen only that distinguished work which he wrote in verse and prose to Frederick, Margrave of Meissen, and the dukes of Saxony, entitled *Dimetronachia*:

*On the conflict of virtues and vices*, one book: “Then you, laurel-bearing [unclear].”

Of the other things which he composed, nothing has come to my knowledge. He lives to this day, writing various works, under Maximilian, most illustrious king, and the supreme pontiff Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493.

Aquinus, of the order of Friars Preachers, having the origin of his life from Sweden, was devoted to the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular literature, a celebrated philosopher and distinguished mathematician, excellent in talent and polished in speech. Imitating Boethius, he wrote a most highly commended work:

*On the proportions of numbers and sounds*, one book.
*Certain letters*.
And certain other works.

He lives to this day among the Bavarian dukes, in esteem with Otto, and writes various works under Maximilian, most unconquered king, in the year of the Lord 1494, indiction 12.

Albertus of Prusa, a Pole by nation, a collegian of the university of Krakow, a man devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, also excellently learned in secular letters, a distinguished theologian, philosopher, and astronomer. Praised in a poem of Conrad Celtis for his skill in the stars, outstanding in talent and clear in speech, he wrote several little works not contemptible in reading, by which he made his name known. But few of them have come to my knowledge:

*Commentaries on the Theoricae of Georgius Burbachius*, one book.
*To the ephemerides of Königsberg*, one book.
And certain other works.

He still lives in the university of Krakow, composing various works under Maximilian, King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1493, indiction 12.

Theodericus of Osnabrück, a Teuton by nation, of the order of Friars Minor of the province of Cologne, a man practiced in the divine Scriptures, a very famous declaimer of popular sermons, whose life all who have known him proclaim at this time to be most holy. In making sermons to the people he is illumined from God with such grace, speaks with such eloquence, and finally affects his hearers with such compunction and sweetness, that he has had no one like him among the Germans. Hence he was appointed general preacher throughout the whole province. Recently, while he was with me at Cologne, he read to me a distinguished little work, fitting for devotion, which he had composed at the instance of Hermann, archbishop of Cologne:

*On the Passion of the Lord*, one book: “Most reverend in Christ.”
*Manual of the simple*, one book: “Because, as Augustine.”
*On the inner army*, one book: “He who shall have persevered.”
And certain other works.

He still lives in the province of Cologne, vigorously pressing on in the office he has begun, under King Maximilian and Pope Alexander VI, in the year of the Lord 1493. Bartho-
