*The Abbot of Sponheim*

Johannes Rusbrock, prior of the regular canons of the Green Valley in Brabant, in the diocese of Cambrai, a German by nationality, a man, as they say, devout but little learned. In his native tongue he wrote certain little works for the edification of the simple; after his death they were translated into Latin by another, and are said by doctors to be excellent in some respects, but erroneous in others. And concerning these, Johannes [Gerson], chancellor of Paris, composed advisements. Of them these are reported:

On the Adornment of Spiritual Nuptials, 3 books. “Behold, the bridegroom comes,” etc.
On the Progress of Religious, 1 book.
Exhortatory Letters, 1 book.
And certain other works. He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.

Johannes Schodehouen, a German by nationality, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of Mechelen, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, of excellent talent in declaiming sermons to the people. He wrote, for the use of preachers of the word of God, volumes not to be despised. Of these the following are extant: a notable work on vices and virtues and on every preach-able subject matter, according to alphabetical order, in two volumes, which he entitled:

Polipodium, 2 books. “They hide themselves much.”
Various Sermons, 2 books.
He also produced a number of other works, which have by no means come into our hands.

Michael Angrianus of Bologna, an Italian by nationality, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in secular philosophy and canon law; Pope Urban VI first appointed him vicar of the whole order, and, finally elected general in the year of the Lord 1381, he governed the order under the obedience of Pope Urban for nine years. He wrote many excellent volumes. Of these the following are reported: to the cardinal of Santa Maria in Trastevere, a notable and excellent work.

On the Conception of Saint Mary, 1 book.
On the Whole Psalter, 5 books.
On the Sentences, 4 books.
Questions on the Sentences, 1 book.
A Table of the Sentences, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Matthew, 1 book.
On the Gospel of Luke, 1 book. “They were both,” etc. [unclear].
A Table of Gregory’s Moralia, 1 book.
A Table of the Decretum, 3 books.
A Lenten Book, 1 book.
He began a Dictionary, 1 book.
On Valerius Maximus, 1 book.
On Aristotle’s Ethics, 1 book.
And certain other works. Overtaken by death, he did not complete this divine work of the new dictionary, but carried it through from A to C inclusive in three volumes. He died in the last year of Emperor Wenceslaus, under Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1416, on the Kalends of December, in the eighth indiction. And, honorably buried before the high altar of his own convent at Bologna, he rests in the peace of Christ.

Philippus Ferrariensis Tholosanus, bishop of the church of Pax in Spain, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, learned and an outstanding declaimer of sermons, ready in talent and clear in eloquence; formerly preaching in the kingdom of Sicily, he converted many from iniquity and brought forth for God great fruits of his learning. He wrote elegant:

Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
And certain other works.
There are those who write that, because of his exceptional learning and uprightness of life, he was raised to the honor of the cardinalate; of this I am not certain.

Galterus Differ, an Englishman by nationality, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, nuncio and legate of Pope Boniface IX in the kingdoms of England, Spain, Portugal, Navarre, Aragon, and in the regions of Gascony, and in the other lands and kingdoms, preaching the cross against infidels and schismatics; a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and an outstanding declaimer of sermons. He wrote little works not to be despised, by which he made known only his name. Of these there are extant:

Against the Lollards, 1 book.
On the Schism also, 1 book.
On Some Psalms, 1 book.
Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book.
And certain other works.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Wenceslaus and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of the Lord 1390.
