These are the works among the writings of the most blessed doctor Jerome which have occurred to us at present to record. Nor do we doubt that he wrote more which are not contained in this catalogue, chiefly for the reason that they have either remained unknown to us or scattered among many things. For among all the doctors of the Church no one was of more benefit than Jerome, who, besides innumerable small works, translated the Old Testament from Hebrew into Latin, corrected the New according to the Greek truth, and faithfully offered it to be read by the Church. He also made many volumes of the Greeks into Latin: namely those of Origen, Josephus the Jew, Eusebius of Caesarea, Didymus of Alexandria, Victorinus the martyr, and others.

At length, after many labors, full of days, he died under the emperors Honorius and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 422, in the fifth indiction, on the last day of the month of September, at the hour of Compline, in the ninety-ninth year of his age. He was buried at Bethlehem, shining with many miracles; Eusebius, his disciple, described his life.

Audentius, a Spanish bishop, a man possessing a mind trained in the divine Scriptures, wrote against the Manichaeans, Sabellians, and Arians, but with his principal intention against the Photinians, who afterward were called Bonosiacs, a notable work which he is said to have [unclear].

He also published some other things, which have by no means come to my knowledge.

Rufinus, a monk and presbyter of the church of Aquileia, an eloquent man, very learned in the divine Scriptures and skilled in Greek and Latin speech, was of no small reputation among the ecclesiastical doctors. Although his writings are many, nevertheless they are counted among the apocrypha, Distinction 15, *Sancta Romana*, because he wrote invective letters against the most holy man Jerome, as can be understood from reading them.
