*249. ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

[Gregory II] …Constantine the pontiff, for 16 years [and] 9 months, a man otherwise learned and most holy, who ordained Boniface—an English monk, a Mainzer by nation—bishop. He wrote certain little works full of erudition, of which the following are reported: To the questions of Justinian, of Responses, 1 book; Against Leo Augustus, 1 book; To John, bishop of Constantinople, 1 book; Of letters to various [persons], 1 book. He dies under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 731, in the 14th indiction, the 3rd [day before] the Ides of February. To whom Gregory III succeeded.

BONIFACE, the first archbishop of Mainz, a Scot by nation, a monk of the order of Saint Benedict, when he was a boy of five years, was offered by his parents to a monastery in England which is called Michael's; in which, devoting [his] study to letters and morals, he turned out a man both learned and most holy. Ordained presbyter, for love of pilgrimage for Christ, he came to Germany, and there, preaching the word of God, was ordained bishop of Mainz by Pope Gregory [II], and by his successor Gregory III was raised to archbishop and primate of Germany. Who, being an erudite man, taught the people committed to him not only by word but also by writings. Of his little works I have found only: Of letters to various [persons], 1 book; the Life of certain saints, 1 book. He dies preaching in Frisia, crowned with martyrdom, in the times of Pippin the emperor, in the year of the Lord 755, in the eighth indiction, in the 36th year of his episcopate, on the Nones of June. Whose body blessed Lullus—whom he had ordained archbishop after himself—caused to be brought back to the monastery of Fulda, situated in Buchonia, and honorably buried.

VERUS, bishop of Seville, of the province of Baetica, where Isidore once held the prelacy, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, [subtle] in talent, [wrote]: Of letters to various [persons], 1 book; the Life of Saint Gallus in prose, 1 book; the same afterward in meter, 1 book. [He flourished under the same period.]
