*ON ECCLESIASTICAL WRITERS.*

[Petrarch, works list:] On the remedies of fortune: 2 books. ["Since [you ponder your] affairs and fortunes…"]; On the seven penitential psalms: 1 book; On the conflict of cares: 1 book; On the present world: 1 book; On the history of Griselda: 1 book; Bucolics, in meter: 1 book. ["Monicus, in [the] tranquil…"]; Of letters to various [persons]: 8 books; The Africa, to King Robert: 1 book; the Life of Scipio Africanus: 1 book; On memorable matters: 1 book; On orchards, to Sagremor: 1 book. He published also many others, which have by no means come into our hands. He dies in his hermitage of the Paduan territory, under Charles IV, emperor, and Gregory [XI], pope, in the year of the Lord 1374, in the 12th indiction.

Michael de Massa, of the order of the hermit-brothers of Saint Augustine, a man studious and erudite in the divine Scriptures, rich in the reading of the ancients, and nobly learned in secular letters, subtle in talent, renowned in eloquence, and a distinguished declaimer of sermons, distinguished also for the honesty of [his] life [and] conversation. He published many illustrious volumes for the edification of the faithful, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the notice of posterity. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, 4 books; On Isaiah; On Matthew; On Mark; On Luke; On John; On the life of Christ; On the passion of the Lord; On the four virtues; Various sermons. And many others. He is said, finally, to have elaborated elegant commentaries on many books of the Bible. He flourished in the times of the emperors Louis IV [and] Charles IV likewise, and of Pope Clement VI, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Richard de Bury, bishop of Durham, chancellor of Edward III, king of the English—to whom there is extant a letter of Francis Petrarch inquiring about the island of Thule—a man most studious [and] very erudite in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed in secular letters, a singular [and] most celebrated lover of books, who at great expense [and] labors founded a distinguished Library in the University of Oxford, which he established, for the public benefit of those studying there, with incredible zeal. He wrote, on the love of books [and] the institution of the said Library, a most beautiful treatise, which is now printed, which he entitled with a Greek name:
Philobiblon: 1 book. ["A desirable treasure…"]; Several letters: 1 book. And certain others.
He flourished under Charles IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1350.

John Walsgram, an Englishman by nation, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures, nobly learned in secular philosophy, subtle [and] acute in talent, adorned with the master's infula in the gymnasium of Paris, [and] most celebrated in reading [and] disputing. He wrote certain illustrious volumes, by which he made his name known. Of these there is extant a distinguished work: On the Sentences, 1 book; Various questions, 1 book; Sermons on the [seasons of the] year, 1 book; A Summa of law, 1 book; Sermons on the saints. The rest which he is said to have composed have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Charles IV, emperor, [and] Clement VI, pope, in the year of the Lord 1350.

Thomas of Strasbourg, a Teuton by nation, of the order of the hermit-brothers of Saint Augustine, after Gregory of Rimini prior general, a man most learned in the holy Scriptures, a distinguished cultivator of secular philosophy, renowned for the honesty of [his] life [and] conversation, who wonderfully exalted his order by word [and] example. He wrote some illustrious volumes on the Scriptures, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the notice of posterity. Of these there is extant a most luminous work: On the Sentences: [—] book[s]. ["The abyss gave [its] voice…"]
