*Abbot of Spanheim*

... engaged in the study of [unclear], he published many little works in both laws, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Among them are reported:

On the Decretals, 1 book. On the Sixth Book of the Decretals, 1 book. On the rules of law, 1 book. On the old Digest, 24 books. On the new Digest, 1 book. And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410, a close associate of Franciscus de Zabarellis.

Vincentius Gruner, a German by nation, a man learned in sacred writings and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech. He published little works not to be despised in reading, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is an important lecture, which, in the new planting of the Leipzig gymnasium, he publicly wrote and read before the scholars.

On the office of the Mass, 3 books: To the honor of the blessed ... Disputed Questions, 1 book. And certain other things.

He flourished at the time of the transfer of the University of Prague to Leipzig, in the province of the margraviate of Meissen, which Pope Alexander V confirmed and endowed with privileges, in the year of the Lord 1410.

Johannes de Auerbach, a German by nation, priest of the church of Bamberg, a man learned in sacred writings and in secular studies, distinguished in talent, scholastic in speech. He published little works of authority not to be regarded as worthless, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these I have seen only a distinguished work, 1 book: To the praise of God and of souls.

Of the rest I have seen nothing.

He flourished in the city of Bamberg and composed various little works.

Dionysius de Burgo Sancti Sepulchri, of the order of the hermit brothers of the divine father Augustine, a man learned in sacred scriptures and most learned in secular letters, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech. He wrote, in both branches of literature, little works not to be despised, by which he left to posterity perpetual memorials of his talent. Among these the following are reported: very beautiful and elegant commentaries, 1 book. On Valerius Maximus, 1 book. On the Metamorphoses, 1 book. On the tragedies of Seneca, 1 book. On the Rhetoric, 1 book. And certain other things. Moreover, he reduced all the fables to a pleasing tropological sense.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

Petrus de Aliaco, bishop of Cambrai and cardinal of the sacrosanct Roman Church, once chancellor of the Parisian gymnasium, teacher of Johannes de Gerson, a man most learned in sacred writings, nobly instructed in secular letters, especially in Aristotelian philosophy, excellent in talent and distinguished in eloquence, and also an outstanding declaimer of sermons. He wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left to posterity the memory of his name and talent. Of these I have found the following: 4 books: Whether this doctrine ... 1 book: Upon this rock I shall build ... 1 book: This certain doctrine ... 1 book: Whether of Peter, of the Church [unclear]. 3 books: Whoever, who from the world ... 1 book: Since many wise men ... 1 book: There are four spirits. 1 book: A shortened word [unclear]. 1 book: The Lord God terrifies me.
