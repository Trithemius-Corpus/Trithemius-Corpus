*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Ticonius the African, whom blessed Augustine, in the third book of *On Christian Doctrine*, records as having belonged to the Donatist party, although in some matters he disagreed with them, was certainly a keen man and much practiced in the divine Scriptures. He wrote not a few short works. Of these the following are reported, namely:

*On Internal War*, three books. *An Expository Work on Various Questions*, one book. *On the Apocalypse of John*, one book. *The Rules of Sacred Scripture*, one book. Several letters to various persons. And many other things. He flourished when Theodosius and his sons were ruling, in the year of the Lord 390.

Heraclides the monk, a man holy in conduct, distinguished for learning in the divine Scriptures, a most diligent investigator and imitator of the holy fathers, anchorites and monks, who, like a resourceful bee, gathered the flowers of virtues from the way of life of each into the little cell of his breast and produced the honey of spiritual sweetness. Among other things, for the edification of readers, he wrote a notable work on the life and conduct of the holy fathers living in the desert or in monasteries under a rule, whose title is:

*Paradise*, one book.

He flourished in the times of the emperor Gratian, in the year of the Lord 380.

Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, a man very devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures [unclear], wrote, among other things, a useful work:

*On the Holy Spirit*, one book.

Of the other works which he is said to have composed, nothing has come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of the devout emperor Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390.
