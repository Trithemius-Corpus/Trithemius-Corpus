Henricus Gulpen, abbot of the monastery of St. Giles in Nuremberg, of the order of the divine father Benedict, a German by nation, an eminent jurist, and excellently learned both in divine Scripture and in secular philosophy, venerable no less for his religious way of life than for his knowledge of the Scriptures; Johannes Nider, having had experience of him, commends the readiness of his talent and the foresight of his counsel in his little work On Ants. He wrote elegantly on the seven distinctions:

On Penance, one book. On Consecration, one book.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book.

And certain other things. He flourished at the Council of Constance in person, while he was still a secular master of arts and student of law, in the year of the Lord 1417.

Jacobus Forliviensis, a philosopher and physician of very celebrated reputation, who is said by many to have far surpassed all the professors of philosophy and medicine of his time, distinguished not only for learning but also for experience in curing human illnesses. In his faculty he wrote works very useful for students, by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these the principal ones are reported to be:

On the Extension of the Degrees of Forms, one book. On the First Canon of Avicenna, one book.
On the Aphorisms of the same, one book. Also on the Tegni, one book.

And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of Sigismund, most Christian emperor, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1430.

Rudolphus of Buxella, a village of Swabia, a German by nation, of the diocese of Speyer, a man studious and learned in divine Scripture, and excellently trained in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, an eminent professor of sacred theology, who acquired great praise for his learning in the Heidelberg academy. In that faculty he wrote works not to be despised, by which he made his name known to posterity, namely:

Questions on the Sentences, one book. Very Useful Sermons, one book.
Various Questions, one book. And if holy mother [unclear]
Several Orations to the Fathers, one book.

And many other things. He flourished in the Heidelberg academy at the time of the Council of Basel and thereafter [unclear] left the memory of his name to posterity. Among these are reported:

On the First Book of Avicenna, one book. On the Aphorisms of Hippocrates, one book.
On the Tegni of Galen, one book. And many other things.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

Henricus de Gauda, a German by nation, magnificently learned in the holy Scriptures, subtle in talent, and a most acute disputant of scholastic questions, easily held the first place in the Heidelberg academy in his time, both by reading and teaching, and then by composing. He wrote certain distinguished little works of his talent, of which few have so far come to my knowledge.

Fount of Gardens, Well,
On Celebration, one book. Various Orations.
Disputed Questions. And certain other things.

He flourished in the times of the Council of Basel and of the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1435.

Sanctes de Arduinis, born from the city of Pesaro, a man most learned in philosophy and medicine, and most experienced in healing human bodies, held in great esteem among the Venetians in his time, published certain distinguished little works in this faculty, by which he transmitted the memory of his very famous name to the knowledge of posterity. Among these there is extant a notable work on all natural and artificial poisons, one book.

Also On Odoration, one book.
On Prolification, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.
