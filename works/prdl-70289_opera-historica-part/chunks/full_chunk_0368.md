*Hildebert*, bishop of Le Mans and afterwards archbishop of Tours, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in secular learning second to none in his time; sharp in intellect and very eloquent in speech; formerly a disciple of Berengar of Tours, but a persecutor of his error; who endured many persecutions and tribulations, even to imprisonment, for ecclesiastical liberty. He wrote in both styles, namely verse and prose, certain excellent little works, of which as yet only a few have come to our knowledge for reading. He wrote, in elegant meter, a very beautiful and useful exposition:

*On the Whole Office of the Mass*, one book: “I have proposed to write what...”
*On His Exile*, one book: “Recently I was wealthy in...”
*On the Misery of This Life*, one book: “We are taken from the midst [unclear]...”
*On the Sacrament of the Altar*, one book.
*Of Hymns and Prayers in Diverse Meter*, one book.
*Letters to Various Persons*, one book: “If anyone [says] one nature...”

He comprised the epitaph of Berengar, once his teacher, in fifty-two verses. He wrote also many other things still unknown to us. He flourished under Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1090.
