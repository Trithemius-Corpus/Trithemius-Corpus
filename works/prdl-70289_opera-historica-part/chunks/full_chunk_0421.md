*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Having begun as if a prophet, he was cast into prison by the ministers of his own order, and a lying spirit was found in his mouth. He wrote in chains to a certain cardinal: His Revelations, one book, “Most reverend in Christ.” Also On the Sentences, four books. And certain other things. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Gerard, bishop of Savona, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, by homeland of Bergamo, a man very learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly taught in canon law, subtle in intellect and renowned in speech, distinguished no less by life than by doctrine, composed for the benefit of readers some little works not to be despised, by which, usefully exercising his talent, he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Of these are reported: On the Sentences, four books; Two Quodlibets, one book; On the Song of Songs, one book; On the Sixth of the Decretals, concerning [unclear], one book; On the Epistle to the Hebrews, one book; and certain other things. He is also said to have written many other little works, which have not come to my knowledge. A Summa of Medicine, one book. And certain other things. He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, fourth emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1340.

Thomas de Garbo, by homeland a Florentine, son of Dino, once a physician, a man learned and skilled in medicine and nobly instructed in the disciplines of the philosophers, adhering to the footsteps of his most learned father, published in his own faculty little works not to be scorned by physicians, by which he transmitted his name with glory to posterity. Of these there exists a very notable [work], four books. Of the rest I have learned nothing.

Alphonsus, bishop of Seville, of the order of the Hermit Friars of Saint Augustine, a man most learned both in divine writings and in secular letters, and in his time most celebrated among ecclesiastical doctors, renowned in intellect and speech. He published several volumes of great [unclear], by which he also made his name known to posterity; of these I [unclear] volumes of such authority, by which he transmitted his name, present in usefulness and distinguished, to posterity. Of these the following are reported: On the Sentences, one book; On the Gospel of Mark, one book. He flourished under Louis IV, emperor, in the year of the Lord 1340. On the Gospel of Matthew, seven books; On the Apocalypse, one book.

John of Bacon, an Englishman, of the order of the friars of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, a man most learned in the divine writings and excellently taught both in canon law and in secular philosophy, in the Parisian school no less in conduct than [unclear]. The Example of His Order, four books; Various Quodlibets, one book; The Mirror of His Order, one book; “You are sons of the saints [unclear],” one book; The Defense of His Order, one book; On the Poverty of Christ, one book; Against the Jews, one book; and many other things.

John Eliger of Gondersleuen, by nation a German, once a hearer of John of Saxony, a most renowned astronomer, a man most learned in secular letters, exercised himself magnificently in [philosophy?].
