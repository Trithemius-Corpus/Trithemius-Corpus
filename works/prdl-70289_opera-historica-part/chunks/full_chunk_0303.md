*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

On Virginity, one book. On the Sense of the Soul, one book.
On Circumcision, one book. On the Blessing of Unleavened Bread, one book.
On Christ, That He Is the Son of God, one book. On Chastity, one book.
Against the Nations, one book. On the Construction of the Tabernacle, one book.
On the Conversion of the Nations, one book. On the Kingdom of the Persians, one book.
On the Persecution of Christians, one book. A Chronicle of the Times, one book.
On the Words of Isaiah, one book.
Thus the whole work just designated comprises 25 books. He flourished under the great emperor Constantine, in the year of the Lord 330.

Methodius, bishop of Olympus in Lycia, and afterward of Tyre, learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, a confessor and distinguished defender of the Christian faith, and a diligent emulator of evangelical perfection, after the publication of many volumes, was crowned with martyrdom for the faith of Christ under the last persecution, or, as others affirm, under Decius and Valerian, at Chalcis in Greece. He wrote, in a polished and well-ordered style, not a few volumes, of which the following are reported:

Against Porphyry, two books. The Symposium on the Ten Virgins, one book.
On the Resurrection, one book. On the Pythoness, one book.
On the Departure of the Soul, one book. On Genesis, one book.
On the Song of Songs, one book. “For it must be known, brothers,” one book.
On the Last Times, one book. Also a Chronicle, one book.

He flourished under the great emperor Constantine, or, as others prefer, under Decius and Valerian, in the year of the Lord 355.

Antonius, monk and abbot, Egyptian by nation, a most holy man and distinguished cultivator of the desert, whose praises the whole world proclaims, furnished to many a rule of religious and blessed life. Although he had not learned letters, he was nevertheless so learned in the divine Scriptures that none of the doctors of his age would presume to put himself before him. He had memory in place of books, understanding and sense in place of letters, grace in place of the study of reading. In the Egyptian language he dictated various letters, of apostolic sense and speech, to different monasteries. Of these, that distinguished one is extant:

To the Arsenoites, one letter. To other monasteries, six letters.

Blessed Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria, described his life in an outstanding volume in the Greek language. He flourished under Constantine the Great and during the reigns of his sons, in the year of the Lord 330, shining with many miracles.

Juvencus, priest, Spanish by nation, a noble man and a most learned philosopher, distinguished poet, rhetorician, and theologian, and venerable to the Church no less for his conduct than for his knowledge of the Scriptures, published for the edification of readers many excellent volumes, both in meter and in prose; but few of them have come to my knowledge. I have read the distinguished work which he composed in hexameter and pentameter verses:

On the Four Gospels, four books. “Nothing immortal [unclear].”
On the Sacraments, one book.

The rest which he is said to have composed have not come into our hands. He flourished under Constantine the Great and his sons, in the year of the Lord 350.

Against the Arians, three books. On the Soul, one book.
Almost innumerable Letters, one book.
Against Origen, one book.

He flourished in the times of Constantine and his sons, in the year of the Lord 350.

Athanasius, nineteenth bishop of the city of Alexandria, a holy man and most learned in the divine Scriptures, endured many persecutions from the Arians on behalf of the catholic faith. Indeed the whole world was narrow for him, with the Arians persecuting him everywhere; they nowhere seemed to be wholly secure. For a man’s enemies were those of his own household. Therefore, fleeing from the face of the emperor and of the Arians, he came to the city of Trier in Gaul, and, honorably received by Saint Maximianus the archbishop, for [unclear] years hid with him in a cistern without water, which to this day is shown in the crypt at the monastery of Saint Maximinus in Trier.
