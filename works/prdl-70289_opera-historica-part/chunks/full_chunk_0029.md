That same night King Marcomirus summoned the ancestral deity by an incantation, with a murmured chant, and brought before him as prophetess one whom, because of her sagacity, they named Aliruna. He prepared a portent and confirmed the prophecy of the priestess by the authority of the Sibyl, and at once ordered the people to be led across, so that their offspring, multiplied exceedingly as the vision foretold, might remain stronger forever.

Marcomirus saw a three-headed deity standing there, and heard the separate prediction of each head; and he saw a distinct diadem placed high above each one. The first head was that of a toad; the second, of a lion; the third, in the form of an eagle, as if standing above with wings spread, holding and cherishing the lion on the right and the toad on the left. And the eagle said: “Marcomirus, your progeny shall subject my head to itself; it shall trample the lion and at the same time slay the toad.” And the toad itself added: “Do not think me to be despised, handsome king, because I appear foul. My outward form is suited to future events. Your generation will first destroy me far away; after this it will keep the lion captive by the sword. Take care to hear from his mouth what remains further.”

Afterward the lion’s head said to King Marcomirus: “Guest about to come into my [land], most valiant king, with your people, you will soon conquer the toad by toil; after much time, then my rights will everywhere yield to their own, of their own accord in wars [unclear]. Afterward the eagle on the right will crown me when conquered, having at the left the toad bound by your seed. What will happen afterward is shown to you by our conjunction.”

When the lion-shaped apparition had uttered these words, the three heads were suddenly changed into one, and the three-headed portent stood transformed into the image of a man, not of lowly condition, but of a king crowned with a golden diadem, holding a scepter in his left hand and a sword in his right. Muttering in a harsh voice, in speech taught by spells, to summon up the shades and to bring down the highest gods from heaven, it answered: “Let them bear with your enchantments, so that the future may be as future: understand, lest we speak in vain. As the transformation of our image confirms the form, so this very voice of prophecy makes certain that a kingdom, varied in condition, is promised in the future to this people; a long time will vary it, and the multitude of the ruling stock, so that it will neither always be one nor divided into very many. Now one, then two, now three, then four; beyond that, now it returns into one; at last it will stand divided into two, when the eagle has been joined as friend to the lion, and the toad, plotting against the eagle, has held the left side.” When all these things had been said, the vision disappeared.

King Marcomirus asked Aliruna what the meaning of the prophecy was. She said to him: “It is the ancestral god, O king, who accompanies you and your people; by his predictions you have been instructed about the future for your people and for the kings who will be born from you. From here you must know that you are to migrate as quickly as possible to the farthest lands of the lion toward the west. The lion designates the people who dwell on this side of the river Rhine, sprung from the ancient stock of the Trojans. On the other side of the river lives the people of the toad, inhabiting a broad and fruitful region. The third people, which is designated by the eagle, are the Romans, whose pride strives to subject the whole world to its dominion. Therefore go with your people toward the west. They will dwell between those two peoples, of the lion and of the toad, between the sea and the Rhine. For it will come to pass that many battles will be waged between your people and the people of the toad, and very many on both sides will fall slain by the sword.”

On the following day, when the nobles had again been summoned to him, he commended the plan and by public edict warned them that their settlements must altogether be transferred. And so an embassy was sent to the Saxons in Germany, who themselves are recorded as descended from the Trojans, and they asked them as brothers to allow the Scythians places to remain in some corner of their land. The Saxons readily gave consent to their petition, promising that they would give them excellent settlements, and nevertheless adding that they should hasten to come as quickly as possible. A cause pressed the Saxons on account of which they willingly agreed to the Scythians their brothers: namely, they very much desired to place them as a wall and bulwark between themselves and the Gauls.
