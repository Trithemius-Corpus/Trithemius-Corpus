Hartmann Schedel of Nuremberg, doctor of arts and medicine, and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, outstanding in talent and distinguished in eloquence, compiled and wrote, among other small works of his talent, with Sebald Schreyer and Sebastian Kammermeister, citizens of Nuremberg, providing the costs and expenses, a great volume of histories from the beginning of the world down to these times of King Maximilian. Whether he wrote anything more I do not know. He is still living in the city of Nuremberg, in the year of the Lord in which these things are being written, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Jacob Philippi, parish priest of the church of Basel, formed bachelor of sacred theology, studious and learned in the holy Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular philosophy, excellent in talent, clear in speech, honorable in life and conduct, wrote certain very distinguished little works, the reading of which is judged especially necessary for the devout. Among these there is said to be a little book entitled *The Precordial of Priests*, one book; also *A Reformer of the Life and Conduct of Clerics and Priests*, one book. There are also said to be several sermons of his to the people, and many other things unknown to us. He is still living in the city of Basel, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495.

Bernard, a monk of the Carthusian order, of the house of the Passion of Jesus Christ near the city of Liegnitz, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and not ignorant of secular literature, ready in talent and distinguished in eloquence, is said to have written many excellent volumes; but I have seen none of them except that most beautiful dialogue which he elegantly composed on the praises and miracles of blessed Mary ever virgin, one book. He flourished in the year of the Lord 1480, under Frederick III, emperor, and I believe that he is still living.

Johannes Gerbrandi of Leiden, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, subprior of the Haarlem convent, a very studious and learned man, outstanding in talent, scholastic in speech, an excellent preacher of popular sermons, is said to have written a notable history of his order, [unclear] books; also sermons on the season and on the saints, not to be rejected, and several other things unknown to us. He is still living in his Haarlem convent, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Bohuslaus of Hassenstein, a noble man and most learned in every respect, an excellent philosopher, orator, and poet, subtle in talent, and therefore eloquent in speech, illustrating the preeminence of his family by learning and erudition, is said to be writing various works both in verse and in prose, in which no less probity than knowledge is said to shine forth. Among these there exists an instructed work, *On the Particular Inventions of the Germans and Italians*, one book; also a book of epigrammatic poems, [unclear] book; and several very ornate letters of his to various persons are reported. And certain other works are to be published from day to day. He is still living in his castle of Hassenstein and writing various things, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which these things are being written, 1495, in the thirteenth indiction.

Aegidius Fabri of Brussels, native of a town of Brabant, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, prior of his Brussels convent, doctor of sacred theology and a distinguished interpreter of scholastic philosophy, ready in talent, composed in speech, mature in life and conduct, is said by those who knew him closely to have composed a work not to be despised on the *Sentences*, four books; also various disputed questions, many elegant sermons to the people, and therefore certain other things. He is still living in his Brussels convent in Brabant, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1495.

Haryngus Sifridi Sinnama, from Hage in Frisia, who is commonly called Hermann, doctor of both laws, properly instructed both in the divine Scriptures and in scholastic philosophy, lively in talent, ready in counsel, who adorns Cologne by teaching, writing, and giving counsel, wrote a very celebrated work on the titles of both laws, no less useful than pleasant to all who wish to be initiated in canons or laws. He once promised me, when at Cologne we were speaking together at some time about writings, that he would also write certain other things; whether he has done so I do not know with sufficient certainty. He is still living in manly age, under Emperor Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495.
