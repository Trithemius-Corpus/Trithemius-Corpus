*282 The Abbot of Spanheim, book I.*

Letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished at the time of the discord which was between Philip, brother of Henry, and Otto, duke of Saxony, in the year of the Lord 1200.

William of Auxerre, as they report a bishop, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, ascending the theological chair in the school of Paris, shone forth with distinction for a long time, and among all the doctors of that time was celebrated with the greatest reputation and esteem. He wrote a very notable and useful volume, or

A Summa of Theology, "As the Apostle says, Faith," one book.
On the divine offices, "On the divine offices," one book.

He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Cyril, priest and monk of Mount Carmel, and third prior general of the same order, by nation, as they report, a Greek, a holy man and illuminated with a prophetic spirit, to whom almighty God is said to have revealed his will by angelic ministry, when he was honored with silver tablets written by the finger of God. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote, to Abbot Joachim, On the Angelic Oracle.

The prologue begins thus, one epistle: "To the house of the whole divine..."
But the Oracle thus, one book: "In the time of the years of Christ..."
On the progress of his order.
Several letters to various persons, one book.

He flourished under Philip and Otto, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Helinand, monk of Froidmont, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures through long practice, and nobly instructed in secular letters, keen in intellect and eloquent in speech, because of the excellence of his learning and the integrity of his life appeared not least among the ecclesiastical doctors of his time. For he wrote many things by which he gave his name to immortality. Of these the following are reported: from the beginning of the world down to his own age, a great and notable volume,

Histories, 48 books.
On the restoration of the fallen, one book: "G. Lamentations and ve..."
Several sermons, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.

And many other things. He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord [unclear].

Gilbert, monk of the Cistercian order, and afterward abbot of a certain monastery, a man studious in the divine writings and excellently learned, subtle in intellect and clear in speech. After the death of St. Bernard the abbot, he wrote, where he had left off, namely from that place, "On my bed by night," down to the end, 46 subtle and most elegant sermons.

On the Song of Songs, one book: "There are different kinds of lovers..."

And certain other things.

He flourished under Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1200.

Aelred, abbot of a certain monastery of the Cistercian order, as one practiced by continual study in the holy Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, gentle in intellect, sweet in speech, outstanding in devotion. He wrote for the benefit of his brothers several little works, by which he both profited them and transmitted knowledge of his name to posterity. Of these there is extant a little work of great usefulness for religious men, filled with spiritual sayings, which he entitled

The Mirror of Charity, one book.
On Luke, one homily: "When Jesus was of the years..."

And other things which I have not seen.

He flourished in the times of Henry VI, around the years of the Lord 1209.

Maurice, bishop of Paris, very studious and learned in the divine writings, and nobly instructed in secular philosophy, as one who before his episcopate taught theology gloriously for many years at Paris, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, was of excellent application in declaiming sermons to the people. He is reported to have composed certain excellent little works, by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these there are reported, through the course of the whole year, both on Sundays and on feast days,

Sermons to the people, one book.
On the care of souls, to priests.

But the rest which he is said to have written have not come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of Henry VI, in the year of the Lord 1190.
