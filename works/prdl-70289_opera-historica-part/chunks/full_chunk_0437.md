Johannes de Duren, of the Order of Friars Minor of St Francis, a German by nation, a studious man and nobly practiced in the sacred writings, of great and distinguished reputation in delivering sermons to the people. He is also said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge.

Tilmanus de Hachenberg, of the Order of Friars Minor, a German by nation, from the diocese of Trier, a man studious in the divine scriptures, learned by long practice, having a language polished by use in speaking, shone forth as great and most renowned in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote certain little works not to be despised by simple people; by profitably applying his talent to these, he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there are extant:

*Sermones de tempore*, one book. “Erunt signa hic praedicti.”

*Sermones per quadragesimam*, one book.

*Sermones de sanctis*.

And certain other things.

He flourished in his convent at Koblenz, where he was buried when he died.

Johannes de Ligniano, a very celebrated jurist, thoroughly instructed in the disciplines of the philosophers, outstanding in talent, distinguished in eloquence. He is said to have published certain excellent little works in canon law; by applying himself to these he spent his time usefully, and also left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, as Franciscus de Zabarellis testifies in the prologue on the Clementines, there are extant collections:

*Super Clementinis*, one book. And certain other things.

He flourished under Rupert the Bavarian, king of the Romans, and Pope Boniface IX, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1400.

Hugo de Slestadt, a German by nation, a man studious and learned in the divine writings, and excellently taught in the disciplines of the philosophers, subtle in talent, a distinguished disputant of scholastic questions. He wrote, as they report, certain excellent volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these there is extant a work:

*Quaestionum sententiarum*, four books. And certain other things.

He was, moreover, a follower in his determinations of the divine father Bonaventure, of the Order of Friars Minor, [unclear] most learned in Aristotelian philosophy, second to none in Italy in his time, subtle in talent, scholastic in speech, an excellent declaimer. He composed many little works, especially in philosophy, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these are reported:

*Contra Iudaeos*, one book. “Plurimorum astrictus.”

*Summa de naturalibus*, one book. *Sermones de sanctis*, one book. “Omnis doctrina.”

*Sermones de tempore*, one book. “Naturali philosophia.”

*Sermones de quadragesima*, two books. “Tecum sapientissime re[x?].”
