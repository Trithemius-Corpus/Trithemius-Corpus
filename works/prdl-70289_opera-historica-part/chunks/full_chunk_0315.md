But afterward, returning to the harmony of brotherly love, Rufinus’s little works were made authentic, insofar as they were not contrary to the statements of Jerome. Concerning the writings of Rufinus, therefore, the following are reported.

He is said besides to have composed many other works, but they have not come into our hands. He also translated the volumes of many Greeks into Latin, namely Clement, Origen, Basil, Gregory the Great the bishop, Pamphilus the martyr, Eusebius of Caesarea, Evagrius, Sixtus the philosopher, and many others; all these he marked beforehand with his own prefaces and letters. He flourished in the times of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, and wrote many things. In the year of the Lord 400.

John, bishop of Constantinople, formerly at Antioch a hearer and disciple of Eusebius, bishop of Emesa, while he was still a priest, who because of the charm of his eloquence is called Chrysostom, that is, Golden-Mouth, was a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, and in declamations and homilies to the people was of excellent talent; he was distinguished no less for holiness than for learning, and was held by the people in great reverence and love. This most blessed prelate suffered many wrongs from Theophilus of Alexandria, and by his fraudulent and deceitful contrivances was driven from his bishopric into exile, as is found in his Life and in the Tripartite History.
