Other and greater works, indeed, are awaited in the faculty of the liberal arts. But, not content with these, the most religious man directed his mind to divine matters and gave himself wholly to them, destined to bring great adornment and assistance to our theologians, the searchers of the divine word, since he explains what is involved, illuminates what is obscure, unties what is knotted, and restores here and there passages that are truncated and mutilated. This can easily be perceived from those things which, in this most sacred discipline of divine wisdom, he either translated, or wrote, or saw to it that they were printed in a more correct and more emended form.

They are the following:

A translation of John of Damascus. Commentaries on Richard.

On the Trinity. Commentaries on Ecclesiastes.

Commentaries on the Epistles of Paul. Scholia on the works of Dionysius the Areopagite.

On the Adornment of Spiritual Nuptials. Commentaries on Hugh of St Victor.

He lives in Paris, daily forging certain new things on his craftsman's anvil.

Guilhelmus Budaeus of Paris, born of the most illustrious family of the Budaei, a historian, poet, philosopher, and most eloquent orator. He is splendidly endowed not only with gifts of fortune but also with gifts of mind. In this our time he is becoming famous throughout all Italy, and not only throughout Gaul, because of his incredible knowledge of all branches of wisdom; day by day he is most learned in both languages, of almost divine talent, grave and serious in speech, most learned in every kind of discipline, and besides his singular learning a man truly most humane, affable, modest, and worthy of great veneration. He wrote very many orations and letters; diverse elegies and epigrams. He published Annotations on twenty-four books of the Pandects, or Digest. He will publish annotations on the remaining books, as he himself testifies in the letter which he writes to Jean de Ganay, great chancellor of France. How great the learning and doctrine of these annotations is, is left for each person to weigh. He lives in Paris, to the great expectation of all; greater works from him will shortly come to light.

Guilhelmus Castellus, or Castalius, a Gaul, a poet and most [unclear] orator of Tours, was born at Tours, his father being Christianus Castellus, a Parisian doctor and royal physician, his mother a woman of Bourges, in the year of the Lord 1468. There, taught the first beginnings of letters, he was sent to Paris, as is the custom, to undertake more fruitful and more solid disciplines, to Johannes Anglicus, his maternal brother, a man exceptionally [unclear] in divine and human letters. Entering the College of Navarre, to use the common word, he was to succeed there, where in liberal studies, through uprightness and learning, he not only equalled those who went before him but even far surpassed them. So great was his thirst for literature, so great his love, so great his care, so great his zeal. Hence, soon known by fame beyond [unclear], he was summoned to the College of Burgundy to instruct the youth in better and more polished letters by Bernhardus Roilletus, a man most richly furnished with all good disciplines and chief rector of the same college, and he was placed over the first class of the humanities. Then, after the contest of dialectic had been endured and his pupils had been presented with the laurel, he returned to the College of Navarre, joining divine studies, for which he had always yearned, to the human disciplines. At length, having obtained the doctorate in the most sacred faculty of theology, he withdrew to Tours, where, honored with honorable ecclesiastical benefices, an archdeaconry and a canonry, he lives among his own, attending to prayer and [unclear], as he has now begun, with many works which he has sent into the light.
