Amulus, bishop of Lyons in Gaul, a man devoted to the divine Scriptures, learned from reading the ancients, and also not moderately instructed in secular letters, distinguished in both the Hebrew and the Latin tongue, keen and forceful in intellect, scholastic and orderly in speech. He wrote little works of reading not to be despised, of which I have read only the notable work to Charles:
Against the Jews, 1 book. “Concerning detesting the Jews ...”
Of the rest I have as yet seen none.
He flourished under Louis, son of Charlemagne, in the year 830.

Bertram, priest and monk, very skilled in the divine writings and excellently learned in the letters of the secular disciplines, subtle in intellect and renowned in speech, and no less distinguished in life than in doctrine. He wrote many excellent little works, of which few have come to my knowledge. To King Charles, brother of the emperor Lothar, he wrote a commendable work:
On the Body and Blood of the Lord, 1 book.
On Predestination [unclear].
He flourished in the times of the emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 830.
Letters to various persons, 2 books.

Turpin, archbishop of Reims, friend and secretary of the great emperor Charles, a man learned in the divine writings and nobly instructed in secular letters, having an intellect exercised in verse and prose, advocate of the poor, beloved by God in life and conduct; he declares that, together with Charles, with his own hands and arms he often fought against the Saracens [unclear], 1 book.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Lothar, in the year of the Lord 860.
The Deeds of Charlemagne, 2 books.
He flourished under the same Charles and Louis his son, in the year of the Lord 830. The same, 1 book.

Anastasius, keeper and most sagacious restorer of the apostolic library, learned in the divine Scriptures and nobly instructed in secular letters, very skilled in Greek and Latin expression, is said to have composed some little works, but nothing has come into our hands. He translated many volumes of the Greeks into Latin. For, by order of Nico[laus?] [unclear], 1 book.

And also other things.
The Life of St. Gregory, the first pope, 2 books.
He flourished under Charles the Bald, emperor, in the year of the Lord 870.

Notger, bishop of Liege, formerly abbot of the monastery of St. Gall, of the order of the holy father Benedict, German by nation, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures and, among all of his time, most learned in the disciplines of secular letters; a distinguished philosopher, poet, and musician, and no less venerable for holiness than for knowledge. He wrote many little works, both in meter and in prose, of which few have come into our hands. For I have read only the volume that he entitled Sequences, which he had written while still a monk, in a distinguished style, a large volume, which contains:
“The sum of holiness [unclear] ...”
Prologue or preface, 1 letter, 1 book.
