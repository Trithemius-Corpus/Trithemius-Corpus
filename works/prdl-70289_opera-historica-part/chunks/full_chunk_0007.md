*In the Trithemian chamber* (which is still seen intact in the monastery of Spanheim, one mile from the town of Kreuznach), inscribed on every side with elegant letters, red and black, there are read very many poems and sayings of various authors, ancient and more recent, both Greek and Latin: especially of Sedulius, Juvencus, Fortunatus, Bishop Hildebert, Peter of Riga, and Walter of Châtillon. To these Conrad Celtis, his contemporary and friend, added the following:

*Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet:*

Look, venerable guest, at these little verses,
which Trithemius has set here with three marks:
how great a cultivator of antiquity he was, or how great a lover
of languages, the written wall teaches both.

*The likeness of the same Celtis, depicted from life in colors, is seen on the wall, with these words added:* *Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate, sang these verses in the year of the Lord 1494, when he was here. To the future abbot.*

Whoever you shall be as future abbot, I ask that you often be willing
to turn over these poems of ours in mindful thought.
Be pious, merciful, a lover of religion;
and, I advise you, learn to follow my Trithemius.

*Johannes Trithemius, abbot, to his successor.*

Look: I have set up memorials of learned men,
and whatever can greatly adorn you.
I exhort and admonish you to follow in our footsteps,
you who will come after me and rule my buildings.
