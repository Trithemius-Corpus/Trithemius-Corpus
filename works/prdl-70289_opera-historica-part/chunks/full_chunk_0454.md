354 *The Abbot of Spanheim*

NICHOLAS de Susato, a German by nation, a very celebrated doctor of sacred theology, and most learned both in secular philosophy and in the divine Scriptures, held in esteem by Ludwig, duke of Bavaria, count palatine of the Rhine, and in the Council of Constance joined with the cardinals from the German nation, together with the others, for electing the supreme pontiff, as is found in the acts of that council, wrote several distinguished little works of his talent, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these are reported:

Questions on the Sentences, one book. Many sermons, one book.
Various questions. And many other things. He flourished at the Council of Constance under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

JOHANNES de Marliano, a very celebrated philosopher and physician, excellent in talent, renowned for experience in this art, and not among the least of the physicians of his time, published certain distinguished and lucid little works both in philosophy and in medicine, the reading and study of which are said to be of no little use to students of these matters, namely:

On reaction, etc., one book. On the heat of bodies, one book.
On antiperistasis, one book. And many other things.
He flourished in the times of Sigismund, most Christian emperor, in the year of the Lord 1430.

JOHANNES Gritch, a German by nation, from Basel as his native place, of the Order of Friars Minor, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently instructed both in canon law and in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent and clear in eloquence, of very celebrated reputation in delivering sermons to the people, wrote little works not to be despised for preachers of the word of God, by which he commended the memory of his name to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

Sermons on the season, one book. Sermons on the saints, one book.
Sermons throughout Lent, one book. “When you fast,” etc., second.
On the Passion of the Lord, one book. And certain other things.
He flourished, as his people say, in the times of Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430.

JOHANNES Nider, of the Order of Friars Preachers, a German by nation, a man zealous and learned in the divine Scriptures, and sufficiently taught in scholastic philosophy, of excellent and distinguished talent in delivering sermons to the people, an inquisitor of heretical depravity and a most keen investigator of the little women whom the common people call witches, wrote in plain speech several volumes by which he made his name known to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

On the contracts of merchants, one book. “Since the office of merchants.”
On moral leprosy, one book. “Once, while we read in Levi.”
On the precepts of the Decalogue, one book. “The most sacred law of the Decalogue.”
On ants morally, one book. “Often traversing those which.”
On the consolation of a fearful conscience, one book. “Among the disciplines I found.”
Mirror of Perfection, one book. “Behold your king,” etc. Three.
Sermons on the season, one book. “Come after me,” etc.
Sermons on the saints, one book. “Turn to,” etc.
Lenten sermons, one book.
And many other things.
He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund and of the Councils of Constance and Basel, in the year of the Lord 1430.

NICHOLAS Siculus, abbot of Monacen. of the Order of Saint Benedict, sprung from the city of Catania, called de Tudeschis, first auditor of the cases of the lord pope and of the Rota, afterward archbishop of Palermo, and finally cardinal of the sacrosanct church, the most celebrated of all [unclear] in law, subtle and acute in talent, ready and firm in counsel, open and orderly in speech, who is deservedly called the lamp of the law. He wrote many distinguished volumes by which he made his name known to posterity; of these the following are reported:

On the Decretum, incomplete, one book.
On the Decretals, one book.
On the Clementines, one book.
For the Council of Basel, one book.
One hundred four counsels, one book.
