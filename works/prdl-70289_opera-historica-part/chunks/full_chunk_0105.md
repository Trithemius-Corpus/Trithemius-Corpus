But the same bishop Modoaldus built a third holy congregation of girls on the bank of the Moselle, near the walls of the city of Trier, in honor of the divine martyr Symphorianus, under the standard of Saint Benedict, where he set his sister, named Severa, as mother and abbess over the holy women. Living in every purity of mind and body, she in no way fell short of the holiness of her brother. At length, when the course of the present race had been most holily completed, she passed to the Lord and was buried in the same monastery of Saint Symphorianus the martyr, whom she had served most devoutly. This Modoaldus, whom others write as Rodoaldus, was the brother of Itha, wife of Pepin the Elder, and the maternal uncle of the blessed virgin Gertrude. This Itha founded in Brabant the monastery of Nivelles for nuns, in which, after Pepin’s death, she thereafter served Almighty God most holily with her daughter.

But the most holy Modoaldus, prelate of Trier, not long after his sister’s death, himself also resting in the Lord, was buried beside her in the church of Saint Symphorianus. Numerianus succeeded him in the archbishopric.

Nor should it have been passed over in silence that Dagobert, king of the Franks, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 627, in the fifteenth indiction of the Romans, began to found the Dologiense monastery of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Trier, by the counsel and aid of the blessed archbishop Modoaldus; Sigebert, king of Austrasia, his son, completed it. And the holy prelate Pirminius, a monk, afterward adorned it more beautifully.

In these times, in the diocese of Trier, men of great holiness were illustrious. One of their number was Paul, who on Mount Gebenna, opposite the city of Trier, across the bridge of the river Moselle, led an eremitic life. Then, coming to the aforementioned Dologiense monastery, he became a monk; finally, ordained bishop of Verdun, he faithfully kept in all things the little rule of his former holiness. The mountain previously called Gebenna, taking its name from him, is called Paulimons, commonly Paulsberg, down to the present day, and shines with countless miracles. At his tomb in the aforesaid little town there is a frequent gathering of peoples out of devotion.

There was another, at almost the same time, Wandalinus, a monk and priest, a disciple of Saint Columbanus, companion in pilgrimage of the divine monk Walaric, a holy man, an outstanding preacher, who converted many by the word and example of his manner of life, who, dead to the living, down to the present day shines with countless miracles. At his tomb in the aforesaid little town there is a frequent gathering of peoples out of devotion.

Wandalinus also, a monk and priest; Carilephus, a monk and priest; Wolffrichus; Bantus his brother; Beatus; monks and priests; Wolff, a layman, most holy men, with many others in the diocese of Trier, in these times led a solitary life in the wilderness. Saint Disibotus also, a monk and priest, in the diocese of Mainz, in the county of Spanheim, two miles from Kreuznach and one from a certain monastery of mine, Spanheim, leading an eremitic life, built a monastery of monks in that place, Sobernheim, where the Nahe and the Glan flow together; it exists today under his name.

In the first year of Clodoveus, king of the Franks, Saint Remaelus is ordained bishop of the church of Maastricht, a man of much learning and great holiness.

In the following year, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 647, in the fifth indiction of the Romans, Pepin the Elder died: count of Namur, duke of Brabant, mayor of the palace, palatine count of Sigebert king of the Franks, who held the seat of the kingdom at Metz in Austrasia; father of the holy virgin Gertrude. He left a son, Grimoald. Grimoald succeeded his father in the leadership of the royal household for eleven years; afterward he was killed, as we shall say in its place. He had a son named Childebert, of whom we shall also speak, namely how he made him king after his own lord had been driven into exile.

In the year noted above, Radulf, duke of the Thuringians, lifted up in pride, refused to pay the accustomed tribute to the Franks. Therefore Sigebert, king of the Franks of Austrasia, having gathered forces, moved an army into Thuringia; and, when battle was joined, he defeated the duke and compelled him to pay the tribute.
