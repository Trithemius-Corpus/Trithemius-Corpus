For love of chastity, that he might dare to preach to the women who followed [him], he had himself castrated—although in this deed zeal remained without knowledge. On account of his doctrine, eloquence, and apostolic life, Mammaea, the most Christian mother of the emperor Alexander, seized with desire of hearing him, came from Alexandria to Antioch, and heard his mellifluous teaching with great admiration. He was so intent on the study of the divine Scriptures that he never took food or sleep unless one of the brothers had first set forth a reading of the Scriptures, with all listening. The New and Old Testament, like another Esdras, he knew by heart; and now an old man, against his age, he learned the Hebrew tongue, and gathered the editions of all the interpreters except the Septuagint into one, by wonderful study. In dictating editions of the Scriptures he was of such admirable memory and subtlety that he wearied seven notaries, and as many girls most swift in writing, besides other young men, writing diverse [things] at once from his mouth, by an incredible agility of pronouncing. And although he was held, and was, so great a doctor, distinguished among all ecclesiastics, yet he is noted for great errors, by which either he himself was foolish, or was depraved by others inserting false [things] into [his] writings. For there was always a varied opinion of the doctors concerning Origen, some asserting [him] a heretic, others affirming that fraud was imposed on him. [It is] not to be wondered at if the first [man], without example, erred, who unfolded so many volumes without a preceding master. Yet it is ours, not only concerning Origen but concerning whatever writers, to think with the holy Roman Church, and to despise superfluous opinions. He published almost infinite volumes, of which I have seen, rendered into Latin, only the following: [a long list of homilies and commentaries follows—On Genesis hom. 16; On Exodus hom. 13; On Leviticus hom. 16; On Numbers; On Joshua hom. 26; On Matthew hom. 26; On Isaiah; On Jeremiah; On Ezekiel hom. 14; On the Song of Songs; On Luke hom. 26; On Romans, 4 books; On the martyrs, 1 book; Against Celsus the philosopher, 8 books; Peri Archon (On First Principles), 15 books; and others—rendered as printed]. He composed, besides, many others, which have not come into our hands. There are [those] who write that they have read six thousand treatises of Origen—which number is both great and unknown to the Latins. Nor [is there] doubt that many of his little works have been newly translated, which by no means came to the notice of our reading. He dies an old man and poor, buried at Tyre, under the princes Valerian and Gallienus, in the year of the Lord 261, in the ninth indiction, in the 70th year of his age.

AMMONIUS of Alexandria, a man erudite in the divine Scriptures and most learned in secular letters, the most celebrated philosopher and rhetorician of all his time, subtle in talent and eloquent in speech, wrote in the Greek tongue certain illustrious volumes, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of whose number the following are extant:
On the consonance of Jesus and Moses: 1 book.
Of the Gospel Canons: 1 book. ["In the first canon, four…"]
"One out of four": 1 book. ["Since indeed many…"]
And certain others. Him the most impious Porphyry, most bitter enemy of the Christians, a gentile philosopher, falsely accuses of having—the faith being denied—become a heathen; [him] who, it is agreed, remained a devout Christian up to death. He flourished under Alexander, son of Mammaea, in the year 250.
