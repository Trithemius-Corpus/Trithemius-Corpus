*On Ecclesiastical Writers*

Six books of *Elegances*, beginning, “When often with myself [unclear] of the ancestors...”

*On the reciprocal use of sui and suus*, one book, beginning, “You, John, are singular...”

Many letters, one book. Also *On Quintilian*, 12 books.

*Dialectic*, one book, beginning, “As I was undertaking, Paul...”

*Panegyric on the True Good*, 3 books, beginning, “I should very much wish, Garcia...”

*On Free Will*, one book, beginning, “I hear that Poggio [unclear] another...”

*Apologue against Poggio*, one book. And many other things.

*Against Antonius Raudensis*, one book. He flourished at the Council of Constance in person, under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1420.

He translated into Latin Homer, Thucydides, and several others.

Of him a certain man says as follows: What did that most biting censor of the Latin language, Laurentius Valla, gain for himself by his harsh and exact criticism? What praise, I ask, did he obtain after death, except that the fine epigram composed about him according to his deserts is read and proclaimed everywhere? “Now, after Valla, dead, has sought the shades, Pluto does not dare to speak Latin words. Jupiter would have deemed him worthy of the honor of heaven as censor of language, but he fears him as censor of his own.” But what he thought concerning the Donation of Constantine, by which Constantine endowed the Church, his book clearly shows; I wish so great a man had never published it.

John Tortelli, by nation an Aretine, a very close friend of Laurentius Valla, to whom [unclear]. Two books of grammatical commentaries, beginning, “The Greek letters by which...” He also published *On Dialectic*, one book, and various letters, and many other things. He translated the life of Athanasius of Alexandria into Latin for Pope Eugenius. He was chamberlain of Eugenius, and afterward a household companion of Pope Nicholas and his most intimate companion in studies, fully instructed in Greek and Latin letters, a most celebrated philosopher, rhetorician, and theologian. Among the other little works of his talent he wrote a large and distinguished volume which he entitled [unclear].

Hermannus Petra of Stutdorp, of the Carthusian order, by nation a German:

*Sermons on the Temporale*, 3 books.

*Sermons on the Saints*, 3 books.

Fifty sermons on the Lord’s Prayer, one book.

*On the Governance of Nuns*, one book.

And many other things. He died under Emperor Sigismund and Pope Martin V, in the year of the Lord 1428, on the ninth day before the Kalends of May, in the sixth indiction.

Omnibonus Leonicenus, a man most learned in humane letters, fully instructed in Greek and Latin eloquence, the most celebrated philosopher and rhetorician of all in his time. He was once a student at Venice of Manuel Chrysoloras of Constantinople, who brought Greek letters back into Italy. He wrote many distinguished volumes, by which he left the memory of himself to posterity. Of these the following are reported:

*On Cicero’s Orator*, 3 books, beginning, “How much of Cicero’s speech...”

*On the poet Lucan*, one book, beginning, “In expounding this...”

*On Quintilian*, one book.

*In Praise of Eloquence*, one book, beginning, “How much admiration...”

Many orations. And he published many other things, which have not yet come into our hands. He flourished under Emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1426.

Henry of Hesse the Younger, of the Carthusian order, prior of the house of the monks of Mary, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect and clear in eloquence, published many most elegant volumes, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity; and before his entrance into religion he publicly lectured on many of them in the universities. Of these the following are reported:

*On the Sentences*, 4 books.

*On Genesis*, one book.

*On Exodus*, one book.

*On the Proverbs of Solomon*, one book.

*On the Apocalypse of John*, one book.

*Dialogue between a Bishop and a Priest*, one book.

Useful sermons, and certain other things.
