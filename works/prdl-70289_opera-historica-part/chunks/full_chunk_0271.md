*Catalogue of Illustrious Men.*

On matters and pleasant novelties, a dialogue, one book; a dialogue on kinds of meters, two books; a dialogue on the accents of Latin words, two books; a panegyric addressed to me, two books; a dialogue on the brotherhood of the Rosary, two books; also a book of epigrams, one book; a consolatory dialogue addressed to me concerning his tribulations and the calamities of these times, in which he consoles himself in turn with words once exchanged with us; on the love of the monastery, zeal for letters, and why studious and learned brothers are hated by the prelates of monks, one book; likewise a dialogue addressed to me on the exposition of his melodies in the hymns of Mary, Mother of God, where he introduces and explains fifty-two kinds of meters, one book; to lord Johannes Dalburg, bishop of Worms, a heroic Sapphic poem, one book; letters, both to me and to others, almost innumerable. Against the above-mentioned brother “weighing hands,” of the Order of Preachers, who attacked the most pure conception of the Mother of God, he produced many letters and verses. And certain other works which are still unfinished in his hands. He is still living, having almost forty-four years of age, in the monastery of Heyn in the diocese of Worms, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord in which we write these things, 1495, indiction XIII.

He wrote in both kinds many outstanding volumes, by which he spread his name far and wide; a man most learned in every respect, subtle in intellect, [unclear] in eloquence. Of these the following are extant. To lord Berthold, archbishop of Mainz, he wrote a very celebrated volume in hexameter and pentameter, most elegantly, on the threefold whiteness of blessed Mary ever virgin, against those attacking her most pure conception, one book. Likewise to Theodicius Gresmund the younger of Mainz, Precepts of Rhetoric, one book; Praises of the distinguished Church of Speyer, one book; a brief postil on the Creed of Athanasius, one book. At the urging of Ludwig, bishop of Speyer, he composed the legend and office of the Compassion of blessed Mary ever virgin, which the same bishop established to be sung publicly in his diocese. He also produced a useful treatise on the poetic art, which was printed, very corruptly, at the end of the New Grammar without the author’s name. A History of a certain idiot Stylpho, one book; also a poem on the angelic message, one book; Prothymologia on the good method of preaching, a work well furnished from the sources of the orators, one book; Isodonius Germanicus on the easier instruction of boys, one book; an oration against the invaders of priests; an oration for ecclesiastical immunity; an oration to the Cardinal of Tournai; an oration to Raymond, nuncio of indulgences; an oration to the Heidelberg Gymnosophists on Saint Catherine; an oration to the same on the Holy Spirit; an oration to the same on the assumption of the divine Virgin; an oration to the synod of Worms; an oration to the synod of Speyer; an oration to the rural synod of Schlettstadt, and also various [others]. A heroic poem to Eberhard, duke of Württemberg; a book of epigrams, one book; against the brother “weighing hands,” who attacked the conception of the Mother of God [unclear], and also various questions. A heroic poem to Eberhard, duke of Württemberg; a book of epigrams, one book; against the brother “weighing hands,” attacking and rejecting the conception of the Mother of God, he wrote elegant and well-furnished letters. He is still living in the city of Speyer, having forty-five years of age, under Maximilian the Emperor, in the year of the Lord in which we wrote these things, 1495, indiction XIII.

Philippus Alberti, from the town of Neuss in the diocese of Cologne, of the order of the brothers of blessed Mary ever virgin of Mount Carmel, of the Frankfurt convent, bachelor formed in sacred theology, a man studious and learned in the divine writings, and exceptionally learned in Aristotelian philosophy, subtle in intellect and distinguished in speech; who first in the Gymnasium of [Parthenice?], and now in the school of Cologne, by his learning and uprightness of morals, [unclear]. He publicly reads at Cologne a notable lecture on the Song of Songs, one book; also many and various sermons preached at Frankfurt, two books. Now he writes and publicly reads in the Cologne convent a singular and beautiful work on the Sentences, four books. He also wrote a lengthy letter, supported by very firm authority and reason, against Wigand of the Order of Preachers, [unclear].
