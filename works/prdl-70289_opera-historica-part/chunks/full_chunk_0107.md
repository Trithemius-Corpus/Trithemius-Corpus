*Annals of Trithemius*

Saint Dado, also called Audoenus, bishop of Rouen and chancellor of King Dagobert, likewise built within the forest of Brie a monastery of our order, which they call Resbac, and appointed over it as first abbot Saint Agil, a man beloved by Christ and renowned for many virtues.

King Dagobert, in the ninth year of his reign, designated his son Sigebert, a boy of seven years, as king over the Austrasians, under the guardianship and direction of Saint Cunibert, archbishop of Cologne, and of Pippin, duke of Brabant and count of Namur.

In the same year, which was the six hundred and fortieth from the Lord’s Nativity, in the thirteenth indiction, Saint Arnulf died: Pippin’s grandfather, formerly mayor of the palace of the Franks, bishop of Metz, and from bishop a solitary hermit. Clodulf, after his father, was ordained bishop of Metz, and imitated his holiness.

Doda, formerly the lawful wife of Saint Arnulf before his episcopate, mother of the aforesaid bishop Clodulf, was enclosed at Trier for love of Christ, whom she had chosen as her spouse; separated from her husband, she shone with [unclear] holiness.

In the eleventh year of his reign, Dagobert, king of the Franks, led an army against the Wends. The Saxons came to his aid with all eagerness. When battle was joined, the Wends were defeated by the Franks and were forced under tribute into servitude to the Franks. But the Saxons, who annually paid the Franks five hundred oxen as tribute, seized the opportunity and, approaching King Dagobert after the victory, asked him to remit the tribute to them. The king, wishing to repay their voluntary service with favor, remitted the tribute.

After this Dagobert, king of the Franks, died on the first day of the month of February. He was buried in the church of Saint Denis, which he himself had built, in the fourteenth year of his reign, and in the six hundred and [forty-]fifth year of the Lord’s Nativity, in the third indiction of the Romans.

*The Fifty-Second*

Clovis, son of King Dagobert, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, reigned seventeen years. His mother’s name was Nathildis. His mayor of the palace, or count, or prince of the palace, was an energetic and most noble man named Erchenwald, [unclear].

Clovis reigned seventeen years over Neustria, that is, over Paris, Amiens, Beauvais, Blois, Reims, Orleans, Tours, and also Aquitaine, in the whole kingdom that had belonged to his father.

But his brother Sigebert, as we have said, reigned in Austrasia: over the people of Metz, Trier, Cologne, Liège, Maastricht, where Saint Amandus was then bishop, over Tongeren, also the Morini, the Hollanders, Frisians, Saxons, Thuringians, Bavarians, Suevi, Alemanni, those on this side of the Rhine, Strasbourg, Worms, Mainz, the [Cynonoti?], and the Burgundians. His mayor of the palace, or prince of the palace, was Pippin the Elder, son of Ansegis, grandson of Saint Arnulf, count of Namur, duke of Brabant, father of Grimoald and of Saint Gertrude, abbess of Nivelles, in the paternal duchy of Brabant.

The chancellor of Sigebert was Saint Cunibert, archbishop of Cologne. In very many of the places or provinces already named, under the dominion of King Sigebert and of Mayor of the Palace Pippin, there were appointed petty kings, dukes, counts, and prefects, by whose administration the people was governed.

Irmina, a virgin of most holy way of life, was one of the daughters of King Dagobert, sister of the kings mentioned before. For her, while he was still living, her father built in the city of Trier, in his very ancient palace which was called the Horrea, a monastery of girls under the rule of Saint Benedict, and a church in honor of blessed Mary ever virgin. In it he placed a congregation of nuns, to whom he gave as mother and abbess that same daughter of his. She was renowned for many virtues and miracles in life and after death; her venerable head is held in the abbey of Sponheim. After this Irmina in the governance of the nuns of the aforesaid monastery succeeded Modesta, a pupil of the monastery of Romaric, daughter of the sister of blessed Modoald, archbishop of Trier; after her Anastasia; in the fourth place Basilissa. All these have been numbered among the saints.

At the same time the aforesaid bishop of Trier, Modoald, at whose urging the monastery in the Horrea already mentioned had been built, also built another monastery in the old palace, which was situated in the suburbs of Trier, in which nuns

Saint Modesta, Saint Anastasia, Saint Basilissa, abbesses in the same place.
