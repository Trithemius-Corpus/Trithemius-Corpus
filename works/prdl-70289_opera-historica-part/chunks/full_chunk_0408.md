*The Abbot of Sponheim*

On Galen’s Tegni. One book. And certain other things.

He flourished in the Carthusian order in the times of Albert, Roman emperor, in the year of the Lord 1300. And thereafter he is said to have written various treatises for spiritual edification.

Peter of Verberie, called Aureolus, of the Order of Friars Minor and archbishop of Aix, a doctor of the Parisian school, most learned in the divine Scriptures, and very skilled in Aristotelian philosophy according to the custom of this time, subtle in intellect and an acute disputant, was outstanding in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote many distinguished little works, by which he greatly spread his name. Of these there are extant:

On the Sentences. Four books. “Quia disciplinati hominis.”
A Breviary of the Bible. One book. “Venite ascendamus.”
On the Conception of Saint Mary. One book. “Nondum erant abyssi.”
Sermons on the Season. One book.
Sermons on the Saints. One book.

And other things which I have not seen. He flourished before his pontificate, lecturing on the Sentences at Paris, in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1317.

Gentilis Fulgin[as?], a distinguished professor of medicine and magnificently learned in the other disciplines of secular philosophy, had no small expertise in that faculty. He wrote in medicine many excellent volumes, as they say, by which his name became known with praise not only to those then present but also to posterity. But I have seen none of them. They are said, however, to be twenty:

Commentaries on Avicenna. Five books.
Against Pestilence. One book.
Also On Fevers. One book.
On the Degrees of Medicine. One book.

And certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Henry VII, in the year of the Lord [1310?].

Dante Alighieri, an Italian by nation, Florentine by homeland, a man most devoted to study in his time both in the divine Scriptures and in secular letters, and very learned, a philosopher and a poet inferior to none in his age, subtle in intellect, clear in eloquence, the keenest disputant of all, wrote in meter and prose many distinguished volumes, by which he transmitted his name to posterity. Driven from his homeland, he lived in exile all his days, for some time in Gaul and afterward with the king of Aragon; concerning his misfortune he composed various things. Of his little works these are reported:

Of Comedies. One book.
On the Monarchy of the World. One book.
Several letters.

And certain other things.

At last he died in exile at Ravenna, under Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1321, in the fourth indiction, and in the fifty-sixth year of his age.

Michael of Cesena, of the Order of Friars Minor, the seventeenth minister general, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, ready in intellect, clear in eloquence, and venerable no less for religion than for learning, published in the holy Scriptures little works of exposition not to be scorned, by which he became known far and wide, both to those then present and now to posterity. Of these I have so far been able to find very few, namely:

On the Sentences.
On the prophet Ezekiel. One book.
Sermons on the Season. One book.
Sermons on the Saints. One book.

He published readings and commentaries on many books of the Bible, which I have not yet seen. He flourished under Louis, the fourth emperor, in the year of the Lord 1320.

Bertrand, of the Order of Friars Minor, Milanese by homeland, born from a noble family, according to some a priest cardinal of the most holy Roman Church, was certainly a man excellently learned in the divine Scriptures, not moderately instructed in secular philosophy, and of outstanding talent in delivering sermons to the people. He wrote several little works of great usefulness, of which I have found:

On the Sentences. Four books.
Sermons on the Epistles.
Sermons on the Gospels. One book. “Videbunt filium,” verse.
Sermons on the Saints. One book.

The rest that he composed have as yet by no means come to my knowledge.

He flourished in the times of Louis the Bavarian, the fourth emperor, and Pope John XXII, in the year of the Lord 1325.
