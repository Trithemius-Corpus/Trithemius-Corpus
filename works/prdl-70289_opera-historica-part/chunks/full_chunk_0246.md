Goswinus de Hex, a Brabantine by birth, of the order of the brothers of Blessed Mary ever Virgin of Mount Carmel, of the convent of Vlissingen, bishop of [Ieropolis?], vicar in pontifical matters of the bishop of Utrecht, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and notably instructed in secular philosophy, ready in intellect and distinguished in eloquence, also a very celebrated preacher of popular sermons. He wrote On the Ten Commandments, 1 book; also A Directory of a Troubled Conscience, 1 book; sermons for the season and on the saints, 2 books. But he is also said to have written several other things, which have not yet come to my knowledge. He died under Emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1475, in the eighth indiction, on the Kalends of April.

Johannes de Aurbach, priest and canon of the church of Bamberg, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures, and an excellent doctor of decrees, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, who for a long time adorned the University of Erfurt by his learning and presence, both in teaching and in writings. He wrote many excellent volumes, by which he transmitted his name to the memory of posterity; of these I have found only the following: Judicial Process, 2 books; also On the Sacraments, 1 book. The rest that he composed have not come into our hands. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Henricus de Herp, of the Order of Friars Minor of the Observance, of the province of Cologne, a man learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of pontifical law, ready in intellect, clear in speech, devout in life and conduct; and among his sermons the following are extant: On the Ten Commandments, a great and distinguished volume, 1 book; On the Three Parts of Penance, 1 book; sermons for the season and on the saints, 2 books; sermons through Advent, 1 book; also sermons through Lent, 1 book. Concerning the rest, however, I have found nothing. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Paul II, in the year of the Lord 1470.

Johannes de Mechilinia, trained by long study in the holy Scriptures and not moderately learned in scholastic philosophy, excellent in intellect and clear in eloquence, taught the divine Scriptures with distinction for many years at the University of Cologne, and acquired great renown for his learning. He wrote On the Sentences, 4 books; On the Whole Psalter, 1 book; Various Questions Disputed in the Schools, 1 book; also sermons. Besides these he is said to have written many other things, which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished under Emperor Frederick III and Pope Pius II, in the year of the Lord 1460.

Dionysius Rikel de Lebis, monk of the Carthusian order, of the house of Bethlehem at Roermond in Gelderland, a man most devoted to the divine writings and thoroughly learned by continual exercise, also not ignorant of secular philosophy, subtle in intellect, scholastic in speech, singularly devout in life and conduct, so much so that he was even held worthy of divine revelations. He wrote so many and such great works that, in number of volumes among the Latins, apart from Augustine, he had no equal. He was accustomed to devote himself to contemplation and prayer with such fervor that he seemed never to have been able to write anything. Again, he was so continually engaged in the study of reading and writing that one would have thought he could neither pray nor contemplate. He slept very little, was admirable in abstinence from food and drink, and, after the example of Saint Jerome, judged it sweet to meditate day and night on the law of the Lord, always either reading or writing something useful, so that prayer often interrupted reading, and reading continued prayer. Of the works of this most studious man I have found the following: On the Sentences, four books; On Genesis, 1 book; On Exodus, 1 book; On Leviticus, 1 book; On Numbers, 1 book; On Deuteronomy, 1 book; On Joshua, 1 book; On Judges, 1 book; On Ruth, 1 book; On Kings, 4 books; On Chronicles, 2 books; On Ezra, 3 books; On Nehemiah, 1 book; On Tobit, 1 book; On Esther, 1 book; On Judith, 1 book; On Job, 1 book; On the Proverbs of Solomon, 1 book; On Ecclesiastes, 1 book; On the Song of Songs, 1 book; On the Book of Wisdom, 1 book; On Ecclesiasticus, 1 book; On Isaiah the Prophet, one book; On Jeremiah, 1 book; On the Book of Baruch, 1 book; On Ezekiel, 1 book; On Daniel, 1 book; On the Twelve Minor Prophets, 12 books; On the Books of Maccabees [unclear].
