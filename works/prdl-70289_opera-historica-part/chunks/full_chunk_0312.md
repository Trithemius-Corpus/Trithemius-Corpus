*The Abbot of Spanheim* ... not unaware of the Luciferian party, and for that reason deservedly blameworthy. He wrote many excellent [unclear]. He flourished under Valentinian and Theodosius, in the year of the Lord 390. One book.

Jerome, priest and monk, born of his father Eusebius from the town of Stridon, a man very learned in secular letters and, among all the doctors, most learned in the divine Scriptures, distinguished by skill in many languages, interpreter of the sacred law, hammer of heretics and most powerful assailant. This is the true evangelical steward, faithful and prudent, who, bringing forth from his treasury new things and old, and giving to his fellow-servants in due season the measure of spiritual wheat, despoiled the proud Hebrews and enriched the humble Latins. This is the eminent doctor of the Church of Christ, who, with his pious mother Rebecca, that is, the grace of God, brought forth the garments of Esau, wandering astray through the byways of errors, laid up unused for the Lord, and clothed the meek Jacob, faithful and serving Christ. This is Jerome, sincere lover of Jesus Christ, who, passing no time in idleness, meditated day and night on the law of the Lord, and, scarcely granting his body the sleep it required, always by days and nights wrote, read, taught, or prayed, as the almost countless books which he published bear witness. This defender of truth, cultivator of virtue, and most bitter enemy of vices, when he was priest of the Roman Church and constantly reproved the crimes of the clerics, [unclear] from the city through their envy; and, coming into Judaea, he visited the glorious sepulchre of the Lord, and, establishing for himself a dwelling-place beside the manger at Bethlehem, became renowned throughout the whole world, this no doubt being arranged by divine providence, so that the light of the world should rest there where the true sun of justice had become known to men by being born. A holy and fitting inhabitant of a most holy place, firm in faith, kindled with charity, mature in conduct, who could not be led astray by prosperity, whom no adversity ever cast down from his holy purpose. But why do I, a foul praiser, touch the holy head of the man with impure lips? It would have been more fitting for me to keep humble silence than to speak inadequately about so most holy a man.
