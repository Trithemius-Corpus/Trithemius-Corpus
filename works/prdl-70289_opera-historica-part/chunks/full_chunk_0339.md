an outstanding poet, learned in the divine Scriptures, and the most learned of all men of his time in secular letters; he was the son-in-law of Symmachus the patrician. A man most abundantly furnished with Greek and Latin eloquence, since he translated many volumes both of Aristotle and of other philosophers from the Attic tongue into Latin; subtle in intellect, ready and Catholic in judgment, in speech not inferior even to Tully. It is known that Saint Benedict had friendship with him, and that on Monte Cassino, with Tertullus, senator and father of Placidus the monk, he ate at his table. He wrote many excellent volumes, of which I have found the following.

He died in the same year as Symmachus the patrician, under Pope John, under Emperor Anastasius, [unclear], in the year of the Lord 524, indiction 2. And under the name of Severinus he is said to have been inserted in the catalogue of saints.

Hormisdas the pope, a Campanian by nation, after the pontiff Symmachus presided over the Church for nine years; a good, learned, and vigorous man, who, when a synod had been gathered at Rome, condemned the Eutychian heresy, and, when the Manichaeans were again sprouting up, burned the books of their errors and banished them into exile.

Among the other little works of his talent he wrote:
book 1. Meanwhile the things which pertain to unity.

Letter 1. the [Eutychi]an heresy he condemned, and, when the Manichaeans were again sprouting up, burned the books of their errors and banished them into exile.
Several letters to various persons, book 1.
He died under Justin the Elder Augustus, in the year 521, indiction 14, on the eighth ides of August. John succeeded him.
*On the Catholic Faith*, book 1.

Evodius, bishop of Pavia, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, of singular eloquence both in Greek and in Latin, was sent by Pope Hormisdas to Constantinople in matters of faith to Anastasius, the heretical emperor. After receiving many insults, he was expelled from the city together with his companions, Anastasius saying: Why do you present commands to us, who are under the sway of no one? The Roman pontiff ought to obey us, not also to command. Evodius, however, wrote to Pope Symmachus, book 1, and certain other things. He flourished under Emperor Anastasius, in the year of the Lord 510.
