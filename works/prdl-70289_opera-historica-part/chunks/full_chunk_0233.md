*Of Johannes Trithemius*

HEINRICH of Hachenberg, of the order of Friars Preachers, of the convent of Koblenz, a studious man, practiced in the Scriptures, of excellent talent and great reputation in delivering sermons to the people. Among other works of his exercise he wrote: Sermons on the Temporale, book 1; Sermons on the Saints, book 1; also Sermons on Lent, book 1. But he is said to have written several other things which have not come to my knowledge.

JORDAN, of the order of the Friars of Saint Augustine of the Hermitage, of Strasbourg, a devout man, an excellent expounder and interpreter of the divine Scriptures, of most excellent talent in delivering sermons. He wrote several elegant treatises useful to devout brethren, by which he left the memory of his name to posterity. Of these, however, few have come into our hands: On the Apocalypse of John, book 1; An Apology for his order, book 1; On the Passion of the Lord, book 1; Sermons on the Temporale, book 1; Sermons on the Saints, book 1; Sermons throughout Lent, book 1; and many other things. He flourished under Rupert the Bavarian, emperor of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1410.

VINCENT Gruner of Zwickau, doctor of Prague, founder of the Leipzig school, a man learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in talent and distinguished in eloquence, published some little works not contemptible in their reading, by which he transmitted his name with glory to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there is extant: A notable lecture on the office of the Mass, which he publicly read at the new planting of the aforesaid Leipzig school, book 1; also On the Sentences, books 4; Various Disputed Questions, book 1. The rest that he composed have not come to my knowledge. He flourished at the time of the transfer of the Prague school to the city of Leipzig, in the province of the Margraviate of Meissen, around the year of the Lord 1410, under Emperor Rupert.

JOHN, bishop of Lombariensis, of the order of Friars Preachers, born in the city of Basel in Germany, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, distinguished in eloquence, and a most celebrated deliverer of sermons. He published several distinguished little works, by which he transmitted his name to the knowledge of posterity. Among them there is extant an outstanding work On the Sentences, books 4; also various sermons; and certain other things unknown to us. He flourished around the times of the emperors Rupert and Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1410.

JOHN, monk of the monastery of Kastl, of the order of our divine father Benedict, in the diocese of Eichstatt, a man studious and learned in the divine Scriptures and not ignorant of secular letters, ready in talent, distinguished in eloquence, a theologian, philosopher, and notable rhetorician. He wrote many distinguished volumes, of which I have found only those set down below: On the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, a very celebrated work in two large volumes; likewise A Breviary of the Bibles, book 1; elegant Sermons on the Temporale and on the Saints, books 2; also forty-two sermons On the Passion of the Lord, book 1; Letters to Various Persons, book 1; On the Illustrious Men of Our Order, book 1. But he published many other things which have not come to the knowledge of our reading. He flourished at about the time when the reform of Kastl was vigorous, under the emperors Rupert and Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1417.

JOHN Zacharias of Erfurt, a man studious and learned in the holy Scriptures and excellently instructed in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, an acute disputant, who adorned the Erfurt school with the majesty of a Sententiary. He wrote: On the Sentences of Peter Lombard, books 4; also On Genesis, book 1; On Exodus, book 1; On Leviticus, book 1; On the Epistles of Paul, books 14; many sermons; various determinations of questions. But he is said to have composed several other things which have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Emperor Sigismund; in the great Council of Constance he was magnificently praised for disputing against the heretical Hussites, in the year of the Lord [unclear].

NICHOLAS of Dinkelsbuhl, doctor of Vienna, a man most learned in the divine Scriptures, well instructed in secular philosophy, which they call Aristotelian, keen in talent, scholastic in speech, outstanding in life and conduct. He wrote many distinguished volumes, of which I have found only those set down below: On the Sentences, books 4; also Questions on the Sentences, book 1; On the Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit, book 1; On the Ten Commandments, book 1; On the Lord’s Prayer, book 1; On the Eight Beatitudes, book 1; On the Three Parts of Penance, book 1; On the Eight Capital Vices, book 1; On the Seven Mortal Sins, book 1; On the Commandment of Charity, books 2; On the Sins of the Tongue, book 1; Sermons on the Temporale and on the Saints, books 2; also other moral sermons, book 1.
