On the concord of theology. On the soul and its accidents, two books. A concord of theology and astronomy, one book. “Since you have very often asked me.” On the sacraments of the Church, one book. Several letters to various persons, one book. Sixteen various sermons. And certain other things. He flourished at the time of the Council of Constance, at which he was also present in person. In the year of the Lord 1416.

Nicholas of Clemanges, archdeacon of Bayeux, a man learned in the divine scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, excellent in verse and prose, [who attained] the mastership of sacred scripture in the school of Paris, [wrote] volumes in which he left the memory of his most brilliant talent. Among these the following are reported: to Lord Peter of Ailly, bishop of Cambrai, the aforesaid cardinal, the distinguished work which he entitled: On the Fruit of the Hermitage, one book. On the Corrupt State of the Church, one book. On New Festivals, one book. On the Matter of the Schism, one book. Letters to various persons, one book. And many other things. He flourished in the times of the emperor Sigismund and of the Council of Constance. In the year of the Lord 1417.

John Dominici, cardinal of the title of Saint Sixtus, commonly called “of Ragusa,” by homeland a Florentine, of the order of the Friars Preachers, who was sent by Pope Gregory XII to the Council of Constance for the sake of unity, a man learned in the divine scriptures, not ignorant of secular letters, of excellent talent in delivering sermons to the people, wrote certain excellent volumes by which he made his name known to posterity. Among these the following are reported: On Ecclesiastes, one book. On the Song of Songs, one book. On the Gospel of Matthew, one book. On the Magnificat, one book. On the Epistle to the Romans, one book. The work entitled The Little Light of Night, one book. The Itinerary of Devotion, one book. “If I speak with the tongues of men.” Sermons of Charity, one book. Sermons on the Saints, one book. Sermons through Lent, one book. “Blessed is the man who has not gone away.” And certain other things. He died at the Council of Constance under the emperor Sigismund. In the year of the Lord 1418, indiction 11.

John, bishop of [Lombaria?], German by nation, Basel by homeland, a man most learned in the divine scriptures, excellently instructed in secular philosophy, outstanding in talent, and distinguished in eloquence, a very celebrated declaimer of sermons, produced little works of reading not to be despised, by which he transmitted the memory of his name with praise to the knowledge of posterity. Of their number the principal ones are reported: On the Sentences, one book. Various Sermons, one book. And certain other things. Moreover, he is said to have delivered many speeches and collationes before the clergy, and also before the supreme pontiff and the assembly of cardinals, which I have not seen.

John Zacharias, German by nation, Erfurt by homeland, a man very studious and learned in the divine scriptures, also an outstanding interpreter of secular philosophy, subtle in talent, a very acute disputant, highly suitable in delivering sermons both to the clergy and to the people, wrote certain excellent little works by which he made his name known. Among these are reported: On the Sentences, one book. On Genesis, one book. On Exodus, one book. On Leviticus, one book. On the Epistles of Paul, one book. Various sermons. And certain other things. He flourished under the emperor Sigismund; in the Council of Constance he was magnificently praised for disputing against the heretics. In the year of the Lord 1426.
