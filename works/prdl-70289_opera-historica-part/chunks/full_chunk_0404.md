JOHANNES of San Gimignano, of the Order of Preachers, a man zealous and very learned in the divine writings, of clear talent and plain eloquence, of excellent diligence in delivering sermons to the people, gained great praise and fruit from his learning in the salvation of souls. He wrote, among other things:

On the Similarities of Things, 1 book. Sermons on the Season, 1 book.
Sermons on the Saints, 1 book. Various Collations, 1 book.

He is said besides to have written certain other things, which have not come to me.

PETRUS of Dacia, philosopher, calculator, and astronomer, most excellent and very celebrated among all men of his time, subtle in talent and clear in eloquence, not ignorant of the divine Scriptures and the Greek language. He is reported to have written many little works in astronomy and the art of calculation, which are judged not useless for ecclesiastical computation, by which he destined his name for the knowledge of posterity. But few of them have come to my knowledge.

On Calculation or Computation, 1 book. Also a Calendar, 1 book.
And of his Tables, 1 book.

The rest, even if there are many works that he composed, have nevertheless not come to me. He flourished in the times of Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

NICOLAUS of Naples, a man skilled and very learned in civil law, nobly instructed also in other secular letters, whose authority among the professors of this faculty flourished in no small degree in his time. He wrote several little works on the laws, by the publication of which he bestowed his name upon immortality. But I have been able to find only the titles of the following.

On the Code, 9 books. On the Infortiatum, 14 books.

Concerning the other works that he is said to have composed, I have as yet been able to find nothing certain. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1305.

HUBERTINUS of Casale, of the Order of Friars Minor, formerly a hearer of Johannes of Parma, the minister general, a devout man and sufficiently learned in the Holy Scriptures, when he saw that his order was falling away from its first institution, began certain new separations under the appearance of reform, and obtained that they be confirmed by the supreme pontiff. These, however, at the petitions of the custodians and ministers, were immediately revoked by the same pope, because they seemed to have been undertaken to the grave scandal and division of the order. Moved by this, Hubertinus entered the Carthusian order. He wrote a very distinguished and notable volume, useful to religious and devout persons, which he entitled:

The Tree of the Life of the Crucified, 5 books. To all who are truly Christ’s.
Sermons and Letters. And certain other things.

Certain Catholics warn us that Hubertinus, in the aforesaid volume, in certain places, especially where he takes up the canticle of Simeon, must be read cautiously, because on certain articles he thinks otherwise than the Church, the mother of us all. Yet the author himself is to be excused, because it had not yet been determined, etc. He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.

JACOBUS of Ravenna, sprung from the province of Lorraine, a man very learned in civil law and the sacred canons, and in his time easily the most skilled of all in both the divine Scriptures and secular letters, professor of sacred Scripture, philosopher, and notable dialectician, who, as Cinus says, converted what had been handed down by the elders in simple meaning to the dialectical mode of argument. He wrote many little works in both laws. Of these there are reported:

On the Code, 19 books. On the Old Digest, 24 books.
On the New Digest, 12 books. On the Digest of the Infortiatum, 14 books.
Various Disputations, 1 book.

He flourished under Emperor Albert, in the year of the Lord 1300.
