First, therefore, stirred with common resolve to recover Aquitaine, they led an army against Himold, duke of that province, and declared war on the prince. But he, seeing that he was able [not?] to resist, sought favor and submitted himself to their will.

At the same time, while Pippin and Carloman were still in Aquitaine, in the castle called Lucasi, before they departed from there, they resolved to divide between themselves the principate of the whole kingdom of the Franks, the administration of which they held under the wretched king. They therefore divided, as they wished, all the provinces of the kingdom, without the advice or consent of the nobles in the province of King Childeric being sought for this. For Childeric was, as we have said, not a king but rather the shadow of a king, bearing the empty name of one of his servants, without substance. The nobles of the kingdom approved the intention of the princes, as the time required.

And so by common decree Pippin and Carloman divided between themselves all the provinces of the whole kingdom of the Franks in that place which is called Old Poitou. In this division Carloman obtained by lot Austrasia, Alemannia, and Thuringia; the names of the districts and cities of these provinces are these: Trier with the Mosellans; Metz with the Austrasians; Liege with the people of Namur, the Brabanders and Hollanders between the sea and the Rhine; Cologne with the Tungri, Batavi, Menapii, Frisians, and Hogii, who are now the Westphalians; Mainz with the [Cristheneni?], Vangiones, Nemetes, Westrasii, and [Cynonoti?]; Strasbourg with the Alsatians; Constance with the Alemanni, Helvetii, Raurici, and the people of Chur, who today are called [Valesii?]; Wurzburg with the Suevi and the eastern Franks, among whom are towns and cities: Nuremberg, Bamberg, Forchheim, Windsheim, Hall, and many counties, Schweinfurt, Rothenburg, Frankenburg, the castle of [Sebalsein?], [Wicharshein?], Wertheim, and many others; Bavaria with its cities, Regensburg, [Viuarium?], [Driopolis?], and others. Thuringia and Saxony, with almost all Germany, fell to Carloman; in almost each of these provinces dukes or counts administered the commonwealth under the power of the Franks, removable at the will of the princes.

Pippin, however, in that division obtained Burgundy, Westria, Provence, Aquitaine, Champagne, Gascony, and whatever lies between them. The names of the cities through the territories of these provinces are these: in Burgundy, indeed, Lyon, Vienne, Avignon, Besancon, [Loanna?], Valence, Marseille, and whatever today is contained in Savoy and the Dauphinate. In Westria: Paris, Chalon, Toul, Tours, [Cathalonia?], Toulouse, [Cotonagus?], whatever there is throughout all Gaul, Reims, Orleans, Soissons, Beauvais, Blois, Amiens, from the Spains as far as the river Scheldt.

After this division had been made between them, Pippin and Carloman returned home. At the same time, as soon as the Alemanni heard of the death of Charles Martel, they withdrew from subjection to the Franks and unanimously conspired in rebellion. As soon as this rebellion of the Suevi became known to Carloman, he gathered troops and moved his army into Alemannia, which he devastated with sword and fire. But the Alemanni, seeing that they were unequal to the Franks in strength, submitted themselves to them again and paid the annual tribute, as they had been accustomed to do before.

In the seventh year of King Childeric, which was the year of the Lord’s nativity 744, Carloman and Pippin, having joined their forces, advanced to war against Odilo, duke of Bavaria, who had himself fallen away from subjection to the Franks. For he had placed the royal diadem upon himself and by his own authority had ordered himself to be called king, which had been forbidden by decree of the Franks, because they had reduced all kingdoms subject to their dominion into provinces, establishing from them all one monarchy, over which the one king of the Franks alone presided. Duke Odilo, however, met the Franks coming against him in war, but was overcome by them, and Bavaria was again reduced into submission to the Franks.

At the same time part of the Saxons also, after the death of Charles Martel, fell away from loyalty to the Franks. Therefore Carloman, returning with his men from the slaughter of the Bavarians, moved an expedition into Saxony, and, after capturing the castle which the inhabitants call Osnabruck, today an episcopal city, received into surrender Theodicic, the chief count of that district, who did not keep faith.
