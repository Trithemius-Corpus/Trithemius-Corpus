She also wrote certain other things. Since Saint Jerome, in a certain letter, states that he had read Homer-centos and Virgil-centos, they must necessarily have been composed before his death.

She flourished with Theodosius her husband, who took up the empire. In the year of the Lord 423.

Primasius, an African bishop, as some maintain once a disciple of blessed Augustine, a man indeed learned in the Holy Scriptures, splendidly imbued with the disciplines of secular letters, subtle in intellect and eloquent in speech, is said to have composed many little works, which among the ancients were held at a high price. Of these I have found only:

*Against Heresies*, to Bishop Fortunatus, three books.

*On the Apocalypse of John*, to Castorius, five books. *Concerning the book of the Apocalypse of blessed John*.

The rest, however, which he composed have not yet come to my knowledge. He flourished under the emperor Theodosius II, in the year of the Lord 440.

Junilius, bishop of a certain city in Africa, but I have not been able to discover the name of the city, a man certainly very learned in the sacred Scriptures, in my judgment sufficiently instructed in secular disciplines, profound in understanding, sweet and ornate in speech, is said to have written many little works; but I have seen only the distinguished work which he wrote to Primasius, the above-mentioned bishop, which he entitled:

*On the Parts of the Divine Law*, one book. *To the holy and most blessed bishop*.

Of the rest I have seen none.

He flourished at the same time as Primasius, in the year of the Lord 446.

Proterius, bishop of Alexandria, a man most learned both in divine Scriptures and in secular letters, a distinguished theologian and computist, whom in the times of Leo Augustus the successors of the heretic Dioscorus, after making a sedition with Timothy as author, cruelly killed. And they set up that same Timothy, like an idol, in his place as bishop of Alexandria. Among other things he wrote to Saint Leo the pope:

*On the Celebration of Easter*, three letters.

To Hilary on the same matter, one letter. *Since your holiness has arisen*.

Several letters to various persons.

And certain other things.

He flourished under the emperor Leo, in the year of the Lord 460.

Pascasinus, a Sicilian bishop, learned in divine Scriptures, nobly instructed in secular letters, venerable no less for his conduct than for his knowledge, is said to have presided in place of blessed Pope Leo the Great at the Council of Chalcedon, in which, together with the holy fathers to the number of two hundred, he condemned the heretics Eutyches and Dioscorus. Now Eutyches was a priest and abbot of the very celebrated monastery of Constantinople, who asserted that Christ was God only, not man; he wrote various things in favor of his error, all of which were condemned together with their author. Pascasinus, however, wrote to Pope Leo:

*On the Paschal Question*, two letters. *The writings of your apostolate*.

To the same, *On the Miracle of the Font*, one letter. *Not one day of a long [unclear]*.

*The Acts of the Council of Chalcedon*, one book. *The rules of the saints through [unclear]*.

He flourished under Theodosius the Younger, in the year of the Lord 450.

Leo, the first pope of that name, a Tuscan by nation, after Sixtus III sat in the chair of Peter for twenty-one years; a man most learned in divine Scriptures, so celebrated and eloquent in delivering homilies to the people that he had no superior in the Roman Church, as his most renowned sermons will testify. For he was the Cicero of ecclesiastical diction, the Homer of sacred theology, the Aristotle of the arguments of the faith, the Peter of apostolic authority, the Paul in the Christian pulpit. His sermons pierce the heart, move the soul, and confirm the Catholic faith in sweet, brief speech. Nor is it strange: bearing the fire of divine love kindled in his breast, what else would he speak but burning words? He wrote many little works. Of these I have found only the following, namely:

*Against Eutyches the Presbyter*, two books. *As of the most skilled, most beloved*.

*On the Nicene Creed*, one book. *We believe in one God*.

To Leo Augustus, *With Testimony*, one book. *This one thing, therefore, immovable*.

*To the Inquiries of Bishop Januarius*, one book. *No reason determines that in*.

*On the Passion of the Lord*, twenty-four homilies, five books. *Always indeed, most beloved*.
