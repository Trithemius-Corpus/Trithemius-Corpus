He published several letters to various persons, and also sermons and various treatises, which either have not yet been translated into Latin or have not come to my knowledge. He died in the time of the emperors Valentinian and Valens, in the year of the Lord 379, the forty-sixth year of his priesthood, on the sixth day before the Nones of May, in the seventh indiction.

Theodorus, bishop of Heraclea in Thrace, devoted to and learned in the divine Scriptures, an outstanding declaimer and a man of polished speech, published commentaries on the sacred volumes that are not to be despised in reading. Of these the following are reported.

On the Psalter, one book.
On the Epistles of Paul, fourteen books.
He is also said to have written some other works, but they have not come to my knowledge. He flourished in the times of Constantine II, in the year of the Lord 340.

Eusebius, bishop of Emesa, a man most learned in the divine writings, and, in delivering homilies to the people, second to none in his own time, a rhetorician and theologian most celebrated in the opinion of all. He is said to have written many, indeed almost countless, little works, but we have been able to find the titles and numbers of only a few, namely:

Against the Gentiles, one book.
Sermons to the People, ten books.
Against the Jews, one book.
Many Homilies, one book.
He flourished when Constantine and his brothers were reigning, in the year of the Lord 350.

Triphilius, bishop of Cyprus or of Leucontheon, a man most learned in both divine and secular writings, and easily the most eloquent prelate of his age, is said to have written not a few volumes and to have expounded the divine Scriptures in many ways, but I do not recall that any of his works have come into our hands. On the Song of Songs, one book.
He flourished in the times of the emperor Constantius, in the year of the Lord 340.
