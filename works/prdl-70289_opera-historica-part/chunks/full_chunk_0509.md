*Of Illustrious Men.*

You have excerpted a portion of the luminaries of Germany, adding not a few who meanwhile came to your notice; I read it with all the more pleasure because I would have believed all the less that so great an abundance of excellent men had existed in Germany. I myself added, as you will see, even only the bare names of some, since I was not able to search out their works. Let us entrust this burden to posterity, so that, if there shall ever be in Germany men who love the praise and glory of their native nation, they may add from their own vein where we have now stopped, themselves to take up the work hereafter.

For henceforth uncouth and raw barbarism will not reign among the Germans, nor will it always be proclaimed as true what, in Lucan, Cato is once represented as having said: “Let the two battle-lines pierce me; let the barbarous throng of the Rhine attack me with missiles.” For many people, accustomed to magnify their own glory and diminish that of others, suppose that this barbarism of the Rhine or of Germany prevailed from the times of Julius down to our own age. Whether this is true, would that they might understand by meditating on the words of blessed Jerome, who, among the other calamities of the lands, also laments the miserable ruin of Germany, by no means passing over the lofty cities of the Rhine.

“Mainz,” he says, “once a noble city, has been captured and overthrown; in the church many thousands of people were butchered. The Vangiones were destroyed by a long siege. [Nemetae?] (which is now Speyer), Argentoratum (which is now called Argentina), were ravaged.” Surely [unclear]. Would Jerome have mentioned the disaster or destruction of those cities, if he had not known that in them there were then both most devout worshippers of Christ and men outstanding in learning and wisdom? Is it not a very strong and victorious argument that very many learned men once existed in Germany: so overflowing an abundance of very ancient volumes? Were the authors of those same works unlearned, or the scribes, or those who ordered them to be written, or those who used them every day? Or did a man of such piety grieve more that splendid walls, very strongly fortified places, or wealth were laid waste than that, in those same places, the most flourishing talents of very many people miserably perished?
