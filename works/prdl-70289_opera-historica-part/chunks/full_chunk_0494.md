Hieronymus Donatus, a Venetian patrician, a most distinguished man, devoted to the divine Scriptures, most learned in secular letters, a very celebrated translator of both languages, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, although he is now distracted by the care of wife and children, nevertheless burns with a wonderful diligence and incredible zeal for letters, writing many things day by day and translating many things from Greek; if another man, even one free from every care, were to compose them, they could be something of a marvel.

An oration to the king of Portugal, one book.
Various orations also, one book.
Letters to various persons, one book.
And very many other things.

He translated into Latin John Damascene on the immaculate mysteries; also Dionysius on the Divine Names, and certain letters of his; also the volume On the Soul by Alexander of Aphrodisias the philosopher; and many other things. He lives to this day at Venice, thirty-seven years old, writing and translating various works, under Maximilian, the most illustrious King of the Romans, in the year of the Lord 1494.

Jodocus Badius of Ghent, German by nation, a man most learned in secular letters and not ignorant of the divine Scriptures, a very distinguished philosopher, rhetorician, and poet, excellent in talent and eloquent in speech, who once at Ferrara heard Baptista Guarinus teaching Greek letters, and in that branch of knowledge also became learned. He now adorns Lyons with his learning, and reads the poets publicly and privately, instructing several sons of magnates. He wrote many excellent little works in verse and prose, of which the following are extant. A notable metrical work against vices, which he entitled:

He lives to this day, thirty-two years old, composing many different works under the renowned King Maximilian. In the year of the Lord 1494, indiction 12.

Helias Regnier, a very celebrated jurist, excellently learned both in the divine Scriptures and in secular philosophy, subtle in talent, distinguished in speech, cautious in counsel, who for a long time publicly taught law in the school of Poitiers, wrote certain excellent little works by which he commended his memory to posterity. Of these I have seen only the following, a celebrated work which contains:

Cases of the Sixth Book of the Decretals, one book. “Boniface the Eighth established ...” 40
Cases of the Clementines, two books. “John, bishop, divide ...”

Of the others I have seen nothing. He is still living in the University of Poitiers, under King Maximilian, in the year of the Lord 1494.
