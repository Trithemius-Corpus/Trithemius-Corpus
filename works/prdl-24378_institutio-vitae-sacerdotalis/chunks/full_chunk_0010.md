He, most wretchedly led astray by the savage love of that harlot whom you know, since for fear of her husband he did not dare to live with her openly, despising his country, his home, his relatives, and the benefices he held, so that he might enjoy her freely, fled away with her in secret. And to this very day no one knows where he went.

I know a certain priest from among the concubinaries. When I once rebuked him privately for his sin, I finally brought him to this point: that he was willing to promise perpetual continence henceforth into my hands. And when I urged him to put away his concubine at once, he answered: “I cannot.” “Why?” I said. “If I were to cast her off,” he said, “who would take care of my little children?” For he already had two boys by her. “I am poor,” he said; “I cannot hire anyone for wages to look after them. Therefore I shall not put away their mother until the little children grow up, lest I become guilty of blood. Yet I shall be chaste with her as with a chaste woman, and henceforth I shall not be joined to her in any mingling.” And I said: “What will you do if, before these have grown up, others arise in their place?” He swore that he wished to take care that this should not happen. “You will not amend your fault,” I said, “unless you cast out the concubine. It is difficult to abandon what one is accustomed to, and, when the occasion is present, to abstain from former vices. No one will cherish a serpent in his bosom and remain unharmed.” The man went back to his house, and to this day has not abandoned his domestic crimes.

I considered the works of God: for no one can set right the man whom he has despised. So difficult is it for a priest who has once fallen into fornication to be able to rise again to true chastity, that out of all of them you can scarcely find one. For according to Scripture, he who commits sin is the servant of sin; and he who once submits himself to shameful love is scarcely ever wholly freed. For fornication is more easily fled than amended. Therefore I warn you and beseech you to flee the company of women, lest you lose the reputation of a good name. A priest once permanently defamed for fornication will never recover the honor he has lost. But also avoid conversations with women; shun frequent greetings in the marketplace, because through slight things one comes to the worst deeds. For careless speech with women has deceived many.

Be watchful at all times, and do not ever think that you should trust yourself, so long as you live in the flesh. For we have known very many who fell in old age, who seemed secure to themselves because of their past victory. Indeed temptation is never lacking to a man as long as he lives in this life. But if you cannot manage domestic care without the service of a maidservant, take an upright old woman from your own kin, one from whose living with you no suspicion could arise among the people. Nevertheless, beware of this before all else: do not take a married woman as a maidservant. For what God has joined, let man not separate. The living together of another man’s wife is not free from suspicion of evil. I wish you, as far as possible, to have good testimony from everyone. Let the dangers of others make you cautious; and where you see that many have fallen, do not wish to walk incautiously.

Remember how many very brave and very wise men, from the beginning of the world down to this day, have perished through the company of women. A woman deceived Adam, the first-formed man, and when he had been overthrown she slew us all. From the beginning of the created world, through women the sons of God sinned, and by their crimes brought the cataclysm upon the whole world. Likewise, when the children of Israel were going out from Egypt, the women of the foreigners overthrew them in the desert. Why do we recount these women, when a vile harlot, having captured Samson, the strongest of all men, by her love, handed him over to death and disgrace? Let no one presume on his own strength; let no one trust in former chastity, since often the man who today is seen to stand bravely is tomorrow seen to fall into the depth of fornication. Most wise Solomon could not guard against the deceits of women; who, then, will now be safe? The strong and wise man falls by the sword of women, and we, fragile and foolish, presume upon security? For the one who fell says: “Better is the wickedness of a man than a woman who does good.” It is safer to live with a wicked man than with a good woman.

For the devil, the adversary of chastity, never ceases to lay snares for the chaste man, if he can find material for deceiving him. Since he is cunning, he knows how to dissemble for a time, until at last in a single hour he can overthrow all the labor and reward of past continence.
