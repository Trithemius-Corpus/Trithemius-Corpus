For no reverence is paid to superiors if their life is confined among their subjects. The one who presides must surpass all others in a certain reverence of maturity, and wisely avoid excessive familiarity with his subjects. Indeed, the eminence of authority is broken down when it is indiscreetly mixed with the actions of subjects. Speak ill of no one, and take no scandal against anyone. It is shameful for a priest to defile his tongue with detraction, the tongue which, placed at the altar, is appointed to pray for all. Nor should you lend an ear to detractors, because the detractor and the willing listener are worthy of equal condemnation. For the one carries the devil on his tongue, the other in his ear. Let such a man learn not readily to detract, when he sees that you are unwilling to listen. Do not be hasty in speech, and do not stand in the company of detractors, because they are hateful to almighty God. Hedge your ears with thorns, lest you hear the words of detractors. Let no lie proceed from your mouth; let no foul speech come from you, but be a guardian of your tongue, lest you offend in speech. Do not be slothful and lazy, nor given over to sleep and idleness, but continually go forward in the fervor of the Holy Spirit, knowing that this is proper to a priest: to have care for the needy. Have gold not so that you may keep it, but so that you may distribute it in alms for the poor. Let the bellies of the hungry poor praise you, not the feasts of the belching rich. Let your table be with the poor and needy, for in such persons Christ is received, and the heavenly court is honored. Avoid the banquets of the proud rich, especially of the powerful, in which nothing is done concerning the salvation of souls, and nothing at all is discussed concerning spiritual conversation. Let your clothing be honorable, not costly. In giving alms, do not seek glory from men, but expect praise and reward from God alone. Be a father of the poor and a guardian of orphans.

Let no poor man depart from you unconsoled. Do not be a striker or quarrelsome among the people; injure no one wrongfully, but do good to all as much as you can, even to enemies. Never coddle sinners, but rebuke them as one who has received authority from God. Do not be a striker of the consciences of the people committed to you, nor utter the word of God indiscreetly, but with the moderation of piety. Be merciful to penitents, and do not break the bruised reed, knowing that you have undertaken the care of the weak, not of the strong. For it is better to send a penitent with compassion to purgatory than, by the austerity of the confessor, to consign one driven to despair to hell. Do not neglect to pray your canonical hours with reverence and devotion at the times appointed by the Church. Likewise, do not omit to recite the hours of blessed Mary ever virgin, the vigils of the dead, together with the other things owed by priestly order. Have care of your household in the fear of God; if you have a household, persuade it by word and example to preserve modesty and honor. Indeed, it harms a priest’s reputation among the people when the people speak ill of his household.

Let your speech be strengthened by the testimony of the Scriptures, because whatever is commanded apart from the holy Scriptures is despised with the same ease with which it is proved. I do not want you to imitate those who, without learning, often cite fables and phantoms of dreams in church, and are ignorant of the mysteries of the divine Scriptures. Brawling and garrulous priests, speaking without reason, do not edify the people; by empty speeches and Aesopian fables they labor to draw them into admiration of themselves. For it is a foolish and damnable abuse, with the Scriptures and the Gospel set aside, to teach the Christian people the fables of Aesop and the fabricated deeds of the Romans.
