You therefore, who await the unfading crown of victory, toil like a good soldier of Jesus Christ: let no care of earthly anxiety weigh you down; let no enticement of fleshly pleasure touch your mind. For nothing is happier than the soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised; nothing more toilsome, than one who daily runs risk of his life; nothing stronger, than one who conquers the devil; nothing weaker, than one who is overcome by the flesh. Let not home draw you away; let not kinship hinder you; let not the flesh constrain you. I wish you to be without anxiety, so that you may freely think on the things that are God’s. Naked and unencumbered fly to heaven, because naked your mother bore you, and naked the earth will receive you. Love what you cannot [unclear]; seek where you may rejoice happily for eternity. Live so that you do not fear to die. A good conscience does not fear death, because through it it knows that it will come to the eternal joys of the heavenly homeland. You now have, most beloved brother, what you asked of me; you have a certain pattern of good conduct on which you may imprint yourself; you have a rule of holy exhortation to which you may diligently have recourse. I have done what I could, not as much as I wished. For the rest, live according to the Gospel and the constitutions of the holy fathers, in which the whole form of Christian perfection is seen. Farewell, mindful of us. From Sponheim, on the Kalends of April, in the year 1486. *Here ends the little book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, On the Life of Priests.*
