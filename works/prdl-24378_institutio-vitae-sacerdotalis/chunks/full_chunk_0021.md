For it is, through fear of the people, to keep silent the words of God. A priest must make use of wine sparingly, lest, if he gives himself over to drinking bouts, he become a reproach among the people. For the Apostle condemns wine-bibbing priests, and the old law forbids them access to the altar. In the Council of Agde also it is read thus: Before all things drunkenness is to be avoided by clerics, for it is the tinder and nurse of all vices. And so he who is found to have been drunk, as his rank allows, is either to be removed from communion for the space of thirty days, or subjected to bodily punishment. For wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness brings reproach; everyone who is mingled with these will not be wise. He who burns without wine, with the heat of youth, and is inflamed by the warmth of the blood, and is of vigorous body, should flee wine as poison, lest, suffocated by a manifold conflagration, he fall into the filth of lust.

A stomach heated with wine stirs up desire, dulls the senses, and weakens the head and the whole body. A fat belly begets a thin understanding. Therefore, when you go as an invited guest to banquets of priests, do not forget clerical honor, but show yourself modest and restrained in all things; and take care that neither your tongue nor your itching ears, that is, that neither you yourself detract from others, nor lend your hearing to those who detract. Know that everything you say about others is to be turned back upon yourself, and perhaps you will be found guilty in the very things that you were reproving in others. But perhaps you will say: I cannot do injury to those who report it, for anyone strives in vain who plans to stop the lips of all. What, then, is another's detraction to me? Surely I cannot forbid men to speak? But, as we said above, so present yourself before the detractor that he may learn to detract from no one, when he sees that you do not willingly hear what is being said. Solomon says: Do not associate with detractors, for their destruction will come suddenly. And who knows the ruin of both: of him, that is, who detracts, and of him who lends his ear to the detractor? Be mature in morals and manner of life, sparing and prudent in words, discreet in silence and taciturnity. Be diligent in investigating the mysteries of the Scriptures; and if anything doubtful occurs to you as you read, know that it must be referred to the interpretations of the elders.
