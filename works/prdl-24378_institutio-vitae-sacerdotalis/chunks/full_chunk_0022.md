Let gold not be dearer to you than knowledge, for the fruit of wisdom is eternal, but the use of riches is brief and momentary. Let there be no one who prevents salutary study: not mother, not brother, not sister. You, do the work of an evangelist; learn what you are to teach, lest you turn out to be a shepherd and an idol. You are one, and have no second: for whom, then, would you store up riches? Live temperately; do not frequent weddings and immoderate banquets, for they both strip the mind of virtues and render the purse empty of coins. Procure for yourself, from the alms offered, copies of divine Scripture, by whose constant reading, made learned, you may worthily instruct the people committed to you, and raise your own self by contemplation to heavenly things. Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and it will exalt you before God and men. Do not consent to the pleasures of the flesh; restrain the impulse of fury; and never forget death.

*What sort the priest ought to be in his inner conduct.*
*Chapter 7.*

Since, however, it is not enough for a minister of Christ to lead honorable conduct before men, unless he also strives to appear pure from every crime before God, I exhort you to direct your heart in the fear of the Almighty, and never admit any thoughts against God. For the priest of God must be holy in mind and body, and pure in thought. Hence the Lord says: Be holy, for I am holy, the Lord your God, who sanctify you. And Peter, the chief apostle, says: You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition, that you may proclaim the virtues of him who called you out of darkness into his wonderful light. Let charity hold the first place in your heart, for it is the fullness of the law and the completion of the prophets. For the greatest of all virtues is charity, which consists in love of God and neighbor.
