Nicolaus, know that I would wish myself and my contemporaries to be persuaded to turn wholly to this flourishing little work. For through this we can not only avoid the penalty of guilt, but also obtain the favor of praise. For he who does not apply the scent of his soul to this will quickly be given over to ruin. Wherefore you know that this very thing, before the first printing of its letters, is fruitful for me. Especially since the father and lord of [praised?] understanding, lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, gathered, compiled, and handed it down with no small sweat. He is much more abundant than I am in the manifold grace of letters; watchful in intellect, ready in speech, burning in zeal, and a useful head of household in the house of the Lord. By his flourishing little work he supplies the instruction of holy life, and teaches me, weak as I am, together with all the clergy, unskilled and learned, for caution. And if this instruction of life, brother, is lacking to us, who, I ask, judges that we are living? Come now, Nicolaus, if this is lacking to us, we are not worthy. For in it he supplies to us so fully all the things that are so necessary for us that without them no one is worthy of life. For if, according to the flourishing instruction of our reverend father in the Lord, Trithemius, which is in hand, we have established ourselves wholly so long as we live here, true salvation is truly promised to us, and it is never taken away from any who live a life worthy of it. Therefore all who are to be regarded as worthy must order their life according to what is handed down in this flourishing little work, so that they may not die, but may live thereafter forever. That you may most worthily comprehend this, Nicolaus, farewell always in him who created all things; and may he preserve by his mercy us who cannot stand by our own powers. Amen. Given in the Studium of Mainz, in the year of our salvation one thousand four hundred ninety-four, in the month of October, on the twenty-second day of the same.

*Here begins the prologue of the treatise of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the holy father Benedict, On the Life of Priests, addressed to Nicolaus of Mernick, priest.*

You ask me, Nicolaus, most beloved brother, and press me with frequent letters, to commit to writing for you a form of living laudably in the priesthood. You compel me to labor beyond the powers of my intellect, and expose me to be torn by the bites of rivals, who on this account will judge me presumptuous, because I wish to show you the way on which I myself have not yet learned to walk. And although the matter stands thus, nevertheless I prefer to undergo the judgment of rivalry rather than suffer detriment to my charity toward you. For I have always cherished you with such affection that I have never been accustomed to deny you anything you asked. Therefore, desiring to satisfy your prayers, I wished to commend to you a rule of living from the writings of the saints: not because it would be wholly sufficient for your state in every respect, but so that, as some sort of beginning, it may lead you, if you wish, to honorable conduct. First, therefore, I admonish you, from the definition of the name, to weigh the state of the conduct enjoined. For we are called clerics, all of us who have been specially chosen into the lot of the Lord. Let us therefore interpret the word of the name, and we shall understand the quality of the prescribed honorableness. For cleros in Greek is interpreted in Latin as lot. From cleros, therefore, clerics are said to be chosen into the Lord’s lot. If, then, we recognize that we have been called into the lot of God, why do we still serve the world? Why do we seek an inheritance in the land of the dying, we who await God as the one promising himself to us? For in the Old Testament we read that the Lord commanded the priests and Levites not to receive an inheritance among the children of Israel, their brothers, because he himself was their inheritance. Now therefore, if you consider yourself to be a cleric, you ought to walk more honorably than other men in the excellence of your conduct, and by no means love the perishable riches of this world. But if you subject your soul to the inheritance of the age, you will be placed with the foolish outside the Lord’s lot. For the Lord will not deign to place you among his elect.
