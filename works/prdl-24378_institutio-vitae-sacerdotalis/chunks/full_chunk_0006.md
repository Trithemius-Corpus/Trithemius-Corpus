Thus the ecclesiastical pastor should, by a good life, offer his sheep an example of righteousness; by salutary teaching he should feed the minds of his hearers, lest, if he does one without the other, he either neglect himself or conduct the care of the flock unfruitfully.

Alas, what shall I say of our priests, unlearned and vicious, greedy, lustful, and utterly wicked? I am ashamed to speak, and I cannot keep silent. Look to yourself, dearest Nicholas, lest you become an imitator of those who, despising the books of the Scriptures, devote the whole day to games and drinking parties; who stain themselves by intercourse with women; who, by their depraved morals, scandalize many in the law of God. Be among the people of God a model of honesty; in all things, as far as possible, show yourself blameless, so that you remove every occasion for evil suspicion concerning you. For one must have testimony even from those who are outside, because the word of a preacher is easily despised when his life is marked among the people by evil suspicions. *On Fleeing the Company of Women. Chapter II.*

But what shall I say concerning familiarity with women, which is most pernicious to the priests of God, when the decrees of the holy fathers forbid it to them with sufficiently strict prohibition? If I set before you the admonitions of the most blessed father Jerome, you will at once cry out: “I cannot govern a household without the ministry of a maidservant; I am not able to prepare food for myself; I do not wish to clean dirty clothes. Why,” you say, “do you cite to me the precepts of Jerome, which bind no one unless he wishes? Who appointed Jerome as my lawgiver?”

The holy universal Church, I say, approves his words; it places among the canons his precepts laid down on this matter; and do you presume to mutter against so great an authority? But you would say: “Contrary custom has abrogated this law, and from long time it is to be regarded as law.” But wickedness lies to itself, and by foolish consideration is deceived in itself. A custom contrary to good morals can never either remove or establish a law. And what is so contrary to priestly honesty as to delight in the company of young women? Chastity is endangered where the fellowship of priests passes among women. For the first and strongest temptations of clerics arise from frequent access to women. Foolish presumption has overthrown many, who, so to speak, handed over to their enemies the instruments of victory with their own hands. Danger is avoided with the greatest difficulty if the occasion is not avoided.

Indeed, while a woman dwells with a cleric, and sits alone with him alone, prepares food for him as he eats, arranges the little bed for him as he sleeps, speaks to him, smiles at him, and administers the incitements of lust: however holy the woman may be, she must always be feared by a cleric. For just as sparks leap out from coals, so the poison of desire is spread from constant familiarity with women. A familiar woman is, to a chaste man, the door of the devil, the gate of hell, the way of iniquity, the dread stroke of a scorpion, and a harmful kind of deception.
