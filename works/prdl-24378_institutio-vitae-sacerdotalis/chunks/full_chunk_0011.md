Then, from that point, they begin to speak more familiarly; they disclose to one another all their more secret matters; little by little they grow accustomed to touching one another with their hands. Finally, through mutual laughter, future unions are prepared; and while by custom they live familiarly, they suddenly fall into the pit of the devil. But afterward, when they have once fallen into mutual guilt, they seem to themselves to shrink somewhat from the deed, but they cannot easily draw back from what has been begun. They therefore continue on the road on which they had set out; and although in the beginning conscience abhors the crime, nevertheless by habit it gradually bears it more lightly. And at first, indeed, they begin to behave wantonly with a certain shamefaced fear; but thereafter, blinded by depraved habit, they are no longer ashamed. Meanwhile the belly, aware of the secret, begins to swell, and to make public the crime that had lain hidden. At last a little infant is born; for a short time father and mother blush, and immediately return to their accustomed crimes. Then, from that point, all shame is cast far away; the eye of conscience is blinded; the fear of God and of hell is utterly driven from memory. They now live licentiously in sins, to the point that he is made a slave to lust, so that he seems to himself unable to live without a concubine. He can indeed amend [unclear]; concubinarii rarely come to the remedies of saving penance. He can indeed promise to amend. But while, amid so many crimes, he puts off the will to do penance, he loses both the time and the ability alike. Hence it comes about that [unclear] priests [unclear] are maddened by diabolical enticements, so that they are able to think of nothing but carnal things and care nothing about future recompense. They love a criminal concubine more than eternal happiness. What sort of penance do you think these men perform at the end, when they are pressed not by will but by necessity? Those who have consumed the whole time of their life in lust rarely obtain true penance in the final moments of their life. For even if the late penance of such persons, by God’s mercy, is sometimes genuine, nevertheless they will not be able to reach the kingdom of heaven without the most grievous torments of purgatory. Finally, if revelations are to be believed, the very great multitude of those being tormented in the underworld suffers, on account of the vice of incontinence, unspeakable punishments that will endure forever. You see whither an incautious familiarity with women draws a cleric: it condemns him to the eternal torments of hell. Therefore the beginnings of temptations must be resisted, because lust is never better conquered than if it is avoided by flight. For if it has once entangled you through habit, it never lets you go free without the special grace of God. If you hesitate to give faith to our words, seek examples, since you can find proofs before your eyes. Behold, today we know many old priests whose life we know to be incontinent. We see age growing old in them, and the vices of youth not ceasing at all. And when they come to extreme old age, they do not give up their depraved habits, but as they have always been from the beginning, so too they go to death. You would be amazed that among them priests already decrepit have drunk in the poison of lust to such a degree that, although because of old age they can no longer behave wantonly in deed, they burn toward accustomed things in thought, word, and nods. Of such persons the Lord says through the prophet: If an Ethiopian can change his skin, and a leopard his spots, then you too will be able to do good, when you have learned evil. In old men, indeed, concubinage with women can cease, but there remains, as before, the desire for the act. Such as you desire to be read as an old man, so now also you should live as a young man. For it is difficult for an old man to correct his habits and to think new thoughts in an old mind. Indeed, old age has habits; it does not receive them.
