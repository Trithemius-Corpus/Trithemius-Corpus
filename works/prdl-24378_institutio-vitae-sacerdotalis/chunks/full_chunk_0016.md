For the Holy Spirit, who teaches man knowledge, is a lover of humility. When you sit at table to take food, remember that the sacred writings must first be sounded aloud, so that while the flesh is fed, the mind may by no means be defrauded of its own nourishment. In the Council of Toledo it is read thus: “For reverence for God and for priests, the whole synod established this: that, because idle tales are often wont to be inserted at meals, a reading of the divine Scriptures should also be joined to every priestly banquet. For by this both souls are built up toward the good, and tales are prohibited [unclear].” For the word of God is the food of the spirit, because man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God. The ancient fathers and priests bestowed the greatest diligence on the holy Scriptures, so that they might both nourish the mind healthfully and provide useful things for the instruction of posterity. Let us cling to their footsteps, so that we may deserve to be found worthy ministers before God.

*On avoiding avarice and greed.*

*Chapter 5.*

Although at the beginning of our discourse we argued against avarice and greed, nevertheless we are led by a certain reason to speak again in this place about this very matter. For action is never taken sufficiently against this vice unless it is utterly rooted out. But our Savior, knowing how difficult it is for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God, said to those following him: “Unless someone renounces all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple.” Hence the psalmist [unclear]. Having food and clothing, let us be content with these. What more do we seek, we who are ministers of God? Greed is disgraceful in a priest of God, avarice is disgraceful, and the continual hunger for gold is damnable. Therefore you, most beloved brother, if you desire to live in freedom of spirit, do not [unclear] money.
