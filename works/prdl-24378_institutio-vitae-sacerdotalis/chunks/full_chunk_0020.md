Nothing is so easy as to deceive the low and unlearned common crowd by fluency of tongue; it admires all the more whatever it does not understand. But you, speak the things that befit sound doctrine, and do not seek the applause of the inexperienced if you wish to find a place among the learned. Do not imprudently desire the care of souls, unless first you have learned justly to govern both yourself and others according to ecclesiastical ordinance. For the office of teaching the people is exposed to many dangers and is troubled by daily anxieties. For a ruler of souls must be without fault, as God’s steward: not proud, not wrathful, not given to wine, not a striker, not greedy for shameful gain, but hospitable, kindly, sober, just, holy, continent, embracing that faithful word which is according to doctrine, so that he may be able to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict it. He who has been appointed ruler of souls must teach no less by example than by word. A thief, indeed, can accuse avarice; but no one can correct it except the man who does what he teaches. He is a dainty teacher who disputes about fasting with a full belly. Therefore life must be required before doctrine, because one teaches more fully by deed than by speech. For the most excellent kind of teaching is the demonstration of example. And indeed the Lord Jesus first acted, afterward taught. For in teaching he uses the greatest authority whose speech is confirmed by the witness of his work. The speech of a ruler is readily received by subjects if it is supported by honorable conduct. But he who teaches well and lives badly condemns himself by the sentence of his own mouth and does not turn his subjects away from bad instruction. Consider at the same time how dangerous the care of souls is, especially in these times, when iniquity abounds everywhere and the charity of many has grown cold. For the days are evil, and men are slow to justice; the whole world is set in wickedness, and faith on earth is rare. Amid these things the word of God is despised by the people, the instruction of the Christian religion is neglected, priestly authority is scorned. The life of the laity, liable to a thousand sins and crimes, neglects the remedies of penance, applies itself to deceits and frauds, and despises the admonitions of priests.

And unless the ruler of souls diligently announces to the people entrusted to him their crimes and the penalties owed to sinners, before God he will also be guilty of their blood. And after these things God will bring all things into judgment, where the shepherd of souls is compelled to render an account for each person; and if he has been negligent in this world, he is condemned more severely. Therefore it is safer humbly to flee the care and rule of souls than arrogantly to usurp it. For if each person in that dreadful judgment is scarcely sufficient to render an account of his own life, how will he answer for so many thousands entrusted to him? O how well he lives who lives for himself. It seems honorable and pleasant among men to live in this world as a bishop, abbot, pastor, or any prelate at all; but who will be able to think how terrible the end of the negligent man is? For a very harsh judgment will be made against those who preside, and the powerful will suffer torments powerfully. Behold, he who lives simply passes through, to whom no earthly honor is entrusted, to whom no care of souls is committed. For pastoral care is never without danger so long as one lives in this world. And the greatest of all dangers is when, the soul having been separated from the body by death, the earthly shepherd must be presented to God, the supreme shepherd, to give an account. Here now the moment of final damnation threatens, when an account of badly administered stewardship is demanded and the blood of many sheep is required from the hand of an unworthy shepherd. What then will an inexperienced shepherd be able to answer to the charges, he who was negligent in the care of the sheep? He must fall silent, because he has nothing to answer. Therefore, brother, if you consider yourself fit for the care of souls, if you are preparing to govern the church of Mernicensem, know that you are to be brought into judgment for these things, and according to the merit of your life will receive either glory or torment. But believe me, he dies secure who, leaving aside concern for others, lives for himself alone in the fear of God. For to whom much is entrusted, much also will be required from him. A priest, especially one having the care of souls, is God’s herald, to announce to the people the will of the Almighty and to heal sinners in the law of the Lord by words of rebuke.
