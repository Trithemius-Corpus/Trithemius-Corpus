Thomas Ruscher of [Ganuudia], professor of sacred letters, unworthy preacher of the evangelical word in the church of Mainz, to the well-famed and Catholic priest Nicolaus Mernicensis, perpetual greeting.

Lest, most learned Nicolaus, I should seem cold in rendering thanks before Him from whom comes every best gift and every perfect gift. First there occurs the Scripture of Deuteronomy, chapter [unclear]: “I have set life and death before your face.” I give thanks because today He grants this also to him who cleaves to Him who is true life. I speak of Him who recently, in my times, breathed into the face of a certain man a breath of new life, for which I render thanks to the Creator of all. And to you, O father Trithemius of praised understanding, because you have chosen life not only for yourself, but also establish my life and that of many, so that they may live, in your so flourishing little work of instruction, which, as soon as it had been commonly brought to me, I made room for among the other things, as one needing a breath of life, with affection of mind and desire of soul, until I might look within.

But, O father of praised understanding, what you have accomplished in me, most devoted to you, through this so flourishing little work of your instructions, or what I felt while reading it, I would not want to be hidden from my contemporaries. For when I proceeded to its inner parts, it so flowed in me that my cheeks grew wet. O brother Nicolaus Mernicensis, most devoted to prayer and charity, how happy, how holy was your petition for this flourishing little work. O man and son of the bride of Christ, Nicolaus Mernicensis, and confessor of God, how necessary in these stormy times especially are the things contained in this little work, where the Christian faith is torn and oppressed by harmful errors that are everywhere sown and sprout up. Necessary, I say, beyond measure. With this flourishing instruction for the priestly pilgrimage cast aside, all vices and disgraces arise. For I know by the tradition of our elders that the soul of man is nourished by understanding and knowledge of old things as by a kind of food. I rightly judge the minds of those who are instructed by no learning, no instruction in morals or life, and have imbibed nothing of the good arts, to be fasting and as it were starving; yet their minds are full, but full of vices and wickedness.

*MONACENSIS.*

Nicolaus, know that I would wish myself and my contemporaries to be persuaded to turn ourselves wholly to this flourishing little work. For through this we can not only avoid the penalty of crime, but also obtain the grace of praise. For he who does not apply the scent of his soul to it will quickly be given over to ruin. Therefore you know that this very thing was fruitful to me before the first printing of its letters, especially since the father and lord of praised understanding, lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, with no small sweat, gathered, compiled, and handed it down. He is, by the varied grace of letters, far more abundant than I am; watchful in genius, ready in eloquence, burning in zeal, and a useful father of a household in the house of the Lord. Ministering instruction in holy life in his flourishing little work, he instructs me, weak as I am, and at the same time every unskilled and learned cleric, for caution.

And surely, brother, if this instruction of life is lacking to us, who, I ask, judges that we live? If this, Nicolaus, is lacking to us, we are not worthy. For in this he supplies everything to us so fully that without these things no one is worthy of life. For if, according to the flourishing instruction of our reverend father in the Lord, Trithemius, which is in hand, we have wholly established ourselves while we live here, true salvation is truly promised to us, and it is never taken away from any who live a life worthy of it. Therefore all who are to be regarded as worthy must establish their life in such a way according to the things handed down in this flourishing little work, so that they may not die, but may live further forever.

That you may most worthily comprehend this, Nicolaus, farewell always in Him who created all things; and may He preserve us, who cannot stand by our own powers, by His mercy. Amen. Given in the University of Mainz, in the year of our salvation 1494, in the month of October, on the twenty-second day of the same.
