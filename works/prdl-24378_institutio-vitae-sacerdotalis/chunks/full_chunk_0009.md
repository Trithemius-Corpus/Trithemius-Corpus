What is done after friendship has been renewed, you may be allowed rather to think than for me to say. The fear of God is not before the eyes of a priest bound by the chains of a concubine; there is no dread of the pains of hell, no thought of priestly dignity, no remembrance of public honor. This vice of incontinence has now so prevailed in the clergy that, because it is public, it has almost begun to be lawful. Miserable is the condition of the human race: those who ought to have corrected the crimes of others commit graver ones. Shameful to say, priests publicly defamed by intercourse with their concubines sit in villages and cities; so great a number of them is found today that, if any prelate wished to correct such men in his diocese alone, he would be frightened away from his purpose, overcome by their sheer multitude. If you were to enter the house of one of such men, you would sometimes come upon the priest carrying in his arms his own little child, witness of his father’s fornication, and, while reading the canonical hours, comforting the crying child with words and songs. You would also see the ill-starred father, rising from bed, take the child upon his knees and, with kisses offered, place him on his right knee and the breviary on his left. While praying he smiles at the laughing infant, soothes him when he cries, and addresses him, when silent, with frequent words and nods. Meanwhile the fond mother comes up; the dear pledge snatches the book from the father’s hand, handles the leaves, tears them, and with saliva flowing from his mouth, by his begetter’s permission, stains them. Amid these things there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention applied to the words, but all concern is spent in comforting the one who cries. The words fall confused from the mouth of the one praying, interrupted by the frequent kisses of the infant, so that when the prayer is completed he scarcely remembers that he has prayed. Meanwhile the sacristan pulls the bell; the altar is prepared for sacrifice; the priest is awaited by the people in the church. The priest runs up, his hands washed in the boy’s chamber-pot, puts on the sacred vestments with all possible haste, and performs the sacrifice of God with a distracted mind. O conscience, where do you remain meanwhile, where do you lie despised? You wretched man, who handle the Son of God with polluted hands: how long do you wound, tear, and destroy your soul? With what rashness, finally, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul? Unless the great mercy of almighty God spared you for a time, the angels, provoked to grave indignation, would strike you down at the altar. Where is the vow of perpetual chastity that you promised into the hands of the bishop when he ordained you? Where is the reverence owed to the priesthood you assumed? You have sinned: why do you not do penance? How long do you despise the long-suffering of God? Or do you not know that the patience of God leads you to penance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the just judgment of Jesus Christ. I think indeed that you plan to repent in the future, but you give no thought to how near death is, the cutter-off of repentance. Truly the merciful God, who promised pardon to the penitent, did not pledge tomorrow’s day to the one who delays. Moreover, a cleric whom a cohabiting woman has once led away captive she never, or rarely, lets go free unless death intervenes. I pass over the fact that many are often driven mad to such a degree by the tricks and sorceries of their concubines that they cannot in any way shake themselves free from their most shameful love of them. The hearing shudders at the crimes of harlots, nor does the integrity of chastity allow one to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by the concubines of priests. They make a stew for their masters from menstrual blood, and, while they desire to lure them to love of themselves, they turn them toward colic and madness. With horrible sorceries and superstitions they entangle priests in their own lusts until, their strength gradually exhausted, they render them wholly useless. Blessed is the man whom God has guarded from an evil and lustful woman. There is no wickedness above the malice of a woman who enjoys evil loves. Whatever she devises she tries; whatever she can do she dares. No one believes by what efforts, ways, and contrivances she acts so that the man whom she plans to deceive she may cast down into her pleasures with an indissoluble bond. She joins herself to her master according to his wishes, not because she loves, but because she desires. For the crowd of concubines follows the prey, not the priest. As long as he is rich, he abounds in everything; she does not leave her master’s side. But if he begins to lack bread, the concubine turns herself to another. Sometimes, indeed, on account of their children, the priest and the concubine are so glued together that no means [unclear] to separate them from each other except by death.
