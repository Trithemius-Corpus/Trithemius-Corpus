Again he also says: From the hope of an everlasting inheritance they take joy, whom the adversity of temporal life humbles; for if divine dispensation did not perceive that they were to be saved forever, it would not thus restrain them, to be instructed, under the rule of discipline. When afflicted we lose earthly things; but, humbly enduring affliction, we multiply heavenly goods for ourselves. For the present life is a road, by which we hasten toward our fatherland; and therefore we are often touched in it by disturbances, lest we think this is our fatherland.

Let the monk therefore rejoice in his adversity; let him be glad when tempted, he to whom the world has been crucified. Let him trust that he belongs to the fellowship of God, who with a pure conscience always works good and receives the contrary in tribulation. For it is the truest sign of divine mercy if a Christian person is struck in this world by various adversities, while serving God with a pure conscience and living in true humility without offense. For the merciful God often strikes in various ways in this world those whom he loves, for whom he has prepared eternal rewards in heaven. But some, knowing that they are able to sin greatly, he forestalls with bodily weakness lest they sin, and in mercy scourges them for their salvation, perceiving that it is more useful for their souls [unclear] the riches of the world and [unclear]; and the unexpected enemy struck him the more heavily because long use of quiet had found him negligent.

Whence holy Pope Gregory again says: Their own prosperity has laid many low more badly; long-lasting peace had rendered many conquered. By a certain moderation of heavenly dispensation they rejoice that they are exercised even by temptations, because they guard the received glory of virtues so much the more robustly as, shaken by the assault of temptation, they more humbly recognize their own weakness. The way of tribulation is a short road to Christ, and he is found more quickly than in tranquillity in the heart.

For Saint Ambrose says: There are many who seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and there are those who seek him in persecutions and quickly find him. In the dangers of his faithful he is present, as he speaks in the psalm concerning the just man, saying: I am with him in tribulation; I will rescue him and glorify him. Let purity of conscience be guarded in adversity; let the unviolated integrity of life always be preserved, and whatever adversities there may be will undoubtedly be for us unto the merit of salvation. The ancient enemy can overthrow no one by fighting, unless he has first found him to have fallen away from himself. He seeks out the soldier who is sluggish in leisure and careless, and most easily lays low the one whom he has found stripped of arms.

Therefore, my brothers, always keep watch in holy prayers; always persist in good works, so that whenever the tempter approaches, he may never find you idle. Remember the words of our Savior Jesus Christ, saying in the holy Gospel: Let your loins be girded and lamps burning in your hands; and you be like men awaiting their lord, when he returns from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may open to him at once. He keeps watch who, standing firm in the fear of the Lord, never sleeps through sluggishness of mind. He keeps watch who preserves his soul, by the indefatigable constancy of a holy purpose, against any adversities whatever of this world. He keeps watch, whoever does not cease to make daily progress in the love of the Lord. He carries a lamp in his hands, whoever adorns the purpose of holiness with fitting works. He also holds a lamp in his hands, whoever, purified in mind and body, shines for his neighbor by works of holy devotion.

Hence the holy lover of Christ, Paul, says in the epistle: I beseech you, dearest ones, by the mercy of God, that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God. He who chastises his body through the pursuits of holy abstinence and reduces it to servitude to the spirit has consecrated a living sacrifice to the Lord. Whoever, moreover, has now learned to preserve his body clean and pure from every pollution of sin has undoubtedly sacrificed a holy victim to the Lord. But whoever perseveres in the purpose of holiness unto the end has without doubt offered a pleasing sacrifice.
