Therefore holy Christophilus says: “We are debtors, brothers, not to the flesh, that we should live according to the flesh. For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” Therefore he serves God rightly who gives to each what is his.

Then we are commanded to have our feet shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that the good which we ourselves have come to know by the Lord’s generosity we may also cheerfully share with others. For our law, brothers, according to which we are commanded to serve the Lord, is the gospel of Jesus Christ, to which all rules and constitutions of cloistered persons are ordered. For monastic constitutions were not made on account of the gospel, but the statutes of monks were made on account of the gospel. For a Christian who is not a monk can be saved, but a monk cannot be saved unless he has been a Christian. Let certain excessively superstitious and foolish cloistered persons be ashamed, who value their rule and statutes more than the gospel of Christ; who keep the traditions of men, customs leading to prisons and chains, but pay no heed to the constitutions of God and the law of the universal Church. The words of our Savior, speaking to the Jews according to Matthew, rightly apply to them: “Why do you also transgress the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition?” For evangelical doctrine must be preferred by far to all the constitutions of the whole world. But after the gospel the statutes of the universal Church hold the first place of authority, and no constitutions or rules of monks are comparable to them in dignity. Let us take care to live according to evangelical purity, and we shall be perfect in the monastic way of life of the fathers.

Let our feet always be shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that we may put to death in ourselves the desires of the flesh, and through love of God and neighbor have peace with all. For without peace and the concord of brotherhood, mortification of the flesh is of no benefit, as Saint Jerome says: “Whoever afflicts the body but abandons concord does indeed praise God with timbrels, but he does not praise him in the choir.” Hence peaceful Solomon, praising the man, says in Proverbs: “Better is a dry morsel with joy than a house full of victims with strife.” And again

And let him who receives it hold it fast; let him who loses it seek it again; let him who has cast it away inquire after it, because whoever is not found in that same peace will be disowned by the Father, disinherited by the Son, and nevertheless made a stranger to the Holy Spirit. He cannot now approach the inheritance of the Lord who has refused to observe the testament of peace. The place of God is in peace, but the dwelling of the devil is in anger and disturbance. Therefore every man who does not have this threefold peace in his heart, who does not offer it with his mouth, nor show it in his works, is to be called neither a monk nor a Christian.

Now the Lord says in the Gospel: “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God.” If those who make peace are called sons of God, without doubt those who confound peace are sons of Satan. Therefore, my dearest brothers, have among yourselves that peace which the world cannot give, which is the peace of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, of which Isaiah the prophet says: “And the work of justice shall be peace, and the service of justice silence and security forever.” For God is the Lord of peace and love; therefore whoever violates peace and brotherly love is made manifest as not being a true lover of God. If the true peace of Christ is in your hearts, you will fear no adversities of this world. Among you also let there be true peace, not feigned, because Christ desires peace not from the mouth but from the heart, and wishes one’s neighbor to be greeted with affection rather than with lips. Therefore if any one of you has saddened his brother in anything, let him make satisfaction to him as soon as possible before sunset; and take care, all of you, that the sun not set upon your anger, because inveterate anger generates hatred and strips the soul of every adornment of virtues. Therefore, monk, if you have sinned against your brother, quickly do penance before him, and falling at his knees beg pardon.
