*Homily XXV.*
*Fol. LVII.*

Come now, my brothers, so that you may have consciences pure from every crime; and if anyone feels himself burdened in some offense, let him return without delay, through true penitence, to the necessary purity. For unless at all times you have clean and good consciences, you live as apostates, not monks.

*On Continual Preparation for Death. Homily XXV.*

The highest philosophy of cloistered men, my dearest brothers, is continual meditation on death. Hence a certain wise man, considering the usefulness of this philosophy, admonished every mortal, saying: Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.

Meditation on death draws a man back from the pleasures of the flesh and brings on contempt for all corruptible things. Meditation on death despises present things through voluntary poverty and drives the mind toward desire for things to come. Meditation on death drives pride far away and plants true humility in the heart of the one meditating. Meditation on death makes the monk solicitous about the observance of regular discipline and never permits him to be idle. Meditation on death cuts off all the vanities of the world and preserves the mind in the highest temperance. Meditation on death makes one despise the vain and transitory honors of the world and think of nothing but the future judgment of God. Meditation on death does not fear the poverty of this life, because it desires eternal riches with its whole longing. Meditation on death endures all adversities of this world most patiently for the love of Christ, because it hopes for nothing but the heavenly reward.

Meditation on death makes the monk humble and obedient in all commands of rectitude. Meditation on death makes the monk quiet and stable in the congregation, and well in concord with all in Christ. Meditation on death makes the monk quiet and peaceful, and tranquil without any disturbance of mind. Meditation on death exterminates all dissolutions and levities, and preserves the monk mature and salutarily sorrowful in all things. Meditation on death makes the monk solitary and continually devoted to holy prayers. Meditation on death hastens penitence and brings in true compunction. Meditation on death withdraws desire from every vanity and keeps the conscience serene and most pure. Meditation on death makes a man despise all the comforts of this world and prepare daily for departure. Meditation on death teaches one to flee idleness and makes a man watch continually in the fear of the Lord.

Meditation on death does injury to no one, because it always fears the strict account of judgment. Meditation on death mitigates anger and all disordered movements, because when a man thinks that he will soon die, nothing external disturbs the mind. Meditation on death makes the heart contrite and humble, because attentive memory of future things permits no one to be proud. Continual meditation on death makes one’s departure safe at the last, because it has made the one meditating over a long time prepared.

Meditation on death is especially fitting for cloistered men and monks, whom renunciation of all earthly things and the very profession of the rule have already made dead and buried to the world. Meditation on death [unclear] to run prudently in the warfare and to renew daily the purpose of holy fervor. Continual meditation on death is very useful to cloistered men, because it both powerfully represses the vices of the flesh and grants no entrance to perverse thoughts of the mind.

For the holy bishop Augustine says: Fear concerning future death necessarily [concutit?] the mind, and, like a nail of the flesh, fastens all movements of pride to the wood of the cross. And the holy champion of the Church Jerome says: Nothing will profit you so much for temperance in all things as frequent thought of the shortness of life, whose end is certain, but nothing is more uncertain than its hour. Therefore meditation on death corrects all intemperance and orders the conduct of the one meditating, stable in beautiful variety. Meditation on death grants true wisdom to a man and perfects the highest philosophy among cloistered men. For it was also Plato’s opinion that the whole life of the wise is meditation on death, because we are all born under this condition: that from here we depart again through death.
