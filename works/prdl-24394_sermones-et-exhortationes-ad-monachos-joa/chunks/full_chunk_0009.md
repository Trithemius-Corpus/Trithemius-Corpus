*On the progress of spiritual warfare.*

Whatever raging things of the world [there may be], he will remain. The true lover of God rejoices in tribulations, is glad in adversities, exults in persecutions for the sake of justice. He follows his king, seated in heaven; there he dwells in mind and in the desire of his soul, where he does not doubt that his savior reigns.

Whatever pleasure and delights are seen anywhere in the world produce, for the servant of Christ who burns with true charity, a stench and horror as of the vilest dung, because he loves nothing earthly, nothing transitory, nothing perishable, but him alone to whom he has devoted himself, with the whole intention of his mind.

For the true soldier of Christ thinks nothing of the things of the world; he cares not for the flesh but for the mind, as one who is not ignorant of what is written: “No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself.” Indeed, all who serve an earthly emperor always obey his commands in everything, and immediately, without contradiction, go wherever he has ordered. All follow him as one, and whatever he has commanded in the name of the king, no one in the whole army contradicts.

You also, soldiers of the king of ages, who have appointed me as your leader and instructor in his place, hear through my mouth the command of your emperor Jesus Christ, and do what he commands us in the holy Gospel. Hear what he orders, and consider how you ought to serve as soldiers in the observance of regular discipline. “He who ministers to me,” he says, “let him follow me.”

Listen, monk, bearing the cloistered campaigns: the heavenly emperor, whom you serve at the altar, speaks to you. If you wish to be my minister, you must follow the virtue of patience and humility which you have learned from me. For I have given you an example, O my soldiers, so that as I have done, you also should do likewise. Charity, O monk, compelled me to be born in the world as man from a virgin; let that same charity preserve you always pure and humble in the cloister for love of me. The Father also enjoined upon me the work of human salvation; for your sake I strove always, with unceasing meditation, to bring it to completion; and you, if you love me, take care to preserve inviolate until death the holy purpose of monastic life which you have undertaken for your sins.

I was always voluntarily subject and obedient to my heavenly Father in all things, even to the most bitter death of the cross; you also, if you wish to come to me, humbly obey your abbot, established in my place, always in all things according to the rule. I have never transgressed in anything the commandment which my Father gave me; and unless you preserve inviolate until death the three principal vows of monastic institution, you are by no means to be numbered among my ministers and soldiers.

Though I was rich, for your salvation I became poor and needy; but unless you, for love of me, have despised and relinquished all things, you will not be able to be my disciple. Poverty, hunger, thirst, heat, cold, and continual labors in the world I endured for your sake; unless in these things, according to your rule and to the best of your strength, you have followed me patiently and voluntarily, you will certainly be neither my minister nor my soldier nor my monk. For in your patience you will possess your soul.

I desired nothing in this world, possessed nothing, loved nothing earthly, but, walking poor, humble, and meek, I always in all things subjected myself most perfectly to my Father’s will, ready to receive injuries and blows. But you, O cloistered monk, who profess the monastic rule under an abbot or prior: if from now on you follow the desires of your flesh; if you possess private property or ownership in the monastery; if you love anything besides me; if you despise the commands of your superior; if you do not serve me perseveringly in humility, know for certain that I shall not have you as a soldier, nor count you as monk, minister, or disciple, but, judging you a fugitive, apostate, faithless man, liar, mocker, perjurer, and unbeliever, I shall condemn you eternally without mercy, as you have deserved, with my betrayer Judas and the fallen angels.

What your lips vowed to me is no longer yours but mine; but if you usurp for yourself what is mine, you do not wish to be my monk and faithful servant, but a thief and wicked robber, impious and unjust; and do not doubt that, when I come, I shall judge you such.
