*Sermon VII.*

... did not guard against them. No sin is so small that it is not made greater by neglect. For although we cannot wholly guard against venial sins, nevertheless we ought to take care with all our strength that they do not multiply in us. But those who, by the Lord’s mercy, make daily progress toward better things in this degree fall into sin more rarely. Yet as often as they have fallen, so often they rise again through penitence. Still, their state is close to danger, because, just as a garment torn too often is at last rendered useless, so a man who slips by sinning too often is frequently choked by evil habit. Yet if any depart from this life in this degree, by the Lord’s mercies, they will be saved not without the very grave pains of purgatory. Therefore one must hasten to higher things, so that not only mortal sins but also venial sins may be guarded against as far as one has strength.

The second degree is believed to be ascended by those who strive, as far as they can, to avoid both venial and mortal crimes. But through excessive commerce with bodily weakness they do not persevere in their purpose, but frequently fall into the various negligences of this life, although they do not commit mortal sins. We who are lukewarm trust that we have somehow ascended to this degree: we who, placed under the observance of regular discipline, guard our minds from the graver sins with the Lord’s help. For because we are excessively lukewarm, we live very negligently and are slow to practise the exercises of holy virtues. But there are many established in the region of this second degree whom servile fear occupies; if it alone is present, it approaches the kingdom of God very little. For obtaining the glory of heavenly felicity, servile fear without charity will not suffice, because he is a slave who does not sin from fear of punishment, but a son does not do evil from the fear of God. Yet it is good to begin from servile fear, so that a man may gradually be able to arrive at filial fear, which is called chaste. For although to withdraw from evil from fear of punishments is quite insufficient for obtaining the kingdom of God, nevertheless it prepares the worker for filial fear. Hence it is the lowest degree of those making progress not to offend from fear of punishment, and not to sin lest they be condemned forever. For charity alone, discerning the merits of all, is admitted as fit to enter into the kingdom of God, and whatever has been done outside it is judged unworthy of the kingdom of God. Therefore whoever you are who have deserved to ascend according to this second degree, whoever you are who have learned not to sin from fear of punishments, if you wish to attain the joy of eternal felicity, take care to hasten as quickly as possible to higher things. For because, as Scripture testifies, no one suddenly becomes highest, we rightly permit you to begin from the lower things; but we judge it dangerous to stand long in the valley of shadows. Ascend, therefore, as much as you can each day, and do not wish to remain with beasts in the dung of foul-smelling actions, because if you do not make progress, you have failed; if you do not ascend, you descend.

The third degree is comprised by those who, persevering in a good purpose, guard themselves from every mortal sin and also withdraw from venial sins as far as they can. They love purity of conscience and in no way commit anything that could stain it. Yet in one thing they are lower than those who follow, because, not withdrawing from evil and doing good simply for the love of God, they still remain with the weak far below the summit of holiness, in the lower places. He who truly loves God with a pure mind serves and ministers to him neither from fear of punishments nor in view of reward, but only for love of him. And for this reason all the aforesaid degrees are set before beginners; ascending gradually through them, they will at last be joined to the perfect. It is good to withdraw from evil from fear of punishments; it is good to do good for love of reward; and although it in no way suffices for perfection, nevertheless it provides the beginning and the path for those who desire to make progress. For servile fear, continued, begets filial and chaste fear; from its [unclear] spring perfect charity flows. But afterward, once charity has entered the mind, it casts out all servile fear and dread thereafter.

The fourth degree, with all their zeal, guards them not only from mortal sins but also from venial ones, as far as is possible for them; they apply themselves most diligently to good and holy works and desire above all things to please God. In one thing they surpass the preceding, because they neither turn aside from evil from fear of punishments nor do good in hope of reward, but do all things for love of God alone and seek nothing other than the honor of God. This is the way set before those striving toward perfection; this is the true rule of holy justice: that we love God with a pure mind above all things and do all our works for his honor. For the true imitator of God neither fears punishment nor thinks of reward, but, walking in breadth of heart, commends his will to the divine ordinance. For God, because he is supremely good, must be loved not for the reward of the heavenly kingdom, nor for anything else, but for his own sake; and in every good work principally to be held in intention...
