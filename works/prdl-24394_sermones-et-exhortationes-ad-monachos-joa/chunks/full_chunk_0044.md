*Homily X*

But we gird these about ourselves when we keep ourselves pure and unspotted from every form of lust, in body and in mind. The virtue of holy chastity is of great merit before God, and, because of the greatness of the struggle, is to be granted with a special reward in the recompense of the just, as the Lord says through the prophet Isaiah: “Thus says the Lord to the eunuchs: Those who keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that I have willed, and hold fast my covenant: I will give them in my house and within my walls a place and a name better than sons and daughters. I will give them an everlasting name, which shall not perish.” And concerning these the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “For there are eunuchs who were so born from their mother’s womb; and there are eunuchs who were made so by men; and there are eunuchs who have castrated themselves for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. He who is able to receive it, let him receive it.”

But why did he say, “He who is able to receive it, let him receive it”? I think that this statement about eunuchs who castrate themselves for love of the kingdom of heaven is not to be referred to the cutting off of the genital members, as many, thinking so, have strayed from the truth of the Gospel, but rather to the rule of the mind, by which chastity, conceived from love of Christ, is preserved whole. For those castrate themselves for the kingdom of God who, for love of God, keep true chastity of mind and body inviolate. And just as no one is easily castrated in the body without the torment of great pain, so no one can attain to the perfection of chastity without the greatest zeal for labor. And certainly no one is chaste by his own strength, no one modest by his own merits. It is God who gives the good will; it is he also who joins virtue to the will, so that it may have power. But because it does not belong to many to live chastely, he leaves each person to his own judgment, saying: “He who is able to receive it, let him receive it.”

The cutting off of members does not make the mind chaste, since the root of lust is within and cannot be removed by the amputation of the private parts. But if the mind is chaste and whole, it will easily restrain the insolence of the members. For nothing is stronger than the mind of a human being who, with a pure heart, sweetly loves God above all things. Consider now, dearest brothers, the future rewards of beauty, and the labor of chastity will not weigh upon you. For the great rewards of high estate are reached by the strenuous perseverance of labor. Therefore whoever flees the labor of the contest will never arrive at the renown of honor. Brief now is the time in which we labor, resisting the devil; but very soon the eternal time will come, in which, reigning without end, we shall rejoice with Christ.

Let the body be chastened by discreet abstinence, lest it kick back against the rule of the mind; let the concupiscence of the flesh be broken by holy exercises, so that it may always obey the counsels of reason. If at any time it begins to strike the mind with wanton impulses, let it also be restrained with blows, because it is better that the back be humbled by the bruise of stripes than that the wantonness of the flesh should cast the soul down into hell. For Scripture says: “He who delicately nourishes his servant from childhood will afterward feel him to be rebellious.” The flesh is the servant of the mind, and it ought always to obey the rule of reason. But if the flesh has been nourished too licentiously, having now grown accustomed to pleasures, it will refuse to bear the rule of the spirit. For a servant is made rebellious against his master whenever, through habit, he is delicately nourished by him. It is difficult, however, to bring a rebellious and defiant servant back to humble obedience after he has long been accustomed to kick back. For it is written: “A servant cannot be instructed by words, because he understands what you say and disdains to answer.” Therefore the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “Fodder and rod and burden for the ass; bread and discipline and work for the servant. He works under discipline and seeks to rest; loosen your hand for him and he seeks freedom. Yoke and thong bend a hard neck, and continual labors bow down a servant.”

If we begin to borrow these testimonies of the Scriptures from the literal sense for the moral sense, we shall find that all the things said about the servant and the ass fit very rightly with the sluggish and delicate flesh. For truly the flesh is the servant of the soul, and it is always raised up against its mistress unless it is wearied every day by discipline and work. For he who at all times treats it delicately in idleness and pleasures will necessarily find it rebellious against spiritual things, because when the flesh is lifted up in pride, the spirit, languishing in idleness and lukewarm, is weakened.
