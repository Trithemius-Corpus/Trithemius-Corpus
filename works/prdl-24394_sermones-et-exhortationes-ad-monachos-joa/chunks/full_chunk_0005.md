*On spiritual warfare.*

It certainly cannot be that we too, his imitators, wicked and most vile sinners, should not have to suffer many things, we who justly endure all that we suffer by our own demerit. For no one comes to the glory of the city above unless he has first been justified here from sin; but since the afflictions of the righteous are many, he will by no means be able to be righteous who has refused to endure adversities for righteousness in this world.

Therefore, my dearest brothers, let us imitate in much patience and humility our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who, made man for us, voluntarily endured the greatest things, and in this world was never without the bitterness of persecution. For from the hour of his most holy nativity until the departure of his spirit from his body, he existed for us most patiently, always in the bitterness of this present life; and the bitterness of his future Passion, foreknown, never withdrew from his foreknowledge. For although he was almighty God, made man for us, he knew all things, understood all things, foresaw all things; nor could anything of those things which he was to suffer be hidden from him, who, that he might suffer, was made passible in the world, while remaining the impassible God that he was.

But born of the Virgin Mary without [unclear] commerce, [unclear], and soon on the eighth day he is circumcised; and he who had no sin is wounded for our offenses. By night, as a child, he is led into Egypt before the face of his persecutor; and he who was Lord of all, poor and needy, unknown in the midst of the nations, is exiled for seven years. Finally, through all the days of his life in this world he most patiently endured continual labors, and never refused to undergo any adversities for the glory of the Father. For the righteous and innocent one willingly suffered all hard and bitter things for us most unworthy ones; by the most bitter death of the cross he redeemed us from the destruction of perpetual damnation, and in his own blood mercifully reconciled us to the Father.

Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, as the prince of the apostles forewarned, let us, my brothers, be armed with the same thought, so that in compassion we may diligently recall to memory those things which he, innocent, endured for us. For if we suffer with him, we shall also reign with him, gloriously triumphant. Behold, Christ calls us to the glory to which he has gone before; he gives the weapons with which he himself first overcame the prince of the world; he prescribed the form by which the soldier ought to follow the king to the crown. To the palace of heaven Christ calls his soldiers, whom [unclear] lead, endowed with the gifts of virtues; and the victors receive rewards, to reign forever.

Therefore, my dearest brothers, always pursuing our warfare most zealously, let us continually declare war on vices and desires, so that, just as the flesh wars against the spirit, so we may fight continually, watching in prayer and fasting against the kindler of vices. As long as we live in this world we are pilgrims away from the Lord, and like gymnosophists we contend in the arena, carrying out the necessary course of life in continual struggle. But we fight not by our own strength, but supported by the mercy of our King, who said to his imitators in the holy Gospel: Without me you can do nothing. And lest we flee the battle of warfare set before us as impossible, he said elsewhere: In the world you will have distress; but take confidence: I have overcome the world. And so that we might know that the Lord Jesus is always present in this world with his servants, he promises us thus in the apostles: Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the age.

Having received this promise of security, O soldiers of Christ, my dearest brothers, why do we hesitate? Why do we not stand bravely against all the insolences of this world? Let us always have our King and protector present as we fight; and do we wretches fear to engage against a weak enemy, who overcomes no one except the willing? Gird yourselves, I pray, for the unconquerable warfare of Christ, which you have professed under a twofold condition: one when, in the sacred font of baptism, renouncing Satan, you swore the words of Christian instruction; the other when you solemnly put on this garment of monastic religion. A great necessity of making progress constrains you, if you weigh your profession with due consideration, since the duty of the Christian name commands that you must observe the evangelical commandments, and the vow which you voluntarily made requires that you keep the precepts of monastic institution. It is no longer enough for us unto salvation to fulfill those precepts which are set down to be observed by Christians in common, but also [unclear] most diligently.
