*Sermon I.*

The monk, therefore, who is strong with true virtue neither dares rashly nor fears without counsel. For courage and constancy together are the royal road, from which the rash and stubborn man turns aside to the right, but the fearful, faint-hearted, and timid man to the left. The monk, constant and strong in spirit, never withdraws from the purpose of holiness, but is neither cast down by adversities nor lifted up by prosperities. Hence also the most holy bishop Ambrose says: “The fool changes like the moon. But the wise man is not broken by fear, is not changed by power, is not exalted by prosperities, is not sunk by sorrows.” For where wisdom and virtue are, there is constancy and strength of spirit. Therefore the wise man is always the same in spirit; he is neither diminished nor increased by changes of circumstances, nor does he, like a little child, waver so as to be carried about by every wind of doctrine, but remains perfect in Christ, founded in charity and rooted in the perfection of faith.

Finally, my dearest brothers, perseverance is necessary for you in every good and virtuous work; without it no one ever comes to the crown of glory. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “He who perseveres to the end will be saved.” Take away perseverance, and neither will virtue have a reward, nor service a recompense, nor a benefit merit grace. For perseverance alone in good works deserves to receive the crown of praise; without it, it profits nothing to have begun a good thing. No virtue has merit if perseverance is not brought to its increase. The kingdom of eternal happiness is promised not to those who begin the good, but to those who persevere in the good until death, because the denarius will be paid to the laborers in the vineyard not in the morning or at midday, but when evening has come.

For he who began well is not to be praised, but he who continued well to the end is to be honored. Therefore, my brothers, I am accustomed diligently to admonish you, and I ought never to cease, that you carefully attend to your calling and walk cautiously in all religion and holiness, as you know you have sworn. Seek the Lord while he can be found.

Let the life of Christ the Savior be your imitation; by the foundation of your institution you are bound to follow him according to your strength, not by steps of the feet but by likeness of affections. But in all things walk [unclear]; always place the fear of God, in true humility, before the gaze of the mind. For you have need of great purity, you who in mind and body have consented to the holy warfare of Christ. It now belongs to you, O brothers, to despise the world and all things that are in the world for God’s sake with perpetual courage; to overcome all motions of carnal pleasures; to flee sin; to occupy yourselves constantly in good and holy works. Your life also, so far as human weakness allows, ought to imitate angelic purity and to flee and detest all depravity. The holy angels are never idle, but are always, without weariness or [unclear], diligent in the praises of God. The holy angels love God above all things, and do not withdraw even for a moment from his charity. In imitation of them the monk ought to be assiduous in the service of Christ, and always occupied in good works according to the Gospel and the Rule.

The monk ought to have the charity of God most fervently, and true [charity] of neighbor [unclear]. The monk ought to have the charity of God most fervently: he who does not wish to become idle should love; how much more strongly does the most chaste love of God impel the mind it possesses toward arduous things. For fervent love toward God perfects the monk and does not allow him to be idle. For the most holy Pope Gregory says: “The love of God is never idle. For it works great things if it exists; but if it refuses to work, it was never true love of God.” For if the mad poet spoke truly of carnal love, “he who does not wish to become idle loves,” how much more strongly does the most chaste love of God impel the mind it possesses toward arduous things. Love God, brothers, with your whole heart, as far as the strength of desire can extend itself, and you will be able most easily to observe all the things that have been commanded you.

For nothing is difficult to one who loves; but all hard things, all heavy and arduous things, burning love makes easy.
