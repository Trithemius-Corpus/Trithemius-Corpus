*On the daily renewal of holy purpose.*

If the fire of divine love now burns in you, apply the necessary fuel, lest through your lukewarmness you should at some time feel the little flame of holy fervor utterly extinguished. Believe me, brothers, for my own experience has taught me: you will preserve the fire of divine fervor burning in you with easier exercise than you will kindle it again once it has been extinguished.

Whoever, therefore, has already experienced in himself the love of God, whoever has received heavenly charity in his heart, let him take the greatest care not to fall back into the negligences of his former life. Let him apply the necessary fuel of spiritual exercises, so that he may be able to preserve in himself the fervor of God as it advances. Let him beware not only of great sins, but also of small negligences, and, as far as he can, of the very least; for he who despises the least things gradually falls, and he who does not always fear danger to spiritual fervor and holy purpose will quickly feel the loss.

For Scripture says: “If you do not steadfastly keep yourself in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown.” Blessed, therefore, is the man who is always fearful, who does not presume upon the virtues he has received, who judges with certainty that he is failing whenever he has not found himself advancing at every moment toward higher things.

But truly, brothers, I judge very strongly, unless the assessment of certain works deceives me, that in some of you the fire of holy fervor is not yet present, since they seem to love the true pursuits of monks but little. That this is manifest from their conduct none of you will be able to ignore. I have indeed not considered it wholly superfluous to admonish those who so far seem to have made little progress, since the hand of the Lord has not been shortened so that it cannot save the penitent.

For I trust in the mercy of the living God, who, because of his very great charity toward them and because of the exercises of piety of those who walk among you according to the purity of the rule, will grant them at some time [to conquer themselves?], to love the pursuits of holy fervor more ardently.

What is this, brothers, that you do not live monastically in the monastery, and, having professed the life of the spirit, walk according to the flesh? Do you not know how perilous it is for a monk to live in worldly fashion in a cenobium, and by lukewarm conduct to violate the vows of holy purity which all of us once made?
