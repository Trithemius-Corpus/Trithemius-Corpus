*Homily XII*

He killed the boys. The evil that a young man receives from a foolish and negligent old man, even when he has been made a priest, he will not amend. Every vessel that is filled with filth is corrupted; and a monk who is negligent in the novitiate, when he has grown old, is not amended. Cursed is the old man by whose evil manner of life young men perish, because he who leaves evil tracks for those who follow is like one who with his own hand has cast a blind man headlong into a pit. Before the eternal Judge each man is worthy of as many deaths as there are innocents who have perished by his wicked example.

Young man, seek an old man as the guide of your life: not one who has completed many years in a lax and lukewarm manner of life, but one whom the fear of the Lord and knowledge of the divine Scriptures commend. Better is the company of a young monk who fears the Lord than the fellowship of an aged teacher whose life does not have the light of divine love and the integrity of regular observance. An old man disordered in morals, lacking purity of mind and without discipline, is like [unclear], lame in the leg, being led to the grave. An old and lax monk is like a foolish man who, not knowing the boundaries, is captured with robbers at a drinking banquet. An old man without maturity is like an elder-wood pipe crushed under the feet of players.

Vainly does he boast that he has been a monk for many days, if he has not grasped the beginning of inward conduct. He who loves the purity of his heart guards himself at all times from the company of undisciplined brothers. Just as a stain of pitch clinging to the garments of dancers grieves virginity, so a good young man blushes at the filthy words of elders. Lascivious speech does not belong to a man who fears God, because out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks; and he who utters filthy words is not clean before the Most High. If sinners begin to hate you because of the fear of God, do not be saddened, because unless you displease the wicked you will not attain the perfection of the good. For to displease the wicked on account of justice is praiseworthy, and a manifest sign of divine love. If you love your soul, flee the counsels of the undisciplined, and do not keep company with mockers. Walk with the good, and let him who fears the Lord be your counselor.

*Concerning monks sent on the road. Homily.*

*Folio XXVII*

It belongs to monks to flee the world, and, for love of solitude, to avoid the crowds of men at all times as much as they can. A monk is named from solitude, although he is called one from unity. He cannot be a monk who delights in the throng of peoples; and he who delights in cities has not yet tasted monastic sweetness. If you are a monk, you must not leave the solitude of the monastery without reasonable cause; and if you are bound to silence, do not delight in the conversations of worldly people. Whoever loves wandering excursions on the road has abandoned his conscience; and he who is often outside the monastery does not preserve the purity of his inward progress.

He who goes about the houses of his parents and delights in the feasts of neighbors does not consider himself a monk; and he who goes to the banquets of drinkers kills his own soul. He who sleeps in the house of his kinsman seeks comforts; and he who avoids the assemblies of the religious follows a destructive freedom. A monk in the house of father and mother is like a lamb in the midst of wolves, and like a hare surrounded by the dogs of hunters. If you are a monk, if you are God’s servant and soldier, what are you doing in the house of your parents, or what are you seeking in the midst of the people? A monk remaining long outside the monastery among secular people is like a man who buries his only son beneath the depth of waters. For just as there is no life for fish on dry land, so there is no salvation of soul for monks in the world. Outside the river the fish dies; and the purity of a monk’s conscience is quickly violated outside the monastery. The life of monks is in the monastery, and the [death?] of souls is in the midst of secular people.

It is a great virtue of the soul to flee the crowds of men, or, when it is entirely necessary to be among them, nevertheless to avoid them in mind. We are sometimes compelled by necessity to make a journey through the world; but the less the desire of the mind is drawn away from the secrecy of the cell, the less the purity of conscience is polluted by meeting men. Therefore every good monk must be admonished by us that, whenever by order of his senior he is sent on the road because of the necessity of any matter, his soul should never wander outward.
