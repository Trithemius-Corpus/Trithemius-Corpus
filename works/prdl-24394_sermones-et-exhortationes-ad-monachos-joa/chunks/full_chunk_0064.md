*Homily XIV.*

Nevertheless I say that perhaps it will not be useless: confession ought to be personal, complete, free, humble, voluntary, and shamefaced. For the holy prelate Ambrose says that shamefaced confession avails very greatly with God for the remission of sins to human beings, and the punishment that we cannot avoid by defense we lighten by humble shame. Due confession of sins is a great thing, because it at once restores to spiritual health the soul of the one confessing, wounded by offenses; it also justifies the humbled mind and grants pardon for sins. For all our hope after the fall of sin consists in the humility of confession, because guilt is not forgiven unless it is revealed in confession, through the priest, to God, who knows all things.

For in confession there is a place for mercy, as Solomon says in the Book of Proverbs: He who hides his crimes will not be directed; but he who has confessed them and left them behind will obtain mercy. Likewise it is written in the Psalm: I said, I will confess my injustice against myself to the Lord, and you forgave the impiety of my sin. The holy evangelist John also says in the first epistle: If we confess our sins, God is faithful and just, so that he forgives us our sins, and he will cleanse us from all iniquity. And the holy prelate Augustine says: Confession is the salvation of souls, the scatterer of vices, the restorer of virtues, the conqueror of demons, and the heaper-up of all good things. It closes the mouth of hell and opens the gates of the heavenly paradise. Cassiodorus also, commending the virtue of true confession, speaks thus: O blessed confession, which removes eternal reproach, because whatever repentance has found as guilt it renders absolved for everlasting ages.

Fourth: for true penitence satisfaction is necessary, by which, namely, cutting out all the causes of sins, we remove them, and for the things [which we have wrongly committed] we appease the Lord with holy works. Hence the divine pope Gregory says: We in no way make satisfaction if we cease from iniquity, unless we also pursue with contrary lamentations the pleasures that we loved. The most holy pope Leo also says in a sermon: By prayer the favor of God is sought; by fasting the desire of the flesh is extinguished; by almsgiving sins are redeemed; and at the same time through all these things the image of God is renewed in us.

Confession, moreover, is the voluntary accusation of the offender, from shame and through the keys of the Church, making satisfaction for sins, with the obligation through penitence to chastise in himself, at the priest's command, those things that he has brought forth. Confession ought to be simple, humble, pure, faithful, frequent, bare and open; discreet, voluntary, personal, shamefaced, complete, secret, full of tears, made at the proper times, strong, and truly accusing oneself from the heart, because true penitence requires all these things; it so bewails past sins that thereafter it commits no more things to be mourned.

*On abstinence and the fasting of monks: Homily XV.*

Abstinence together with fasting, dearest brothers, is a great and necessary good for a monk, so that, namely, in mind he may not sin through desire of the flesh and evil thoughts, and in body he may abstain from foods and drinks that generate wantonness of spirit. For the desire of the mind is quickly inflamed into lust if the body is nourished too carefully. Considering the very great usefulness of this good, the most holy fathers of old, who handed down precepts of life to monks, all with one judgment established for us a fixed measure of fasts; under its moderation the body would take as much refreshment of foods as nature required, not as much as the intemperance of the will desired.

Holy Scripture also commends fasting to us in many ways, since it so often mentions it with praise. For the Lord God says through the mouth of his prophet Joel: Turn to me with your whole heart, in fasting, mourning, and weeping. Likewise in the Book of Judith it is read thus: Know that the Lord will hear your prayers if, remaining steadfast, you continue in fasts and prayers in the sight of the Lord. Similarly we read in Tobit: Prayer with fasting and almsgiving is good, more than storing up treasures of gold, because almsgiving frees from death, and it is what purges sins. Our Lord Jesus Christ also said to his disciples in the holy Gospel: Blessed are you who now hunger, because you shall be filled.
