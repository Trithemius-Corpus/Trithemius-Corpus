But the spirit of fornication first grievously darkens the mind; afterward it fights until it has cast the blinded man down into sin. Then, after it has cast the monk into the mire of unspeakable crime, it interposes itself in two ways so that he may not rise again to penitence. For either it subtly strives to make the gravity of the sin seem light, so that he may not fear a great punishment; or it recalls the strictness of judgment to the fallen man’s memory, so that he may fall into the pit of despair. By these snares the sinful soul is caught, and the longer it has lain in the depth of misery, the more hardened it becomes.

Flee idleness, O cloistered men, and shun gluttony and fullness of the belly with all your strength, since the monk who fills his belly with food and drink supplies the demon of fornication with materials for doing harm. Take up true contrition of heart, and restrain the illicit motions of the flesh through the tears of prayer. For no one is able rightly to conquer his body who has not first taken up the goad of contrition. But he cannot attain the grace of holy contrition who [unclear] perfectly deny himself. Yet whoever has perfectly denied himself for the love of Christ, already overcoming the assault of the flesh, will more easily obtain the grace of contrition. For he who is mortified in will desires nothing carnal, nothing shameful.

But who among us, brothers, would dare to say that he has attained this grace of mortification? Who among us does not know himself to be weak? We run in the stadium of spiritual warfare, and in daily contest we fight against vices. We have received no confidence of peace, even if at times we have peace. The more familiar to us is the weakness of the flesh, the harder are the battles of chastity. Hence we must be especially watchful, since we carry about with us a familiar enemy and endure, as it were, a household instigator of evil. Therefore, as long as we live in this mortality, let us always fear the corrupter of chastity. Let us avoid everything that excites lust, and, as far as we can, let us flee sloth and idleness. For fullness of bread and idleness are the strongest enemies of true chastity; unless they are driven far away by abstinence and by the work of salutary exercise, they threaten chastity with imminent shipwreck.

Guard your hearts with every watchfulness, O my dearest brothers, and always keep watch in spirit against your adversary, who never sleeps. Arm yourselves with the breastplate of perfect faith in Jesus Christ, so that in deed and belief you may be true Christians. Always be of one mind in brotherly charity, and be without pretense concordant in the true observance of regular discipline. Love fasting and continence; pray always without ceasing. Flee the eye as a destructive plague of the soul, and continually exercise yourselves in holy works. Love silence, and do not flee monastic solitude, if you desire to obtain true purity of mind and body. Keep continual guard over eyes, ears, and speech, lest death enter the soul through the windows. Flee conversations with women, and shun even the touch of holy women as the kindling of fire. No one safely gazes willingly upon what brings the desired sting of death. Always behold with the faithful eyes of the mind, through love, our Lord Jesus Christ dying for you on the cross; occupy yourselves wholesomely each day in his evangelical teaching; give humble thanks alike for his passion and death. If, with continual perseverance, you do these things in true simplicity of heart until death, we trust that you will be rewarded with fellowship among the saints.

*On the brotherly charity of monks.*
*Homily XXII.*

We have heard, my dearest brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ commanding in the holy Gospel: Whatever you wish that men should do to you, do the same also to them. This is indeed a brief but solid commendation of brotherly charity, without which no one can ascend to the love of God. For no one can love God who is convicted of not loving his fellow Christian. Hence the blessed apostle John says: Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him. Hence, if anyone says that I love God, and hates his brother, he is a liar. Therefore holy Paul, lover of Christ, exhorts us, saying: Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.
