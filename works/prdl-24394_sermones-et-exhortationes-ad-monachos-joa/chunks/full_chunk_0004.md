*Homily I. Folio III.*

*In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Here begins the first book of exhortations to monks, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim and later of Würzburg. On the spiritual warfare of monks.*

It befits Christian warfare, brothers, and especially that of monks, to be subject to the various calamities of this world, and, like gold in the furnace, to be continually exercised and tested by tribulations. For it belongs to monks to surpass, by a higher manner of living, the common rule of Christian warfare, and to obey not only the Lord's commandments but also his counsels. It belongs to monks, for love of God, utterly to despise riches, honors, and all the profane pleasures of the world, and, once fleshly desires have been overcome, to be constantly free for God alone. For a monk is so called as being solitary, from unity, so that by the very designation of his name he may be admonished to contempt of the world.

Accordingly the office of a monk is to stand with himself in solitude, clean and purified from vices, and sweetly to love Christ above all things. For Christ is the goal of monks, toward which regular discipline is directed; whoever refuses to imitate him is indeed neither a monk nor a Christian. Therefore, my brothers, we must know that not everyone who says to me, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my Father who is in heaven, he shall enter into the kingdom of heaven. And what the will of God is, the holy Apostle disclosed, saying: This is the will of God, your sanctification. But since the necessary consummation of this commanded sanctification requires from us great endurance of evils in all things, and humility, because no one is holy by his own powers, we must, my brothers, keep continual consideration of our vocation before ourselves, lest, growing sluggish in most destructive idleness, we be overcome in the military contest by the enemy, who, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking some incautious person, and one languishing in soul, to devour as he perishes.

For we are soldiers of God, fighting against a threefold adversary, namely the flesh, the world, and the devil. It is a two-sided war and an exceedingly hard contest: to suffer as enemy the one whom nature has given as familiar. Let us therefore arm ourselves, dearest brothers, with the ornaments of the virtues, because whoever is adorned with the splendor of good works is not easily overcome by the adversary. First, let us fortify ourselves with the most constant faith of the holy and undivided Trinity; its devout and sincere confession by mind and mouth drives far away all the wicked devices and deceits of demons, and, overcoming the greatest enemy, casts him down and utterly enfeebles him. Then let us take up for ourselves a most fervent charity toward God and neighbor, which is fearful to demons above all things, especially if it has been rooted in true Christian humility. Let us not give confidence to our soul, since he is called blessed who is always fearful; but he who is of hard mind will fall into evil. Let us follow Christ our light, who said in the Gospel: I am the way, the truth, and the life, because no one comes to the Father except through me. Now therefore, brothers, attend to those things which are read in the holy Scriptures concerning our Savior, because he who says that he remains in Christ, that is, in the way of truth, should strive to walk just as he walked.

But it was necessary for him first to undergo the reproach of the cross and so to enter into his glory, by which we too, his soldiers, however unworthy, are admonished that we must enter the kingdom of God through many tribulations. For this reason the Lord himself, king of all things, admonished us in the Gospel, saying: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me. Behold, Christ is set before us as the way to eternal life; though he was Lord of heavenly and worldly things, for our sake he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, in which, voluntarily and not compelled, he bore our weaknesses, becoming obedient to the Father even unto death, even the death of the cross.
