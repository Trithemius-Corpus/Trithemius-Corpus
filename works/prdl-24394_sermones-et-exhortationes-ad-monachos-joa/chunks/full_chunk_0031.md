*Homily VIII.*

Whether at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning, lest when he comes suddenly he find you sleeping. Watch, therefore, at every time, praying that you may be deemed worthy to escape all the evils that are to come, and to stand with confidence before the sight of the Son of God who judges all things. For, as the divine [apostle of Christ?] bears witness, we shall all stand before the judgment-seat of Christ, so that each may receive the things proper to the body according as he has done, whether good or evil.

Blessed is that monk whom the Lord shall find watching in good works, who has prepared himself for the wedding feast of the heavenly Lamb in true purity of heart, who will not fear to meet the Judge. Let that memorable saying of Saint Jerome on the Gospel according to Matthew always be in your hearts: “Whether I eat or drink, or do anything else, that voice always seems to sound in my ears: Rise, dead, and come to judgment.” As often as I think of that day of future examination, so often I tremble in heart and body. Therefore, my brothers, if there is any joy in the present life, it must be conducted in such a way that the bitterness of that dreadful judgment never recedes from memory.

O that day, great, terrible, exceedingly fearful, and very bitter, on which the sun and moon will be darkened, not by diminution of light but by comparison with majesty; where every argument of excuse will cease; where all things will be manifest, and nothing will be able to lie hidden from the Judge. For he who knows the hidden thoughts of minds will then judge openly; he is neither forestalled by favor, nor bent by mercy; he is not corrupted by gifts and presents; he will not be softened by prayers; nor thereafter will any satisfaction appease him.

Placed in this life, therefore, brothers, let each of you do as much as he can for his soul, because repentance for sins made here alone is useful and fruitful, while there is still a place for pardon and the sentence of justice is still deferred. He who delays doing good and doing penance until the [hour?] of death is [near?] to damnation, because the good that he neglects while healthy and unharmed he will rarely restore when sick and already about to die. For Saint Pope Gregory says: Whoever does not fear to defer the fruits of repentance until the hour of death is himself witness against himself that truly repenting was never in his mind. Now therefore turn yourselves to our Lord with your whole heart, doing penance before him, and so live, I beg you, that when it is time to depart from here, you may not fear to appear before his face.

*That One Must Pray Without Intermission, Homily VII.*

Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, brothers, in the Gospel according to Luke, wishing us to be constant in prayers, warned us thus, saying: It is necessary always to pray and never to fail. This counsel of the Lord befits monks above all mortals, and all cloistered persons in whatever religious order, whose every action and whole life, indeed whatever they do, should at least be in conformity with prayer.

For all who profess the monastic warfare are called spiritual not without reason, insofar as by that very name they are admonished to despise the pleasures of the flesh for the love of God, and through true charity to walk in fervor of spirit. For the holy apostle of Christ says: Those who are in the flesh cannot please God, because if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live. But we know that without persistence in prayer the fervor of the spirit cannot long subsist in us. Again, to the Thessalonians he says: Rejoice always; pray without intermission; give thanks in all things, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for all of you.

But what the manner of praying is, the Savior taught us in the Gospel, saying: But you, when you pray, enter into your chamber, and with the door closed, pray to your Father in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you. And in another place he said to the Samaritan woman: Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you worship the Father. But the hour is coming, and now is, when true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth. For the Father also seeks such as will worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.

That prayer is of the heart rather than a lengthy discourse of the mouth is plainly established from Christ’s teaching, because, since God is spirit, he must be worshiped in no other way than with inward fervor of spirit.
