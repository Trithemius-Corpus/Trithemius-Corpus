For we are frail and weak, and we do not remain long steadfast in a holy purpose unless by daily exercises we continually renew the fervor of the spirit. For by reason of my office I am bound to set before you publicly those things which can conduce to your salvation; it belongs to you to listen patiently in all things to me as I speak, as the pastor of your souls. For although, being young, I am not fit to teach so great a multitude of elders, nevertheless, since you have appointed me pastor over you, I am compelled, under necessity of salvation, to govern the flock which I have undertaken.

What is in me I willingly do according to my powers, and although I have scarcely lived long in this anxiety of pastoral care, I, a young man, will teach you elders, and I will show without interruption how you ought to serve as soldiers for him under whose arms you have sworn. For the dreadful warning of our lawgiver does not escape me, when he says: Let the abbot know that whatever the father of the household may find less useful in the sheep lies as the fault of the shepherd. And what follows concerns you, my brothers, if, when I preach and admonish, contempt for the word of God should arise. Again, he says: Only then will he be free, if all diligence has been applied by the shepherd to a restless or disobedient flock, and every care has been shown for their diseased acts; their shepherd, absolved in the judgment of the Lord, may say with the prophet to the Lord: I have not hidden your justice in my heart; I have spoken your truth and your salvation; but they, despising, have spurned me. And then for the disobedient sheep entrusted to his care, death itself becomes for them a prevailing penalty.

Here no covering of excuse is left; no place for evasion is granted, but the danger of both parties is publicly described. For I, holding the place of abbot, if I have not announced to you the justice of God, if I have not corrected you when you sin according to the Rule, have denied my office; and whatever holiness is found lacking in you will be imputed wholly to me. For it is mine, by reason of the pastoral office, to say what is expedient for the salvation of your souls; but yours is humbly to hear those things which I shall pronounce in the name of the Savior, and faithfully to fulfill them from a great heart, so far as each one is able. You have heard the danger along with me if you despise me when I preach to you the word of God.

But that, by the Savior’s mercy, we may all escape secure, let each one of us diligently pursue what is commanded him in this spiritual warfare, taking the greatest care lest, having become deaf hearers of the truth, we lie to the Lord. Let us diligently remember the vow which we have made, and let us preserve inviolate until death the holy order which we profess. Let us hear our king saying in the Gospel to his soldiers: If anyone serves me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also my servant will be.

We priests and Levites, all cloistered monks, are servants of the eternal king, and are bound by a twofold reason to great holiness, since we have at once received the order of clerics and profess the rule of monastic purity. Therefore let us follow our teacher, for whom we serve as soldiers, along the path of the severity of all virtues; and when his [unclear] begins to burn in the soldier’s heart, he fears no adversities. For charity, as Saint Gregory says, is never idle, but does great things if it is true; but if it refuses to work, it was not perfect charity.

Therefore, cloistered soldiers, you who have pledged that you will serve the eternal king forever, cast away now the works of darkness and put on charity toward God and neighbor, which is the strongest armor of salvation for all. If anyone has been clothed with it, he fears no labors, however hard, no adversities of this world, no intimidation by men: not thirst, not persecutions of men, not reproaches, not mockeries, not prisons, not chains, not any harsh and adverse things whatever; but everyone who has once perfectly tasted the charity of God
