*Sermon V.*

Sixth, it strongly draws the mind back from sin if, by habit, it continues always occupied in meditation on the Lord’s Passion. For who will be able to devote his effort to the pleasures of the flesh, if he has considered Jesus Christ, the ruler of the world, hanging for him on the cross? The death of Christ freed the world from eternal death; whoever carries this benefit with him in memory will easily abstain from every crime of vices. Finally, if anyone has considered God, present to him in every place and knowing and understanding all things, with the whole attention of his mind, he will without doubt always abhor every sin. For God is everywhere for us, not only present, but truly inward and most present; no secret escapes him, nothing remains hidden from him, but everything lies open and manifest to him. He searches the hearts of all; he knows the most secret things of thoughts, since he alone has true knowledge of all things. For who among mortals would dare, without the greatest shame, in the presence of so great a majesty, which he believes sees all things, if he weighs who and how great is the one who sees?

Therefore, whenever the disordered motions of the flesh disturb the soul, think of the Lord God, who is the avenger of evils, as present; recall to memory the bitterness of the Passion of Jesus Christ; devoutly implore the aid of heavenly grace. Harder than stone is the man whom these things, considered rather diligently, have not been able to recall from the intention of sinning.

The seventh thing that recalls the mind of a studious monk from the desire of sinning is that by continual reading he run through the passions of the holy martyrs. For indeed whoever rather often recalls to memory the invincible constancy of the holy martyrs of Christ cannot fail to become stronger and more robust in resisting sins. For this reason the brave deeds of the saints have been committed to writing: so that whenever we, weak as we are, read such things, we may rise up to imitation of those who have gone before. Do not look to those brothers who, with proud rashness, deny faith to the histories of the saints and think that the glorious things narrated about the most blessed friends of Christ are fables. For the virtues and good things that they do not find in themselves, they disdain to believe that the saints of God possessed. For there are many, and among them especially those who think themselves great because of their learning, who too rashly reject the histories of the holy fathers; and, since they themselves cannot enter upon the arduous path of virtue, they invent with windy talk that no one, or only rare persons, could have done so. Whatever arduous or great thing is read before them concerning the ways of life of the saints, they cry out that it is invented and fabulous; and, such as they themselves live, placed in the darkness of vices, they think that those whose histories they deride likewise passed their lives.

But [unclear] a prudent and devout monk is not moved by the sloth of the proud, since he understands well that a certain effective power of exhortation is present in the histories of the saints. For, to confess meanwhile what is true, I am moved more toward devotion of inward fervor by the way of life of one saint than by many elaborate sermons, because, as the most holy pope Leo says, examples are more powerful than words, and it is more to teach by deed than by voice. But just as not all things please all people, so not all things suit all people. We advise each person to read more frequently and more zealously that by the reading of which the mind is stirred to greater perfection.

The eighth thing that most strongly draws a man back from sin is charity, of which the holy apostle says: Charity is patient, is kind; it does not act perversely; it is not puffed up; it is not ambitious; it does not seek what is its own; it is not provoked; it does not think evil; it does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth; it bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Charity never falls away. He who loves God flees sin; but he who is the servant of crime certainly does not love God. The lover of God does not commit sin; and he who truly loves his Lord and always beholds him as present does not work iniquity. This is the highest and unchangeable good, for love of which sin is both to be fled and detested, since he who has deserved to sin only from fear of punishments does not ascend to the rewards of charity. For a servant does not sin from fear of blows, but a son withdraws from evil out of love for his parent, lest he offend his father’s mind.

Let us avoid sin, my brothers, and with as much purity of mind as we can let us live in the sight of the Lord, because just as deadly poison, when taken, kills the body, so sin kills the soul of the offender. Therefore the wise man admonishes us in Ecclesiasticus, saying: Flee sins as from the face of a serpent, because if you approach them, they will receive you. The teeth of a lion are the teeth of sin, killing the souls of men. Like a two-edged sword is the iniquity of man; for its wound there is no healing.
