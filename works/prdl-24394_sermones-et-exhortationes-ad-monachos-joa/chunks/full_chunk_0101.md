*All, XXII.*

Charity judges no one rashly, but humbly corrects a neighbor who sins with the gentlest admonitions. Charity is terrible and fearful to the reprobate, but loving and kindly to the gentle. Charity counts others’ inconveniences as its own, and weeps for the affliction of the brethren as though it itself suffered. Charity suffers with all who are placed in necessity, and is saddened no less by others’ misfortunes than by its own. Charity reproaches the neighbor with nothing, but for the love of God always bears the weaknesses of all with equanimity. Charity is constant in good works, and as long as it remains in the heart it is never idle. Charity neglects none of those things to which it is bound, but keeps all things with the greatest diligence. Fraternal charity is the fulfillment of the law, since whatever is commanded after the worship of God is referred to love of neighbor. Hence the holy lover of Christ says: “He who loves his neighbor has fulfilled the law. For, You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness, you shall not covet the property of your neighbor; and if there is any other commandment, it is renewed in this word: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. Love of neighbor does no evil. Therefore love is the fullness of the law.” And so, my dearest brethren, we beseech you through our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead, that you always keep fraternal charity unharmed, without which you can neither truly love God nor fulfill any commandment of his. For the keeping of fraternal charity is as necessary to you for salvation as the nourishment of food and drink is to bodies for the preservation of life. For the body dies without necessary nourishment, and all the virtues of the soul are scattered without fraternal charity. For fraternal charity preserves the multitude of monks in unity of spirit, since without the bond of mutual love none of those in the cloister perseveres without disturbance of the community. The monk who pursues his brother with hatred is removed far from the charity of God. For the holy evangelist John says: “He who hates his brother is in darkness and walks in darkness, and does not know where he goes, because darkness has blinded his eyes.” But the chaste and prudent lover of neighbor walks secure in the light of the Holy Spirit, and he is not disturbed by anyone, nor does he disturb anyone else. *Folio LIII.*

For true love of neighbor is mild, sweet, and most dutiful, and in all its companionship gentle, peaceable, and tranquil. Yet among those in authority the vigor of discipline guides gentleness, and the clemency of fraternal consideration beautifully tempers vigor. For each must be so tempered by the other that vigor is not rigid, nor love lax. The lover of fraternal charity does not leave the way of justice, and he who rightly loves his neighbor introduces nothing of corruption. For true love of neighbor neither covets another’s possessions nor requires the services of attendants, but, having despised all things that can overthrow the state of the soul, pursues only the soul’s increase. For it is not sincere charity to seek gifts of corruption from one’s neighbors, or, in the manner of children, to love with an affection of likeness one who comes running because little presents are being distributed. The friendship of monks among good men who are zealous for the same purpose ought to be common, and far removed from every affection of the flesh. Let it be pure, simple, right, and lasting without injury to anyone, and let it have in itself nothing of suspicious conspiracy. This is not the rule of fraternal charity: to seek private little meetings of forbidden consolation, and without the prior’s blessing to devote oneself to secret, indeed prohibited, conversations. You expend your love upon your neighbor not rightly but rather perniciously whenever, contrary to the form of regular observance, it comes together for the solace of the flesh. The love of a monk ought to be solid and very far removed from every transgression of regular discipline, since charity that either gives to a neighbor or receives consolations harmful to the spirit was never sincere. Therefore take care to direct the eye of the mind not to the beauty of the flesh but rather to the contemplation of the soul, if you wish rightly and laudably to love your neighbors. For when the flesh is loved without regard for the soul, not sincere but carnal and impure love is generated. Consider that your brother has a soul like yours, and always think that both of you await an equal happiness. Whether we understand it of the belly or of the soul, we are immortal by soul, not by body.
