Therefore let hatreds depart far from this holy congregation of yours; let there be among you no destructive contests of contention; let no zeal of bitterness be found there; let no speech of [unclear] pride be heard. Let the concord of brotherly unity flourish here continually; let the innocence of Christian purity adorn the consciences of each one, so that we may live in this brief time in such a way that after death we may glory with Christ in eternity.

*On Man’s Love toward God.*

*Homily XXIII.*

Nothing is so just, nothing so holy; nothing in human actions can be so honorable as always to love God with the whole heart and with all one’s strength above all things, and for love of him to fear neither death nor any kinds of punishments. There is in us no greater cause for any action than divine love, because, since God is the author of all things, nothing is loved more justly than he; and since he is the greatest of all goods, nothing is loved more fruitfully than he. God first loved us: the highest loved the lowest, the greatest the little ones, the omnipotent the weak. Therefore, my brothers, let us love the Lord God with all our heart, all our soul, and all our strength, by whom we are most sweetly loved; he not only created us in his own image and likeness, but, which is the greatest proof of love, mercifully called us through the teaching and death of his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, to the kingdom of eternal blessedness. Never did any fleshly mother love her only-begotten son so tenderly, sincerely, and strongly as almighty God loved us; for our instruction he not only willed to become man, but also deigned to undergo death in the flesh he had assumed for our salvation. No greater love has ever existed, if we rightly consider who it was who endured such things for us. For out of love for us, he who was free from the debt of death voluntarily dies; and he who had always remained immune from every sin, innocent and righteous, is condemned [unclear] for our sins. Therefore it is commanded us by the most just reasoning that we love the Lord our God with our whole heart, who, when we did not exist, created us out of his goodness alone, and when we had perished, mercifully freed us.

Love of God is the greatest of all virtues, since whatever good, whatever virtue, is done in this world, if it is not done from charity toward God, is void of reward. Charity toward God purifies the conscience from every fault, because he who truly loves the Lord his God with his whole soul and in his whole heart and with all his strength undoubtedly grants no place in his conscience to vices. For it is certain that into the heart which the love of God occupies, no uncleanness of vices enters. The love of God is an unfailing treasure: he who possesses it is richer than all the wealthy of the world; he who lacks it is more wretched than all the poor. Hence the holy apostle of Christ says: “For those who love God, all things work together for good,” since nothing is lacking to those who fear him. Happy is the man in whose heart no attachment of earthly love dwells besides the love of Christ; who sighs for the memory of no human being; in whose conscience purity resides, and divine love always burns most ardently. Love him, O my brothers, who for love of you descended into the Virgin’s womb; always carry him in your hearts, who by his death opened heaven to you. Let the love of Christ be in you at all times, brothers, strong as death, so that your life may be conformed to Christ’s teaching; and when it is necessary to die for his love, you may not then fear, just as he himself first deigned calmly and kindly to undergo death for us. For the love of Christ makes a monk near to God, and he who is always fervent in the charity of the Lord will attain the fellowship of angels.
