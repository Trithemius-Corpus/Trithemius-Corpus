For thus the canon admonishes you; thus the monastic institution commands; thus reason necessary for salvation persuades. You are ordered to be content with every baseness and extremity, and always fearful in all things. But I see very many of you perverting the rule of salvation, and living in the monastery otherwise than you ought. Monks now do not fear to seek delicacies in monasteries, though they came from the world to conversion poor and beggarly. For the things they could not have outside, if now they are lacking in the monastery, they rebel against humility as though they had suffered a most grave insult. Woe to the monks whom not the love of God but want has made cloistered; whose god is their belly, and who think of nothing less than poverty.

It is not for you, O monks, henceforth to seek in the monastery the delicacies which the rule commands you utterly to deny. It befits us to despise all the pleasures of the present life, and to love absolutely nothing in this world except God. For your whole way of life ought always to be bound to divine services, you who promised to live according to the spirit and not according to the flesh. He who is a true monk does not live without repentance, but daily chastens his body by abstinence, whoever truly longs for the banquets of eternal happiness. For it cannot be that a monk should enjoy the delicacies of the present life and eternal ones; but if he longs for those, he must despise these. For the monk who professes the cenobitic canon is commanded to be a disciple of Christ the Savior; if he desires truly to please him, he must become his imitator. Thus he says to him in the holy Gospel: Whoever wishes to be my disciple, let him follow me. But what “let him follow me” means, Saint John the Apostle teaches in a certain epistle, saying: He who says that he remains in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked.

And the Lord himself again says in the Gospel: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. He who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me. And he who loves son or daughter above me is not worthy of me. And he who does not take up his cross and follow me is not worthy of me. Your teacher has not spoken obscurely, O monk, nor has he wrapped his sentence in riddles, but he has put forth an evident speech, by which you can pretend no excuse. For Christ himself is the exemplar and rule of a good and perfect way of life for every Christian, and especially for the monk; the cloistered man who does not follow him with all his strength and with his whole heart boasts in vain of the profession of faith.

For just as one is called a Christian from Christ, so a disciple is named from imitation of the master. Therefore imitate the master in your conduct, so that you may prove yourself a true disciple of Christ. For the Lord Jesus Christ, whose disciple you profess yourself to be, O monk, though he was rich, became poor for us; but you, though base and needy, seek abundance of all things in the school of poverty, and though the preceptor commands you to despise all things for love of him, you again desire to possess all things in the monastery.

Your Lord and teacher was obedient to the Father for us even unto the death of the cross; but you, full of pride and impatience, either refuse to obey your superiors for love of him, or obey unwillingly and with indignation. He, for your salvation, not only endured with the greatest patience throughout his whole life the words and reproaches of wicked men, but also tolerated blows and livid stripes and horrid wounds for love of you. Nor did he refuse to undergo a most shameful death, in order to recall your soul from the error of death to the life of eternal light. From the day of his birth he lived poor and humble in the world, and was sated with no tribulations and oppressions, so that he might restore you, a most lost man, to the knowledge and love of God by teaching and benefits. For what would we be if the Son of God had not appeared as man, except most lost? And what are we, if we do not imitate the master according to our strength, except ungrateful? Woe to us ungrateful mortals, who scorn to follow humbly our Lord and Savior. Let us examine our way of life, brothers, and compare the disciples to the master whose name has been invoked over us, and we shall plainly understand how far we have withdrawn from the paths of our most kindly preceptor. But let us return to him with our whole heart, I pray, and let us do repentance, because we have withdrawn far from the way of salvation, serving the flesh rather than the spirit.
