*Of continence and true chastity.*

…to God, in whose words you have sworn; and so live that you may not fear to appear in his judgment. You are monks, segregated from the world by voluntary promise into the ministry of our Lord Jesus Christ; whence it behooves you, under the necessity of salvation, to lead a life higher and holier than the rest of your fellow Christians, and to overcome all depravity of flesh and soul. For it behooves you to be always most chaste in mind and body alike, and stained by no filth of corruptible flesh. An incontinent monk is an apostate; and he who defiles his flesh with the filth of women is like one violating an oath. Lust is the pit of hell; and despair is always next to incontinence. A monk who once slips into the fervor of the flesh is rarely kindled to true compunction of heart. For the vice of fornication, growing strong by long habit, is not easily conquered, unless it be amended by force through contrary remedies. It is of greater difficulty to rise again to true chastity after a fall of the flesh than to remain in continence [already] possessed. It is very easy, by the cunning of [feigned] uprightness, to fall into any vice whatever, [rather] than to amend a sin strengthened by habit. For, as a certain gentile truly says, "easy is the descent"; [but] to come together to true [chastity] from the flesh—"this is the work, this the labor." Let us consider those who have fallen into this most shameful pit, and afterward, compelled to penance by the authority of [their] elders, [observe] how they behave in the midst of the brothers. Truly they have no signs of [true] compunction, but in dryness of heart they lead a restless life. No devotion of mind is seen in them, but everywhere they follow the solace of the flesh. O how rarely is anyone converted to the sweetness of inner love who is once cast headlong into the pit of fornication! For unless, before he comes into this misery, he has been founded in the fear of the Lord, [once] fallen he recurs to true compunction with difficulty. Live therefore soberly, dearest brothers, and always watch in holy prayers; for unless you walk most cautiously in all things, you will by no means be able to escape the snares of the demons. Love abstinence; guard humility above all things. For no one can press down the motions of his flesh who has scorned to subdue gluttony and pride. The insolent demon of fornication is never better conquered than if true humility of heart be preserved in the fullness of all virtues. For just as the omnipotent God, resisting the proud, gives grace to the humble, so the devil, fleeing the humble, casts the proud headlong into fornication. He, therefore, who—gluttonous, eating and drinking to satiety—desires to conquer the spirit of fornication, as it were carries oil to the fire. And he who strives to extinguish the motions of lust by abstinence alone is like a man who is seen to swim in the midst of the waves with one hand. Let continual prayer to God be joined to abstinence, and one will sail securely in the ship of holy humility. Let prayer always be in the monk's mouth, and the memory of the passion and death of the Lord Jesus; let him never withdraw from the meditation of it. For nothing so strengthens the mind and soul against the demon of fornication as devout meditation on the passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. As often, therefore, as you feel an inordinate motion of the flesh, as often as [you] perceive the invisible enemy at hand to harm [you], straightway flee to the Son of God hanging on the cross; and with knees bent to the ground let such a prayer be formed by [you]: "O Jesus, my crucified love, by the pain of your wounds have mercy on me; by the shedding of your most holy blood help me in this struggle." And by the power of the Savior all petitions are made firm. Nothing more attenuates the ambushes of the ancient enemy, nothing more weakens the strength of his malice, than if a devout servant of Christ [perseveres] in the meditation of the Lord's most bitter passion. As anyone flees before the face of the pursuing judge, so the devil swiftly vanishes from the sight of a monk meditating with attention on the passion and death of the Savior; [and] his motion of wicked thought is extinguished, when the bitterness of Christ's passion is recalled to the mind. Just as snow is dissolved by the warming sun, so the devil is put to flight by meditation on the Lord's passion. The greatest defense of chastity is always to invoke the divine help. But unless straightway, from the beginning of the temptation, you run to the Lord—but [delay]—little by little, from the delay [comes] detriment. For [if] you admit delay in pernicious thoughts, the demons soon bring on an insensible blindness of mind
