*Homily XVIII.*
*Fol. XLII.*

...someone passing along the public road, and, as though he had presented himself as a traveler because of the rain, he threw him into the mud; and by abusing the man with injury he made a pitiable spectacle of his own shamelessness. No one was present to restrain the insolent men; nevertheless the patience of the monk overcame the furious men, and, full of love for Christ, when he was being badly treated he answered nothing other than “Thanks be to God.” Hence, the more he showed himself tranquil in mind and spirit, the more the rascals dragged about the innocent man, thinking that he was heated with wine, whom the love of Christ had made joyful and glad.

He left to all monks a great lesson of patience: although he could have avenged himself, he did not wish to do so when wronged. Would that, my brothers, for the love of God and for our sins, we were as patient in the smallest things as that good man was in the greatest. There are among you certain men, I say it unwillingly, too insolent and impatient, who are moved to anger by any word, even one not insulting; and when they are justly corrected for their negligences, they murmur unjustly, adding sin to sin through impatience.

But to what end do I set before them the patience of that remembered man, when they do not heed Jesus Christ, the most patient of all men? For indeed, whoever does not embrace patience and humility by the example of the King of heaven, by the imitation of no [unclear] [unclear].
