Let us therefore pray in the highest fervor of spirit, and let us live with as much purity of conscience as we can, since that man's prayer is perfect and pleasing to God whose cause and tongue both cry out, whose deed and speech agree with his supplication, whose life and thought lend support to his prayers for protection. For without the righteousness of truth every prayer will be fruitless, since we know that God does not hear sinners, and that salvation is far from sinners, as Scripture says: He who turns his ears away so that he does not hear the law, his prayer will be abominable. The holy prophet also says in the psalm: If I have regarded iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me. For just as no medicine helps in a wound unless the iron is first drawn out, so the prayer of that man profits nothing if deceit still remains in his mind or hatred in his breast.

But in order that God may hear your prayer, first of all the conscience must be cleansed from every stain of mortal sin, and the mind must take up a true purpose of amendment. To pray to God and at the same time offend in many things: what else is this than to mock God? For in two ways prayer is intercepted so that it is not heard by the Lord: one, when the one praying still commits evil by sinning; the other, if for God's sake he does not forgive his debtor the debt. You, therefore, who desire to have the fruit of your prayers, who wish to come with the penitent to the mercy of God, take care that you do nothing evil against the will of God, and take diligent care that you truly forgive from the heart all things committed against you by men. For you know that the Lord said: Forgive, and you shall be forgiven.

But after you have cleansed your conscience from evil and forgiven every offense to your neighbor, then you will be fit for prayer if, in the fervor of holy purpose, you have been rightly ordered toward God. Now approach confidently to pray to the Lord; prostrate yourself humbly in his sight, and pray with purity of heart and fervor of spirit. Do not cease from prayer as long as you live in the flesh. Let your prayer be pure, simple, clear, and manifest; let it be full of gravity and weight, and adorned with the beauty of true humility. Do not ask in prayer for anything contrary to the salvation of the soul, nor pray too importunately for a temporal thing, since it is shamelessness to ask God for riches, which provide the greatest occasion for pride and frequently stain Christian purity.

*Folio XVIII.*

It is not shameless if you ask for daily bread, since it is one thing to seek material from which you may become proud, and another to ask for the necessary food by which you may live. Do not justify yourself while praying, lest you depart condemned like the Pharisee; nor ask anything from the Lord as though it were owed because of your merits. Rather, humble yourself in all things, so that you may be worthy to receive grace. Remember the humility of the publican, and pray with such humility that you may find with him forgiveness of sins and justification of the soul. For the grace of God is more abundant than the prayer of the suppliant, and when we pray he grants us more than we ask.

You should not pray with a cry of the voice to him who knows the hidden things of the heart, but in fervor of spirit and with the inward cry of the mind, strike the ears of divine majesty. Nor should you draw out a length of words before him who is spirit, because God will be appeased not by much speaking but by the pure mind of the one praying. At the time of prayer cast away from yourself all malice and disquiet of heart, and, as we often say, forgive if you have anything against your neighbor. For unless you have forgiven your neighbor every debt for love of the Savior, you will obtain nothing from the Lord by your prayers.

It is said that there is a certain kind of serpent which, when it wishes to go into the water to drink, first vomits out all its poison. You too, monk, imitate this serpent in this respect, and vomit out every poison of anger and bitterness, forgiving your brother the hundred denarii, so that the Lord may forgive you ten thousand talents. As you desire the Lord to be toward you, so you too should show yourself toward your fellow servant. Call upon the Lord with your whole heart and you will find him. Let your prayer be ardent and very pure, so that your mind may be joined to his most holy love.

Strive for this throughout your whole life: to offer pure prayer to the Lord God at all times. Never cease in prayer, and do not despair of the mercy of the Lord. What God is going to grant, even if he delays it, he does not take away. He does not act with fickleness, nor is his judgment easily moved; what he has promised is fixed. If you pray earnestly, you will see a miracle. Remember that you stand beneath the eyes of God, who understands all the secrets of hearts and knows the hidden things of the mind.
