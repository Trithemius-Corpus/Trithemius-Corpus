*On the reading and study of the divine Scriptures: Homily Four.*

Since every Scripture, divinely inspired, as the holy apostle of Christ says, is useful for teaching, or for arguing, for correcting, for training in justice, so that the man of God may be perfect, equipped for every good work, we ought gladly to devote ourselves to the reading of the divine Scriptures, and to direct all the zeal of secular literature in which we once labored toward the point of this contemplation. For in the divine Scriptures whatever saving knowledge requires is found most abundantly expressed, so that what you do not find there is in no way necessary for salvation. For the majesty of the divine Scriptures far surpasses all the knowledge and teaching of the whole world: the Holy Spirit poured it into the men of God so that it might be written, and, so that it may be understood by studious readers, bestows the grace of his illumination.

Turn this Scripture over diligently, my brothers, with purity of mind and the fervor of twofold charity, because whoever truly loves God spends in sacred readings all the time he can find for leisure. Let there be for you, I pray, incentives toward the wholesome study of the Scriptures in the frenzied deeds, so to speak, of carnal lovers: when they receive the letters and gifts of their mistresses, they do not cease to read them, and often read them again and kiss them; and, what is a greater sign of love, they hang them either from the neck or at the breast, so that the dear memory of her whom they desire may never depart from the mind. Consider, brothers, the madness of carnal people in love, and blush at your own lukewarmness toward the love of God. Which of you is affected toward the holy Scriptures of God with such love of heart as a blind lover is inflamed with desire toward the filthy, utterly vain, and most mad love-letters of one little prostitute? He, because of love for the absent woman whom he desires, follows her letters and little gifts with repeated kisses; examining each stroke because of excessive love, he searches it out with repeated looks; in everything he does he always thinks of the absent woman; anxiously he hastens to come into her sight.

O if we who are called Christian monks, who serve the supreme and immortal king Christ Jesus, who are commanded to love our God above all things, and are ordered to love nothing in this world except him: if, I say, we pursued our creator with as great a love as carnally minded men observe and love their corruptible and mortal girlfriends. Holy Scripture, read every day, brings God to our minds; it makes his will manifest to us; it instructs us what we ought to do, and lays open what we should beware. Through it the Almighty speaks with us, and, forming us by an invisible instruction, consoles us, as the holy apostle says: For whatever things have been written were written for our instruction, so that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we may have hope.

Therefore the true soldier of Christ, and especially the cloistered one, who loves God in his whole heart and desires to please him alone, embraces the divine Scriptures, and most of all the holy Gospel, with affection, and is constantly occupied in reading it with meditation. But whoever neglects the reading of the divine Scriptures plainly shows that he is not a true lover of God. But what does Scripture itself say? He who turns away his ear so as not to hear the law, his prayer will be detestable. Blessed indeed are those who search the testimonies of the Lord; with their whole heart they seek him. Therefore you, my brothers, if you truly love the Lord Jesus in your whole heart, if as true soldiers you desire to please him above all things, if finally you desire to advance toward the perfection of the monastic life, ought always to occupy yourselves in the reading of the divine Scriptures with fervent affection.

How useful the reading of the Scriptures is, blessed Jerome indicates, saying: Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh. For after God nothing in this life is taken up more sweetly, nothing is felt more pleasantly, nothing more powerfully separates the mind from love of this world, nothing so strengthens the soldier of Christ against all the temptations of adversaries, nothing disposes and confirms the monk so greatly for every good work, as frequent reading and ardent study of the Scriptures.
