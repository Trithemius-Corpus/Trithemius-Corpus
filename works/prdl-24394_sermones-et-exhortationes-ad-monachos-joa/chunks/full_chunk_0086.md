And great is the humility of power, which made the king of heaven and earth withdraw from the face of one most vile man, and dwell for a seven-year exile in the land of the Egyptians. You compelled Christ Jesus, the only-begotten Son of God, to assume a human body for our salvation, and made him undergo also the most bitter passion and death by his own voluntary will. And great is your dominion, O venerable humility of holiness, since the kingdom of heaven is promised to you, to whose worshippers the gift of everlasting blessedness is granted; and whoever keeps to your footprints is raised up to the height of the angels, as Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.” And the king of heaven says to certain [unclear]: “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” The most blessed mother also, the untouched Virgin Mary, in her sweetest canticle of praise, speaks thus of Almighty God: “He has put down the mighty from their seats, and has exalted the humble.” And the prophet says in the psalm: “The Lord has looked upon the prayers of the humble, and has not despised their petitions.” For the prayer of the humble penetrates the clouds, and until it draws near it will not be comforted, and it will not depart until the Most High looks upon it. Humility makes the monk who is pure in his conscience most pleasing to God; and the more he casts himself down by humbling himself for God’s honor, the more glorious he will appear in the kingdom. The monk who truly humbles himself becomes a son of the Most High through participation in grace, and with the king of heaven, Jesus Christ, he will rejoice forever. He who truly desires to imitate Christ should also take up true humility; clothed and fortified with it as with a breastplate of strength, he may do great things, and, thinking only lowly things of himself, may not incur the stain of vainglory. For this is the true perfection of monks: always humbly to recognize one’s own imperfection, and to consider rather what good is lacking to him than what he has in the world. For true humility always stretches forward to what lies ahead through the desire of charity, and, carefully mindful of things past lest it lose the merits it has gained, and desiring to acquire through the zeal of devotion the things not yet possessed, in the fervor of holiness
