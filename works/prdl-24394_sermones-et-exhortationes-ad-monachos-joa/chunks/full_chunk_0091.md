The disobedience of the first man lost the life of eternal happiness from the beginning; the obedience of Jesus Christ restored it. For he was made obedient to the Father for us even unto death, and by his most innocent passion he restored to us the happiness that had been lost. Therefore the path to salvation is humble obedience, as our holy lawgiver, blessed Benedict, cries out in the Rule: “Listen, O son, to the precepts of the master, and incline the ear of your heart, and willingly receive and effectively fulfill the admonition of a loving father, so that by the labor of obedience you may return to him from whom you had withdrawn through the sloth of disobedience.”

Obedience is the tree of eternal salvation, which grows in the garden of monastic humility. Obedience is the dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and the quiet tomb of one’s own will. Obedience is the merit of heavenly happiness and the one lesson of monastic discipline. Obedience is the extermination of all self-exaltation and pride, and the persecutor of all vices. Obedience overcomes wickedness and extinguishes every disordered pleasure of the flesh. Obedience makes the conscience pure and clean, and preserves true humility in the mind. Obedience is the remedy of the wounded soul and the foundation of the whole monastic observance. Obedience is the faithful custody of all good works and the discipline of a sincere mind. Obedience is the beginning of mortification and the glorious ornament of the monk. Obedience is the preserver of peace and concord; without it the vigor of monastic discipline perishes. Obedience always directs the keen edge of the mind toward God; and he who has truly been mortified in spirit despises all the consolations of this world. Obedience makes the monk near to God and far removed from all the filth of the soul. Obedience is the adornment of holiness and the garment of beauty; without it no one would come to the kingdom of eternal glory. Obedience is the mother of victory, and he who knows how, for the honor of God, to be humbly subject will obtain the crown of martyrdom in heaven. Obedience separates the monk from the world, and whoever follows it with his whole heart will be great before the Most High. Obedience is the consummation of Christian perfection; in its humble workshop, the more a monk advances, the holier he becomes. Obedience is sure security at the hour of death; for the more ready the monk was for obedience to commands, the more securely he will fly away when he departs from here. Obedience is the rewarder of humility and the chief mark of merits. Obedience is the rule of Christian gentleness and the strongest nourishment of inward charity. Obedience is the shelter of chastity and the purest ornament of innocence. Obedience is [unclear] voluntary mortification; its merit is of such dignity that he who is rebellious and proud wears the monastic habit without cause. Obedience is the voluntary mortification of the members; its merit is of such dignity that man has nothing greater to offer in honor of God.

Let each one offer, for love of God, what he can: without doubt that man has offered more than all who has, because of God, killed his own will through obedience. It is indeed a great good to distribute one’s possessions, for love of Christ, to the use of the poor, and to relieve the want of the needy with alms. But as much as the whole outweighs the part, as much as the free choice of the will is nobler than necessity, as much as heaven is distant from earth, so much more has that man offered who, out of love for God, has by a spontaneous will denied himself forever. For whatever temporal substance you have offered to God is not yours but another’s, and is of less value than your own, both in estimation and in reward. You have one thing of your own which, offered above all things, is pleasing to God. What is this, unless the free will which the almighty Creator has placed in your power? A good will, however, is greater than all riches, because nothing richer is offered to God, nothing is consecrated more acceptably. This most precious gift to God, my brothers, you once offered when, denying yourselves and all worldly things for his honor, you put on the monastic habit.
