Our monks seek holiness without the merit of virtues; they wish to have merit without the exercise of labor; for that reason a saint is not found today in the monastery. The fervor of holy compunction in very many is either rare or lukewarm, because where one lives for flesh and blood, the fervor of inward charity is not possessed. It is not enough for you, O monk who glory in observance, to have nothing of your own, not to touch a woman, to reside in the monastery under obedience to the shepherd, not to eat meat, and in the other things that are external matters of regular discipline, for the acquisition of which all other things have chiefly been instituted. Therefore without the charity of God all things profit you nothing, because it alone and uniquely is what distinguishes between good and evil works. O monk, monk, whoever you are who live lukewarmly in the monastery without the fervor of charity, do you think that the outward apparatus of discipline can make you safe? Charity is what sanctifies the one who has it; charity commends the other virtues and all good works to God. Have charity, and all the works of piety that you do will be pleasing to God.

*On the arduous way that leads to life.*

*Sermon IV.*

You have heard, most dear brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ saying in today’s reading in the holy Gospel: “How narrow is the gate and strait the way that leads to life, and few are those who find it.” Is not Christ himself the way, who had said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life”? But how does he now call it strait in the Gospel, when he himself is the one who had said: “Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, because I am meek and humble of heart. For my yoke is sweet, and my burden light”? What else is the yoke of Christ but the strait and narrow way to the kingdom of God? How then is it also narrow? Let your charity hear. The yoke of the Lord Jesus is sweet to the good, hard and unbearable to the reprobate. The way of Christ to life is broad and pleasant to those trained in humble charity, but narrow and bitter to the proud. Nothing is so hard, nothing so terrible, nothing so arduous, that it is not easy and pleasant to one who loves God. Again, nothing is so light, nothing so easy, nothing so small, that it is not heavy and burdensome to an evil will. Therefore the Lord said: “Learn from me, because I am meek and humble of heart.” If you wish to carry my yoke upon you, first learn the meekness and humility of my heart, because unless you are meek and humble, you will by no means be able to carry my burden, as though it were a heavy weight and an unbearable load. I am a humble way; I do not contain the proud. I am a narrow gate; I do not admit the swollen with pride. Unless you despise in spirit all things that are worldly, unless [unclear] the pleasures of the flesh with necessary diligence, as [unclear] burdened with sins, you cannot enter the kingdom through me. I am a humble and meek way, and I receive the humble; I am a strait door, and I admit the poor in spirit and the clean of heart.

Brothers, we are hastening to eternal life by the narrow way of truth; it is necessary that we procure in time all things that are necessary for so arduous a journey. There are, moreover, four things especially necessary for travelers; without them the journey is dangerous, and the goal is rarely reached.

The first thing that we say is necessary for travelers we believe to be knowledge of the roads. For one who strives to walk far through trackless places without knowledge of the roads is sometimes entangled in dangerous errors. Therefore let the prudent traveler either first learn the road, or take to himself a skilled companion for the journey, lest he either fall into the hands of robbers or depart from the straight path of the journey. Therefore, since we desire to return to the fatherland of the heavenly kingdom by a very narrow way known to few, it is necessary that we have as guide and companion of the journey Jesus Christ himself, who named himself the way and the truth. Outside Christ there is no true knowledge; he who neglects to have Christ as the companion of his journey runs in vain and without fruit. Therefore let us learn the way of life, most dear brothers, without knowledge of which no one reaches the fatherland, no one will come to eternal life. Let us firmly believe our Lord Jesus Christ, the one mediator of God and human beings, true God and human being, outside faith in whom no one will be saved, however innocently he may seem to live.
