For God wills that everyone fulfill his holy will, which no one who is ignorant of it is able to do. Therefore the Lord first commands that you learn what is pleasing to him; secondly, that you do it. How then will you think ignorance of the law excusable, through which you have first become transgressors of the will of God?

Why do you think almighty God handed over to human beings the volumes of his Scriptures? Was it so that they should read them, and that they should be understood by them, or so that, unread, they should be despised? Certainly the Scriptures of God’s commandments were given to mortals so that they should read them, and thereafter so that they should fulfill them in deed. But you, who with deliberate mind wish to be ignorant of God’s writings: how [unclear] God’s pleasure handed over the volumes of Scripture to all his own? Or will you be able to be useful, who were not hearers before?

For it can happen that one who knows the will of God either observes it or neglects it; but it cannot happen that one who is ignorant of it fulfills it. Indeed, someone will more easily fail to do the good that he knows than fulfill in deed what he does not know. No one does the good that he does not know. Saint Augustine says in a certain place concerning the ignorant: Not everyone who is ignorant is free from fault. For that ignorant person can be excused from punishment who did not find what he might learn. But it will not be possible to pardon those who, having persons from whom they might learn, did not give their effort to instruction.

Hence also Saint Leo the pope says: If ignorance seems intolerable in laypeople, how much more in those who are in authority; nor is it worthy of excuse or pardon. Therefore, brothers, ignorance of the divine Scriptures does not make you excused before God, but doubly guilty, you who desire to be utterly ignorant of what it is fitting for you to do concerning the will of God. For a man ought not to be ignorant of the divine will [unclear]; it is necessary for the salvation of every mortal, which [unclear] he will find without knowledge of Scripture.

Indeed, just as from knowledge [unclear] the fear of God comes into human beings, and from its knowledge love is born, so from ignorance there comes [unclear] you will think excusable, through which you have first become transgressors of the will of God. The Scriptures of God’s commandments were given; thereafter, that they should be fulfilled in deed.

But you, who [unclear] disciples, shamefully ignorant in mind, unruly and wandering, to whom we impute ignorance all the more because they are less inclined to discipline. It is shameful indeed not to know what you are ordered to know; more shameful not to learn it; but most shameful of all to despise it. And there are some among you who do not know saving knowledge [unclear], if I should add that there are also others who utterly despise the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Certainly I do not lie: you know that what I say is true.

For some of you have opposed my studies gravely, even to the point of envy, seeking, at their urging, to bring me into denial of the Scriptures, so that they might make me a tiller of fields and a keeper of pigs rather than of souls. But they strive in vain to hinder the divine oracle; under its impulse I have resolved, as long as I live, to set all the honors and riches of the world beneath the study of the Scriptures.

I am your abbot as long as he who called me shall will it. If one of the two is necessary, I prefer to give up the abbacy rather than renounce the holy study of the Scriptures. For I know truly that nothing more salutary can befall a cloistered man in this life than if he has begun truly to love the divine Scriptures.

Whoever among you wishes to follow my instruction will have me as leader of the journey and likewise as companion. But whoever has scorned to follow me as I learn will nevertheless be compelled to hear me as I teach, even unwillingly. These things have been said against those who, savoring earthly and passing things, cast away from themselves the fear of the Lord; who, ignorant of almost everything, despise learning.
