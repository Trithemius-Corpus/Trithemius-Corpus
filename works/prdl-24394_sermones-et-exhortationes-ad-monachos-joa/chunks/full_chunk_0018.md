*On the reading and study of the divine Scriptures*

A man polluted with filth is forbidden. For thus it is read in Exodus: “Take care that you do not go up onto the mountain, nor touch its borders. Everyone who touches the mountain shall surely die. Whether it be beast or man, it shall not live.” A man indeed touches the mountain and dies whenever someone, puffed up with secular literature, takes offense at the simplicity of the divine Scriptures. But a beast touches the mountain and is overwhelmed with stones whenever a man, sunk in the fury of carnal passions, advances to the study of sacred Scripture without a purified mind.

If, therefore, you wish profitably to turn over the readings of the divine Scriptures, if by reading you desire to arrive at true knowledge, take care first of all to put away every swelling of pride and every rashness of unbelief, and then to cleanse the chamber of your heart from all uncleanness of carnal pleasures. For God, as canonical Scripture says, resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. No proud, inflated, arrogant, and rash man, no man of carnal mind, luxurious or restless, is held fit for the study of sacred Scripture, but only the humble, gentle, mild, modest, chaste man, tranquil in body and mind.

[Dine prosper?] says in an epigram: “A mind that is not quiet cannot search the commandments of God. Therefore, that religious study may be exercised, the quarrels of the wicked must be cast aside. Hearts free from the tumult of the world may search the law in whatever quiet they can. Therefore, that the mind may be found intent upon pious studies, let divine love conquer fleshly battles. For the temple of the Lord grows through sacred threads, and silently the house that will not fall is built.”

Therefore proceed to the study of the Scriptures with devoted minds and with a heart pierced in the love of God and humbled in true contrition, because the Lord will not enlighten you unless he has first made you cleansed from every iniquity. For, as Scripture itself says, perverse thoughts separate from God; but proven virtue corrects the foolish, since wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sins; for the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit and will withdraw himself from thoughts that are without understanding. Therefore we must pray more often to the Lord with holy David: “Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within my inward parts.” For no one understands the holy Scriptures of God usefully who does not turn himself to the Lord with his whole heart through [unclear].

Therefore, if you wish to understand the divine utterances both rightly and profitably, take care first of all with the utmost zeal to keep yourselves, as far as you can, clean from every pollution of sin. I beg you, do not let the mad folly of certain monks seduce you, who try to excuse their ignorance with a worn-out proverb, saying: “What need have we of knowledge of the Scriptures, we who have no office of preaching? We embrace the counsel of the wise man, who says in the book of Proverbs: ‘He who walks simply walks confidently.’ It is enough for us to live simply, since the servant who knew his lord’s will and did not do it will be beaten with many blows; but he who did not know it, and committed things worthy of blows, will be beaten with few.”

O foolish and utterly ruined [unclear], who despise saving knowledge and love ignorance of the divine Scriptures rather than understanding. Will deliberately sought ignorance make you liable to fewer blows? Or will you therefore be guiltless before God when you sin, because you are voluntarily ignorant of God’s commandments? You will be tormented with a twofold punishment: one because you have despised knowing the way of God’s commandments, the other because you do not keep his words. What you allege is not simplicity, but rather a double impiety. For if, according to the prophet, blessed are they who search the testimonies of the Lord, who could doubt that those are accursed who neither seek God nor attend to his Scriptures, but rather despise them with hardened mind?

Hear what Solomon says to you in Proverbs: “Where there is no knowledge of the soul, there is no good, and the folly of a man supplants his steps.” Of you also, O most foolish men of darkness, that saying of Saint Jerome is understood: that there ignorance is no longer excusable, where it is known what it is that is being ignored. But you must beware still more lest at last you be counted with those who said to God: “Depart from us; we do not want the way of your sciences.”

You understand Solomon’s word wrongly, who think it was said simply about ignorance. “He who walks simply,” without any deception of his neighbor, “walks confidently” in keeping God’s commandments; but he who corrupts his ways cannot lie hidden, but will be made manifest.
