Sixth, what we judge necessary for our traveler is that he diligently love the saving study of the Scriptures. Let him not be curious in reading, nor given over to vain studies, but simple, pure, and upright in mind.

The monk who recognizes that he is a pilgrim and stranger in this world never forgets the heavenly homeland, since he sees nothing on the way of the present life in which the soul should take delight.

The eternal traveler [unclear] considers no spectacles of the road; nothing among passing things delights him in his pilgrimage, but at every time he sighs for the end of the journey. He always thinks sweetly of the homeland; he bears heavily that his pilgrimage is prolonged or delayed, and therefore he desires to have nothing in this world except the Lord Jesus, into whose sight he hastens diligently to appear.

Eighth, necessary for our traveler is the tireless exercise of the love of God.

For no one will be able to come to the heavenly homeland who departs, he says, from the path of charity. The charity of God is the way to God; if anyone has neglected it, burning within himself even briefly, he has strayed miserably from the true road of the heavenly homeland.
