*Homily IX. Folio XXII.*

... the necessity of sinning is generated. Call it the most harsh dominion of death: here is the uttermost depth of evils. Whoever has once entered this abyss of sins rarely, indeed not easily, rises again to purity of heart without the special grace of God and the very great labor of repentance. By his own will the sinner has fallen into this pit of death; but without God's mercy he will not be able to rise again. Most harsh is the yoke of evil habit; if anyone has once submitted his neck to it, he rarely grows strong again for the former freedom of the spirit.

All these things perverse thought brings about when it is admitted into the mind and, through delight, is changed into consent and deed. For no thought is so small that it does not grow little by little if it is not driven from the mind. Blessed, therefore, is that monk who casts every thought of his upon the Lord; who always and everywhere refuses a place to wicked thoughts; who despises all the perishable riches of the world; who contemns all comforts of the body. He prepares cleanness of heart for his Lord; he loves nothing in this world except the Lord; he orders all his thoughts to the glory of the Savior; and he observes his commandments most diligently.

What is happier than that monk who presents himself to the world not as a citizen but as a pilgrim? He lives in the world as though he were not in the world; indeed he is greater than the world, for his whole way of life is in heaven. Consider now, therefore, dearest brothers, the brevity of this time, and always remember that you are mortal and soon to pass away. For we have here no abiding city, but we seek the one to come; and since we know that so many thousands of human beings have preceded us through death, let us think how soon we too must necessarily die.

A time, now indeed brief, but very acceptable, is granted to us, in which we may work the fruits of eternal life. For now is the time for laboring; now is the hour for sowing what is good, because after death each one will receive the reward of his labors, when no further opportunity for laboring is granted. Therefore, my brothers, anticipate this hour of death with holy works, and do not now fail in the sowing of labor, so that in eternal recompense you may be able to rejoice without end. Let no day pass in which each one of you has not become better and has not added something new to the good works he has already sent ahead. Always think upon the value of the future harvest, and no greatness of labor will terrify you.

*On the Daily Struggle of Temptation: Homily Ten.*

In yesterday's sermon, dearest brothers, I admonished your Charity to resist the beginnings of evil thoughts, and to prepare yourselves with all your strength, by good and holy works, for the prize of future reward. But since, as Scripture bears witness, the life of man upon earth is a temptation, I have considered it necessary to discourse in your hearing, as much as the Lord shall grant, on this very matter, so that, forewarned, you may be able to fight more strongly against the invisible adversary.

You are not ignorant of the words of the wise man, who says in Ecclesiasticus: “Son, when you come to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear, and prepare your soul for temptation.” Let no one think himself free from temptation, brothers, as long as we walk in this mortal way of life; but the more each one has advanced in the fear of God, the more cautiously he should know that he must live. For holy Pope Gregory says: “The more our enemy sees that he is being rebelled against, the more fully he strives to attack.”

You, O men, the more fully you advance in the spiritual way of life, the more attentively must you keep watch against the adversary of your salvation. For, as Saint John Chrysostom says, our adversary never sleeps, but always keeps watch for the destruction of the human race; none of us can resist him except one who has learned at all times to keep watch in the fear of the Lord. And Saint Leo of the city of Rome says in a certain sermon: “The ancient enemy does not cease everywhere to set out the snares of temptations, so that in whatever way he may be able, by corrupting, to blacken the faith of believers. For he knows into whom to cast the enticements of the flesh, into whom to pour the poison of envy, to whom to apply the incitements of lust.
