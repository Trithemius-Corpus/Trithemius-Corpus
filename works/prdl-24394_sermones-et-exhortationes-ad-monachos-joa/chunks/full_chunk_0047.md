For the monk who is negligent as a novice and young man passes all the days of his life in sluggishness and idleness. For Scripture says: “A young man walking according to his own way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.”

Therefore I say to you, venerable elders; I beg you, I admonish you, and by the mercy of God I ask you with all the insistence I can: so live from now on, so conduct yourselves, so walk, that your habits may accord with the years of old age, and that you who have already advanced far into old age may provide the younger men with an example of holiness for imitation. Without doubt there is ascribed to you whatever is found lacking in the habits of the younger men. For you ought to go before the younger men in all holiness, in word and in deed; you are commanded to show them an example of all perfection, so that, when they behold in you the perfect works of all virtues, stirred as if by a certain impulse of shame, they may be roused to imitation of your holiness. For the habits of elders provide the younger with an example of living, as is said in the proverb: what the old men have sung, this the young have learned.

But you younger men, show reverence with humility to the elders, considering their grey hair; and those whom you know to be more fervent in the observance of regular discipline, rejoice to have them as instructors by their company. Beware of those who either walk restlessly or observe the purity of regular discipline rather negligently. Not all who are called elders are worthy of the imitation of the younger; nor do years make a monk venerable, but habits established in the love of God with prudence make him worthy of reverence.

Therefore, whoever among you, dearest brothers, desires to be a true lover of God, whoever desires to make progress in the holy monastic way of life which he professes, let him always flee the company of undisciplined brothers, and let him seek comrades in arms who consent with his own purpose toward the good. For the prophet says in the psalm: “With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man you will be innocent, and with the chosen you will be chosen, and with the perverse you will be perverted.” For evil fellowship has harmed many, and he who delights to dwell with the reprobate will not be free from the contagion of sin.

Indeed everyone who keeps company with evil and reprobate men must necessarily have his integrity somewhat spoiled by their perversity. For either he will love them and, made like them, will become evil; or he will hate them and will be the enemy of many, and for that reason necessarily restless. But if, overcome by the multitude of the wicked, he has once begun to delight in their company, then he too has already been corrupted, and little by little he will be corrupted still more. But if he has detested the works of the evil, he will labor under a grave distress of mind; and his desire for progressing to what is better will be so much slower in him, the more he is disturbed by the company of the perverse.

Yet I give thanks to almighty God for you, because, even if not all of you are fervent in charity as I would wish, nevertheless, as I trust, you all live far removed from great sins and from the rashness of pride. And the exhortations and fatherly admonitions which I give you, as I am bound to do daily, you receive gladly, although you do not yet all display them perfectly in practice. But I hope in the Lord Jesus that he will perfect in you the good which he has begun, so that my joy may be fulfilled, and I may see you all, with one zeal, one purpose, and one flame of holy fervor burning, delighting and exercising yourselves in the workshop of divine love.

But because some of you defer this desire of mine, not yet taking up the spirit of fuller amendment which I desire, I am compelled by just necessity to admonish those who are making progress, that, rising up with a manly spirit, they flee the private and harmful associations of the lukewarm. For as yet the fire of divine love is small in their hearts; as yet the newly kindled and weak flame of holy fervor burns, and unless it is fostered by daily increases it will easily suffer loss. For that reason again and again I admonish you: if you find any opposed, by deed or speech, to the beginnings of inward love, avoid them as a pestilential poison. For just as water extinguishes the fire on which it is poured, so the holy purpose of novices is violated by the examples of the lukewarm.
