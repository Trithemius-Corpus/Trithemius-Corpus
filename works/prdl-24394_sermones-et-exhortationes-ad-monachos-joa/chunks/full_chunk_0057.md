There are some among you who take corrections very hard. To them we declare what we have often said, that in the future they must endure very grievous things, they who refuse to endure small and light things here. But I, considering their weakness and ignorance, am gravely tormented within myself, because I see their minds wounded, and yet I am afraid to apply the necessary medicines. For if I apply remedies suited to the diseases, I fear lest, made fainthearted, they fall into despair, or certainly, given over to a reprobate mind, withdraw entirely from their holy purpose. Therefore I use as much discretion as I can, as all of you will bear witness for me, preferring to send the weak through patience with charity to purgatory rather than, from just severity, to have the despairing descend into hell.

For who in this mortality could ever make satisfaction for sins who did not apply a good will to penitence? For good will alone commends the works of penitents. But although many are punished unwillingly, they are still far removed from perfect satisfaction. Believe me, my most beloved brothers, that satisfaction for sins is commended more by suffering than by acting. For even the Lord and Savior of us all, Jesus, by suffering expiated the sin of the whole world on the altar of the cross. Would that you knew how effective a medicine it is for the soul wounded by sins to receive proclamations and corrections with humble patience, and also, for the increase of perfect satisfaction, to bear injuries and insults with equanimity. I am certain that humble patience in adversity far surpasses every kind of satisfaction; and the readier the mind has been to bear insults and injuries for the love of Christ, the more easily and quickly it will obtain from the Lord pardon for all its sins and a fuller indulgence.

For this place in which we now speak is the workshop of purgation and expiation, in which zeal for regular discipline holds primacy, discretion presides over examination, charity directs and completes all things. Hence in this very place patience is very necessary for you; without its presence no satisfaction will be consummated. The monk who is a zealous lover of his salvation loves this place as a workshop of expiation, and never goes forth from it without having been made better than himself. For this place is to be feared and dreaded by demons, but dear and greatly venerable to the holy angels of God; in it the fault of humble monks is washed away, and whatever is committed openly is also expiated publicly before all. But if any hidden fault has been committed by any of you, the secret confession of the penitent will likewise purge it away. In both cases pure confession with humility is required, and both here and there patience is judged necessary for you.

Therefore, whoever you are, monk, who desire to be good, prepare your soul in all patience, so that you may hold the tranquility of mind most constant as much in adversities as in prosperities. When you have been proclaimed by some brother for your negligences, patiently [unclear] the rebukes from the senior, you who offend daily in many things. He endured countless injuries most patiently for your salvation, and why should you not suffer humbly for your sins? Bear patiently with all humility, faithfully bringing to mind how innocently the mighty Son of God was accused. For if he, just and innocent, who committed no sin, suffered insults, why should you not patiently bear rebuke with tolerance in satisfaction for sins? But if you did not deserve the proclamation in judgment, nevertheless endure it patiently for the love of Christ; now it will be reckoned to you not only as satisfaction for preceding sins, but much more as justification. Therefore whether a monk is proclaimed justly or unjustly, he has no ground for just defense, because if he has sinned he suffers according to his desert; but if he is innocent, he is the more bound to patience. For no one unless patient and humble will be able to imitate Christ. But since the monk is especially bound to be a disciple and imitator of Christ, it is especially fitting for him to be patient.
