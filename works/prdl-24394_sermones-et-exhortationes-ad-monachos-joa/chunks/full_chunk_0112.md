*Letter.*

John Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Sponheim, though unworthy, to the reverend father lord Johannes de Colenhausen, abbot of Seligenstadt, formerly of Sponheim, my dearest predecessor: with prayers, a mind prompt and ready to give pleasure.

Venerable teacher, I recently sent you my first book of exhortations to monks, containing twenty-five homilies; after I learned that it had been approved by your examination, I judged that this second one also, which I now send, should confidently be inscribed to your name. In that one, for the instruction of the brothers, I collected twenty-five homilies which I had delivered before them; in this one I have arranged the sermons by number, according to the order in which they were spoken in their presence.

I do not fear to undergo the examination of your paternity, since I have always had you as a gentle teacher, and long ago found you to be a sincere lover of truth. For you are strong in talent, both mild and profound, directed toward progress. The management of household affairs and the poverty of the place often draw me back from salutary study, and turn my mind away, against the purpose of my will, to the concerns of others. Only on feast days and Sundays is leisure granted me for writing; on these, out of reverence for God, I am not accustomed to admit any business of the peasants. Sometimes also, after the customary matins have been sung, I am accustomed to devote what remains of the night to the study of letters, and, as far as I can, to work by lamplight at something for the instruction of my brothers.

For I desire in all things to follow in your footsteps, and, if I can, perpetually to [unclear] the praiseworthy custom of teaching one’s subjects which you introduced. I know that I am bound to this by my office; and although I am rude and unlearned, nevertheless I willingly wish, according to my powers, to be diligent both in learning and in teaching. I submit these sermons to your examination, and with humble supplication I ask that the file of your mastery correct them.

I have collected a few out of many into order, since I neither committed them all to writing, nor did an excessive extension of the book seem to me either necessary or useful. For on feast days, when we are free from the tumult of worldly business, we devote ourselves to reading according to the rule of the Scriptures. And lest I seem to occupy the place of pastor in vain, what I have worked out by lamplight at night I am accustomed to speak after None, in place of the regular study, and to recite from a small sheet before the brothers in chapter. For I find by experience that this diligence of mine not only profits the brothers for edification, but also helps me toward skill in composition.

Farewell, venerable teacher, and pray for us. From Sponheim, on the twelfth day of the month of September, in the year of the Christians 1486.
