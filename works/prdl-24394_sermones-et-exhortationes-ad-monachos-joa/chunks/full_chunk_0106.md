Let each one of you, as much as he can, strive with the whole strength of his mind to love the Lord his God above all things, because love for God alone is what will open for you the gate of paradise. For the more ardently you now love God, the greater glory you will receive in the heavenly homeland. Happy is that monk in whose conscience, besides the love of Christ, no imagining of any occupation remains; who, waking, thinks of nothing other than Christ; sleeping, dreams of Christ; living and dying, sighs most steadfastly for Christ. O how well and securely he dies who has always been solicitous in the exercise of divine love, and at all times zealous in the observance of God's commandments. For only the love of God is admitted into the kingdom of heaven; and the more fervent the mind has been in loving, the greater will be the reward in the kingdom of heaven. He who loves much, even then, is to receive less in glory. Therefore let each person love as much as he can, from his whole heart and from his whole soul and from his whole strength, because our God is not to be loved lukewarmly or sluggishly, but fervently, wisely, and sweetly. The love of God grows and is increased by spiritual exercises, just as a blazing fire spreads when wood is supplied. For indeed, if the wood is withdrawn, the fire is extinguished; and if spiritual exercises are interrupted, charity is soon shipwrecked. But true love of God is never idle, but at all times is delightfully occupied in those things that nourish and preserve charity. For the lover of his God never forgets his beloved, but always meditates on his presence and beauty; and now in most devout prayers he speaks to Jesus, now in the holy Scriptures he marvels at him speaking to himself. The true lover of Christ meditates day and night on the law of the Lord, and with the greatest diligence does everything that he understands to be pleasing to God. O how sweetly he burns whom the love of God has truly inflamed; how pleasantly he glows whose mind the flame of the Holy Spirit has set alight. Blessed is that monk who, full of divine love, wholly withdraws himself from earthly vanities and rests in the shade of heavenly desire. O if we had love for God so strong, so whole—

*On serenity of conscience.*

*Homily 23.*

With great solicitude we must keep watch, dearly beloved brothers, and with continual meditation take care that we always keep our conscience, whose testimony we cannot evade, clean and unstained from every contagion. Nothing is more beautiful, nothing more pleasant, nothing, finally, more precious than a good conscience; when a mind, having in itself the manifest testimony of its own rectitude, suffers no remorse of destructive unease. An upright and good conscience is an incredible exultation of the mind and a sure consolation in every adversity. The monk who has obtained the testimony of a good conscience neither refuses even to live nor fears the coming of future death. A good conscience is always joyful, always pleasant, always fearless, and truly tranquil in the peace of Jesus Christ. In a good conscience there is always peace, piety, goodness, light, virtue, honor, joy, gladness, sweetness, and pleasantness without dissolution. Conscience is the treasury of the soul, because whatever is placed in it is preserved for the provision of the future life. A good conscience is the best treasure; if anyone neglects to have it, he must be judged the most wretched and most unhappy of all men. A good conscience is the dwelling place of the Holy Spirit and an earthly paradise of all graces.
