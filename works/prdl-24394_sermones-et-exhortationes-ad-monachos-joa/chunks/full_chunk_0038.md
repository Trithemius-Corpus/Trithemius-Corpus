*On the keeping and origin of thoughts*

The holy [fear?] of the Lord never deserts the mind of a wise man, but, like a strong and watchful fighter always standing at the side of his prince, so it guards the [dwelling?] of the heart. It turns its ears away from all buffoonery of words, and closes its eyes lest they see evil. It always walks according to the twelfth degree of humility, with its gaze fixed on the ground, praying with the prophet to the Lord: “Turn my eyes away, lest they see vanity; in your way give me life, Lord.”

Thus I admonish you, brothers: direct your senses in the fear of the Lord, so that you may always be able to keep the dwelling-place of the heart clean and inviolate. Remember the scripture saying: “Be clean, you who bear the vessels of the Lord.” Consider, I beg you, with how great cleanliness and purity of mind you must shine, you who not only by reason of the priestly order bear the vessels of the Lord, but, what is more, are yourselves also vessels of the Lord. For the holy Christ-loving Paul says: “Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ?” And again he says: “Or do you not know that your members are the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own?” And elsewhere: “For the temple of God is holy, which you are.” And concerning this same Paul in the Acts of the Apostles the Lord said to Ananias: “Go, because this man is a vessel of election for me.”

The monk who exercises the priestly order in the church of God is bound by a twofold reason to the observance of most holy purity. For Christ’s soldier must, because of the vow that he made to God, be pure, holy, and constantly unwearied in good works; and by reason of the priestly ministry, circumspect and most diligently watchful. For it is written: “Cursed is the man who does the work of God negligently.” You are ministers of almighty God, who daily at the altar offer up his Son, whose work is entrusted to your hands; and therefore great purity of mind and body is required in your manner of life.

Therefore, so that chaste purity of mind may remain, it is necessary that the instability of thought be restrained. The senses must be guarded at every time, through which the integrity of the mind is corrupted. For you are dead to the world and have now begun to live for Christ. It is not permitted to you to think anything except what is holy; nor is it fitting for you, ministers of God, to love anything earthly. For he whom you serve, O monks, is God; he to whom you minister, my brothers, is almighty. Live pure, holy, and innocent, because it is not fitting for you to be occupied with the business of the world, you who have been called to the soldiery of God. Let the love of God and purity of heart grow in you with time, so that the longer you live in this world, the holier you may become.

Always turn your sight away from every evil, so that you may be able to remain without failure in good thoughts. Always raise the eyes of your mind to heaven, and do not occupy the sight of the flesh with perishable things. Also always close your hearing against every useless, vain, and foolish speech, and always think with fear on the future judgment of God. Do not pollute your hands with illicit and shameful contact of any things, so that you may be able to offer up the Son of God with a clean body and a pure conscience.

In food and drink it is not for monks to discern flavors, nor is it fitting for Christ’s soldiers to love bodily delights. Nature requires few things, and rustic foods by which it may be nourished; but appetite, unless it is restrained by the command of reason, is carried off to illicit things, through which the simplicity of nature is violated. Flee excess in all things, and observe regular temperance in your manner of living.

Let none of you patiently listen to someone disparaging his brother or speaking ill of his neighbor. Let each one of you restrain his tongue from useless speech, and let a good monk blush to speak ill of Christian people. Let that threat of the Lord in the holy Gospel never depart from your memory, when he says: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. A good man brings forth good things from a good treasure, and an evil man brings forth evil things from an evil treasure. But I say to you that every idle word that men have spoken, they shall render an account of it on the day of judgment.”

O sentence greatly to be feared! If an account is demanded for idle speech, what do detraction, buffoonery, and foul speech deserve? Or if a useless word is judged liable to the reason of strict examination, what will he answer who so easily speaks what is evil and pernicious? An idle word is every speech of men that has been uttered without just usefulness, lacking the reason of lawful necessity.
