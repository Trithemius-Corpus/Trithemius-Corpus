Therefore let the monk, Christ’s soldier, be like a clean animal of God, which chews the cud, always having in the mouth of his heart the nourishment of the holy Scriptures, lest the mind lie open to vain and pernicious thoughts, or a foul or idle word break forth from the mouth of the speaker.

Let him always be mindful while working of the Scripture that says: Blessed is the man who meditates on the law of the Lord day and night. For everyone who loves the Lord Jesus in truth renews in his heart every day, with tears and prayers, both his teaching and his passion. For he is not a true lover of God who forgets his commandments even for a moment. Indeed, as Scripture says: Those who love the Lord seek the things that are well pleasing to him; and those who fear his holy name will be filled with his law. Hence one of the saints said to the Lord: In my heart I have hidden your words, that I may not sin against you.

Therefore the soul must always be stirred up, and renewed daily with spiritual goads, lest it grow lukewarm through sloth or languish in the idleness of inertia. The exercise of labor in monastic discipline, zeal for reading and prayer, and diligent keeping of vigils are true incitements to the virtues. But nothing is more destructive to the holy purpose of a monk than idleness, since it both excites the flesh to lust and weakens every virtue of the mind and extinguishes the fervor of the spirit. For idleness in the cloister is the fuel and nourishment of evils. Therefore wherever you may be, whether in the field or in the cloister, always avoid despisers of their own [unclear]. For he who touches pitch will be defiled by it; and he who makes himself a companion of sinners will not be immune from crime. Consider your words carefully, so that you detract from no one at all, nor listen to another detracting; for the detractor and the willing listener alike both serve the demon, the one by speaking, the other by listening.

Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus, and diligently work with your hands at whatever is enjoined on you by obedience; nor let it seem hard to you to obey another’s command, for in return for it a crown of eternal happiness is prepared in heaven for your spirit. Hence no labor, no length of time, no observance ought to seem burdensome to a monk, by which the glory of eternal blessedness is acquired.

Consider, my brothers, the brevity of this life, and do good while it is permitted; for he who now sows sparingly will also reap sparingly after death, and each person will receive a fitting reward for his own work, not for another’s. The time for laboring is very short, but the reward that is owed to good works is everlasting. Consider, my brothers, how strong men entangled in the world and all lovers of this age are in earthly and perishable matters, but how weak and infirm in heavenly matters, so that you should take care to please God as much as they labor continually to appear rich and glorious to the world. For for temporal glory many do not refuse to be defiled even unto death, but for the hope of future happiness they disdain either to do or to endure even a little. For earthly gains they endure any injuries whatever with the greatest patience, and for a heavenly reward they refuse to endure even the insult of the slightest word. They can stand all day with strength before an earthly king, prince, or judge; but in prayer before the almighty Lord they cannot endure for the space of one hour. They willingly endure many adversities to acquire perishable riches, but they neglect heavenly goods all the more, the more delightfully they are occupied in love of transitory things.

Let the conduct of these men admonish you, dearest brothers, to flee all cares of this world so much the more, since it is now permitted to you to handle the business of earthly things less. Always devote all your solicitude and care to the acquisition of heavenly goods, and labor most diligently at all times for the reward of future blessedness. For, as Scripture testifies, your life upon earth is a warfare, and whatever a man has sown, this also he will reap. Know that death hastens on, and at every moment a part of life, never to return again, is extinguished.
