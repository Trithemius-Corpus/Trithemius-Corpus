And hope does not confound, because the charity of God has been poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.” For through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. Indeed, through temptations we become known to ourselves, and we discover whether we truly fear God. Hence the Lord speaks through Moses to the people, saying: “The Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him with all your heart and with all your soul.”

This world, dearest brothers, is to be compared to a furnace, in which the fire of temptation and adversity continually burns, through which we must necessarily pass. In this furnace of tribulation, straw is burned up, but gold and silver are cleansed from every stain. Straw is turned into ash; gold is reserved for the ornament and adornment of the royal palace. The furnace, I say, is the world; the fire is temptation or tribulation; the gold is righteous men; the straw is the unrighteous and sinners; the craftsman who purifies the gold is almighty God himself, who favors our progress and cooperates by grace toward the merit of virtue. Therefore let us do what God wills; what he imposes, let us patiently bear for love of him, since, as holy [Christophilus?] says, “the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which shall be revealed in us.” Hence Saint Jerome says: “Who would not wish to be troubled? Who would not desire to be cursed, so as to gain praise by the voice of Christ and to be rewarded with an abundant heavenly recompense? Would that, for the name and righteousness of my Lord, the whole crowd of unbelievers would persecute and trouble me. Would that this whole world would rise up to my reproach, if only I might deserve to be praised by Christ and to hasten to the reward of his promise.” And the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men.”

Therefore, whoever among you, my brothers, desires to reach the kingdom of heaven, whoever takes care above all things to please God, let him learn gladly to undergo, for love of him, the tribulations of temptations, and joyfully to endure whatever adversity may occur. For there is none of us who does not need daily cleansing; none who does not offend almost at every moment. If, therefore, we love our own salvation, if we demand that our offenses be forgiven us and that we be cleansed from every stain of the heart, let us patiently endure whatever adversity befalls us, since the evils of tribulation, which the soul patiently tolerates for love of God, purify it more than the good works that it performs of its own accord here in this world.

A great occasion for suffering has been left to us in this place, where want heavily oppresses us, poverty weighs us down, and the debt of another’s [unclear] also constrains us. What adversity do we not experience in this monastery? What storms and tempests do we not suffer, brothers? By what inconveniences are we not harassed? How desolate all the goods of this monastery were when, fifteen years ago, regular observance was introduced, none of you can fail to know. But the tribulation of common want rages more heavily against me, since I must provide you with the necessities of life, and often I do not well know from what source I may pay. Yet your patience consoles my spirit, and the hope that you humble ones have conceived in the Lord Savior, so that I do not doubt that his mercy will come in time; and I hear him saying to me through Isaiah the prophet: “Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.” Gladly, then, I shall have patience in the poverty that, God thus ordaining, we now suffer, so that after death we may deserve to come to the riches of eternity. For the more in this pilgrimage of ours we have been afflicted by persecutions, poverty, want, labors, and tribulations, the greater rewards we shall obtain, the Lord granting them, in the future recompense of the righteous.
