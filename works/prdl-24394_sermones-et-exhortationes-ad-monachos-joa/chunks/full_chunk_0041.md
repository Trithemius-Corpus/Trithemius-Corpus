There are no works of virtue without the trial of temptations; no faith without disturbances; no struggle without an enemy; no victory without an encounter. Hence Saint James the Apostle says in his epistle: Blessed is the man who endures temptation, for when he has been proved he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him. Hence again Scripture says: The furnace tests the vessels of the potter, and the temptation of tribulation tests just men. But lest anyone should think that the devil is permitted to tempt human beings as much as he wishes, Saint [Christophilus?] cries out: But God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make a way out, so that you may be able to endure it. And Saint Augustine says: The devil is permitted to tempt only so much as is profitable for you, so that you may be exercised, so that you may be proved, so that you, who did not know yourself, may be discovered by yourself. For the devil can, with God’s permission, stir up the impulse of temptation; but it lies in your own most free power whether you wish to give or deny consent to the tempter. Hence, when he tempted the Son of God in the desert, he did not drive him on, did not touch him, did not compel him, but only said: Cast yourself down, so that you may understand that he cannot conquer except one who is willing. Therefore the divine [Christian?] chief apostle said: Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. For the devil can suggest evil; he cannot compel, nor overcome unless you consent. Our life, as Saint Augustine bears witness, in this pilgrimage cannot be without sin and temptation, because our progress is increased through temptations; nor does anyone become known to himself unless he is tempted; nor can he be crowned unless he has conquered; nor can he conquer unless he has struggled; nor can he struggle unless he has had an enemy and temptations. For Scripture says: He who has not been tempted, what does he know? For no one will be crowned except one who has struggled lawfully.

Therefore, coming to the service of God, dearest brothers, stand in justice and fear; and as long as you can be conquered by the adversary and subjected to him, do not glory in security. Always keep watch, always be afraid, because the enemy with whom you are engaged in struggle never sleeps, does not rest, does not cease from persecution, always lying in wait for you, and is never wearied. But if at some time he has been overcome, he at once rises up to a new struggle, nor does he desist from attacking the good until, defeated, he utterly succumbs, or else surely conquers those who struggle against him by troublesome perseverance. Hence Saint Pope Gregory also says: When the ancient enemy sees that he is succumbing in the first struggle, he renews other wars of temptations. The crafty adversary of the human race observes the conduct of each and every person, and those things to which they are nearer by vice he places before the face of the heart, and he uses great force to overthrow the state of the mind.

Stand firmly rooted in the fear of the Lord; you will easily overcome all the temptations of the enemy. For it is written in the book of Proverbs: In the fear of the Lord is confidence of strength. And again: By mercy and faith sins are purged; but through the fear of the Lord everyone turns away from evil. In Ecclesiasticus, moreover, the wise man said: The fear of the Lord drives out sin. For one who is without fear will not be able to be justified; for the wrath of his animosity overturns him. Therefore, by Solomon’s testimony, that man is called blessed who is always fearful; but he who is hard of heart will fall into evil. Prepare your souls also, through zeal for holy conduct, for temptation, so that when it comes you may be able to resist and stand the stronger for it. For you will easily overcome the devil if, thus prepared, you always approach to do battle. Behold, your adversary, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking whom he may devour; and you must resist him in the faith of our Lord Jesus if you desire to obtain victory. Here no place for flight is granted; no brief respite of security is allowed to those who fight; rather, it is necessary that you engage in combat.
