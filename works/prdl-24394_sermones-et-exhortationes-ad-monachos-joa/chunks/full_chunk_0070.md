Fasting detests idleness; it does not love verbosity; it loves seclusion; it applies itself to prayers and tears. Fasting, as Saint Ambrose bears witness, is the death of fault, the destruction of offenses, the remedy of salvation, the root of grace, the foundation of chastity. By this step Moses, the righteous man, came to God; by this gift Elijah ascended into heaven in a chariot before he died. Those who walk in the spirit love fasting, and those who profess chastity necessarily flee an abundance of delights. For hunger is the friend of virginity, the enemy of wantonness. Fullness, however, betrays chastity and nourishes wantonness. For fasting extinguishes the burning arrows of the devil; persistence in prayer restrains them; and continuance in vigils in the love of Christ repels all the snares of demons. Hence Saint Leo the Pope says in a certain sermon: What can be more effective than fasting? By its observance we draw near to God, and, resisting the devil, overcome seductive vices. For fasting has always been food for virtue, and abstinence the nourishment of holy chastity. From abstinence, indeed, chaste thoughts proceed; it begets reasonable desires and modest counsels as well; and through voluntary afflictions the flesh dies to concupiscences, while the spirit is renewed in virtues.

Therefore, my brothers, let us fast a fasting of virtue out of love for him who did not refuse to undergo death for us, and let us abstain not only from unlawful things but also, as far as possible, from permitted things, so that we who in many matters offend against the commandments of salvation may, in recompense of satisfaction, henceforth restrain ourselves from every pleasure. Let us fast from every sin; let us take no food of malice; let us devour no banquets of pleasure; let us not become drunk with the wine of luxury. Let us fast from all depravities; let us abstain from evil works; let us not speak useless, empty, idle, scurrilous, and pernicious words; and let us always beware of harmful and perverse thoughts that separate us from God. Let us always keep watch most zealously in prayers to God, knowing that he whom we invoke with our prayers is everywhere most present to us. Let his praise always be in our mouth; and when we cannot praise his magnificence with speech, let us adore him in the heart with the desires of a most chaste love. For then our fasts are pleasing to him, when they have been adorned with holy works and continual prayers. For the prayer of the righteous man, as sacred Scripture bears witness, penetrates the heavens; it becomes the more effective, the more it is found adorned with charity and fasting. Let us join to it, as it were, two wings, fasting and charity, and it will obtain for us from the Lord whatever we have asked that is conducive to salvation.

*On the Lenten fast of monks: Homily XVI.*

Since, by the bounty of our Lord Jesus Christ, dearly beloved brothers, we have begun the Lenten fast, reason requires that we say something briefly about its observance. For although you have many writings of the holy fathers concerning this most sacred exercise of fasts, nevertheless, because I know by experience that my sermons among you are not wholly without fruit, I gladly impart to your edification what the Lord has bestowed. For, as blessed Jerome bears witness, the action of the living voice has a certain hidden inner operation, which in Greek speech he more elegantly called energia; and the speech of the teacher, delivered by his mouth, is stronger for persuading.

But I, set apart in your midst by the name of shepherd alone, confess that I am young, unlearned, and lacking the experience, together with discretion, necessary for the governance of souls. Hence it comes about that I speak with a certain shame and embarrassment, a young man among elders, an ignorant man among the learned, a disciple among masters. Therefore I beg that none of you despise the simplicity of my speech; let no one take it ill that I speak, because even if I am not fit for speaking, nevertheless by reason of the duty of the pastoral care entrusted to me I confess before God that I am bound to admonish you. Charity gives me lively boldness for speaking, and the more ready you show yourselves to hear, the more you provoke me to speak. Nor will the labor be heavy to me, however great it may seem, by which the salvation of your souls is perfected. Help me with your prayers, that the Lord Jesus may speak in my mouth and grant strength to my little talent to bring forth into the midst whatever can produce in you an increase of holy fervor. For I am unable to profit your charity by my sermons as much as I desire, because I am compelled to be occupied too much with the care of secular business.
