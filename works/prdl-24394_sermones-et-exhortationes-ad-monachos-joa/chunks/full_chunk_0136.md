*Sermon VI. Folio LXXI.*

But in order that he may learn wisely to distinguish the Spirit of God from the impulses of the ancient enemy, mark what we say. First, then, let him judge that he has been inspired by the grace of God if he becomes daily purer in conscience and perceives [unclear] of the world to be decreasing. For wherever the Spirit of God has poured forth the gift of heavenly grace, every inclination to sin soon withdraws. But if a horror of sinning has not entered the mind, it is certainly manifest that the impulse of the devil has taken possession of the person. For when the Holy Spirit has penetrated a person’s mind, it either softens every fire of vice or wholly extinguishes it.

Hence let each one of you examine himself diligently, and weigh by whose impulse he is moved. For the impulse of God diminishes a wicked habit, but that of the enemy increases it. Those whom the Spirit of God moves have sin diminished in them daily; but those who follow the impulse of the devil are rarely truly amended. The second sign of divine impulse is recognized from the increase of virtues, because the grace of the Holy Spirit always works great virtues in a person. Therefore, when you feel yourself advancing daily in the love of God and in good works, when you experience in yourself that the habit of sinning and every vice has less power from day to day, you ought humbly to acknowledge that the grace of God is present. For the love of God, which grace brings with it, is not idle, but is always usefully occupied in good works; it covers a multitude of sins, and does not permit the mind, exercised in useful things, to sin. The grace of the Spirit of God cannot stand together with sins, nor is it at all accustomed to be in a person without the fruit of action. *On the Knowledge of Spiritual Grace.*

[They] despise all things of the present life. The soul which has God present within itself through love despises all comforts of the flesh; it longs to pass through hardships and to come to its God. It does not love the riches of this world; it spurns transitory honors and dignities; it strongly contemns the pleasures of the flesh. It meditates always on the law of the Lord, and never forgets the most bitter passion of Jesus Christ. Whoever among you is [unclear], brothers, let him do these things: let him [unclear] all the deceptive little consolations of this age, so that he thinks of nothing else, seeks nothing, desires nothing except the love of God. Whoever shall have obtained this sign of the present Holy Spirit, if he perseveres to the end of life, will be saved.
