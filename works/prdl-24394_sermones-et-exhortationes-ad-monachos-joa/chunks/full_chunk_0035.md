*Homily VIII.*

... but to obtain. Then, when we pray, we receive the confidence of obtaining what we ask from the Lord, when no perversity of life contradicts our prayers. For the Lord is the hearer of righteous prayer, namely when integrity of life commends the prayer, and no guilt of conscience makes the one praying hesitate. Friendship will not stand between those who are unlike, and between God and a sinner there can be no communion. If you wish to be heard when you pray, take care first of all that you do not disagree with the will of God. Live in spirit very like God, and you will suffer no repulse in prayer.

Indeed, purity of conscience, in the fervor of divine love, makes a person like God in understanding. Likeness causes love; love, in turn, introduces familiarity; familiarity introduces confidence; and confidence in petition easily obtains what it asks. For just as there is no union among unlike things, so between God and an evil person prayer is of no avail.

Therefore whoever among you desires to obtain the effect of his prayers must first cleanse the dwelling of his heart from every sin through true repentance; then he must strive to inflame himself by the exercise of holy compunction; afterward he must continually feed his mind in the pursuit of God’s love, so that he may never afterward cease from the good that he begins. But afterward, when, with the desire of divine love always increasing in him, he has brought the exercise of holy purity by long practice into habit, and the mind has begun to have the necessary firmness in a good purpose, then for the first time the likeness of divine communion is gradually introduced, and prayer is formed to its proper state. For the more the mind, stirred up in the love of God, has advanced, the more quickly prayer will obtain from the Lord what it asks. For the love of God alone makes prayer powerful; and the more fervently the mind burns in it, the more easily it will obtain the effect of its petitions. For if you love much, you can do much. God is love, and he who remains in love remains in God, and God in him. But in the one in whom God remains, nothing is stronger than he, except God himself alone.

There are among you some weak and feeble in spirit who already think themselves strong and perfect in the way of the Lord; their devotion is rare and momentary. Without the wedding garment they boast that they have drawn near to the kingdom of heaven, although they still lie far below. To these I say again what I have often said: because no one becomes the highest suddenly, and unless a monk has subdued all his animal passions through the mortification of the flesh, he does not reach the loftiest mountain of divine contemplation.

*Folio XIX.*

For whoever, being placed in a monastery under authority and obedience, follows his own will against monastic institution, is not worthy to be heard by the Lord in his prayers. But those of whom we are speaking are great and wise in their own eyes: loving their own will, they murmur at everything, and whatever they themselves have not ordered they think badly done. They examine the actions of their brothers with curious inquiry, but they scorn to discern their own negligences. In manners they are lax and disorderly; in words frivolous and loud; unwilling for obedience; and always slow in good works and full of sloth. They neglect all the exercises of the order and devote themselves to private ones according to their own will. And as long as they remain in their rashness, they do not rise up to the true love of God.

Let lovers of their own will hear what the Lord says: “I have not come to do my own will, but that of the Father who sent me.” Now, brothers, attend to the teacher; unless you live in imitation of him, you will pray without fruit. For the Lord freely hears the prayer of the one who keeps his commandments in truth. But the one who transgresses God’s commandment, who follows with desire the pleasures of the flesh and the consolations of the world, his prayer is not heard.

For God, just and upright, is believed to hear your prayer gladly when mind and good life together make supplication. But if someone’s manner of life displeases God, how will his prayer please him? For God is spirit, and the person who walks not in the spirit but in the flesh certainly draws very little near to God. You, therefore, monk, who do not walk according to the purity of the Gospel and of the Rule, who love your own will and not God’s, know that you will not feel the efficacy of your prayers unless you first amend your life. For unless the manner of your life agrees with your prayer, you will not obtain what you pray for. Then you cry to the Lord with a great voice, when even with the tongue silent you cry out by good works. For good prayers and an evil manner of life deserve nothing good.
