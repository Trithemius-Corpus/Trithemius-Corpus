*Homily XVI.*

For food is not fruitfully withheld from the body unless the mind is recalled from all iniquity. The blessed Jerome also says: What does it profit for the body to be made thin by abstinence, if the spirit swells with pride? What virtue is there in not drinking wine, and in being drunk with anger, hatred, and the other poisonous cups of the soul?

Therefore let none of you promise himself sanctity from abstinence from foods alone, since whatever is required for true penitence is judged necessary also for fasting. For fasts profit unto salvation for those who are contrite for their sins in a true purpose of amendment, who fervently devote themselves to good and holy works. Hence those who fast from food and drink must also abstain from all vices, so that the mind, adorned with the beauty of virtues, may sweetly burn in the love of Christ, and may in no way grow lukewarm from the progress of habitual conduct. For the virtue of abstinence is nothing if it has not been adorned with the works of charity. Therefore hear my counsel, monk, if you wish to make your fast fruitful before God. First cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin, so that no desire of evil may remain within; so that the mind may become fervent in the exercise of divine love, and also be praiseworthily adorned with all virtues. Through the remedy of contrition and confession wash away past sins, and by persisting in prayers with tears, strengthen your mind thereafter against all temptations with God’s help. Toward [unclear], as much as you can, be fervent in the exercise of love; toward yourself, in a pure conscience, be rightly tranquil; toward your brother, be ordered in true charity. In humility always be foremost; firm in constancy and obedience. Diligently exercise chastity by pious action, and your mind in the Passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, so that thereafter there be no day on which you have not at least once gone over his life and death in meditation with thanksgiving and tears. For rude and too unpracticed, not to say ungrateful, is the Christian who is so immersed either in temporal affairs or occupied with the pleasures of the flesh that he does not daily remember, at least once, with thanksgiving, the most bitter Passion of his Redeemer.

*Fol. XXXIX.*

But the monk who forgets the benefits of our Lord Jesus Christ, who does not once each day call to mind his Passion with thanksgiving, who does not every day meditate on the evangelical law of the Lord, is deservedly judged the most ungrateful of all men. Nothing is more powerful than that prayer which is offered to God the Father in union with and in the power of the Lord’s Passion. Too justly is that spirit to be condemned which, redeemed by the most precious blood of Christ, is led away by the pleasures of its flesh, so that it does not remember such great benefits of God toward it. For there was no cause for the Son of God to become man except that he wished to justify us sinners: born in the world for us, suffered in the world for our sins. It is fitting, therefore, my brothers, that with all devotion we celebrate this Lenten fast, dedicated to his most sacred Passion, who first deigned to do such great things for our salvation, who, in order to rescue us from the destruction of death, did not hesitate to give himself for us unto death. Let us do, for the love of Christ, whatever good we can; let us endure, in consideration of his Passion and death, every adversity that may befall us in this world, since the Lord Jesus, who out of charity willed to suffer for us, has truly shown himself most worthy that we should love him with all our heart. For if, brother, you acknowledge yourself a sinner, Christ has saved you by his death. Contemplate him hanging on the cross as is fitting, and you will be healed from every bite of sin. For nothing is found in this world more effective for curing the wounds of conscience, nothing more powerful for purging the sight of the fearful mind, than continual meditation on the wounds of Christ. For the most precious blood of the Lord, poured out, has been made a sufficient medicine for sin. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: Brothers, that we, wounded by sin, may be healed, let us look upon Christ crucified.
