*That one must pray without ceasing.*

... no usefulness at all. Hence Jerome also says: God is to be prayed to not in words only, but with the heart. Therefore the chanting of fifty psalms with purity of heart, serenity, and spiritual gladness is better than the recitation of the whole Psalter with anxiety of heart and sadness. And the blessed pope Gregory says: True petition is not in the sounds of the mouth, but in the voices of the heart, that is, in holy thoughts. For it is not words but desires that make the stronger voices in the ears of God.

Except, therefore, for those prayers which we are bound to say either by decree of the Church or by the institution of the monastic order, abundance and multiplication of words in the rest contributes nothing, or little, to true inward devotion of mind; for where there is no fervent desire in the love of God, a lengthening of discourse is brought in to support it. For holy Augustine says: Let much speaking be absent from prayer, but let fervent intention persevere; for to speak much in praying is to conduct a necessary matter with superfluous words. For prayer comprehends many things in few words, so that the simplicity of faith might quickly learn the sufficiency of its salvation, or prudent reflection might stand amazed at the profundity of the mysteries.

But for those to whom, because of hardness of the inner heart, the compunction of fervor is lacking, when they prostrate themselves for prayer there is need of more extended speech, crying, and tears, so that long practice of the mind may at last strike out the burning fire of divine love in [unclear], until in the affection of holy prayer they are lifted up in the Spirit of God. But afterward, when the fervor of divine love and compunction has penetrated the mind, thereafter there is no longer need for the exercise of words; rather, the continuation of spiritual kindling is required, lest the fire of spiritual fervor be extinguished. To this end our holy father wished the prayers in the community to be shortened, so that if anyone wished to pray by himself in spirit, he could do so.

But there are some who are so wandering, so hard of heart, and find little or no spiritual compunction while praying; for them liberty is ruinous, and solitude often gives rise to despair. Pay attention, I ask you, dearest brothers, to these things which I say to you, and consider how greatly you are obliged to pray without ceasing.
