*Homily XVIII.*

You have been called to the [choir?] of God, and you, given over to the pleasures of the flesh, think of Christ least of all. You have been invited to love the Lord Jesus above all things, and you walk after the evil desires of your heart; you show yourself ungrateful to your Redeemer. You who ought to live by the Spirit savor earthly things, and, sunk in pride, despise all the holy exercises of spiritual conduct. And what will you answer on that day of judgment to the Judge, who will say to you in your utter ingratitude: “I, who am Lord of all, humbled myself for your sake, taking the form of a servant, and being found in fashion as a man. I poured out my blood for you; for you I endured blows; and for you I underwent a most bitter death by my own willing choice. I loved you, O monk, with my whole heart; I redeemed you by my blood from perpetual death; I, out of love, called you to the heavenly kingdom, if you had kept my commandments. But you lived in opposition to my precepts; and, what is more serious, though you had been sworn to my words, you did not fear to show yourself against me, your Lord, as an apostate, impious traitor. Now therefore render an account of your stewardship.”

What then will you answer, O lazy and idle monk, who now consume this acceptable time so fruitlessly, and despise living in true monastic purity according to the Rule? What, I ask, will you answer? Will you deny the works of wickedness which you have committed, and through deceit display the good works which you now scorn to perform, so that by lying you may circumvent the Judge and thus escape the penalty owed to your demerits? But you must not be unaware that all things you have done, said, or thought throughout your whole life, good and evil, are manifest in the sight of the Judge; and nothing can be hidden from him, who alone knows all things. For all things are written in the book of God, and your conscience will bear open testimony against you concerning all the evil things you have done, so that not even the smallest thought will be able to lie hidden. For the Scripture of the prophets says to God: “For the thought of man will confess to you.” You are to be judged before the tribunal of the One who knows all things; before whose terrible judgment every creature will stand with very great fear and trembling, where no excuse at all is admitted, but each person, with appeal set aside, will receive the sentence owed to his merits. will stand beside him; and do you trust that you can deceive yourself by lying while he is judge? But hear my counsel, and, rising from your destructive sloth, at last awaken, and in due time flee from justice to the mercy of God through true repentance. Do not put it off from day to day, because it is very dangerous for one who remains in sins to be confident, and to refuse the remedy of salvation that is offered. Remember that you are going to die, and that when you do not expect it you will be called to judgment. Consider, I beg you, how the uncertain span of life slips away, and how quickly all mortal things decay. Nothing among human things is stable, nothing fixed, nothing firm; but all things that we see are corruptible, and change with time. Consider also where our brothers Valentinus, Martinus, and Ioannes now are, who in the present year were living with us, healthy, unharmed, and joyful; they spoke with us, ate, drank, and worked together with us, thinking least of all that they would die this year. Behold, when they did not think it, death came and snatched them away, and set their souls before the tribunal of the dread Judge, who rendered to each according to the works of his hands. Behold, now learn from the example of your brothers that you too are going to die, and perhaps you will pass from this world much sooner than you can estimate.

Come to your senses, wretched man, while you still can; do penance in the time of your health, when the Judge promises mercy, and do not defer today’s good to the uncertain [tomorrow?]. Rise up, even now, late though it is, from the sloth of your more negligent past conduct, in which until now you have shamefully rotted, and purge your conscience of every stain of sin; wipe away with tears the evil you have committed; and henceforth live innocently, as befits a monk. Take hold of the perfect purpose of amendment, and understand that the kingdom of heaven is promised not to those who begin the good, but to those who persevere to the end. Know also that one does not arrive at heaven by sloth and torpor, but by great labor, by the very greatest effort of a will to make progress, and by unwearied zeal in spiritual exercise. Therefore, wishing to serve the Lord and to please him in truth, always remain within yourself, fearful in awe, and prepare your soul for temptation.
