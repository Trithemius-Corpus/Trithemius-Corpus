The thirteenth sign of true humility is not only to despise praises and favors in the truth of the mind, but also prudently to avoid those by whom he sees that he is praised. Those who are truly humble flee their praisers, and whenever they are praised, making no reply, they fall silent. The esteem of a great reputation is burdensome to the humble man, and the more he is proclaimed by men, the more he is confounded within himself. For true humility advances when it is reviled; when praised, it incurs danger.

The fourteenth perfect sign of monastic humility: in rebukes and disparagements, if he suffers any, he conceives spiritual joy, and a great increase of divine love. When wronged he is pierced with compunction, and is all the more strongly stirred to contempt of the world. Whenever he is reproved insultingly by someone, if he is humble, he judges himself justly corrected. For he does not consider his own innocence in the charge, but humbly and compunctiously weighs that he has gravely offended God in other things.

The fifteenth sign of perfect humility: he never kicks back against one who corrects him, but preserves silence in all tranquility of soul. He is not accustomed to rebuke to his face, nor word for word, even the faults very well known in his reprover, but, as if mute, to keep silence about everything.

The sixteenth sign of profound humility: he despises all the consolations of the world and seeks no friendships of mortals. For true humility, which is taken up for love of Christ, delights nowhere except in Christ alone. But he cannot abide in the love of Christ unless he has learned to deny both his own will and the world with all its disordered pleasures. Thus the Lord says in the holy Gospel: If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, and even his own soul, he cannot be my disciple.

The seventeenth sign of humility: he does not divide himself among many things; he does not greatly seek the assistance and benefits of men, but remains humbly in his own moderation or even extremity. For benefits received from men he is not accustomed to applaud much, but to show himself grateful in few and simple words. The little gifts of the weak loosen minds, and often render the unwary useless and superfluous in speech. But the mind that is truly humble does not delight in the conversations of worldly men, because they often impede the purpose of purity. The love of Christ does not love publicity.

The eighteenth sign of humility: from the heart he has compassion for all who suffer for the love of Christ, and he most patiently tolerates the defects and weaknesses of others, and prays for the amendment of sinners. For true humility shows to the weak who sin the inward feelings of piety and compassion; but false and simulated humility, because it is truly pride, swells against the frail with hard and undiscerning severity, and becomes the cause of danger to many when, in correcting, it exceeds the norm of the charity of God. But he who is truly humble always fears to exceed the due measure of correction, and therefore is inclined more to mercy than to excessive severity.

The nineteenth sign of true humility: the mind perseveres free from all envy, and favors all men and prays for them. By divine judgment, [unclear] of God favors all, and rejoices together in the perfections of others. He judges no one unworthy of God's benefits; he thinks all better than himself. He regards all as his foremost ones, because he loves all as himself.

The twentieth sign of perfect humility: with bitterness of heart he recalls to memory the multitude of his sins, and therefore considers himself unworthy of every benefit and grace of God. Hence, seeking the depth of humility, he imitates the publican of the Gospel, who, having the memory of his offenses, did not wish even to lift his eyes to heaven. For the monk who is truly humble for the love of Christ always bears in his heart the holy sadness of compunction and repentance for the evils that he has done.

The twenty-first sign of cenobitic humility: he trusts the judgment of others more than his own, and for Christ's sake totally subjects his own will to another's command. When we attend to the signs of monastic humility, we perceive today few observers of it, though we see many monks professing it. O times, O morals, in which we all promise humility, and very few of us truly take care to keep inviolate what has been promised.
