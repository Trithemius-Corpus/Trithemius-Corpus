The Creator supremely loves his creature, which he made from nothing; it is fitting that that creature itself should most greatly love its Creator, by whose goodness it was brought into being. God loves us with the greatest charity, than which no greater can be spoken or thought; and he loves us so much that he neither ought nor could love more, since in God all things are perfect, and in the immutable one there is no capacity for change.

Consider now, my brothers, with how much fervor of mind and how much desire of heart a man ought to love his God, by whom, even without merits, he is [loved?] with the highest degree of love. Let us stretch out our soul in love of him above all things as much as we can; he is worthy that we should love him with all our strength. However eminently we may have loved God, who loves us, nevertheless we shall by no means reach that measure by which he loves us. For he himself, as we have said, loves us supremely, and his love toward us cannot grow further, since it is agreed that he loves his creature most perfectly. But however much we have loved him, we can always become more perfect in loving, and love more ardently until death.

O we weak ones, we lukewarm ones, we consigned to a muddy prison: what shall we render to our lover [unclear]. After the death of Christ I reckon this to be [unclear] among the things set forth, since there is no comparison between the thing made and the maker.

The sixth thing which can [bring?] us into the love of God is this: that no one outside the Christian faith will be saved, whether Christ is announced to him or he is wholly ignorant of him. How many kingdoms there are today in Asia and Africa, how many peoples, how many islands of the sea, among whom there is no knowledge at all of Christ and no faith, but every religion is in error and without the merit of salvation, even if outside Christianity they seem to adore and worship one God. For Christ is the savior of faithful souls; whoever has not believed in him is already judged, and without doubt will be condemned.

[Unclear] what thanksgivings we shall be able to render to God, because he established us in the Catholic faith, so to speak, as [native-born?], and freed us from the destruction of death in the blood of his only-begotten Son. Let us therefore be mindful of so great a benefit, and let us take care to live in such a way that we may not be judged for ingratitude. Let us bless God the Father at all times, because he poured out the gifts of the Holy Spirit upon us, and did not permit us to be born far from the [bosom?] of the Church’s Catholic unity.

The seventh thing which is believed able to produce love of him in our hearts is considered in the promise of everlasting happiness. For if we consider how great good things he has promised he will give in heaven, love of him will deservedly burn sweetly in our hearts. He promised his whole self to us as a reward, he who alone is the highest and utterly immutable good. Since he is indivisible, he will grant himself to be enjoyed by all in such a way that nevertheless he is possessed whole by each. He loves all by whom he is loved, and he loves each by whom he is possessed. The more we now love God with a pure mind, the greater reward we shall receive there.

*On the Knowledge of Spiritual Grace: Sermon Six.*

Saint Paul has taught us that not every spirit is always to be believed, but that impulses must be examined carefully, whether they are from God, who is the true light, lest perhaps something rise up against us from the side of darkness. For our adversary, since he is cunning, never sleeps, but devises a thousand ways by which he may catch us unawares.
