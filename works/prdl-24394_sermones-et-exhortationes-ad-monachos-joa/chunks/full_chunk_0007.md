For the Lord in the Gospel did not proclaim just any poor persons blessed and destined to be given the kingdom of heaven; rather, as you know, he declared that only the poor in spirit are to be blessed. For contempt of riches is not sufficient for salvation, unless the renouncer of gold also strives with all his strength, through patience and humility, constantly to imitate the Savior. For it is easier to despise the world than to spurn and deny oneself. Yet whoever wishes to imitate the Son of God and attain evangelical perfection must become a despiser not only of the world and temporal things, but also of his very self. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: Unless someone has renounced all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple. And lest we should think this sufficient for perfection, he again testifies more openly concerning contempt of oneself, saying: If anyone wishes to follow me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his soul will lose it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake and the Gospel’s will save it. Christ became obedient to the Father even unto death; if, as you have promised, you wish to imitate him, brothers, it is necessary that you deny yourselves to yourselves. For he will not be able to be a monk who has not first wholly renounced his own will. You who before God and all his saints have promised to live according to the monastic rule may not thereafter appropriate your own will, since what has once been consecrated to God must no longer be recalled to human uses. For you are ruled not by yourselves but by Christ; hence you retain in your power neither willing nor refusing. For you are dead, as holy Christophilus says, and your life is hidden with Christ in God; but when Christ, your life, appears, then you also will appear with him in glory. If you desire to rejoice without end with him, you must now in this time fight vigorously for love of him.

For no one will be crowned with Christ except the one who, striving lawfully, has persevered to the end, since he who does not know how to persevere cannot conquer.

*On the Advancement of Spiritual Warfare: Second Homily*

[unclear]

As often, dearest brothers, as remembrance comes to my mind of that moment when, though I cried out against it and resisted, you appointed me, unworthy as I am, as abbot over you, so often I am wholly shaken within myself by the bitterness of my heart, and I am compelled, even unwillingly and unknowingly, to shed tears. For I know that by my own merits I have not been placed over you; but whether this was done by yours, I do not know. May almighty God grant that this appointment of me was not made by reason of my demerits or yours. Why did you wish it so, brothers? You raised up as leader a young man, unlearned and by no means fit for the warfare that we profess, so that it is now altogether necessary for me both to teach and to learn, when neither perfection of life commends my teaching, nor consideration of age yet obtains for the shepherd the necessary authority. In these three years already I have sufficiently experienced with how many miseries I am entangled, while I alone am compelled to bear the weaknesses of you all, and I, unhappy man, am supported by no one. Who will deliver me from the vanities of this world, into which I have been recalled, I who entered the monastery in order to escape every care and solicitude of the present life? But since it is truly necessary to bear the burden placed upon my shoulders, I ask you, brothers, earnestly extend to me the helping hands of your prayers, so that in your midst, by the Lord’s granting, I may deserve to conduct myself in such a way that I do not transgress the condition of spiritual warfare which the rule prescribes for us. For we are professors of the same warfare, nor does the vow distinguish the office by which solemn profession, at the beginning of this life, made me bound as shepherd together with the sheep.
