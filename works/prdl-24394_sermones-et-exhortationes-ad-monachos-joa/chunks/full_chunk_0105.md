*Homily 23.*

*Fol. LV.*

... while we walk innocently in spirit. He truly loves God who, always intent on good works, keeps himself pure and unstained from this world. But he loves the Lord God rightly who desires absolutely nothing in this world. He truly loves God with all his heart who, for love of him, bravely despises the world’s honors, riches, pleasures, and all comforts of the flesh. He loves God with all his soul who, always solicitous in the observance of his commandments, does not neglect to do any of the things that are pleasing to him. For the love of God neglects [nothing], but is accustomed to preserve and fulfill most diligently everything that he has commanded. Whoever loves God in true charity has fulfilled the law, the prophets, and the Gospel. Therefore by your works you will bear witness whether you truly love God.

For everyone who has deserved to receive the root of divine love always shows great and manifest signs of love. Indeed, the fire of divine love makes the one who possesses it pious in all things, chaste, gentle, and most humble, and, both in adversity and in prosperity, always tranquil in the same constancy of mind, without disturbance. The monk who truly loves the Lord God will, according to the Rule of our most holy father Benedict, always obey the commands of his seniors cheerfully in all things; in hardships and adversities he embraces patience and everywhere preserves true humility. The love of God makes its possessor very rich in all good works, and renders him far removed from every kind of vice. The lover of God does not murmur at the command of obedience; in accusations he does not rashly excuse himself; he does not touch his neighbor with the gall of bitterness, nor is he disturbed even when injured. Whoever is a true lover of God preserves his life irreproachable in all purity of mind and body, and demonstrates in himself a fervent charity not only in word but also in deed and truth.

Whatever beautiful, whatever pleasant or delightful thing is seen in the world, whatever flatters the wantonness of lascivious flesh, the true lover of God despises it all with the utmost constancy, and desires nothing other than to please Christ and to live with him as soon as possible. Great and incredible to those who do not know it is the power of divine love, which always impels a man to work well and never allows him to live idle. For what else is the inward love of God than a sweet drunkenness of the soul, by which a man is snatched away from all the consolations of the world and, as if he felt nothing of what mortals do, is enriched in the fellowship of the Holy Spirit alone? Whoever is a true lover of God, being a monk, has put away for Christ’s sake every curiosity of the world and every solicitude for worldly things; and, as if he did not feel them, he transcends in his soul all the stings of cares. Hence with the Apostle he cries: The world has been crucified, and I to the world.

O wine of divine love, how sweetly you make drunk, how innocently you satisfy, how wisely you make foolish, how chastely you inflame. For everyone who has been made drunk by your savor, separated from every tumult of carnal pleasures, rests sweetly in the shadow of the Holy Spirit. Because of the exceeding drunkenness of the spirit, he speaks things alien to carnal and worldly people, and nothing contrary to God is heard from his mouth. This is the unstained satiety of spiritual wine, which is extended so much the more through desire, the longer it is continued. O holy and venerable simplicity of the lover of Christ: even if to many who savor earthly things it seems to be folly, nevertheless before God and all his saints it is the greatest and truly the highest wisdom. Let the world have its prudence; let it have honors, dignities, and riches. But I would choose the merit of one simple monk truly loving God rather than the sovereignty of the whole world.

The love of God is the soul’s highest and always most chaste pleasure; its burning enlightens the mind and sweetly inflames the affection. The love of God is the fountain of heavenly grace, because the grace of the Holy Spirit makes fruitful with the fruits of all virtues the mind which Christ’s charity purifies by ploughing. The love of Christ reconciles human beings to the friendship of almighty God and reveals to mortals the will of the highest majesty. The love of God makes the monk acceptable to God, because he requires from us not knowledge, not learning, not eloquence, but charity. Therefore the monk who has publicly offered himself to the Lord God ought to care for nothing except God, lest he seem to have a divided heart; for just as God is one, so love toward God cannot be divided. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: He loves you less, Lord, who loves anything together with you which he does not love for your sake.
