*Homily XXI.*

[unclear]: for love of him you have set me, an unworthy man, over you as abbot. For I shall not be burdensome to you while you run well; but if anyone turns aside from the way of truth, I am compelled, even unwillingly, to oppose him as best I can.

A monk should always think of his entrance into the monastery, and never forget the first purpose of his institution. In all your works also, O monk, always consider that you will not remain long upon the earth, but that shortly you will depart from this life, and perhaps will die when you do not expect it. Therefore keep inviolate the purpose of holiness, and always be vigilant in good works, so that when the Lord comes and calls you, he may find you prepared. Remember that you are a pilgrim on earth, and have here no abiding city; but just as a traveler hastens quickly toward the end of his journey, so death draws near to you daily without interruption. Therefore while you are healthy, set in order the house of your conscience, so that you may not fear death; for he rarely dies well who is not prepared as though he were to die daily.

O how securely does the monk depart from this life who, keeping his conscience clean in all purity, lives at all times without reproach according to the integrity of the rule of our most holy lawgiver, even unto death. Let your mind continually hold in memory our Lord Jesus Christ as present, indeed as nowhere absent; and let no hour pass in which you do not think with thanksgiving of him suffering for you. Stand in humility and true obedience, and no adversities will ever prevail against you. Just as a tree, sending many great roots into the earth, is not easily torn from its place, so a monk who possesses charity and obedience in humility, together with the other virtues, is in no way overcome by demons. Obedience, brothers, is a great virtue, which brings the other virtues into the mind, and, once brought in, preserves them through the service of humility. May our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who was made obedient for us unto the death of the cross, illumine your hearts by his grace; who lives and reigns with God the Father, God in the unity of the Holy Spirit, for all ages of ages. Amen.

*On continence and true chastity: Homily XXI.*

I would very greatly wish, my dearest brothers, that I had been endowed by God with that grace by which, as your pastor, I might be worthy and suitable in deed as well as in speech. For I know, nor does it escape me, how dangerous a thing the governance of souls is for a young and inexperienced man such as I am, when one who has not yet learned to direct and govern himself according to the rule of justice presumes to enter upon the teaching of others. I blush to open my mouth in your midst to teach, since, after only a short time as a disciple, I am compelled before maturity to be a teacher.

But since it has so pleased you, and you have wished to have me, a young man, as master, pardon me, because necessity compels me to fulfill, as best I can, the office entrusted to me. Help me, I pray, brothers, with your prayers, so that the mercy of God may bestow the strength that the inexperience of my age denies. For I desire you to walk before God in all purity of mind and body, so that you may be pleasing to him to whom you gave yourselves from the beginning.

Look upon our Lord Jesus Christ as the leader of the journey undertaken toward heaven: he, born of Mary, the ever most chaste virgin, preserved forever the most perfect chastity of mind and body. Follow this teacher, in whose service you have consecrated your hands, so that you may keep the continence enjoined upon you not only in body, but also perpetually guard chastity with a mind brighter than sapphire and carbuncle. For chastity, a most beautiful virtue, especially befits cloistered persons; through it every impulse of lust is restrained under the yoke of reason, and, when the fury of vices has been extinguished, the bearing of the body preserves all propriety. An incontinent and lascivious monk, who follows the filthy lust of the flesh, is counted with the demons who withdrew from the Lord.

For there are three things without the perpetual observance of which no one is judged worthy to be named among monks. The first of them is the obedience of humble and true subjection; the second is the renunciation of property, as the rule commands; the third is the inviolate keeping of perpetual continence and chastity. Whatever one of these a monk violates, he has corrupted the purpose of his order. For chastity is sustained by companions, namely fasting and temperance; if at any time it has been deprived of these, without doubt it quickly totters. Chastity is the greatest ornament of monks; adorned with it, they are introduced into the company of angels.
