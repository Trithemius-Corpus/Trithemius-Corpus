*On the progress of spiritual warfare.*

Let [no one?], brothers, promise himself a long [life]; rather let each one think that death is near at hand, and therefore let him live so prepared as though he were to depart from the body tomorrow. For the hope of a longer life has deceived many, and, by holding them back from the fervor of a holy purpose, has suddenly plunged them into the lower world. Therefore perfect your warfare, which of necessity will end not long from now, so that you may not fear to appear before your king when he comes. Always remember that you do not have here an abiding city, nor can you remain long in this mortal life, but death will come quickly and compel you to depart.

For whoever is a true monk and desires to be a faithful soldier of Christ despises all the delights of this world, always thinks of his Lord as present to him, and, casting away from himself all impediments and little burdens, cares above all things how he may be able to please him greatly. He ought not to be anxious in seeking the necessities of life, since the providence of the pastor disposes all these things for those subject to him. Let the whole care of the monk be to keep himself unstained from this world, so that, free and released from all things, he may seek nothing except God, think of nothing except Jesus crucified for us.

O happy soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised, if he shall remain faithful, prudent, and watchful in the love of God even to the end. Certainly nothing is happier than a good monk, to whom the Son of God promises himself as reward; nothing also is more laborious, since, placed in the arena of combat, he is daily in peril concerning his salvation; and nothing is stronger than the monk who conquers the world together with the devil, nor anything weaker than he who is overcome by his own flesh.

Therefore you, O monk, must always keep watch against the hidden enemy of your salvation, who is never more easily conquered than if he is kept far from the entrance of the mind. Hence, if you desire to come away victorious against him, do not allow the engines of his deceit and fraud to be brought nearer to you. Guard first of all your senses, lest death enter through the windows; and then especially beware the ambushes of the enemy, when for a time he has allowed you to be secure in your devotion. Never forget the admonition of the wise man, who says: If you do not hold yourself steadfastly in the fear of the Lord, your house, that is, your conscience, will quickly be overthrown. Therefore Solomon said in Proverbs: Blessed is the man who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil. For as soon as a monk has presumed upon security, already without doubt he tends toward destruction.

Therefore it is fitting that the soldier of Christ always be prepared against his adversaries, so that, as long as he lives in this mortal body, he may not trust that he has perfectly overcome them. For the flesh always desires against the spirit, and, lest the soul ever be overcome, as long as one happens to live, it is necessary to fight. As often as you have conquered your invisible enemy, rising again he always engages you with greater savagery, so that he who cannot obtain victory by strength may at least by persistence bring on despair. But you, most valiant soldier of Christ, take care that you do not fail in the arena of this very brief contest; take care that you do not lay down the arms of necessary defense before the battle is completed at the end. For the Savior says: He who has persevered to the end will be saved.

Do not give rest to your soul as long as it has been granted you to remain in this miserable pilgrimage, but always do some good, lest the devil ever find you idle. Always remember the admonition of Scripture, saying: Whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly, because neither work nor reason nor wisdom nor knowledge will be among the dead, where you are hastening. For there is no one who lives forever, but we all die, and we fall down like waters into the earth, which will no more return to their former state. Then there will no longer be time for working, nor for earning eternal happiness, but whatever a man has sown here now in time, then he will receive as reward.

Therefore, again, labor in holy things like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. But no one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself. For of those too who contend in the arena, no one will be crowned unless he has contended lawfully. Moreover everyone who contends in the arena abstains from all things. In another place also the same lover of Christ says: I wish you to be without anxiety.
