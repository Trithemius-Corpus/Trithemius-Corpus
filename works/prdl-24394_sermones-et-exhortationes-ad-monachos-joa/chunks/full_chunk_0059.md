And therefore, since there is none of us who has not offended in many things, arm yourselves, I beg you, with patience and humility, so that whenever you are corrected for your sins, you may bear it with all gentleness. For in this time of free choice you can, with a small expiation, make sufficient satisfaction for many debts, for which after death in purgatory the soul will be tormented, even for small ones, for a long time and most grievously.

Indeed voluntary satisfaction is of great value; but that which is done from necessity earns less toward grace. Altogether, brothers, it is fitting and necessary for the soul to be cleansed from fault, if it is to reign in heaven and rejoice with Christ. But the soul that has neglected the cleansing of its faults in the present life will be heavily punished in the life to come. Let none of you say, my dearest brothers, what many ignorant people have been accustomed to say: “It is enough for me if my soul has undergone the pains of purgatory.” Believe me, brothers, whoever says this word from the heart does not consider what purgatory is to be. For he does not know what he is asking for, who desires purgatory. Just as a man is foolish who, when he could pay his debt free, scorns to do so and willingly goes to prison, so he deserves to be judged the most foolish of all men who, when he can make satisfaction to God for his sins in this life, puts it off to the future.

In this life repentance is fruitful, and voluntary satisfaction avails much by merit; but after death there will be no place for repentance, but each one will then receive according as he has done in his body, whether good or evil. Think on these things, brothers, while you live in health, lest, overtaken by the command of death, you at last grieve too late. Let the salvation of today not wait for the morrow, and let the man who can pay his debt with grace while at liberty not allow himself to be dragged to prison with torture. As much as each one can in his whole life, so much let him take care to make satisfaction for his sins, since the spirit returns securely to the Lord if, while still existing in the body, it is daily purified by the fire of divine love.

Here you have a place in which daily expiation is made for public guilt: if you kept true and humble patience in your mind, you would without doubt obtain certain remission of all. But what you ought to do for secret and hidden sins, not only the rule admonishes you, but also daily sacramental confession instructs you in all things. At the altar we are accustomed every day, each one devoutly, to offer the Son of God’s majesty; and none of us approaches that holy washing and mystery unless he has first confessed according to custom, since the highest purity is required where, through the Mass, the salvation of the whole world is renewed.

Who among us, dearest brothers, amid these things does not daily make progress in the love of God, unless he shamefully and wholly lays aside every desire for progress? For I dare to say that the monk who does not make progress in this holy congregation is utterly convicted of having forgotten his own salvation. Believe me, brothers: God will save no one against his will. If you neglect your own progress, nothing but yourselves will be blamed. For God is the intellectual light that enlightens every man coming into this world, as John, the eagle that soars aloft, says; but those who do not receive the light will necessarily remain in darkness. He who turns his mind away from the faith that is in the Son of God remains in darkness; and for him who does not do the works of light, nothing that he sees will profit him for salvation. Outside the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ there is no salvation, and he who thinks otherwise is not a Christian.

Let not a foolish consideration of piety seduce you, O man, so that you believe anyone outside the faith of Christ, in any religion whatever, is to be saved, however innocently he may live or according to the law of nature; for just as outside Noah’s ark there was no life for men, so outside the Christian faith there is salvation for no one unto eternal life. For Christ, the only Son of God, is the one and only Savior of the world; whoever does not believe in him, the wrath of God remains upon him, and he will be condemned with the devil. But you, my brothers, who now believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, I beg you henceforth to live in such a way that, Christian in name and in deed, you may be able to come to his fellowship: who lives and reigns with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, through all ages of ages.
