But since you now faithfully believe also what he commands, and have set the foundation of the spiritual building upon a firm rock, now you must also without delay do the remaining things that are left for perfection. Therefore love peace and truth, so that in the faith of Christ, as true soldiers, you may be able at all times to remain stable and most firm. For, as Scripture bears witness, truth is great and stronger than all things, and there is nothing unjust with it. He who loves truth is a true disciple of Christ, and loves Christ himself, who says in the Gospel: “I am the way, the truth, and the life.” All the ways of the Lord likewise are mercy and truth; from this [unclear] fell through pride, because he did not remain in eternal truth.

Therefore holy [Christophilus?] Paul commands the soldiers of the Lord to gird themselves in truth; in this he wished to show the state of perfection toward which cloistered persons especially ought to aspire. For what else is it to love truth than to be the person one is commanded to be, and to live according to the rule of justice, true and solid, such as holy perfection by grace requires of nature? Hence Firmianus Lactantius says: “He who is zealous for truth should cast away pleasures hostile to it and troubling, which bind the soul to themselves and do not allow it to be free. But let him always prefer true things to false, eternal things to brief ones, useful things to pleasant ones. Let nothing be pleasing to the sight except what can be done justly, piously, religiously; nothing sweet to the hearing except what makes the soul better.”

Thus soldiers have their loins girded in truth when, out of love for God, despising the enticements of the flesh and the vanities of the world, they strive according to their powers so to conduct themselves and live that they may be able in all things to please the highest Truth, which is God. After this the soldier of Christ is commanded to put on the breastplate of justice, without which no one will be able to serve the Lord rightly and fruitfully. Justice is to preserve and distribute to each one what is owed to him according to law or nature. And this is the most excellent of all virtues; without it there can be no piety, no religion, no holiness, and no discernment of goods and evils. For it is light and great splendor, from which the force and dominion of every good arises among mortals. Justice is the mother of innocence, the nurse of concord, the parent of friendship, the begetter of piety, and the guardian of religion. Justice, as Scripture bears witness, is perpetual and immortal; injustice, however, is the acquisition of death.

By what means the Christian soldier ought to put on this breastplate of justice, our Savior himself declares in the holy Gospel, saying: “All things whatsoever you wish that men should do to you, do you also do likewise to them.” Therefore, my brothers, keep this manner of living in our [unclear]: that each one do to his fellow-soldier in all things just as, according to the right judgment of reason, he desires at all times that it be done to him by others. For this is the first duty of justice, that one harm no one; then that in common uses [unclear] to his brother what conduces to salvation by just reason as his own. O what great peace mortals would enjoy if they followed the exhortations of the Apostle, by which each is commanded to bear the burdens of the other. Everyone, says Scripture, who does justice is now born of God.

This, brothers, is the breastplate of justice: to keep the commandments of God, to love one’s neighbor, and to love God most ardently above all things. This is the strongest garment of our warfare: to show worship and piety to God, holiness to ourselves, and brotherly charity to our neighbor. Whoever has not been under justice shows that he belongs not to Christ’s soldiery but rather to the company of demons. For justice is the greatest virtue, which distributes to each one what is his; unless you keep it most diligently, you lose the name both of monk and soldier. Therefore render worship and piety to God; obedience to your [unclear] or rector, together with reverence, of which one belongs to the heart, the other to action. Nor is it enough to obey superiors outwardly, unless from the inward affection of the heart we likewise think rightly about them. And the true justice of monks, brothers, is this: that each one conducts himself without offense according to the prescribed rule of the law, granting to each his own proper dignity: reverence to his superior, concord to his equal, discipline to the lesser, worship and obedience to God.
