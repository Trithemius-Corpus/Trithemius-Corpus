*On the Brotherly Charity of Monks.*

It covers a multitude of sins. How necessary brotherly charity is for salvation the Lord of all, Jesus Christ, made clear, saying: “Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and greatest commandment. The second is like unto it: Thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.” For whatever is commanded anywhere in the whole course of the Scriptures is without doubt referred to these two commandments.

Here by “neighbor” we understand not one joined by the necessity of blood, but every man by the fellowship of reason, and especially the Christian, whom each person is commanded to love as himself, with whom he is seen to await eternal salvation. But you, brothers, it especially befits to love one another in the true charity of the Savior Jesus, upon whom the monastic profession enjoins with all your strength to strive toward the summit of perfection. For as covetousness is the root of all evils, so charity is the root of all goods. First you ought to love God above all things with a pure heart for his own sake, because he is the highest good; and then to love your neighbor with sincere charity for the Lord’s sake.

These two precepts of charity are joined by the authority of Christ, so that one can in no way be fulfilled without the other. For he who truly loves God cannot hate his neighbor. If he hates his neighbor, he does not truly love God. For the love of God begets love of neighbor, and through love of neighbor the love of God is nourished. If one of these loves is lacking, the other by no means grows strong. For he who does not truly love his neighbor shows himself empty of the love of God. Therefore we love truly when we love our neighbor, that is, every Christian man, more diligently with sincere intention.

For brotherly charity is the bond of peace, which alone nourishes concord in a dwelling and preserves Christian unity among brothers. Charity is the begetter of gentleness and the noble mother of catholic piety. Brotherly charity is fearful to demons, since it makes victorious all those whose inner court of the heart it surrounds. Charity extinguishes the fire of lust and covers every multitude of sins. Charity renders men most dear to God and makes them companions of the holy angels. Charity preserves the church in the unity of the saints, outside which no one attains eternal salvation. Brotherly charity orders the morals of the cloistered and makes [God?] dwell in the midst of monks. Charity does injury to no one, but ministers help to all through compassion. Charity drives out every vice, because it is the sole nurse of all virtues. The charity of brotherhood preserves the reform of the cloistered in vigor, and works great salvation of souls. Mutual charity confirms obedience and makes brotherly correction fruitful. Charity nourishes true concord among brothers and preserves the sweet-smelling chastity both of minds and of bodies. Charity makes monks in the house of God to be of one mind, and laudably concordant in every good work. Mutual charity lightens the rigor of the order; and all things that seem hard and harsh to carnal men it changes through habit into nature, as though they were certain very light things. Charity is the most beautiful beauty of monks, adorned with all virtues. Charity utterly drives hatred and envy out of the monasteries of the servants of God, and by its presence makes the remaining deadly crimes flee far away. The charity of brothers is a fitting dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and the fortress of a serene conscience. Charity keeps minds harmless from cruelty and supplies to all a gentle ease of spirit. Brotherly charity seeks the salvation of all and does not allow the one who has it to grow sluggish in idleness. Charity does not abhor want, but from love of the brothers is ready to endure every poverty. Charity is the water of heavenly sweetness; if anyone drinks it in the fervor of the Holy Spirit, he does not dread the perpetual thirst of hell. Brotherly charity does not seek the enticements of the flesh, but directs pure, holy, and unstained affections toward the neighbor. Charity has the name of chaste beauty, which cannot be shared with things or actions of most impure pleasure. Charity loves purity and cleanness, and admits no impurity. Charity, in the operation of love, considers not the flesh of the neighbor but the soul, which it does not doubt was created in the image of God. Brotherly charity raises up the delinquent neighbor by pious admonitions, and keeps all its actions pure and harmless from every malice. Charity walks in peace and tranquillity of mind, and saddens the neighbor neither by word, nor by deed, nor by sign.
