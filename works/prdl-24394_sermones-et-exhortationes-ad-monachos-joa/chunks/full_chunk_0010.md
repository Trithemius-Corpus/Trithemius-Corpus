*All things. II. Fol. VI.*

...if you shall have kept chastity with humility, for love of my name, unto the end; if you shall always have striven to please me alone in all your actions; if you shall have taken care to follow me without interruption along the steep road of the virtues: knowing this without doubt, know most certainly that, as a servant made my very dear friend, I shall have you as a soldier to be crowned with me in my kingdom; I shall love you, I shall cherish you, I shall establish you glorious forever in the midst of my saints.

Hear me now, my servant, you who, for reverence and honor of my name, have enrolled yourself in the warfare of monks; and with a great and perfect heart follow the footsteps of my feet, as that teacher of yours, the blessed friend, once taught you and with all his efforts imitated. Let not the enticements of the flesh deceive you, O strenuous soldier; let not the vain and destructive dealings of this world seduce your soul, dedicated to me. Never yield to the exceedingly deceitful suggestions of the unclean spirit, who rages wholly for your destruction; but remember that your sojourn in this age is very brief, and that death stands before the doors, which no one of mortals has ever been able to escape.

Let those last things which you must necessarily undergo always stand in your memory; and from the very swift passing-away of the pleasures of the flesh which have gone by, take up for yourself a necessary dread and contempt of the future things of this sojourn. Just as labor for my sake, considered brief in the world and borne with patience and charity, prepares eternal happiness for my soldiers, so the desires and pleasures of the flesh, which suddenly pass away, plunge the soul of a sinful man down to hell, to be tormented without end.

Therefore direct all your desires to me, monk who minister to me and who follow me by the steep road of regular observance. Offer me your heart if you desire happiness; seek me alone if you love your own life and salvation. When you have loved God, you are tranquil and holy; when you have followed him, you are blessed and glorious without end.

What, brothers, would you do if it happened to you to hear these words which we have spoken from the mouth of our Savior and King as he spoke? What, I say, would you do? I certainly believe that there would be great compunction in your hearts if you heard Christ speaking in person. But we, holding the place of Christ in the monastery, speak the words which he has given, and you do not attend. We set before you the necessary arena of salvation, and you show no signs of compunction.

Why it was fitting that I say these things, if you do not know, I pray you, understand. I see some of you shrinking from sacred readings, given over to vanities, and caring for nothing less than the perfection of the spiritual warfare; their god is the belly, and their glory is in confusion. Do you think, most worthless knaves, who have no taste for anything spiritual, that Christ is to come again from heaven to speak to you those things which he committed to his preachers to be spoken? Have you not heard Paul saying: “Christ, rising from the dead, now dies no more; death shall have no further dominion over him. For in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God.”

Once Christ was seen on earth; once he appeared visible among mortals; once he preached the way of eternal salvation; he will not add a second time. He appointed pastors and vicars over his household to teach the ignorant; wishing to make manifest their authority, he said in the Gospel: “He who hears you hears me, and he who despises you despises me.” Therefore hear those whom Christ has set over you as rulers, to whom he entrusted the distribution of spiritual wheat, as he said in the holy Gospel: “Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward whom the lord has set over his household, to give them their measure of wheat in due season? Blessed is that servant whom, when the lord comes, he shall find doing thus.”

We therefore exhort you, brothers, in the Lord Jesus, and with what diligence we can we beg you, do not consume your days in vanity, but consider the exceeding brevity of this life, and bring forth worthy fruits of repentance while it is granted to you at the proper time, lest, if you neglect to repent, the time be cut off. For you know what the divine Scripture says to us: “The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night.” Cease, I pray, to put off the good, because no one of you knows when he is to die; rather, attend to that true saying of the wanton poet Naso, who thus admonishes: “But make haste, and do not defer yourself to the coming hours: he who is not ready today will be less fit tomorrow.” No one [unclear] life for himself
