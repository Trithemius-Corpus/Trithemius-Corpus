*On the True Humility of Monks.*

Humility eagerly obeys the commandments of God with a willing disposition pleasing to him; pride resists the precepts of the Most High. Humility heaps up the merits of everlasting blessedness; pride, however, prepares for itself the most grievous torments of hell. Humility provides security at the point of death; pride brings horrible fear and calamity to the dying man. Humility frees the soul of its devotee from every distress in the dread judgment of God; pride, however, accuses before the judge all the haughty whom it deceived in the world. Humility will place the servants of God at the judge’s right hand; pride will set those to be damned at the left. Humility will plead the cause for its own and will prevail in judgment; pride, surely confounded, will hand the haughty over to the devil. Humility will repay the reward of eternal blessedness to its friends; pride, however, will give the torment of perpetual damnation to all the haughty. Humility will be crowned without end in heaven; pride, however, will suffer immeasurable torment with the demons forever in the lower regions.

Humility, crowned with Christ, reigns in the heavens; and pride, despised, will be tormented with [unclear] in punishments. Humility, joyful and glorious, will sit forever at the right hand of [unclear]; pride, however, sorrowful and ignominious, will lie without end in the torments of hell. Then the humble will exult in glory, and all the proud, because of excessive pain, will cry out in punishment for all ages. There the humble will stand in the sight of God and of all the saints in great joy and everlasting exultation, receiving, for the voluntary poverty of this life, unfading riches; for meekness, a kingdom; for sorrow [unclear], and for the spontaneous mortification of the flesh, heaven. But the proud and arrogant, placed in hell, seeing such great glory of the humble whom they despised in the world, will lament in so sudden a change of [unclear], and will be troubled with horrible fear, groaning under the very great anguish of spirit and saying in the fury of pride: These are the poor and humble monks, once clothed in vile garments, separated from men, abstaining from the eating of meats and from every filthiness of the flesh, whom we held at one time in derision and as a likeness of reproach; senseless as we were, we judged their life madness, and their end without honor. Behold how they are counted among the sons of God, and their lot is among the saints.

Therefore we have strayed from the way of truth, and the light of justice has not shone upon us, and the sun of understanding has not risen for us. What does pride now profit us, by which, when we were then puffed up, we despised the humble? And what does the boastfulness of riches possessed in the world bring us? All things have passed away, and through continual contumacy we have been counted among the demons; because we always had humility in hatred, we now suffer immeasurable torments. But the humble will live forever, and their reward is with the Lord, and the care of them is with the Most High. These things, my brothers, always think upon beforehand, because without doubt they are thus to be; and therefore assume humility of mind, flee every [unclear] through it, keep a pure conscience, and may you deserve to obtain the rewards of eternal life. Amen.

*On the True Obedience of Monks: Homily XX.*

If the humility of monks has been true and perfect, it brings forth the whole fullness of the holy virtues. But if it has lacked the splendor of the virtues, true humility was never in the mind. For first it brings forth true obedience, and a profound subjection in all things for the love of God. For the monk who truly lives as a pilgrim in the flesh shows himself in all things an eager [disciple?] of our Savior Jesus Christ; for love of him, despising every vileness and extremity, he glories in being subject to his abbot, obedient in all things. Obedience is a great virtue: it opens heaven to the humble monk and by its strength closes hell. Obedience restores the way of paradise and reforms peace between God and man. This, however, is true monastic obedience: that which is rendered to the commands of superiors not from servile fear but from an inward affection of charity, which, persevering until death not through fear of punishment but through love of justice, works good in cheerfulness of mind. There are two most beautiful wings of the mind by which the monk flies from earth to heaven; one of these is called humility, the other is known as the obedience of subjection. The monk who has not been adorned with these wings has entirely lost both the name and the merit of his dignity.
