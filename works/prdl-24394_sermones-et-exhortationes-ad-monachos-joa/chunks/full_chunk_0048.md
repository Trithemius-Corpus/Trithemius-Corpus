*Homily XI.*

...with the carnal: out of the abundance of the heart they speak nothing but vain and carnal things. For the wicked conversations of old men corrupt the good morals of the young. All their pursuits are vain, and their speeches useless, indeed pestilential, since, being carnally minded, they have not known the exercises of divine love. Indeed, just as one sick sheep gradually corrupts the whole sheepfold, so one slothful and perverse monk in a congregation often becomes the cause of many transgressions. As Saint [Christophilus?] bears witness, a little leaven corrupts with its odor the whole mass of flour with which it is mixed; so young monks, and [unclear], who associate with lukewarm and slothful elders, are imbued with their depraved habits and thereafter are never restored to the integrity of holy fervor. Therefore I say to you elders: be, by good works and chaste and always modest speech, a model of life for the young, so that whenever they are corrected for their offenses, they may not defend themselves by the examples of the elders. For it is most shameful that elders should be guilty in those things for which the younger are deservedly reproved. Live, I pray, old men, in such a way that in your presence the younger may presume neither to say nor to do anything that seems blameworthy.

Let them fear you as judges of evils, not as acting the part of patrons, since as many as sin by your example are reckoned to have perished by your teaching. Let no one among you become lukewarm or negligent, since desires kill the lazy man, and the vanity of all things destroys the idle monk. Let there be among you no talkative man, no wandering monk, no roving idler, murmuring and restless, since whoever delights in such pursuits both ruins himself by evil conduct and kills his brother by his examples. Let there be among you no mocker of the simple, no despiser of the humble, nor any rash censurer, since he who is quick to mock will be consumed in his foolishness and will not be glorious. He opens his mouth against the humble, is always seeking quarrels, and rejoices that he is talkative about the deeds of others.

He will not prosper in his counsel; and if he is raised to some honor, he will quickly fall into the depth. His pride is hateful to God, since, although he is stupid and foolish, he boasts that he is learned and wise. Soon his foolishness will be laid bare, and a grievous destruction will seize him.

*Folio XXVI.*

His heart meditates folly, and there is no peace around him. He will be hateful to those near him and to his neighbors, and no faithful friend will accompany him. He leads his life in hypocrisy, having assumed a pretense of dissimulation, and by his foolishness he will draw many into aversion. He hates peace and loves discord; he will be caught in the sloth of his mind, and there will be no one to grieve with him. He will be crushed by a double affliction, because he did not permit peace to those who had it. The devil has made a nest in his tongue, and Satan has fixed his dwelling in his mind. Therefore there is no love of the brethren in his heart, since he sows discord between the shepherd and the sheep. He boasts that he is a friend of God and accepts no one as his equal in regular observance, but he does not understand humility, the greatest ornament of a monk.

He does not open his mouth for words of spiritual edification, and from his heart nothing but vanity proceeds. All day long, dissolved in mind and wandering in body, he runs about in the monastery; he gives himself to useless and utterly idle speech; he thinks nothing of the salvation of his soul; he cares for his belly as God. Guard your mind from his speech, and do not set your foot in the way of his words.

Whoever desires to show himself a monk and minister of God, whoever truly loves the Lord Jesus with his whole heart, be cautious and watchful in all your works, and always flee the companionship of those in whose life nothing of heavenly doctrine is seen. Be the companion of humble monks seeking perfection and [unclear], who yields, shaken, to every wind; but strengthen your soul in a good purpose [unclear] to love, or be deceived by the counsel of the depraved. For it is best that the heart be established by grace; and the one who instructs by example toward the good, love and venerate as a father. Have no commerce of familiarity with a restless brother, lest at some time, strengthened by his depraved example, you fall into the rebellion of the proud.
