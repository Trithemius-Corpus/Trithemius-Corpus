*On the knowledge of spiritual grace.*

Third, if you do not wish to lose the grace of inward compunction, you should strive to love the quiet of solitude. For divine Scripture says: “Upon whom shall my spirit rest, if not upon the humble and quiet?” And again it says in another place: “In silence and in hope shall be your strength.” For the grace of inward compunction is quickly lost when the secrecy of solitude is not loved. Therefore let the monk who wishes to possess the grace of God learn gladly to remain with himself in his cell.

Fourth, one must take the greatest care that you do not despise in your heart any person, however evil; for by the just judgment of God the grace of compunction is withdrawn whenever rash judgment concerning one’s neighbor is not avoided. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “Do not judge, and you shall not be judged; do not condemn, and you shall not be condemned.”

Fifth, the monk who does not wish to lose the grace of inward devotion should strive to exercise himself in the cultivation of all virtues. Let him always keep watch in the fear of the Lord, always occupy himself in the exercise of divine love; let him be constantly assiduous in good works, and, as far as he is able, devout at all times in the remembrance of the Lord’s passion. For attentive meditation on the passion of Christ strengthens a person against all vices and preserves the grace of compunction.

Sixth, be constant in holy prayers, because he who interrupts the practice of prayer is not able to preserve the grace of compunction. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: “Watch and pray, that you may not enter into temptation.” The help of the Lord is close at hand for those who call upon him; but he who neglects to pray loses grace. But if you lose the grace of devotion in any way, you will quickly recover it by the following same six remedies.

First, consider that without the grace of God you cannot make progress toward salvation, but that in all the good things you are going to do you are in the greatest need of his mercy. Recognize, therefore, that you are unworthy of the grace of God for doing good, although, because of the multitude of your sins, you know yourself to be unworthy. Call upon him, for he is near; and make every time that is granted you as free from the common exercises of the brothers be spent usefully and fruitfully. The grace of God is necessary for you for doing good; it is acquired by breadth of charity, guarded by humility, and recovered by the practice of saving penance. The Lord is with you so long as you have been with him in good will. But in order that you may be able to recover the lost grace of devotion, you must submit yourself to God in true humility.

Second, for the recovery of the lost grace of compunction it seems necessary that you never lift yourself up against the Lord, but conform your will in all things to the divine ordering. We say in the Lord’s Prayer, “May his will be done”; it is fitting that we always voluntarily agree with that very prayer in mind and affection.

Third, he who desires to recover the lost grace of devotion must strongly despise every pleasure of this world for the love of God. For it cannot happen that he recovers the spirit of inward compunction who opens the dwelling-place of his heart to earthly joys and pleasures. Therefore you must cast away every consolation of the present life, if you wish to have the divine spirit of love with its gifts.

Fourth, you are commanded to withdraw yourself from the vain cares and occupations of this world for your own sake, and at every time to occupy your mind delightfully in the service of God. For, as the Lord says, no one can serve God and mammon; but whoever desires to serve the Lord in truth of heart must despise all the occupations of the world.

Fifth, we advise that you remember always to occupy your heart in meditation on the passion of Christ, because there is no other way more expedient for recovering the delay [unclear] of compunction, nor any manner of prayer more effective for obtaining from the Lord whatever we have asked.

Sixth, you should strive daily to cleanse your conscience by true penance; and whenever you have fallen into some fault, at once come to your senses through contrition. If you have taken care to keep these things with good will, by the mercy of God you will recover the lost grace of compunction.

*On the nine degrees of ascent.*

*Sermon VII.*

With the pen we describe for you, dearly beloved brothers, the nine degrees by which the ascent of the human spirit into heaven is made. And although one is higher than another, nevertheless all and each direct one to the kingdom of God, and they are distinguished according to the merits of those ascending. Therefore attend diligently to what we are going to say, and consider how much you have made progress in spiritual advancement. All, as many as reach the kingdom of God, have ascended by the degrees of this ladder. The more anyone has made progress in the love of God, the higher he has already ascended.
