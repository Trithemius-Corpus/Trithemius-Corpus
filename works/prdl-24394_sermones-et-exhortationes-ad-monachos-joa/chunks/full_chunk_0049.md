Whomever you find wholly solicitous in every zeal to live in regular observance, love and venerate as a father and a most beloved teacher. With confidence enjoy the familiarity of him whose friendship always makes you better in the love of God. With all zeal imitate good men, and follow the conduct of those who live in conformity with monastic perfection. Have as a friend the brother of good will; but turn your ears away from him who is of hard heart. Let the man who is soft in spirit and dissolute in conduct be to you like a man who breaks down hedges in a field. Do not be the companion of him who despises the rule and silence, who distinguishes neither places nor times, who without the prior’s blessing everywhere [unclear] and multiplies [unclear]. The monk who, given to wine and drinking, rather often exceeds the common canon of the monastery is the fuel of lust and the spring [unclear] of all vices.

The monk who relies on the counsel of his own head against the [unclear] command is close to perdition, and walks festively in danger of his soul. Do not share in the works of an obstinate monk, nor have friendship with the man who, laughing at the deserved deeds of his superior, speaks against them. Be continually in the midst of disciplined men, and let your soul gladly walk with the humble. For he who is joined to good men shares in their virtue, and he who walks with the wicked will fall into a pit. But a monk of perverse will leads very many astray; and he who despises obedience will never be exalted. He who lifts up his voice and cries out with loud shouting cannot hide the foolishness of his heart, and glories in his vanity.

The friendship of good men is firm salvation for friends, and in the company of transgressors simple innocence is corrupted. The company of those men is best in whom the fear of God has confirmed examples of good works. Nothing is more precious than a friend who fears God, and nothing more dangerous than a companion who is of perverse will. A brother who fears God with all his heart will be a wall of strength for his friend; and the man joined to such a one in holiness of mind is sanctified at all times by the blessing of the Spirit of God. He who rejoices in the midst of those who fear God is sustained, lest he fail, as if by a very strong wall around him.

From his words the servant of the Most High is known, and works of holiness reveal the good monk. No foul or scurrilous word comes quickly from the mouth of one who fears the Lord; and he who is a true lover of Jesus does nothing that offends goodness. In the mouth of monks who love [God], Christ always resounds; and he who is a true worshipper of God demonstrates the discourse of righteousness both in his mouth and in his work. He who fears God is the best counsellor; and he who walks according to the canon is a prudent judge of thoughts. An elder who fears God and has long been proved and experienced in regular observance is a director of the young; and he who has no stain in his garment should be a good instructor for novices. He who is an understanding teacher and expounder of the divine scriptures, guarding his soul in humility: receive all his words gladly without [unclear], since he has the sinews and solidity of future eternal happiness.

He who teaches truly also suffers with the infirm; and he who loves Jesus encourages the wavering spirit to strength. He who walks with a verbose man will not escape contention; and he who delights in falsehood will never be a friend of wisdom. He who is assiduous in external things will not make progress, and boasts without cause that he is a monk. The purpose of holiness is quickly corrupted when a monk wanders outside his cell rather too often through [unclear] desire. He who abandons the secrecy of his cell without the command of an elder does not strive toward the perfection of the holy purpose. Beautiful is the company of good men and of those who fear God; and he who has chosen to live with them will not feel confusion.
