And whatever holy Church, the mother of the faithful, has decreed either to be believed or to be observed, let him believe and keep faithfully and firmly, and let him in no way depart from her determination. For all who once remained outside the ark the flood destroyed; Noah alone, with those who were in the ark, escaped safe. The ark furnished a figure of the Church, and the thing done prefigured a mystery. Hence there is no salvation for human beings outside the Church, which our Lord Jesus Christ built upon the firmest rock. Every unbeliever is not in the Church, and therefore will necessarily perish, just as those once did who were not in the ark. For all who do not believe in the Lord and Savior of the world, Jesus Christ, Jews, pagans, Gentiles, Saracens, and heretics and schismatics who have withdrawn themselves from the unity of the Catholic Church, are placed outside the ark of salvation; and if they persevere in error, without doubt they are to be damned forever with the demons.

Since, therefore, faith is the foundation of salvation, without which it is impossible to please God, the monk must know what he ought to believe, and [unclear]. It is most shameful for a monk not to know the rules of the Christian faith, and to be utterly ignorant of what he is bound to believe for salvation. We do not say that by curious argumentation you ought to examine the things proposed to you for belief, since, as holy Pope Gregory says, faith has no merit where human reason provides proof. But this we advise: that the monk ought to be diligent in the divine Scriptures, so that he may know what holy mother Church commands to be believed, and may be able to guard against all errors contrary to the faith. Therefore let every monk, a soldier of Jesus Christ, be firm and steadfast in the Catholic faith; let him be sober and diligent in the Scriptures and constant in good works.

The second thing which we believe necessary for a monk walking on the way of eternal salvation is that he should strive to know himself. For knowledge of oneself is the best of all forms of knowledge, and therefore rightly to be preferred to all the studies of mortals. Yet the beginning of this salutary knowledge is exceedingly difficult, because there are very few who do not love the world. For everyone who follows the world and loves the vanities that pass away cannot be a knower of himself. And nothing impedes a human being more from knowledge of himself than that human being himself. He who is fed on the pleasures of his flesh, who is lifted up into pride by the vain knowledge of this world, or by riches or honors, is not permitted to come to knowledge of himself. But to know many things and neglect oneself is not wisdom but rather madness. It belongs to the heap of a heavier damnation to learn many things and shamefully to be ignorant of oneself.

Let each of you therefore consider what he is, whence he is, and what he is going to be. A human being, certainly, is nothing through himself; whatever he is, he is from God and for God. What is a human being except a wayfarer and pilgrim of, as it were, one day upon the earth, who today is seen alive in the world, but in the morning, snatched away by death, is seen no more? All our life, every action, every day, every hour shows how nothing we are: we who by living die, by acting are corrupted, with time are extinguished and changed. O tearful race of human beings, unstable, wretched, and weak, how airy, blind, and vain I perceive you to be, when I make comparison from those who have preceded us to death to us who are present.

He spoke truly who compared you to the leaf of a tree. The wind, snatching it from the tree, casts it to the ground; in its place another new one afterward grows up, subject to a like fall. But what has fallen to the ground is dissolved in the ground, and thereafter never returns to the tree. Hence a certain sage of the Greeks says: “As is the race of leaves, so too is the race of men.” The race of man is very like the race of leaves, because it grows green for a time and is dissolved in the earth; neither does the leaf return to the tree nor the human being to life.

But because true humility belongs to few, for that reason the way of salvation is called narrow and is found by few. By humility one comes to the kingdom, as the Lord says in the holy Gospel: everyone who humbles himself for God’s sake will be exalted, and he who exalts himself will be humbled.
