The food of chastity is fasting and abstinence, and in every action the firm support of wholesome continence. But when you have attained all things, do not trust; for he whom we know to have eaten nothing fell from heaven. The mortification of the body is both very necessary and useful for preserving the beauty of chastity; but however much you have advanced in both, do not trust in your own self, but, always watchful and hoping in the help of the Lord, fear. For unless you continually and most urgently keep yourself in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overturned.

He runs in vain who presumes to guard chastity by his own strength. For unless the Lord has built the chaste dwelling, he labors fruitlessly who constructs the wall of abstinence. And unless you, with all your strength, have stood forth as a co-worker of divine grace, you will in no way obtain the beauty of chastity. For God will not make you chaste without your will, nor will you obtain this great gift unless he comes to the aid of your weakness through his mercy. It will therefore be yours, O monk, with good will, to offer the desire of chastity, and in humble confidence to await sure increase from the Lord. Apply effort, and the Lord’s help will not be lacking.

Chastity is a gift of grace, not of nature; and to live in the flesh beyond the flesh belongs to angelic strength, not human. Nevertheless it is not difficult for a man of good will to preserve chastity, especially one who is separated from the tumult of worldly people and lives solitary in the midst of monks. A monk also who, young and innocent, enters a monastery of good reformation, if he continually occupies his mind in the fear of the Lord, is less assaulted by lust. For experience of desire is pernicious, since, as someone truly said, Venus wishes to conquer souls, not to satisfy them. But a monk who is zealous for his own salvation can easily extinguish the burning of desire if, fleeing idleness and fullness, he is willing to occupy himself continually in spiritual exercise.

Indeed, among all vices none assaults chastity more greatly and dangerously than security and idleness. If a monk has overcome these two evils, he will easily guard chastity. Hence the most holy princes of monks, wishing to cut off the causes of desire, established for us a sparing and slender diet according to a fixed measure, so that the body may be sustained for necessity and not grow fat for desire. They also decreed sparing sleep to be useful for monks, because immoderate rest stretches the flesh no less than gluttony. But idleness, as the most pernicious plague of the soul, they wished to be far from cloistered men, most wisely dividing the time so that a monk would never be found idle.

Hear these things, brothers, and grasp them with the mind, you who carry a great treasure in a fragile little body. Flee idleness; always do something good, so that whenever the tempter, the devil, approaches, he may find you usefully occupied. For truly, the man who is idle lies open to the snares of the tempter; but he who is occupied in a good work has closed every entrance to snares. Therefore the soul must be occupied with continual exercise, so that it is never found idle.

Blessed is that monk who occupies his mind at every time in good exercises, and never drowses in the sleep of torpor and sloth. Blessed is the spiritual soldier of Christ who always watches in the fear of the Lord and immediately restrains from the beginning all disordered movements of the flesh. For it is not enough, for guarding true chastity unharmed, if anyone dallies as though deliberating with unclean thoughts. The first movements of wicked thoughts must be resisted, since where delay begets danger, one must immediately fight with strength. One must always keep watch against the adversary of our salvation, who is never more quickly conquered than if he is at once suffocated at the beginning.

Continual exercise is needed, brothers, because the mind always occupied in holy studies is like a very high tower of strength; stationed in it, the most watchful soldier of Jesus Christ fears no snares of hostility. Therefore I beseech you, my dearest brothers, do not consume time in idleness, lest you increase in yourselves the matter of vices, you who are not ignorant of the cunning of the malign spirit. Preserve continence in all things, and apply temperance, the directress of virtues, to all your works. Always consider how you ought to walk before the Lord in all religion and holiness.
