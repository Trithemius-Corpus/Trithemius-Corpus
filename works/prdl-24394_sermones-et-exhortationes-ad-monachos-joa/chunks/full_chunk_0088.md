*On the true humility of monks.*

But the continual [fear?] of God forbids every wantonness to the heart. The free compassion of God also forestalls some, and makes them humble by the indwelling of the Holy Spirit. Everyone who is led by the Spirit of God utterly detests pride.

He who truly knows himself is most wise, and cannot depart from the meekness of humility. But every proud and arrogant man is ignorant, even if he seems great and famous among mortals. For true wisdom will rest in the dwelling-place of holy humility, and it is itself shown more clearly through the lesson of patience. Humility is born from knowledge of God and of oneself, and this is rightly called true wisdom.

He who has truly known himself now has nothing for which to be proud, since, knowing his own vileness, he thinks nothing lofty, but, placed in sorrow of soul, cries to the Lord: What is man, that you are mindful of him, or the son of man, that you account him? Man has been made like vanity; his days, as it is written, [unclear] under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation.

For humility with chaste fear greatly pleases the Lord God in his servants, since he approves no virtue of a man unto salvation without it. Therefore, if we desire to climb to the lofty threshold of the kingdom of heaven, we must be very humble, because there is no other road to heaven except that of the deepest humility, by which both the Son of God himself and all the saints ascended. But all who have tried to enter the kingdom by another gate, their head of pride having been struck back on the threshold of humility, could by no means enter. For the gate of humility does not admit the proud, and the narrow road of meekness does not receive lovers of greed and self-exaltation.

Therefore whoever has [unclear] to come to the height of the heavenly [unclear], let him ascend by the steps of humility; without these, whoever begins to ascend undoubtedly falls into the depth of perdition and death. For humility is the ladder of heaven, beautifully distinguished by twelve rungs; concerning its ascent our lawgiver, blessed Benedict, says in the Rule: If we wish to reach the highest summit of humility, and swiftly to arrive at that heavenly exaltation, we must set up for ourselves that ladder which appeared to Jacob in dreams, by which angels were shown to him ascending and descending. Without doubt that descent and ascent is understood by us to be nothing other than to descend by exaltation and to ascend by humility, as divine Scripture tells us: Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.

The ladder itself, set upright, is our life in this world, which is raised up to heaven by the Lord when the heart has been humbled. For we say that the sides of the same ladder are our body and soul. Into these sides the divine calling has inserted the different steps of humility and discipline that are to be ascended. Thereafter, indeed, he enumerates those twelve steps in order with a sufficiently beautiful arrangement; since they are all very well known to you, it would be superfluous to spend longer on reviewing them on this occasion.

For it is enough in this place to remember the holy canon, upon the words of which you all long ago willingly swore, so that you may know that humility is no less necessary for salvation for you than love of God and, at the same time, of neighbor. For humility is said to be the monk’s greatest virtue, and it is so necessary to his profession that without it he cannot be saved. A monk exalted in mind and proud is not the bondman of Christ, but of the devil; he will have no share with the Son of God in heaven, but will be tormented without end with the demons in hell.

For Christ, the teacher and instructor of humility, detests all the proud, and admits into his company only the humble, the gentle, and the meek. But the devil is the head and inventor of pride, whose servants and disciples are all who follow his pride and self-exaltation. For he himself, the king of the proud, cannot bear the humble; he flees the gentle, and does not dare to stand in the sight of the meek in heart. For Christ dwells in the assembly of the humble servants of God; but in the midst of the proud the devil stirs up the contentions and quarrels of those at discord.

Where there is humility, there is true peace and the highest tranquillity; but where pride rules, there rancor, anger, disturbance, and discord confuse everything without ceasing. Therefore, my brothers, avoid pride, since if it cast down the exalted angels from heaven, when would it not condemn poor cloistered men?
