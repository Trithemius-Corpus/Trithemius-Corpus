For Scripture says to the Lord: “Those who withdraw far from you will perish; and you have destroyed all who work iniquity.” For if the mind, through sloth, has withdrawn farther from the fervor of divine love, it stands very near to hell and despair; more rarely and with greater difficulty does it recover to true repentance.

Whoever therefore among you, my brothers, desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the inward fervor of the spirit, let him with the utmost and continual effort strive to drive from himself the torpor of mind and sloth, and daily to renew the purpose of divine love. It is a hard thing, I admit, to resist the evil habit of the flesh, and, in an old [unclear], to think new thoughts against custom. Certainly, brothers, no one with a lukewarm will can afterwards rise again from the depths of hell; nor will anyone be able to overcome an evil habit without great violence and a long contrary habit. For no one becomes holy by his own powers, but it is God who works all things in all; nevertheless it is plainly established for us that the kingdom of God suffers violence, and whoever neglects to do violence to the motions of his flesh will not attain to the fellowship of the saints of the Lord.

Hear now, monk, whoever you are who are lukewarm toward the love of God and slothful; hear, whoever you are, and take thought: unless you rise continually from the torpor of an evil habit of life, unless you bring violence upon yourself by a habit contrary to [it], unless you prepare your heart to resist in the fervor of divine love, [unclear] you will see the face of God in glory. Sloth is the root of despair; and not only the root, but also its nurse and mother. If once it has oppressed the mind of a man dedicated to God, it at once closes off every way to salvation. For the kingdom of heaven has not been promised to those who sleep; eternal blessedness has not been prepared for those who grow drowsy in leisure and idleness. Rather, the crown is laid up for those who manfully command the motions of the flesh, and the happiness of glory is given to those who follow the spirit, not the flesh.

No virtue is acquired without labor; no enemy is overcome without an encounter. If you desire to be crowned, if you desire to conquer, you must fight bravely and perseveringly. Wherefore also Solomon cries out to you, lazy sluggard: “When will you rise from your sleep?” If you are lazy, your harvest will come like a spring [unclear], and poverty and want will flee from you [unclear]. For he who gathers in harvest is a wise son, and he who sleeps in summer is a son of confusion. For he who works his own field will be filled with bread; but he who delights in idleness will be filled with poverty.

This, brothers, is the good season for working; this is the opportunity for meriting the eternal blessedness granted to us by the Lord God, which is suddenly taken away in death. For Scripture says: “Whatever your hand is able to do, work earnestly; for neither work, nor reason, nor wisdom, nor knowledge will be among the dead, to whom you are hastening.” If the eternal good of blessedness that is promised delights you, the labors of the struggle that are set before you must not frighten your minds. For no one will be crowned by the Lord unless he has striven lawfully; no one attains the summit of eternal blessedness except those who, by fervor of spirit, have overcome the illicit movements of the flesh.

We must always fight, brothers, against the threefold battle-line of the devil so long as we live in this pilgrimage; for no one here is seen to become unsurpassable who is not gradually overcome by tepidity [unclear]. Saint Augustine proclaims: “No one ought to be secure in this life, which is wholly called temptation; for he who could have become better from worse may not become worse from better.” Hence Solomon says in the Book of Proverbs: “Desires kill the lazy man.” Just as constancy bestows perfection on every good work, so sloth, the stepmother of all religion and perfection, destroys the spiritual mind. Saint Jerome says: “Nothing in a holy purpose is worse than leisure; for not only is no new good acquired, but even what has been acquired is shamefully made worse.” The principle of a holy spiritual life advances by continual progress, and the joy grows and increases; but through cessation it becomes torpid and fails.

Therefore, brothers, the mind must be renewed by daily and fresh increases of the virtues, and those things to be avoided must be measured not by what has passed, but by what remains. Idleness, which is the mother and nurse of evils, and foremost of the enemies of the virtues, must be fled; but sloth is to be overcome not by fleeing, but by resisting. For Scripture says: “Idleness has taught much evil.” For every idle man is in desires vain and pernicious [unclear]. Therefore the monk who desires to be a soldier of Christ and of true purity of mind must always be occupied in some exercise, so that idleness may not beget vice.
