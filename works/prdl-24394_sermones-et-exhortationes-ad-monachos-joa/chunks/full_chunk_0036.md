*That one must pray without intermission.*

…may be forgiven you. Before [your] prayers, prepare your soul, and be not as a man who tempts God. Let the mind be pure and ardent in the love of the Lord; let the conscience be clean and strengthened in the purpose of a holy way of life; and the prayer will be pleasing to the divine ears. If you pray otherwise, you will obtain nothing. He prays well who lives well; and he easily obtains who prays with holy works. Finally, prayer is rendered much effective and pleasing in the sight of God, if it be at all times consonant with the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. For Christ is God and man, the mediator between God and us, through whom we have safe access to the Father, as he himself testifies in the holy Gospel: "No one," he says, "comes to the Father but through me." Whence let no day pass on which you do not at least once give thanks to Christ by praying—who by the pain of his wounds healed the languors of your soul, who shed his blood for you, and by his most innocent death redeemed you, wretched, from the destruction of perpetual death. Truly a man is most ungrateful, and unworthy of the Christian name, who is so far forgetful of Christ's so great benefits that he does not daily, or at least once, render thanksgivings to him. But let the monk—who, dead to the world, is bidden to live for God alone—carry continually in his breast the memory of the Lord's passion, always giving thanks to his Redeemer, always blessing, always adoring; and daily—nay, as much as can be done, in every single moment—let him with tears, compassionating, turn over [in his mind] the bitterness of Christ's wounds. Every day, after the several regular prayers which the Greeks call canonical, with knees bent and mind raised to heaven let him say: "I give thanks to you, and blessing [you] I adore you, O mediator of God and men, Christ Jesus, most sweet God and man, for your most holy incarnation, for your nativity from the Virgin, for [your] circumcision, for the flight into Egypt and the seven years in exile which you endured for us. I adore you, Lord Savior Jesus Christ, for your labors, for [your] hunger and thirst, cold and nakedness; for your journeys, for your teaching and preaching, for your miracles, for the calling of the nations, and for the justification of sinners. I give you thanks, Lord, Son of the living God, for the pain of your wounds; I bless you for the shedding of your most holy blood; I adore you for your death which you most innocently endured; and I beseech you, by your whole passion, [your] piety and clemency, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death." This most brief prayer the devout monk will recite at least seven times a day, so that as often as he passes before the image of the Crucified, in like manner, with fervor of mind, he may adore Christ. He who is a true lover of Christ always thinks of Christ, speaks of and venerates Christ, [and] does not doubt that Christ is everywhere present to him. The passion of Christ is never absent from his memory; and through the greatness of love he forgets himself, and in Christ is wholly occupied in mind and desire. Whoever is fervent in soul in the love of Christ despises earthly things, conquers the flesh, [and] desires only heavenly things. Waking, he ever thinks of Christ with desire; sleeping, he dreams of Christ; and, continually sighing, with the divine Ignatius he cries: "My love is crucified." He joins sighs to sighs, and Jesus—whom he loves with his whole heart—he discerns present, not with his eyes but with his spirit. With the desire of love urging [him], as often as he gazes on the Crucified, he soon dissolves into tears, and, glowing with the fire of charity, cries out: "O love stronger than all strengths! How sweetly he burns who is inflamed with the desire of Christ!" Rightly did he speak who said: "Love is strong as death." For what can be stronger than the love of Christ, by whose ardor the holy martyrs, despising so many thousand kinds of death, overcame [them]? O my crucified love, have mercy on me; grant me to feel that sweetness of your love of which I speak, that I may always carry about in my heart your most bitter passion. I know, my brothers, I know that there is no more effective mode of praying, nor more useful to us who are still weak, than to be in the memory and power of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. Whence, since it is manifest that without a mediator there is no ascent of the mind to God, every good work must be begun in the name of Christ; for then we usefully pour out prayers to God, when with purified conscience we pray through his only-begotten Son.
