Therefore flee drowsiness and sluggishness of mind as a deadly poison, because sleep and idleness are the fountain of all vices. The watchful eye, however, is the servant of inward devotion; and the monk who truly loves Jesus Christ keeps himself from every excess. The man who is watchful sharpens his mind with understanding; but the monk who sleeps much shamefully blinds his soul. The monk watchful in prayer and fasting is fearsome to the devil; but he who is sated with food and sleep is not permitted to rest by the spirit of fornication. For wakefulness dedicated to prayers breaks the heat of lust; it also powerfully extinguishes the fire of concupiscence; it purifies the inmost parts of the heart; it illumines the mind; it inflames the affection toward love of heavenly things. Wakefulness is the parent, nurse, and guardian of good thoughts; the certain victory over the temptations of the ancient enemy; the flight of phantasms; the death of vices, the life and fountain of all virtues. Sleep, however, is to many the companion and fomenter of lust; the robber of a man's life; the image of death; the bringer of forgetfulness; the spouse of sloth. Immoderate sleep corrupts the stomach, injures the brain, and dissolves body and soul alike. A monk frequently given over to sleep spurns wisdom, prostitutes chastity, neglects the salvation of his soul, wastes precious time, slays the mind, nourishes vices.

Therefore whoever of you, most beloved brothers, desires to come to the rest of heavenly happiness, let him strongly repel from himself all sluggishness and laziness of mind; let him first learn to conquer himself, so that thereafter he may be able to overcome the devil, the enemy of his salvation. For unless you first [unclear] the evil that is in you, when will you overcome that which is external? Choose to rule over your passions, and, with the Lord's help, you will easily overcome all things that befall from without. Purge your mind of every disordered concupiscence, so that you may love Jesus alone in truth above all things, with whom you hope to rejoice [unclear]. No one, however, will reign with Christ in heaven who has not imitated him on earth with holy manners, as holy Scripture says: He who says that he abides in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked. For the Lord Jesus, who by his death freed us from the destruction of eternal damnation, was himself made willingly the mediator of God and [unclear]; he himself is the way by which the path of life is to be continued for us; he himself is the mighty guide of the journey; he himself is the norm and rule of the saving conduct of Christianity. No one comes to God except through Christ; no one will see the Father except through the Son. Therefore, if you wish to come to salvation, you must pass through the Savior. For he himself said in the holy Gospel: No one comes to the Father except through me. And that other saying: I am the door; if anyone enters through me, he will find the pasture of eternal life. And again: I am the way, the truth, and the life. Along this way, by living with pious and holy manners, I pray you, my brothers, always walk; this truth love fervently with a pure heart; to this life, which alone is lasting, hasten ardently. Let the teaching of Christ never depart from your hearts, but remain in memory at all times, since he refuses to become a true imitator of him who is not exercised day and night in meditation on his words. Therefore, always watchful in prayers, live with such purity of mind that, when he calls you in the hour of death, you may deserve with joy to hear Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with God the Father in unity through all ages of ages. Amen.

*On Patience and Meekness. Homily, xviii.*

Among all professors of the Christian faith there is today no kind of men for whom the virtue of patience seems to be more necessary than for the company of monks. For, as charity among many grows cold in these our days, to use Paul's language, we have been made a spectacle to mortals, so that wherever a monk comes among worldly men, he is marked out by the fingers of nearly all who see him, to be mocked and laughed at.
