*Homily IV. Fol. X.*

...bestowed upon the human race, when by reading through the whole wide extent of Scripture we call it back to memory with zealous devotion, they will furnish us the greatest matter and occasion by which, burning more deeply in the love of God, we may advance. For what else does the whole sequence of the divine Scriptures contain than a certain ordered fabric of good things which God has either already bestowed on human beings, or has promised that he will also give? Or what is there that can so strengthen us for the endurance of evils and render us willingly patient as if we read in the Gospel that the Son of God suffered innocently for us, and after him the victories of the holy martyrs?

For sacred Scripture contains the things that are necessary for us unto saving knowledge, and it warns us what, as soldiers in this pilgrimage, we ought profitably to seek, and what also to avoid. Hence again Saint Jerome says: "Our riches are to meditate on the law of God day and night." Let us therefore prepare strength for ourselves; let us take up perfect might, so that amid the darkness of errors and the confusion of night the light of Christ's knowledge may appear, and days and nights may have a protecting light, so that with Scripture as guide we may be able to come to the holy land.

For what is better than this instruction? What sweeter than this discipline? What more delightful than our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the true salvation and sweetness of faithful souls? Let Christ be our banquet; let sacred reading be our food and drink. For what can the life of a monk or priest be without knowledge of the Scriptures? We know Christ not by sight, not by hearing, not by touch, but by the reading of the Scriptures; and the more we advance by reading toward true knowledge, the more we take delight in the holy Scriptures.

For what is sweeter than to know the will of God and not to be ignorant of the prizes of his promises? For as Jerome says, the field of the divine word is exceedingly rich, having in itself every delight, and whatever you wish is born from it, just as they report of the manna in the desert, that when it was eaten it tasted in the mouth of the eater according to his will and desire.

Therefore, my brothers, read the divine Scriptures more often, through which you can plainly know the will of God and arm yourselves most strongly against the snares of the devil. For if, according to the Apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, he who does not know the Scriptures does not know the power and wisdom of God; and how will he know Christ? For ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ, and he who does not know Christ will not belong to his fellowship, as holy Christophilus says, because if anyone is ignorant, surely he will also be ignored.

Moreover, the more constant anyone has been in the study of the divine Scriptures, the greater understanding and sweetness he receives from their reading. But it especially befits you, brothers, to turn over the divine Scriptures daily by reading and learning, since to you both abundant time free from occupations is granted and a place sufficiently suitable because of the rarity of those who come by is offered. Therefore I exhort and admonish you, if you love your salvation, always to have divine readings in your hands, so that all the time which, after the worship of prayer, is allowed you without labor may be spent on the study of sacred Scripture.

Let no day pass, my brothers, on which the monk serving as Christ's soldier does not read something from the sacred volumes that may make him better in his holy purpose. Rather let him follow the counsel of Saint Jerome, who says: "Let the divine Scriptures always be in your hands and be continually turned over in the mind; nor should you think it enough to store the commandments of God in memory, unless you strive also to fulfill them in works."

For God requires two things of us, my brothers. First, he wishes us to know his most holy will; second, that we do what we have come to know. But we cannot know his commandments if we are ignorant of the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Nor does he do the will of God who is ignorant of it; nor will he know it who has not learned it. Learn, therefore, what is the pleasing will of God, so that you may do it; do what is known, so that you may understand more fully. For no one understands the will of God better than he who, once he has known it, does it out of love.

And so, if you desire to become a most learned interpreter of the divine Scriptures, learn to begin the study of knowledge not from words but in holy works. For then you will grasp the rich knowledge of sacred reading, if you have kept your heart clean from every sin and, fervent in the love of God, have continually turned over the holy Scriptures with compunction.
