*On the brotherly charity of monks.*

[Unclear] one is commanded to love in another, and to direct the regard of charity toward the soul rather than toward the body. For just as we are distinguished from brute animals by reason and understanding, so the gaze of our love should be referred to the mind, by which we understand, not to the body, which we have in common with beasts.

Far from holy monks be all affections of the flesh, for they know the rule of mutual and sincere charity. Let love toward one another be pure, upright, and conceived from fear of God alone, so that no advantage of any corruptible thing intervene, but the simple intention of charity remain.

But charity, simple and pure, [unclear] does not seek the byways of evil conspiracy; nor does it [unclear]. In giving to each his due, it does injury to no one, and whatever it does not wish to be done to itself, it never transfers to the person of another. Charity, giving to each one his due, does injury to no one, and whatever it does not wish to be done to itself, it never transfers to the person of another. Do not think that you have true brotherly charity when you love one another with a view to the flesh or to the advantage of some temporal thing; for where the Lord is not the cause of love, the affection of the one who loves is always faulty, either in part or in whole. But he rightly loves his brother who loves him with the love of God and on account of the common participation in the kingdom which we await.

Therefore, as Saint John the bishop says, we have been baptized into one body of the Church, so that we may not be dissolved, but in that concord and friendship which belong to one body we may preserve forever charity and mutual harmony with one another. Just as no one outside the unity of the Catholic Church can obtain salvation, so no one without brotherly charity is believed able to be saved.

Charity, then, is necessary for you before all things for salvation; without it, the remaining works of the virtues are utterly fruitless. For there can be nothing good, nothing of saving merit for life, which has not drawn its origin from it. Charity is a kind of short road to the virtues; if anyone enters upon it by the straight path of the journey, he will need no guidance of a leader.

Pure, simple, and upright charity is not accustomed to injure one’s neighbor, nor to inflict evil on anyone. Charity is accustomed to expend its affection [unclear] for all, and whatever it hopes will conduce to its own salvation, it desires this to be common to all people in Christ Jesus. Charity performs the office of love without any disturbance; and though it loves all people exceedingly, yet for no one is it provoked to bitter zeal. It is always solid, always constant, always stable and firm, because it is touched neither by passions nor agitated by movements of unrest.

It offers itself as common to all, and bestows its love on individuals in such a way that nevertheless it seems nearest to all. Charity, in those things which pertain to the reason of the mind and soul, does not neglect itself; but in those things which concern the advantage of the body, it often sets the causes of others before its own benefits. Great is the command of true charity, which compels a person to make another’s inconvenience his own.

Therefore you, my brothers, who have solemnly girded yourselves for Christ’s warfare, who through the arduous road of obedience long for eternal life: if you do not wish to run in vain in the arena of so laborious a contest, take care, we exhort you, above all things always to preserve brotherly charity inviolable within you. For unless you have sincere and perfect charity toward one another, you labor without fruit in regular observance.

Abstinence from eating meat will contribute nothing to you toward the salvation of souls; the renunciation of all private property will profit nothing toward the attainment of eternal happiness, if in your hearts [unclear] does not hold a place.

Therefore, desiring to be saved with Christ in truth, love one another; and as Christ loved you, so, we pray, love one another. Whatever each one of you desires to be done to himself, let him bestow that very thing upon his brother without envy, for the love of God, at all times.

Let divine love conquer in you all quarrels of the flesh and any contentions whatsoever; and let no destructive dissension of the flesh find a place where the fullness of brotherly charity ought continually to dwell. For if all of you in the monastery live according to the law of Christian instruction, you necessarily always abstain from injuring one another. But if through contentions and wrongs you wound one another mutually, you are certainly neither monks nor true Christians. For it befits all cloistered persons and monks to be outstanding in the observance of charity and to be far removed from the rancor of envy and hatred.
