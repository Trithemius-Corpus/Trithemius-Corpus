*Homily XIX.*

At all times, with all your strength and as much as you can, flee all pride and ostentation, since unless you greatly humble your souls, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven. Walk always before the Lord Jesus in the simplicity of your heart, having a pure conscience in all things, lest your humility become bestial; for it contributes nothing to salvation for a sinner to acknowledge himself publicly and, by persevering in evil, not to amend his fault. For the true servant of Christ sanctifies his humility by good works, and amends all the guilt of his past life by continual penitence. Daily he recalls to memory the evil that he remembers having committed, and groans deeply that he has offended the Lord. He walks very cautiously, and so laments past offenses that he by no means commits further things to be lamented. The monk who is truly humble follows the fasting Lord in a pure conscience, and fears nothing more in this world than to offend the teacher whom he imitates. Whatever he does and wherever he goes, he always thinks of the Lord Jesus as present, and by continual meditation examines how he may please him alone. For this is truly Christian humility, and humble Christianity: to [unclear] sin in true compunction, and to preserve the conscience in all purity at all times, not only from fear of punishments but rather from love of Christ. The true humility of monks continually does whatever good it can for the honor of God, and thinks that it will do nothing that it knows displeases Christ. True humility of monks [unclear] human [unclear], and, serving the Lord in the fervor of charity, is a companion of the holy angels, a friend to the citizens of heaven, and very formidable to demons. For demons fear and flee nothing more than the humility of the servants of Jesus Christ, because, since they themselves are most proud by their vicious will, they are most violently tormented by humble devotion. For where true humility is, demons cannot remain; for as ice is dissolved by fire, so malignant spirits are scattered from the presence of a humble monk. O humility, venerable mother and preserver of all virtues: yours is the kingdom, yours the power; you confer the kingdom upon the humble; you bestow the glory of eternal happiness upon the meek; without your guidance no one comes to heaven; whoever follows you does not fear hell. Whoever gathers works of virtues without you is like one who carries dust into the wind. You are the seasoning of all good works; for just as food in the mouth of the eater is not savory without salt, so without your companionship no one will be established as a friend of God. Humility made the Son of God man; humility will make the servant of Christ equal to a holy angel. An angel, through pride by his own choice, became the devil; and a Christian man through humility becomes an angel. Humility opens heaven to the meek servants of God, and pride plunges arrogant men down to hell. Humility makes friends of God, and pride makes companions of demons. Humility enlightens the mind to true wisdom, and pride blinds the understanding of the one whom it possesses. Humility purges the man whom it possesses from all disordered affections; and pride fills its worshipper with all crimes and filthiness. Humility makes its lover a servant of God; pride, however, makes him a slave of the devil. Humility makes one wise in the law of the Lord; pride, however, renders man foolish, away from the knowledge of the Most High. Humility brings the poor man into the fellowship of God; and pride makes the rich man a member of the devil. Humility is the mother and nurse of all virtues; and pride is the [source?] of all vices. Humility, with charity as mediator, covers a multitude of sins; pride, however, by itself introduces the fullness of all vices. Humility makes a monk’s good works most pleasing to God; pride, however, renders idle the endeavors of the proud before the Creator. Humility receives from the Lord whatever it has asked; but almighty God resists the prayers of the proud. Humility always has, in the Lord’s mercies, a pious confidence with cheerfulness of mind; pride, however, with the sting of despair, begets disturbance in man. Humility always has joy of spirit in the love of Christ; pride, however, is always occupied by pernicious fear. Humility, the [support?] of good works, neglects none of the spiritual exercises; pride, however, despises all pursuits of salvation. Humility justifies sinners; pride, however, condemns its followers. Humility makes one near to God; pride, however, makes one alien from the grace of God.
