*On continence and true chastity.*

Nothing is more beautiful than chastity: it makes clean what was conceived from unclean seed; from an enemy it also makes a member of the household, and from an earthly man an angel. Chastity is the fairest virtue of all virtues; when it is vowed to be kept for the love of Christ even unto death, it is rewarded with an inestimable prize. Chastity is next to the fellowship of angels, and the supreme medicine of the Christian mind. Chastity is the lovely dwelling of Jesus Christ, the eternal king, and the wondrous beginning of future incorruption. Chastity is the fullness of heavenly grace, through which every corruption of nature is restored. Chastity is the most powerful of all virtues; through it, in a fragile vessel, nature is overcome in a wondrous contest. Chastity imitates the angelic life, and in the midst of mud knows no sprinkling of stain. Blessed is the person who has truly deserved to receive from the Lord God the gift of chastity, and has guarded it inviolate in Christian humility unto death.

Chaste is he who, for the love of Jesus Christ, pure in mind and body, keeps himself from every defilement of the flesh; who burns sweetly in the love of God with the fervor of the Holy Spirit; who desires absolutely nothing in this world; who firmly restrains [unclear] and his senses.

Chaste is he who, mindful of the profession of spiritual warfare, suppresses the movements of the flesh by reason, and immediately from the beginning strongly extinguishes all the itching sparks of lust. Chaste is he who flees every material and occasion of lust; who has made a covenant with his eyes that he should not even think of a woman; and who sets a guard over his senses, lest any foul or immodest things be brought in. Chaste is he who avoids the company of the wanton, and always turns his eyes away lest he behold vain things. Chaste is he who detests the frequent company of all women, and who at all times regards his own frailty with fear and vigilance as suspect. Indeed, chaste is he who in mind and body imitates angelic nature, and who by holy conduct admits no corruption. Chastity is a gift of grace, not a work of nature or of human industry. Chastity makes the humble monk most pleasing to God, since one who lives in mortal flesh without corruption is a companion of the angels. Whoever has obtained true chastity is always humble; and whoever understands its nature does not arrogate possession of it to himself, but to the Lord who gave it. For no one is chaste by his own powers, since frail man is not sufficient to overcome nature unless he who created nature and all things has granted it. For whenever nature is overcome through chastity, God manifestly works together with man. But chastity itself has a difficult beginning in the young, until the movement of unlawful thought is brought into contempt. Chastity overcomes fornication by fleeing it rather than by fighting it, since lust is girded about with so many subtle weapons that it must be avoided of one’s own accord before it engages. Therefore whenever the mind is touched by movements of lust, divine help should be invoked without delay; no space for deliberation should be given to unclean thought, but as soon as it begins to be felt, it should be brought into contempt by the remedy of forgetfulness.

With a deceitful enemy, one who always uses ambushes, no conversations at all are to be entered into; and with unclean thoughts [unclear]; and unless you immediately suppress the movements of lust from the beginning, they are strengthened. The holy apostle of Christ, Paul, warns that one must fight against any vices, but fornication alone he commands to be fled.

We carry about against ourselves the material of [unclear]; therefore he wishes us to flee the enemy. Chastity is nourished by fasting; by gluttony it suffers shipwreck. Therefore, if you love chastity, love fasting, so that it may have necessary nourishment. He who is chaste follows the industry of the bee; and he who is truly a monk, as though adorned with all flowers, is beautified with every virtue. Not everyone who does not know lust is chaste, but he who, in pure and clean flesh, for the love of Christ, obtains [unclear] holiness of mind. Otherwise, what profit is the purity of the dwelling if its inhabitant is foul and unclean? Blind paganism once had its virgins too, sacrificing to Vesta; but because they did not offer the chastity of their bodies to the true God, nor hold right faith in mind, they descended with their demons, those whom they worshiped, into the depth of hell.

For chaste is he who, for love of the Creator, restrains the flesh from every lust, and offers his mind to God himself, adorned in every way with holy virtues. [unclear]
