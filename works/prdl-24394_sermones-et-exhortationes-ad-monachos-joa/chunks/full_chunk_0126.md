What does it mean that wrath remains upon him, except that the first, original sin is not removed from him? For after the institution of the New Testament, original sin is removed from those who believe in him through faith in Christ alone. Circumcision was given to the fathers as a sign of future salvation; nevertheless none of them could enter the kingdom of heaven before Christ the Savior rose from the dead. Therefore outside faith in Christ there is no salvation, since, as he himself said, he who has not believed has already been judged and will be condemned. Let us remain in Christ through faith and love, because he himself is the way of salvation; he is truth without error; he is life without death. If we have the Lord Jesus as the guide of our journey, we walk confidently.

Secondly, it is necessary for travelers to pass through the lands of their journey without injury to anyone, because if they commit either theft or robbery, or any other similar sin against the commonwealth, in the end they would come to be punished by penalty and would not reach the intended destination of their journey. But we, brothers, placed on the road by which we are making our way to the homeland of the heavenly kingdom, must take the greatest care that we commit nothing, in word, deed, or thought, contrary to the justice of God, but that we render to each one his own right and due: to God first of all piety, worship, and charity; to our neighbor love; to ourselves the holiness of perfect purity. For indeed, if we live otherwise than Christ taught, we do not remain on the straight road of salvation that leads to the kingdom. We shall remain on the road if we keep the commandments of God; we shall walk in truth if we love the Lord our God himself with the whole strength of the mind; we shall come to life if, turning away from evil, we continually persevere in the practice of good.

Thirdly, it is necessary to have provision for the journey, because if one reckons the journey within himself, he prepares travel-money and expenses according as necessity requires. A journey both great and arduous remains for us; we must prepare expenses for many years, lest we fail on this road by which we are going. The provision for this journey of ours to the supercelestial homeland is not gold, not silver, not the deceitful riches of the world, but faith in Christ and love of God. Holy virtues are unfailing provision for the soul toward the kingdom of heaven; strengthened by them, it walks the road of God in cheerfulness. The soul faithful to Christ and accompanied by holy virtues cheerfully completes the arduous road of this long pilgrimage. To love God above all things, to exercise oneself constantly in good works, to keep conscience in all purity, to do injury to no one: this provides true provision for the soul toward the everlasting homeland in heaven.

Fourthly, it is considered necessary for travelers that, before they arrive at the end of the journey they have begun, they not forget their purpose. For he must be called a foolish traveler who is called back from his journey by the games of children and forgets where he was tending to go. And we, dearest brothers, who have taken up the road of monastic religion, must always think of our end, because every day death hastens on, even when we do not notice, and presents both the unprepared and the prepared alike for judgment. Meanwhile let us do good while we may, as much as we can, and let us not delay to be converted to the Lord with our whole heart. Christ is the way through which we go to the kingdom of God; Christ is the truth in which, remaining, we do not err; Christ is the life for which we hope. Hear, brothers, what he himself says in the holy Gospel: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. This is the way that you have entered from the beginning of your monastic profession; if you wish to proceed along it rightly and laudably, you will have to love Christ above all things, living purely in observance of the Rule. It is yours, brothers, to love nothing in this world and to chastise the flesh for love of God. It belongs to monks to despise all delights and pleasures of the present life and to desire nothing except the everlasting feasts. The faithful minister of Christ follows his Lord, and where Christ is, there also his minister will be. Therefore whoever among you, as a lover of his own salvation, desires to come after Christ to the kingdom of heaven, whoever wishes to guard and preserve to the end those things that he promised on entering religion, let him keep these things also.
