For we know, as the holy Apostle says, that the flesh lusts against the spirit, and unless by the spirit we have mortified the works of the flesh, we shall die in its corruption. Nothing hurls the soul more deeply into the pit of sin than the insolence of the flesh, which, when it is permitted to live by its own will, struggles against its lord like a rebellious servant. Unhappy flesh, you who by the desire of vanity hurl down the soul: it will not pass unpunished for you if, at your prompting, it commits any evil, since God will judge both. Woe to you when it has sinned with you, because when Adam, as a transgressor of the commandments, is expelled from paradise, Eve, the instigator of evils, necessarily accompanies the wretched man.

Be in agreement with the spirit, O flesh, because the Apostle says: “Those who are Christ’s have crucified their flesh with its vices and desires.” And you, O men and brothers, who profess the warfare of Christ, who have voluntarily sworn yourselves to the law of monastic life, who have promised to imitate the footsteps of the Savior: attend to your calling, so that you may walk cautiously, not as the unwise who are driven by every wind, but as true lovers of Jesus Christ, who by the spirit have mortified the deeds of the flesh. You, I say, my most beloved fellow-soldiers, be always watchful in good works, mortifying for the love of Christ our bodies which are upon the earth, constant in fasts and prayers, and continually occupied in meditation on the divine Scriptures. Always stretch yourselves toward the things ahead; daily renew the fervor of your holy purpose with fresh zeal. Flee idleness and all sloth, and [unclear] diligently how brief the time of this life is; and the devil, however fiercely he may rage, is overcome as soon as you have invoked Christ.

Remember the words of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, saying in the holy Gospel: “Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.” And what is our cross, my brothers, if not a life full of temptations and adversities, and the burden of regular discipline under which we are wearied day and night? But let us gather our hearts, dearest brothers, and gladly endure for the love of God whatever he has wished us to endure, so that at his judgment we may deserve to say: “We have passed through fire and water, and you have brought us into refreshment.” O how glorious a reward is laid up in the kingdom of heaven for the servants of God, who, contending now in this brief racecourse, are wearied for love of him. Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, the immensity of that good which almighty God has prepared in eternal happiness for those who love him, to remain without end forever. The highest happiness of this good is acquired by humility; it is possessed by purity of heart and by the fervor of divine love.

Let us hasten there, dearest brothers; let us run there with as great devotion of mind as we can, where the spirit is joined to its creator through the enjoyment of sweetness, where the intellect will be perfected in the knowledge of the highest good, which is God. There is the joy of everlasting happiness, which is not diminished; there is gladness which is not brought to an end; there is the perpetual rest from all labors; there is the glorious reward for tribulations and injuries suffered for Christ. Whatever good we now do, there without doubt we shall find laid up; whatever adversity we have patiently endured for the love of Christ, there we shall receive it repaid with a most abundant reward.

O monk, monk, you who spend fruitlessly the time mercifully granted to you by the Lord God for doing good: what will you reap, or what reward will you receive in that terrible hour, you who are now so sluggish and unwilling to sow? Have you not read what is written about the labors of the saints? “Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming, they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves.” Now is the time to work the field of your heart, O monk; now is the hour to sow good seed with the blessing of cheerfulness, because he who now sows sparingly will also reap sparingly in that repayment of future reward. Now one must labor and never cease from good work, since whatever we have done here now, there without doubt we shall receive. Therefore, if there is any faithful monk among you, if anyone is a zealous lover of his own salvation, let him always think upon the hour of future reward, which no one can escape; let him always prepare himself to render the account of his stewardship. Let no day ever pass on which he has not done some particular good work which he may send ahead for future recompense.
