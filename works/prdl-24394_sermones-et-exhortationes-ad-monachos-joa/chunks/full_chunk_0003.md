*Letter*

Johannes Trithemius, called the Sponheimite, to his reverend father and teacher, lord Johannes of Colenhausen, abbot of the monastery of the holy martyrs Marcellinus and Peter in Seligenstadt, but formerly my predecessor at Sponheim: reverence and honor.

I do not know, my ever-venerable teacher, under what unlucky star that monastery of Sponheim was built, since an abbot rarely dies in it, but almost all of them, overcome by weariness, as I find from the annals, have for nearly seventy years now resigned of their own free will. We are not wholly ignorant why you too, following the example of those before you, deserted us poor men before the time; we rightly blame both our poverty and the rebellious rashness of certain persons. I now experience, alas, the cause that moved your mind to leave the Sponheim abbacy, wholly barren and unfruitful, over which you presided for thirteen years from the reform, not without continual anxiety of mind and infinite labors. I pass over the wrongs of very many of the brothers which you endured; all these are indeed daily doubled upon me.

You ordered me to send to your fatherliness the book of my exhortations to the brothers, which I recently composed by lamplight amid the raging occupations of worldly affairs. Although it is unworthy of your reading, nevertheless I have been unwilling to resist a parent's command. Therefore I beg your gentleness graciously to apply the file of correction: if anything is lacking, add it; cut away what is superfluous; and either commit what is inept to the flames or certainly set it in order. Farewell, sweetest father, and remember me. From Sponheim, the sixth day of the month of August, in the year of the Christians 1386.
