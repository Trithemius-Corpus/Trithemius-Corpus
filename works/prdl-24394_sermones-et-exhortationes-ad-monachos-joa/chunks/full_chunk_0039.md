You have heard, then, the fearful sentence of the Lord, and you are not ignorant of the precept of our holy lawgiver, who says in the Rule: "Buffooneries, idle words, and words that provoke laughter we condemn everywhere to perpetual enclosure, and for such speech we do not permit a disciple to open his mouth." Here the holy father Benedict forbade three kinds of words to monks, and condemned them everywhere to perpetual enclosure: namely buffooneries, idle words, and words that provoke laughter.

But what buffoonery is, interpretation by the grammarian and by us has made clear. For the vice of buffoonery is opposed to urbanity and modesty, and is a kind of wanton jesting, immoderately provoking the laughter of hearers without shame or honesty, by words and gestures that do not accord with reason, do not befit a good man, and are not suitable to a wise man. It is called buffoonery from the buffoon, who follows the houses of the rich, provoking hearers to laughter with wanton and dishonest words and gestures for the sake of reward or alms. And this kind of words the holy father Benedict forbade to monks and utterly condemned, since those who serve God are rightly judged bound always to abstain in his sight from foul and dishonest words.

Between buffoonery and excessive speech this seems to be the difference: the former exceeds reason by reason of dishonesty, the latter by reason of superfluity. The former is foul and dishonest, and superfluous without virtue. Foolish talk is rightly joined to these, since it is forbidden not only to monks but also to all Christians. Hence the holy apostle of Christ says: "But fornication and all uncleanness or covetousness, let it not even be named among you, as befits saints of God; nor filthiness, nor foolish talk, nor buffoonery, which is not to the purpose, but rather thanksgiving." Idle speech is likewise forbidden to all monks, since, as you have heard, it is prohibited by the Lord in the holy Gospel. Words that provoke laughter do not befit Christian men, and much less are they suitable for cloistered men. For the monk, who is dead to the world, is commanded always to think on his last things; but laughter and every insolence of the present life he is ordered everywhere to contemn for the love of God. From his words a man will be known; he who multiplies utterances in his mouth is foolish in heart. A man's speech is the witness of his conscience: he who loves chastity and innocence of heart, his speeches ought to be chaste and without vanity. For you are ministers of God, brothers of mine; let no vain, useless, or idle speech go out from your mouth, but only that which is good for the edification of hearers, so that it may increase charity in their minds for the illumination of the heart in the knowledge and love of the highest truth. Precious is the tongue of the monk that says nothing at all except holy things; worthy of blessing is the mouth from which nothing is brought forth except what is divine.

Therefore let each one guard his heart from every wicked thought, since he who is good in heart does not speak an evil word; he who truly loves God does not utter an idle speech. Apply every care and consideration to your thoughts, because there evil must be watched with chief attention, where it takes the beginning of its own processes, so that as soon as it begins to arise it may be cut off at the root. For there is no evil thought so weak that it does not grow with time, if it is nourished by the consent of the thinker. Therefore evil must be conquered at the beginning, while it is still weak, because when delay is interposed, evil gains strength, however small it may seem at first in the heart. A great fire often springs from a small spark; so from a tiny thought a great sin sometimes arises. When a thought gives birth to delight, it is accustomed soon to break out into consent, and consequently even into deed, if it can cast itself headlong.

But when the will has granted consent through delight to an evil thought for the deed, even if what the soul desires is not brought to completion, nevertheless the sin has already been consummated before God. But what does Scripture say? "Sin, when it has been consummated, begets death." For almighty God looks upon the will of man, whether he wills good or evil. Therefore in the holy Gospel he says: "Whoever has looked at a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart."
