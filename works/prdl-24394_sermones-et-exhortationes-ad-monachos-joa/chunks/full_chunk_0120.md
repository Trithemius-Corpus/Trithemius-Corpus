For I say what experienced men attest to be true: no prayers are more effective for obtaining what is sought than those which they offer to God the Father with remembrance of the Lord’s Passion and with gratitude.

The fourth thing which we have indicated to you as necessary for regular observance is this: that you keep watch in guarding the outer senses, because no one can adequately guard the city of his own heart who neglects to close the gates. Love silence, and flee all the tumult of this world. Apply holy prudence in all things that you do, and love retirement wherever you are. For a monk who loves publicity and wandering excursions was never a true lover of observance.

Fifth, we admonish you to flee the pernicious idleness of the soul; for this pestiferous [unclear] nourishes grave vices and always gives birth to unclean thoughts. Therefore whoever is a lover of virtues and loves his own salvation, whoever desires to come to eternal happiness, let him flee idleness as from the face of the devil, if he desires to avoid sin.

Sixth, for the purity of regular observance, by necessity of salvation, it is required that the renunciation of private property be kept inviolate by all until death. For a monk who has private property prepares for himself a road to hell. But the one who receives and spends money in the monastery should be a public minister; let no private person dare presume to do this. Possession of riches does not befit monks, since they are commanded to imitate Jesus in poverty of spirit. Let us hear the holy Apostle of Christ saying: Having food and clothing, with these let us be content. By God’s mercy we provide you with necessary things: not as much as the disordered desire of pleasure demands, but what nature, content with few things, requires. And so, just as it is not lawful for you to have anything without the abbot’s consent, so, since we supply everything, it is not necessary either.

The seventh thing that makes a monk is humble obedience to preceptors. No one can be a true monk who scorns obedience and denies to his superiors the humble subjection which he promised. This disease of disobedience arises from elation and pride of mind, just as pus is generated from a bad ulcer. But, as Scripture says: pride is hateful before God and men, and the beginning of every transgression. The delight of monks is the imitation of Christ, who obeyed the Father even unto death.

The most beautiful virtue, chastity, adorns the eighth place; when it is scorned, all the integrity of regular life is necessarily shipwrecked. For a monk, by necessity of his salvation, is commanded to live chastely until death and perpetually to abstain from every venereal pollution. There are three principal vows of monastic life; for monks to live contrary to their observance is certain damnation. For the profession of monks, under sentence of eternal damnation, commands them to live without property, under obedience to the pastor, and in perpetual chastity. When one of these three is taken away, monastic integrity is violated, and transgression condemns the contemner.

In the ninth place, therefore, we admonish every person dedicated to God always to beware of so great a venomous serpent: his own will. Unless he holds it suspect at every time, he easily falls into the pit of damnation. Attend to the Rule: for monks are not permitted to have either their bodies or their wills in their own power. No one sins unwillingly. If you have perfectly submitted your will to God and to your abbot in all things, you will easily avoid sin.

The tenth thing which can preserve in you the integrity of regular observance, my dearest brothers, is that in all adversities and contradictions you have most steadfast patience for the love of God. For patience is a great virtue, which by another name we call endurance; without it no one will come away victor over his temptations. Therefore the Lord says in the holy Gospel: In your patience you shall possess your souls. What is patience, if not to bear calmly one’s own injuries and those of one’s people, and all other adversities whatsoever, for reverence and love of God? Believe me, brothers, [and] the saying of the Apostle when he says: Patience is very necessary for you, so that, doing the will of God, you may receive [the promise].
