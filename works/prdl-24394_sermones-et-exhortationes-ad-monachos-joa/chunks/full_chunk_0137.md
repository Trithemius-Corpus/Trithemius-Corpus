*The indication of the eighth good course.* And then we receive the true sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, when we humbly conform our wills in all things to the divine ordinances. This spiritual degree, when perfected, separates the monk from all the concerns of the world and calls him wholly to inward things. For the will, pleasingly subjected by affection to the divine will, separates him far from all desires that war against the spirit. Whoever has received this gift of grace of the Holy Spirit should first give thanks with humility, and should not vainly exalt himself on account of the gift; but rather, the more he has advanced, the more he should learn to humble himself in all things. Often the lofty man deserves to lose the grace of God, because he neglects to preserve it in the [unclear] of true humility. Therefore almighty God sometimes withdraws from those making progress the grace of sensible devotion for a short time, so that humility may increase in the mind, and the fervor of desire may burn more ardently to seek more. But since there are many causes among men for which God withdraws his grace from anyone, it will be worthwhile for them to be set out in order. First, then, God withdraws grace from a man because of sin, which stains the conscience with the foulness of vice. For our God, as Scripture says, is jealous and wishes to be loved alone; he does not love with any partner. Therefore whenever the mind, by desiring inordinately, turns back either upon itself or upon something else, alienated from its own beginning, which is God, it removes itself; whence it deserves to lose the grace which it had, because, when present, it would not guard the conscience inviolate. Hence the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, exhorts us not to receive the grace of God in vain, which indeed is then received fruitlessly and in vain when the mind of the recipient is made empty by the filth of vices. The little vessel into which we receive the grace of God is our conscience; when it is purified from vices and adorned with holy virtues, it is made worthy of the dwelling of the Holy Spirit.
