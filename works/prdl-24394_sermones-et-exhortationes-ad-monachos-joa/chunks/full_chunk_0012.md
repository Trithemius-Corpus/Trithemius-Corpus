*Homily III.*

VII

... lest the crown be overcome, he abstains from everything, so that naked and free he may run the more swiftly. Therefore, if you desire to walk the way of God in true purity, if you are concerned to conquer the enemy in the contest of struggle, put off the garments of earthly cares and all the hairs of fleshly pleasures. For whoever has learned to run naked, with hair and clothing cast away from himself, carries around nothing by which he can be held by the adversary except the unencumbered body. For he who runs naked has stripped himself of every anxiety over things that clamor around him, and thinks of nothing other than victory; nor does he care what meanwhile is happening with wife or children. Nothing present recalls him from his course; the swiftness of his companions does not frighten him; among many he competes as though alone; he hastens breathless toward the prize as though certain. Imitate him, soldier of Christ, in the spiritual contest; contemplate the naked Savior hanging naked on the cross, so that you may be able to overthrow the adversary so much the more easily, the less he sees you to possess of the vanity of the world. Therefore fight naked, light, and unencumbered on every side; naked and released from all things, fly into heaven. Naked you came into this world; naked you are to return to the earth. Considering these things earnestly, my brothers, so run that you may lay hold of eternal life with all the saints. Amen. *The homily ends.*

... and take up the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, praying at every time in the Spirit with every prayer and supplication, and in it keeping watch with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints. To the Thessalonians likewise he says: But we, who are of the day, let us be sober, having put on the breastplate of faith and charity, and for a helmet the hope of salvation. These are the arms, brothers, with which the soldiers of Christ, clothed, war against the fierce adversary; these should be the badges of the virtues of monks, without which no one obtains victory over vices. The equipment of human warfare, which most mortals pursue far too zealously, consists of helmet, shield, breastplate, dagger, cuirass, belt, lance, and many other things of that kind, with which men about to go out to war arm themselves most carefully; and unless they have been most diligently defended over the whole body, they judge that they are not certainly fit for battle. But the arms of our spiritual warfare are faith, hope, charity, patience, humility, obedience, chastity, together with the holy virtues of the soul enumerated by our holy teacher Benedict in the Rule, under the fourth chapter, where he makes fine mention of the instruments of good works. But would, my brothers, that we were as careful and diligent for these spiritual arms of our warfare as we see worldly men to be solicitous for their corruptible armor. But now, what I say with too much grief, since we shamefully cast away the arms of salvation with which we ought to have been armed, what else remains except that we succumb to our enemy in the contest? But perhaps you will say: If the Christian faith, as the apostle bears witness, belongs to the armor of spiritual warfare, we certainly have never cast it away. For we firmly believe whatever holy mother Church has set before us to be believed, and in that profession of faith we intend steadfastly to remain until death. But so be it. What, however, does Saint James say in the epistle? What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith but does not have works? Can faith save him? Just as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead. Therefore, whenever Scripture speaks of faith, [unclear] not common and [unclear] ... feet in preparation for the gospel of peace. In all things taking up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the fiery darts of the wicked one.
