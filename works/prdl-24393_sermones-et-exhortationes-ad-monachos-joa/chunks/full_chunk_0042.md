A man is known from his words, and he who multiplies utterances in his mouth is foolish in heart. A man’s speech is the witness of his conscience, and for one who loves modesty and innocence of heart, his words ought to be modest and without vanity. For you are ministers of the living God, my brothers: let no vain, useless, and idle word go out from your mouth, but only what is good for the edification of the hearers, so that it may increase charity in their minds, toward the illumination of the heart in the knowledge and love of the highest truth. Precious is the tongue of the monk that speaks nothing at all except what is holy, and worthy of blessing is the mouth from which nothing is brought forth except what is divine. Let each one keep his heart clean from every wicked thought, since he who is good in heart does not speak an evil word, and he who truly loves God does not utter idle speech. Apply every care and consideration to your thoughts, since evil must be watched there with principal attention, where it takes the beginning of its proceedings, so that as soon as it has begun to arise it may be cut off by the roots. For there is no thought of evil so weak that it does not grow with time, if it is nourished by the consent of the thinker. Therefore evil, while still weak, must be conquered immediately at the beginning, because when delay intervenes, even a very small evil gains strength, though in the beginning it may seem [unclear]. A great fire often arises from a small spark, and from a lesser thought a great sin sometimes rises up. When thought gives birth to delight, it is accustomed soon to burst into consent, and consequently into deed also, if it can cast itself headlong. But when the will, through delight, has granted consent to an evil thought for the deed, even if what the soul desires is not completed, nevertheless the sin has already been consummated before God. But what does Scripture say? Sin, when it has been consummated, brings forth death. For almighty God regards the will of man, whether he wills good or evil. Therefore he says in the holy Gospel: Whoever has looked at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart. God reckons the will as the deed whenever a person cannot bring into effect what he wished. Therefore again and again I admonish you, brothers, and with as much diligence as I can I solemnly urge you, that according to the holy Apostle you walk cautiously, not as fools, redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Watch most cautiously for the snares of the evil demon, and resist evil and unclean thoughts immediately from the beginning, because remedy is considered far too late when the disease has gained strength through long time. Accustom yourselves at all times to dwell upon good and holy thoughts, and you will easily be able to overcome the insolence of evil ones. For just as vessels filled with good liquids receive no external corruption, so minds filled with good thoughts do not admit the intemperance of evil ones. Since the eye of the heart is that by which God is seen, and through purity of conscience one arrives at the glory of eternal happiness. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God. A clean heart is the dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit, and where evil thought is not admitted, God without doubt abides. A clean heart is the seat of wisdom, and where purity of mind is guarded, no perils of adversity will harm. Be mindful of Scripture saying: Perverse thoughts separate from God, but tested virtue corrects the foolish. For wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor will it dwell in a body subject to sins. For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the deceitful, and will withdraw himself from thoughts that are without understanding. But a perverse thought is any thought admitted that is contrary to evangelical purity; by commerce with it the mind of the thinker is defiled, whenever by thinking about and turning over those things that are opposed to God it takes delight in them. And those perverse thoughts separate the mind of the thinker from God and little by little cast it down into the depth of evils. For an evil and perverse thought, when it has penetrated, first poisons through the venom of delight; then it clouds reason; afterward it draws the will into consenting to the evil suggestion; finally it consummates sin in deed. From then on, an evil once presumed is practiced more freely, and all shame or fear is removed through habit. Sin is joined to sin, and little by little the habit of doing evil is strengthened.
