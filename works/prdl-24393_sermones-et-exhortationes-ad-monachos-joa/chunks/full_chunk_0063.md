For thus the canon admonishes you; thus the monastic institution commands; thus reason necessary for salvation advises. You are commanded to be content with every lowliness and extremity, and always to be [ready?] for all things. But I see very many of you perverting the rule of salvation and living in the monastery otherwise than one ought. Monks now do not fear to seek delights in the monasteries, they who came from the world to conversion poor and beggarly. For the things they could not have outside, now, if they are lacking in the monastery, as though they had suffered the gravest insult, they rebel against humility. Woe to the monks whom not the love of God but want made cloistered, whose god is the belly, and who think of nothing less than poverty. It is not for you, O monks, henceforth to seek in the monastery the delights which the rule commands to be wholly renounced. It befits you to spurn all the pleasures of the present life and to love absolutely nothing in this world except God. For your whole way of life ought always to be given over to divine services, since you promised to live by the spirit and not according to the flesh. No one who is a true monk lives without penance, but whoever truly longs for the feasts of eternal happiness daily chastens his body through abstinence. For it cannot be that a monk enjoy the delights of the present life and the eternal ones; but if he longs for those, he must despise these. For the monk who professes the cenobitic canon is commanded to be a disciple of Christ the Savior; if he desires to please him in truth, he must become his imitator. Thus he says to him in the holy Gospel: He who wishes to be my disciple, let him follow me. And what it is to follow me, John the Apostle, knowing this, teaches in a certain epistle, saying: He who says that he remains in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked. And the Lord himself again says in the Gospel: He who wishes to come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. He who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me. And he who loves son or daughter above [unclear] is not worthy of me. Your master has not spoken obscurely, O monk, nor wrapped his meaning in riddles, but has set forth plain speech, by which you can pretend no excuse. For Christ himself is the example and rule of a good and perfect way of life for every Christian, and especially for the monk; if the cloistered man does not follow him with his whole heart in [unclear], he glories in vain in the profession of faith. For just as a Christian is named from Christ, so a disciple is named from imitation of the master. Therefore imitate the master in your conduct, so that you may prove yourself a true disciple of Christ. For the Lord Jesus Christ, whose discipline you profess, O monk, though he was rich, became poor for us; but you, though you are lowly and needy, seek abundance of all things in the school of poverty, and although the teacher commands everything to be despised for love of him, again you desire to possess everything in the monastery. The Lord and your master was obedient to the Father for us even unto the death of the cross; but you, full of pride and impatience, either refuse to obey your superiors for love of him, or you obey unwillingly and with indignation. He, for your salvation, not only endured most patiently throughout his whole life the words and insults of wicked men, but also, for love of you, bore blows and livid wounds and dreadful gashes. Nor did he refuse to undergo the most shameful death, in order to call back your soul from the error of death to the life of eternal light. From the day of his birth he lived in the world poor and humble, and, [unclear], filled with tribulations and reproaches, so that by teaching and benefits he might restore you, a most ruined man, to knowledge and love. For what would we be, if the Son of God had not appeared as man, except utterly ruined? And what are we, if we do not imitate the master according to our strength, except ungrateful? Woe to monks, ungrateful mortals, who contemptuously refuse to follow our Lord and Savior in humility. Let us examine our way of life, brothers, and compare the disciples with the master whose name has been invoked over us, and we shall plainly understand how far we have withdrawn from the paths of our most kindly teacher. But let us return [unclear] with our whole heart, I pray, and do penance, because we have withdrawn far from the way of salvation, serving the flesh rather than the spirit.
