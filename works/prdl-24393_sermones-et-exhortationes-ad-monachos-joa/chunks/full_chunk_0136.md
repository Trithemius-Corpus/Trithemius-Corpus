*Sermon V.*

Sixthly, it draws the mind powerfully back from sin if, by habit, it persists always occupied in meditation on the Lord’s Passion. Who will be able to devote his works to the pleasures of the flesh, if he has reflected that Jesus Christ, the ruler of the world, hung for him on the cross? The death of Christ freed the world from eternal death; whoever carries this benefit with him in memory will easily abstain from every wickedness of vices. Finally, if anyone with the whole attention of his mind has considered God present to him in every place, knowing and understanding all things, he will without doubt always abhor every sin. For God is everywhere with us, not only present, but truly most inward and most present; no secret escapes him, nothing remains hidden from him, but all lies plainly manifest to him. He searches the hearts of all; he knows the most secret thoughts, since he alone has true knowledge of all things. Who among mortals would dare, without the greatest shame, in the presence of so great a majesty, which he believes sees all things, if he weighs who and how great he is who sees? Therefore, whenever some wicked thought troubles the mind, whenever the disordered motions of the flesh disturb the soul, think of the Lord God, who is the avenger of evils, as present; recall to memory the bitterness of the Passion of Jesus Christ; devoutly implore the aid of heavenly grace. He is harder than stone whom these things, diligently considered, have not been able to recall from the purpose of sinning.

The [seventh] thing that recalls the mind of the studious monk from the desire of sinning is that by continual reading he should run through the passions of the holy martyrs. For indeed, whoever often recalls to memory the unconquerable constancy of the holy martyrs of Christ cannot fail to become stronger and more steadfast in resisting sins. For this reason the brave deeds of the saints were committed to writing: so that whenever we, weak as we are, read such things, we may rise up to imitate those who went before. Do not look, brothers, to those who with proud rashness deny credence to the histories of the saints, and think that the glorious things narrated about the most blessed friends of Christ are fables. For the virtues and good qualities which they do not find in themselves, they scorn to believe that the saints of God possessed. For there are many, and especially among them those who think themselves great because of their learning, who very rashly reject the histories of the holy fathers; and since they themselves cannot enter upon the arduous path of virtue, they invent with windy loquacity that no one, or only a few, could have done so. Whatever is read before them concerning the lives of the saints, whether lofty or great, they cry out is invented and fabulous; and, such as they themselves live, placed in the darkness of vices, so they judge those whose histories they mock to have gone before. But the prudent and devout monk will not be shaken by the sloth of the proud, since he well understands that an effective power of exhorting is present in the histories of the saints. For, to confess meanwhile what I feel about myself, I am moved more toward the devotion of inward fervor by the life of one saint than by many curious sermons, because, as the most holy pope Leo says, examples are stronger than words, and it is more to teach by deed than by voice. But just as not all things please all people, so not all things suit all people; we advise each person to read more frequently and more studiously that by the reading of which his mind is stirred to greater perfection.

The eighth thing that most powerfully draws a man back from sin is charity, concerning which the holy apostle says: Charity is patient, is kind; it does not act perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, does not seek the things that are its own, is not provoked, does not think evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth; it bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Charity never falls away. He who loves God flees sin; but he who is a servant of crimes assuredly does not love God. A lover of God does not commit sin; and he who truly loves his Lord and always beholds him as present does not work iniquity. God is the highest and unchangeable good; for love of him sin must both be fled and detested, since he who fears to sin only from fear of punishments does not ascend to the heights of charity. For a slave does not sin through fear of the lash; but a son withdraws from evil out of love for his father, lest he offend his father’s heart. Let us avoid sin, my brothers, and live in the sight of the Lord with as much purity of soul as we can, since just as deadly poison, once taken, kills the body, so sin kills the soul of the offender. Hence the wise man admonishes us in Ecclesiasticus, saying: Flee sins as from the face of a serpent, because if you come near to them, they will receive you.
