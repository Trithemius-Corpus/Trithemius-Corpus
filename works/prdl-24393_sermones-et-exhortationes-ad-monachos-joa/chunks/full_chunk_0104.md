Charity judges no one rashly, but humbly corrects erring neighbors with the gentlest admonitions. Charity is terrible and fearsome to the reprobate, but tender and kindly to the meek. Charity reckons another’s misfortune as its own, and weeps over a brother’s tribulation as though it suffered it itself. Charity suffers with all who are placed in necessity, and is no less saddened by others’ adversities than by its own. Charity imputes nothing evil first, but for the love of God always bears the infirmities of all with equanimity. Charity is diligent in good works, and as long as it remains in the heart it is never idle. Charity neglects none of the things to which it is bound, but guards all things most diligently. Brotherly charity is the fulfillment of the law, since whatever is commanded after the worship of God is referred to love of neighbor. Hence the holy love of Christ says: He who loves his neighbor has fulfilled the laws. For, You shall not commit adultery, you shall not kill, you shall not steal, you shall not bear false witness, you shall not covet another’s property; and if there is any other commandment, it is renewed in this word: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. Love of neighbor does not work evil. Therefore the fullness of the law is love.

And so, my dearest brothers, we beseech you through our Lord and Savior Christ, who is to judge the living and the dead, that you always keep brotherly charity unharmed, without which you can neither truly love God nor in any other way fulfill his commandment. The keeping of brotherly charity is as necessary for your salvation as the nourishment of food and drink is for preserving the body’s life. For the body dies without necessary nourishment, and all the virtues of the soul are scattered without brotherly charity. For brotherly charity preserves a multitude of monks in unity of spirit; for without the bond of mutual love, no cloistered man perseveres content without disturbance. The monk who persecutes his brother with hatred is far removed from God’s charity. For the holy evangelist John says: He who hates his brother is in darkness and walks in darkness, and does not know where he is going, because darkness has blinded his eyes. But the chaste and prudent lover of his neighbor walks safely in the light of the Holy Spirit, and he is not disturbed by another, nor does he disturb anyone else. For true love of neighbor is gentle, sweet, and most tender, and in all its intercourse meek, peaceable, and tranquil.

Yet the vigor of discipline directs the meekness of a chaste ruler, and the clemency of brotherly consideration beautifully tempers vigor. For one must be struck from the other in such a way that vigor is not rigid, nor love slack. The lover of brotherly charity does not abandon the way of justice; he who rightly loves his neighbor introduces no corruption. For true first love neither covets gold nor requires the services of ministry, but, content with all things that could overthrow the condition of the mind, pursues only the increase of the soul. For charity is not sincere when it seeks gifts because of corrupt morals, or [unclear] a boy because of a little gift, so as to love with an affection of likeness one who runs together for distributions. The friendship of monks among good men who are zealous for the same purpose ought to be common, and far removed from every affection of the flesh. Let it be pure, simple, upright, and durable without injury to anyone, and let it have in itself nothing of suspicious conspiracy.

This is not the rule of brotherly charity: to seek forbidden private gatherings for consolation, and without the prior’s blessing to be occupied with furtive, indeed prohibited, conversations. You do not rightly and [unclear] hinder love of your neighbor whenever you come together for the comforts of the flesh against the rule of regular observance. A monk’s love ought to be solid and very far removed from every transgression of regular discipline, since unless charity has been much [unclear] by the signs of carnal conversation, it does not have within itself the mark of divine love. Now examine your conversations, brothers, to see whether you have them rooted in your heart even in true charity. For charity that either gives or receives consolations harmful to the spirit was never sincere. Therefore take care to direct the eye of the mind not toward the beauties of the flesh, but rather toward contemplation of the soul, if you wish rightly and laudably to love your neighbors mutually. For when the flesh is loved without regard for the soul, not sincere but carnal and impure love is generated. Consider that your brother has a soul like yours, and always think that you both equally await happiness. [unclear] without the soul we understand; in soul, not in body, we are immortal.
