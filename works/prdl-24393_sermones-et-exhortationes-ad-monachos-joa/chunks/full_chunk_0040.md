The holy fear of the Lord never deserts the mind of a wise man; but, just as a strong and ever-watchful champion always stands at the prince’s side, so it guards the [house?] of the heart. He turns his ears away from all scurrility of words, and closes his eyes lest they see evil. He always walks according to the twelfth degree of humility, with his gaze fixed upon the ground, praying to the Lord with the prophet: “Turn my eyes away, lest they see vanity; in your way give me life, O Lord.”

Go, I admonish you, brothers: direct your senses in the fear of the Lord, so that you may always be able to keep the dwelling of the heart clean and inviolate. Remember the Scripture, which says: “Be clean, you who carry the vessels of the Lord.” Consider, I pray, with how great a cleanliness and purity of mind you ought to shine, you who not only, by reason of the priestly order, carry the vessels of the Lord, but, what is more, are even yourselves the vessels of the Lord. For the holy lover of Christ, Paul, says: “Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ?” And again he says: “Or do you not know that your members are the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own?” And elsewhere: “For the temple of God is holy, which you are.” And concerning that same Paul, in the Acts of the Apostles, the Lord said to Ananias: “Go, for this man is a vessel of election for me.”

The monk who performs priestly office in the Church of God is bound by a double reason to the observance of most holy purity. For the soldier of Christ, because of the vow which he made to God, must be pure, holy, and continually tireless in good works; and by reason of the priestly ministry, circumspect and most diligently watchful. For it is written: “Cursed is the man who does the work of God negligently.” You are ministers of almighty God, who daily sacrifice his Son at the altar; his work is entrusted into your hands, and therefore great purity of mind and body is demanded in your manner of life.

Therefore, in order that the chaste purity of the mind may remain, the instability of thought must be restrained. The senses must be guarded at all times, for through them the integrity of the mind is corrupted. For you are dead to the world and have now begun to live for Christ; it is not lawful for you to think anything except what is holy, nor is it fitting for you, ministers of God, to love anything earthly. For it is God whom you serve, O monks; it is the Almighty whom you minister to, my brothers. Live pure, holy, and innocent, because it is not fitting for you to be occupied with the business of the world, you who have been called to the soldiery of God.

Let love of God and purity of heart grow in you with time, so that the longer you live in this world, the holier you may become. Always turn yourselves away from every evil, so that you may be able to persevere without failing in good thoughts. Always raise the eyes of your mind to heaven, and do not occupy the sight of the flesh with perishable things. Also always close your hearing to every useless, vain, and foolish speech, and always think with fear on the future judgment of God. Do not pollute your hands with the unlawful and shameful touch of any things whatever, so that you may be able to sacrifice the Son of God with a clean body and a pure conscience.

In food and drink it is not the part of monks to distinguish flavors, nor is it fitting for the soldiers of Christ to love bodily delights. Nature requires few things, and rustic foods by which it may be nourished; appetite, however, unless it is restrained by the command of reason, is dragged toward unlawful things, through which the simplicity of nature is violated. Therefore flee excess in all things, and observe regular moderation in your way of living. Let none of you patiently listen to one who detracts from his brothers or speaks evil of his neighbor. Let each one of you restrain his tongue from useless speech, and let good monks blush to speak evil of any Christians.

Never let that warning of the Lord depart from your memory, spoken in the holy Gospel: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. A good man from a good treasure brings forth good things, and an evil man from an evil treasure brings forth evil things. But I say to you that every idle word which men shall have spoken, they shall render an account for it on the day of judgment.” O sentence too greatly to be feared. If an account is demanded for idle speech, what do detraction, scurrility, and filthy speech deserve? Or if a useless word is judged guilty under the strict reckoning of examination, what will he answer who so readily speaks what is evil and harmful? An idle word is every utterance of men which has been spoken without just usefulness and lacks the reason of lawful necessity. Therefore, brothers, consider your words, and let no one speak a word not pertinent to the matter, or to just usefulness, or to necessary [unclear].
