To have faith without works of charity is to imitate the demons, who, though they are wholly estranged from all charity, nevertheless both believe that God is just and tremble. Hence concerning believers who do not show works of charity, the Lord speaks through the prophet Isaiah, and afterward through himself in the holy Gospel, saying: This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Whoever praises Christ with his mouth, without work, is like a man who doubts in faith. For if he truly believed, he would certainly fear God. Faith without works is like a body dead without the spirit, which, removed from the sight of the living, is hidden in the tomb. If you believe well and live badly, you destroy yourself by your own sword, because for one who knows the good and does not do it, it is sin. Beware, my brothers, lest you admit any error in faith, and take the utmost care that there be no wickedness in action. Follow the faith which holy Church commends to us; live the life prescribed by the Gospel and by the cenobitic rule.

Then have hope in the manifestation of God at all times most certain, but never divorced from fear of the Lord. Hope is the certain expectation, through the grace of the merciful God, of future goods, and solicitude for deserving the crown of justice through the exercise of charity. Hope is the certain expectation of future blessedness, which the grace of God works in us, accompanied by the uprightness of a good will. Hope is that by which a person hopes to come to the life of eternal happiness which he desires, and, through the mediation of God’s grace, also disposes and prepares his own will to meet it.

Hope concerns good things, difficult things, future things, and possible things; in these no one hopes for the contrary. For no one hopes for evil, nor for something trivial, nor for what is present or past; nor are we ignorant that hope is not of impossible things. Hope is the moderator of all labors, and the sole consoler amid tribulations. For hope of future goods gives rest to those laboring for Christ in this life, just as expectation of the crown, through continual victory, softens the pain of wounds for those contending in the contest. Hope strengthened the martyrs in torments, and made them, in the power of Christ, stronger than all persecutors. Hope of eternal blessedness preserves the Church, and gave the holy confessors and virgins true charity in Christ. Hope strongly raises up the soul cast down by grief; and the higher it has lifted itself through love, the less it feels the contrary things of this world. Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord, because hope does not confound, as the holy Apostle promised, by which we have been made saved.

But so that hope may not break out into presumption, fear of the Lord is necessary for our salvation, so that we may not doubt that he whom we believe merciful and compassionate is also just and upright. For the most blessed Pope Gregory says: Nothing is safer for us than to tremble humbly under hope, lest the incautious mind either cast itself into the depth of vices by despairing, or fall presumptuously by being exalted. For the more humbly it trembles before the eyes of the strict judge under hope, the more robustly it stands in him. Fear of God corrects the mind and keeps it pure from all malice. The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, because on the way of God one begins from fear, so that little by little one may come to the strength of charity. Fear of the Lord orders the monk, and makes him zealous in the observance of regular discipline. Fear of the Lord guards the rule and preserves true purity of mind and body. Fear of the Lord neglects none of the things that are commanded, but is solicitous in all things. Walls are [unclear]. A city without the protection of walls is quickly taken by enemies, and the monk who does not have the fear of God is easily overcome by demons. For the fear of God alone is what corrects minds, drives away crimes, preserves the innocent, and makes the monk persevering in the purpose of holiness.

From fear there gradually arises charity; when, with God’s help, it has truly been rooted in the mind, overcoming servile fear, it now casts it out. The root of all goods is charity, since whatever good is done in this life is ordered by charity alone, so that it may please God. Charity is the greatest of all virtues; without it all deeds of mortals are nothing. For the most holy Apostle of Christ says: If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have charity, I have become like sounding bronze or a clanging cymbal.
