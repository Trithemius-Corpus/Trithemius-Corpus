If the fire of divine love is now burning in you, apply fuel, lest at any time, through your lukewarmness, you feel the little sparks of holy fervor utterly extinguished. Believe me, brothers, for experience has taught me: while the fire of divine fervor is burning in you, you will preserve it with easier exercise than, once it is extinguished, you will kindle it again. Therefore whoever now experiences the love of God in himself, whoever has received heavenly charity in his heart, let each one take care with solicitude not to fall back into the negligences of his former life, and let him apply the necessary fuel of spiritual exercises, so that he may be able to preserve the fervor of God as it advances within him. Let him beware not only great sins, but also small negligences, and, as far as he can, the least ones; for he who despises the least thing falls little by little, and he who does not always fear danger will soon feel the loss of spiritual fervor and of his former purpose. For Scripture says: If you do not steadfastly hold yourself in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overturned.

Blessed, therefore, is the man who is always fearful, who does not presume upon the virtues he has received, who knows with certainty that he is about to fail whenever he does not find that he has advanced at every moment toward higher things. But truly, brothers, I fear greatly, unless the estimation of works somehow deceives me, that in some of you the fire of this holy fervor is not yet present, since they seem to love little the true pursuits of monks; and this, from their manner of life, although it is clear to anyone, none of you can be ignorant of. I have not thought it wholly superfluous to admonish them, since thus far they seem to have advanced little, for the hand of the Lord has not been shortened so that it cannot save the penitent. For I trust in the mercy of the living God, that, on account of my charity toward them and the exercises of piety of those among you who walk according to the purity of the rule, he will grant them at some time to come to their senses and to love more ardently the pursuits of holy fervor.

What is this, brothers, that you do not live monastically in the monastery, and, having professed a spiritual manner of life, walk according to the flesh? Do you not know how dangerous it is for a monk to live secularly in a cenobium, and to violate by a lukewarm manner of life the vow of ancient purity which we have all made? Acknowledge, I pray, that the last things of all cloistered persons are nearest to the extremes, and that good and zealous monks, lovers of their own salvation, receive in heaven not the common rewards but the very greatest rewards of the saints, while those who live badly or lukewarmly in monastic religion are punished not with a moderate or common torment, but with the gravest torment of demons. For the higher the state in which someone sins, the heavier will be the punishment of the offender in it.

Therefore, if anyone among you has already learned to walk the way of divine love with spiritual sweetness, it is necessary that he go forward, so that he may advance more each day. But he who still lies in the torpor of a sluggish mind should strive as quickly as possible to rise again, since just as punishment is promised to those who lie fallen, so the crown of victory is denied to those who do not persevere. Beware, therefore, lest ruinous lukewarmness overturn the mind, for it is exceedingly dangerous to cloistered persons, because it is most grievous and full of miseries and sorrows, and plainly very near to hell; from its abyss a monk who has once fallen into it rarely rises up. And alas, how many faint-hearted cloistered persons are found today, lax and idle, who frequently succumb under the burden of spiritual warfare and rarely rise again to true fervor of spirit. Their condition is truly to be pitied: they have a name that they live, and they are dead; neither the memory of death has been able to lead them to true compunction of heart, nor has the remembrance of hell set before them been able to soften them.

Monks of this kind, thereafter, so long as they remain thus growing torpid in sloth, are either oppressed and overcome by evil habit, or they are in a certain sense in hell, so that they never breathe fully again in the light of the mercies of the Lord, nor do they come to the liberty of the spirit, which alone is nearest to the kingdom of heaven and makes the yoke of the Lord sweet and the burden light. These evils befall them for this reason: because their will has not yet been purged of carnal passions, nor do they will the good as they have known it, having been enticed and drawn away by their own concupiscence. For they love the perishable consolations of the flesh; even if at times they interrupt them, they nevertheless never break them off completely. Hence it is that they very rarely direct their affections toward God, and if at any time they are moved to compunction for an hour, they do not persevere long, but soon, through sloth and inertia, are wearied by sleep, suddenly loathing, out of sadness and melancholy, the good which they had begun.
