[Through] whatever raging of the world, he will remain. The true lover of God rejoices in tribulations, is glad in adversities, exults in persecutions for the sake of righteousness. He follows his king, who is seated in the heavens; there he dwells in mind and in the desire of his soul, where he does not doubt that his savior reigns.

Whatever pleasure and delights are seen anywhere in the world produce for the servant of Christ, burning in true charity, stench and horror as if they were the vilest dung, because he loves nothing earthly, nothing transitory, nothing perishable, but only him to whom he has devoted himself, with the whole intention of his mind. For the true soldier of Christ thinks nothing of the things of the world; he cares not for the flesh but for the mind, as one who is not ignorant of what is written: No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has proved himself.

Indeed, all who serve an earthly emperor always obey his commands in everything, and immediately go, without contradiction, wherever he has ordered. All follow one leader, and whatever he commands in the king’s name, no one in the whole army contradicts. You also, soldiers of the king of ages, who have appointed me as your leader in his place and as preceptor, hear the command of your emperor Jesus Christ through my mouth, and do what he commands us in the holy Gospel. Hear what he orders, and consider how you ought to serve as soldiers in the observance of regular discipline.

“He who ministers to me,” he says, “let him follow me.” Listen, cloistered monk, bearing the warfare; pay attention. Christ, to whom you serve as soldier, speaks to you; the heavenly emperor, at whose altar you minister, addresses you. If you wish to be my minister, you must follow the virtue of patience and humility which you have learned from me. For I have given you an example, O my soldiers, that just as I have done, you also should do likewise. Charity, O monk, compelled me to be born in the world as a man from a virgin; may that same charity, for love of me, always preserve you pure and humble in the cloister.

The work of human salvation which the Father commanded me I strove always to accomplish, for your sake, with unceasing meditation; and you, if you love me, take care to preserve inviolate until death the holy purpose of monastic life which you have undertaken for your sins. I was always, in all things, voluntarily subject and obedient to my heavenly Father, even to the most bitter death of the cross; you also, if you wish to come to me, obey your abbot, appointed in my place, humbly in all things and always according to the rule.

I never transgressed in any respect the command which my Father gave me; and unless you preserve inviolate until death the three principal vows of the monastic institution, you are by no means to be numbered among my ministers and soldiers. Though I was rich, for the sake of your salvation I became poor and needy; but unless you, for love of me, have despised and abandoned all things, you will not be able to be my disciple. Poverty, hunger, thirst, heat, cold, and continual labors in the world I endured for your sake; unless in these you have followed me according to your rule, as far as you are able, patiently and voluntarily, you will certainly be neither my minister, nor my soldier, nor my monk. For in your patience you will possess your soul.

In this world I desired nothing, had nothing, loved nothing earthly; but poor, humble, and meek, [unclear], I most perfectly subjected myself always in all things to the will of the Father, ready to receive injuries and blows. But you, O cloistered monk, who profess the monastic rule under an abbot or prior, if from now on you have followed the desires of your flesh, if you have possessed a private purse or property of your own in the monastery, if you have loved anything besides me, if you have despised the commands of your preceptor, if you have not served me perseveringly in humility, know for certain that I shall not hold you as a soldier, I shall not count you as a monk, a minister, or a disciple, but judging you a fugitive, an apostate, faithless, false, a mocker, and an unfaithful perjurer, I shall condemn you without mercy forever, as you have deserved, with my betrayer Judas and the fallen angels.

What your lips vowed to me is no longer yours but mine. But if you have usurped for yourself what is mine, you do not wish to be a monk and my faithful servant, but a thief and a perverse robber, impious and unjust; have no doubt that when I come I shall judge you as such.
