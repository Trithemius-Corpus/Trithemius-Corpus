*On continence and true chastity.*

You are monks, set apart from the world for the service of our Lord Jesus Christ by a voluntary promise; hence, under necessity of salvation, you must lead a life higher and holier than the rest of your fellow Christians, and overcome every depravity of flesh and soul. For you must always be most chaste in mind and body alike, and stained by no filth of corruptible flesh. An incontinent monk is an apostate; and he who defiles his flesh with the filth of women is like one violating an oath. Lust is a lake of hell, and despair is always very near to incontinence. A monk who once slips into the pit of the flesh is rarely kindled to true compunction of heart. For the vice of fornication, strengthened by long habit, is not easily conquered unless it is corrected by force through contrary remedies. It is a matter of greater difficulty to rise again after a fall of the flesh to true chastity than to remain in continence once possessed. It is very easy to fall from the state of uprightness into any vice whatever, rather than to amend a sin strengthened by habit. For, as a certain pagan truthfully says, easy is the descent to Avernus; but to recall the step and escape upward to the breezes: this is the work, this is the labor.

Let us consider those who have fallen into this most shameful pit and afterward, by the authority of their superiors, were compelled to penance: how do they live among the brothers? They do not have the signs of true compunction, but pass a restless life in dryness of heart. No devotion of mind is seen in them, but everywhere they follow the comforts of the flesh. O how rarely is one converted to the sweetness of inward love who has once been cast down into the lake of fornication. For unless, before he comes into this misery, he has been grounded in the fear of the Lord, when fallen he returns with difficulty to true compunction.

Therefore live soberly, dearest brothers, and always keep watch in holy prayers, since unless you walk most cautiously in all things, you will by no means be able to escape the snares of demons. Love abstinence; guard humility above all things. For no one can suppress the movements of his flesh who has disdained to subdue gluttony and pride. The insolent demon of fornication is never better conquered than if true humility of heart is preserved in the fullness of all virtues. For just as almighty God, resisting the proud, gives grace to the humble, so the devil, fleeing the humble, casts the proud down into fornication. Therefore he who, being gluttonous, eating and drinking to satiety, desires to conquer the spirit of fornication, is as one carrying oil to a fire. And he who strives to extinguish the movements of lust by abstinence alone is like a man who is seen swimming in the midst of waves with one hand. Let continual prayer to God be joined to abstinence, and in the ship of holy humility one will sail securely.

Let prayer always be in the mouth of the monk, and the memory of the passion and death of the Lord Jesus Christ. Therefore whenever you feel a disordered movement of the flesh, whenever you perceive the invisible enemy to be present to do harm, immediately flee to the Son of God hanging on the cross, and, with your knees bent to the ground, let such a prayer be formed to him: O crucified Jesus, my love, through the pain of your wounds have mercy on me; through the outpouring of your most holy blood help me in this struggle, and through the power of your most innocent death free me from every contagion of mind and body.

Nothing is more salutary than this manner of prayer, in which the memory of Christ’s passion is cherished and petitions are strengthened in the power of the Savior of all. Nothing weakens the snares of the ancient enemy more; nothing more quickly enfeebles the forces of his malignity, than if a devout servant of Christ humbly perseveres in meditation on the most bitter passion of the Lord. Just as some thief flees before the face of a pursuing judge, so the devil quickly vanishes from the sight of a monk who attentively meditates on the passion and death of the Savior. He quickly extinguishes the movement of wicked thought when the bitterness of Christ’s passion is recalled to mind. Just as snow melts when the sun grows warm, so the devil is driven away by meditation on the Lord’s passion.

The greatest protection of chastity is always to invoke divine aid. And unless immediately at the beginning of temptation you run back to the Lord, you will little by little incur harm from delay. For if you admit delay in pernicious thoughts, demons soon cast a perceptible blindness into the mind.
