*Sermon III. Fol. LXVII*

Whoever thinks that he is walking in the sight of him who always sees all things keeps his conscience pure and observes his conduct with the greatest diligence. Let him always keep watch in the fear of the Lord; let him always chastise [his body?] with fasting and [abstinence?], for love of the heavenly Father. The monk who recognizes himself as a pilgrim and stranger in this world never forgets the heavenly homeland; he sees nothing on the road of the present life in which his soul may take delight. For the prudent traveller considers none of the sights along the roads; nothing of passing things delights him in his pilgrimage, but at every time he sighs for the end of the journey. He always thinks sweetly of his homeland; he bears it grievously that his pilgrimage is prolonged or delayed; and therefore he desires to have nothing in this world except the Lord, in whose sight he hastens earnestly to appear. The charity of God is the way to God; if anyone has neglected to keep it burning in himself, he wanders miserably from the true road of the heavenly homeland. Scripture says: God is charity; and he who remains in charity remains in God, and God in him. But he who is not in charity has withdrawn far from the way of salvation; and walking in outer darkness, he will never come to the kingdom.

*How Sin Is to Be Fled*

*Sermon V.*

You have heard, my very dear brothers, what Saint John the Apostle says about sinners: Whoever commits sin is from the devil, for the devil sins from the beginning. A truly fearful and greatly dreaded sentence. If everyone who commits sin is from the devil, because everyone offends in many things, whom shall we find immune from sin? Yet he says subsequently: Everyone who is born of God does not commit sin, because his seed remains in him; and he cannot sin, because he is born of God. But he had said before: If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. What does this very contradiction mean? that no one is without sin, and that he who is born of God does not commit sin? There is no contradiction here, because it is true on both sides: no one is without sin, inasmuch as no one is without the law of sin.
