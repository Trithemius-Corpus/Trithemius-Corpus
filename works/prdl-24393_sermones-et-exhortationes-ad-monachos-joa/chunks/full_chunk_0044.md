There are no works of virtue without the trial of temptations; no faith without disturbances; no contest without an enemy; no victory without an encounter. Hence Saint James the Apostle says in his epistle: Blessed is the man who endures temptation, for when he has been proved he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him. Hence again Scripture says: The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men. But lest anyone think that the devil is permitted to tempt men as much as he desires, Saint [Christophilus?] cries out: But God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but will also make with the temptation an outcome, so that you may be able to endure it. And Saint Augustine says: The devil is permitted to tempt only so much as is profitable for you: that you may be exercised, that you may be proved, that you, who did not know yourself, may be found by yourself. For the devil, with God’s permission, can stir up the motion of temptation; but it lies in your most free power, if you wish, to give or deny consent to the tempter. Hence, when he tempted the Son of God in the desert, he did not drive him, did not touch him, did not compel him, but only said: Cast yourself down; so that you may understand that he cannot conquer except one who wills it. Therefore the divine chief apostle of Christ said: Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. For the devil can suggest evil; he cannot compel, nor overcome unless you consent.

Our life, as Saint Augustine bears witness, in this pilgrimage cannot be without sin and temptation, because our progress is increased through temptations; nor does anyone know himself unless he has been tempted; nor can he be crowned unless he has conquered; nor can he conquer unless he has fought; nor can he fight unless he has had an enemy and temptations. For Scripture says: He who has not been tempted, what does he know? For no one will be crowned except one who has fought lawfully. Therefore, brothers most dear, as you come to the service of God, stand in justice and fear; and so long as you can be conquered and subjected by the adversary, do not boast of security. You know that he with whom you have entered the contest never sleeps, does not rest, does not cease from persecution, and, always lying in wait for you, is never wearied. But if at any time he has been overcome, immediately he rises up for a new contest, nor does he desist from assaulting the good until, defeated, he altogether falls back, or else by untimely perseverance conquers the combatant. Hence also Saint Pope Gregory says: The ancient enemy, when from the first contest he sees that he is falling back, renews other wars of temptations. The wary adversary of the human race observes the character of each person, and whatever vices they are nearer to, these he places before the face of the heart; and he uses great strength to overthrow the state of the mind.

Stand firmly rooted in the fear of the Lord, and you will easily overcome all the temptations of the enemy. For it is written in the Book of Proverbs: In the fear of the Lord is confidence of strength. And again: Through mercy and faith sins are purged; but through the fear of the Lord everyone turns away from evil. In Ecclesiasticus, moreover, the wise man said: The fear of the Lord drives out sin. For he who is without fear will not be able to be justified. For the anger of his animosity overturns him. Therefore, by Solomon’s testimony, that man is called blessed who is always fearful; but he who is of a hard mind will fall into evil. Prepare your souls also, through zeal for holy conduct, for temptation, so that when it comes you may be able to resist and stand the stronger for it. For you will easily overcome the devil if you always come prepared for battle. Behold, your adversary, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking whom he may devour; you must resist him in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ if you desire to obtain victory. No place for flight is granted here; no little delay of security is allowed to those who fight; but it is necessary that you engage: you will overcome the enemy if you have stood bravely, or be conquered by him if you have fought badly. By this law we have all entered the world, that by fighting we may conquer; on this condition we have been made men, that, having overcome the allurements of the flesh, we ought to become equal to the holy angels. Contests are never lacking to monks, since for as long as they live in the flesh, we are necessarily exposed to temptations. For Scripture says: Many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these the Lord will deliver them.
