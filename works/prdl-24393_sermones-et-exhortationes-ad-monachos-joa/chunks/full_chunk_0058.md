For the first, our holy lawgivers, desiring you to be prepared, command that the brave deeds of the martyrs, whose imitators you ought to become, be read; and then, that you may be teachable in mind, they ordered [unclear] to be subjected to regular discipline. Thus, too, that you may be brave and patient, in imitation of the holy martyrs, under necessary rebukes, the account of their passion is read here in your ears, so that, your minds having been prepared for saving doctrine, a reading from the Rule which you profess may be recited to you at the same time. The holy martyrs of Christ encourage you by their example to patience; and blessed Benedict admonishes you by his teaching that you also be ready for injury.

Therefore let none of you, most beloved brothers, refuse in this place humbly to receive penance every day for his negligences; let no one disdain it. This place is the public one for expiating sin, in which the severity of judgment is so tempered that the kindness of discretion is not violated. For I wish to be the physician of your souls, not their destroyer. Therefore approach the examination of our judgment without fear, so that, when accused concerning negligences committed openly, you may humbly proclaim them. I am unwilling to burden you beyond the Rule, so as to transfer the most secret offenses of conscience from sacramental confession to the public examination of the one accused, which we know certain persons have done, to the grave detriment of the souls of their subjects. For thus says our teacher Benedict in the Rule: “But if the cause of the sin has lain hidden in the soul, let him disclose it only to the abbot or to spiritual elders, who know how to heal their own wounds and those of others, and not to uncover or make them public.”

There are two places in the monastery for the expiation of sin: one for those things that are manifest, and the other for those that are hidden. For manifest sins are to be expiated publicly before all in the daily proclamation of faults; but hidden sins, and those whose manifestation would scandalize the weak, are to be referred to confession. For when sin is manifest it must also be punished publicly; and he who has offended in secret must also be corrected in secret. There are certain rulers of cloistered persons who [bring] the hidden sins of their subjects to public examination
