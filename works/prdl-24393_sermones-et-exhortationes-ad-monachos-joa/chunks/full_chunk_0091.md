Always walk before Jesus in the simplicity of your heart, having a pure conscience in all things, lest your humility become bestial; for it contributes nothing to salvation for a sinner to acknowledge himself publicly and, by persevering in evil, not amend his fault. For the true servant of Christ sanctifies his humility by good works, and amends every guilt of his past life by continual penitence. Daily he recalls to memory the evil that he remembers himself to have committed, and groans that he has gravely offended the Lord. Let him walk very cautiously, and so lament past sins committed that he in no way commit further things that must be lamented.

The monk who is truly humble follows the Lord Jesus with a pure conscience, and fears nothing in this world more than to offend the teacher whom he imitates. Whatever he does and wherever he goes, he always thinks of the Lord Jesus as present, and by continual meditation examines how he may please him alone. For this is true and Christian humility, and humble Christianity: to wash away sin in true compunction, and to preserve the conscience in all purity at all times, not only from fear, but rather from love of Christ. The true humility of monks continually does whatever good it can for the honor of God, and thinks that it will do nothing that it knows to displease Christ. The true humility of monks neglects none of those things which God has commanded, and always flees as deadly those things which he has forbidden.

The penitent monk, humble in compunction of tears and serving the Lord in the fervor of charity, is a companion of the holy angels, a friend to the citizens of heaven, and very formidable to demons. For demons fear and flee nothing more than the humility of the servants of Jesus Christ, because, since they themselves are most proud by their vicious will, they are most violently tormented by the devotion of the humble. For where there is true humility, demons cannot remain; for as ice is dissolved by fire, so malignant spirits are scattered from the presence of a humble monk.

O venerable mother and preserver of all virtues, humility: yours is the kingdom, yours the power; you confer the kingdom on the humble; you bestow the glory of eternal felicity on the meek; without your guidance no one comes to heaven; he who follows you does not fear hell. Whoever gathers works of virtue without you is like one who carries dust into the wind. You are the seasoning of all good works, for just as food in the mouth of the eater is not savory without salt, so without your companionship no one is established as a friend of God.

Humility made the Son of God man. Humility will make the servant of Christ equal to the holy angel. An angel, through pride in his own judgment, became a devil; and the Christian man through humility becomes an angel. Humility opens heaven to the meek servants of God, and pride plunges exalted men down to hell. Humility makes friends of God, and pride makes companions of demons. Humility illuminates the mind to true wisdom, and pride blinds the understanding of the one whom it has possessed. Humility, once all disordered affections have been purged, [unclear] the man whom it possesses; and pride fills its worshiper with all crimes and filthiness. Humility makes its lover a servant of God, but pride makes him a slave of the devil. Humility makes a man wise in the law of the Lord, but pride renders a man foolish in the knowledge of the Most High. Humility brings the poor man into fellowship with God, and pride makes the rich man a member of the devil. Humility is the mother and nurse of all virtues, and pride is the source of all vices. Humility, with charity mediating, covers a multitude of sins; but pride alone brings in the fullness of all vices. Humility makes the good works of monks most pleasing to God, but pride renders the pursuits of the proud idle before the Creator. Humility receives from the Lord whatever it has asked; but God almighty resists the prayers of the proud. Humility always has, in the mercies of the Lord, a pious confidence with gladness of mind; but pride, by the sting of despair, generates disturbance in man. Humility always has joy of spirit in the love of Christ; but pernicious fear always occupies pride. Humility, the fosterer of good works, neglects none of the spiritual exercises; but pride despises all pursuits of salvation. Humility justifies sinners; but pride condemns its followers. Humility makes one near to God; but pride makes one alien from the grace of God.
