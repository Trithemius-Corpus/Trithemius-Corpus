Without the charity of God, therefore, all things profit you nothing; for it is itself the one and only thing that distinguishes between good and evil works. O monk, monk, whoever you are who live lukewarmly in the monastery without the fervor of charity, do you think that the outward equipment of discipline can save you? Charity is what sanctifies the one who has it; charity commends the other virtues and all good works to God. Have charity, and all the works of piety that you do will be pleasing to God.

*On the arduous way that leads to life. Sermon 4.*

You have heard, dearly beloved brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ saying in today’s reading in the holy Gospel: “How narrow is the gate and strait the way that leads to life, and few are they who find it.” Is not Christ himself the way, who had said: “I am the way, the truth, and the life”? And how does he now say that it is strait and narrow, when he himself is the one who had said: “Take my yoke upon you and learn, for I am meek and humble of heart. For my yoke is sweet, and my burden light”? What else is the yoke of Christ than the strait and narrow way to the kingdom of God? How, then, is it both light and narrow? Let your charity hear. The yoke of the Lord Jesus is sweet to the good, hard and unbearable to the reprobate. The way of Christ to life is broad and pleasant to those exercised in the charity of the humble, but narrow and bitter to the proud. For nothing is so hard, nothing so terrible, nothing so arduous that it is not easy and pleasant to one who loves God. Again, nothing is so light, nothing so easy, nothing so small that it is not heavy and burdensome to an evil will. Therefore the Lord said: “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart.” If you wish to bear my yoke upon you, first learn the meekness and humility of my heart, since unless you are meek and humble, you will by no means be able to carry my load, as though it were a heavy weight and an unbearable burden. I am a humble way; I do not contain the proud. I am a narrow gate; I do not admit the swollen. Unless your soul has despised all worldly things, unless you have inflicted the necessary force upon the pleasures of the flesh, you cannot enter through me into the kingdom like an animal burdened with sins. I am a humble and meek way, and I receive the humble; I am a strait doorway, and I admit the poor in spirit and the clean of heart.

To eternal life, brothers, we strive by the narrow way of truth; it is necessary that in time we provide for ourselves all things that are necessary for so arduous a journey. There are, moreover, four things especially necessary for travelers, without which the journey is dangerous and one rarely arrives at the goal. The first thing that we say is necessary for travelers we believe to be knowledge of the roads. For one who tries to walk far through trackless places without knowledge of the roads is sometimes entangled in dangerous errors. Therefore let the prudent traveler either first learn the road, or take to himself a companion skilled in the journey, lest he either fall into the hands of robbers or depart from the path of the right road. Therefore, desiring to return to the homeland of the heavenly kingdom by a way exceedingly narrow and known to few, we must have as guide and companion of the journey Jesus Christ himself, who called himself the way and the truth. Outside Christ there is no true knowledge of the ways. Whoever neglects to have Christ as the companion of his journey runs in vain and without fruit. Therefore let us learn the way of life, dearly beloved brothers, without knowledge of which no one reaches the homeland; no one reaches life [unclear].
