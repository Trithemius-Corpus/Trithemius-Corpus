What does it mean that wrath remains upon him, except that the first, original sin is not removed from him? For after the establishment of the New Testament, original sin is removed from those who believe in him through faith in Christ alone. Circumcision was given to the fathers as a sign of future salvation; nevertheless none of them could enter the kingdom of heaven before Christ the Savior rose from the dead. Therefore outside faith in Christ there is no salvation, since, as he himself says, he who has not believed is judged and will be condemned. We remain in Christ through faith and love: he himself is the way of salvation, he the truth without error, he life without death. If we have the Lord Jesus as the guide of our journey, we walk confidently.

Secondly, it is necessary for wayfarers to pass through the lands of their journey without injury to anyone; for if they commit theft or robbery, or any other similar sin against the commonwealth, in the end they would come to be punished by penalty and would not reach the intended destination of the journey. But we, brothers, placed on the road by which we return to the homeland of the heavenly kingdom, must most diligently take care that we commit nothing, in word, deed, or thought, contrary to the justice of God, but render to each one his right and due: to God first of all devotion in worship and charity, to our neighbor love, to ourselves the holiness of perfect purity. Indeed, if we live according to the things Christ taught, do we not remain on the straight way of salvation which leads to the kingdom? We shall remain on the way if we keep the commandments of God; we shall walk in the truth if we love the Lord our God himself with all the strength of our mind; we shall come to life if, turning aside from evil, we continually persevere in the practice of good.

Thirdly, it is necessary to have provision for the journey, because if wayfarers lack travelling supplies, they will not be able to reach the intended place. But the prudent wayfarer, before he leaves his house, reckons within himself the time and length of the journey, and prepares travelling supplies and expenses according as necessity requires. A great and hard journey remains for us; we must prepare provisions for many years, lest we fail on this road on which we are going. The travelling provision of this journey of ours to the supercelestial homeland is not gold, not silver, not the deceitful riches of the world, but faith in Christ and love of God. The holy virtues are the unfailing travelling provision of the soul for the kingdom of heaven; strengthened by them, it walks on the way of God in gladness. The soul faithful to Christ and accompanied by the holy virtues gladly completes the hard road of this long pilgrimage. To love God above all things, to exercise oneself constantly in good works, to keep one's conscience in all purity, to do injury to no one: this provides the soul with true travelling provision for the eternal homeland in heaven.

Fourthly, it is advised as necessary for wayfarers that they not forget the purpose undertaken before they come to the end of the journey begun. For he must be called a foolish wayfarer who is recalled from his journey by children's games and forgets where he was tending. And we, dearest brothers, who have taken up the way of monastic religion, must always think upon our end, since death hastens every day even when we do not notice it, and presents the unprepared as well as the prepared before judgment. Meanwhile let us do good while it is permitted, as much as we can, and let us not delay to be converted to the Lord with all our heart. Christ is the way by which we proceed to the kingdom of God; Christ is the truth in which, remaining, we do not err; Christ is the life for which we hope. Hear, brothers, what he himself says in the holy Gospel: Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. This is the way upon which you entered at the beginning of your monastic profession; if you wish to proceed on it rightly and laudably, you will have to love Christ above all things, living purely in regular observance. It is yours, brothers, to love nothing in this world and to chastise the flesh for the love of God. It belongs to monks to despise all the delights and pleasures of the present life and to desire nothing other than the everlasting feasts. It belongs to monks to imitate Christ according to their powers, and to establish their life in evangelical purity. To imitate Christ is the highest perfection. The faithful minister of Christ follows his Lord, and where Christ is, there his minister also will be. Therefore whoever among you is a lover of his own salvation, desires to come after Christ to the kingdom of heaven, and wishes to keep and preserve until the end those things which he promised at his entrance into religion, let him keep these things.
