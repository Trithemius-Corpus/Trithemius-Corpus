For although we cannot wholly avoid venial sins, nevertheless we ought to take care with all our strength that they do not multiply in us. But those who, by the Lord’s mercy, daily make progress toward better things in this degree fall into sin more rarely. And whenever they have fallen, they rise again through penitence. Yet their state is very near to danger, because just as a torn garment, often [torn], is at last rendered useless, so the man who falls by sinning more frequently is often choked by an evil habit. But if any from this degree depart this life in the Lord’s mercies, they will be saved not without the very heavy pains of purgatory. Therefore one must hasten toward higher things, so that not only mortal sins but also venial sins may be avoided with all one’s strength.

The second degree is believed to be ascended by those who strive, as much as they can, to avoid both venial and mortal crimes. But because of excessive commerce with bodily weakness they have not advanced in their purpose, but often fall into the various negligences of this life, although they do not commit mortal sins. We who are lukewarm trust that we have somehow ascended this degree, who, established under the observance of regular discipline, keep our minds from graver sins with the Lord’s help. But since we are surnamed lukewarm, we live very negligently and are slow to perform the exercises of holy virtues. There are, moreover, many set on the threshold of this second degree whom servile fear occupies; if that alone exists, it by no means approaches the kingdom of God. For servile fear without charity will not suffice for obtaining the glory of heavenly felicity, since he is a slave who does not sin from fear of punishment, but a son does not do evil for love of God. Yet it is good to begin from servile fear, so that a person may gradually be able to arrive at filial fear, which is called chaste. For although to withdraw from evil from fear of punishments is by no means sufficient for attaining the kingdom of God, nevertheless it prepares those who act for filial fear. Hence the lowest degree of those making progress is not to transgress from fear of punishment, and not to sin lest they be damned forever. For charity alone, discerning the merits of all, is admitted as fit to enter the kingdom of God; and whatever has been done outside it is judged unworthy of the kingdom of God. Therefore whoever has merited to ascend according to this second degree, whoever has learned not to sin from fear of punishments, if you wish to arrive at the joy of eternal felicity, take care to hasten as quickly as possible toward higher things. For, with Scripture bearing witness, we grant that you are indeed beginning rightly; yet we judge it dangerous to stand long in the valley of shadows. Ascend, therefore, as much as you can, daily, and do not persist with beasts in the dung of stinking actions, since if you do not make progress, you have failed; if you do not ascend, you descend.

The third degree is comprised by those who, persevering in a good purpose, keep themselves from every mortal sin and also withdraw from venial sins with all their strength. They love purity of conscience, and by no means commit anything that could stain it. Yet in one respect they are lower than those that follow, because, not simply withdrawing from evil for the love of God, they do good; but, still weak and far from the height of holiness, they remain among lower things. He who truly loves God with a pure mind serves and obeys him neither from fear of punishments nor with regard for reward, but only for love of him. And therefore all the degrees written above are proposed for beginners, through which, ascending gradually, they are at last joined to the perfect. It is good to withdraw from evil from fear of punishments; it is good to do good for love of reward; and although this by no means suffices for perfection, it nevertheless provides the beginning and the way for those who desire to make progress. For servile fear, if continued, begets filial and chaste fear, from whose [unclear] perfect charity flows. But holy charity, after it has once kindled minds, thereafter casts out every servile fear and [unclear] fear.

The fourth degree strives to show the way of perfection to those ascending, and for that reason is rightly judged greater than the degrees written above. Whatever of good is seen in them is possessed more perfectly in this one. For the ascenders of this fourth degree, with every zeal, keep themselves not only from mortal sins but also from venial sins as far as is possible for them; they most diligently devote themselves to good and holy works, and desire to please God above all things. In one respect they surpass those preceding, because they turn aside from evil neither from fear of punishments nor perform good in hope of reward, but do all things for the love of God alone, and [seek] nothing other than the honor of God. For the true lover of God neither fears punishment nor thinks of reward, but, walking in breadth of heart, commends his will to the divine ordinance. For God, because he is supremely good, is to be loved not for the reward of the heavenly kingdom, nor on account of anything else, but for his very self; and in every good work [he is] principally to be held in intention.
