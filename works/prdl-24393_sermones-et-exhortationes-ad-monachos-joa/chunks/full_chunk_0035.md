Let us therefore pray in the highest fervor of spirit, and let us live with as much purity of conscience as we can, because that prayer is perfect and pleasing to God whose cause and tongue alike cry out, whose deeds and speech agree with its supplication, whose life and thought lend patronage to its petitions. For without the justice of truth every prayer will be fruitless, because we know that God does not hear sinners, and that salvation is far from sinners, as Scripture says: “He who turns away his ears so as not to hear the laws, his prayer will be abominable.” The holy prophet also says in the psalm: “If I have regarded iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me.” For just as in a wound no medicine is of any benefit unless the iron is first drawn out, so the prayer of that man is of no benefit while deceit still remains in his mind or hatred in his breast.

But in order that God may hear your prayer, first of all the conscience must be cleansed from every stain of mortal sin, and the mind must take on a true purpose of amendment. To pray much and at the same time offend in many things: what else is this than to mock God? For in two ways prayer is hindered, so that it is not heard by the Lord: one, when the one praying still commits evil by sinning; the other, if he has not, for God’s sake, forgiven his debtor the debt. Therefore you who desire to have the fruit of your prayers, who wish to come with the penitent to God’s mercy, take care that you do nothing evil against God’s will, and diligently see to it that you truly forgive from the heart all things committed against you by men. For you know that the Lord said: “Forgive, and you shall be forgiven.”

But after you have cleansed your conscience from evil and forgiven every offense to your neighbor, then you will be ready to entreat the Lord, casting yourself humbly down in his sight; and pray with purity of heart and fervor of spirit, nor cease from prayer as long as you live in the flesh. Let your prayer be pure, simple, clear, and manifest; let it be full of gravity and weight, and adorned with the beauty of true humility. Do not ask anything in prayer contrary to the salvation of the soul, nor pray too importunately for a temporal thing, because it is shamelessness to ask riches from God, which furnish the greatest occasion for pride and often defile Christian purity. It is not shamelessness if you ask for daily bread, because it is one thing to ask for material by which you may become proud, and another to ask for necessary nourishment by which you may live.

Do not justify yourself when praying, lest like the Pharisee you go away condemned; nor ask anything from the Lord as if by debt owed to your merits, but humble yourself in all things, so that you may deserve to receive grace. Remember the humility of the publican, and pray with such humility that you may find with him pardon for sins and justification of the soul. For God’s grace is more abundant than the prayer of the suppliant, and he grants us more when we pray than we ask. You will not pray with a cry of the voice to him who knows the hidden things of the heart, but in the fervor of spirit and the inward cry of the mind strike the ears of the divine majesty. Nor should you draw out a length of words before him who is spirit, because God will be appeased not by much speaking but by the devout mind of the one praying.

At the time of prayer cast away from yourself every malice and unrest of heart, and, as we often say, forgive if you have anything against your neighbor. For unless for love of the Savior you forgive your neighbor every debt, you will obtain nothing from the Lord by your prayers. It is said that there is a certain kind of serpent which, when it wishes to go into the water to drink, first vomits out all its poison. You also, monk, imitate this serpent in this respect: vomit out all the poison of anger and bitterness, forgiving your brother a hundred denarii, so that the Lord may forgive you ten thousand talents. As you desire the Lord to be toward you, so also show yourself toward your fellow servant. Call upon the Lord with your whole heart and you will find him; let your prayer be ardent and most pure, so that your mind may be joined to him in most holy love.

Strive for this throughout your whole life: that you may offer pure prayer to the Lord God at all times. He does not act with levity, nor is his judgment easily moved; what he promised is fixed. If you pray earnestly, you will see a miracle. Remember that you stand beneath the gaze of God, who understands all the secrets of hearts and knows the hidden things of minds.
