*Sermon I. Folio LIX.*

*In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ begins the second book of exhortations to monks, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, afterwards of [unclear]. How cautiously monks must live: Sermon One.*

Many indeed, most beloved brothers, are the things necessary for you to consider in this life, if you desire to arrive at the glory of eternal salvation. For you know, Scripture bearing witness, that your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour; and we know that he must be resisted with great vigilance in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ through works of charity. Therefore let no one presume on his own powers, lest he trust in vanity; for unless you humbly keep yourselves in the fear of the Lord, you will quickly fall into the snare of the devil. “The greater you are,” Scripture commands, “humble yourself in all things,” for God resists the proud and grants grace to the humble, and to the true despiser of this world.

A monk must always be solicitous in the observance of regular discipline, and continually zealous in the fear of the Lord. A monk must be chaste and modest in mind and body alike, so that he may serve God just as he promised, in all purity. A monk ought not to live according to his own will, but according to the Rule and according to the direction of his pastor, conformed to the norm of cenobitic institution. A monk ought to love busy and holy silence, and usefully occupy his mind in the reading of the Scriptures. A monk must at all times, even unto death, be solicitous that he be made perfect in the observance of the commandments of God and of the Rule.

But so that you may know what things are necessary for you, living in holy religion, for perfection, I shall describe for you a certain path of virtues, by which one must direct the way to the kingdom of heaven. All the virtues, however, cohere with one another by the bond of kinship, so that he who possesses one perfectly seems to have them all, and he who lacks even one is said to possess none at all.

The first of all virtues necessary for salvation is the most holy Catholic faith, separated from every pollution of errors and heresies, and originally rooted in the doctrine of our Lord Jesus Christ. For, as Scripture bears witness, without faith it is impossible to please God; and he who does not believe in the only-begotten Son of God is already judged, and the wrath of God remains upon him. Therefore faith is the origin of all virtues, from which all pursuits of salvation arise.

True and right faith in God begets fear of the Lord, for if we believe God to be just, we shall rightly confess that he is to be feared. Right faith brings forth hope and confidence, because when we believe God to be merciful, soon we hope that by his goodness we can attain salvation. Faith begets charity, because if we believe God to be the highest good, without doubt we love him. For who would not love what he does not doubt to be the supreme good? Faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not appearing. Faith joins the soul to God, and through the mediation of charity makes it the friend of the eternal King.

For the most holy prelate Augustine says: There are no greater riches, no treasures, no honors, no substance of this world greater than the Catholic faith, which saves sinful men, enlightens the blind, cures the sick, baptizes catechumens, justifies the faithful, restores penitents, increases the just, crowns martyrs, preserves virgins, widows, and married persons in chaste modesty, ordains clerics, consecrates priests, and places believers with the holy angels in the eternal inheritance of the heavens.

But where sound faith is not, there justice cannot be, because the just man lives by faith; and he who lacks the merit of justice will have no portion with Christ. Faith is the beginning of human salvation, without which no one can reach the number of the children of God, nor possess any share in the kingdom of God. Faith is the foundation of all virtues; whatever is done outside faith is without doubt built for Gehenna. Christian faith is a most beautiful lamp illuminating the soul, and through the observance of the commandments leading it into eternal happiness.

Faith first brings the mind back to the knowledge of God; afterward it gives the precepts of living unto obedience; and when a man has kept the commandments of God, hope of attaining salvation follows; then charity is made firm in hearts. Faith gives knowledge to the soul, knowledge [unclear]; because when right faith is corrupted by the falsehood of heresies, by no action is one brought to the harbor of salvation.
