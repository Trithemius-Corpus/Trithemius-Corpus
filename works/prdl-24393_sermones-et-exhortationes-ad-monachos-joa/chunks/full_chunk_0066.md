*Homily XIV. Folio XXXIII.*

I say, however, that perhaps it will not be useless: that confession ought to be one’s own, complete, free, humble, spontaneous, and shamefaced. For the holy bishop Ambrose says that shamefaced confession greatly avails with God for remitting sins to human beings, and the penalty that we cannot avoid by defense we relieve by humble shame. A great thing is the due confession of sins, since it immediately restores to spiritual health the soul of the confessor wounded by offenses; it also justifies the humbled mind and grants pardon for sins. For all our hope after the fall of sin consists in the humility of confession, because guilt is not forgiven unless it is revealed in confession, through the priest, to God who knows all things.

For in confession there is a place of mercy, as Solomon says in the book of Proverbs: “He who hides his crimes will not prosper; but he who has confessed and abandoned them will obtain mercy.” It is also written in the Psalm: “I said, I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord; and you forgave the impiety of my sin.” The holy evangelist John also says in his first epistle: “If we confess our sins, God is faithful and just, so that he remits our sins for us; and he will cleanse us from all iniquity.” And the holy bishop Augustine says: “Confession is the salvation of souls, the disperser of vices, the restorer of virtues, the conqueror of demons, and the heap of all goods. It closes the mouth of hell and opens the gates of the heavenly paradise.” Cassiodorus also, commending the power of true confession, speaks thus: “O blessed confession, which takes away everlasting reproach, since whatever guilt penitence has found, it renders absolved for eternal ages.”

Fourth, satisfaction is necessary for true penitence; by it, namely, we remove all causes that cut off sins, and for those things that we have wrongly committed we placate the Lord with holy works. Hence the divine Pope Gregory says: “We do not truly make satisfaction if we cease from iniquity, unless we also pursue with opposed acts the pleasures that we have loved.” The most holy Pope Leo also says in a sermon: “By prayer God’s propitiation is sought; by fasting the concupiscence of the flesh is extinguished; by alms sins are redeemed; and at the same time through all these the image of God is renewed in us.”

Confession, moreover, is the voluntary accusation of the offender, from blushing shame, and, through the keys of the Church, making satisfaction for sins, with the obligation, through penitence, of chastising in himself the things he has brought forth, by the command of the priest. Confession ought to be simple, humble, pure, faithful, frequent, naked and open, discreet, voluntary, one’s own, shamefaced, complete, secret, full of tears, made at the due times, strong and [secret/secure?]; voluntary, secret, full of tears, made at the due times, strong and secure; voluntary, secret, full of tears, are held. For true penitence requires all these things: it so laments past sins that henceforth it does not commit further things to be lamented.

*On the abstinence and fasting of monks. Homily XV.*

A great and necessary good for a monk, dearest brothers, is abstinence with fasting: namely, that he not offend in mind through the concupiscence of the flesh and evil thoughts, and that in body he abstain from foods and drinks that beget wantonness of spirit. For the concupiscence of the mind is quickly kindled into lust if the body is nourished too carefully. Considering the very great usefulness of this good, the most holy fathers of old, who handed down precepts of living to monks, all with one judgment established for us a fixed measure of fasts; under its moderation the body should take such refreshment of foods as nature required, not such as the intemperance of the will might desire.

Holy Scripture also commends fasting to us in many ways, and so often mentions it with praise.
