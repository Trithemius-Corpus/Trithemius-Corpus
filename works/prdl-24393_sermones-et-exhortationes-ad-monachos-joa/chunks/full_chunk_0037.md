For the Lord is the hearer of a righteous prayer, namely when integrity of life commends the prayer, and no guilt of conscience makes the one who prays hesitate. Friendship will not stand between those unlike each other, and between God and a sinner there can be no communion. If you wish to be heard when you pray, take care first that you do not differ from the will of God. Live in the spirit most like to God, and you will suffer no rejection in prayer. For indeed purity of conscience, in the fervor of divine love, makes a man like to God in understanding; likeness causes love; love introduces familiarity; familiarity, confidence; and upon confidence there follows the easy obtaining of one’s petition. For just as there is no union among things unlike, so between God and an evil man prayer avails nothing.

Therefore whoever among you desires to obtain the effect of his prayers, let him first, through true penitence, cleanse the dwelling-place of his heart from every fault; then let him strive to inflame himself by the exercise of holy compunction; afterward let him continually feed his mind in the pursuit of the love of God, so that the good which he begins he may thereafter at no time interrupt. But afterward, when, with the desire of divine love always increasing in him, he has by long effort brought the exercise of holy purity into habit, and the mind has conceived the necessary firmness in its good purpose, then for the first time the likeness of divine communion is gradually introduced, and prayer is formed to its due state. For the more the mind, stirred up in the love of God, has advanced, the more quickly will prayer obtain from the Lord what it asks. For the love of God alone makes one powerful in prayer; and in it, the more fervently the mind has burned, the more easily it will obtain the effect of its petitions. For if you love much, you can do much. For God is charity, and he who remains in charity remains in God, and God in him. But in him in whom God remains, there is nothing stronger than he, except God himself alone.

There are among you certain men weak and feeble in spirit, who already consider themselves strong and perfect in the way of the Lord; their devotion is both rare and fleeting, and they boast that without the wedding garment they have approached the kingdom of heaven, although they still lie far off in the lower places. To these I say again what I have often said: that no one suddenly becomes the highest, and unless a monk has subdued all his animal passions through the mortification of the flesh, he does not reach the very high mountain of divine contemplation. For whoever, placed in a monastery under authority and obedience, follows his own will against monastic discipline, is not worthy to be heard by the Lord in his prayers.

But those of whom we speak are great and wise in their own eyes: loving their own will, they murmur at everything, and whatever they themselves have not arranged they think badly done. They examine the actions of their brothers with curious inquiry, but are content to neglect discerning their own negligences. In their morals they are lax and disorderly; in words frivolous and loud; unwilling toward obedience, and always slow in good works and full of sloth. They neglect the common exercises of the order and attend to private ones according to their own will. And as long as they remain in their own rashness, they do not rise up to the true love of God. Let monks who love their own will hear what the Lord says: “I came not to do my own will, but that of the Father who sent me.” Brothers, attend to this teacher; unless you live in imitation of him, you will pray without fruit. For the Lord gladly hears the prayer of him who keeps his commandments in truth. But he who transgresses the commandment of God, who follows with desire the pleasures of the flesh and the consolations of the world, his prayer is not heard.

For God, just and upright, is believed then gladly to hear your prayer when a good mind and a good life make supplication together. But if a man’s conduct displeases God, how will his prayer please him? For God is spirit, and a man who walks not in the spirit but in the flesh certainly does not approach God at all. Therefore you, monk, who do not walk according to the purity of the Gospel and the Rule, who love your own will and not God’s, know that you will not feel the efficacy of your prayers unless you first amend your life. For unless your conduct agrees with your prayer, you will not obtain what you ask. Then you cry to the Lord with one great voice, when even with the tongue silent you cry out with good works. For good prayers and evil conduct merit nothing good.
