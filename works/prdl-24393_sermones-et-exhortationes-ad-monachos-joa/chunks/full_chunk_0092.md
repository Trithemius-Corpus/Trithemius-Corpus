*On the true humility of monks.*

Humility eagerly obeys the commandments of God with a peaceful will; pride struggles against the precepts of the Most High. Humility heaps up the merits of everlasting happiness; pride, however, prepares for itself the most grievous torments of hell. Humility affords security at the point of death; but pride brings horrible fear and calamity to the dying man. Humility frees the soul of its worshipper from every distress in the dread judgment of God; but pride accuses before the Judge all the haughty whom it deceived in the world. Humility will place the servants of God at the Judge’s right hand; but pride will set those to be damned on the left. Humility will plead the cause for its own and will prevail in the judgment; but pride, utterly confounded, will hand over the haughty to the devil. Humility will repay its friends with the reward of eternal happiness; but pride will give the perpetual torments of damnation to all the haughty. Humility will be crowned in heaven without end; but pride in the lower world will suffer forever with the demons an inestimable torment. Humility, crowned, will reign with Christ in heaven; and pride, despised, will be tortured with the devil in punishments. Humility, [glad?] and glorious, will sit forever at the right hand of God; but pride, sad and ignominious, will lie without end in the torments of hell.

Then the humble will exult in glory, and all the proud, because of exceeding pain, will cry out in punishment for everlasting ages. There the humble will stand in the sight of God and of all the saints in great joy and everlasting exultation, receiving, for the voluntary poverty of this life, unfading riches; for meekness, a kingdom; for sadness, joy; for love of God, dominion; for contempt of the world and voluntary chastening of the flesh, heaven. But when the proud and arrogant, placed in hell, see the great glory of the humble whom they despised in the world, they will marvel at so sudden a change of the saints, and they will be troubled with horrible fear, groaning under the very great anguish of spirit and saying in the fury of pride: “These are the poor and humble monks, formerly clothed in vile garments, separated from men, abstaining from the eating of meats and from all uncleanness of the flesh, whom we once held in derision and as a likeness of reproach. Senseless, we judged their life madness and their end without honor. Behold how they have been counted among the sons of God, and their lot is among the saints.

Therefore we have strayed from the way of truth, and the light of justice has not shone upon us, and the sun of understanding has not risen for us. What now does pride profit us, by which, then puffed up, we despised the humble? And what does the boastfulness of riches possessed in the world confer on us? All things have passed away, and we, because of obstinacy, have been counted among the demons, since we always held humility in hatred; and without end we suffer inestimable torments. But the humble will live forever, and their reward is with the Lord, and the thought of them is with the Most High.”

Always think on these things beforehand, my brothers, since without doubt they will be so; and therefore seek true humility of mind, so that you may merit to obtain the rewards of eternal life. Amen.

*On the true obedience of monks: Homily 20.*

If the humility of monks is true and perfect, it brings forth the whole fullness of the holy virtues. But if it lacks the splendor of virtues, there has never been true humility in the mind. For first of all it brings forth true obedience, and a profound subjection in all things for love of God. For the monk who in truth humbly sojourns shows himself in all things an eager disciple of our Savior Jesus Christ; for love of him, content with every advantage and extremity, he rejoices that he is subject to his abbot, obedient in all things. True [unclear] of the monk is a great virtue, which is rendered to the commands of superiors not through servile fear but through the affection of inward charity; which, persevering even unto death not from fear of punishment but from love of justice, performs good with cheerfulness of mind. There are two very beautiful wings of the mind by which the monk flies from earth to heaven; one of them is called humility, the other is marked as the obedience of subjection. The monk who has not been adorned with these wings has utterly lost both the name and the merit of his dignity.
