*Letter.*

John Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of Sponheim, though unworthy, to the reverend father Lord Johannes de Colenhausen, abbot of Seligenstadt and formerly of Sponheim, my most dear predecessor: with prayers, a spirit ready and prepared to give satisfaction.

Recently, venerable teacher, I had sent to you my first book of exhortations to monks, containing twenty-five homilies. After I understood that it had been approved by your examination, I judged that this second book also, which I now send, should confidently be inscribed to your name. In the former I collected, for the instruction of the brothers, twenty-five homilies which I had delivered before them; in this one I have arranged the sermons in number according to the order in which they were spoken in their presence. I do not fear to undergo the examination of your paternity, since I have always had you as a gentle teacher, and long ago found you to be a sincere lover of truth. For you are vigorous in intellect, both gentle and profound, able easily to distinguish between true and false, and, where necessity requires, to amend everything sufficiently. Therefore I pray that, according to the gentleness innate in you, you may patiently bear the rudeness of my talent, for I am both a little child in knowledge of the Scriptures and a novice in the order of prelates. Exercise in the Scriptures will give me practice, as I trust, and continued solicitude will give progress.

The occupation of household business and the poverty of the place very often draw me back from salutary study, and carry my mind elsewhere against the purpose of my will. Only on feast days and Sundays is leisure granted me for writing; on these, out of reverence for God, I am accustomed to admit no cases of the country people. Sometimes also, after the customary chanting of matins, I am accustomed to spend what remains of the night on the study of letters, and, as far as I can, to work by lamplight on something for the instruction of my brothers. For I desire in all things to follow your footsteps, and by teaching to be solicitous for what is praiseworthy. I submit these sermons to your examination, and with humble supplication I beg that the file of your mastery may correct them. I have collected a few out of many into order, since I neither committed them all to writing, nor did an excessive extension of the book seem to me either necessary or useful. For on feast days, when we have respite from the tumult of worldly business, we devote ourselves to reading according to the rule of the Scriptures. And lest I should seem to occupy the place of shepherd in vain, what I have worked out by night I am accustomed after none, in place of the regular study, to say and to recite from a small sheet before the brothers in chapter. For I consider that this solicitude of mine not only profits the brothers for edification, but also conduces to my skill in composition. Farewell, venerable teacher, and pray for us. From Sponheim, on the twelfth day of the month of September, in the year of the Christians one thousand four hundred eighty-six.
