Every vessel that is filled with vinegar is spoiled; and a monk who is neglected in the novitiate, when he grows old, is not amended. Cursed is the old man by whose evil conduct young men perish, since one who has left bad footprints for those who follow is like one who with his own hand has cast a blind man headlong into a pit. Before the inward judge, each man is worthy of as many deaths as the innocents who have perished through his depraved example.

Young man, seek an old man as the guide of your life: not one who has completed many years in his lax and lukewarm conduct, but one whom the fear of the Lord and knowledge of the divine Scriptures commend. Better is the company of a young monk who fears the Lord than the fellowship of an old teacher whose life does not have the light of divine love and of intact regular observance. An old man disordered in his morals, lacking cleanness of mind and devoid of discipline, is like one injured in the shin, led lame to the grave. An old and lax monk is like a foolish man who, not knowing the end, is seized with robbers at a drinking banquet. An elder without maturity is like a little pipe of elderwood crushed under the feet of those at play.

Vainly does he boast that he has been a monk for many days, who has not perceived the beginning of inward conduct. He who loves the cleanness of his heart keeps himself at all times from the company of undisciplined brothers. As a stain of pitch clinging to the garments of those dancing saddens virgins, so a good young man blushes at the foul words of elders. Wanton speech is not that of a man who fears God, since from the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks; and he who utters foul words is not clean before the Most High. If sinners begin to hate you because of the fear of God, do not be saddened, because unless you displease the wicked you will not attain the perfection of the good. For to displease the wicked because of justice is praiseworthy, and a manifest indication of divine love. If you love your soul, flee the counsels of the undisciplined, and do not have fellowship with mockers. Walk with the good, and let him who fears the Lord be your counselor.

*On monks sent on the road. Homily 12*

Folio 27.

It belongs to monks to flee the world and, for love of solitude, to avoid crowds of people at all times as far as they can. A monk is so called from solitude, just as one is named from unity. He cannot be a monk who seeks the throng of peoples; and he who delights in cities has not yet tasted monastic sweetness. If you are a monk, you must not leave the solitude of the monastery without reasonable cause; and you who are bound to silence must not take delight in the conversations of worldly people.

Whoever loves wandering courses in the city has abandoned his conscience; and he who is often outside his monastery does not keep the purity of inward progress. He who goes about the houses of his parents and delights in the feasts of his neighbors does not regard himself as a monk; and he who goes to the banquets of drinkers kills his soul. He who sleeps in the house of his kinsman seeks comfort; and he who avoids the assemblies of religious men follows a pernicious liberty. A monk in the house of his father and mother is like a lamb in the midst of wolves, and like a hare surrounded by the hounds of hunters.

If you are a monk, if a servant and soldier of God, what are you doing in the house of your parents? Or what are you seeking in the midst of peoples? A monk remaining long outside the monastery among secular people is like a man who has buried his only-begotten son beneath the depth of waters. For just as there is no life for fish on dry land, so there is no salvation of soul for monks in the world. Outside the river the fish dies; and the purity of a monk’s conscience is quickly defiled outside the monastery. The life of monks is in the monastery, and the death of souls in the midst of secular affairs.

It is a great virtue of the soul to flee the crowds of men, or, if it is altogether necessary to be among them, nevertheless to withdraw in mind. We are sometimes compelled by necessity to make a journey through the world; but the purity of conscience is so much the less polluted by meeting people, the less the desire of the mind is drawn away from the secrecy of the cell. Therefore every good monk among us must be warned that, whenever by command of his superior he is sent on the road because of the necessity of some matter, he should never wander abroad in mind.
