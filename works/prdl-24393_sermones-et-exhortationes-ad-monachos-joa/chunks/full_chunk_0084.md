Indeed, when, in the Christian year one thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, Frederick, count palatine and duke of Bavaria, a most Christian man who resided in Simmern, held the corrupt life of the monks in his principality in very great hatred, he reformed three monasteries after expelling the deformed monks, and introduced men very well trained from other monasteries. And first indeed he brought back two monasteries of regular canons of Saint Augustine to the observance of regular discipline: Ravengiersburg, namely, among the [unclear], and Schwabenheim among the [unclear], in the district of the Nahegau. Lastly, in the aforesaid year of the Lord, he also renewed this present monastery of ours, Spanheim, for the reform of the order; and, after removing the abbot together with five monks, he restored in their place four others by observance, over whom he set my predecessor Johannes of Kölnhausen, now serving as abbot in Seligenstadt.

But they found the place oppressed by such poverty and burdened with another’s debt that there was no one who believed it could be restored, since all the annual revenues had either been sold or alienated under title of redemption; nor was anything at all left from which they might live. Present as reporter and witness of the things we say is our brother Heinrich, one of the first four reformers sent from Mainz; from his mouth you will learn everything more fully. And behold, the seventeenth year is now passing since this place began to be restored under the chapter of the reformed union of Bursfelde, and still we are being tried by no small poverty.

I have said these things for this reason, brothers: that you may know that we do not bear alone the necessity we suffer, since those four men who first reformed the monastery endured heavier things; for beans and peas had satisfied their bellies, and a thin drink of pear-cider furnished them drink by measure. The mercy of God called us to this place, that through patience we may overcome the [unclear] of our salvation, so that, according to the counsel of the divine apostle, having scant food and a garment with which to cover ourselves, content and giving thanks to the Lord, we may serve in cheerfulness of mind.

For we are not called to feasts of the flesh, nor to the delights of this world, not to pleasures nor to the comforts of the present life, but to serve as soldiers for the Lord and our emperor Jesus Christ in abstinence from all the delights of this age, in a pure conscience and humility of heart. Attend now therefore to your calling, monks, and do not corrupt the purpose of holiness, if you desire to come to the glory of God.

Woe to you, faithless monk, who, made a despiser of your calling and a transgressor of your vow, live in the monastery not monastically; you who were called to tolerate tribulation patiently for love of God, and inquire about refreshment. For your profession commands you to go humbly content in every vileness and extremity of clothing, and you do not fear to devote effort to curiosity about garments. You have been called to thirst, and you, despising drinking water, delight in drinking wine to satiety, against the monastic canon. You were made a monk to fight bravely against the adversary of your salvation, and you, having cast away from yourself the arms of the virtues, proceed lazy, idle, and naked; indeed, what is more serious, accompanied by a troop of vices. You were invited to keep watch in holy prayers, and you are dissolved by sluggish sleep and base idleness. You were called into the subjection of humble obedience, and you, swollen with pride, despise obeying the commands of the elders. You were called into charity, and you do not fear to carry hatred against your brother in your breast. You were called to weeping and tears, and [unclear] with deep, disordered laughter. The Lord called you to penitence in the monastery, and you, dissolved into wantonness, join sin to sin. You were called to meditate day and night on the law of our Lord Jesus Christ, and you walk after vanity, applying yourself to tales and perishable things. You were called to heaven, and you, loving the world, pursue earthly things. The Lord called you into the meekness of humility, and you proceed full of rancor and pride.
