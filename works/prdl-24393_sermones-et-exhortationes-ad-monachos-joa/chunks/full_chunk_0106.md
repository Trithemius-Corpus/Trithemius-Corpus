For whoever perfectly loves the Lord God from his whole heart, and preserves his conscience clean and pure from every pollution of sin, and always shows sincere charity to his neighbor. But he whom either envy burns or hatred stirs up against his brother, it is clear that he neither truly loves God nor has a conscience pure from sin. Therefore everyone who persecutes his brother with hatred and indignation is neither a true monk nor worthy of the name Christian, but by merit and conduct a servant and companion of demons. Therefore let hatreds depart far from this holy congregation of yours; let there be no destructive contests of contention in your midst; let no zeal of bitterness be found here, let no word of displeasing pride be heard. Let the concord of brotherly unity flourish here continually; let the innocence of Christian purity adorn the consciences of each, so that we may live in this short time in such a way that after death we may be glorified with Christ in eternity.

*Homily XXIV.*

Nothing is so just, nothing so holy, nothing in human actions can be so honorable, as always to love God above all things from the whole heart, and in the whole mind, and with the whole strength, and for love of him to fear neither death nor any kinds of punishments. There is no greater cause of action in us than divine love, because, since God is the author of all things, nothing is loved more justly than he; and since he is the greatest of all goods, nothing is loved more fruitfully than he. God first loved us: we are lowest, he greatest; we little, he almighty; we weak. Therefore, my brothers, let us love the Lord God with our whole heart, whole soul, and whole strength, by whom we are most sweetly loved, who not only made us in his image and likeness, but, which is a sign of the greatest love, mercifully called us to the kingdom of eternal happiness through the teaching and death of his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. No fleshly mother ever loved her only-begotten son so tenderly, sweetly, and strongly as almighty God loved us, who not only willed to become man for our instruction, but also deigned to undergo death in the flesh he had assumed for our salvation. No greater love ever existed, if we rightly weigh who it was who endured such things for us. For out of love for us, free from the debt of death, he voluntarily dies; and he who always remained immune from every sin, innocent and just, willingly is condemned for our sins. By the most just reason, therefore, we are commanded to love the Lord our God with our whole heart: he who, when we did not exist, made us from his goodness alone, and when we had perished, mercifully freed us. Love of God is the greatest of all virtues, because whatever good, whatever virtue is done in this world, if it is not done from charity toward God, is emptied of reward. Charity toward God purifies the conscience from every fault, since he who truly loves the Lord his God from his whole soul and in his whole heart and with all his strength undoubtedly grants no place in his conscience to vices. For the heart which the love of God occupies, no uncleanness of vices enters. The love of God is an unfailing treasure: whoever possesses it is richer than all the wealthy of the world; whoever lacks it is more wretched than all the poor. Hence the holy apostle of Christ says: To those who love God, all things work together for good, since nothing is lacking to those who fear him. Happy is the man in whose heart no bond of earthly affection dwells except the love of Christ, who sighs for the memory of no human being, in whose conscience purity resides and divine love always burns most ardently. Love him, O my brothers, who for love of you descended into the Virgin's womb; always carry him in your hearts, who by his death opened heaven for you. Let the love of Christ be in you at all times, brothers, as strong as death, so that your life may be conformed to Christ's teaching, and, if at any time it should be necessary to die for love of him, you may not fear, just as he first deigned calmly and kindly to undergo death for us. For the love of Christ makes a monk near to God, and he who is always fervent in the charity of the Lord will obtain the fellowship of the angels.
