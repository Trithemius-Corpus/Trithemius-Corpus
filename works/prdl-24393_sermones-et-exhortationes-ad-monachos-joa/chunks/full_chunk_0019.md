*Homily IV.*
*Fol. X.*

...he has bestowed upon the human race, when by reading through the whole breadth of Scripture we recall them to memory with studious devotion, they will furnish us the greatest material and occasion by which, burning more greatly in the love of God, we may make progress. For what else does the whole sequence of the divine Scriptures contain than a certain right order of the good things which God has either already bestowed on men or has promised that he will also give? Or what is there that can so strengthen us for the endurance of evils and render us willingly patient, as if we read in the Gospel that the Son of God suffered innocently for us, and after him the victories of the holy martyrs?

For sacred Scripture contains the things necessary for us for saving knowledge, and it admonishes us what, as soldiers in this pilgrimage, we ought usefully to seek, and what also to avoid. Hence again Saint Jerome says: “Our riches are to meditate on the law of God day and night.” Let us therefore prepare strength for ourselves; let us assume perfect might, so that amid the darkness of errors and the confusion of night the light of the knowledge of Christ may appear, and days and nights may have a protecting light, so that, with Scripture as our guide, we may be able to arrive at the holy land.

For what is better than this learning, what discipline sweeter? What is sweeter than our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the true salvation and sweetness of faithful souls? Let Christ be our banquet; let sacred reading be our food and drink. For what can the life of a monk or priest be without knowledge of the Scriptures? We know Christ not by sight, not by hearing, not by touch, but by reading the Scriptures; and the more we advance by reading toward true knowledge, the more we are delighted in the holy Scriptures.

For what is sweeter than to know the will of God and not to be ignorant of the rewards of his promises? For, as Jerome says, the field of the divine word is most rich: it has in itself all delights, and whatever you wish springs from it, just as the Jews report that manna in the desert, when it was eaten, tasted in the mouth of the eater according to his will and desire. Therefore, my brothers, read the divine Scriptures often; through them you can plainly know the will of God and arm yourselves most strongly against the snares of the devil.

For if, according to the apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, he who does not know the Scriptures does not know the power and wisdom of God; and how will he know Christ? Ignorance is of Christ; and he who does not know Christ will not belong to his fellowship, as Saint Christophilus says, because if anyone is ignorant, surely he too will be ignored.

Moreover, the more constant anyone has been in the study of the divine Scriptures, the greater understanding and sweetness he receives from reading them. But it especially befits you, brothers, to turn over the divine Scriptures daily by reading and learning, since both abundant time, free from occupations, is granted to you, and a place sufficiently suitable is offered by the rarity of those who come by.

Therefore I exhort and admonish you, if you love your salvation, always to have the divine readings in your hands, so that all the time which, after the worship of prayer, is permitted to you without labor may be spent on the study of sacred Scripture. Let no day pass, my brothers, on which a monk serving Christ as a soldier does not read something from the sacred volumes that may make him better in his holy purpose. Rather, let him follow the counsel of Saint Jerome, who says: “Let the divine Scriptures always be in your hands and be continually turned over in the mind; nor should you think it enough to store up the commandments of God in memory, unless you strive also to fulfill them in works.”

For God, my brothers, requires two things of us. First, he wishes us to know his most holy will; second, that, once known, we do it. But we cannot know his commandments if we are ignorant of the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Nor does he do the will of God who is ignorant of it, nor has he known it who has not learned it. Learn, therefore, what is the pleasing will of God, that you may do it; do it once known, that you may understand more fully.

For no one understands the will of God better than he who, once he has known it, does it from love. And so, if you desire to become a most learned interpreter of the divine Scriptures, learn to begin the pursuit of knowledge not from words but in holy works. For then you will comprehend the rich knowledge of sacred reading, if you have kept your heart clean from every sin, and, struck and pierced in holy compunction in the love of God, you continually turn over Scripture.
