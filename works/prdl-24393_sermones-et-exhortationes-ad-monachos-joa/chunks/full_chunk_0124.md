Fourth, what we have judged necessary for you for regular observance is that you keep watch in the guarding of the outward senses, because no one is able to guard well the city of his own heart who disdains to close the gates. Love silence, and flee every tumult of this world. Apply holy prudence to all that you do, and love the secrecy of quiet. For a monk who loves public life and wandering excursions was never a true lover of observance.

Fifth, we admonish you to flee idleness, destructive to the soul, because this pestiferous poison nourishes the gravest vices and always begets unclean thoughts. Therefore whoever, as a lover of virtues, loves his own salvation, whoever desires to arrive at eternal happiness, let him flee idleness as from the face of the devil, if he desires to avoid sin.

Sixth, for the purity of regular observance there is required, by necessity of salvation, that the renunciation of property be kept inviolate by all until death. For a monk having private possessions prepares for himself a way to hell. But whoever receives and disburses money in the monastery, let him be a public minister; let no private person dare to presume this. The possession of riches does not befit monks, who are commanded to imitate the Lord Jesus in poverty of spirit. Let us hear the holy apostle of Christ saying: Having food and clothing, with these we are content. By God’s mercy we provide you with necessities: not as much as the desire of disordered pleasure demands, but as much as nature, content with little, requires. And so, just as it is not lawful for you to have anything without the abbot’s consent, so, since we minister all things, not even what is necessary [unclear].

The seventh thing that makes a monk is humble obedience to commands. For he cannot be a true monk who despises obedience and denies to his superiors the humble subjection which he promised. This disease of disobedience arises from elation and pride of mind, just as from evil matter pus is generated in ulcers. But what does Scripture say? Pride is hateful before God and men, and is the beginning of every transgression. The warfare of monks is the imitation of Christ, who obeyed the Father even unto death.

The eighth place is adorned by the most beautiful virtue, chastity; when this is despised, it is now necessary that all integrity of regular observance suffer shipwreck. For a monk, by the necessity of his salvation, is commanded to live chastely until death and to abstain perpetually from every venereal pollution. For let these be the three principal vows of monastic conversion: against the observance of which to live is certain damnation for monks. For the profession of monks, under sentence of eternal damnation, commands them to live without property, under obedience to a pastor, and in perpetual chastity. If one of these three is taken away, monastic integrity has been violated, and transgression condemns the one who despises it.

In the ninth place, therefore, we admonish every person dedicated to God that he always beware his own will as a venomous serpent, because unless he has held it suspect at all times, he easily falls into the pit of damnation. Attend to the rule, which permits monks neither the will of the body nor that of their own will. No one sins unwillingly. If you have perfectly submitted your will to God and to your abbot in all things, you will easily avoid sin.

The tenth thing, my dearest brothers, that can preserve in you the integrity of regular observance is that in all adversities and contrary things you have most steadfast patience for the love of God. For patience is a great virtue, which by another name we call endurance, without which no one will come forth victor over his temptations. Therefore the Lord said in the holy Gospel: In your patience you shall possess your souls. What is patience, if not to bear with equanimity both the injuries done by one’s own people and all other adversities whatever, out of reverence and love for God? Believe me, brothers, I speak the sentence of the Apostle: patience is very necessary for you, so that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise. Let the Lord Jesus be for you the living example of patience, whom you should follow with all your strength.

Eleventh, my brothers, take care that your hearts not be weighed down in gluttony, because a monk ought not to eat and drink to satiety, but, from love of abstinence, always to cease with a certain appetite and hunger. For the wise man says: He who loves feasts will be in want, and he who loves wine and rich foods will not be made rich. Love fasting; cherish abstinence; preserve continence and frugality in all things. These are the weapons of cloistered persons; these are the insignia of the spiritual warfare of monks.
