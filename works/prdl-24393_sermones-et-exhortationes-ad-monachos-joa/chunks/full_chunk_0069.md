Woe to our times, in which already very many clerics and monks have shamefully cast away the virtue of abstinence, who ought to have shown the people of God the way of salvation no less by example than by word. Given over to gluttonies, and immersed in continual drinkings and pleasures, contrary to all clerical propriety, they hasten to go before [unclear] into the perdition of eternal death. Scripture says: The beginning of a man’s life is bread and water. But now our monks and cloistered men buy for themselves at great cost whatever is sold more dearly, whatever they find more sumptuous; by gorging their bellies they bury it, and through intemperance corrupt soul and body alike. Amid banquets they debate fasting, and when they have become drunk they praise temperance. There each one boasts that he is victorious over vice, although he has already been conquered by gluttony and drunkenness. Our Lord Jesus Christ says in the holy Gospel according to Luke: See that your hearts are not weighed down in excess. It does not befit a man dedicated to God to eat or drink to satiety; nor is it lawful for a monk to eat more than nature requires. Let eating still hunger; let it not induce fullness. Let belches be far away, which satiety is accustomed to produce at feasts; let vomiting and undigested [unclear] not be heard among monks. It is necessary that all of us cloistered men use great moderation of discretion, so that one nourishes the body with foods and drinks in such a way that, serving the spirit, it may be able to live, and not, having grown fat, begin to grow wanton against the command of the mind. Let satiety always be far away, for nothing so inflames the body and stirs the genital members as undigested food and a belly hot with wine or beer. Hence Saint Jerome, admonishing Eustochium concerning temperance, the preserver of all virtues, says in a certain letter: Let the bride of Christ flee wine as poison; for wine and youth itself, more prone to gluttony, are weapons against adolescence. Avarice does not so shake adolescence, nor does pride puff it up, nor does ambition delight it, as do those [unclear] which seem to us innate in pleasure.
