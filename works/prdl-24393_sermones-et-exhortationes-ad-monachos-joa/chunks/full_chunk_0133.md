Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: No saint and just man is without sin; nor yet on that account does he cease to be just or holy, if he holds fast to holiness with affection. But those sins of the just are not to be thought mortal or enormous, but small and venial, without which the life of a man is carried on; for every mortal sin must be far from the just man, if he is to retain the name of justice. For mortal sin separates a man from God, kills the soul, and condemns it with the demons forever. This pernicious kind of mortal sin we can avoid by the grace of God, and by the rectitude of free choice we ought carefully to do so. Therefore we have been made monks, so that, living most cautiously under the rule of saving obedience, we should not fall into the pit of mortal sin. Thus a monk must always keep watch in good works, so that he may live free from mortal crime. But whoever on the way of God feels the flesh troublesome and contrary to him must make it subject to the spirit, and, as it were, obedient through fear, by the memory of punishments.

First, therefore, the monk who loves his own salvation [unclear] the deceitful flesh. For what is the world but a great, deep, and restless sea, in which there are as many creeping things of evils as there are evil desires of men ordered against the good? O world, deceitful, deceiver, and unclean, you shamefully deceive those who cling to you; you plunge all who follow you into the depth of hell. How happy are those who despise you for the love of Christ and, making a useful separation from your vanities, depart from you in time. Whatever we perceive in the world is vain, and every lover of the world is made vain and will soon be corrupted. Think that you will shortly die, for nothing draws men back from sin as frequent and intent meditation on death does. Hence Scripture says: Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever. Think on the necessity of dying: it shakes the mind with fear and restrains it from vice.

The second thing that urges one to flee sin is fear of the judgment to come, in which all men must be presented. No one can escape this dreadful judgment, but we must all appear in it. Two judgments to be feared hang over us, neither of which we shall be able to evade; necessity compels us to appear in each. One is called particular, in which immediately after death the soul is judged according to its merits either to grace or to punishment. The other is named general, which will be at the end of the world, where we shall all be judged publicly by the only-begotten Son of God. Each judgment is exceedingly terrible and greatly to be feared by all mortals. Hence the prophet Joel says: Great is the day of the Lord and very terrible, and who will endure it? Likewise in Ecclesiastes King Solomon says: All things that are done under the sun God will bring into judgment, for every error, whether it be good or evil. Likewise in Isaiah it is read thus: The Lord will come to judgment with the elders of his people and its princes. And in the book of the prophet Amos it is written thus: Woe to those who desire the day of the Lord. What is it to you? For that day of the Lord is darkness and not light. Likewise Zephaniah, prophet of the Lord, foretelling the bitterness of the future judgment, said: The voice of the day of the Lord is bitter; the strong man will be troubled there. That day is a day of wrath, a day of tribulation and distress, a day of calamity and misery, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and whirlwind. In the fire of the zeal of the Lord the whole earth will be devoured, because he will make a consummation with haste for all who dwell on the earth. This day, exceedingly terrible and to be dreaded by all mortals, which you believe will certainly come, always keep in memory, dearest brothers. For the most holy bishop Ambrose says: There is nothing that profits more toward an honorable life than that we believe him to be the future judge, whom neither the hidden things of minds deceive nor dishonorable things delight. For all of us, as Saint Christophilus says, must be made manifest before the tribunal of Christ, so that each may receive the proper things of the body, as he has done, whether good or evil. How greatly that day of the future and terrible judgment is to be feared Saint Jerome the priest indicates, saying: Whether I eat or drink, or do anything else, that voice always seems to sound in my ears: Arise, dead, and come to judgment. As often as I think of the day of judgment, I tremble wholly in heart and body. Hence, if there is any joy in the present life, it must be conducted in such a way that the bitterness of the future judgment never departs from memory.
