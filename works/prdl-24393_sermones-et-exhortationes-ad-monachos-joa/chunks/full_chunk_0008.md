*On the Progress of Spiritual Warfare.*

...patience in poverty itself. For in the Gospel the Lord did not proclaim all poor persons whatsoever blessed and to be given the kingdom of heaven; rather, as you know, he foretold that only the poor in spirit were to be blessed. For contempt of riches is not enough for salvation, unless the renouncer of gold also strives with all his strength, through patience and humility, constantly to imitate the Savior. For it is easier to despise the world than to despise and deny oneself. Yet whoever wishes to imitate the Son of God and attain evangelical perfection must become a despiser not only of the world and temporal things, but also of his very self. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: Unless someone has renounced all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple. And lest we should think this enough for perfection, he again bears witness more openly concerning contempt of oneself, saying: If anyone wishes to follow me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his soul will lose it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake and the Gospel’s will save it. Christ became obedient to the Father even unto death; if you wish to imitate him, as you have promised, it is necessary, brothers, that you deny yourselves to yourselves. For he cannot be a monk who has not first wholly renounced his own will. You who, before God and all his saints, promised to live according to the monastic rule may not hereafter appropriate your own will, since what has once been consecrated to God must no longer be recalled to human uses. For you serve as soldiers not for yourselves but for Christ the King; hence you retain in your own power neither willing nor refusing. For you are dead, as holy [Christophilus?] says, and your life is hidden with Christ in God; but when Christ, your life, shall appear, then you too will appear with him in glory. If you desire to rejoice with him without end, you must now in this time serve strenuously as soldiers for love of him. For no one will be crowned with Christ unless he has persevered in lawful combat to the end, since one who does not know how to persevere can [not?] conquer,

*On the Progress of Spiritual Warfare: Second Homily*

Most beloved brothers, whenever the recollection of that moment comes to mind, when, though I protested and resisted, you appointed me, unworthy as I am, as abbot over you, so often I am shaken wholly within myself by the bitterness of my heart, and I am compelled to shed tears even unwillingly and unawares. For I know that I was not set over you by my own merits; but whether this was done by yours, I do not know. May almighty God grant that this appointment concerning me was not made because of the demerits of either me or you. Why did you will it so, brothers? You raised me up, young, unlearned, and by no means fit, as leader for the warfare that we profess, so that now it is altogether necessary for me both to teach and to learn, when neither perfection of life commends my teaching, nor consideration of age as yet obtains for the shepherd the necessary authority. In these three years now I have learned sufficiently in how many miseries I am entangled, while I alone am compelled to bear the weaknesses of all of you, and I, unhappy man, am supported by no one. Who will free me from the vanities of this world, into which I have been called back, I who entered the monastery in order to escape every care and anxiety of the present life? Since, however, it is necessary to bear the burden placed on my shoulders, I beg you, brothers: diligently extend to me the helping hands of your prayers, so that, by the Lord’s granting, I may deserve to live among you in such a way that I do not transgress the condition of spiritual warfare which the Rule commands us. For we are professed soldiers of the same warfare, and the vow does not distinguish office, since solemn profession at the beginning of this life made the shepherd bound together with the sheep. Hence, when I speak to you about the spiritual warfare which, by God’s mercy, we have professed, my intention is, insofar as the Lord Jesus shall deign to grant, also to shake myself out from the feathers of sloth and lukewarmness toward the fervor of holy newness...
