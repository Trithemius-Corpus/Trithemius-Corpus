*On Avoiding the Company of the Depraved.*

Whomever you see violating the rule of fasting without the elder’s blessing, flee as an enemy of salvation; and [flee] the man who by talkativeness, [unclear] when there is no cause, overturns peace and stirs up quarrels. Flee a rebellious monk, disobedient to his elders, as from the face of a snake, because, filled with the poison of malice, he seethes, and in the pride of his mind has lost his soul. He who does not obey the elder’s command is perverse; and he who, when corrected, is not humbled is an apostate. Let not one who disparages his brother and speaks against him be in conversation with you, for he is far worse than a thief. Him whom you have seen wholly zealous in regular observance you shall love as a father and venerate as a most loving teacher. You may confidently make use of the familiarity of him whose friendship always makes you better in the love of God. Imitate good men with all zeal, and follow the habits of those who live in conformity with monastic perfection. Have as a friend a brother of good will; but from him who is hard-hearted turn away your ears. He who is light-minded and dissolute in morals should be to you like a man who destroys a hedge in a field. Do not be the companion of one who despises the Rule and silence, who does not distinguish places or times, who without the prior’s blessing multiplies words everywhere. Avoid as fuel of lust and a spring of all vices the monk given to wine and drink, who too often exceeds the monastery’s canonical measure. A monk who, by the counsel of his own head, strives against the elder’s command is near perdition, and hastens breathlessly into danger for his soul. Do not share in the works of a stubborn monk, and do not have the friendship of one who, with his own rashness, laughing, speaks against his superiors [unclear]. Be continually in the midst of the disciplined, and let your soul gladly walk with the humble. For he who joins himself to [unclear] men will never be exalted. He who raises his voice in speaking and cries aloud, [unclear] of voice, cannot hide the folly of his heart, and he will glory in his vanity.

The friendship of good men is the firm salvation of the soul, and in the company of the depraved the innocence of the simple is corrupted. The best company is that of those whose fear of God has confirmed examples of good works. Nothing is more precious than a friend who fears God, and nothing more dangerous than a companion who is of depraved will. A brother who fears God with his whole heart will be a wall of strength for his friend; and he who is joined in soundness of mind with such a one is sanctified at all times by the blessing of the Spirit of God. He who walks in the midst of those who fear God is upheld as though by a very strong wall round about him, so that he may not fail. From his words the servant of the Most High will be known, and works of holiness mark out the good monk. No foul, scurrilous, or idle word comes forth from the mouth of one who fears the Lord; and he who is a true lover of Jesus does nothing that offends goodness. In the mouth of a monk who loves God, Christ always resounds; and he who is a true worshipper of God displays the speech of righteousness both in his mouth and in his work. He who fears God is the best counselor, and he who walks according to the canon is a prudent judge of thoughts.

An elder who fears God and has been proved and experienced for a long time in regular observance is the director of youths; and he who has no stain on his garment knows how to be a good instructor of novices. He who is an understanding teacher and expositor of the divine Scriptures, keeping his soul in humility: gladly receive all the words of his mouth as though they were golden, because they have [unclear] and the solidity of future eternal happiness. He who is truly learned has compassion on the weak; and he who loves Jesus encourages the man wavering in soul toward strength. He who walks with a talkative man will not escape contention; and he who delights in lying will never be a friend of wisdom. He who is constantly occupied with external things will not be making progress, and glories without cause that he is a monk. The purpose of holiness is quickly corrupted when a monk wanders too frequently outside his cell with desire. He who abandons the secrecy of his cell without the elder’s command does not strive toward the perfection of the holy purpose. Beautiful is the company of good men who fear God, and he who has become accustomed to dwell with them will not experience confusion. The heart of a young monk is a clean tablet, upon which the conduct of elders is inscribed, never to be erased.
