Fasting detests idleness; it does not love wordiness; it cherishes secrecy; it devotes itself to prayers and tears. Fasting, as Saint Ambrose bears witness, is the death of fault, the destruction of sins, the remedy of salvation, the root of grace, the foundation of chastity. By this step the righteous Moses came to God; by this gift Elijah, before he died, ascended into heaven in a chariot. Those who walk in the Spirit love fasting, and those who profess chastity necessarily flee an abundance of delights. For hunger is a friend to virginity, an enemy to wantonness. Fullness, however, betrays chastity and nourishes wantonness. For fasting extinguishes the burning arrows of the devil; the urgency of prayer restrains them; and the continuation of vigils in the love of Christ repels all the snares of demons. Hence Saint Leo the pope says in a certain sermon: What can be more effective than fasting, by the observance of which we draw near to God and, resisting the devil, overcome [his] alluring enticements? For fasting has always been food for virtue, and abstinence the nourishment of holy chastity. From abstinence, indeed, proceed chaste thoughts; it begets reasonable desires, and also modest counsels; and through voluntary afflictions the flesh dies to concupiscences, while the spirit is renewed in virtues. Let us therefore love fasting, my brothers, for love of virtue, on account of him who did not refuse to undergo death for us; and let us abstain not only from unlawful things but also, as far as can be done, from permitted ones, so that we who in many ways have offended against the commandments of salvation may, as recompense of satisfaction, henceforth restrain ourselves from every pleasure. Let us fast from every sin; let us take no food of malice; let us devour no banquets of pleasure, nor be made drunk with the wine of luxury. Let us fast from all perversities; let us abstain from evil works, and let us not speak harmful things; and let us always beware of hurtful and perverse thoughts, which separate us from God. Let us always keep most zealous watch in prayers to God, knowing that he whom we invoke with prayers is everywhere most present to us. Let his praise always be in our mouth; and since we cannot praise his magnificence in speech, let us adore him in the heart with the desires of a most chaste love. For then our fasts are pleasing to him, when they have been adorned with holy works and continual prayers. For the prayer of the righteous man, as sacred Scripture bears witness, penetrates the heavens, and it becomes the more effective, the more it is found adorned with charity and fasting. Let us join to it, as it were, two wings, fasting and charity, and it will obtain for us from the Lord whatever conducive to salvation we shall have asked.

*On the Lenten fast of monks: Homily XVI.*

Because, by the bounty of our Lord Jesus Christ, dearly beloved brothers, we have begun the Lenten fast, reason demands that we say something briefly about its observance. For although you have many writings of the holy fathers on this most sacred exercise of fasts, nevertheless, because I know by experience that my sermons among you are not wholly fruitless, I gladly impart to your edification what the Lord has granted. For, as blessed Jerome bears witness, the act of the living voice has within it a certain hidden operation, which in Greek speech he more elegantly called energy; and the discourse of a teacher, uttered by the mouth, is stronger for persuading. But I, set apart in your midst only by the name of pastor, confess that I am a young man, unlearned and lacking both the experience and the discretion necessary for the governance of souls; whence it happens that I speak with a certain blush and shame, a young man among elders, an ignorant man among the learned, a disciple among masters. Therefore I beg that none of you despise the simplicity of my speech; let no one take it ill that I speak, because even if I am not fit for speaking, nevertheless by reason of the office of pastoral care entrusted to me I confess that before God I am bound to admonish you. Your charity gives me boldness to speak, and the readier you show yourselves to hear, the more you move me to speak. Nor will the labor be burdensome to me, however great it may seem, by which the salvation of your souls is perfected. Help me with your prayers, that the Lord Jesus may speak in my mouth, and may grant strength to my poor little talent to bring forth in your midst whatever can produce in you an increase of holy fervor.
