*All things. XVIII. Fol. XLIII.*

Promise. Daily there are adversities at hand for us by which we may be exercised; therefore, just as a cause for endurance is never lacking, so, I pray, may necessary patience always be present. We are oppressed by want and poverty of necessary things; we are burdened by traffic with the weak flesh; besides, the world tempts us in order to entice us; the devil tempts us in order to seduce us. Among so many adversaries of our salvation, it is necessary, brothers, that we keep watch in all patience and humility, since in this pilgrimage, as long as we live, we are placed, so to speak, in the midst of evils. Our poverty ought to suffice us, because it does not belong to monastic religion to be exalted in temporal things, but rather to be cast down, so that monks, poor in imitation of Christ, who was made poor for us, may deserve to glory with him forever in heaven.

What if we see certain wicked men and sinners in the world abounding in riches? If good and just men are rich in this world, provision is made by God’s providence not for their strength but for their weakness, so that by benefits they may be called forth to better things. “I was young,” says the prophet, “and have grown old, and I have not seen the just man forsaken, nor his seed begging.” “Cast,” he says, “your thought upon the Lord, and he himself will nourish you; he will not forever give wavering to the just.” The Lord Jesus knows what is fitting for each one of his little servants for salvation, and therefore he dispenses the gifts of the present life unequally according to the capacity of individuals. For all things are not fitting for all; therefore, among the just in this world, with respect to temporal goods, some are poor, while others are wealthy. Concerning the poor the Lord Jesus says in the holy Gospel: “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” And through the prophet he speaks, saying: “Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.” For it does not conduce to eternal salvation for all to possess the riches of this world; rather, it is expedient for some to be poor and for others to be wealthy, according as the Lord of all has provided what is more useful for each.

*Concerning just and good rich men.* The blessing of the Lord makes men rich, and affliction will not be joined to it. But if the wicked and reprobate abound in riches in this age, this ought not move the good to indignation; rather let them fear him who leaves no good unrewarded and no evil unpunished. For the wicked, whom he cannot reward in heaven because of their iniquity, he rewards in this life for their good deeds; and for the good, whom he nourishes here in poverty, he preserves eternal rewards in heaven for the merit of justice. But just as not all the poor in this world are just and good, so not all those rich in temporal goods in this life are wicked. The reward of the wicked on earth should rightly be borne without indignation, since not love of God but justice has willed it to be present. Hence, just as the wicked have nothing in heaven, so you who have professed monastic poverty ought to have nothing on earth; but in hope of that good toward which you strive, whatever adversity befalls you on the way, you ought to bear it most patiently. For, as Paul, the holy lover of Christ, says: “The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that will be revealed in us.” Let those who are rich in Christ join mercy to humility; but those who are poor, let them take patience with innocence for the love of God. For the soldier of Christ is neither cast down by poverty nor lifted up by riches, but, standing firm in himself, with tranquil spirit he serves the King of heaven and cares nothing for transitory things. Thus the true monk is neither exalted by praises nor broken by reproach; he is not swollen by an abundance of necessary things, nor contracted by poverty; he despises both prosperity and adversity alike, because, as the prophet had said, “the sun shall not burn him by day, nor the moon by night.”

But we, dearest brothers, let us make a virtue and a good will out of necessity, and out of the disadvantage of poverty, which we must necessarily endure, let us acquire for ourselves a great and useful patrimony of goods; this can at last be done if, for the love of God, we bear this very want manfully and with tranquil spirit. The deformed conduct of our predecessors brought upon this monastery the poverty that we suffer; but the mercy of almighty God, if we have kept his commandments with a perfect heart, will without doubt relieve it in time.
