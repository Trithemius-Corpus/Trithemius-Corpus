*On the True Penitence of Monks.*

... let us invoke [him] with supplications. Let us preserve charity inviolate in all things, because it is charity that covers a multitude of sins. Each day the good soldier of Christ, who desires to be a faithful soldier to his Lord, should examine himself by scrutiny at least once, and should consider more carefully how much on that day he has advanced or fallen back in holy desire. Whenever he finds that he has sinned in anything, let him at once do penance, and with tears ask mercy from the Lord. Let him not wait for the doubtful morrow: salvation is for today; and he who can immediately find pardon should by no means introduce delay. Let him at once grieve in his heart that he has sinned against the Lord; let him also renew his holy purpose with new fervor; and let him judge that he has utterly lost that day on which he has not become better by making progress.

Prayer should always be on the monk’s lips; continual compunction should be in his heart; tears of devotion should continually flow from his eyes; and the exercises of inward love should never depart from his memory. Let the most bitter remembrance of the Lord’s Passion often be in the mind of the cloistered man; and let all the prayers that he makes to the Father be [unclear] in the power of the blood and wounds of our Lord, and let either prayer be offered to God or meditation on his words be turned over in the mind. Let the devout monk turn away from evil and do the good that he can, with cheerfulness and discretion of the Spirit of God; because, as has been said, for doing penance it is not enough to change one’s ways for the better and withdraw from evil deeds, unless one also strives to adorn one’s conscience with good works.

Therefore, my brothers, as you know, four things are required for true penitence. The first is true contrition for sins, which is sorrow of heart voluntarily assumed in detestation of the evils that one has committed. Of this Scripture says: “A contrite and humbled heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.” And again: “Those who sow in tears” [unclear]. The third [unclear] is the sorrow of contrition: the greater it is, the more fruitful penitence will be. For true contrition is that which both sorrow of mind commends and continual cleansing by tears bears witness to. Great is the usefulness of tears, because there is no sin so grave that contrition, accompanied by tears, does not wipe it away.

Hence the holy doctor Augustine says: “O humble tear, how powerful and virtuous you are, because yours is the kingdom, yours is the power. You do not fear the gaze of the judge; you impose silence on accusing enemies; you alone enter before the king, but you do not depart alone; you conquer the unconquerable and bind the omnipotent.” He likewise says: “The holier anyone is, and the more full of holy desire, the more abundant is his weeping in prayer.” True compunction has sorrow; sorrow is shown by tears; and penitence is adorned by these as by certain ornaments. True compunction makes the soul shudder at sin, and when it has begun from fear, increased by its own advances, it gradually rises into love, whose virtue is great and whose power is truly admirable; before it every enormity of sins soon yields. For whoever true compunction of heart has made to supplicate, the merciful Lord soon hears him.

O happy tears, O saving affliction and truly sweet bitterness, [unclear], through which the compunct soul rises to the mercy of God. For, as the holy [unclear], [unclear] is renewed. The second thing that pertains to the truth of penitence is a firm purpose of amendment, because whoever has the word of confession in his mouth and does not have in his heart the purpose of amendment is either deceitful or vain. But whoever has it in the heart and not in the mouth is either proud or timid, and in no way fit for true penitence. For Scripture says: “With the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.” This judgment, lovers of Christ, especially [holds] at the time when the statute of the Church decreed confession necessary for salvation. The third, therefore, is confession of the mouth for the truth of penitence; contrition of heart alone, especially from that [unclear], is sufficient neither [without] the purpose of future amendment. But what this saving confession ought to be, I know that none of you is ignorant.
