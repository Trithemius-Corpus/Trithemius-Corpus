The soul which has God present within itself through love despises all the comforts of the flesh; it longs to come to its Lord through hardships and rough places. It does not love the riches of this world; it spurns transitory honors and dignities; it strongly contemns the pleasures of the flesh. It meditates always on the law of the Lord and never forgets the most bitter passion of Jesus.

Who among you, brothers, does these things? Who detests all the little consolations of this age, who thinks of nothing else, seeks nothing, desires nothing except the love of God? Whoever shall have attained this sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, if he perseveres to the end of life, shall be saved.

The eighth indication of a good course, and a true sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, we receive when we humbly conform our wills in all things to the divine ordinances. This degree of spiritual progress separates the monk from all the cares of the world and calls him wholly to inward things. For his will is subjected to the divine will with the affection of a peaceful mind, and is separated far from all desires which war against the spirit.

Whoever has received this gift of the grace of the Holy Spirit, let him first give thanks with humility, and not vainly exalt himself on account of the gift; but the more he has advanced, so much the more let him learn to humble himself in all things. For often a man deserves to lose the grace of God when he neglects to preserve it in the vessel of true humility. Therefore almighty God sometimes withdraws the grace of sensible devotion from those who are advancing for a short time, so that humility may persevere in the mind and the fervor of desire may burn more strongly to seek further. But since there are many causes in human beings by which the grace of God is sometimes withdrawn, it will be worth the effort for them to be stated in order.

First, indeed, the grace of God is withdrawn from a person because of sin, when the conscience is stained by the foulness of vices. For our God, as Scripture says, is jealous: either he is loved alone, or he is not loved with any companion. Therefore whenever the mind, by desiring in a disordered way, turns back either to itself or to something else, it is removed, alienated from its principle, which is God; whence it deserves to lose the grace which it had, because, by consenting to sin, it was unwilling to guard it inviolate. Hence the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, exhorts us not to receive the grace of God in vain; for then indeed it is received fruitlessly and in vain, when the mind of the recipient is made unworthy by the filth of vices.

The little vessel into which we receive the grace of God is our conscience; when it is cleansed from vices and adorned with holy virtues, it becomes a worthy dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and produces great progress in us. But if the vessel of our conscience has been defiled by the rottenness of sin, at once the Spirit of God withdraws with all the gifts of grace. Is not our Lord Jesus Christ the lover and faithful spouse of the soul, which he purchased for himself by the outpouring of his own blood? With what face, with what rashness, O man, can you despise this so great lover and savior of yours, and love anything else whatsoever in this world? God, who is your creator, loves you supremely; by his death he delivered you from eternal destruction. What will you repay him, O mortal man, from whom you have received such great benefits? He asks nothing from you, seeks nothing, demands nothing, except gratitude, affection, and love. Guard innocence and purity of conscience, lest you lose the impulse of grace.

The second cause, then, on account of which grace is withdrawn from a person is called the pride of ingratitude. But God, as Scripture testifies, resists the proud, while to the humble he gives grace. For by the just judgment of God the grace of the Holy Spirit is lost whenever the one who has it is lifted up into pride. For everyone who humbles himself will be exalted, and he who exalts himself will be humbled. Hence again almighty God says: Upon whom shall my spirit rest, unless upon the humble and quiet man, and the one trembling at my words? The more anyone humbles himself in true purity of conscience for the love of Christ, the more fit he will be to receive the grace of the Holy Spirit. But whenever the mind rises up in pride, deservedly the spirit of devotion is taken away from it as from an unclean vessel.

Therefore, my brothers, let no one grow proud because of the grace of the Holy Spirit which he has received; let no one vainly exalt himself, because unless we have been humble, we shall by no means be able to be sharers in the mercies of the Lord. For as the holy lover of Christ Paul says: he who thinks himself to be something when he is nothing deceives himself.
