... by your return afterward into the land of your temporal birth. I give you thanks, most sweet Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for all your labors, journeys, and weariness; for hunger, thirst, cold, nakedness, poverty, and want; and for the necessities, injuries, insults, mockeries, and slanders which throughout your whole life you willingly, not under compulsion, endured for us. And as often as you moved your hand to work, your foot to walk, or as often as you opened your mouth to speak, so often I bless you, praise you, glorify you, humbly adore you, and with all the powers of my heart always give thanks to you, mediator of God and men, most sweet Jesus Christ.

I give you thanks, Lord Jesus Christ, most merciful king of the heavens, for your most holy teaching, for the establishment of your faith and of the evangelical sacraments, for the calling of the nations, for the justification of sinners, and for all your wondrous acts and virtues, and for all your benefits, which either you have bestowed upon me in particular or upon all others together with me in general.

I give you thanks, Lord Jesus Christ, most benign savior, for your charity which you had toward the human race, and for your whole most bitter passion, and all the pains which you endured on account of us wretched ones. I give you thanks, Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for the grief and sorrow of your most innocent heart which you endured for us at the time of your impending passion, when you said to your disciples who were following you: “My soul is sorrowful even unto death.” I beseech you, by this sorrow of your heart, O God, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death, and take from me all melancholy and disordered grief, granting me at all times a salutary sorrow for my sins.

I give you thanks for the sadness of your most holy heart, when, placed in agony, you prayed to the Father a second and third time that he would take away from you the chalice of the passion; and, pouring forth sweat in marvelous anguish, you resigned yourself wholly to his will for us. I give you thanks, O king of heaven and earth, for your captivity, for the insults, injuries, and mockeries which you endured throughout the whole time of the passion on account of the salvation of us all.

I give you thanks, O Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you, bound to the column, for the most harsh scourging of your most tender body, and for the shedding of your most sacred blood from the wounds, with immeasurable pain. I adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, crowned with thorns, and I give you thanks for the piercings and pains of your most holy head, for the blows, spittings, and all the mockeries cruelly inflicted upon your majesty by the wicked. I adore you, Christ Jesus, Son of the living God, bearing the very heavy weight of your cross; I adore you, stripped of your garments before the multitude; I adore you, stretched out by hands and feet with iron nails upon the cross and very cruelly fastened to it.

I adore you, Jesus Christ, everlasting king, hanging and dying on the cross for me; and I give thanks for the pains of your wounds, I give thanks for the shedding of your most precious blood, I give thanks for your most innocent death which you willingly endured for our sins and the sins of the whole world. I give thanks to your boundless goodness, because you called me, your own, to the faith; because you caused me to be baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity; and because you deigned to call me away from the vanity of the world, to serve you in true tranquillity of mind, into this holy congregation.

I beseech you by your most holy name, O most sweet Jesus, mediator of God and men; I beseech you by the pain of your wounds and by the merits of your most bitter passion; I beseech you by the shedding of your most holy blood and by the power of your most innocent death: grant me indulgence of all my sins; pour into my heart the sweetness of your most fervent love; give me, with progress in all virtues, perseverance; and have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death, guarding me today and at all times, in your mercy, loving Jesus, from every sin and danger of mind and body. In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

And which of you, brothers, is there who cannot say this short prayer, or one like it if he has a better, once each day? It is not long nor is it wrapped in foreign words, but short and plain, gathering the life and passion of Christ in a compressed summary. A Christian person, and especially one in the cloister, is exceedingly ungrateful to God if he does not at least once in the day recall the Lord’s passion to memory, devoutly rendering acts of thanks. But if to any of you this prayer has seemed too lengthy, in the eighth homily of our exhortations to you we recently set down a shorter one, to which the weary man can have recourse. Above all, take care, I beg you, that among so many benefits of God you not be ungrateful for even one. Let this be done...
