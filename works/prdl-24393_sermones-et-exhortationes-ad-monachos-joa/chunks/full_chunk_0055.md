*Concerning monks sent on the road.*

He who has God as his friend easily disdains human familiarity; and he who speaks with angels takes very little delight in the conversations of mortals. Whenever on the road you see a sign of the Lord's Passion or of the Crucified One, do not pass by unless you say: I give you thanks, Lord Jesus Christ, for the pain of your wounds, for the shedding of your most holy blood, and for your most innocent death, which you endured for us on the altar of the cross; and I beseech you, by your bitter Passion, have mercy on me now and in the dread hour of my death. Repeat this very short prayer often as you go, and never pass by an image of the Crucified One unless you have given thanks to the Savior.

Do not quarrel with the man who walks with you on the road, lest, having returned to the monastery, he falsely say some evil thing against you. He who provokes his companion on a journey to impatience is like one who drives a rabid dog to barking with a blow. The wicked man is overcome by gentleness, and he who is humble of heart calms one stirred up in mind. Among secular men do not pour forth speech, lest perhaps they take occasion to mock you.

The weary ploughman hastens to rest; and the monk, sound in mind, hastening by byways, returns with longing to his cell. Let the word be held in the mouth until reason brings forth the judgment of equity; and speech that can instruct its hearers should not be hidden.

Do not trust the songs of flatterers, for when they have [unclear] the innocent man, they soothe ears with disordered speeches. A monk who is nourished on contentions is like [unclear], and like a man afflicted with spasms who stirs up quarrels under the name of religion. Do not presume to speak before the learned, lest perhaps, when you do not understand the parable, you be laughed at by those standing around. He who recognizes himself as a monk does not use profound speech; and he who teaches little ones makes questions plain.

When you go to men unlike your profession, do not boast in anything; but if you are questioned, answer humbly in few words.
