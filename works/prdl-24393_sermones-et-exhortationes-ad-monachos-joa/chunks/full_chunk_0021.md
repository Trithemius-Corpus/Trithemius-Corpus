Homilies, four.

...those who are unlearned will die in poverty of heart. What is more wretched than an unlearned priest? Even if he does not have the office of preaching, nevertheless by reason of the order he has received he is bound to knowledge of the Scriptures. For God wills that every human being fulfil his holy will; and no one who is ignorant is able to do this. Therefore the Lord first commands that you learn what is pleasing to him, and secondly that you do it. How, then, will you think ignorance of the law excusable, through which you have become transgressors of the first will of God?

Why do you think almighty God handed over the scrolls of his Scriptures to men? Was it so that they should be read by them and understood, or so that, unread, they should be despised? Certainly the Scriptures of God’s commandments were given to mortals so that they should read them, and afterwards so that they should fulfil them in deed. But you, who with deliberate mind wish to be ignorant of God’s Scriptures, how can you do the will of him which they teach? Or in what way will you be able to be doers of the law, who have not first been hearers? For it can happen that someone who knows the will of God either observes it or neglects it; but it cannot happen that someone ignorant of it fulfils it. Indeed, someone will more easily fail to do a good thing that he knows than carry out in deed what he does not know. No one does a good thing that he does not know.

Saint Augustine says of the ignorant in a certain place: “Not everyone who is ignorant is free from fault. For that ignorant man can be excused from punishment who found no way to learn. But pardon cannot be granted to those who, when they had from whom they might learn, did not devote labor to discipline.” Hence also Saint Leo the pope says: “If ignorance seems intolerable in laymen, how much more in those who are in charge; nor is it worthy of excuse or pardon.” Therefore, brothers, do not think yourselves excused before God by ignorance of the divine Scriptures, but rather doubly guilty, since, lest you should have to do God’s will, you desire to be wholly ignorant of it. For a human being ought not to be ignorant of the divine will, since he is placed in the world for this purpose: that through illumination of the understanding and purity of affection he may merit the everlasting enjoyment of the vision of him. For both kinds of knowledge, namely of God and of oneself, are necessary for every mortal for salvation, and he will nowhere find this without knowledge of the Scriptures. Indeed, just as from knowledge of oneself there comes into a man the fear of God, and from knowledge of him love is born, so from ignorance of oneself there proceeds pride, and from neglected knowledge of God contempt, with despair of salvation.

Nothing is more unhappy than an unlearned monk who neglects or despises the study of the holy Scriptures, because he can never abide pure with himself in true tranquillity of heart, but is compelled by his own restlessness, contrary to the integrity of monastic life, to occupy his mind with external and useless things. We see among you certain men who are ignorant of the Scriptures, and who are the less inclined toward discipline. It is indeed shameful not to know what you are commanded to know; more shameful not to learn it; but most shameful to despise it. And there are among you certain men who do not know saving knowledge; there are also those who neglect to learn. What if I add that there are still others who utterly despise knowledge of the divine Scriptures? Certainly I am not lying; you know that what I say is true. For some of you have resisted my studies bitterly, even to the point of envy, cavilling that you would rather have a ploughman set over you than a man of prayer. Did they not also try to draw the visitors into their opinion, wishing by their encouragement to lead me away into renunciation of the Scriptures, so that they might make me a field-worker and a keeper of pigs rather than of souls? But in vain do they try to hinder the divine oracle; under its impulse I have resolved, as long as I live, to set all the honors and riches of the world after the study of the Scriptures. I am your abbot for as long as he who called me shall will it; if one of the two must be chosen, I prefer to give up the abbacy rather than renounce the study of holy Scripture. For I know truly that nothing more salutary can befall a cloistered man in this life than if he begins truly to love the divine Scriptures.

Whoever of you wishes to follow this instruction of mine will have me alike as leader and companion of the journey. But whoever despises me as I learn will nevertheless be compelled to hear me as I teach, even against his will. I have said these things against those who, savoring earthly and perishable things, cast the fear of the Lord away from themselves; who, being utterly ignorant of everything, despise learning. I know the faces of my flock, and I believe that I am not wholly ignorant of the inclinations of the hearts of each of you.
