And nothing hinders a man more from knowledge of himself than the man himself. Those who feed on the pleasures of the flesh, who are lifted up into pride by the vain knowledge of this world, whether by riches or by honors, are not permitted to come to knowledge of themselves. Yet to know many things and neglect oneself is not to be wise, but rather to be mad. It belongs to the accumulation of a graver damnation to learn many things and shamefully to be ignorant of oneself. Let each of you therefore consider what he is, whence he is, and what he is going to be. Man through himself is certainly nothing; but whatever he is, he is from God and for God. What is man but a wayfarer and pilgrim, as it were of one day upon the earth, who today lives in the world [unclear] but tomorrow, snatched away by death, is seen no more? Our whole life, every action, every day and hour, shows how nothing we are: we die by living, we are corrupted by acting; with time running on, I make comparison of us present to death. He spoke truly who compared you to the leaf of a tree. The wind, tearing it from the tree, casts it to the ground; in its place another new one afterward grows up, subject to a similar fall. But what has fallen to the ground is dissolved into the ground and never afterward returns to the tree. Hence a certain wise man of the Greeks says: as is the race of leaves, so too is the race of men. The race of men is very like the race of leaves, because it grows green for a time and is dissolved into the earth; and neither does the leaf return to the tree nor man to life.

Now follows the third thing, which I think very necessary for a monk walking in the way of God: the man who knows himself to be a wayfarer of this life and understands that the activity of life is of a very brief time truly shows himself humble in all things. For why should earth and ashes be proud? He cannot be exalted who truly knows himself. My brothers, we have no cause at all for exaltation and pride, but much and continual cause for humility, since indeed we are earth and a certain compacting of dust, and after a few days are to return again into dust. But because true humility belongs to few, therefore the way of salvation is called narrow and is found by few. By humility one comes to the kingdom, as the Lord says in the holy Gospel: everyone who humbles himself for God’s sake will be exalted, and he who exalts himself will be humbled.
