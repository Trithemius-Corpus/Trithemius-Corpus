He for whom this time, namely seven hours, is not sufficient for rest shows himself a very sluggish and slothful monk. Hence we are admonished to keep watch not only in mind but also in body, since he who disdains to keep watch bodily in the service of God is scarcely able to preserve his body in true purity.

Let us keep watch, therefore, most beloved brothers, especially in these holy days of Lent, in which we have declared war on vices and are firmly prepared to conquer and overcome the ancient enemy, the devil. For you have heard the holy prince of the apostles admonishing in these words: “Brothers, be sober and keep watch.” Why did he give this warning? Because, he says, “your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour; resist him, strong in faith.”

And so, my brothers, let us keep watch as long as we are set in battle, lest at any time, weighed down by drowsiness or idleness, we be overcome by the adversary of our salvation. For as long as we live in the flesh, it is necessary that by watchfulness and chastisement of our body we fight bravely against the enemy. Let the labor of the contest not frighten you, because although our adversary is troublesome, nevertheless he is so weak and feeble that he can overcome no one except one who is willing, and one who has utterly cast off all guard over himself.

But the Lord Jesus Christ is our strong helper and protector, who grants strength to those who fight eagerly and will bestow on the victors an unfading crown in heaven with all his saints. As dust is scattered by the wind, so our adversary the devil, confounded and terrified by the voice of those singing psalms with humble devotion, is driven away. Therefore let none of you, most beloved brothers, act negligently; let no one in the time of prayer grow dull through sluggishness of mind and drowsiness; let no one fear the contest with the devil with disordered fear.

Stand manfully for the perpetual salvation of your souls, fighting against a weak adversary, and hold your own flesh no less suspect than demons. For you know, as not only Scripture testifies but also long experience teaches, that the flesh desires against the spirit; and therefore the monk who is not harmed by the wantonness of his own flesh is rarely overcome by demons in the contest. Walk, therefore, in the Spirit, and you will not fulfill the desires of the flesh.

Let him who is watchful and a strong fighter rouse the weaker brother, who is sluggish in devotion, and restore him as far as he can, both by words and by examples, to the holy fervor of the spirit. Let him who is spiritual among you not despise his disorderly brother, but rather bring him back through humble admonition to the pursuit of piety. For Scripture says: “Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.”

For indeed, if, consenting together in brotherly love toward one another through faith in Jesus Christ, you have always been of one mind, without doubt you will easily conquer your adversary the devil. For where the true charity of Christ makes monks living in a monastery of one mind, there God is worshiped in all holiness; there the invisible presence of holy angels is believed to be present, through whom the rashness of malignant spirits is restrained. In such a congregation of saints God dwells, whom the army of the heavenly kingdom praises together with the voices of the monks singing psalms. A multitude of monks fervent in the love of God is the paradise of the delights of heavenly graces. A city adorned with a multitude of towers is a congregation of monks thinking the same thing in the Lord.

Keep watch, my brothers, I beseech you, keep watch in holy prayers, and especially at that time shake off drowsiness and dullness of mind when you stand in church before the Lord to sing psalms. For if, standing before God in psalmody, you are fervent in prayer, you will without doubt feel his grace present with you. For he is estranged in mind from the purpose of a monk who while praying thinks of something else, and offers his mind to be stirred not by divine things but by transient matters or occupations.
