Let each one of you, as much as he can, strive with the whole strength of his mind to love the Lord his God above all things, because love alone toward God is what will open for you the gate of paradise. For the more ardently you now love God, the greater the glory in which you will be received in the heavenly homeland.

Happy is that monk in whose conscience, besides the love of Christ, there remains no imagining of any occupation; who, waking, thinks of nothing but Christ; sleeping, dreams of Christ; living and dying, most steadfastly sighs after Christ. O how well and securely he dies who has always been solicitous in the exercise of divine love, and at all times zealous in observing the commandments of God. For love of God alone is admitted into the kingdom of heaven, and the more fervent the mind has been in loving. He who loves much receives much; and he who now loves less is also then to receive less in glory. Therefore let each person, as much as he can, love with his whole heart and with his whole soul and with all his strength, because our God is not to be loved lukewarmly or sluggishly, but fervently, wisely, and sweetly.

But love of God grows and is increased by spiritual exercises, just as a burning fire spreads when supplied with wood. For truly, if the wood is withdrawn the fire is extinguished; and if spiritual exercises are interrupted, charity is soon shipwrecked. True love of God is never idle, but at all times is delightfully occupied in those things that nourish and preserve charity. For the lover of his God never forgets his beloved, but always meditates on his presence and beauty; and now, most devout in prayers, he admires him as Jesus speaking to him. The true lover of Christ meditates day and night on the law of the Lord, and does with the greatest diligence all things that he understands to be pleasing to God.

O how sweetly he burns whom the love of God has truly inflamed; how pleasantly he glows whose mind the flame of the Holy Spirit kindles. Blessed is that monk who, full of divine love, withdraws himself entirely from earthly vanities and rests in the shade of heavenly desire. O if only we had love toward God as strong, whole, and vehement as mad and foolish lovers have toward their corruptible mistresses, for desire of whom lovers often grow shamefully mad and fear no scandals of this world. But the love of God is stronger than all things, if it has truly been rooted in us. Whoever truly loves the Lord God always keeps in his memory his most bitter passion, because nothing so causes perfect love of God in the human heart, nothing so brings on true compunction, as the never-interrupted recollection of God’s benefits. Therefore let the written sequence of Christ’s passion always be in your memory, and let this word never depart from your mouth: O Jesus crucified, my love, by the power of your most innocent death have mercy on me and make me your faithful servant.

*On Serenity of Conscience.*

*Homily 24.*

Most beloved brothers, we must keep watch with great solicitude, and must take care with continual meditation that we always guard our conscience, whose testimony we cannot escape, clean from every contagion of sin and unstained. Nothing is more beautiful, nothing more pleasing, nothing finally more precious than a good conscience, when the mind has in itself a manifest testimony of rectitude; it suffers no remorse of destructive unrest. An upright and good conscience is an incredible exultation of the mind and a sure consolation in every adversity. The monk who has obtained the testimony of a good conscience neither refuses to live nor fears the coming of future death. A good conscience is always glad, always joyful, always fearless, and truly tranquil in the peace of Jesus Christ. In a good conscience there is always peace, piety, goodness, light, virtue, honor, joy, gladness, sweetness, and delight without dissipation. Conscience is the treasury of the soul, because whatever is placed in it is reserved as provision for the life to come. A good conscience is the best treasure; if anyone has neglected to have it, he must be judged the most wretched and unhappy of all men. A good conscience is the dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and the earthly paradise of all graces.
