*Of the delight in and study of the divine Scriptures.*

A man polluted with filth is forbidden [the mount]. For so it is read in Exodus: "Beware that you go not up into the mount, nor touch the borders of it. Everyone that toucheth the mount shall die the death. Whether it be beast or man, it shall not live." For a man touches the mount and dies as often as anyone, puffed up with secular learning, is scandalized at the simplicity of the divine Scriptures. But a beast touches the mount and is overwhelmed with stones as often as a man, immersed in the carnal fury of the passions, proceeds to the study of sacred Scripture with a soul unpurged. If, therefore, you wish to unroll the readings of the divine Scriptures wholesomely, if by reading you desire to attain to true knowledge, take care first of all to put off all relation of pride and the rashness of perfidy, and then to purge the chamber of your heart from all the uncleanness of carnal pleasures. For God, as the canonical Scripture says, "resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble." No one proud, puffed up, arrogant, and rash—no one carnal in mind, lustful or restless—is held fit for the study of sacred Scripture, but only the humble, the meek, the gentle, the modest, the chaste, the tranquil in body and mind. Hence Prosper says in an epigram: "The commands of God a mind not quiet cannot search out." Therefore, that religious study may be exercised, the quarrels of the malignant must be cast away. The quiet can in some measure search out the law, if, free from the noise of the world, they are at leisure. That the mind, then, intent on pious studies, may be helped, let divine love conquer the wars of the flesh. For the temple of the Lord grows by sacred silences and is silently built—a house not to fall. With minds cleansed, therefore, and the heart compunct in the love of God and humbled in true contrition, proceed to the study of the Scriptures; for the Lord will not enlighten you unless he has first made you purged from all iniquity. For, as Scripture itself says, "perverse thoughts separate from God; but virtue proved corrects the foolish, since wisdom will not enter into a malevolent soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sins; for the holy spirit of discipline will flee the deceitful, and will withdraw himself from thoughts that are without understanding." And therefore we must cry out with holy David, more frequently, to the Lord: "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within my bowels."

For no one usefully understands the sacred Scriptures of God who does not turn himself with his whole heart to the Lord through [his] power. And therefore, if you wish to understand the divine words both rightly and wholesomely, take care above all, with [your] study, to keep yourselves clean from all pollution of sin according to your strength. Let not the insane folly of certain monks seduce you, I pray—who try to excuse their ignorance with a trite proverb, saying: "What is the knowledge of the Scriptures to us, who have no office of preaching?" [Rather] let us embrace the counsel of the wise man, who says in the book of Proverbs: "He that walks simply walks confidently." "It is enough for us to live simply"—since the servant who knows the will of his lord and does it not shall be beaten with many stripes, but he who did not know [and] committed [things] worthy of stripes shall be beaten with few. O most hateful and abandoned scoundrels, who despise saving knowledge, and love the ignorance of the divine Scriptures rather than the understanding [of them]! Will affected ignorance render you liable to fewer stripes? Or will you therefore, sinning, be guiltless before God, because you are voluntarily ignorant of the commands of God? You shall be tortured with a double penalty: the one, that you despise to know the way of God's commands; the other, that you keep not his words. It is not the simplicity which you allege, but rather a twofold impiety. For if, according to the prophet, "blessed are they that keep the testimonies of the Lord," who would doubt that those are accursed who neither seek out God nor heed his Scriptures, but rather with a hardened mind the more despise [them]?
