For the hope of a longer life has deceived many, and, holding them back from the fervor of their holy purpose, has suddenly plunged them down to the lower world. Therefore complete your service, which necessarily will be ended before long, in such a way that you may not fear to appear before your king when he comes. Always be mindful that you have no abiding city here, nor can you remain long in this mortal life, but death will come quickly and compel you to depart.

For whoever is a true monk and desires to be a faithful soldier of Christ despises all the delights of this world; he always thinks of his Lord as present to him; and, casting away from himself every impediment and every little burden, he cares above all how he may be able to please him. He ought not to be anxious in seeking the necessities of life, since the providence of the shepherd arranges all these things for those subject to him. Let the whole care of the monk be to keep himself clean from this age, so that, free and released from all things, he may seek nothing but God, think of nothing but Jesus crucified for us.

O happy soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised, if faithful, prudent, and vigilant in the love of God he shall remain even to the end. Certainly nothing is happier than a good monk, to whom the Son of God promises himself as a reward; nor anything more laborious, since, placed in the arena of battle, he is daily in peril concerning his salvation; and nothing is stronger than the monk who conquers the world together with the devil, nor anything weaker than one who is overcome by his own flesh.

Therefore, O monk, you must always keep watch against the hidden enemy of your salvation; for he is never more easily conquered than if he is kept far from the entrance of the mind. Hence, if you desire to escape victorious against him, do not allow the engines of his deceit and fraud to be brought nearer to you. First of all, guard your senses, lest death enter through the windows; and take the greatest care against the enemy's snares when for a time he has allowed you to be secure in your devotion. Never forget the admonition of the wise man, who says: If you do not hold yourself constantly in the fear of the Lord, your house, that is, your conscience, will quickly be overthrown. Therefore Solomon said in Proverbs: Blessed is the man who is always fearful, but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil. For the monk who is presumptuous through security is without doubt tending toward destruction.

It is therefore necessary that the soldier of Christ always be prepared against his adversaries, so that as long as he lives in this mortal body he may not trust that he has perfectly overcome them. For the flesh always desires against the spirit; and lest the soul at any time be overcome, as long as one happens to live, it is necessary to fight. Whenever you have conquered your invisible enemy, he rises up again and again, having gathered greater [unclear] for you, so that he who cannot obtain victory by strength may at least bring on despair by persistence. But you, most valiant soldier of Christ, take care that you do not fail in the arena of this very brief combat; take care that before the battle has been completed at the end, you do not lay down the arms of necessary defense. For the Savior says: He who has persevered to the end will be saved.

Do not give rest to your soul as long as it has been granted to you to remain in this miserable pilgrimage, but always do something good, lest the devil ever find you idle. Always remember the admonition of Scripture, saying: Whatever your hand is able to do, work at it earnestly, because neither work nor reason nor wisdom nor knowledge will exist in the lower world, to which you hasten. There is no one who lives forever, but we all die, and like waters we fall away into the earth, which will not return again to their former state. Then there will no longer be time to do good for the merit of eternal happiness; but whatever a man has sown here and now in this present time, then he will receive as recompense. Therefore holy Scripture says again: In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening let not your hand cease. And Saint Paul, admonishing Timothy, says: Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. For no one serving God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself. For even of those who contend in the arena, no one will be crowned unless he has contended lawfully. But everyone who contends in the arena abstains from all things. In another place also the same lover of Christ says: I want you to be without anxiety.
