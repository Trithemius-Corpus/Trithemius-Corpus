But we gird our loins when we keep ourselves pure and unstained in body and mind from every form of lust. The virtue of holy chastity is of great merit before God, and, in proportion to the greatness of the struggle, is to be granted a singular reward in the recompense of the righteous, as the Lord says through the prophet Isaiah: “Thus says the Lord to the eunuchs who keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that I have willed, and hold fast my covenant: I will give them in my house and within my walls a place and a name better than sons and daughters. I will give them an everlasting name, which shall not perish.” And concerning these the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “For there are eunuchs who were born so from their mother’s womb; and there are eunuchs who were made so by men; and there are eunuchs who have castrated themselves for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. He who can receive it, let him receive it.”

But why did he say, “He who can receive it, let him receive it”? I think that this introduction of eunuchs, who castrate themselves for love of the kingdom of heaven, is not to be referred to the cutting off of the genital members, in which many, so thinking, have wandered from the truth of the Gospel, but rather to the command of the mind, by which chastity is preserved entire, conceived from love of Christ. For those castrate themselves for the sake of the kingdom of God who keep true chastity of mind and body inviolate for love of God. And just as bodily no one is easily castrated without the great pain of torment, so no one can arrive at the perfection of chastity without the utmost exertion of labor. And certainly no one is modest by his own powers, no one modest by his own merits. It is God who gives the good will; he it is also who joins strength to the will, so that it may be able. But because it is not for many to live chastely, he leaves each one to his own judgment, saying: “He who can receive it, let him receive it.” The cutting off of members does not make the mind chaste, since the root of lust is interior, and cannot be removed by the amputation of the shameful parts. But if the mind has been chaste and whole, it will easily restrain the insolence of the members. For nothing is stronger than the mind of a man [unclear]. Whoever, therefore, flees from the labor of the struggle will never attain the height of honor. Brief now is the time in which we labor, resisting the devil; but very soon the eternal time will come, in which, reigning without end, we shall exult with Christ. Let the body be chastised with discreet abstinence, lest it kick back against the command of the mind; let the concupiscence of the flesh be broken by holy exercises, so that it may always obey the counsels of reason. If at any time it begins to strike the mind with wanton impulses, let it even be restrained with blows, because it is better that the back, humbled by useful stripes, should be subdued than that the petulance of the flesh should hurl the soul down into hell. For Scripture says: “He who delicately nourishes his servant from boyhood will afterward find him insolent.” The flesh is the servant of the mind, which must always obey the command of reason. But if the flesh has been nourished too indulgently, then, accustomed to pleasures, it will refuse to bear the command of the spirit. For a servant becomes insolent toward his master whenever he is nourished delicately by him through habit. And it is difficult to bring a rebellious and insolent servant back to humble obedience after he has long been accustomed to kick back.

For it is written: “A servant cannot be instructed by words, because he understands what you say and scorns to answer.” Therefore the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “Fodder and a rod and a burden for an ass; bread and discipline and work for a servant. He works under discipline and seeks rest; loosen your hand for him, and he seeks freedom. The yoke and the strap bend a hard neck, and constant tasks bow a servant down.” If we begin to borrow these testimonies of the Scriptures from the literal sense for the moral sense, we shall find that all the things said about the servant and the ass fit very rightly with lazy and delicate flesh. For truly the flesh is the servant of the soul, which always rises up against its mistress unless it is wearied every day by discipline and work.

Indeed, he who keeps this flesh delicately at all times in idleness and pleasures will necessarily feel it insolent against spiritual impulses, because while the flesh is lifted up into pride, the spirit, languishing in idleness and lukewarm, is weakened.
