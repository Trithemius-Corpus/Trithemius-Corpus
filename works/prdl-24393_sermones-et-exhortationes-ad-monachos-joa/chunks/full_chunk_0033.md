Therefore watch at every time, praying that you may be accounted worthy to escape all the evils that are to come, and to stand with confidence before the sight of the Son of God, who judges all things. For, as the divine lover of Christ bears witness, we shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ, so that each may receive the things proper to the body, according as he has done, whether good or evil.

Blessed is that monk whom the Lord shall find watching in good works, who has prepared himself for the wedding of the heavenly Lamb in true purity of heart, who will not fear to meet the judge. Let that memorable saying of divine Jerome on the Gospel according to Matthew always be in your hearts, when he says: Whether I eat or drink, or do anything else, that voice always seems to sound in my ears: Rise, dead, and come to judgment.

As often as I think of that day of the future examination, I tremble with my whole heart and body. Therefore, if there is any joy in the present life, my brothers, it must be so conducted that the bitterness of that dreadful judgment never withdraw from memory. O that great, terrible, dreadful, exceeding and very bitter day, on which the sun and moon will be darkened, not by diminution of light but by comparison with majesty; where every argument of excuse will cease; where all things will be manifest, and nothing will be able to lie hidden from the judge, because he who knows the hidden thoughts of minds will then judge openly; who is neither anticipated by favor, nor bent by mercy; is not corrupted by gifts and presents; will not be softened by prayers; nor thereafter will he be appeased by any satisfaction.

Therefore, brothers, while you are placed in this life, let each of you do as much as he can for his soul, because penance for sins done here alone is useful and fruitful, so long as there is room for pardon and the sentence of justice is still delayed. He who postpones doing good and performing penance until death is very near to damnation, because the good that he neglects when healthy and unharmed he will rarely restore when sick and already about to die. For holy Pope Gregory says: Whoever does not fear to defer the fruits of penance until the hour of death is himself a witness against himself that true repentance was never in his mind. Now therefore turn yourselves to our Lord with your whole heart, doing penance before him, and live, I pray, in such a way that, when you must depart from here, you may not fear to appear before his face.

*That one must pray without ceasing. Homily IX.*

Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, brothers, in the Gospel according to Luke, wishing us to be continual in prayers, admonished us thus, saying: It is necessary always to pray and never to fail. This counsel of the Lord befits, before all mortals, monks most of all, and all cloistered persons in whatever religious life, whose every action and whole life, indeed whatever they do, ought either to be prayer or at least conformed to prayer. For all who profess the monastic service are called spiritual, not without reason, in order that they may be admonished by the very word to despise the pleasures of the flesh for the love of God, and to walk through true charity in the ardor of the spirit.

For the holy apostle of Christ says: Those who are in the flesh cannot please God; for if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live. Knowing, however, that without constancy in prayer the fervor of the spirit cannot long endure in us, he likewise says to the Thessalonians: Always rejoice; pray without ceasing; in all things give thanks, because this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for all of you.

But what the manner of praying is, the Savior taught us in the Gospel, saying: But you, when you pray, enter into your chamber, and with the door shut, pray to your Father in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will repay you. And in another place he said to the Samaritan woman: Woman, believe me, because the hour will come [unclear] the Father seeks such as will adore him. God is spirit, and those who adore him must adore in spirit and truth. It is manifest from Christ’s teaching that prayer is of the heart rather than a lengthy speech of the mouth, because, since God is spirit, he must be adored in no other way than by the innermost fervor of the spirit.
