O humility of great power, which made the king of heaven and earth withdraw from the face of one most worthless little man, and dwell in seven years’ exile in the land of the Egyptians. You compelled Christ Jesus, the only-begotten Son of God, to assume a human body for our salvation, and made him undergo the most bitter passion and death by his voluntary will. Great is your dominion, O venerable humility of holiness, since to you the kingdom of heaven is promised; to your worshippers the gift of everlasting blessedness is granted; and whoever keeps to your footsteps is raised to the height of the angels, as Christ says in the holy Gospel: “Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be exalted.” The [unclear] of heaven taught [unclear] when he said: “Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” Likewise the most blessed mother, the inviolate Virgin Mary, in her own sweetest canticle of praise, speaks thus of the Lord God: “He has cast down the mighty from their seats, and exalted the humble.” And the prophet says in the psalm: “The Lord has looked upon the prayers of the humble, and has not despised their petitions.” O prayer of one who humbles himself: it penetrates the clouds, and until it draws near it will not be comforted, and it will not depart until the Most High looks upon [what is] good. Humility makes the monk pure in his conscience most pleasing to God; and the more he casts himself down by humbling himself for the honor of God, the more glorious he will appear in the kingdom. The monk who truly humbles himself is made a son of the Most High through participation in grace, and will rejoice forever with the king of heaven, Jesus Christ. He who truly desires to imitate Christ should also assume true humility; clothed and fortified with it as with a breastplate of strength, he may do great things, and, thinking nothing of himself except humble things, may not incur the stain of vainglory. For this is the true perfection of monks: always humbly to recognize one’s own imperfection, and rather to reflect diligently on what good is lacking to him than on what he has. For true humility, through the desire of charity, is always stretched toward the things before it; and, a cautious rememberer of things past, it fears lest it lose what it has, and, desiring to acquire through the zeal of devotion what it does not yet have, in the fervor of holiness
