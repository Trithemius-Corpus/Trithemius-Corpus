But if you trust that you have been justified from sin, all the more and more fervently must Christ hanging on the cross be contemplated by you; without the merit of his passion no one lives righteous on earth. For outside faith in Christ there is no righteousness. However religiously a man may live, if he does not believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, he will by no means attain eternal salvation; rather, the wrath of God remains upon him, and he will necessarily perish forever. The death of Christ opened the gate of salvation, through which no one else can enter except one who faithfully believes in Christ. For this reason Christ humbly subjected himself to the condemnation of death, that he might free believers from the yoke of perpetual damnation. O infinite charity, O immeasurable loving-kindness: who, believing in you, eternal majesty, would not marvel at such astonishing benefits granted to us? For your passion, Lord Jesus, is the price of our salvation, the refuge [unclear] of sinners, the singular remedy of infirm minds, the fountain, origin, and end of all good things. Therefore, now, dearest brothers, let us recall and diligently bring back to memory the bitterness of the Lord’s passion, so that, since we necessarily acknowledge ourselves to be sinners, we may at last be able to be justified through the merit of that very savior of ours. For meditation on the Lord’s passion surpasses all exercises of this kind, if it has been continued for a longer time with the fervor of inward devotion. That mind of a monk which, kindled by zeal for divine love, has been accustomed every day, at least for a little hour, to keep the memory of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ with tears, does not easily lie open to the suggestions of demons. For that heart which the love of Jesus possesses always remains inaccessible and closed to all evil thoughts. Truly blessed is that monk, and never to be named except with honor, who meditates day and night with loving zeal on the teaching and passion of our Lord Jesus Christ; who never forgets his Savior; who, whether he eats or drinks or does anything else, always beholds with the eyes of the mind the Lord Jesus hanging on the cross. *On the sleep and wakefulness of monks. Homily XVII.*

The monk who always thinks that he is going to die should permit his eyes to pass no time [unclear]. For Scripture says: “Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.” Against the forgetful and uncertain, however, it is written: “A people without counsel and without prudence. Would that they were wise and understood and provided for their last things.” The remembrance of death brings watchfulness into the soul and extinguishes every disordered thought of the mind. He who rightly fears death never grows sluggish in good work.
