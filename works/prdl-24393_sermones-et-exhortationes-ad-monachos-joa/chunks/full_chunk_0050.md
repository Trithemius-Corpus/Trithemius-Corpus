For the monk who, as a novice and a young man, neglects [discipline?] spends all the days of his life in torpor and sloth. For Scripture says: “A young man walking according to his own way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” Therefore I say to you, venerable elders, I beseech you, I admonish you, and by the mercy of God I beg you with what urgency I can: from now on live thus, conduct yourselves thus, walk thus, so that your conduct may agree with the years of old age; and you who have already advanced far into old age, offer an example of holiness for the instruction of the younger men.

To you, without doubt, is ascribed whatever is found in the conduct of these younger men. For you ought to go before the younger men in all holiness, in word and in deed; you are commanded to display to them an example of all perfection, so that, when they see in you all the works of virtue made perfect, they may be moved, as it were, by a certain impulse of shame, and be stirred to imitate your holiness. For the manners of elders provide the younger with an example of living, as is said in the proverb: “What the old men have sung, the young men have learned.”

But you younger men, show reverence with humility to the elders, considering their gray hair; and those whom you know to be more fervent in the observance of regular discipline, rejoice to have as teachers by keeping their company. Avoid those who either walk restlessly or observe the purity of regular discipline too negligently. Not all who are called elders are worthy of the imitation of younger men; nor do years make a monk venerable, but conduct established in the love of God with prudence makes him worthy of veneration.

Therefore, whichever of you, dearest brothers, desires to be a true lover of God, whatever progress he desires to make in the holy monastic way of life that he professes, let him always flee the companionship of undisciplined brothers, and seek fellow-soldiers who agree with his purpose in good. For the prophet says in the psalm: “With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man you will be innocent, and with the chosen you will be chosen, and with the perverse you will be perverted.” For evil company has harmed many; and he who delights to dwell with the reprobate will not be free from the contagion of sin. *Homily XI. Fol. XXVI.*

For evil conversations corrupt the good morals of young men. All their pursuits are vain, and their words useless, indeed destructive, who, being carnally minded, have not known the exercises of divine love. For truly, just as one diseased sheep gradually corrupts the whole fold, so one slothful and perverse monk in a congregation often becomes the cause of many transgressions. As [unclear] says, a little leaven corrupts the whole mass of flour into which it is mixed with its odor; so young monks and novices who associate with lukewarm and slothful elders are imbued with their evil ways, and never thereafter are reformed to the integrity of holy fervor.

Therefore I say to you elders: be, by good works and by chaste and always modest speech, a pattern of life for the young, so that whenever they are corrected for their faults they may not defend themselves by the examples of elders. For it is most shameful for elders to be culpable in those things for which younger men are rightly rebuked. Live, I beg you elders, so that in your presence the younger men may presume neither to say nor to do anything that seems reprehensible. Let the wicked fear you as judges, not as men acting the part of patrons, because as many as have sinned by your examples are reckoned to have perished by your instruction.

Let there not be among you anyone lukewarm or slothful, because desires kill the lazy man, and vanity destroys every idle monk. Let there not be among you a verbose man, a wandering monk, a circumcellion, murmuring and restless, because whoever delights in such things and pursuits both ruins himself by evil work and kills his brother by his examples. Let there not be among you a mocker of the simple, nor a despiser of the humble, nor anyone rash and censorious; because he who is quick to mock will be consumed in his own folly and will not be glorious. He opens his mouth against the humble, always seeks quarrels, and rejoices to be talkative about the deeds of others. He will not prosper in his counsel, and if he has been raised to some honor, he will quickly fall into the depths. His pride is hateful to God, because, though he is foolish and witless, he boasts that he is learned and wise. Soon his foolishness will be laid bare, and a grievous [destruction?] will seize him within.
