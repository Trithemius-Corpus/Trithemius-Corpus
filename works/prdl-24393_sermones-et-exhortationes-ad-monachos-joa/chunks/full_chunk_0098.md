*On continence and true chastity.*

Nothing is more beautiful than chastity: it makes clean what has been conceived from unclean seed; it also makes an enemy into a member of the household, and an earthly man into an angel. Chastity is the most beautiful of all the virtues; when, for love of Christ, it promises that it is to be preserved even unto death, it is rewarded with an inestimable prize. Chastity is next to the fellowship of the angels, and the highest medicine of the Christian mind. Chastity is the lovable dwelling-place of Jesus Christ, the eternal king, and the wondrous beginning of future incorruption. Chastity is the fullness of heavenly grace, through which every corruption of nature is restored. Chastity is the most powerful of all virtues, through which, in a fragile vessel, nature is marvelously conquered in combat. Chastity imitates the angelic life, and, in the midst of mud, knows no sprinkling of stain.

Blessed is the man who has merited to receive from the Lord God the true gift of chastity, and has kept it inviolate, in Christian humility, even unto death. Chaste is he who, for love of Jesus Christ, pure in mind and body, abstains from every defilement of the flesh; who, by the fervor of the Holy Spirit, burns sweetly in the charity of God; who desires nothing at all in this age, but always desires to be and to live with Christ. Chaste is he who, mindful of the profession of spiritual warfare, restrains the motions of the flesh by reason, and at once, from the beginning, strongly checks all the itching sparks of lust. Chaste is he who, as soon as carnal thought begins to strike the mind, implores divine aid with tears, and does not cease from prayers until he has felt the fire of itching ardor wholly extinguished. Chaste is he who flees every material and occasion of lust; who has made a covenant with his eyes that he should not even think about a woman; and who sets a guard over his senses, lest foul or immodest things be brought in. Chaste is he who avoids the company of the wanton, and always turns his eyes away so that he may not behold vain things. Chaste is he who detests the frequent company of all women, and who at every time regards his own frailty with fear and vigilance as suspect. Chaste indeed is he who in mind and body imitates angelic nature, and who by holy morals admits no corruption.

Chastity is a gift of grace, and not a work of nature or of human effort. Chastity makes the humble monk most pleasing to God, since he who lives in mortal flesh without corruption is a companion of the angels. He who has attained true chastity is always humble; and he who understands its nature does not arrogate possession of it to himself, but to the Lord who gave it. For no one is chaste by his own strength, since frail man is not sufficient to conquer nature, unless he who created nature and all things has given it. For as often as nature is conquered through chastity, God manifestly works together with man. Chastity itself has, however, a difficult beginning in the young, until the motion of unlawful thought is brought into contempt. Chastity overcomes fornication by fleeing rather than by fighting, since lust is surrounded with so many subtle weapons that it must be avoided of one’s own accord before it is engaged.

Therefore, as often as the soul is touched by the motions of lust, divine aid should be invoked without delay; no space for deliberation should be given to unclean thought, but as soon as it begins to be felt, it should be brought into contempt by the remedy of forgetfulness. With a fraudulent enemy, and one who always uses ambushes, no dealings at all are to be joined in conversation; and plans are not to be considered with the unclean thoughts of the flesh. Indeed, unless you crush the serpent’s head, it slips away; and unless you at once restrain the motions of lust, they are strengthened from the beginning. The holy apostle of Christ, Paul, admonishes that one must fight against any vices whatever; fornication alone he commands to be fled. We carry about with us the material of victory against ourselves; therefore he wills that we flee the enemy. Chastity is nourished by fasting; by gluttony it is shipwrecked. Hence, if you love chastity, love fasting, so that it may have its necessary nourishment. He who is chaste follows the industry of the bee; and he who is truly a monk, adorned as if with all flowers, is decorated with all virtues.

Not everyone who does not know lust is chaste, but he who, in pure and clean flesh, for love of Christ alone, also obtains holiness of mind. Otherwise, what does the purity of the dwelling profit, if the inhabitant is foul and unclean? Blind paganism too once had its virgins, sacrificing in garments; but because they did not sacrifice the modesty of their bodies to the true God, nor hold the right faith in their mind, they descended into the depth of hell with their demons whom they worshiped.
