Therefore the holy Christ-lover says: “We are debtors, brothers, not to the flesh, that we should live according to the flesh. For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” He therefore fights rightly who gives to each what is his own. Next we are commanded to have our feet shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that the good which, by the Lord’s generosity, we ourselves have come to know, we may also gladly communicate to others. For our law, brothers, according to which we are commanded to serve as soldiers for the Lord, is the gospel of Jesus Christ, to which all the rule and constitutions of cloistered persons are ordered. For our monastic constitutions have not been established as the gospel, but the statutes of monks have been made on account of the gospel. For a Christian can be saved without being a monk; but a monk cannot be saved unless he is a Christian. Let certain excessively superstitious and foolish cloistered persons be ashamed, who value their own rules and statutes more than the gospel of Christ; who observe the traditions of men even to prisons and chains, but do not heed the constitutions of God and of holy universal Church. To these the words of our Savior, speaking to the Jews according to Matthew, rightly apply: “Why do you also transgress the commandment of God because of your tradition?” And the evangelical teaching is to be preferred by far to all the constitutions of the whole world. After the gospel, however, the statutes of the universal Church hold the first place of authority, and neither the constitutions nor the rules of monks are compared with them in dignity. Let us take care to live according to evangelical purity, and we shall be perfect in the monastic way of life of the fathers. Let our feet always be shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that we may mortify in ourselves the desires of the flesh, and through love of God and neighbor may have peace with all. For without peace and the concord of brotherhood, mortification of the flesh is of no profit, as Saint Jerome says: “Whoever afflicts the body but abandons concord does indeed praise God on the timbrel, but does not praise him in the choir.” Hence Solomon says: “Better is a dry morsel with joy than a house full of victims with strife.” And again he says: “It is an honor for a man to separate himself from contentions; but all fools are mingled with insults.” The holy Christ-lover also, a lover of peace, admonishes us, saying: “Follow peace with all, and holiness, without which no one will see God.” Hence also the prophet Zechariah: “Love peace and truth,” says the Lord almighty. And our Savior says in the holy gospel according to Mark: “Have salt in yourselves, and have peace among yourselves.” “There is no peace for the impious,” says the Lord. But he is truly a soldier of Christ who has peace with God, with his neighbor, and within himself. A man is said to have peace with God when, moved by good will and turned toward him through the continual desire of love, despising transitory things, he directs the gaze of his mind to things above, and most diligently keeps all the things that the Lord has commanded. For when the Son of God was born in the flesh, the holy angels sang glory to the Lord, who sits above all things in the heights, and announced peace to men of good will. But impious and unjust men cannot have this peace with God, because they have turned their wills away from the Lord. He has peace with his neighbor who does injury to no one, who, when injured, does not repay vengeance, but, overcoming evil with good, never abandons brotherly charity. If any of his neighbors unjustly murmurs against such a man, the soldier of Christ has not on that account abandoned peace, because perhaps another has begun an unjust murmuring without just cause. A just man, who has peace with God and desires to harm none of his neighbors, will rightly be called peaceable, even if someone, disturbed unjustly, rises up against him. For the just man says: “With those who hated peace I was peaceable; when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause.” Hence Saint Augustine speaks: “Among the true worshippers of God, even wars themselves are peaceful, because they are waged not from greed or cruelty, but from zeal for peace, so that the evil may be restrained and the good relieved.” He has peace within himself who, enlightened by purity and holiness, lives secure in his conscience without error, whom anxious synteresis does not bite back, nor any unjust or wicked thought disturb. Hence again Augustine: “Peace is serenity of mind, tranquility of soul, simplicity of heart, the bond of love, the fellowship of charity.” For it removes enmities and rivalries; it restrains wars.
