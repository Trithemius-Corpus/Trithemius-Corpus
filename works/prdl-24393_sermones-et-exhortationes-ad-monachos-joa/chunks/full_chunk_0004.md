*With the grace and privilege of the Imperial Majesty.*

Maximilian, by the favor of divine clemency elected Emperor of the Romans, ever Augustus, and Count Palatine of Germany, Hungary, Dalmatia, and Croatia, to the honorable and devout man beloved by us, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James the Greater at Wurzburg: imperial grace and every good thing.

Since you have humbly represented to us that you have composed various editions of both ancient and modern authors, collected not without great labor, vigils, and diligence, which you desired with your whole mind to have brought into the light and published; and since our beloved subject of the Holy Roman Empire, Master Johannes Haselberg of Constance, has offered himself with a most ready spirit to be the agent of such a matter, and for this purpose has procured at great expense the type with which he may print the aforementioned works: we therefore, in order that [unclear] of the aforesaid Master Johannes may shine forth more and more, have given and granted, and by the tenor of the present letters do give and grant, to the aforementioned Master Johannes Haselberg of Constance full power and authority of every kind.

We decree that he may print, and sell when printed, wherever and at whatever price he wishes, whatever among the treatises and little works edited by you, the aforementioned Trithemius, abbot of Saint James the Greater at Wurzburg, shall have been reviewed and approved by the honorable man faithful and beloved to us, Conrad Peutinger, doctor and our councillor. And we forbid all and singular printers of books, of whatever condition they may be, to dare or presume to print, or to publish, buy, or sell when printed by others, those books that have been published, throughout the whole Roman Empire and in our hereditary lands.

Moreover, we command all and singular our faithful beloved subjects of the Holy Roman Empire, of whatever estate, condition, dignity, and office they may be, whether ecclesiastical or secular, under pain of our most grave indignation and that of the Holy Roman Empire, and of ten marks of pure gold, to be applied as often as the contrary is done, one part to our fisc and the other part to the aforesaid Master Johannes Haselberg, that they assist the same Master Johannes of Constance with their favor and aid in their places and lands, and by no means permit the said works and treatises to be printed, or, when printed elsewhere, to be sold. Rather, all those presuming to do anything against this our grant and prohibition they shall forthwith declare, in our name, to have incurred the sentence of this indignation of ours and of the Holy Roman Empire, executing therein our express will.

In testimony whereof, these letters have been furnished with the impression of our seal on the back. Given in our town of Innsbruck on the tenth day of the month of November, in the year of the Lord 1514, in the twenty-ninth year of our Roman reign, and the twenty-fifth of Hungary.

By the king himself.

At the lord Emperor’s own command.

N. Ziegler.

Corrected by Paul Obersteiner.
