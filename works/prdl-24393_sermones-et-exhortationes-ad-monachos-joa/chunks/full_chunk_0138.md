The Creator loved his creature, which he made from nothing; it is fitting that the creature itself should love above all its Creator, by whose goodness it was brought into being. God loves us with the greatest charity, than which none greater can be spoken or conceived; and he loves us so greatly that he neither ought nor could love more, since in God all things are perfect, and in the immutable there is no capacity for changes.

Consider now, my brothers, with how great fervor [of mind?], and with how great desire of heart, a man ought to love his God, by whom he is loved, even without merits, with the above-written degree of love. Let us stretch our mind toward love of him above all things, as much as we can; for he is worthy that we love him with all our strength. The seventh thing that is believed able to produce the love of God in our hearts is considered in the promise of eternal happiness. For if one considers what great goods he has promised that he will give us in heaven, love of him will deservedly burn sweetly in our hearts. He promised his whole self to us [as a reward?], he who alone is the highest and wholly immutable good.
