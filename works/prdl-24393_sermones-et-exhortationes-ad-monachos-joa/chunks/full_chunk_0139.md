They seem to themselves great before God in devotion and [unclear], and in their animal passions they are not mortified. You err, O wretched men, and you shamefully deceive yourselves; without the foundation of humble subjection and obedience, [one] thinks to build a tower of sanctity. First, the disordered desires of sensuality must be mortified; the animal passions must be restrained: anger, murmuring, the spite of indignation, and all impatience. For so long as vices rule over you, whatever gifts of private singularity you offer are not pleasing to God. All things are vain where there is no pure conscience.

The conduct of a young monk is suspect to us, however devout it may seem, when the humility of voluntary subjection does not commend it. A monk without humility and obedience cannot be good, even though by fasting and prayer they daily hunt after sanctity. The devil deceives many in the hiddenness of elation and pride, whom he permits continually to keep vigil, fast, and pray. For none of these works is pleasing to God without humility and charity: fasting, vigils, prayers, and the studies of monks without these are not feared by the devil. [Unclear] does not move the devil; [unclear] does not torment him. Nor does he greatly value the vigils of the proud man, nor does he heed the fasting of the swollen with pride; nor does he fear the prayers of the proud.

But if he finds you humble, cheerfully obedient in all things to the commands of the elders; if he sees you doing great things and thinking humble things about yourself; if he perceives the charity of God and neighbor burning in you, then certainly he is moved against you, for he is tormented by envy of your virtues. He turns against you all the weapons of his cunning that he can, and with all his strength strives to overturn your purpose of sanctity. For nothing in a monk displeases the demons more, nothing torments them more vehemently, than if, for the love of God, he has truly humbled himself in all things and has always kept the service of the monastic institution under the guard of obedience with a cheerful spirit.

You have been deceived by the prompting of demons, because you think yourselves to be something great without the true merit of virtues. I see you unwilling toward the commands of the elders, impatient and murmuring under correction, quick to rebuke another's life, but very slow in considering your own defects. These are the surest signs of the promptings of demons: that the necessary [things] have been despised...

But so that [unclear] may learn wisely to discern [unclear] from the promptings of the ancient enemy, mark what we say. First, therefore, let him reckon himself inspired by the grace of God whenever he diligently observes that he is becoming purer in conscience and that the tinder of sin is diminishing. Wherever the Spirit of God has poured himself out by the gift of heavenly grace, every inclination to sin soon withdraws. But if a horror of sin enters the mind, it is certainly manifest that the prompting of the devil has not taken possession of the man. For as soon as the Holy Spirit has penetrated the mind of a man, every spark of vice is either mitigated or utterly extinguished.

Hence let each one of you diligently examine himself and weigh by whose prompting he is moved. For the prompting of God is accustomed to lessen sins, but that of Satan, the enemy, to increase them. In those who follow the Spirit of God, sins diminish daily; but he who follows the prompting of the devil is rarely truly amended. The second sign of divine prompting is recognized from the increase of virtues, since the grace of the Holy Spirit always produces great virtues in a man. Therefore, when you feel yourself advancing daily in the love of God and in good works; when you experience in yourself that the habit of sinning and the vices prevail less from day to day, you ought humbly to acknowledge that the grace of God is present. For the love of God, which grace brings, is never idle, but is always usefully occupied in good works; and as it covers the multitude of sins, so by the exercise of virtues it does not permit the soul to sin. For the grace of God cannot stand with sins, and is not at all accustomed to be in a man without the working of virtues.

The third sign of the Spirit of God is shown in a man when the mind does not waver in its purpose of sanctity, but, growing stronger from day to day, is strengthened. For there are many who, touched by a passing compunction, sometimes receive the first two signs, but, despising perseverance, do not reach the third. Without perseverance there is no reward for a good work, as the Lord says in the holy Gospel: "He who has persevered to the end, he shall be saved." The devil knows how to pretend himself absent for a short time, and [unclear] the two true written signs of the Spirit of God, when he does not know how, or fears, to disguise [them]. Soon thereafter, bursting out from ambush, he attacks the devout but unwary person, and, fostering him in former negligence, either casts him headlong or wounds him more gravely.
