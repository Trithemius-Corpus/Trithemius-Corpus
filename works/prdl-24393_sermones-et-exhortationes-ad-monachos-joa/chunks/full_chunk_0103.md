*On the brotherly charity of monks.*

It covers a multitude of sins. How necessary brotherly charity is for salvation, the Lord of all, Jesus Christ, revealed, saying: “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind. This is the first and greatest commandment. The second is like to this: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang the whole law and the prophets.” For whatever is commanded anywhere in the whole course of the Scriptures is without doubt referred to these two commandments.

By “neighbor” here we understand not one joined by closeness of blood, but every human being by the fellowship of reason, and especially the Christian, whom each person is commanded to love as himself, with whom he is seen to await eternal salvation. But it is especially fitting for you, brothers, to love one another in the true charity of the Savior Jesus Christ, since monastic profession enjoins you to strive with all your strength toward the height of perfection. For just as covetousness is the root of all evils, so charity is the root of all goods. In the first place you ought to love God above all things with a pure heart for his own sake, because he is the highest good, and then to love your neighbor with sincere charity for the Lord’s sake. These two precepts of charity are joined by the authority of Christ, so that one can in no way be fulfilled without the other. For he who truly loves God cannot hate his neighbor. If he hates his neighbor, he does not truly love God. The love of God begets in him love of neighbor, and through love of neighbor the love of God is nourished. If one of the loves is lacking, the other by no means grows strong. For he who certainly does not love his neighbor shows himself empty of the love of God. Therefore we truly love God when we love our neighbor, that is, every Christian human being, more diligently with sincere intention.

For brotherly charity is the bond of peace, which alone nourishes concord in the dwelling and preserves Christian unity among brothers. Charity is the begetter of meekness and the noble mother of Catholic piety. Brotherly charity is fearsome to demons, since it makes victorious all whose inner chamber of the heart it embraces. Charity extinguishes the fire of lust and covers every multitude of sins. Charity renders good people most dear to God and makes them companions of the holy angels. Charity preserves the Church in the unity of the saints, outside which no one obtains eternal salvation. Brotherly charity orders the morals of cloistered men and makes God dwell in the midst of monks. Charity does injury to no one, but supplies help to all through compassion. Charity drives out every vice, because it is the sole nurse of all virtues. Charity preserves the reform of brotherhood in vigor among cloistered men and works a great salvation of souls. Mutual charity confirms obedience and makes brotherly correction fruitful. True charity nourishes concord among brothers and preserves a sweet-smelling chastity of both minds and bodies. Charity makes monks of one mind in the house of God, and laudably concordant in every good work. Mutual charity lightens the rigor of the order, and all things that seem hard and harsh to the carnal it changes through custom into nature, as though they were something very gentle. Charity is the most beautiful beauty of monks, adorned with all virtues. Charity utterly drives hatred and envy out of the monasteries of God’s servants, and by its presence puts to flight far away the remaining crimes of death. The charity of brothers is a fitting dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and a fortress of serene conscience. Charity is the handmaid of justice and the sole preserver of regular discipline in a monastery. Charity keeps the mind innocent of cruelty and provides for all a peaceful lightness of spirit. Brotherly charity seeks the salvation of all, and does not allow the one who has it to grow sluggish in idleness. Charity does not loathe poverty, but for love of the brothers is ready to endure every poverty. Charity is the water of heavenly sweetness; if anyone drinks it in the fervor of the Holy Spirit, he does not fear the perpetual thirst of hell. Brotherly charity does not seek the enticements of the flesh, but directs pure, holy, and unstained affections toward the neighbor. Charity has the name of chaste beauty, which cannot be shared with things or actions of most impure pleasure. Charity loves purity and cleanness, and admits no impurity. Charity, in the working of love, considers not the flesh of the neighbor but the soul, which it does not doubt was created in the image of God. Brotherly charity raises up a delinquent neighbor with pious admonitions, and keeps all its own actions pure and innocent of every malice. Charity walks in peace and tranquility of mind, and saddens the neighbor neither by word nor deed nor sign. Charity humbles itself as much as it can in all things, and ministers most lovingly to an infirm neighbor as to a son of God. Cha
