*On the reading and study of the divine Scriptures: Homily Four.*

Since every Scripture, divinely inspired, as the holy [apostle of Christ?] says, is useful for teaching, or for arguing, for correcting, for instructing in justice, so that the man of God may be perfect, instructed for every good work, we ought gladly to direct to the reading of the divine Scriptures some portion of the zeal for secular literature in which we once labored. For in the divine Scriptures whatever saving knowledge requires is found expressed most abundantly, so that what you do not find there is by no means necessary for salvation. For the majesty of the divine Scriptures far surpasses all the knowledge and teaching of the whole world, which the Holy Spirit poured into the men of God so that it might be written, and, so that it may be understood, he bestows the grace of his illumination upon studious readers.

This Scripture, my brothers, turn over diligently with purity of mind and the fervor of twin charity, for whoever truly loves God spends all the time he can find free in sacred reading. Let the frenzied deeds, so to speak, of fleshly lovers be incentives to you, I pray, toward the wholesome study of the Scriptures: they, receiving letters and little pledges from their mistresses, do not cease to read them, and often reread and kiss them; and, what is a greater sign of love, they hang them either from the neck or at the breast, so that the beloved memory of her whom they desire may never depart from the mind.

Consider, brothers, the madness of carnal men in love, and blush at your own lukewarmness concerning the love of God. Which of you is affected toward the holy Scriptures of God with so great a love of heart as a blind lover, insane with affection, is inflamed toward the filthy and utterly vain love-letters of one little harlot? He, because of love for the absent woman whom he desires, guards her letters and little gifts with frequent kisses; pondering every single stroke with excessive love, he examines them again and again; in all that he does he is always thinking of the absent woman; anxiously he hastens to come into her sight.

O if we who are called Christian monks, who serve the supreme and immortal king Christ Jesus, who are commanded to love our God above all things and are enjoined to love nothing in this world except him, if, I say, we were to pursue our creator with as much love as carnally minded men observe and love their corruptible and mortal girlfriends. The holy Scripture read daily brings God before us, manifests his will to us, instructs us what we ought to do, and discloses what we ought to avoid. Through it the almighty speaks with us, and, instructing us by an invisible teaching, consoles us, as the holy apostle says: For whatever things were written were written for our instruction, that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope.

Therefore the true soldier of Christ, and especially the cloistered one, who loves God in his whole heart and longs to please him alone, embraces the divine Scriptures, and above all the holy Gospel, with affection, and is constantly occupied in its reading and meditation. But whoever neglects readings of the divine Scriptures clearly shows that he is not a true lover of God. But what does Scripture itself say? He who turns away his ear so as not to hear the laws, his prayer will be detestable. But blessed are they who examine the testimonies of the Lord; with their whole heart they seek him.

And so you, my brothers, if you truly love the Lord Jesus in your whole heart, if you desire above all things to please him as true soldiers, if finally you wish to advance toward the perfection of the monastic life, you ought always to occupy yourselves with fervent affection in the reading of the divine Scriptures. How useful the reading of the Scriptures is, blessed Jerome intimates, saying: Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh. For after God nothing in this life is taken in more pleasantly, nothing is felt more sweetly, nothing separates the mind more powerfully from love of this world, nothing so strengthens the soldier of Christ against all the temptations of adversaries, nothing disposes and confirms a monk so much for every good work, as frequent reading and ardent study of the Scriptures.
