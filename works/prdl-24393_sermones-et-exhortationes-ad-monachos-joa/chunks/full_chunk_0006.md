*On Spiritual Warfare.*

It certainly cannot be otherwise than that we too, his imitators, wicked and most vile sinners, ought to suffer many things, since all that we suffer we justly endure by our own demerit. For no one comes to the glory of the city above unless he has first been justified here from sin; but since the tribulations of the just are many, he will in no way be able to be just who has refused to endure adversities for righteousness in this world. Therefore, my dearest brothers, imitate in great patience and humility our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who, having become man for us, voluntarily endured the greatest things, and in this world was never without the bitterness of persecution. For from the hour of his most sacred birth until the departure of his spirit from his body, he was always most patient for us in the bitterness of this present life; and the bitterness of his future passion, foreknown by him, never withdrew from his foreknowledge. For since he was almighty God made man for us, he knew all things, understood all things, foresaw all things; nor could anything of those things which he was going to suffer be hidden from him, who, in order to suffer, was made passible in the world, while remaining God, as he was, impassible. Born, moreover, of the Virgin Mary without commerce with a man, in order to fulfil the law, he is soon circumcised on the eighth day; and he who had no sin is wounded for our offenses. By night the child is led into Egypt from the face of the persecutor; and he who was Lord of all, poor and needy in the midst of the nations, unknown, is exiled for seven years. Finally, through all the days of his life in this world he most patiently endured continual labors, and never refused to undergo any adversities for the glory of the Father.

For all hard and bitter things the just and innocent one voluntarily suffered for us, most unworthy as we are; by the most bitter death of the cross he redeemed us from the destruction of perpetual damnation, and in his own blood mercifully reconciled us to the Father. Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, as the prince of the apostles has warned, my brothers, let us be armed with the same thought, so that we, suffering together with the innocent one, may diligently recall to memory those things which he endured for us.

For if we suffer with him, we shall also reign together with him when he triumphs in glory. Behold, Christ calls us to the glory to which he has gone before; he gives the weapons with which he himself first overcame the prince of the world; he prescribed the form by which a soldier ought to follow the king to the crown. Christ calls his soldiers to the palace of heaven; the attendant gifts of virtues lead them through distresses, and the victors receive rewards, to reign forever. Therefore, my dearest brothers, always pursuing our warfare most zealously, let us continually declare war on vices and concupiscences, so that, just as the flesh wars against the spirit, so we too may fight constantly, watching in prayer and fasting against the kindler of vices. For as long as we live in this world we are pilgrims away from the Lord, and like gymnosophists we contend in the stadium, carrying out the necessary course of life in continual struggle. Yet we fight not by our own strength, but supported by the mercy of our king, who said to his imitators in the holy Gospel: Without me you can do nothing. And lest we should flee the battle of the warfare set before us as impossible, he said elsewhere: In the world you will have distress; but have confidence: I have overcome the world. And so that we might know that the Lord Jesus is always present in this world to his little servants, he promises us thus in the apostles: Behold, I am with you all days, even to the consummation of the age. Having received security by this promise, O soldiers of Christ, my dearest brothers, why do we hesitate, why do we not stand bravely against all the insolences of this world? Let us always have our king and protector present with us as we fight; and do we fear to engage against a weak enemy, who overcomes no one except the willing? Gird yourselves, I pray, for the unconquerable Christian warfare, which you have professed under a twofold condition: the one when, at the font of sacred baptism, renouncing Satan, you swore the words of Christian instruction; the other when in the world you solemnly put on the monastic garment of religion.

A great necessity of making progress constrains you, if you weigh your profession with due consideration, since the obligation of the Christian name commands that you must keep the evangelical commandments, and the vow which you voluntarily emitted requires that you observe the precepts of monastic institution.
