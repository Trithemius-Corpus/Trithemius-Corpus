Again also he says: Those whom the adversity of temporal life humbles take joy from the hope of the everlasting inheritance, because divine dispensation would see that they were not to be saved forever if it did not restrain them, to be instructed under the rule of discipline.

When afflicted, we lose earthly things; but by humbly enduring affliction we multiply heavenly goods for ourselves. For the present life is a road, by which we hasten toward our homeland; and for that reason we are often touched in it by disturbances, lest we think that our homeland is here. Therefore let the monk rejoice in his adversity; let him who is tempted, to whom the world has been crucified, be glad; let him trust that he belongs to the fellowship of God, who with a pure conscience always does good and receives the contrary in tribulation.

For it is the truest sign of divine compassion if a Christian man is struck in this world by various adversities, who, serving God with a pure conscience, has prepared [unclear] in heaven. Knowing that some can sin greatly, he forestalls them with bodily weakness lest they sin, and mercifully scourges them for their salvation, seeing that it is more useful for their souls to be broken by illnesses unto salvation than to live unharmed unto damnation. Others he wills to be poor, whom he foreknew could not use riches unto salvation. Hence holy men, when they trust that they are making much progress by the prosperity of virtues, rejoice that by a certain governance of heavenly dispensation they are also exercised by temptations, because they guard the glory of the virtues they have received all the more robustly when, shaken by the assault of temptation, they recognize their own weakness more humbly. The way of tribulation is a short road to Christ, and he is found in it more quickly than in tranquility. For the holy bishop Ambrose says: There are many who seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and there are those who seek him in persecutions and quickly find him. He is present in the dangers of his faithful, just as in the psalm he speaks of the just man, saying: I am with him in tribulation; I will rescue him and glorify him.

Let purity of conscience keep guard in adversity; let the inviolate integrity of life always be preserved; and whatever adversity there is will undoubtedly be for us to the merit of salvation. The ancient enemy can overcome no one by fighting, unless he has first found him to have fallen away from himself. He seeks out those who are sluggish in idleness; he trips up the unwary soldier, and he most easily lays low the one whom he finds stripped of arms. Therefore, my brothers, always keep watch in holy prayers; always persist in good works, so that whenever the tempter approaches, he may never find you idle.

Remember the words of our savior Jesus Christ, saying in the holy Gospel: Let your loins be girded and lamps burning in your hands; and be like men [waiting for their lord, when] he returns from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may immediately open to him. He keeps watch who, standing firm in the fear of the Lord, is never drowsy with sluggishness of mind. He keeps watch who preserves his soul against whatever adversities of this [unclear] by the tireless constancy of a holy purpose. He keeps watch, whoever does not cease to make progress daily in the love of the Lord.

He carries a lamp in his hands, whoever adorns the purpose of holiness with fitting works. He also holds a lamp in his hands, whoever, purified in mind and body, shines for his neighbor by works of holy piety. Hence the holy lover of Christ, Paul, says in the epistle: I beseech you, most beloved, by the mercy of God, that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God. He who, chastising his body by the pursuits of holy abstinence, brings it under servitude to the spirit has consecrated a living sacrifice to the Lord. But whoever has now learned to preserve his heart clean and pure from every pollution of sin has undoubtedly sacrificed a holy offering to the Lord. Whoever indeed perseveres in the purpose of holiness to the end has without doubt offered a pleasing sacrifice.
