For we are frail and weak, and we do not long remain stable in holy purpose unless by daily exercises we continually renew the fervor of the spirit. For by reason of my office I am bound to set before you those things which can conduce to your salvation; it belongs to you to hear me speaking, as the shepherd of your souls, patiently in all things. For although, being young, I am not fit to teach so great a multitude of elders, nevertheless, because you have appointed me your shepherd, I am compelled, under the necessity of salvation, to feed the flock which I undertook to govern. What is mine to do, I both do willingly according to my strength and will do as long as I shall live in this solicitude of pastoral care: young though I am, I shall teach you elders, and without interruption I shall show how you ought to serve as soldiers for him in whose arms you have sworn.

For I am not unaware of the dreadful threat of our lawgiver, who says: Let the abbot know that whatever profit the master of the household may find lacking in the sheep is laid upon the shepherd’s fault. And what follows concerns you, my brothers, if, while I preach and admonish, contempt for the word of God arises. Again, he says, he will be free only insofar as, if all diligence has been applied by the shepherd to a restless or disobedient flock, and every care has been shown for their diseased actions, their shepherd, absolved in the judgment of the Lord, may say to the Lord with the prophet: I have not hidden your justice in my heart; I have spoken your truth and your salvation; but they, despising, have spurned me. And then at last, for the sheep disobedient to his care, let death itself be the punishment prevailing against them.

Here no covering of excuse is left; no place for evasion is afforded; but the danger of both parties is publicly described. For I, holding the place of the abbot, if I have not announced to you the justice of God, if I have not corrected you when sinning according to the Rule, have denied my office, and the whole of whatever is found lacking in holiness among you will be imputed to me. For it is mine, by reason of the pastoral office, to say what is expedient for the salvation of your souls; but yours is humbly to hear those things which I shall proclaim in the name of the Savior, and, so far as each one has strength, faithfully to fulfill them with a great heart.

You have heard my danger if I do not cry out; you have heard yours also, if you despise me as I preach the word of God to you. But so that by the Savior’s mercy we may all escape secure, let each one of us zealously pursue what is commanded him in this spiritual warfare, taking the greatest care that, made deaf hearers of the truth, we do not lie to the Lord. Let us diligently remember the vow which we have made, and let us preserve inviolate until death the holy order which we profess. Let us hear our king saying in the Gospel to his soldiers: If anyone ministers to me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also will my minister be. We are ministers of the eternal king, priests and Levites, all cloistered monks, and bound by a double reason to great holiness, since we have at once taken up the order of clerics and profess the rule of monastic purity.

Therefore let us follow our commander, under whom we serve, along the path of the austerity of all virtues; and let us arm ourselves with right faith, firm hope, and the most fervent charity toward God and neighbor, in which the perfection of all holiness is comprised. For whoever is girded with the defense of charity is never overcome by the adversary. For in charity the military service of Christ truly grows sweet; and when its fervor begins to burn in the soldier’s heart, it fears no adversities. For charity, as Saint Gregory says, is never idle, but does great things if it is true; but if it refuses to work, it was not perfect charity. Therefore, beloved cloistered men, you who have promised that you will serve the eternal king perpetually, cast away now the works of darkness, and put on the charity of God and neighbor, which is the strongest armor of salvation for all. If anyone has been clothed with it, he will fear no labors, however hard, no adversities of this world.
