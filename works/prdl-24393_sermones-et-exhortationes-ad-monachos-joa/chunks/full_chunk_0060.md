*All Things. XIII.*

By sword and fire a monk can be a martyr, if for the love of God he has preserved true patience everywhere in his soul. For the true monk is neither puffed up by praises nor saddened by reproach; he shows himself patient under injuries; in the face of insults and reproaches he is glad and humbly keeps silence. No one is stronger than the patient man, who knows how to conquer himself and humbly to endure any adversities whatever, for the love of God, with a tranquil mind. But every impatient man is proud and arrogant, since he does not follow Jesus, the teacher of humility, and therefore he is indignant at the just rebukes that he has deserved. As the furnace tests gold, so insults and injuries test the monk, whether he truly loves Christ. Humility and patience in adversity are the true marks of monks; whoever is not clothed with their adornment boasts of the name in vain. He who is not humble cannot truly be a monk; and he who does not preserve patience in adversities cannot be a true disciple of Christ. Hence King Solomon said in the Book of Proverbs: “A patient man is better than a strong man, and he who rules his own spirit than a conqueror of cities.” For a man’s teaching is discerned through patience, and his glory is to pass over wrongs. The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the trial of tribulation tests just men. A monk who follows Christ in truth is patient under all injuries; and he who is not forgetful, but a mindful meditator on the Lord’s Passion, exults and rejoices in his soul at insults. For all, as holy [Christophilus?] says, who wish to live piously in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution, and whatever evil befalls them they overcome with the greatest constancy. And although patience is necessary for a monk in every place and time, nevertheless in the daily examination of faults it must be guarded more diligently with especial attention, where the correction of morals is in question, and whatever has been committed in each person against the norm of monastic purity is amended by the senior. Therefore before we enter this place in the morning, all things must be considered, and the mind must be strengthened against those things that can happen, so that if perhaps a rather harsh rebuke occurs, it may not overthrow the mind from the state of tranquillity. For even if we have resolved never to offer insult or do injury to any of you, we do not deny that it can happen that whoever weighs our fatherly correction by the words rather than by the intention may think himself rebuked beyond what he deserves. As God knows, in all things we seek your salvation and nothing else; to cut away vices by the root, according to the statutes of our religion, and not to harm anyone, is what we sincerely intend. We cannot please all men in all things; but we are especially compelled to displease those who, sinning daily against the canon, promise themselves impunity for their crimes. Of them King Solomon said: “Evil instruction belongs to those who abandon the way of life, and he who hates rebukes will die.” The pestilent man does not love him who rebukes him, nor does he go to the wise. He who deliberately does evil flees the light and hates correction, because he is a fool; but he who is sensible in mind will give thanks to his corrector. Therefore in this matter I do not fear the hateful murmurings of subjects, knowing that I have undertaken the care of sick sheep, to whom, whether they wish it or not, I am bound to apply the remedies of salvation. If it were possible, brothers, I would most earnestly wish not to disturb any of you in anything, even slightly, but to live among you with the greatest tranquillity, and cheerfully to serve the Lord our God in peace of mind and body. But because not all of you seek in truth the things that are God’s, I must rebuke and sometimes disturb the restless, lest impunity for small crimes give boldness for greater ones. For I know what necessity of danger I undergo through the care of pastoral office, and therefore I neither can nor ought to be silent for you, [unclear] by the danger of your sins. We ask you by our Lord Jesus Christ, crucified for us, that for the love of God, for the remission of your sins, you sustain our fatherly and necessary rebukes at all times patiently and with true humility. Let each one think within himself that there is no day on which he does well and does not offend in something; and let him know that in a short time he will receive the reward of his works: good things for good, and likewise evil things for evil. Therefore let him strive, as much as he can, in this life to make satisfaction for his offenses
