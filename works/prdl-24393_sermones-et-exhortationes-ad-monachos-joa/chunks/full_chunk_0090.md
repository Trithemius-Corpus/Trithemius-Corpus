*On the true humility of monks.*

Let continual sorrow forbid every wantonness to the heart. God’s gratuitous compassion also anticipates some men, and by the indwelling of the Holy Spirit makes them humble. Everyone who is led by the Spirit of God utterly detests pride. He who truly knows himself is most wise, and cannot depart from the meekness of humility. But everyone proud and arrogant is ignorant, even if he seems great and renowned among mortals. For true wisdom rests in the dwelling-place of holy humility, and it is shown the more clearly through the lesson of patience. From the knowledge of God and of oneself humility is born, and this is rightly called true wisdom. He who has truly known himself now has nothing from which to grow proud, because, knowing his own vileness, he thinks nothing lofty, but, placed in sorrow of soul, cries to the Lord: What is man, that you are mindful of him? or the son of man, that you regard him? Man has become like vanity; his days pass away like a shadow. Why then is earth and ashes proud? Why does the spirit of frail and mortal man swell against God?

Humble yourselves, therefore, my dearest brothers, as it is written, under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation. For humility with chaste fear greatly pleases the Lord God in his servants, since he approves no virtue of man without it unto salvation. Therefore, if we desire to climb to the throne of the high kingdom of heaven, we must be very humble, because there is no other road to heaven except that of deepest humility, by which both the Son of God himself and all the saints ascended. But all who tried to enter the kingdom by another gate, struck back on the threshold of humility, with the head of pride [unclear], could by no means enter. For the gate of humility does not admit the proud, and [unclear] the narrow way to the kingdom [unclear], he undoubtedly falls into the depth of perdition and death.

For humility is the ladder of heaven, beautifully marked off by twelve steps; concerning its ascent our lawgiver Benedict says in the Rule: If we wish to attain the highest summit of humility, and to come swiftly to that heavenly exaltation, we must set up for ourselves that ladder which appeared to Jacob in a dream, by which angels were shown to him ascending and descending. Without doubt that descent and ascent is understood by us as nothing other than to descend by exaltation and to ascend by humility, as divine Scripture indicates to us: Everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled, and he who humbles himself shall be exalted. But that ladder set upright is our life in this world, which, when the heart has been humbled, is raised up by the Lord into heaven. For we say that the sides of this same ladder are our body and soul. Into these sides the divine calling has inserted the various steps of humility and discipline to be climbed.

Thereafter he enumerates those twelve steps themselves in order with a sufficiently beautiful arrangement; since they are entirely well known to you, it would be superfluous to be occupied at greater length this time in reciting them. For it is enough in this place to remember the holy canon, upon whose words all of you long ago swore voluntarily, so that you may know that humility is no less necessary for your salvation than love of God and at the same time of neighbor. For the greatest virtue of a monk is said to be humility, and by reason of his profession it is so necessary that without it he cannot be saved. A monk lifted up in spirit and proud will not have Christ, the Son of God, in heaven, but with the demons in hell he will be tormented without end. For Christ, the master and teacher of humility, detests all the proud, and admits into his fellowship only the humble, the mild, and the meek. But the devil is the head and inventor of pride; his servants and disciples are all who follow his pride and haughtiness. For he himself, the king of the proud, cannot bear humility; he flees the meek, and does not presume to stand in the sight of those meek in heart. For in the assembly of the humble servants of God Christ dwells; but in the midst of the proud the devil stirs up quarrels and disputes among those at discord. Where humility has been, there is true peace and the highest tranquillity; but where pride rules, wrath, anger, disturbance, and discord without interruption throw everything into confusion.
