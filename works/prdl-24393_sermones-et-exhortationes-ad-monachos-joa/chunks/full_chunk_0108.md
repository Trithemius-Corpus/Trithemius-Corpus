But he loves the Lord God rightly who desires absolutely nothing in this world. He truly loves God with his whole heart who, for love of him, resolutely despises worldly honors, riches, pleasures, and all the comforts of the flesh. He loves God with his whole soul who, at all times careful in observing his commandments, omits to do none of the things that are pleasing to him. For the love of God neglects nothing, but is accustomed most diligently to keep and fulfill everything that he has commanded. He who loves God in true charity has fulfilled the law, the prophets, and the Gospel. Therefore by your works you will give testimony whether you truly love God. For everyone who has deserved to receive a ray of divine love always shows great and manifest signs of love. Indeed, the fire of divine love makes its possessor pious in all things, chaste, gentle, and very humble, and, both in adversity and in prosperity, always calm in the same constancy of mind, without disturbance.

The monk who truly loves the Lord God, according to the rule of our most holy father Benedict, always obeys the commands of his seniors cheerfully in all things; in hard and contrary circumstances he embraces patience, and everywhere preserves true humility. The love of God makes its possessor richest in all good works, and renders him far removed from any vices whatever. The lover of God does not murmur at the command of obedience; in [reproofs?] he does not rashly excuse himself; he does not touch his neighbor with the gall of bitterness, nor is he disturbed even when injured. He who is a true lover of God keeps his life irreproachable in all purity of mind and body, and demonstrates in himself a fervent charity not only in word but also in deed and in truth.

Whatever in the world is beautiful, whatever is pleasant or delightful to behold, whatever flatters the wantonness of lascivious flesh, the true lover of God most steadfastly despises it all, and desires nothing other than to please Christ and to live with him [unclear]. Great and incredible to those who do not know it is the force of divine love, which always impels a man to work well, and never permits him to live idle. For what else is the inward love of God than a sweet drunkenness of the soul, by which a man is snatched away from all the consolations of the world, and, as though he felt nothing of what mortals do, feasts in the banquet of the Holy Spirit alone?

The monk who is a true lover of God has put aside, for Christ’s sake, every curiosity of the world and concern for worldly things; and, as though he did not feel them, he transcends in his mind all the stings of cares. He cries out with the apostle: “The world is naked and crucified to me, and I to the world.” O wine of divine love, how sweetly you intoxicate, how innocently you satisfy, how wisely you make foolish, how chastely you inflame. For everyone who has been intoxicated by your savor, separated from every tumult of carnal pleasures, rests sweetly in the shadow of the Holy Spirit. Because of the excessive drunkenness of the spirit, he speaks things alien to carnal and worldly people, and nothing contrary to God is heard from his mouth. This is the unstained satiety of spiritual wine, which is extended by desire the more, the longer it is continued.

O holy and venerable simplicity of the lover of Christ, which, although to many who savor earthly things it may seem to be folly, nevertheless before God and all his saints is the greatest and truly highest wisdom. Let the world have its prudence, let it have honors, dignities, and riches; but I would choose the merit of one simple monk truly loving God rather than the sovereignty of the whole world. The love of God is both the highest pleasure of the soul and always the most chaste; its fire illuminates the mind and gently inflames the affection. The love of God is the fountain of heavenly grace, because the grace of the Holy Spirit makes fruitful, with the fruits of all the virtues, the mind which the charity of Christ purifies by plowing. The love of Christ wins the friendship of almighty God for men, and reveals to mortals the will of the highest majesty.

The love of God makes a monk acceptable to God, because he requires from us not knowledge, not learning, not eloquence, but charity. Therefore the monk who has publicly offered himself to the Lord God ought to care for nothing except God, lest he seem to have a divided heart; since, just as God is one, so love directed toward God cannot be divided.
