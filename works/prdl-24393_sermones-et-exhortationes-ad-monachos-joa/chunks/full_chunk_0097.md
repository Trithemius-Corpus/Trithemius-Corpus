*Homily XXI.*

[unclear]: for love of him you have set me, an unworthy man, over you as abbot. For I shall not be troublesome to you if you run well; but if anyone turns aside from the way of truth, I am compelled, even unwillingly, to meet him as best I can.

Let the monk always think on his entrance into the monastery, and never forget the first purpose of his profession. In all your works also, O monk, always consider that you are not long-lived upon the earth, but will shortly depart from this life, and that perhaps when you do not expect it, you will die. Therefore keep inviolate the purpose of holiness, and always be watchful in good works, so that when the Lord comes and calls you, he may find you prepared. Remember that you are a pilgrim on earth, and do not have here a lasting city; but just as a traveler hastens quickly toward the end of his journey, so death draws near to you daily without ceasing. Therefore, while you are healthy, set the house of your conscience in order, so that you may not fear death; for rarely does he die well who is not prepared as though he were to die daily.

How securely the monk departs from this life who, keeping his conscience clean in all purity, lives at all times, without reproach, according to the full integrity of the rule of our most holy lawgiver, even unto death. Let your mind continually hold in memory our Lord Jesus Christ as present, indeed as nowhere absent; and let no hour pass in which you do not think with thanksgiving of him suffering for you. Stand in humility and true obedience, and no adversities will ever prevail against you. Just as a tree sending many great roots into the earth is not easily torn from its place, so the monk who possesses charity and obedience in humility, together with the other virtues, is by no means overcome by demons. Obedience, brothers, is a great virtue, which brings the other virtues into the mind, and, once brought in, preserves them through the ministry of humility. May our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who became obedient for us unto the death of the cross, illumine your hearts by his grace, and sweetly inflame your minds at all times for the observance of regular life; who lives and reigns with God the Father, in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, unto all ages of ages. Amen.

*On continence and true chastity: Homily XXI.*

I would most earnestly wish, my dearest brothers, that I had obtained from God that grace by which I might be for you a worthy and suitable shepherd, both in deed and in speech. For I know, and it does not escape me, how perilous the governance of souls is for a young and inexperienced man such as I am, when one who has not yet learned to direct and govern himself according to the rule of justice presumes to approach the instruction of others. I blush to open my mouth among you to teach, since, having been a disciple for a short time, I am compelled before maturity to be a teacher. But since it so pleased you, and you wished to have me, a young man, as master, pardon me, because necessity compels me to fulfill, as best I can, the office committed to me.

Assist me with your prayers, I beg you, brothers, so that the mercy of God may grant the strength that the command of authority denies. For I desire you to walk before God in all purity of mind and body, so that you may please him to whom you handed yourselves over from the beginning. Having taken him as the leader of the journey to heaven, look upon our Lord Jesus Christ, who, born of the ever most chaste Virgin Mary, perpetually preserved the most perfect chastity of mind and body. Imitate this teacher, into whose service you have consecrated your hands, so that you may preserve not only in body the continence enjoined upon you, but also with a mind brighter than sapphire and carbuncle may perpetually guard chastity.

For chastity is a most beautiful virtue, especially fitting for cloistered men, so that under the yoke of reason it may restrain every assault of lust, and, the madness of vices being extinguished, the bearing of the body may preserve all honorableness. An incontinent and wanton monk, who follows the filthy lust of the flesh, is counted with the demons who withdrew from the Lord.

There are three things whose perpetual observance [unclear] our [unclear], without which no one among monks is to be judged worthy of being named. The first of these is the obedience of humble and true subjection; the second, the renunciation of all property, as the rule commands; the third, the inviolate guardianship of perpetual continence and chastity. Whatever one of these the monk violates, he corrupts the purpose of his order. Chastity is sustained by two companions: fasting, namely, and temperance; if it is ever deprived of these, it quickly, without doubt, collapses. The chastity of monks is the greatest ornament, by which, adorned, it will lead them into the fellowship of the angels.
