*All. III. Fol. IX.*

It restrains anger; it treads down the proud; it loves the humble; it quiets the discordant; it reconciles enemies. It is calm toward all, burdensome to none, troublesome to none, onerous to none; it does not know how to exalt itself, it does not know how to be puffed up. Whoever receives this, let him hold it; whoever has lost it, let him seek it again; whoever has let it go, let him search it out, because whoever is not found in it is disowned by the Father, disinherited by the Son, and, no less, is made alien to the Holy Spirit. He can never come to the inheritance of God who refused to observe the testament of peace. For the place of God is in peace, but the devil’s dwelling is in anger and disturbance.

Therefore every person who does not have this threefold peace in his heart, who does not offer it in his mouth, nor show it in his works, is to be called neither a monk nor a Christian. Hence the Lord says in the Gospel: Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God. If those who make peace are called sons of God, beyond doubt those who confound peace are sons of Satan. Therefore, my dearest brothers, have among yourselves that peace which the world cannot give, which is the peace of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, concerning which Isaiah the prophet said: And the work of justice shall be peace, and the service of justice silence, and security forever. For God is the Lord of peace and love; therefore whoever violates peace and brotherly love shows that he is not a true lover of God. Let the true peace of Christ be in your hearts, and you will fear no adversities of this world.

Let true peace also be among you, not feigned, because Christ desires peace not from the mouth but from the heart, and wishes one’s neighbor to be greeted by affection rather than by the lips. Therefore if any one of you has in anything saddened his brother, let him make satisfaction to him as soon as possible before the setting of the sun, and take care, all of you, that the sun not set upon your anger before him; and, falling at his knees, ask pardon. And the sword, says the Spirit, which is the word of God, through every prayer and supplication, let us, soldiers of Christ, always hold in our hands. The word of God, which is contained in the holy scriptures, is a victorious defense against all assaults of demons, as we are admonished by the example of our Savior, who, when tempted by the devil, with tranquil gentleness hurled the testimonies of the divine scriptures in his face, saying: It is written, Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.

And you, monk, serving as a soldier for Christ, whenever you are tempted by the devil, at once impress the sign of the holy cross upon your forehead, and say with Saint John Chrysostom in a certain homily: I renounce you, Satan, with all your iniquities, and I join myself to you, Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, because it is written: The soul that has sinned, it shall die. This is the sword of the Spirit, most sharp on both sides; if, from the very beginning of temptation, you always brandish it in your hands against the enemy, with God protecting you, you will easily overcome all his contrivances and snares. For scripture says: Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

And so, rising from bed in the morning, at once make that protestation which I have described against the adversary of your salvation, and neither the devil nor any perverse person will be able to harm you by incantation or sorcery, whether that be [unclear] or something else. Do this same thing when you are about to sleep at night, and whenever you perceive some temptation of the malignant spirit rising up in your thought. And thus you will be safe in all your ways, and the devil will flee far from you and will not dare to come nearer to you, unless your own sloth first trips you up, so that either you cast away the sword or immediately commit some mortal sin. For scripture says: Those who commit iniquity and sin are enemies of their own soul. Solomon also says in Proverbs: His own iniquities seize the impious man, and by the cords [unclear]. If, therefore, brothers, you wish to be secure under the protection of Almighty God, flee sin, because sin separates us from the help of the Lord, just as purity and cleanness of conscience join us to God, as Isaiah the prophet says: Behold, the hand of the Lord is not shortened so that it cannot save, nor is his ear heavy so that it cannot hear; but your iniquities have divided between you and your God, and your sins have hidden his face from you, so that he would not hear.
