For Scripture says to the Lord: “Those who distance themselves from you will perish, and you have destroyed all who work iniquity.” For the farther the mind has withdrawn through acedia from the fervor of divine love, the nearer it is to hell and despair, and the more rarely and with greater difficulty does it recover strength for true penitence.

Whoever among you, therefore, my brothers, desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the inward fervor of the spirit, let him always strive with the greatest effort to drive from himself the torpor of mind and acedia, and daily to renew the holy purpose of divine love. It is very hard, I confess, to resist the depraved habit of the flesh, and in an old heart to think new thoughts against custom. Certainly, brothers, no one can rise from the depths of hell with a lukewarm will, nor will anyone be able to overcome an evil habit without great violence and a long-lasting contrary habit.

Therefore, even if no one becomes holy by his own powers, but it is God who works all things in all, nevertheless it is plainly clear to us that the kingdom of God suffers violence; and he who neglects to bring violence upon his own fleshly impulses does not attain to the fellowship of the saints of the Lord. Hear now, monk, whoever you are who are lukewarm and acedious toward the love of God; hear, I say, and tremble, because unless you immediately rise from the evil habit of a torpid life, unless you inflict force upon yourself through a contrary habit, unless you earnestly prepare your heart for the fervor of divine love, you will never see the face of God in glory.

Acedia is the root of despair; and not only its root, but also its nurse and mother. If it has once oppressed the mind of a person dedicated to God, it at once closes off every way to salvation. For the kingdom of heaven is not promised to sleepers; the blessedness of eternity is not prepared for those growing torpid in idleness and sloth. Rather, the crown is laid up for those who manfully command the impulses of the flesh, and those who live by the spirit, not by the flesh, are endowed with the glory of eternal felicity.

No virtue is acquired without labor; no enemy is overcome without battle. If you wish to be crowned, conquer; if you desire to conquer, it is necessary that you fight bravely and perseveringly. “How long, sluggard, will you sleep?” Solomon cries to you. “When will you rise from your sleep? If you are diligent, your harvest will come like a spring, and poverty will flee far from you.” For he who gathers at harvest is a wise son, and he who snores in summer is a son of confusion. For he who works his land will be satisfied with bread, but he who follows idleness will be filled with want.

Now, brothers, is the time for doing good; now the opportunity of meriting eternal blessedness is granted to us by the Lord God, and it is suddenly cut off in death. For Scripture says: “Whatever your hand can do, work at it earnestly, because neither work nor reason, nor wisdom nor knowledge, will be among the dead, to whom you are hastening.” If the good of eternal blessedness that is promised delights you, the labors of the struggle that are set before you ought not to frighten the mind. For no one will be crowned by the Lord unless he has contended lawfully; no one will come to the summit of eternal height unless by the fervor of the spirit he has overcome the illicit impulses of the flesh.

We must always fight, brothers, against the threefold battle-line of the devil as long as we live in this pilgrimage, because here he becomes insuperable to this extent: he is not overcome little by little by one who grows lukewarm. Hence Saint Augustine proclaims: “No one ought to be secure in this life, which is wholly called temptation, lest he who was able to become better from worse should at some time become worse from better.” And so Solomon too in the Book of Proverbs says: “Desires kill the sluggard.” For just as constancy bestows perfection in every work, so acedia is the destroyer and stepmother of all religion and of the spiritual progress of the soul.

For, as blessed Jerome says, nothing in a holy purpose is worse than idleness, which not only does not acquire new things, but also shamefully consumes what was previously acquired. The rule of a holy spiritual life advances and grows with joy by continuous progress; by cessation, however, it grows torpid and fails. Therefore, brothers, the mind must be renewed by daily and fresh increases of virtues, and this journey of living is to be measured not by what has passed, but by what remains.

Therefore flee idleness above all, since it is the mother and nurse of all vices; but overcome acedia not by fleeing it, but by resisting it bravely. Scripture says: “Idleness has taught much evil.” For every idle person is occupied with vain and pernicious desires.
