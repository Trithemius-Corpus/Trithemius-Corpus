He who does not repent while healthy is foolish; and he who does not amend his faults when he can, by the just judgment of God, loses the power that has been deferred. For repentance is then fruitful when, by freedom of choice, it has been voluntary; but when the danger of death is already pressing, it seems rather to be necessary than undertaken by the repentant person's own choice.

Let no one say in his heart, my brothers, let no one think that the labor of repentance is hard, and therefore that it should be put off until the very last particle of this life. That thought is exceedingly dangerous, since the fixed end of life, which divine providence has appointed for each person, just as he has wished it to be hidden from all, so he will not prolong it for anyone who intentionally defers repentance.

If we wish to be safe and secure in conscience, let us do penance while we are healthy, while we are able to sin, while no necessity of approaching death yet presses us. For it is a very doubtful matter to do penance when necessity already compels, and the imminence of death makes you no longer able to sin, when, that is, not you have dismissed your sins, but they have dismissed you, powerless before them.

O how many, deceived by the hope of a longer life, descend into the abyss of eternal damnation, who when healthy scorned to do penance. For the longer you have deferred repentance, the less disposed to repent you will always be found. For wickedness gains strength with time, and there is no sin so small that it does not grow when neglected. Therefore embrace repentance while you are healthy, and do not put it off from day to day, since you understand what you possess today, but you do not know what will be tomorrow. For each new day brings its own inconveniences, and he who today is not disposed toward repentance will perhaps be less fit tomorrow.

But let none of you say, “What have I sinned, that repentance should seem necessary to me, when my whole life is nothing other than a certain hard and prolonged necessity of repenting?” You have heard Scripture testify that there is no man on earth who does good and does not sin, since no one is clean before God. For even if you do not commit the great crimes of human impiety, such as homicide, theft, adultery, fornications, and things like these, nevertheless, because you have been established in that calling in which you ought with all your strength to raise every effort toward the state of perfection, sins that are small for mortals living in the world are certainly imputed to you as more serious.

For all sins, so far as possible, must be avoided by cloistered men, not only those that impede salvation but also those that stain the perfection enjoined upon them. And who among you lives with as much purity as monastic perfection requires? It is a great sin for a monk if he has set down his effort from perfection, if he has lived negligently in the monastery, if he has transgressed even one commandment of the canon and the Gospel. And who among you is there, brothers, who loves the Lord God with such sincerity, such charity, and such fervor of love as a man is bound to love his maker and redeemer? Or whom will you give me from among all of you who does not at times exceed, either in taking food or in drink, more than befits the integrity of monks? Which of you does not offend in speech, or who is not sometimes troubled by evil thoughts? Whom among you has acedia not struck, and whom has sad sloth not disturbed? Who is there in this small congregation of yours whom pride does not touch, and whom impatience has not quite often made murmuring?

Let each one of you consider the paths of his way of life, and he will find that he fails in many things, even at any moment. For what if a monk has committed no evil? What if he has kept himself clean from this wicked age? He will not be able to be perfect unless he has both turned away from evil and done the good that his perfection requires. For one without the other does not suffice, but from both perfection rises up in a man.

Therefore, brothers, we must always keep watch with the highest zeal, so that we may satisfy the institution of the holy fathers, according to which, by public profession, we have solemnly obligated ourselves to live, in order that without interruption we may direct our effort toward the perfection set before us. Let not the consideration of our own weakness draw us back from holy effort, since we are aided no less by the grace of the creator than we run by our own desire. Just as every day, in many things, by living rather negligently, we fail on any occasion, so also at each moment, rising from the torpor of mind, let us renew holy effort in a good purpose.
