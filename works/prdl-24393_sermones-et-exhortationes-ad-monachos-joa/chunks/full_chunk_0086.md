No drowsy, lazy, and slothful person has ever come to the kingdom of God; no one has obtained heaven without continual labor and much sweat. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: The kingdom of God suffers violence, and the violent, that is, those who have learned to resist steadfastly the evil desires of the flesh, seize it.

Review the deeds of all the saints, and nowhere will you find anyone made holy without great and continual labor. For although no one becomes holy by his own powers, nevertheless, by the gracious mercy of the almighty Lord, which grants to those who struggle both the will to do good and the strength, no one is sanctified without works of a good and right will. Since, therefore, God wishes all human beings to be saved and none to perish, we, my brothers, must take the greatest care not to receive his grace in vain, but rather must strive with all our powers, so that, as [a field?] [unclear], we may always respond to the free gifts of so great a sower with worthy fruits from works of good will.

Let us therefore arm ourselves with the shield of faith, and put on the breastplate of steadfastness and saving patience, so that, prepared against all things that can befall us, through humility and the fervor of the Holy Spirit, with our Lord Jesus Christ helping us, we may conquer. In all these things patience is very necessary for us; without its aid there never was, nor could there ever be, perseverance in good and holy works. For there was no other road to heaven for the saints than patience, nor could anyone ever be sanctified who cast patience away.

Indeed, all who have pleased almighty God from the beginning of the world down to this our age have triumphed over the prince of the world in no other way than through patience. Hence all of them could most rightly say that saying of the psalmist: For your sake we are put to death all day long; we are regarded as sheep for slaughter. The most holy apostle of Christ, Paul, also, commemorating the patience of the saints of old, says among other things: They went about in sheepskins and goatskins, needy, distressed, afflicted, of whom the world was not worthy, wandering in deserts, in mountains, in caves and caverns of the earth. For all who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution, because, as the prophet says: Many are the tribulations of the righteous, and from all these the Lord will deliver them.

Patience, therefore, is necessary for all mortals, and it especially befits cloistered persons, since just as adversities are never lacking to the righteous in this world, so no one will be justified without patience. There are, moreover, three principal degrees of patience, as the diligence of the ancients has distinguished them. The first, and weakest, is that of those people who neither seek adversities nor willingly endure them, but when they occur are weighed down in spirit and cast down by sorrow. They even complain at times and murmur for a while against those who inflict them; but after the inflamed agitation of the mind has passed, they return to themselves, and seem to endure patiently, without vengeance, the insult inflicted upon them. Yet the patience of these people, being of no great virtue, is always very near to the fault of an unquiet mind, and therefore must often be judged of no merit, and sometimes of lesser merit.

And although such people in our times are said to have the virtue of patience, who, though they do not seek to suffer evils, nevertheless when they come receive them willingly and kindly, and embrace them most patiently as a gift sent from God. And such people are truly to be called patient, who, kindled by divine love, endure with equanimity whatever adversity may come, and in tribulation preserve the same disposition that they had before. I would not doubt that the most holy Job the Idumaean was of their number: struck not only by extreme adversity, he bore all things patiently, but even when placed in the abyss of evils, giving thanks, he blessed the Lord, saying: The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done; blessed be the name of the Lord.
