The thirteenth sign of true humility is not only to despise praises and favors in the truth of one’s mind, but also prudently to avoid those by whom one sees oneself being praised. Those who are truly humble flee their praisers, and whenever they are praised, they fall silent, answering nothing. The esteem of a great reputation is burdensome to the humble man, and he is confounded in himself all the more, the more he is proclaimed by men. For true humility, when blamed, advances; when praised, it runs into danger.

The fourteenth perfect sign of monastic humility: in corrections and disparagements, if he suffers any, he conceives spiritual joy and a great increase of divine love. When wronged he is pierced with compunction, and is provoked much more to contempt of the world. Whenever he is reproved insultingly by anyone, if he is humble, he judges himself justly corrected. For he does not consider his own innocence in the accusation, but, humble and compunct, weighs that he has gravely offended God in other matters.

The fifteenth sign of perfect humility: he never kicks back against one correcting him, but preserves silence in all tranquility of mind. He does not cast even the very well-known vices of his reprover in his face, nor does he seek any grounds for argument. He does not complain that insult is being done to him; he sets no excuse against a rash man, nor is he accustomed to answer words with words, but, as if mute, to be silent toward all things.

The sixteenth sign of deep humiliation: he despises all comforts of the world and seeks no friendships of mortals. True serene humility, which is assumed for the love of Christ, delights nowhere but in Christ alone. But one cannot stand in the love of Christ unless he has learned to deny both his own will and the world with all its disordered pleasures. Thus the Lord says to him in the holy Gospel: If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, and even his own soul, he cannot be my disciple.

The seventeenth sign of humility: he does not divide himself into many things; he does not greatly seek the aid and benefits of men, but humbly remains in his own moderation, or even extremity. For benefits received from men he is not accustomed to applaud much, but to show himself grateful with few and simple words. Little gifts weaken the minds of the infirm, and often render the unwary useless and superfluous in speech. Indeed, he who is truly humble does not delight in the conversations of givers, because they often hinder the purity of his purpose. The love of Christ does not love publicity.

The eighteenth sign of humility: from the heart he suffers with all who suffer for the love of Christ, and most patiently bears the defects and infirmities of others, and prays for the correction of those who offend. For true humility shows to sinners and the weak the bowels of piety and compassion; but false and simulated humility, because it is truly pride, swells against the frail with hard and indiscreet severity, and often becomes the cause of danger when in correcting it exceeds the rule of charity. But he who is truly humble always fears to exceed the due measure of correction, and therefore is more inclined to mercy than to excessive severity.

The nineteenth sign of true humility: the mind perseveres free from all envy, and he favors and prays for all men. He envies no one the gifts of divine kindness; he favors the grace of God in all, and rejoices together in the progress of others. He judges no one unworthy of God’s benefits; he thinks all better than himself. He considers the advantage of his neighbors his own, because he loves all as himself.

The twentieth sign of perfect humility: with bitterness of heart he recalls to memory the multitude of his sins, and therefore thinks himself unworthy of every benefit and grace of God. Hence, seeking the depth of humility, he imitates the evangelical publican, who had remembrance of his own offenses and did not wish to lift his eyes to heaven. For the monk who is truly humble by the love of Christ always bears in his heart the holy sorrow of compunction and repentance for the evils he has done.

The twenty-first sign of cenobitic humility: he trusts the judgment of others more than his own, and because of Christ subjects his own will wholly to another’s command. Let those among you who follow the judgment of their own head attend whether they can or will apprehend this sign of monastic humility. For today we see few observers of true monastic humility, though we see many monks professing it. O times, O morals, in which we all promise humility, and very few of us truly discern how to keep what has been promised inviolate.
