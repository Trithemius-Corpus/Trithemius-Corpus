*On the love of man toward God.*

...in prosperity long-suffering and at the same time tranquil. The love of Christ teaches one truly to despise the world, and to reckon as nothing all its delights and all the comforts of the flesh. The love of Christ swiftly suppresses the illicit movements of the flesh and brings the purest chastity into the mind. The love of Christ sweetly lifts the mind with desire to heavenly things, and keeps it, separated from earthly things, in spiritual exercise.

The love of God lightens all hard and harsh things, for where Christ is loved in truth, tribulations and any adversities whatever are counted as delights. The love of God is always constant in good works and is never idle. The love of God despises present things and strongly raises the mind to eternal things. The love of God neither seeks honors nor seeks riches, but, joined to Christ by desire, covets nothing at all except him. The love of God covers the multitude of sins and remits all offenses to the penitent. The love of God suddenly justifies the impious; and the more love has increased, the more quickly and strongly perfection also rises up.

The love of God is a tower of strength, to which all the ministers of diabolical wickedness cannot draw near. The love of God is the consummation of all justice and the end of monastic discipline. The love of God perfects the monk and makes him consummate in all good works. The love of God is the sweet yoke of inward sweetness, by which all filth of earthly corruption is driven out from the mind. The love of God honors souls and makes them pure and immaculate from all stains. The love of God perfectly illuminates the mind in every knowledge of heavenly honors, and purges the very affection from all unclean vanities.

The love of God makes those who truly have it most learned and wondrously instructed in all Scripture. The love of God renders the monk fearsome to all enemies and bestows angelic power on those who persevere. The love of God defends a man in dangers, since whoever truly loves God fears no opposing things. The love of God makes the one who has it secure in the hour of death and safe from demons, and joins the soul to the heavenly hosts. The love of God leads its possessor back to the heavenly homeland and bestows everlasting glory on those who love God.

The love of God is the greatest of all good works and the single foundation of all virtues in the faith of Christ. The love of God is the best ointment, in which all wounds of languishing souls are healed. The love of God is an abundant remedy for poverty and want, and an effective consolation above every possession of riches. The love of Christ far surpasses all the learning of this world, and forms those who have it most perfectly for every knowledge necessary to salvation. The love of God prepares a delightful dwelling for the Holy Spirit in the heart of man, and preserves him, as he perseveres, in all purity.

The love of God is the swiftest of all teachers, because what carnal men learn over time and with much study, the Holy Spirit has handed on in a moment to those who love God. The love of God gives knowledge to little ones and makes rustics more learned than the educated. The love of God is stronger than all things and by its power rules over all things. The love of God commands demons by a single word and puts them to flight from bodies possessed by the sign of the cross. The love of God bestowed eternal blessedness on the holy spirits in heaven, whom the grace of the Creator confirmed as lovers.

The love of God beatifies in eternal felicity [unclear] baptized in the faith of Jesus Christ, because no one has ever been able to reach the fellowship of angels unless the persevering love of God was proved to have inflamed him. For the love of God alone is what makes works both good and meritorious, since God accepts from us only that gift of a good work which the flame of charity has made worthy in his sight. Whatever is done outside charity is judged unworthy in the sight of God.

Now attend, my brothers, to our admonitions, and carefully consider how necessary it is for you to love God above all things, if you desire to arrive at his kingdom and to triumph happily with him forever. Let none of you deceive his soul with empty words, so that he believes that he truly loves God whose commandments he either despises or neglects to keep. You have heard the Lord Jesus saying in the holy Gospel: “He who loves me will keep my words.” And as the holy pope Gregory says, the performance of the commanded work is the proof of the love enjoined. For then we truly love the Lord our God with our whole heart, when, having despised worldly pleasures, we perfectly keep all his commandments even unto death.
