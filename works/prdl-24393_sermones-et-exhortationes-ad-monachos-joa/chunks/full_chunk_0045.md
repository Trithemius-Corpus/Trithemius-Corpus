Let us recall all the words of him who says: “You who have been weaned from milk, you who have been drawn away from the breast, expect tribulation upon tribulation, hope upon hope, yet a little while, because of the wickedness of lips, because of the malignant tongue.” Those weaned and drawn away from the breast are men advanced in age, whose part it is to receive tribulation upon tribulation for God’s sake, so that they may deserve to bring back hope upon hope, as sacred Scripture says: “But we glory also in our tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience; and patience, trial; and trial, hope. And hope does not confound, because the charity of God has been poured forth in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.” For through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. For by temptations we become known to ourselves, and we find out whether we truly fear God. Hence the Lord speaks through Moses to the people, saying: “The Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him with all your heart and with all your soul.”

This world, dearly beloved brothers, is to be compared to a furnace, in which the fire of temptation and adversity continually burns, and through which we must necessarily pass. In this furnace of tribulation the chaff is burned up, but gold and silver are cleansed of every stain. The chaff is turned into ash; the gold is kept for the ornament and beauty of the royal palace. The furnace, I say, is the world; the fire is temptation or tribulation; the gold is righteous men; the chaff, however, is the unrighteous and sinners; the craftsman who purifies the gold is almighty God himself, who favors our progress and cooperates by grace toward the merit of virtue. Therefore let us do what God wills; what he imposes, let us patiently bear for love of him, since, as the holy [apostle of Christ?] says, “the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that shall be revealed in us.” Hence Saint Jerome says: “Who would not wish to be troubled? Who would not desire to be reviled, that he may gain being praised by Christ’s voice and be rewarded with an abundant heavenly recompense? Would that, because of the name of my Lord and because of justice, the whole crowd of unbelievers might persecute and trouble me. Would that this whole world might rise up to my reproach, provided only that I may deserve to be praised by Christ and hasten toward the reward of his promise.” And the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men.”

Therefore, whichever of you, my brothers, desires to come to the kingdom of heaven, whichever of you cares above all to please God, let him learn gladly to receive the tribulations of temptations for love of him, and joyfully to endure whatever adversity shall occur. For there is none of us who does not need daily purification, none who does not offend almost at every moment. If, then, we love our own salvation, if we demand that our sins be forgiven us and that we be cleansed from every stain of heart, let us patiently sustain whatever adversity happens to us, since the evils of tribulation, which the soul patiently tolerates for love of God, cleanse it more than the good works which it now performs of its own will in this world.

Great matter for suffering has been left to us in this place: we who are heavily pressed by need, weighed down by poverty, and also constrained by the debt of borrowed money. What adversity do we not experience in this monastery? What storms and tempests do we not suffer, brothers? By what inconveniences are we not harassed? How desolate all the goods of this monastery were when, fifteen years ago, regular observance was introduced, none of you can be ignorant. But the tribulation of common want rages more severely against me, who must provide the necessities of life for you, and very often do not sufficiently know from what source I may pay. Yet your patience consoles my spirit, and the hope which you have conceived in the Lord, the savior of the humble, so that I do not doubt that his mercy will be present in time; and I hear him saying to me through Isaiah the prophet: “Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.” Gladly, then, I shall have patience in the poverty which, God thus ordaining, we now suffer, so that after death we may deserve to come to the riches of eternity. For the more, in this our pilgrimage, we shall have been afflicted by persecutions, poverty, need, labors, and tribulations, the greater rewards we shall obtain in the future recompense of the righteous, when God grants them. Therefore with me, my brothers, patiently endure whatever almighty God has preordained us to suffer, since, as the holy pope Gregory says, confidence in almighty God is then certain
