*All. II. Fol. VI.*

If you have kept chastity with humility for the love of my name even to the end; if you have always striven to please me alone in all your actions; if you have taken care to follow me without interruption along the steep path of the virtues: knowing without doubt, know most certainly that I shall hold you, made from a servant into a very dear friend, as a soldier to be crowned with me in my kingdom; I shall love you; I shall cherish you; I shall set you gloriously forever in the midst of my saints. Hear me now, my servant, you who, out of reverence and honor for my name, have enrolled yourself in the warfare of monks; and with a great and perfect heart follow the footprints of my feet, just as that blessed teacher of yours, once your friend, taught you and strove with all his efforts to imitate. Let not the enticements of the flesh deceive you, O my strenuous soldier; let not the vain and pernicious dealings of this world seduce your soul, dedicated to me; [unclear] of the unclean spirit, which no mortal has ever been able to escape. Let those last things which you must necessarily undergo always remain in your memory; and from their swiftest passage take up for yourself the necessary dread and contempt of the pleasures of the flesh, which are passing away, and of the future things of this sojourning. Just as brief labor borne patiently and charitably in the world for my sake prepares eternal happiness for my soldiers, so the desires and pleasures of the flesh, which quickly pass away, sink the soul of a sinful man down to hell to be tormented without end. Therefore let all your desires be toward me, monk, you who serve me and who follow me along the steep path of regular observance. Give me your heart if you desire happiness; seek me alone if you love your own life and salvation. When you have loved God, you are tranquil and holy; when you have followed him, you are blessed and glorious without end.

What would you do, my brothers, if it should happen to you to hear these words which we have spoken from the mouth of our savior and king as he spoke them? What, I ask, would you do? I certainly believe that there would be great compunction in your hearts, if you heard Christ speaking personally in this way. But we, holding Christ’s place in the monastery, speak the words which he has given, and you do not attend; we declare to you the necessary contest of salvation, and you show no signs of compunction. If you do not know what cause there was for my saying these things, I now beg you to understand. I see certain of you abhorring sacred readings, given over to vanities, and caring for nothing less than the perfection of spiritual warfare; their god is their belly, and their glory is in confusion. Do you suppose, most worthless scoundrels, who have no taste for anything spiritual, that Christ will come again from heaven to speak to you those things which he entrusted to his preachers to be spoken? Have you not heard Paul saying: Christ, rising from the dead, now dies no more; death will no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God. Once Christ was seen on earth; once he appeared visibly among mortals; once he preached the way of eternal salvation; he will not add a second time. He appointed pastors and vicars over his household to teach the ignorant; wishing to manifest their authority, he said in the Gospel: He who hears you hears me, and he who despises you despises me. Therefore hear those whom Christ has set over you as rectors, to whom he entrusted the distribution of spiritual wheat, as he said in the holy Gospel: Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward whom the lord appointed over his household, to give them wheat in due season? For you know what divine Scripture says to us: The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night. Beware, I beg you, of delaying the good, because none of you knows when he is to die; rather, attend to that true saying of the wanton poet Naso, who admonishes thus: But hasten, and do not put yourself off to coming hours; he who is not ready today will be less ready tomorrow.
