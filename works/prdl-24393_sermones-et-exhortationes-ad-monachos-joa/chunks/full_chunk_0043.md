*Homily IX.*
*Folio XXII.*

...generates the necessity of sinning. Here is the hardest dominion of death; here the deepest depth of evils. Whoever has once entered this abyss of sins rarely, indeed not easily, rises again to purity of heart without the special grace of God and the greatest labor of repentance. By his own will the sinner has fallen into this pit of death; but without God’s mercy he will not be able to rise again. Most harsh is the yoke of evil habit: if anyone has once submitted his neck to it, he rarely recovers his former freedom of spirit.

All these things are wrought by perverse thought when it is admitted into the mind and, through delight, is changed into consent and deed. For no thought is so small that it does not gradually grow if it is not driven from the mind. Blessed, therefore, is that monk who casts all his thought upon the Lord; who always and everywhere denies a place to wicked thoughts; who despises all the perishable riches of the world; who scorns all comforts of the body. He prepares the cleanness of his heart for his Lord; he loves nothing in this world except the Lord; he orders all his thoughts to the glory of the Savior, and observes his commandments with the utmost diligence.

What is happier than that monk who presents himself to the world not as a citizen but as a pilgrim? He lives in the world as if he were not in the world; indeed, greater than the world is he whose whole conversation is in heaven. Consider now, therefore, dearest brothers, the shortness of this time, and always remember that you are mortal and will soon pass away. For we have here no abiding city, but seek the one to come; and since we know that so many thousands of men have gone before us through death, let us consider how soon we too must necessarily die.

Now indeed a short time is granted to us, but one very acceptable, in which we may work the fruits of eternal life. For now is the time for laboring; now is the hour for sowing good, because after death each one will receive the reward of his labors, when no further opportunity for laboring is granted. Therefore, my brothers, anticipate this hour of death with holy works, and do not fail now in the seedtime of labor, so that in eternal recompense you may rejoice without end. Let no day pass on which each one of you has not become better, and has not added some new good work to those he has already performed. Always think on the price of the harvest to come, and let no greatness of labor terrify you.

*On The Daily Struggle Of Temptation: Homily Ten.*

In yesterday’s sermon, dearest brothers, I admonished your charities to resist the beginnings of evil thoughts, and with all your strength to prepare yourselves by good and holy works for the prize of the future reward. But since, as Scripture bears witness, the life of man upon earth is temptation, I have thought it necessary to discuss this same matter in your hearing, as much as the Lord shall grant, so that, having been forewarned, you may be able to fight more strongly against the invisible adversary.

You know the words of the wise man, who says in Ecclesiasticus: “Son, when you come to the service of God, stand in justice, and prepare your soul for temptation.” Let no one think himself free from temptation, brothers, so long as he walks in this mortal way of life; but the more anyone advances in the fear of God, the more cautiously let him know that he must live. For Saint Pope Gregory says: “The more our enemy sees that rebellion is made against him, the more fully he strives to attack. For he neglects to strike those whom he feels he possesses by undisturbed right.” The devil pursues the one fleeing from him and endeavors to overthrow the one who rebels against his command.

You, O men, the more you advance in the spiritual way of life, the more urgently you must keep watch against the adversary of your salvation. For, as Saint John Chrysostom says, our adversary never sleeps, but always keeps watch for the destruction of the human race; none of us can resist him except the one who has learned to keep watch at all times in the fear of the Lord. And Saint Leo, pope of the city of Rome, says in a certain sermon: “The ancient enemy does not cease to spread everywhere the snares of temptations, so that in whatever way he may be able, by corrupting the faith of believers, to blacken it. He knows to whom he should offer the enticements of the flesh, into whom he should pour the poison of envy, before whom he should set the incitements of lust. He knows whom he should disturb with sorrow, whom he should deceive with joy, whom he should oppress with fear, whom he should seduce by admiration [unclear].”
