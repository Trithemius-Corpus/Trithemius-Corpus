For the monk who, for God’s sake, of his own free will binds himself to the perpetual obedience of religion, if he lives rightly according to his vow, surely bears a prolonged martyrdom. Obedience, therefore, is martyrdom without the shedding of blood; and voluntarily to subject oneself to a man for God’s sake is a great torment [of the flesh?]. The soul that truly loves humility and obedience undoubtedly advances to the crown of martyrdom. It is a hard work and an exceedingly laborious contest for a free man to despise himself, and to resign himself wholly, and all his will, perpetually under the command and power of another. But let us behold with faithful eyes the reward of this contest, and we shall reckon all the hard and rough things of this life to be like a certain childish game, which is now seen for an hour [unclear] will appear afterwards. Hence the apostle, speaking in the person of a lover of Christ, says: The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the coming glory that shall be revealed in us. The crown that martyrdom once bestowed on the saints for Christ, humble obedience will today prepare for the true lovers of Christ. For a monk who is truly humble and obedient for Christ dies secure, without fear, since, serving faithfully under the command of his elder, he is freed from every demand for an account. For he who, living at the nod of his shepherd, walks uprightly in all commands, can commit nothing that may be brought before the judge. He is defended by the testimony of the abbot, according to whose direction he has lived rightly and without complaint. Consider, brothers, the law of obedience according to which you have promised to live, and know that you have made necessary what had been voluntary, when you solemnly consented to the sentence of monastic tradition. It is no longer lawful for you to withdraw from your holy purpose, nor can you revoke a vow once uttered. If you do everything according to the holy Gospel of Christ and the rule of father Benedict, which you have vowed, let there be no doubtful hesitation for you: you will be blessed, and you will receive from the Lord the reward owed to the saints. But if, unmindful of your promises, you violate the vow of holy purity made to God, if you live according to the flesh in contempt of spiritual conduct, then I now denounce to you by your own law that you are most certainly to be condemned by our Lord Jesus Christ, the judge of all, whom you have mocked and despised.
