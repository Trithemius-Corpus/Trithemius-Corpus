Therefore, if you desire to walk the way of God in true purity, if you wish to defeat the enemy in the contest of a steadfast mind, cast from yourself all the garments of fleshly pleasures. For whoever has learned to run naked, having thrown off hair and clothing, carries around nothing besides the unencumbered body by which he can be held by the adversary. For he who runs naked has stripped himself of every anxiety over the things clamoring round about, and thinks of nothing other than victory; nor does he care meanwhile what is being done at home with wife or children. Nothing present calls him back from the course; the agility of his companions does not frighten him off. Among many he contends as though alone; he hastens breathless toward the prize as though certain. Imitate him, soldier of Christ, in the spiritual struggle: naked, contemplate the naked Savior hanging on the cross, so that you may be able to cast down the adversary the more easily, the less he sees you to possess of the vanities of the world. Therefore fight naked, light, and unencumbered on every side; naked and freed from everything, fly into heaven. Naked you came into this world; naked you are to return to the earth. Pondering these things diligently, my brothers, so run that you may obtain eternal life with all the saints. Amen. The homily ends.

*What the Armor of Spiritual Warfare Is. Homily III.*

Most beloved brothers, today we have heard the holy apostle writing in the epistle to the Ephesians: Put on the armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil; for our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against princes and powers, against the rulers of the world of these darknesses, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places. Therefore take up the armor of God, that you may be able to resist on the evil day, and in all things to stand perfected. He then adds what the armor of God is: Stand therefore, having girded your loins in truth, and having put on the breastplate of justice, and having shod your feet in preparation for the gospel of peace. In all things take up the shield of faith, with which you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one; and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, praying at all times in the Spirit with every prayer and supplication, and in the same keeping watch with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints. To the Thessalonians likewise he says: But let us, who are of the day, be sober, having put on the breastplate of faith and charity, and for a helmet the hope of salvation.

These are the arms, brothers, with which the soldiers of Christ, once clothed, vanquish the savage adversary; these are the monks’ signs of virtues, without which no one attains victory over vices. The instruments of human warfare, which very many mortals pursue too eagerly, are helmet, shield, breastplate, sword, dagger, cuirass, belt, lance, and many other things of that kind. Men about to proceed to war are armed with these most carefully, and unless they have been most diligently protected over the whole body, they are certainly judged less fit for battle. But the arms of our spiritual warfare are faith, hope, charity, patience, humility, obedience, chastity, together with each of the holy virtues of the soul enumerated by our holy teacher Benedict under chapter four, where he makes beautiful mention of the instruments of good works. And would that, my brothers, we were as careful and diligent concerning these spiritual arms of our warfare as we see worldly men to be anxious about their corruptible armor.

But now, which I say with too much grief, since we shamefully cast aside the arms of salvation with which we ought to have overcome all our adversaries, what else remains except that we succumb to our enemy in the struggle? But perhaps you will say: If the Christian faith, as the apostle testifies, belongs to the arms of spiritual warfare, we certainly intend to remain in it. But granted. What then does Saint James say in his epistle? What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith, but does not have works? Can faith save him? For just as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead. Therefore, whenever Scripture speaks of faith, it must always be understood not as common and dead, but as formed by good works and illumined by the sweetness of divine love. For no one will be saved by faith so [unclear].
