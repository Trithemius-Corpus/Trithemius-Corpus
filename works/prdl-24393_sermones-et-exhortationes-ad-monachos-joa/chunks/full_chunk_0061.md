*On the daily expiation of sins.*

[unclear]: and humbly to expiate all their iniquities by the daily chastisement of the body. Know, my brothers, that there is no sin so small that the justice of almighty God leaves it unpunished. Therefore, since there is none of us who has not offended in many things, arm yourselves, I beg you, with patience and humility, so that whenever you are corrected for sins, you may endure it with all gentleness. For in this time of freedom of choice you can, with a small expiation, make satisfaction for many debts, whereas after death in purgatory the soul will be tormented for a long time and most grievously even for small ones.

Indeed, voluntary satisfaction avails greatly; but what is done from necessity earns less toward grace. Altogether, brothers, it is fitting and necessary for the soul to be purged of fault, if it is to reign in heaven and rejoice with Christ. But the soul that has neglected the purgation of its faults in the present life will be punished more heavily in the life to come. Let none of you say, my dearest brothers, what many ignorant people are accustomed to say: “It is enough for me, if my soul shall have reached the punishments of purgatory.” Believe me, brothers: whoever has said this word from the heart does not weigh what purgatory will be. For he does not know what he asks who desires purgatory.

Just as he is foolish who, when he could pay his debt as a free man, scorns to do so and willingly goes to prison, so he is deservedly to be judged the most foolish of all men who, when he can make satisfaction to God for his sins in this life, postpones it to the future. The repentance of this life is fruitful, and voluntary satisfaction is also very meritorious; but after death there will be no place for repentance, but then each person will receive according as he has done in his body, whether good or evil. Think on these things, brothers, while you live in health, lest, forestalled by the command of death, you at last grieve too late. Let today’s salvation not wait for a doubtful tomorrow; and he who can, with grace and in freedom, pay his debt, should not allow himself to be dragged to prison with torture.

As much as each person can throughout his whole life, so much let him take care to make satisfaction for sins, since the spirit returns securely to the Lord when, while still existing in the body, it is daily purged by the fire of divine love. Here you have a place in which the daily expiation of public offenses is carried out; if you hold true and humble patience in your mind, without doubt you will obtain certain remission of them all. As for secret and hidden faults, not only does the Rule admonish you what you ought to do, but daily sacramental confession also instructs you in all things. For at the altar we are each accustomed daily to offer devoutly the Son of God; and none of us approaches that most holy mystery unless he has first confessed according to custom, since the highest purity is required where the salvation of the whole world is renewed through the Mass.

Amid these things, most beloved brothers, who among us would not daily make progress in the love of God, unless he shamefully and utterly laid aside every desire for progress? For I dare to say that the monk who does not make progress in this holy congregation is proved to have utterly forgotten his own salvation. Believe me, brothers: God will save no one unwillingly. If you neglect your own progress, it will be imputed not to him but to you. For God is the intellectual light that enlightens every man coming into this world, as John, soaring aloft, says; but those who do not receive the light necessarily remain in darkness. He who turns his mind away from the faith that is in the Son of God remains in darkness; and for him who does not do the works of light, faith will profit nothing toward salvation.

Outside the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ there is no salvation, and he who thinks otherwise is not a Christian. Do not let the consideration of foolish piety seduce you, O man, so that you believe [unclear] outside the Christian faith; there is no salvation for anyone unto eternal life. For Christ, the only Son of God, is the sole Savior of the world; whoever does not believe in him, the wrath of God remains upon him, and he will be condemned with the devil. But you, my brothers, who now believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, I beg you, live hereafter in such a way that, Christians in name and deed, you may be able to come to his fellowship: he who lives and reigns with God the Father, God in the unity of the Holy Spirit, forever and ever.
