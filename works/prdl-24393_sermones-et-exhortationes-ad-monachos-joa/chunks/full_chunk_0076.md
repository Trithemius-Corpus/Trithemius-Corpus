*[Homily?] XVI.*

For food is by no means fruitfully withheld from the body unless the mind is recalled from all iniquity. Saint Jerome also says: What does it profit for the body to be made thin by abstinence, if the soul swells with pride? What virtue is there in not drinking wine, and being drunk with anger, hatred, and the other poisonous cups of the soul?

Therefore let none of you promise himself sanctity from abstinence from food alone, since whatever is required for true penitence is judged necessary also for fasting. For fasts profit unto salvation for those who are contrite for their sins in a true purpose of amendment, who fervently devote themselves to good and holy works. Hence all who fast from food and drink must also abstain from [unclear], so that the mind, adorned with the beauty of the virtues, may sweetly burn in the love of Christ and may in no way grow lukewarm from progress in inner conduct. For the virtue of abstinence is nothing if it has not been adorned with works of charity.

Therefore, monk, listen to my counsel: if you wish to make your fast fruitful before God, first cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin, so that no desire of evil may intervene; so that the mind may become fervent in the exercise of divine love and be laudably adorned with the remaining virtues. By the remedy of contrition and confession wipe away past sins, and by standing firm in prayers with tears strengthen your mind against all temptations with God’s help. Toward God, as much as you can, be fervent in the exercise of love; toward yourself, tranquil in a pure conscience; toward your brother, ordered in true charity. Always be foremost in humility, firm in constancy and obedience, shining in chastity of mind and body, pious in meekness, a monk in conduct, continually inflamed with desire for perfection. If you commend your abstinence by this kind of cleanness and purity, you will fast rightly.

Exercise your mind diligently in meditation on the passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, so that no day passes in which you do not run through his life and death at least once with thanksgiving. I will not say irreligious, but ungrateful, is the Christian who is so immersed either in temporal affairs or occupied with the pleasures of the flesh that he does not call to mind, at least once each day with thanksgiving, the most bitter passion of his Redeemer.

*Fol. XXIX.*

But the monk who forgets the benefits of our Lord Jesus Christ, who does not recall his passion once every day with thanksgiving, who does not meditate every day on the law of the Lord or on the Gospel, is rightly considered the most ungrateful of all men. Nothing is more powerful for him than that prayer which is offered to God the Father in union with, and in the power of, the Lord’s passion. Too justly is that spirit to be condemned which, though redeemed by the most precious blood of Christ, is led astray by the pleasures of its flesh so as not to remember such great benefits of God toward itself.

For there was no reason that the Son of God should become man except that he willed to justify us sinners: born in the world for us, suffering in the world for us, and cruelly dead on the wood for our crimes. That just one came into the world to justify us who were sinners; the pious one came to the impious, that we might learn piety, humility, and patience from him. The physician came to the sick, life to the dead, mercy to the desperate; it was not our merits that drew him down from heaven into the world, but our sins.

It is fitting, therefore, brothers in [unclear], that we celebrate with all devotion this Lenten fast dedicated to his most holy passion, who first deigned to do such great things for our salvation, who, in order to rescue us from the destruction of death, did not hesitate to give himself for us unto death. Let us do, for the love of Christ, whatever good we can; let us endure, in consideration of his passion and death, every adversity that happens to us in this world, since the Lord Jesus, who in charity willed to suffer for us, has truly shown himself most worthy that we love him with all our heart.

For if, brother, you recognize yourself as a sinner, Christ saves you by his death. Contemplate him as it is fitting, hanging on the cross, and you will be healed from every bite of sin. For nothing in this world is found more effective for cleansing and curing the wounds of conscience than meditation on Christ. For the most precious blood of the Lord, poured out, has become sufficient medicine for sin. Hence the holy bishop Augustine says: Brothers, that we who are wounded by sin may be healed, let us behold Christ crucified.
