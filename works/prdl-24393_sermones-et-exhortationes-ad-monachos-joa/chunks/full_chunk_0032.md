Therefore let the monk, Christ’s soldier, be like the clean animal of God that chews the cud, always having in the mouth of his heart the fodder of the holy Scriptures, lest the mind lie open to vain and pernicious thoughts, or a foul or idle word burst from the speaker’s mouth. Let him always remember, while working, the Scripture that says: Blessed is the man who meditates on the law of the Lord day and night. For everyone who loves the Lord Jesus in truth daily turns over in his heart, with tears and prayers, both his teaching and his passion. For he is not a true lover of God who forgets his commandments even for a moment. Indeed, as Scripture says: Those who love the Lord seek the things that are pleasing to him, and those who fear his holy name shall be filled with his law. Hence one of the saints says to the Lord: In my heart I have hidden your words, that I may not sin against you. The spirit, therefore, must always be stirred up and renewed daily by spiritual goads, lest it grow numb through sloth or languish in uncertain idleness. The labor of monastic discipline, the practice of reading and prayer, and the diligent keeping of vigils are true incitements to virtue. Nothing, however, is more harmful to the holy purpose of the monk than idleness, since it both excites the flesh to lust and weakens every strength of the mind and extinguishes the fervor of the spirit. For idleness in the cloister is the tinder and nourishment of all evils. Wherever, therefore, you may be, whether in the field or in the cloister, always flee idleness as the most pernicious plague of the soul, adorning and exercising the mind either with prayer or with meditation on the Scriptures. Converse gladly with the good and zealous lovers of God; but avoid the negligent and the despisers of their own salvation. For he who touches pitch will be defiled by it, and he who makes himself a companion of sinners will not be free from crime. Consider your words carefully, so that you detract from no one at all, nor listen to another who is detracting, since both the detractor and the willing hearer serve the demon: the one by speaking, the other by listening. Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus, and diligently do with your hands whatever is enjoined upon you by obedience; nor let it seem hard to you to obey another’s command, for which eternal happiness, a crown for your spirit, is prepared in heaven. Therefore no labor, no length of time, no observance ought to seem hard to a monk, by which the glory of eternal blessedness is acquired. Consider, my brothers, the brevity of this life and do good while it is permitted, since he who now sows sparingly will also after death reap sparingly, and each one will receive a fitting reward for his own work, not for another’s. The time for laboring is very brief, but the recompense that is owed to good works is everlasting. Consider, my brothers, men entangled in the world, and all lovers of this age, how strong they are in earthly and perishable matters, but weak and feeble in heavenly things; so that you may take care to please God as much as they labor continually to appear rich and glorious to the world. For for temporal glory many have not refused to sweat even unto death; but for the hope of future happiness they despise even doing or enduring a little. For earthly gains they most patiently tolerate all sorts of injuries; and for a heavenly reward they refuse to endure the insult of even a very slight word. They can stand all day before an earthly king, prince, or judge; but in prayer before the almighty Lord they cannot continue for the space of one hour. They gladly tolerate many adversities for the acquisition of perishable riches; but they neglect the more to seek heavenly goods, the more pleasantly they are occupied in love of earthly things. Let their way of life, dearest brothers, admonish you all the more to flee all the cares of this world, inasmuch as you are now permitted to handle fewer of the affairs of earthly things. Always devote all your solicitude and care to the acquisition of heavenly goods, and labor most diligently at all times for the reward of future blessedness. For, as Scripture testifies, your life upon earth is a warfare, and whatever a man has sown, this he shall also reap. Know that death hastens daily, and at every moment a part of life, never to return, is extinguished. Watch, therefore, my dearest brothers, watch, for you do not know when the Lord our God will come to the judgment of each one’s death
