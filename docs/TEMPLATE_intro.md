# Per-work intro essay template

Copy this file to `works/<work-id>/intro.md` and fill in the sections below. The site builder renders `intro.md` into the work page just above the English translation, between the metadata table and the body text.

## What to include (target: 80–150 words; one or two paragraphs)

1. **What this work is** — title in English plus the Latin shape (`tractatus`, `liber octo questionum`, `sermones`, `ecloga`, etc.), and a one-sentence description of subject matter.
2. **Date and occasion** — when Trithemius wrote it (date or decade), where he was (Sponheim until 1505, Würzburg after), and what prompted the work (a dedicatee, a reform agenda, a specific controversy, a request from a patron).
3. **Place in the corpus** — how it relates to his other writings; whether it's standalone or part of a larger project; any controversies it generated.
4. **A note for English readers** — does this work already have an English translation by someone else? (If yes, cite it and recommend the reader prefer the scholarly edition for citation.) Is this an English first? Is the Latin OCR especially clean or especially damaged?

## What to keep out

- Don't summarize the content chapter-by-chapter — that's the translation's job.
- Don't make literary judgments ("this is one of his most beautiful…") without a citation to a scholar who said so first.
- Don't speculate about Trithemius's psychology or intent beyond what the work itself states.
- Don't repeat the source-provenance fields (those render automatically from `manifest.json`).

## Style

Plain prose. Markdown links are fine. Use `*italics*` for Latin titles and quoted phrases. Don't over-format — the parchment-themed page does the visual lifting.

## When uncertain

If you can't write a confident intro for a work because the Latin OCR is dense and you haven't actually read it yet, write a placeholder:

> *Intro essay pending. This work [is/was] part of Trithemius's [monastic-reform / bibliographic / cryptographic / etc.] corpus. See the source-provenance link above for the original edition.*

Better a placeholder than a fabricated paragraph.
