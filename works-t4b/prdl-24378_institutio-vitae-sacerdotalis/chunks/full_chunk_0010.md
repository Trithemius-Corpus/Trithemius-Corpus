One must pray to the Lord without interruption against the temptations of the flesh and say: “Pierce my flesh with your fear, for I have feared your judgments.” Do not love the delights of this world, which will quickly make you vanish away and will easily set you on fire with the heat of lust. In your diet let excess be avoided, and let not variety of foods stir appetite, but hunger. Let no incitements to lust be set before you, but things that nourish the body and do not inflame it. Wine, in which there is wantonness, should always be suspect to you, lest you be set ablaze with a double heat, namely that of drink and of youth. He who constantly uses wine nourishes the fire of lust in his lap. The uncovering of Noah’s shameful parts and the incest of Lot should admonish you always to fear wine. For drunkenness is the burial of a living man: it destroys the senses, extinguishes reason, binds the mind, wounds the conscience, lessens the health of the body, and kills the soul. Drunkenness, the spring of evils, rouses the flesh to lust, defiles the heart with the worst thoughts, and lets the tongue loose into the foulest words. This is the saying of most holy Jerome: “A filled belly easily foams over into lust.” Therefore let a chaste man’s meal be moderate; let his drink be sparing and light; let his speech be modest. Let him beware intimacy with women; let him direct his senses with every guard, lest he fall into the pit of lust. Whenever a foul thought begins to beat upon his mind, let him take up the memory of the Lord’s passion; let him set his thought upon the wounds of Jesus Christ. For it is written: “Blessed is he who shall take and dash his little ones against the rock.” And the rock, says the Apostle, was Christ. For the recollection of the Lord’s passion is the most effective remedy against all temptations. Indeed, one whom the passion of Christ does not soften, no exercise will cure. Therefore, when the lust of the flesh begins to attack the spirit, let the worshipper of chastity lift his hands to heaven and say: “Lord Jesus Christ, lover of chaste counsel, you know that my strength to resist is slight; help my weakness through your bitter passion.” I believe in the Lord that if he persists in praying with devotion, he will easily obtain victory against the flesh. But I want you, most beloved brother, to avoid not only shameful deeds but also shameful words. For evil conversations corrupt good morals. B iij

And do not wish to imitate those who, while giving themselves to filthy talk, think themselves chaste in heart. For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. Vain speech is the sign of a vain heart. What sort of man each person is hiding within himself is proved by the speech he utters. There are some, and indeed many, priests who by their wanton habits violate the sincerity of their consciences, although they do not bodily proceed to the act of lust. They sit with women speaking shameful things; they exchange itching touches and kisses; and amid these things they boast that they are chaste. And where does the Lord’s saying remain: “Whoever looks at a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart”? If it is not permitted to a chaste man to look at a woman in order to lust after her, how much less to touch her immodestly, kiss her, and handle her with his hands? For the Apostle says: “Abstain from every appearance of evil,” as befits the saints. Does it not seem to you an appearance of evil to sit among chaste men and go mad over the kisses and touches of young women? I would lie if I said that I had not seen priests, and prelates of higher rank, spend the whole day on shipboard in obscene touches and kisses of women, and show no reverence for their dignity. It is a sign of inward lust when someone has grown accustomed to speak shameful words and to add touches to them. Therefore, dearest one, if you desire to show yourself a worthy minister of God, if you wish to escape the very hard yoke of lust, abstain not only from deeds but also from shameful words and from the touch of women. It is impossible for the mind not to be wounded when, behaving wantonly, you touch a woman with kiss and hand. “Remember your last things,” the wise man warns, “and you will not sin forever.” For nothing restrains a man from sin so much as the attentive memory of death.

On Reading and the Study of the Scriptures. Chapter 4.

I know, and I have learned by experience, that the most effective remedy against all temptations is the study and reading of the Scriptures, and frequent prayer. For such a remedy extinguishes the assault of vices.
