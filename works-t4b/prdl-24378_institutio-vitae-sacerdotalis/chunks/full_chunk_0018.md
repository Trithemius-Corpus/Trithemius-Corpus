But God is to be loved with the whole heart, for his own sake, and one’s neighbor for God’s sake. No one truly loves God if he hates his neighbor. For Christ is both God and man. Therefore he does not love the whole Christ who hates a human being. God accepts no good work unless the flame of charity kindles it on the altar of the heart. For charity holds the chief place among all the virtues.

Humility follows it, which is remembered as the guardian of the virtues. So great is the excellence of this virtue that every other virtue quickly perishes unless it is strengthened by the foundation of humility. For holy Pope Gregory says: He who gathers virtues without humility carries dust into the wind. From the very place where he seems to carry something, there he is more grievously blinded. Therefore charity is the greatest ornament of a priest, and humility the most precious pearl in his garment. You also, brother, display these twin virtues in yourself, and you will be a fruitful preacher in the Church of God. Whatever you say, you will readily persuade your hearers, if you have charity and humility in your heart and diligently show them in your work.

Let accursed ambition have no place in your heart; once it has invaded the mind, it so blinds it that it brings forgetfulness of God’s salvation and of its own. Be a Christian lover of human beings and an enemy of vices. Keep the commandments of God, and do not neglect ecclesiastical sanctions. Honor all people for God’s sake, and never say that anyone should be despised. What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another. Let not the sun go down upon your anger. Flee the flattery of the rich, and never stroke sinners. Deny yourself your desires, so that you may become a disciple of Christ. Chastise your body, according to the apostle, bringing it into servitude. Scorn pleasures; love chaste fasting. Be devoted to the poor; feed the hungry; clothe the naked; visit the sick; free the captive; console the sorrowful; bury the dead. Do not love the world; do not seek riches; do not run after vanity. For no one serving as God’s soldier entangles himself in secular affairs. Do injury to no one, but learn patiently to bear injuries inflicted on you, following the example of your Lord, who, hanging on the cross, prayed for his persecutors, that they might not perish. Have peace with all people,

but concord and unanimity only with the devout. Do not be proud and arrogant, not sleepy and lazy, not drunken and gluttonous, not a slanderer, and not one who rashly judges anyone. Guard your actions at every hour, and let the memory of the things to come always turn in your thought. For Scripture says: Son, remember your last things, and you will not sin forever. Therefore always think that you are going to die; fear the day of judgment; dread the torments of hell. If you do these things, you will easily overcome all temptations of the flesh. Scorn the pleasures of the world, so that you may be able to enter rejoicing into the joys of heavenly happiness.

Do not deceive your soul: no one can rejoice here with the world and afterward reign with Christ. For through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God. Therefore, despising present things, let us hasten with every desire toward eternal joys. Keep your heart with all vigilance, because life proceeds from it. If you have been overtaken by some fault, do not delay to seek the remedies of penitence and to cleanse yourself in confession through true contrition.

Strive to enter by the narrow way into life, that is, not to fulfill the desires of the flesh, to hate the world, and to live by the spirit. You have been called to serve God as a soldier against the vices of the flesh. Do not rest before victory; do not cease from the battle, because no one will be crowned unless he is victorious. In this life, however, there is no secure victory, but continual battle. Whoever perseveres to the end will be saved. At his end a man will be tested. The life of a human being upon earth is military service. As a soldier of the highest king, it is not permitted to you to desert his contest. A good soldier follows his king wherever he goes. Where, then, is your king? In heaven. Why do you not follow him? What are you doing on earth, if you are a soldier of the heavenly, supreme king? Why do you not follow your Lord? He himself says: Whoever ministers to me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also my minister will be. “Let him follow,” he says, not with the steps of the feet, but with the movements of the affections. The apostle also says: No one serving as God’s soldier entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please the one to whom he has proved himself.
