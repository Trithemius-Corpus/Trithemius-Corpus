Therefore the ecclesiastical shepherd should, by a good life, offer his sheep an example of justice, and by salutary doctrine feed the minds of his hearers, lest, if he does one without the other, he either neglect himself or exercise care of the flock fruitlessly. Alas, what shall I say about our priests who are unlearned and vicious, avaricious, luxurious, and utterly wicked? I am ashamed to speak, and I cannot be silent. Take thought for yourself, dearest Nicolaus, lest you become an imitator of those who, despising the books of the Scriptures, spend the whole day free for games and drinking parties; who stain themselves by mingling with women; who by their depraved conduct scandalize many in the law of God. Be in the people of God a model of honorable conduct; in all things, so far as can be done, show yourself irreproachable, so that you may remove every occasion for evil suspicion concerning you. For you ought to have testimony even from those who are outside, since the sermon of a preacher is easily despised when his life is marked among the people by evil suspicions. Therefore show yourself a cultivator of good works, so that you may be able to instruct the people of God no less by example than by speech. In order that you may be able to accomplish this salutarily, take care to avoid the companionship of the undisciplined; for he who does not flee the companionship of the wicked can scarcely live without reproach. For Scripture says: “With the holy you will be holy, and with the perverse you will be perverted.” And again: “He who touches pitch will be defiled by it.” Each person delights to have companions of his own purpose. If, therefore, you are good, love the company of the good. For if you do not keep your foot from the assembly of evildoers, you will at all times be subject to the slanders of those under you. A youthful mind is easily deceived by the companionship of the perverse. You are young, and flexible in either direction; therefore you must watch over yourself with the greatest diligence, lest you be corrupted by the company of the undisciplined. For nothing harms young men so much as the bad examples of their elders. Indeed, the younger you are, the greater the solicitude with which you must take care to guard your life in sincerity of the fear of the Lord. For it is written: “A young man according to his way, even when he has grown old, will not depart from it.” What you have learned as a young man, you will practice in old age.

On fleeing the companionship of women.
Chapter 2.

But what shall I say about familiarity with women, which is most destructive to the priests of God, since the decrees of the holy fathers forbid it to them by a sufficiently strict prohibition? If I set before you the admonitions of the most blessed father Jerome, you will immediately cry out: “I cannot govern a household without the service of a maidservant; I am not able to prepare food for myself; I do not want to clean dirty clothes. Why,” you say, “do you bring forward Jerome’s precepts against me, which bind no one except the willing? Who appointed Jerome as my lawgiver?” The sacrosanct universal Church, I say, approves his sayings; it places among the canons his precepts set forth on this matter; and do you presume to mutter against so great an authority? But you will say: “Contrary custom has abrogated this law, and after a long time it must be regarded as law.” But iniquity lies to itself, and by foolish reasoning is deceived in itself. A custom contrary to good morals can never either take away or establish law. And what is so contrary to priestly honor as to delight in the household intimacy of young women? Chastity is endangered where the society of priests passes among little women. For the first and strongest temptations of clerics arise from frequent access to women. Foolish presumption has overthrown many, who, as I might say, have handed the instruments of victory to their enemies with their own hands. Danger is avoided with the greatest difficulty if the occasion is not avoided. For while a woman stays with a cleric, and sits alone with him alone, prepares food for him as he eats, arranges his little bed as he sleeps, speaks with him, smiles at him, she supplies the kindling of lust. However holy a woman may be, she must always be feared by a cleric. For just as sparks leap out from coals, so the poison of concupiscence spreads from the constant familiarity of women. A woman familiar with a chaste man is the gate of the devil, the door of hell, the way of iniquity, the dreadful sting of the scorpion, and a harmful kind of deception.
