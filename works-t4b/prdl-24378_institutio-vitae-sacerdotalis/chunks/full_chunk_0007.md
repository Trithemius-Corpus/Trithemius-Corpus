You know what happened to your first preceptor. He, wretchedly seduced by the savage love of that prostitute whom you know, since he did not dare live with her openly for fear of her husband, despised his homeland, home, relatives, and the benefices he had, and secretly fled away with her so that he might enjoy her freely. And to this day no one knows where he has gone.

I know a certain priest who kept concubines. When at one time I rebuked him for his sin, between myself and him, I finally brought him to the point that he was willing to promise perpetual continence from then on into my hands. But when I urged him to cast out his concubine at once, he answered, “I cannot.” “Why?” I said. “If I cast her out,” he said, “who would look after my little children?” For he already had two boys by her. “I am poor,” he said; “I cannot hire people to care for them for wages. Therefore I will not cast out their mother until the little children grow up, lest I be guilty of bloodshed. Yet I will be chaste with her as with a chaste woman, and from now on I will be joined to her in no intercourse.” And I said, “What will you do if, before these children grow up, others arise in their place?” He swore that he wished to take care that this not happen. “You will not amend your fault,” I said, “unless you cast out the concubine.” It is difficult to leave habitual things behind and, while the occasion is present, to abstain from former vices. No one will warm a serpent in his bosom and remain unharmed. The man went away to his house, and to this day he has not abandoned his domestic crimes.

I have considered the works of God: whom he has despised, no one can correct. So difficult is it for a priest who has once fallen into fornication to rise again to true chastity that among all of them you can scarcely find one. For according to Scripture, “He who commits sin is the servant of sin,” and he who once submits to filthy love is scarcely ever wholly set free. For fornication is more easily fled than corrected. Therefore I warn you and entreat you to flee the company of women, lest you lose the reputation of a good name. For once a priest has been disgraced by the charge of fornication, he will never recover the honor he has lost. Avoid even conversations with women; decline frequent greetings in the marketplace, because by light words one comes to the worst deeds. For many have been deceived by a woman’s careless speech.

Be watchful at all times, and never think that you should trust yourself so long as you live in the flesh. For we have known many who fell in old age, though they seemed secure because of past victory. Indeed temptation is never absent from a person so long as he lives in this life. But if you cannot manage your household affairs without the service of a maidservant, take a respectable old woman from your own kin, one whose living with you could give rise to no suspicion among the people. Yet take care of this above all: do not take a married woman as your servant. For what God has joined, let no man separate. The cohabitation of another man’s wife is not free from suspicion of evil. I want you, so far as possible, to have a good testimony from everyone. Let the dangers of others make you cautious; and where you see that many have fallen, do not choose to walk carelessly.

Remember how many very strong and very wise men, from the beginning of the world down to this day, have perished through the company of women. A woman deceived Adam, the first-formed man, and when he had been overthrown she killed us all. From the beginning of the created world the sons of God sinned through women, and by their crimes brought the flood upon the whole world. Women of the foreigners also laid low the children of Israel as they came out of Egypt, in the desert. Why do we recount these, when a vile prostitute, having captured Samson, the strongest of all men, by her loves, handed him over to death and disgrace? Let no one presume upon his own strength; let no one trust in former chastity, since often the man who today is seen to stand bravely is tomorrow seen to fall into the depths of fornication. The wisest Solomon could not guard against the deceits of women; who, then, will now be safe? The strong and wise man fell by the sword of women, and do we, frail and foolish, presume upon security? For the man who fell says, “Better is the iniquity of a man than a woman doing good.” It is safer to live with a wicked man than with a good woman.

For the devil, the adversary of chastity, never ceases to set snares for a chaste man, if he can find material for deceiving him. Since he is crafty, he knows how to dissemble for a time, until at last in a single hour he can overturn all the labor and reward of past continence. He gradually insinuates himself among those who love.
