Nothing so diminishes ecclesiastical dignity and renders it contemptible as the ignorance of prelates. When formerly bishops, abbots, and the other prelates and priests had directed their minds to the Scriptures, so easy an entrance did not lie open to crimes and sins. For love and knowledge of the Scriptures teach one to despise the world, flee vices, and love the most sacred virtues. As long as the Israelite people remained in the law of the Lord, no power of the enemy prevailed against them. But after it cast away knowledge of the law, it was subjected to many adversities. For Scripture says: “Because they did not have wisdom, they perished because of their foolishness.”

Today also there are priests and rectors of churches in great number, but few of them attend to knowledge of the law and the Scriptures. And because, as the Lord speaks through the prophet, the priests have cast away knowledge of the law, for that reason they have been made contemptible among the people. One does indeed cry to the Lord in tribulation, but he does not hear them, because “he who turns his ears away so that he does not hear the law, his prayer will be execrable.” Therefore priests should not be surprised that laypeople despise them, since they themselves scorn the commandments of Christ. I greatly fear that graver things will soon come against the clergy. But enough about these matters.

You, however, should consider not what others do, but what you yourself must do. For many are called to the priesthood in the Church of God, but few are chosen. Therefore we must take the greatest care that we who are taken into the lot of the Lord may also be chosen. What does it profit to exercise the priesthood here for a very brief time, if one happens to be separated from the sight of God? Therefore nothing is better than to fix one’s desire on heavenly things, to persist in the study of sacred reading, to despise present things for the love of God, and to think on future things.

Hence Peter says in the Aurora: “Nothing is better for a man than if, by reading divine things, he fixes his mind there whence life comes to him.” Therefore continuous study must be devoted to reading the Scriptures, because reading nourishes the mind and supplies fuel to the virtues. Therefore, so that you do not lose the value of time, read the sacred Scriptures; write books; persist in devout prayers; and at the proper times also work with your hands. Do not be ashamed to learn what you do not know, because then you will be able usefully to instruct others, if you always seek to be informed by those more learned than yourself.

Do not be ashamed to take on the name of pupil, if you desire to attain the honor of mastery. For the Holy Spirit, who teaches man knowledge, is a lover of humility. When you sit at table to take food, remember that the sacred letters must first be sounded forth, so that while the flesh is fed, the mind may by no means be defrauded of its own nourishment. In the Council of Toledo it is read thus: “Out of reverence for God and for priests, the whole synod established this: because idle tales are often accustomed to be introduced at meals, reading from the divine Scriptures should be mixed with every priestly banquet. For by this both souls are built up toward the good, and tales are not prohibited by necessity.” For the word of God is the food of the spirit, because man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.

The ancient fathers and priests devoted the greatest diligence to the holy Scriptures, so that they might both nourish the mind in a wholesome way and provide useful things for the instruction of posterity. Let us adhere to their footsteps, so that we may deserve to be found worthy ministers before God.

On Fleeing Avarice and Greed

Chapter 5

Although at the beginning of our discourse we argued against avarice and greed, nevertheless we are led by a sure reason to speak again in this place about this very matter. For enough is never done against this vice unless it is rooted out entirely. Since our Savior knew how difficult it is for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God, he said to those following him: “Unless someone renounces all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple.” Hence the psalmist admonishes us: “If riches abound, do not set your heart upon them.” The Apostle also says: “Having food and clothing, let us be content with these. What more do we seek, we who are ministers of God?

Greed is shameful in a priest of God; avarice is shameful, and the continual hunger for gold is damnable. Therefore, most beloved brother, if you desire to live in freedom of spirit, do not seek perishable money.
