We read that Saint Augustine fled women so greatly that he would not allow even his own sister to enter his episcopal house. We ourselves have known several priests who for many years governed both their household and the people entrusted to them innocently, without the service of a maidservant. Nor indeed will it be difficult for you to imitate what you see others have practiced. Beware, therefore, and do not draw near to the company of young women, since it is difficult to live chastely even among old women.

Remember with whom you speak at the altar, and do not pour out in kisses for prostitutes a mouth consecrated to divine ministries. It is most shameful, with hands consecrated by the oil of holy anointing, by which the Son of God is held and sacrificed, to touch a woman with polluted contacts. Do not give a stain to your glory and your name as a reproach to all. Everyone in public, and in the field the peasant plowmen and vine-dressers, will daily tear you fiercely apart if, as a young man, you presume to live with women, especially younger ones. You will be a proverb and a mockery to all the people if you often enter the dwellings of young women.

Against your invisible enemy, who always flies around your side laying snares, keep watch at every time, and according to Naso, resist evils at their beginnings, when the temptations are still weaker. For, as we see, after a cleric has once offered his body to the embraces of prostitutes, rarely or never does he return to perfect amendment. For all who are troubled by perverse love, the longer they have lain in the filth of lust, the more difficultly do they return to health of mind. A vice strongly grown old fixes its roots in the human heart.

O how many priests there are today in the Church of God who, submerged in the depth of lust, despise the long-suffering of God, as it is written: When the impious man has come into the depths of evils, he despises. There are, no doubt, many among clerics defiled by lust who, although at the beginning of their promotion they resolved to live chastely, deceived by negligent guarding of the senses, now without any fear of God consort with prostitutes as if licitly and by custom. Nothing is worse than the custom of sin, by which the gravest crimes come to be esteemed as if very slight.

The strongest bond of sin is the habit of sinning, which grows with time and from delay takes increase of strength. Therefore he who said in verse gave very good counsel against the fires of the flesh: Resist at the beginnings; medicine is prepared too late when evils have gained strength through long delays. But hasten, and do not defer yourself to coming hours: he who is not ready today will be less ready tomorrow. Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, says Scripture, and do not put it off from day to day, since the longer you have delayed repentance, the less fit you will be for repenting.

Now ascend the watchtower of your mind, and see the madness of foolish priests who are led captive by base concubines, so that they do not do whatever they wish, but what is commanded them by prostitutes. You would meanwhile see a solid cleric sitting beside his concubine, gazing at her with open mouth; and if she smiles at him, he laughs; if she becomes angry, he flatters her until she is reconciled to favor. The unhappy priest is led captive by a woman, and he who ought to have ruled others is overcome by the dominion of his maidservant.

O who would believe what great groans the wretch emits amid these things, whenever he thinks of casting off this harshest yoke of the arch-devil, what pains he carries in his heart? Sometimes, indeed, he is touched by such great displeasure against his beast that he plans to drive her away from himself on the next day. Meanwhile he gives insulting words and receives them; finally after words they come to blows: he strikes and is struck back; he curses and is blasphemed. O astonishing spectacle: the hands of a prostitute tear, strike, and bruise the head of a priest, which a prince is not permitted to touch.

Finally, after curses and blows, the woman turns herself to weeping; with the devil pouring them in, she sheds tears, until at last she recalls the unhappy captive to mercy and renders him appeasable to herself. You would then see the cleric suddenly changed, and so moved by the tears of his concubine that he who had suffered the injury is the first to beg forgiveness. Then a full table is set out, a feast is renewed, sighs are mixed with words, and the bargains of new nuptials are negotiated. Amid these things the solid priest sits disputing about patience and gentleness; he sighs that he has offended against his mistress, and labors with all his strength to recover her favor.
