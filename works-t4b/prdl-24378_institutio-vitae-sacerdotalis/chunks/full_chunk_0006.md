What is done after the friendship has been renewed, you may rather think than I may say. There is no fear of God before the eyes of a priest bound by the chains of a concubine; there is no dread of the punishments of hell, no thought of priestly dignity, no memory of public honor. This vice of incontinence has now prevailed so much among the clergy that what is public has almost begun to be lawful. Wretched condition of the human race: those who ought to have corrected the crimes of others commit graver ones themselves.

Shameful to say, in villages and cities priests sit openly, notorious for intercourse with their concubines. So great a number of them is found today that if some prelate wished to correct such men in his own diocese alone, overcome by sheer multitude, he would be frightened away from his purpose. If you entered the house of any one of such men, you would sometimes come upon the priest carrying in his arms his little child, the witness of his fatherly fornication, and, while reading the canonical hours, consoling the crying child with words and songs. You would also see the father, rising from bed, taking the ill-fated offspring onto his knees, offering kisses, placing him on his right knee and the breviary on his left. While praying, he smiles back at the smiling infant, soothes him when he cries, and, when he is silent, addresses him often with words and nods.

Meanwhile the coaxing mother runs up; the sweet pledge snatches the book from the father’s hand, handles the leaves, tears them, and, with saliva flowing from his mouth, stains it with his parent’s permission. Amid all this there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention given to the words; instead, all concern is spent in consoling the crying child. The words fall confused from the mouth of the one praying, interrupted by the infant’s frequent kisses, so that when the prayer is finished he scarcely remembers that he has prayed.

Meanwhile the sacristan rings the bell; the altar is prepared for sacrifice; the priest is awaited by the people in the church. The priest rushes in, his hands washed in place of the child, puts on the sacred vestments with all the haste he can, and performs the sacrifice of God with a distracted mind. O conscience, where do you remain meanwhile? Where do you lie despised? O miserable man, you who handle the Son of God with polluted hands, how long will you wound, tear, and destroy your soul? With what rashness, finally, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul?

If the great mercy of almighty God did not spare you for a time, the angels, provoked, would strike you at the altar with grave indignation. Where is the vow of perpetual chastity that you promised into the hands of the bishop when he ordained you? Where is the reverence due to the priesthood you have assumed? You have sinned: why do you not do penance? How long will you despise the long-suffering of God? Or do you not know that the patience of God leads you to penance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart, you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the just judgment of Jesus Christ.

I think, indeed, that you plan to repent in the future; but you give no thought at all to how near death is, the cutter-off of penance. Truly, the merciful God who promised pardon to the penitent did not pledge tomorrow’s day to the one who delays. But a cleric whom a cohabiting woman has once led away captive she never, or rarely, releases as free unless death intervenes.

I pass over the fact that many, through the deceits and sorceries of their concubines, are often driven so far out of their minds that they cannot in any way shake themselves free from those women’s most shameful love. The hearing shudders at the crimes of harlots, and the purity of chastity cannot bear to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by priests’ concubines. They prepare food for their masters from menstrual blood, and while they desire to entice them to love them, they turn them toward colic and madness. With dreadful sorceries and superstitions they entangle priests in their lusts, until, their strength gradually drained away, they render them utterly useless.

Blessed is the man whom God has guarded from an evil and lustful woman. There is no wickedness above the malice of a woman who enjoys evil loves. Whatever she devises, she attempts; whatever she can do, she dares. No one believes what efforts, ways, and inventions she uses, so that she may cast down, by an indissoluble bond to her pleasures, the man whom she plans to deceive. She joins herself to her master for the sake of her own desires, not because she loves him, but because she covets. For the crowd of concubines follows the prey, not the priest. So long as he is rich and abounds in all things, she does not leave her master’s side; but if he begins to lack bread, the concubine turns herself to another. Sometimes, however, because of the children, the priest and the concubine are glued together to such a degree that by no means can they be separated from one another, except by death.
