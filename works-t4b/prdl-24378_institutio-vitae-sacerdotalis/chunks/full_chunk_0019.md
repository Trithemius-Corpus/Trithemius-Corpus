You, therefore, who await the unfading crown of victory, labor like a good soldier of Jesus Christ. Let no care of earthly anxiety weigh you down; let no enticement of fleshly pleasure touch your mind. For nothing is happier than the soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised; nothing more laborious than one who daily faces peril to his life; nothing stronger than one who conquers the devil; nothing weaker than one who is overcome by the flesh. Let no household call you away, no kinship hinder you, no flesh constrain you. I want you to be without anxiety, so that you may freely think upon the things that are God’s. Naked and unencumbered, fly to heaven, because your mother bore you naked and the earth will receive you naked. Love what you cannot lose; despise what you cannot hold; seek that in which you may rejoice happily forever. Live in such a way that you do not fear to die. A good conscience does not fear death, because it knows that through death it will come to the eternal joys of the heavenly homeland. Now, most beloved brother, you have what you asked of me. You have some small pattern of good conduct upon which you may impress yourself; you have a standard of holy exhortation to which you may diligently have recourse. I have done what I could, not as much as I wished. For the rest, live according to the Gospel and the constitutions of the holy fathers, in which the whole form of Christian perfection is seen.

Farewell, mindful of us. From Sponheim, on the Kalends of April, in the year of the Lord 1486.

Here ends the little book of lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, On the Life of the Priest.
