Do not seek perishing things; do not love riches. Cast your thought upon the Lord, and he himself will nourish you; he himself will feed you when you hunger; he himself will guard you. Nor do I wish you to cast aside domestic prudence altogether, but neither should you go beyond measure in greed. Cupidity is the root of all evils; it does not allow a man to live for God in freedom of spirit, but always torments the mind with bitter cares and permits him to think of nothing other than riches.

Moderation, however, is the safest thing, for it is neither weighed down by poverty nor lifted up into pride by abundance. We see among the priests of God worshippers of idols, who are always watchful in gathering money, who devote themselves to greed, who think nothing or little about eternal life. They live as though they had been born for gain; they cast far away piety toward the poor; they long for nothing but to seek and find gold. Among them you will see certain greedy men and wretched rich men, who have a great supply and abundance of temporal things, but lack the ability to use what they have acquired. All the days in which they dwell on earth they live wretchedly; they do not presume to eat and drink according to the need of the body; they are always desiring to add something to the heap of money. They keep watch over profits; they care nothing at all for the salvation of souls. They grieve more over a lost penny than if ten souls entrusted to them were to perish. But it is no wonder if he neglects another’s soul who damns his own.

For whoever has once subjected his heart to greed and cupidity will never, or only rarely, be free. He is always fevered for greater profits of money; he is always panting for an increase of gold; and the more his money happens to have grown, the more his desire to possess grows. From then on one benefice, however fat, is not enough; the greedy priest runs about to obtain others as well; by right and wrong alike he labors to get what he wickedly desires. Meanwhile he runs to the apostolic see and recounts the grounds of his petition; if these are examined by the truth, they are immediately found false. Then one man obtains many prebends; he hires out the care of the sheep to mercenaries; he receives wool and milk from the sheep, but scorns feeding the flock. “Woe,” says the prophet, “to the shepherds who feed themselves.” They think themselves free from guilt because they obtain many benefices by dispensation of the supreme pontiff, but they do not consider what is required for a just obtaining of them. But you, stand firm and do not transgress the ancient boundaries which

your fathers set; rather, set a limit to desire, lest you fall into sin. I wish you to be content with one benefice, which is sufficient for you according to the moderation of your state, lest, if you take on several without just necessity, you be judged more severely for it before God. Do not take as your example the multitude of those who act otherwise; do not walk along the broad road of sinners, if you wish to arrive at life. He who serves the altar should live from the altar; but he who neglects to feed the sheep, with what rashness does he demand wool and milk from them?

Ecclesiastical benefices have been instituted for the sustenance of those who serve them, not for the greed of those who seek their own things. He who bears the burden and the care is worthy to receive sustenance. But he who flees the Lord’s solicitude does not receive the profit of the milk without fault. Therefore, if you refuse to feed Christ’s sheep, you should by no means seek wool and milk from them. For the sheep were not made for the sake of the shepherd, but the shepherd was appointed for the sake of the sheep. Nor is the care of souls to be sought so that food may be had; rather, food is to be sought so that due care may be applied to the sheep.

But now the order is overturned by many, who seek prelacies and benefices for this reason, that they may have money in a chest, not that they may feed souls for the love of God. O shepherd and idol, how wretchedly you deceive yourself! You take care only that you may eat, though you ought to have eaten so that you might be able to take care of souls.

Cupidity is the root of all evils, the mother of transgression, the teacher of doing harm, the charioteer of malice, the inventor of deceit, the origin of sedition, the pit of scandal, the promoter of falsehood. It commits sacrileges and thefts; it carries out plunderings and arsons; it wages wars and murders. Finally, cupidity alone is what taught men to buy and sell simoniacally; it seeks and receives unjustly; it trades unjustly; it relies on deceits; it is nourished by lying and perjury. It is what breaks faith, dissolves agreement, corrupts testimony, and violates an oath. Cupidity also extinguishes charity between God and man; it breaks off brotherly love; it disturbs concord; it makes counsel foolish; it perverts the judgment of equity; it destroys unity. Therefore, if you desire to show yourself a minister worthy of God, tear this root of all evils from your heart as much as you can. Brief is every possession of earthly riches.
