Then they begin to speak more familiarly; each opens his more secret matters to the other; little by little they grow accustomed to touching one another with their hands. Finally, through mutual laughter, future marriages are prepared; and while they live familiarly by habit, they suddenly fall into the devil’s pit.

After they have once fallen into mutual guilt, the deed seems to them somewhat abhorrent; but they cannot easily withdraw from what they have begun. Therefore they go on in the way they had started; and although at the beginning conscience abhors the crime, nevertheless through habit it gradually bears it more lightly. At first, indeed, they begin to act wantonly with a certain bashful fear; but afterward, blinded by wicked habit, they no longer blush. Meanwhile the belly, conscious of the secret, begins to swell and to reveal publicly the crime that had lain hidden. At last a little child is born; for a short time father and mother blush, and immediately they return to their customary crimes.

Thereafter all shame is now cast far away; the eye of conscience is blinded; the fear of God and of hell is driven utterly from memory. Now, joined by the devil’s glue, they live freely in sins, disgraced before men, alienated from God. Soon the long-established crime turns into despair; the remedy of penitence is mocked; the long-suffering patience of God is despised. Meanwhile the priest is made a slave to lust to such a degree that he thinks he cannot live without his concubine. He can indeed amend his sin, but he refuses, because he feels himself pleasantly entangled in the bonds of lust. He promises himself that later he will undertake penitence in the future; he promises to amend his life. But while, amid so many crimes, he delays with the intention of repenting, he loses both the time and the ability alike. Hence it happens that such priests who keep concubines rarely come to the remedies of saving penitence, because they hope that their life will still be prolonged for a long time. Indeed, some are so driven mad by devilish enticements that they can think of nothing except carnal things, and care nothing for future repayment. They love a wicked concubine more than eternal happiness.

What sort of penitence do you think these men perform at the end, when they are driven not by will but by necessity? Those who have consumed the whole time of their life in lust rarely obtain true penitence in the final moments of their life. For even if the late penitence of such men, by God’s mercy, is sometimes true, nevertheless they will not be able to reach the kingdom of heaven without the most grievous torments of purgatory. Finally, if revelations are to be believed, a very great multitude of clerics among the dead suffer unimaginable punishments, to last forever, because of the vice of incontinence.

You see to what end incautious familiarity with women drags a cleric: it condemns him to the eternal torments of hell. Therefore the beginnings of temptations must be resisted, because lust is never conquered better than if it is avoided by flight. For if it has once entangled you through habit, it never lets you go free without the special grace of God. If you hesitate to put faith in our words, look for examples, since you can find proofs before your eyes. Behold, today we know many old priests whose life we know to be incontinent. We see age grow old in them, and the vices of youth by no means cease. And when they come to extreme old age, they do not abandon their wicked habits; but as they always were from the beginning, so also they go to death.

You would see among them priests already decrepit who have drunk in the poison of lust to such a degree that, when because of old age they can no longer act wantonly in deed, they burn for their customary practices in thought, word, and gestures. Of such men the Lord says through the prophet: If the Ethiopian can change his skin and the leopard his spots, then you also will be able to do good, when you have learned evil. The concubinage of women can cease in old men, but the desire for the act remains as before. Therefore, as you desire to be as an old man, so live now also as a young man. For it is difficult for an old man to correct his habits and to think new things in an old mind. Indeed, old age has habits; it does not receive them.
