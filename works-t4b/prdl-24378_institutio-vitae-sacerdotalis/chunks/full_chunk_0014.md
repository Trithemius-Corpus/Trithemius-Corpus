The possession of earthly riches is brief; the action of mortal life is brief; the daily advance of the human race toward death is pressing. Why, then, do you delay, for the love of God, to despise the things which, when death cuts you off, you will soon be compelled to leave, even against your will? For we do not have here a lasting city, but we seek the one to come. Where your treasure is, there also will your heart be. Do not seek the perishable riches of the world; do not bind your heart with the chains of avarice. For it befits a priest of Christ to be a disciple, meek and merciful, one who does not covet what belongs to another, but distributes his own goods generously among the poor.

On the honorable outward conduct of priests. Chapter 6.

Consider now what manner of conduct you ought to establish for yourself, and how you ought to live, so that you may be able to please almighty God, whose minister you are, with your whole heart. As far as inward conduct is concerned, you must shine clean and pure in conscience, since you minister before the Lord. For the prophet of the Lord says to priests: “Be sanctified, you who carry the vessels of the Lord.” And the Lord says: “Wash yourselves; be clean; remove the evil of your thoughts from before my eyes; cease to do perversely; learn to do good,” says the almighty Lord. As far as the outward person is concerned, the Apostle, instructing the bishop, says: “A bishop must be irreproachable, sober, kindly, well-ordered in conduct, hospitable, teachable.” But whatever is prescribed here for a bishop is also commanded for other prelates and priests.

And first, when he says that he must be irreproachable, he declares him free from all vices and from the slanders of the people. For the life of a priest is set before the people as an example, and is compared to a lamp, so that just as the light of a lamp shines in darkness, so the conduct of a priest may shine among the crowds. For the Lord says in the Gospel: “Let your light so shine before men that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven.” Then, however, a priest walks worthily before God

when he adorns his life daily with good and holy works, and for the love of Christ mortifies his members that are upon the earth. For he is the servant of the Lord, and this above all is required of him: that he carry out the will of God in words and deeds. But no one fulfills the will of God unless, after expelling the vices, he makes his heart a dwelling-place of the virtues. Beware lest you stain the Church, the bride of Christ, through the shamefulness of your life; beware lest through a bad example you scandalize anyone, because for as many as you lead into sin by your fault, for so many will you be tormented in punishment.

Let yourself be seen rarely in public; accustom yourself to remain at home and to devote yourself to readings. Be quiet in mind and body. Do not run about through the streets and the houses of neighbors; do not take part in the tales of rustics. For presence lessens reputation, and excessive familiarity with a priest breeds contempt among the people. A rare going-out into public, however, will display for you among the people great reverence for your maturity. Live in such a way that many may be edified by your example.

In the midst of elders, speak little, because talkativeness is often reproved where silence is praised. Indeed, among elders it befits a young man to listen, not to speak. Do not desire to seem wise in the company of priests, lest before them you be confounded as foolish. Do not be wordy and arrogant. When questioned, answer with humility and goodwill, and do not, in the manner of ranters, be clamorous in voice while you speak. Do not love forced laughter, and always and everywhere keep yourself from every dishonorable levity. Avoid useless conversations and vain words, knowing that for every idle word you have spoken you will have to render account to God. Let your speech be moderate, and do not let an oath be made in vain.

Do not enter taverns with rustics, because that is not permitted to your honor. For it is very unfitting that one who ought to rebuke sinners should, by his presence, wish to show himself a patron of sins. For entry into taverns is forbidden to priests by the holy fathers, unless they are on a journey. It is shameful for a priest of God to enter a tavern with rustics, and for the mouth that has been consecrated to God to be defiled by impure speech.
