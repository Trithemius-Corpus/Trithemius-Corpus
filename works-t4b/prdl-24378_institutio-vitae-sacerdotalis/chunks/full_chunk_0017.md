For it is shameful for a priest, out of fear of the people, to keep silent the words of God. A priest should use a little wine, lest, if he devotes himself to drinking bouts, he become a disgrace among the people. For the old law condemns drunken priests and keeps them from access to the altar. Likewise in the Council of Agde it is read as follows: “Before all things drunkenness is to be avoided by clerics, for it is the tinder and nurse of all vices. And so, whoever is found to have been drunken, as his rank permits, shall either be removed from communion for the space of thirty days or be subjected to bodily punishment.”

For wine is a luxurious thing, and drunkenness is disgraceful; everyone who is entangled in these things will not be wise. He who burns without wine, with the fire of youth, and is inflamed by the heat of the blood, and is strong in body, should flee wine as poison, lest, suffocated by a manifold fire, he fall into the mire of lust.

A stomach heated with wine stirs up desire, dulls the sense, and weakens the head and the whole body. A fat belly produces a thin understanding. Therefore, when you are called and come to the banquets of priests, do not forget clerical honor, but show yourself modest and restrained in all things. Take care that you have neither an itching tongue nor itching ears, that is, that you neither detract from others yourself nor lend your hearing to those who do. Know that all the things you say about others are to be turned back upon yourself, and perhaps you will be found blameworthy in the very things you used to reproach in others.

But perhaps you will say: “I cannot do injury to those who report such things, because anyone who thinks about stopping everyone’s lips is attempting the impossible. What, then, is others’ detraction to me? Surely I cannot keep people from speaking?” But, as we said above, conduct yourself before a detractor in such a way that he learns not to detract from anyone, when he sees that you do not gladly listen to what he says. “Do not mingle with detractors,” says Solomon, “for their destruction will come suddenly.” And who knows the ruin of both: both, that is, of the one who detracts and of the one who lends an ear to the detractor?

Be mature in morals and conduct, sparing and prudent in words, discerning in silence and restraint. Be diligent in searching out the mysteries of the Scriptures; and if any matter of doubt occurs to you as you read, know that it should be referred to the interpretations of the elders. You have nearby the school of the most ancient city of Trier, in which there is an abundance of learned men; to them you will be able to have recourse in the ambiguity of questions.

Let nothing be dearer to you than knowledge, because the fruit of wisdom is eternal, but the use of riches is brief and momentary. Let there be no one who keeps you from salutary study: not mother, not brother, not sister. Do the work of an evangelist; learn what you are to teach, lest you prove to be a shepherd and an idol. You are one man and have no second self; for whom, then, will you store up riches? Live temperately. Do not frequent weddings and immoderate banquets, for they strip the mind of virtues and leave the purse empty of coins.

Procure for yourself, from the alms offered to you, books of divine Scripture, so that, made learned by constant reading of them, you may be able worthily to instruct the people entrusted to you, and to lift yourself up through contemplation to heavenly things. Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and it will exalt you before God and human beings. Do not consent to the pleasures of the flesh; restrain the impulse of fury; and never forget death.

What a Priest Ought to Be Like in His Inner Conduct.
Chapter 7.

But since it is not enough for a minister of Christ to lead an honorable life before human beings, unless he also strives to appear before God clean from every crime, I exhort you to direct your heart in the fear of the Almighty and never admit any thoughts against God. For a priest of God ought to be holy in mind and body, and clean in thought. Hence the Lord says: “Be holy, because I am holy, the Lord your God, who sanctify you.” And Peter, the chief apostle, says: “You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition, that you may proclaim the virtues of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.”

Let charity hold the first place in your heart, for it is the fullness of the law and the completion of the prophets. For the greatest of all virtues is charity, which consists in love of God and neighbor.
