For blessed Jerome says: “Love knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” Scripture alone is what promises us a heavenly remedy in tribulation. What could we know or believe about the heavenly homeland, if we did not have the Scriptures? For the Apostle says: “All Scripture is divinely inspired,” so that through the patience and consolation of the Scriptures we may have hope. Therefore I admonish you to take up the study of the holy Scriptures as a consolation in this pilgrimage, since if you are constant in reading them, you will easily despise the world with all its vanities. For nothing so lifts the mind from vain anxiety to heavenly things; nothing so calms the fires of the flesh, as diligence in sacred reading. Finally, study is especially fitting for your office, as Malachi says: “The lips of the priest keep knowledge, and they will seek the law from his mouth, because he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts.” For, as we said a little above, the priest of Christ must be learned in the divine law, so that he may know and have matter from which to bring forth a sermon. For he who has undertaken to rule souls, for whom he will render an account to God the supreme judge, must read and understand much. Indeed, as that great Gregory Nazianzen said, and after him the Roman pope: the governance of souls is the art of arts. But if someone has learned nothing in the holy Scriptures, what will he teach others? Or what benefit will a priest be able to bring his hearers in preaching, if he does not know the Scriptures?

Woe now to our times, in which the priest is like the people. Our priests cast aside the study of the Scriptures; they neglect learning; instead of books of Scripture they keep birds and dogs. Let the bishops see to it, who promote such ignorant and unknowing men to the dignity of the priesthood, who entrust the care of Christ’s sheep to unskilled shepherds. Withdraw from the company of those who cast aside knowledge, who neglect the reading of the Scriptures, who despise study. They savor nothing except what is worldly; they love nothing except what is vain; they understand nothing except what is carnal. They will quickly overturn you, if you often pass into their assembly. They sit in ambush with drinkers in taverns, devoting themselves to games and feasts.

The fear of God is not before their eyes. They are angry if anyone begins to speak of the Scriptures before them; they disdain to hear reading, but turn instead to fables. What have you to do with them? They are priests in name, asses in conduct. They understand absolutely nothing of the Scriptures; they despise learning. They do not know how to write or speak the Roman tongue; scarcely have they learned to explain the Gospels in the vernacular. How many errors, fables, and heresies they announce to the people by preaching in church: who could believe it unless he had experienced it? Instead of books they acquire children for themselves; instead of study they love concubines. These are blind guides of the blind, who do not instruct the people of God toward justice, but rather lead them astray. O how many poor Christians live in the countryside who, because of the ignorance of their priests, do not know what befits a Christian. Amid these things there is no one who pays attention, no one who grieves over the danger of souls, no one who applies a remedy. All savor the things that are earthly; all seek their own interests; all run after profits. But what are these things to us? Each one stands or falls before his own lord. And although it does not belong to me to correct these things, nevertheless I cannot help grieving when I see every evil in the Church proceeding from the ignorance of priests.

For who would not grieve that those men do not know the commandments who have been appointed to instruct others? Behold, no knowledge is sought in ordaining priests; those not called are permitted to approach; the life of those to be ordained is never examined, as above all it ought to have been. Therefore the Christian people is led away captive by the cords of sins; the ardor of charity is extinguished; the concord of fraternal love is violated. Our priests overflow with worldly desires; they do not presume to correct the vicious, because they recognize that they themselves have offended in greater matters. For indeed there are few who now shine in the Church with holy morals; few love knowledge of the Scriptures; few instruct the flock entrusted to them by word and examples. For the most part they are chosen not because they are more learned, but because they are more eager to gather perishable riches. They have either no codices of Scripture or few, since they pursue knowledge with hatred.
