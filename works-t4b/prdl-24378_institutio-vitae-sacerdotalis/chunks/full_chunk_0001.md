Thomas Ruscher of Gamundia, professor of sacred letters, unworthy preacher of the evangelical word in the church of Mainz, sends perpetual greeting to the well-reputed and catholic priest Nicolaus Mernicensis.

Lest I should seem cold in giving thanks before him from whom every best gift and every perfect gift is given, most learned Nicolaus, there comes first to mind the Scripture of Deuteronomy, chapter 30: “I have set life and death before your face.” I give thanks because today he grants this also to the one who clings to him who is true life. And I say: he who recently, in my own times, breathed into the face of someone a breath of new life; for this I give thanks to the creator of all.

And to you, O father Trithemius, worthy of praise for your understanding, I give thanks because you have chosen life not only for yourself, but establish life also for me and for many others, so that they may live, in this most flourishing little work of instruction of yours, which soon had been brought all the way to me. Giving place to nothing else, as one in need of the breath of life, I looked into it with affection of mind and desire of soul.

But, O father worthy of praise for your understanding, what you have effected in me, most devoted to you, through this so flourishing little work of your instruction, and what I felt while reading it, I would not wish to hide from my contemporaries. For when I advanced into its inner parts, it so flooded into me that my cheeks were wet.

O brother Nicolaus Mernicensis, most devoted to prayer and charity, how fortunate and how holy was your request for this flourishing little work. O man, spouse of Christ, and son Nicolaus Mernicensis, and confessor of God, how necessary are the things contained in this little work, especially in these present stormy times, when the Christian faith is torn and oppressed by harmful errors that are everywhere sown and sprout up. Necessary, I say, beyond measure.

When this flourishing instruction for the pilgrimage of the priestly life has been cast aside, all vices and disgraces rise up. For I know by the tradition of our elders that the soul of a man is nourished by understanding and by knowledge as by a certain food. I rightly judge the minds of those who have been instructed by no disciplines, by no training in morals or life, and who have drunk in nothing of the good arts, to be fasting and almost famished; yet their minds are full, but full of vices and wickedness.

MONACENSIS.

--- Page 009 ---
Nicolaus, know that I would wish myself and my contemporaries to be persuaded by this: that we should turn ourselves wholly to this flourishing little work. For through it we are able not only to avoid the penalty of crime, but also to obtain the favor of praise. For whoever does not apply to this the sense of smell of the soul will quickly be brought to ruin. For that reason you know that this very thing was fruitful for me before the first impression of its letters.

Especially since the father and lord of praiseworthy understanding, Dom Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, gathered, compiled, and handed it down with no small sweat. He is, by the manifold grace of letters, far more abundant than I am; watchful in talent, ready in eloquence, burning with zeal, and a useful householder in the house of the Lord. By providing instruction in holy life through his flourishing little work, he instructs me, weak as I am, and at the same time the whole clergy, unskilled and learned alike, for their protection.

And surely, brother, if this instruction in life is lacking to us, who, I ask, judges that we are alive? If this is lacking to us, Nicolaus, we are not worthy. For in it everything is supplied to us so fully that without these things no one is worthy of life. For if, according to the flourishing instruction of our reverend father in the Lord, Trithemius, which is at hand, we shall wholly establish ourselves while we live here, true salvation is truly promised to us; and it is never taken away from any who live a life worthy of it.

Therefore all who wish to be regarded as worthy must order their life according to the things handed down in this flourishing little work, so that they may not die, but live forevermore. Most worthy Nicolaus, so that you may be able to comprehend this, farewell always in him who created all things, and may he preserve in his mercy us who cannot stand by our own strength. Amen.

Given in the Studium at Mainz, in the year of our salvation 1494, in the month of October, on the twenty-second day of the same month.
