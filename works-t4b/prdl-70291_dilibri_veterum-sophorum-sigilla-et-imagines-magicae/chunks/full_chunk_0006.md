THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF SOLOMON.

A very ancient little book was found in the desert among the children of Israel; because many works of Solomon were found in it, it was ascribed to Solomon. The title of the book was: On the Seals of Stones According to the Name of the Lord and According to the Course of the Stars.

The figure of a man sitting upon a plough, having a small neck and a long beard, with four men lying on his neck, and holding in one hand a fox and in the other a vulture: know that this seal, hung from the neck, is effective for all plantings and all discoveries of treasures. You shall make trial of it in this way: take pure black wool without grease, wrap the stone in it, place it among wheat chaff, and put it under your head. Then in sleep you will see all the treasures of that region in which it is. At the same time it has the power of healing all the illnesses of beasts, if they drink the water in which it has been washed.

MAGIC.

A man carved in green jasper, who has a shield hanging from his neck, and above his head a helmet with an upright sword, and who treads a serpent under his feet: put it to your neck, and you will fear no enemy. In all matters you will be victorious, especially in martial ones; and it ought to be bound in bronze.

A horse bearing a crocodile on its back, carved in white hyacinth, makes one lovable to men and beasts.

The figure of a seated man and of a woman standing before him, having hair reaching down to her thigh and raising her eyes upward: when it has been carved in carnelian, know that its power is to make every person, whether male or female, obedient. It ought either to be bound with as much weight as the stone itself has, and beneath it put betony and amber.

A foaming horse, with a man upon it holding a scepter in his hand, carved in amethyst: know that this seal benefits the one who wears it in all the things he has, and all princes and chief men will be obedient to him. It ought to be bound in gold or silver, in twice the weight of the seal.

The image of a man's head with a long beard and a little blood around the neck, if it is found carved in diamond: know that it contributes to victory and boldness, and preserves the body from every injury; it is also effective for obtaining favor from princes.

SEALS

The figure of a man wishing to play on instruments, carved in sapphire, makes him most pleasing to all.

The image of a cock, or of three girls, when found in agate, makes a person pleasing before God and men.

The figure of a lion carved in garnet supplies riches and honors, gives joy, and drives away sadness.

The image of a stag or dove carved in onyx gives the wearer courage against demons.

The figure of a man in the likeness of a merchant carrying goods to sell, or of a man sitting under a centurion, when found in emerald, gives riches, makes him victorious, and relieves him from distress.

The image of a bull or calf found in magnetite gives the wearer fortunate journeys through all places, and is effective against all enchantments.

The figure of a horse or wolf found in jasper drives away fevers and stops blood.

The figure of a crowned man in topaz makes the wearer good, well behaved, and beloved by God and men, and grants honors and dignities.

MAGIC.

The image of an armed man with a sword in his hand, when found carved in sard or amethyst, makes the wearer acquire a perfect and good memory, and makes him prudent.

The figure of a stag or goat found carved in chalcedony gives the power of increasing riches when it is kept in a money box.

The image of a hare in jasper does not allow the one who carries it to be harmed by any demon.

A sea-locust carved in beryl reconciles quarrels and joins marriage in love.

The figure of a night-raven, because it is the bird of Pallas, carved in a stone that is conducive to knowledge, makes a person very learned and eloquent.

The image of a peacock carved in a suitable stone makes the wearer rich.

The figure of a man killing a lion, or another beast, with a sword makes the wearer shrewd, victorious, and lovable.
