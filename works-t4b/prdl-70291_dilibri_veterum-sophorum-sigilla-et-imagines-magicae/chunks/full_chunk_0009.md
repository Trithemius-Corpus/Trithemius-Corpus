Likewise there are many images of Venus, but the following is more frequently carved on stones: a woman with a great garment and robe, holding a laurel in her hand. Its virtue is to give agility in affairs to be carried out, to bring everything to the desired end, to remove fear of drowning in water, and to bring feminine honors.

The figure of Mercury is a slender man, having a fine beard and winged sandals, holding the caduceus wand, with a cock before his feet, or with a serpent under his feet. Its virtue is to help in acquiring knowledge and oratory, and it also provides very great assistance in enriching merchants.

But it must be carefully observed, as was also warned above, that the planet whose image you desire to have carved on a suitable ring ought to be very well disposed, that is, not combust, retrograde, oppressed or touched by the rays of malefics, but in a good place of heaven, in the Ascendant or Midheaven, with Jupiter or Venus, and in its essential dignities; otherwise the image will be empty and of no importance.

Furthermore, the ancients formed the image of the Bear in this way.

They carve two bears wound around by a serpent: the greater Bear is contained in the head of the bend, the lesser in the tail. This image, carved on some stone, makes the wearer knowledgeable, crafty, and powerful.

The image of the Crown has the likeness of a royal crown with many stars. Sometimes it is represented in the likeness of a king with a crowned head. It exists in the northern part and in the sign of Sagittarius, and is of the nature of Venus and Mercury. When this image has been carved on a stone, it gives wearers honors and knowledge, and royal favor.

A man fallen to his knees, holding a club in his hand and killing a lion; sometimes a man with a lion skin in his hand. Such figures, carved on a suitable stone, contribute to victory in war, especially if worn in agate.

The image of a swan or hen, with wings spread and neck bent, makes the wearer pleasing to the people, knowledgeable, and rich, and drives away gout, paralysis, and quartan fever.

The figure of Cepheus, having a sword girded on, with arms and hands spread out, carved on a stone, makes one cautious and knowledgeable; and when it is placed under the pillow of a sleeper, it causes delightful things to appear in sleep.

Cassiopeia is the image of a woman seated in a chair, having her hands stretched out in the manner of a cross, sometimes with a triangle on her head. She is established in the sign of Taurus and in the northern part, and her nature is from Saturn and Venus. When she has been carved on a suitable stone, her virtue will be to provide health to human beings, to restore weak and exhausted bodies, to provide rest and tranquility after labor undertaken to those who wear it, and to furnish pleasant and sweet sleep.
