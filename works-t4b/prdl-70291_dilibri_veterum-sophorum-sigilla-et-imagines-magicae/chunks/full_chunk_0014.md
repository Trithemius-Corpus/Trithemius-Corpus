Therefore, to seek the principles of this occult influence from principles and manifest qualities, from temperament, or from the whole substance, is not so much absurd as ridiculous, since the actions of those from whom these powers proceed are not corporeal, and are poured in from heaven without a body. Hence there is no need here for any measure drawn from the senses, no ambitious battle of words, and no empty effort at demonstrations. That the beginning of every philosophical demonstration cannot be sought from the senses and qualities, but must be sought elsewhere, is evident even from this common example: if someone asks about the white color of a rose, namely whence it takes its origin, and wishes to ascribe the cause to a colder temperament, what, I ask, will he say about the red one? Will you say that species of endive pour forth a milky juice for this reason, because they are cold? Yet very many very hot plants, such as the spurges, swell and drip with the same juice. And is aloe bitter for this reason, because it is hot? Yet in opium, which kills by its deadly coldness, there is extraordinary bitterness. And although it may be granted that such things proceed from a property of the whole substance, nevertheless a higher and more excellent maker, engraver, and former must be placed and sought above it in our mystery. In vain, therefore, did the Alexandrians try to refer everything to manifest qualities,

which Scaliger, Exercise 318, says is the height of shamelessness; and at least in the case of poisons they ought to have stopped. We see certain things burst forth from the womb and bosom of nature whose causes, even if they are altogether inexplicable to us, nevertheless meanwhile it delights and pleases the lover of medicine and the industrious and sagacious investigator of natural things to devise probable reasons for them. If he does not fully attain these reasons, he nevertheless does not take away trust from manifest facts, nor does he slander their effects, but follows nature and its maker with admiration.

Hence it is clear that the things elicited concerning characteristical cure, which accomplishes natural operations by engraved images hung from the neck, through the celestial properties of the stars agreeing with our bodies by a wondrous influence, are not altogether vain. I say that those things are not plainly vain which the most ancient investigators of nature committed to letters and monuments concerning the written word, or characters and seals, fashioned at fixed times according to the power and influx of heaven, without any superstition, which only ignorance of this mystery has invented, apart from profanation of the divine name, scandal, or any violation of faith and religion. Although these things have hitherto, and especially in these last times, lain hidden, concealed and covered over among certain persons,

who abused them rather than used them lawfully, since, having slipped into superstitions and not understanding the first principles, they dared to attribute more to them than God, the creator of all things, granted and willed to be made manifest through them. But so far am I from attributing any power in themselves to these images, or from affirming that characters or named constellated things have power by reason of their figure, since figure, number, size, position, and the division of the continuous are principles of no action, that unless there is some other thing acting and moving, by whose power the characters are effective, I wholly deny it. God heals human beings through the prophets by the mere utterance of the word. For God is living, and the name of the living God is also living, and the letters of the living name also live. God lives on account of himself; his name lives on account of him; and the letters live on account of the name. And just as God has life in himself, so also he has given to his name to have life in itself, and further to the letters through the name.

Images or characters, therefore, are effective by reason of the power or office ordained by God or by nature for such a name or character. For there is no power, either in heaven or on earth, that does not descend from God. Thus medicines are visible bodies, words invisible bodies; whether herb or word heals, God is the cause, that is, the Spirit of God, the helper of nature.
