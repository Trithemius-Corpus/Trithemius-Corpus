Bind the sign of Capricorn, carved in cornelian or in another stone, in a silver ring and carry it with you; for you will be harmed by enemies neither in person nor by speech, nor will a judge pronounce an unjust sentence against you. You will abound in business and honor, and will gain the friendship of many; and all enchantments that have been made against you will be destroyed. TREATISE III.

THE SEALS OR IMAGES OF HERMES.

Hermes, in book 14 of the Quadripartite work, records these images.

An image of a man, carved in diadochoc, standing and of great stature, holding an obol in his right hand and a serpent in the other, with the figure of the Sun above the man’s head, and with a lion prostrate beneath his feet: when it has been placed in a lead ring with a little artemisia and root of fenugreek, and you have it with you on the bank of a river and call upon the aquatic spirits, you will receive from them answers concerning the things asked.

The figure of a standing man holding a bundle of herb hanging from his neck, with thick loins and broad shoulders: when it has been carved in green jasper, know that it gives aid to those suffering from fever and frees them from fever. And if anyone practices the art of medicine and carries it with him, it gives him very great favor in discerning illnesses, recognizing medicines and herbs, and administering potions; and in every hemorrhage and flow of blood it helps, and immediately checks it.

The image of a sea turtle, if it is carved in a sea-stone and is worn bound in a lead ring, cannot be harmed by anyone, and will be loved by the milder ones and by the lords of lands.

The figure of Aquarius, if it has been carved in green jasper and someone carries it with him, gives profit in selling and buying goods; merchants will seek counsel from him and will bring merchandise to his dwelling.

A bird holding a leaf in its mouth, and in its sight the head of a man looking at that bird: if it is carved in pangonus-stone, and you bind it in gold and carry it with you, you will be rich and wealthy, and venerable to all.

The figure of Jupiter, which is a man sitting on a four-footed chair, with four men standing there before Jupiter, and Jupiter’s hands raised toward heaven, and a diadem on his head: if it is carved in hyacinth and bound in gold, and someone carries such a ring with him, or hangs from his neck the figure of this ring impressed in wax, he will obtain from kings and wise men whatever he wishes.
