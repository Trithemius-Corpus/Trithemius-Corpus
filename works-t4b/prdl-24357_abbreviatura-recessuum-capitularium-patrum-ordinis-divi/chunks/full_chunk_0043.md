In the beginnings of this new practice, however, let them summon two neighboring abbots of the Cistercian Order to provide them suitable counsel and assistance, since through long custom they are more fully instructed in celebrating chapters of this kind. These abbots, without contradiction, shall associate with themselves two from among the others whom they think expedient; and those four shall preside over the whole chapter, so that from this no one of them assumes for himself the authority of a prelate, since, when expedient, it can be changed by prudent deliberation. Let a chapter of this kind be celebrated continuously for several fixed days according to the custom of the Cistercian Order. In it let there be diligent discussion concerning the reform of the order and regular observance. And what shall have been established, with those four approving, shall be inviolably observed, every excuse, contradiction, and appeal removed; nevertheless provision shall be made where the chapter ought to be celebrated at the following term.

And those who have assembled shall lead a common life and pay proportionally all common expenses, so that if not all can stay in the same house, at least several may stay in different houses and eat in the same manner. In the same chapter let religious and circumspect persons also be appointed, who, according to the form prescribed to them, shall strive in our stead to visit each abbey of the same kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, correcting and reforming whatever they see to need the office of correction and reform. Thus, if they learn that the rector of a place must be entirely removed from administration, let them denounce it to the proper bishop, so that he may see to removing him. If he does not do this, let the visitors refer the matter to the examination of the Apostolic See. We will and command that this same thing be observed for regular canons according to their order.

But if in this new practice any difficulty should arise which cannot be resolved by the aforesaid persons, let it be referred without scandal to the judgment of the Apostolic See, while the other things which have been provided by common deliberation are to be observed without opposition.

Furthermore, diocesan bishops shall take care to reform the monasteries subject to them in such a way that, when the aforesaid visitors come to them, they find in them more that is worthy of commendation than of correction, taking the greatest care that through them the said monasteries are not burdened in due obligations; for we wish the rights of superiors to be observed in such a way that we do not wish injuries to inferiors to be endured.

Moreover, we strictly command both diocesan bishops and the persons who preside over chapters to be celebrated that, by ecclesiastical censure, with appeal removed, they restrain advocates, patrons, vice-lords, rectors, consuls, magnates, knights, or any others, lest they presume to injure monasteries in persons or property. And if perhaps they have injured them, they shall not neglect to compel them to satisfaction, so that the monks may be able to serve God more freely and peacefully.
