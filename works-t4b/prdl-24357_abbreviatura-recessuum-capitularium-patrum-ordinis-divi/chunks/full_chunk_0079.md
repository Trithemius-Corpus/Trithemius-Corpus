Concerning the Places of Monasteries. Rubric Eight.

The Apostle says: “What is spiritual is not first, but what is natural.” Therefore, first, as has been said, one must consider external things, that is, places, as bodily matters. Places are of three kinds. Some are necessary, such as the oratory, cloister, refectory, dormitory, infirmary, bath, storehouse, cellar, a place for guests with the abbot’s house, kitchen, and the porter’s cell with the chapter room. If any of these places is lacking, it must be built within a year, by virtue of holy obedience, or repaired if it has fallen down; and those who are negligent must actually be punished by the visitors, as above, chapter 3.

Some places, however, are matters of well-being, such as a garden, bakery, mill, workshop for binding books, and the like. Others are superfluous, harmful and contrary to the regular norm, such as wandering, secluded, and suspicious lodgings belonging to certain houses. These must either be utterly destroyed or converted to some other, better use of the monastery, under the same obedience as above.

The oratory, which is first among the necessary places, must be furnished with lights, altars, and ornaments that are clean, pure, and decent; the choir with seats, pulpits, and chant books; the sacristy with chalices, corporals, missal books, sacred treasures, and vestments and ornaments pertaining to the divine office and ministries, as is said in chapter 1, where these words are also added: “We will,” it says, “that within a month provision be made for clean, decent, and secure vessels and a place in which the Eucharist is to be reserved.” The same statute has provisions there and in the following sections concerning the baptistery. Books, with the other ornaments, are to be kept under careful custody. These things are to be understood and observed concerning treasures and seals, and certain following chapters likewise wish the same. Therefore the visitors must diligently attend to each of these matters concerning the oratory.

And concerning these things they must consider: what revenues, offerings, and so forth pertain to the oratory and sacristy; likewise, in what manner, whether simoniacally or not; likewise, under what appearance of vice property is possessed; and so concerning the other matters. And in the same way a similar ordinance applies to the other officials, such as the abbot, cellarer, and so forth: whether every year they render an account, and in what manner.

For it is prescribed in the above-mentioned third chapter, and also in the first, by virtue of holy obedience and under penalty of excommunication to be imposed by the visitors, that every abbot shall hand over to the visitors in writing a distinct account of the ancient and present state of his monastery, and that all officials shall present to them a faithful account of receipts and distributions. Likewise, inventories are to be made; places are not to be leased out except according to the Benedictine constitution, which must also be observed in receiving spoils from benefices and in loans and sales to be made. Transgressors must be punished severely, even beyond the penalties contained in the aforesaid constitutions. These things concern the oratory. The rest concerning the other places will appear below in the section on the discipline of persons.

Concerning the Persons Who Dwell Within Monasteries. Rubric 9.

The persons who dwell within monasteries, if they are not professed, must be not only necessary and useful, but also respectable and free from suspicion. Therefore all who are superfluous, disreputable, and suspicious must be removed far from the boundaries of the monasteries. Those, however, who are already professed or soon to be professed must be considered very carefully, both according to the quantity of their number and according to the quality of their condition. For everywhere the number of monks once established ought, if resources suffice, to be preserved whole, or at least as many as can conveniently be supported, as above, chapter 1, and in certain following chapters; through them the divine worship may be performed perfectly and laudably. From them sufficient and necessary officials are to be appointed according to the disposition of the Rule: namely, abbot, prior, deans, cellarer, porter, infirmarian, and so concerning the rest.
