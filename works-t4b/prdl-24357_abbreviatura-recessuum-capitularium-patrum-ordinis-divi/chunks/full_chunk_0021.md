When these things have been completed, or as much as shall please the presidents has been read, the names of the dead, if any occur, shall immediately be recited from the book of the monastery. Then, from the slips brought by the priors, the names of the brothers of the order who have died since the last chapter was celebrated shall be recited by the same reader of the martyrology. When these are finished, the president shall add: May the souls of the brothers, familiars, and benefactors of our order rest in peace. And when the others have said Amen, let them read, as is customary, the psalm De profundis to the end, concluding with Requiem eternam. And after each has said the Pater noster silently, the official shall add Et ne nos, and the other things contained in the acts of the daily chapter.

When these things have been completed, one of the presidents shall enjoin the abbot of the house that after vespers the brothers shall perform vigils in choir for those dead. And he shall appoint one of the priors who on the next day shall solemnly sing the high mass for the same dead. After this the promoter of the order shall read the chapter In singulis from the Decretals, as follows: Innocent III, in the general council.

In each kingdom or province, every three years, with the right of the diocesan bishops preserved, there shall be held a common chapter of abbots and of priors who do not have their own abbots and who have not been accustomed to celebrate such a chapter. All who do not have a canonical impediment shall come together for it at one of the monasteries suited for this purpose, with this moderation observed: that none of them bring more than six mounts and seven persons. At the beginnings of this new practice, however, they shall summon two neighboring abbots of the Cistercian order to provide them suitable counsel and assistance, since they are, by long custom, more fully instructed in celebrating chapters of this kind.

XXIII

These, without contradiction, shall associate with themselves two from among them whom they think suitable. And those four shall preside over the whole chapter, in such a way that from this none of them assumes for himself the authority of prelacy; hence, when it is expedient, he may be changed by prudent deliberation. They shall celebrate this kind of chapter continuously for several fixed days according to the custom of the Cistercian order, and in it they shall hold a careful discussion concerning the reform of the order and regular observance. And what has been established with the approval of those four shall be observed inviolably, all excuse, contradiction, and appeal being removed; provision nevertheless having been made where the chapter should be celebrated at the next term. And those who have assembled shall lead a common life and shall all together meet the common expenses proportionally, so that, if not all can stay in the same house, at least several may stay together in different houses.

They shall also appoint in the same chapter religious and circumspect persons who, according to the form prescribed to them, shall in our stead endeavor to visit every abbey of the same kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns, correcting and reforming what they see to be in need of the office of correction and reform. Thus, if they recognize that the rector of a place must be wholly removed from administration, they shall denounce him to his own bishop, so that he may see to removing him. And if he does not do so, the visitors shall refer this to the examination of the apostolic see.

The things that follow in that chapter are not customarily read in sequence. The following chapter will continue it: Honorius III, to the abbots and monks established in Lombardy and the March of Treviso.

From Quae pro religionis honestate, etc. Moreover, when the visitors appointed according to the statute of the general council by the general chapter of abbots have proceeded to carry out the office of visitation, they shall diligently inquire into the state of the monks and their regular observances; and they shall correct and reform, both in spiritual and in temporal matters, what they see should be corrected.
