*Concerning the habit of those riding or walking on a journey.*

We have decreed that it be ordered in this way: namely, that the outer habit shall be a mantle hanging down to the middle of the calves, cut with one opening on each side; and beneath it a scapular, which shall exceed the mantle by the length of three transverse fingers, and whose appendage shall appear outside. Because of the fear of excommunication laid upon such persons, those who conceal or alter the habit without reasonable cause [unclear].

*Privileges of the monasteries. Concerning the custody of privileges.*

We command that privileges and the other jewels, relics, and treasures be preserved under faithful custody and firm enclosure.

*Concerning conspiracy and defamation.*

We strictly forbid every conspiracy against abbots and other prelates and rectors, as well as against private persons; also defamation of abbots and brothers, and every betrayal of the secret of the chapter. We command that transgressors of such things be corrected by their superiors, so that the others may take fear.

*That beggars are henceforth not to be received into our order.*

We also forbid, in virtue of holy obedience, all prelates subject to our chapter to receive beggars henceforth into our order. Those, however, who have already been received and wander here and there shall be recalled as quickly as possible to their monasteries.

*Concerning deceased brothers.*

“Mercy alone is the companion of the dead.” We establish and ordain concerning the dead that each abbot, or their proctors, shall henceforth bring and present in each chapter brief notices of the names of deceased brothers, and of the days of their deaths, who have died between the chapter already celebrated and the chapter to be celebrated, so that prayers and suffrages may be made for them. And this is the form of the brief: “Such-and-such a priest or levite, etc., died on such a day in such a place,” etc.

*Concerning nuns.*

Those things that have been said concerning monasteries of men we will also to be inviolably observed in monasteries of nuns, insofar as they pertain to the articles applicable to them.

The statute concerning the publication of the processes of the whole present chapter and of the statutes of this chapter, often set down above, and concerning the general chapter to be observed within the year, is here again set down and renewed in all respects as above, chapter 1, at the end, where it may be read.

*Conclusion.*

Many other things also were here established, ordained, and treated, which partly are contained above and partly, as less necessary, I have passed over; but all are contained in the book of recesses.

*The fifth provincial chapter was celebrated at Saint Stephen’s in Würzburg, in the year of the Lord 1424.*

In the year of the Lord 1424, in the second indiction, on the fourteenth day of the month of May, which was Jubilate Sunday, in the seventh year of the pontificate of the lord Pope Martin V, the fifth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Stephen at Würzburg. In it there presided the venerable fathers Balduin in Lüneburg, Theoderic in Northeim, Appello in Sülzheim, and Helfric of Saint John’s in Rheingau, abbots of monasteries.

*Concerning the old statutes.*

In this chapter almost all the statutes of the preceding chapters were approved and inserted into the recess word for word, though a few things were added; of these I have taken care briefly to mark some here, for greater authority.

First, the ordinances concerning the divine office; concerning ringing; concerning the chapter of faults to be observed daily; concerning the creation of monks; that monks are not to be received for a price; concerning the celebration of masses; concerning the common life of all the brothers; that provisions are not to be given in money; concerning the dormitory; concerning silence; concerning collapsed places to be reformed; concerning the account to be made to the visitors; concerning the old porter; concerning the propriety of vestments; concerning conspiracies; concerning games; concerning beggars not to be received into the order; concerning the publication of the statutes; concerning nuns; concerning visitors.
