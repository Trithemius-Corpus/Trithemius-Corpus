If, however, any one of the officials is absent, the others will nevertheless be able to proceed with the acts, according to the ordinance of chapter 20. But in the event that none of the presidents is present, the others who have assembled for the celebration may, for that occasion, appoint other suitable presidents, by the indult of Pope Martin V, whose bull is included among the acts of the first chapter. By the same indult, those presidents may also change the place of the chapter, together with the extension of the term, for due causes. Likewise, no one there, whether he is an official or not, may leave before the end of the chapter without the presidents’ permission, under the penalty stated above, chapters 3 and 4, and under sentence of excommunication to be imposed, as above, chapter 20. Also, no one coming to the chapter ought to have more than six mounts; if anyone does so, he must, within a month, place in the chest for the common benefit of the chapter three times as much as he is shown to have spent for the rest, as above, chapter 3; and if he has not observed this, at the next following chapter he must be condemned by the presidents to double payment, without any remission. Likewise, each one must contribute there three Rhenish florins, which they must pay either then or within the three-day period during which the visitators happen to be with them, without further delay, under penalty of excommunication; once the term has elapsed, the visitators are most strictly ordered to pronounce this against them, according to chapter 5 of the Benedictine statutes, as above, chapter 1.

How the chapter is to be celebrated, and how the fathers ought to conduct themselves in it. Rubric Two.

Therefore, if the time is at hand for the chapter to be celebrated, which has been appointed always to be celebrated every three years unless, for a certain and reasonable cause, it is extended longer by the presidents, then first, after the Mass and sermon have been held beforehand, and after the fathers have been gathered together in one place in the chapter room, and after the Benedicite has been said, following the reading of the martyrology and the passage from the text of the Rule recited by the reader, a formal protestation is to be made concerning the non-admission of excommunicated persons, as above, chapter 20. Secondly, the place for the future chapter is to be named and announced by the presidents.

That place must not be changed except for a grave and reasonable cause, and only by the presidents, and it can be changed as stated above, chapter 1. Then, according to the ordinance of chapter 17 and certain others, the presidents and the other officials of the future chapter are to be designated, and also the visitators of the monasteries, as well as the one who is to give the sermon and another who is to celebrate the Mass; not, indeed, under their proper names, but only under the names of their offices, so that if anyone should resign or die, or certainly be deposed, then the abbot of the same place would carry out the office enjoined upon him. For although the person changes, nevertheless the dignity, which does not die, always remains. Finally, action is to be taken concerning those matters that are to be corrected in the order, and those that perhaps are newly to be ordered and established for the benefit of the whole order. There one must begin first with those who are present: each of them must state his fault on the mat and voluntarily offer himself for suitable correction. Their visitators must accuse and proclaim them. But those who for that time are procurators of absent persons must undergo disciplines and other suitable penalties not only for themselves, but also for those who appointed them, such as the presidents themselves, according to the report of the visitators or other certain and evident knowledge of any kind, shall justly and reasonably have decreed are to be imposed. Secondly, the absent are to be dealt with, and so, in order, the remaining business pressing upon the order. Lastly, each matter issued and discussed is to be reduced to a certain form and inserted into the charter or recess. Nor may any layman be present at those discussions or be admitted there, except only a public notary and honorable witnesses. But even these must not be admitted indiscriminately; indeed, only when the act itself is now to be reduced into public form. In that form there must also be written the names of all the abbots present and of the procurators of the absent. All who are present must receive a copy of it.
