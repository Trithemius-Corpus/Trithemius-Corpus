*Concerning the claustral prior, what sort he ought to be.*

This sacred chapter has decreed that a prudent and discreet prior be appointed in every monastery, as the more suitable one can be found in the congregation, who is capable in deed and speech, diligent concerning regular discipline, and observes the articles of the order that concern him or the brothers. But if he is found to be a transgressor of the aforesaid matters, or blameworthy in anything, let him be admonished once and again. If he does not amend, let him be removed by the abbot from the office of the priorate, and let another who is suitable be substituted in his place. This must be understood not only of the prior, but also of all officials of the monastery.

*The lord presidents, visitors, and treasurers are to come before the day of the chapter.*

We further enact and ordain that the presidents of the chapter, both past and future, shall henceforth come before the appointed day, together with the treasurers and visitors: so that the treasurers are there on the penultimate Saturday before the Sunday on which the chapter is to be held; the presidents, however, on the Monday preceding that Sunday; and the visitors on the following Tuesday, so that they may make a timely report to the presidents concerning their visitations. Therefore these same presidents and treasurers also are ordered, as has been said, to come before the day on which the chapter is to be held, so that they may inform one another about each matter and render account to the new presidents and to the chapter concerning receipts and expenditures. This statute had also been issued in the earlier chapters, namely in the first and the following ones.

*Concerning visitors.*

We further decree and ordain that the visitors appointed in chapter, immediately after the chapter has been held, if it can conveniently be done, or as soon as possible, shall begin and complete the act of their visitation, having God alone before their eyes. We also will that these visitors, appointed and to be appointed, should not omit to visit also the monasteries of nuns in the dioceses in which they are to conduct visitation.

*Concerning the penalty of visitors who neglect to visit at the proper time.*

If the visitors neglect to visit within the time appointed for them, namely within the year, they shall pay the penalty, as do those who come to the chapter neither in person nor through suitable proctors, namely thirty Rhenish florins, saving moderation and just assessment at the discretion of the presidents in the provincial chapter.

*Concerning the procurations of the visitors.*

We also command and will that every abbot and convent, when their visitors have come to them for the purpose of visitation, shall satisfy them within three days with the sum of three Rhenish florins.

*Concerning the confessions of monks.*

We prohibit and forbid all monks, conversi, and brothers subject to us that anyone henceforth should dare to confess to anyone, inside or outside the monastery, without the permission of his superior.

*Concerning the celebration of masses.*

As for celebrations, let them observe those things that are contained in the constitutions of lord Pope Benedict XII and in the statutes of the preceding chapters. We also perpetually forbid monks to become godfathers, and forbid them every game and dishonorable exercise.
