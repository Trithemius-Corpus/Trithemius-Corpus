*That provisions are not to be given in money. Article 34.* We forbid provisions, clothing, and other necessities to be supplied to monks in money or revenues in any way, notwithstanding any constitution or ordinance whatsoever. And in this matter the constitution of the aforesaid Pope Benedict XII is to be observed. We also will that the aforesaid clothing and provisions be supplied decently to Benedictine religious at the appointed times.

*On the vice of incontinence. Article 35.* The visitors of the chapter also are to exercise exact diligence by inquiring whether in any place there are any persons marked by incontinence. And where they find this to be so, especially among superiors, or find persons conniving at the incontinence of others, they are to take every care to purge out that vice with due punishment. And if perhaps they cannot do this because of favor shown to persons, they are to report it to the provincial chapter. The said visitors are to inquire whether abbots are too fleshly in distributing the offices and benefices of their monastery, so that they pay more regard to the advancement of their relatives and blood-kin, and whether on account of them the monastery suffers ruin or loss in spiritual matters and is damaged in temporal matters; and they are to rebuke the guilty very severely and report them to the provincial chapter. They are also to inquire whether an abbot, in pride of heart, treats his monks not in brotherhood, as though he were not one of them; and whether he does not permit them to speak anything, even charitably, for the good of the monastery and of morals; whether he himself, by his own fury, is overturning his house; and whomever they find guilty they are to take care to correct diligently, or else denounce them to the provincial chapter.

*On the obligation of these constitutions with respect to penalty. Article 36.* By these things we do not understand that the aforesaid provisions bind more to fault than they formerly bound, but only to penalty.

XLII

We also will that the declarations of the Roman pontiffs issued concerning the aforesaid Benedictine constitutions have force in the foregoing matters.

*That visitors be diligent in all things. Article 38.* In all the foregoing articles it is committed to the visitation of the visitors that they be solicitous for the profit of souls and, according to the disposition alike of persons and of places, act prudently in all things.

*That laymen are not to be appointed over the needs of the monastery. Article 39.* Laymen are not to be appointed over offices, nor monks to receive and collect the goods of the monastery and distribute them, except insofar as they are subject to and servants of the officials themselves of the monasteries, and are bound to render an account to them of what has been received and distributed. Given at Basel, on the tenth day before the Kalends of March, in the year from the birth of the Lord 1439. Here ends the bull of the Council of Basel for the reform of the order, transmitted to the chapter.

*On the life and morals of the visitors, especially while they are visiting.* Since formerly, and below. Therefore, first of all, we will and, under appeal to the promise made to us and to the strict examination of the Last Judgment, enjoin upon you that at all times, and especially during your visitation, in all conduct, life, morals, and discipline, you take care to bear and conduct yourselves so religiously, holily, and honorably that to the others to be visited by you, not only by word but also by example, you may offer a model of complete perfection. During the time of visitation you are to proceed in suitable dress, with a household and mounts appropriate and honorable, not excessive, so that each of you is content with a chapel and household and with three horses.
