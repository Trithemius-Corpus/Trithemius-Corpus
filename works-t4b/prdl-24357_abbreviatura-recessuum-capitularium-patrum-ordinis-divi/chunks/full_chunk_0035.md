Yet, with the abbot’s permission, they may use decent hoods in the proper places where they are accustomed to wear them, split over the shoulders to a length of at least four transverse fingers.

They are not to wear sendal in their garments for linings, or miniver, vair, or any other costly material; but for the said linings let them use only the skins of lambs or young sheep, or at least other cloths that are not costly. Nor are the linings to appear in any way above the collar, or at the hems of the garments or sleeves.

Concerning the Clothing of Those Traveling. Article 6.

Further, when it happens that they set out outside the monastery, they are to wear a frock, or a cowl, or a cloak; and beneath the cloak, if they wear it, they are to have a cowl or scapular.

Concerning the Clothing of Novices and Conversi. Article 7.

Novices, however, are to use a cowl, inside and outside the monastery, open on both sides. Conversi are to use a scapular with a hood attached to it and closed, inside and outside the monastery.

Concerning the Length and Width of Garments. Article 8.

But because in the aforesaid habits some use garments more ample and longer, and others less so than is fitting, for this reason we have thought it necessary to insert here the length and width of the aforesaid garments, which we wish to be observed generally. And first, beginning with the hood of the frock, we ordain that its width is not to reach the elbow by the thickness of two transverse fingers; nor may it be narrower, or so short, that it does not extend as far as the middle of the elbow and of the upper bone near the shoulder. At the back, however, it is to extend at least as far as the belt, and is not to extend beyond it by more than four fingers.

The length of the sleeves is to extend one cubit beyond the hand and no more; and their width is not to exceed one and a half cubits. The frock below is to be so wide that steps can easily be taken within it.

And what has been said about the frock we wish to be understood of the cowl also, except for the sleeves, since cowls have none. Within the monastery, however, in places where there are twelve monks, all are to use frocks or large cowls. In other places, however, where there is not so large a congregation, they are to use cowls, not frocks, except for the president of the monastery and the one second after him, whom we wish to wear the frock in every case. Yet during work and the divine office beneath the alb, and while riding beneath the cloak, they may wear a scapular at least half a cubit wide.

And they are to wear boots or high strapped shoes, so that their width covers a space of four fingers on the leg above the ankle. But if in any of the foregoing matters they have not worn the habits as set out above, beyond the penalties inflicted by law we wish them to be punished by their superiors in their religious house, or, if they have no superior, by the visitors of the provincial chapter, in such a way that it may serve as an example to the rest.

That Monks Should Live in Common in the Refectory Without Eating Meat. Article 9.

Likewise, the brothers are to live and eat in common in the refectory, in which no one at all is to eat meat. And the visitors are to be diligent in seeing that they cause the constitution of Benedict concerning the eating of and abstinence from meats to be observed inviolably.

Concerning the Reading to Be Done at the Brothers’ Table. Article 10.

At the brothers’ table, too, sacred reading is not to be lacking; and the one who is to read for the whole week is to enter on Sunday after the blessing, as is stated in the Rule, chapter 38, concerning the weekly reader.

How Monks Should Sleep. Article 11.

Each monk is to sleep clothed in his own bed, in the same place if this can be done, or at least in groups of ten or twenty; but not in separate rooms, especially if they are not suitably partitioned, so that the bedding in them can be plainly and clearly seen, according to the manner of their way of life.
