XLVIII

From the Council of Constance. When and why the provincial chapter was reestablished, and at what time.

Ancient authority teaches, and experience makes clear, that it is a small thing to establish laws, however renowned, unless they are duly committed to execution. In former times, at various dates, both the general council and Honorius, and also Benedict XII, Roman pontiffs of happy memory, are read to have issued, with much mature deliberation, salutary statutes concerning provincial chapters to be celebrated every three years in this religion and order, and concerning their general chapters to be held every year. These provincial chapters, and also general chapters, are reported to have been put into execution only rarely, and for a long time, in very many regions. From their omission many inconveniences in spiritual and temporal matters, and almost irrecoverable losses, have come upon this religion.

Therefore, since from the celebration of chapters of this kind there are no doubt accustomed to arise the correction of the depraved, the reform of morals, the avoidance of many evils, and the promotion of manifold good, we, who carry in the depths of charity the reform of this sacred religion and order, whose knowledge and veneration the teachings of the fathers display, have resolved to restore what has been omitted in this matter, and especially, before the holy council is dissolved, to appoint new executors of the said constitutions in the regions where the old ones are not being ruled according to their tenors.
