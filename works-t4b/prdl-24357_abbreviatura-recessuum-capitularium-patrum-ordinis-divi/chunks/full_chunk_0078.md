Likewise, in whatever diocese they have been appointed to visit monasteries of men, they ought also to visit the monasteries of nuns in it, just as do the others to whom care of them has been committed. Nevertheless, they must take care never to visit them out of desire to see them, since the sight of them is dangerous; and when they have finished there, they ought to withdraw at once, etc., 16.

They ought likewise to visit provostships, priories, and deaneries that are subject to certain monasteries, admitting, however, the abbots to whom they are subject, if they wish to be present. And those visited in such places shall pay half the collection.

They ought to perform the act of visitation itself according to the form prescribed to them by the presidents of the provincial chapter, without exacting an oath, after first setting forth the word of God, etc., 6. Nor ought they to constrain anyone by oath, or otherwise indiscreetly, to reveal and publish hidden offenses, unless the order of fraternal correction has been observed.

They may also visit again a monastery previously visited by them, if they see that this is expedient, but not for that reason exact procuration again and as often. Likewise, they can impose penalties, and ought to do so according to the requirement of the faults; and they may exact penalties instituted either by the provincial chapter or otherwise by anyone, both corporal and monetary. But the money which they receive, whether tributary or penal, they ought to send to the guardians of the chest, so that it may be deposited by them in the chest.

Likewise, for contumacy or rebellion and disobedience they can pronounce sentences of excommunication, and absolve from an imposed penalty after suitable satisfaction has first been promised. Likewise, if they find any rebels and resisters whom they cannot correct by themselves, they ought to denounce them to the neighboring president, so that he, with the assistance of such persons brought in, may proceed through ecclesiastical censure according to the measure of the fault. For all those are also excommunicated by the very fact who in any way either impede visitations from being carried out or abandon a reformation once begun, unless within the time to be set by the visitors

they attempt to begin it again. Likewise, they ought to provide the record to every monastery visited by them. To these things, therefore, the visitors themselves are bound under obedience and the penalties noted above. If they have been negligent, whether in many matters or in few, they ought to be compelled by the presidents and procurators of the chapter, through ecclesiastical censure or another penalty, not to neglect the office entrusted to them.

Yet, lest they be compelled to do labor without reward and, contrary to apostolic tradition, to serve as soldiers at their own expense, it has been ordained that each monastery visited by them ought to give them three Rhenish florins for travel expenses. It has been committed to their consciences either to remit or even to increase those same florins for good reason.

Likewise, the visited monasteries are to guard their horses and other goods with the utmost diligence. And if, which God forbid, anything from their goods should happen to be lost or carried off, before all else the abbot and convent of such a monastery must pay without delay the value of the goods or things lost, without contradiction; and nevertheless they are to be punished for negligence by a penalty to be moderated by the presidents. And the same abbot must also provide safe-conduct for the visitors. If the visitors have been despoiled or harmed, he must likewise provide safe-conduct. The visitation is not to have six mounts, neither in the act of visitation nor in coming to chapter.

Concerning the Act of Visitation. Rubric Seven.

In the act of visitation, the eye of consideration of the fathers making the visitation must be directed chiefly to four things. First, namely, to the sites of the monasteries, as the more visible things. Second, to the persons dwelling within the sites of the monasteries. Third, to the conduct and discipline of the persons.
