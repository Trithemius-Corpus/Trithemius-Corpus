*Those being visited are to guard diligently the goods and horses of the visitors.*

Furthermore, when the visitors, for the sake of carrying out visitation, come to some monastery, the abbot and convent of that monastery are immediately to see that the horses, property, and goods of the visitors are guarded with the greatest diligence. But if anything from their property, goods, or horses should happen to be lost or stolen, the abbot and convent of that place themselves, without any contradiction, are to pay the value of the lost goods or property to the visitors without delay; and nevertheless, for such negligence, they are to be punished in chapter at the discretion of the presidents.

*If the visitors should happen to be robbed while on the way in carrying out their office.*

If, however, which God forbid, the visitors, while on the way to carry out visitation, should be robbed or suffer damage in any respect, the provincial chapter shall compensate them for such loss as is fitting.

*Concerning safe conduct for the visitors.*

It was further decreed that every abbot who is to be visited shall provide the visitors, according to his ability, safe conduct as far as another monastery to be visited, if he has been asked by the visitors.

*No one is to be procurator for more than three.*

We also forbid anyone to be procurator for more than three. But if anyone has presumed to be procurator for more, that procuration of his is not to be admitted.

*If any convent wishes to send letters to the chapter.*

It pleased all those resident in the chapter that, if any convent or monastery of our province has a need to come to the provincial chapter itself, for whatever reason, it should choose from among itself one or two suitable and faithful men, and no more, to present the matter of its delegation diligently. But if they have presumed to send three, let them be punished by their superiors as disobedient.

*Concerning the chest and the custodians of the chest.*

It was further decreed that, for the common use of the chapter, a chest is to be kept at Nuremberg in the monastery of Saint Giles of the Order of Saint Benedict. From there treasurers are to be designated.

*Concerning the mortuary briefs.*

We further decree that individual abbots, or their procurators, when they come to any provincial chapter to be held in the future, are to bring with them briefs containing the names of the deceased brothers of their monasteries who have died between the chapter already held and the chapter to be held, so that they may be commended to the prayers of the fathers.

*This concludes the fifth chapter.*

Many other things were repeated and decreed in the present chapter which have not been set out here item by item, because they are already contained above once in the same words, and it would be tedious to repeat the same things so often in writing.

*The sixth provincial chapter was held at Saint Peter’s in Erfurt in the year of the Lord 1426.*

In the year of the Lord 1426, in the fourth indiction, in the ninth year of the pontificate of Pope Martin V, on the twenty-first day of the month of April, which was Jubilate Sunday, the sixth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of Saint Peter in Erfurt. In it these venerable fathers presided: the lord abbots Johannes in Vrauwe, Heinrich of Saint Giles in Brunswick, Georg of Saint Giles in Nuremberg, and Nikolaus in Bretzenhausen.

*The old statutes are first commended by common consent of all.*

First, we approve, ratify, and renew all and each of the statutes issued in the past chapters, and we will them to have force as expressly confirmed, just as if they had been inserted here.

*Sentences of excommunication and censures are here removed.*

It pleased the lord presidents and all those resident in the chapter to remove and relax the sentences of excommunication and the censures issued in past chapters, lest they produce a snare for souls. Therefore they said: The penalties and sentences of excommunication, suspension, and interdict pronounced by the presidents in past chapters, lest they bring forth a snare for souls, are removed in the name of God.
