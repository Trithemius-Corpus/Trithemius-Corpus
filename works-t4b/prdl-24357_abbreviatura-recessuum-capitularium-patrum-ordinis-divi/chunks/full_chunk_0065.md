*With what caution nuns are to be visited by prelates.*

LXVIII

Since the sight of women is dangerous for spiritual men, let the one to whom the care of souls is entrusted take care that he never undertakes the visitation of nuns out of a desire to see them, but only for the sake of necessity; and once that necessity has been dealt with, let him depart quickly. Nor should he presume to enter the enclosures of the monastery unless great necessity or usefulness requires it.

*Concerning the appointment of a confessor for nuns.*

Let no confessor be appointed for nuns without the consent of at least one president. But if anyone, whether prelate or subject, shall presume to attempt otherwise, let him be punished most severely by the visitors. Entrance into the enclosures of nuns is to be entirely forbidden to the confessor.

*That monks are to be received without regard to persons or nobility.*

Furthermore, it is reported that there are some monasteries of either sex in our province in which only knights and nobles have customarily been received to profession. From this has come the complete dissolution and abandonment of regular observance, and the total ruin of those monasteries in spiritual and temporal matters. Let the visitors inquire into the reason for this custom; and if privileges are alleged, let them examine them diligently, so that, once the truth has been discovered, such an elevated abuse may be abolished, and henceforth persons otherwise suitable may make profession there indiscriminately according to the rule, without regard to nobility.

*No one from a reformed monastery is to be received elsewhere without letters from his abbot.*

We forbid anyone to receive in another monastery any monks, converts, or donates who have gone out from a reformed monastery, unless they have the permission of their abbot.

*No guest monk is to govern a parish.*

Let no guest monk be permitted to govern a parish cure.

*The incorrigible are to be referred to the presidents to be corrected.*

If any monks or religious are found to be such that they cannot be restrained by their own prelates and visitors, let such persons be reported to the presidents, so that they may make provision concerning them.

*Concerning the proclamation of the fathers in chapter.*

Because the fathers of the order ought, among other reasons, to come together and be gathered in the Lord for this purpose, that they may celebrate provincial chapters and clear themselves before the presidents concerning their various negligences and faults, the lord presidents decree and establish that henceforth, in the celebration of chapters of this kind, the individual fathers must proclaim themselves concerning their faults, excesses, and negligences with a faithful and humble mind, ready to receive the punishment of the presidents.

*Concerning supplying for the negligence of visitors.*

If any of the visitors, because of a legitimate impediment, is unable to fulfill the office assigned to him, let him signify this to the nearest president, who will supply his place or will certainly appoint some other one of the prelates to supply it.

*That the presidents and visitors should arrive at least four days before the day for celebrating chapter.*

We will that the visitors, together with the presidents, hasten to arrive at least four days before the day on which the chapter is to begin, in order to report to the presidents what they have discovered, done, and carried out in their visitations, so that the presidents themselves may be ready, before the assembled fathers, to correct the excesses of the rebellious and disobedient, or also to punish the negligence of those same visitors, if any has been found.

*The seventeenth provincial chapter, held at Nuremberg for the third time, in the year of the Lord 1459.*

In the year of the Lord 1459, on the Sunday Misericordia Domini, the seventeenth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of St. Giles at Nuremberg. In it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots George of St. Giles there, Berthold of St. Giles in Brunswick, Eberhard of St. James at Mainz, and Heinrich of Blessed Mary at Ettenmunster.
