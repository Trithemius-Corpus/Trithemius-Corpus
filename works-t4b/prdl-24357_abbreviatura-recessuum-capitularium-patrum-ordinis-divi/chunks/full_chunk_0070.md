LXXIII

In the year of the Lord 1479, in the twelfth indiction, on the Sunday Misericordia Domini, the twenty-fourth provincial chapter was held at Saint Giles in Nuremberg for the fifth time. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Martin in Schwarzach, Leonhard of Saint Peter in Castello, Bernhard in Hirsau, and Hermann of Saint James in Mainz.

*Which statutes of the preceding chapters were here repeated again.*

In this holy provincial chapter several statutes were repeated as though anew, although they are known to have been ordained earlier in other chapters of preceding times, with few exceptions, in almost the same words. Among these are the following. First, concerning apostolic indults surreptitiously obtained and not to be used, as above, chapter 20, at the end, but with this clause added: Matters that must be transacted or obtained in the Roman curia for the order, or for monasteries jointly or separately, by the order's proctor residing in the curia for the time being, or with his express consent, all the prelates and monks of our province and order shall cause to be transacted through him.

Likewise, the statute was repeated that those who are visited are to receive and treat their visitors kindly, and are to pay them three Rhenish florins for travel expenses, under the added penalty of triple payment; and that they are to provide them with safe-conduct. And certain other things were also repeated, which nevertheless, because of their penalties and certain particular provisions, we have decided to set out again. The first of these is this.

*That a monk is not to be received without letters commendatory from his abbot, under penalty of thirty florins.*

We also forbid more strictly, under penalty of thirty Rhenish florins, all prelates and superiors of our order and province henceforth to receive any brother or monk in their monasteries without letters commendatory from that monk's own abbot. Those already received otherwise are to dismiss as soon as possible, under the same penalty, to be paid to the chapter without contradiction as often as they act to the contrary.

*That a general license is not to be granted to anyone, under penalty of fifty florins.*

We also enjoin the same upon all prelates, that henceforth they are not to grant their monks and professed brethren a general license without the consent of those presiding in the provincial chapter, under penalty of fifty florins, to be paid to the same chapter as often as they transgress this our constitution.

*Concerning visitors.*

We also strictly enjoin all those subject to our chapter by law or custom not in any way to contrive, either by themselves or through others, with diocesan bishops, princes, or nobles, that the visitors may not freely carry out their office. Nor are they to object that visitors are not to be admitted without the consent of the ordinaries, since they are sent by apostolic authority and visit the monasteries assigned to them in the place of the lord pope, as is found in Extra, De statu monachorum, chapter In singulis, paragraph Ordinentur. Nor are any to impose conditions on the visitors by which they would admit them, or without which they would refuse to admit them, under penalty of thirty florins.

*Concerning visitation charters.*

Under the same penalty we command all the aforesaid abbots, provosts, priors, deans, monks, and communities to keep diligently, read, preserve, and put into practice the charters, ordinances, and statutes made, or to be made, for them by the visitors.

*That an abbot is not to grant property to a monk.*

Since private property is always ruinous to monks and the overthrow of all religion, we most strictly forbid all prelates to allow any of their own monks to possess property. For what the supreme pontiff admits that he does not dare to do is judged very rash for an inferior to presume in any way, as we clearly have in Extra, De statu monachorum, Cum ad monasterium, at the end.
