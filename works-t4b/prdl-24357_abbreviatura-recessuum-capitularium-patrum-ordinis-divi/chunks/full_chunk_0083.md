Nothing is to be preferred to this work; but as soon as the signal has been heard, leaving behind everything that was in their hands, all must hasten to it with due gravity, lest levity find fuel there, as above, chapters 1 and 3.

For this reason also the signal for Matins, which must be held at midnight, as above, chapter 1, and for Prime, and for Vespers, must be prolonged for at least a quarter of an hour, where above and in several of the following chapters. And lest, because of a shortage of persons, some might be able to excuse themselves for performing this worship less perfectly, it has been ordained above, chapter 3, that everywhere as many monks must be kept and received as can conveniently be supported from the goods of the monastery; and those negligent in these matters must be punished most severely.

The abbots and the others must also celebrate, confess, and communicate as is contained in the constitutions of Benedict XII concerning the celebration of Masses. And all who according to law and the Benedictine sanctions ought to have been constituted in orders must be promoted to orders within the year, as above, chapter 1, and in the following chapters.

This too must be considered by the visitors: whether there are any churches annexed to the monastery, and whether any Masses are to be performed within or outside the monastery; how many they are, how they were founded and received, how they are performed, and by whom. Likewise the same must be done concerning anniversaries and other foundations or votive actions. And finally concerning readings, manual labors, and also meditations, whether they are regularly applied to them.

Concerning Monasteries of Nuns. Rubric 13.

Those things which have been said concerning monasteries of men must also be observed in monasteries of nuns, insofar as they pertain to the articles relevant to them, as above, chapter 1 and following. Nor should secular girls who are not there for the sake of conversion be permitted there, as above, chapter 16. They must be under perpetual enclosure, as above, chapter 1, and as is contained in the constitution of Boniface which begins Periculoso, De statu monachorum, book 6. These women the visitors and others to whom care of them has been committed must never, out of a desire to see them,

Concerning the Method and Form of Reforming Monasteries, according to the tenor of the bull of lord Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal priest of the Roman church of the title of Saint Peter in Chains, and legate from the side for Germany. Rubric 14.

First, that the prelates of the monasteries to be visited be so instructed that, by fitting habit and tonsure, by uprightness of life, gravity of conduct, the word of teaching, and the example of holiness, they may preside laudably and fruitfully over their subjects. They are to correct and amend the excesses of individuals according to the Rule and the statutes of the order, without any dissimulation. They are to recall all their fugitive brothers and absent monks, within a term to be established for them by the visitors for this purpose, except those who preside over annexed parishes. And no one is to be permitted to use damnable private property; rather, all things are to be brought into common use.

Against the incontinent, both prelates and subjects, let stricter discipline and censures proceed, so that not only the vices of public incontinence may be punished worthily, but also every occasion from which suspicion of incontinence could arise may be removed from their midst.

The three substantial points of the Rule are especially to be preserved most completely by all, so that no dispensation or contrary custom may ever avail anyone against them. Silence is to be kept in the oratory, refectory, and cloister, etc. Likewise the brothers are to be refreshed under table reading, which is to continue until the end of the meal. The eating of meat is to be most strictly forbidden to all healthy persons in every place and time.
