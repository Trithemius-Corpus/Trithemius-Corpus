Those, however, which are made in individual churches, monasteries, or other places of the same order or religion shall be made by three upright and faithful monks of each church, monastery, or place in which, as stated above, they are made. Of these, the abbot or other prelate of that place shall appoint one, the prior or priors and administrators or officials another, and the chapter of the church or the convent of the monastery or place of this kind the third. The money collected shall be faithfully and diligently kept in one chest, locked with three different locks and keys, and shall be applied only to the use of those for whom the collections were made, and shall not be diverted to any other uses. For faithfully observing and fulfilling these things, the said three abbots shall take a bodily oath in the presence of those presiding, and the aforesaid monks in the presence of the abbot and chapter, and also of the convent of the church or monastery, or of the prelate of the other principal place where the said annual chapter shall have been held. Nor may another contribution be made so long as any sufficient amount of the first contribution happens to remain.

The aforesaid presiding officers, and each of them, who are for such provincial chapters, as well as the abbots and greater men or others already mentioned, shall have power, by the apostolic authority stated above, to demand, collect, and levy collections, subsidies, and contributions of this kind from all and each of the subject abbots, if they have or shall have any, and from priors, prelates, administrators, officials, and beneficiaries of every kind who shall be subject to that annual chapter, or to the church, monastery, or other principal place; and to compel all and each of the aforesaid persons, by the same authority, through ecclesiastical censure, without appeal, and by other remedies of law and of that order or religion, to pay in full the aforesaid collections, subsidies, and contributions, as shall have been ordained in such provincial and annual chapters, with deadlines assigned for this; and freely to publish and execute the aforesaid things, and processes and

sentences in cathedral churches, monasteries, and other places of the same order or religion, when and as often as it shall seem expedient to them; and also to absolve from the said sentences, after suitable satisfaction has first been made; and to relax and remove all processes and sentences of suspension and interdict; and to dispense with them concerning any irregularity which they, while thus bound, may have incurred by profaning divine services or otherwise involving themselves in them, provided this was not in contempt of the keys.

After these things have been read by the promoter of the order, other items from the constitutions of earlier chapters may also be read at the pleasure of the lords presiding. When all these have been completed, one of those presiding, beginning the provincial chapter, shall say: “Bless.” Let the Lord respond. And again let him add:

“Let us now speak. Behold, venerable fathers, from the sacred canons on the orders and from the constitutions of the supreme pontiffs, you have heard the authority of this sacred provincial chapter, to celebrate which God has gathered us together into one. Supported by this authority, we begin this provincial chapter itself in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.”

Then let him add:

“Therefore we first protest that we wish this place and the other places of this monastery necessary for us for celebrating this chapter of ours to be deputed, and we depute them, as the chapter-place. And we wish this protestation to be considered repeated as often as, being assembled capitularly in those places or in any of them, we shall ordain, establish, or discuss anything.”

Then the same presiding officer shall receive from the notary a manual promise concerning keeping the acts of the chapter secret and not revealing them publicly. And the promoter shall say:

“We protest further that, if there is anyone here who is excommunicated, suspended, or under interdict, we do not intend to celebrate such a chapter in his presence. Therefore, if anyone knows himself to be such, let him leave this chapter-place, and let him not presume to be present unless absolved.”
