Likewise, not only nobles are to be received, nor only Scots, but according to the rule one sort as well as another: the freeborn as well as one converted from servile status, the poor man who has nothing at all as well as the rich, powerful, and very well supplied man, as above, in the same chapter, and commonly in many others, as 16 and 17. Nor should anything be demanded from them, nor should any of them promise anything for his reception, whether through himself or through others, nor otherwise promise that he will give anything, except what, without agreement, he himself or his parents may wish or have wished by free will to offer at the altar, or otherwise to the monastery or to God. And those received in this way, when the year of probation has elapsed, are to make profession and promise stability according to the rule, as above, chapter 2. Without that probation, too, no one is to be received into obedientiaries: neither one recently coming from the world, nor one arriving from another monastery with letters or without letters, as above, chapters 8, 13, 15, etc.

Also, henceforth brothers should not be received without the permission of their superiors, nor be permitted to wander. If any have been received up to now, even by authority of the Apostolic See or with otherwise sufficient permission, they should nevertheless all be recalled within two months, namely, and instructed according to the statutes of our order, under penalty of excommunication and suspension, as above, chapters 1 and 2. Or they should even be sent back to their own monasteries, if they were received without sufficient permission and authority, under the same penalty, as above, chapter 8. Fugitive or wandering monks should not be received without letters of commendation from their abbots or from the nearer president; nor should they even be lodged except for three days, as above, chapters 8 and 12, under the same penalty. Likewise, no one should be received or permitted to remain in the province if he has laid, or had laid, violent hands either on a monk or on an abbot, as above, chapter 12, under the same penalty.

Likewise, concerning persons it must further be considered whether all are present; whether some are sick or in prison; whether some are fugitives and not recalled, or dismissed with general permissions, which henceforth should not be done; whether some are in priories

Concerning the discipline of persons dwelling within monasteries. Rubric 10.

The discipline of monks is to be weighed and considered in many respects, both according to the rule and also according to the statutes of the fathers. First, however, concerning the exterior things of a person, namely tonsure and clothing: by whom garments are supplied to the brothers; whether by a common wardrobe-keeper, or whether indeed they have them through friends or from some property of their own; and whether provision is made for each equally, or differently, without illness or another reasonable cause. Likewise, whether the garments are according to the statutes of the fathers: only such as are necessary, namely, and religious both in material and in form.

Second, concerning food and fasting: whether all, except the sick, are fed in the refectory without eating meat; whether there is a reader at table and ministers who, according to the rule, enter on Sunday with a blessing. For it should not be permitted that individual monks, or any of them, have special youths or servants as ministers, as it has been found to happen in certain monasteries. Nor should anyone obtain anything for himself through himself, through labors, through friends, or through any others whatever, or receive anything even not obtained in advance but offered freely without permission; rather, he should expect everything from the father of the monastery. And if the eating of meat is at times granted to some for cause, this should be done only on certain days and only in the infirmary, where no layperson should be admitted. But on the road, or outside the monastery, no healthy person should eat meat, just as neither should he within the monastery. Likewise, whether alms are given from what is surplus. And whether the ministers perform their washings on Saturday at the Mandatum.
