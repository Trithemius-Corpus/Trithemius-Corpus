The statute formerly issued in chapters 8 and 9, concerning the recalling of fugitives and not giving a general letter of license to anyone, is again renewed word for word, as above, chapter 15.

That procurators of absentees may take part in capitular deliberations.

It pleased the lord presidents and the chapter that monks who are procurators of absent fathers, having sufficient procuratorial authority, may take part in capitular deliberations, just as they have taken part up to now.

Likewise concerning procurators of absentees.

The procurator of absentees must be fully informed about the visitations of those sending him, if any visitors have been there, and must report all these things to the lord presidents; he must also take upon himself, in place of those sending him, the penances and humiliations, and within a space of two months send the recess of the chapter to those by whom he was sent. But if he does not do this and is found negligent in the aforesaid matters, if he is an abbot he shall be punished by a fine of two florins for each monastery; if he is a simple monk, by suspension from divine offices.

Concerning the propriety and quality of clothing.

We also ordain and enact that abbots and all religious, whatever offices they may have been assigned, both within and outside the monastery, shall wear a decent habit, or a rain-cloak over the scapulars, with cowls sewn on and with covered birettas; and that they go about also with a decent retinue, all ostentation set aside.

If an abbot, in excuse for himself, sends letters of princes to the chapter.

under penalty of three Rhenish florins, to be paid to the chapter without any remission.

What sort of person ought to be elected.

The lord presidents, together with the fathers, enacted concerning an abbot, provost, or prior that no one henceforth is to be elected or postulated as abbot, provost, or prior unless he has remained, without pretense, in observance and regular life for at least the space of a year, and has held and observed it. But if otherwise is done, there shall be done concerning such a man what the lord cardinal legate commanded by his letters to be done in such cases.

Whether a general letter of license should be granted to monks by their prelates.

We most strictly prohibit any prelate of our province or chapter henceforth from granting to his brothers, when they are being sent to any monastery whatever, a general letter of license; nor shall he permit them to use those already granted in this way, but rather, as quickly as possible, he shall prudently revoke what he inconsiderately granted to the peril of souls.

Concerning surreptitious apostolic indults.

We have also salutarily decreed that apostolic indults already obtained, or perhaps to be obtained in the future without the knowledge of the presidents, especially those that tend to the loss of observance and regular discipline, are henceforth not to be used, as being useless and surreptitious.

The twenty-first provincial chapter, held at Nuremberg for the fourth time, celebrated in the year of the Lord 1473.

In the year of the Lord 1473, in the sixth indiction, on Misericordia Domini Sunday, the twenty-first provincial chapter was held at Nuremberg, at St Egidius, for the fourth time. In it the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Theodericus of Bursfelde, Gunther of St Peter in Erfurt, Johannes of St Egidius in Nuremberg, and Johannes of Holy Cross in Werdea, presided.

Which statutes of the old chapters are repeated here.

First, the lord presidents and fathers commanded that all statutes of the preceding chapters, as though they were inserted here word for word, be kept inviolably by all those subject to the chapter.
