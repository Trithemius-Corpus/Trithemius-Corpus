If, however, chambers are found in the dormitory, we order that they be destroyed by the visitors or by their command, or suitably barred off.

Concerning silence in four places. Article 12.

Silence shall be observed in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and cloister. And after Compline no one may say anything, except as is contained in the Rule. And before the said Compline, let there be read the Collation of the Fathers or some other edifying work.

Concerning the porter of the monastery. Article 13.

At the gate of each monastery let one porter or doorkeeper, provident and discreet, be appointed, so that without the permission of the abbot or of another president no one may be granted freedom to leave that monastery. But if anyone goes out without such permission, let him be subjected to fitting and due penalties, as is contained in the constitutions of Benedict XII concerning permission to go outside the monastery.

Furthermore, he shall be attentive that he in no way permit women to enter the monastery.

That no one from the order of mendicants be received into our order. Article 14.

Moreover, since we have heard that certain abbots and other presidents in monasteries have received mendicant religious as monks without the permission of their own superiors, and afterward, what is worse, permit them to wander about to the detriment of their souls and the scandal of religion, we forbid them to presume such things henceforth. And those whom they have already received, unless by authority and mandate of the Apostolic See or by sufficient permission, they shall be bound to recall as quickly as they can, together with others wandering outside the monastery, or at least within a year, to the monastery; otherwise they shall be punished by the visitors of the provincial chapter with grave penalties.

Concerning nuns. Article 15.

But the things that have been said concerning monasteries of men we wish also to be observed in monasteries of nuns, as regards the articles applicable to them. Wishing nevertheless, and ordaining,

that they be kept under due and perpetual enclosure, as is contained in the constitution of Boniface that begins Periculoso, concerning the state of monks, and as this sacred Council of Basel commands to be observed, we instruct the visitors of the chapter, in virtue of holy obedience, to apply diligence in this matter.

Concerning the master of novices. Article 16.

Likewise we ordain that abbots and other presidents in their monasteries shall have suitable and sufficient masters, who shall introduce their novices to the elementary branches of learning; and afterward, once they have been instructed, shall suitably send them, according to the form of the statutes of the aforesaid Benedict XII, to the general studies, assigning them a pension.

Concerning the number of monks. Article 17.

We wish and ordain that in every monastery where the resources and revenues suffice, the number of monks established from the foundation shall be maintained; or, if they do not suffice, let as many be maintained as can conveniently be supported. Thus, under pretext of any privilege, pact, transaction, or custom which is called corruption, no one shall be excused, especially since we reject that corrupt practice by which some say that they can receive no one unless he is noble. For although nobles, other things being equal, are preferred in reception, nevertheless others are not excluded on this account, nor ought they to be excluded.

That the goods of monasteries are not to be divided, but provision is to be made for all in common. Article 18.

Since all the goods of a monastery are ordained for the support of the monks and for bearing other necessities, from which provision must be made for each according to the requirement of all, as is contained in the Rule, chapter 33, it is utterly absurd that abbots and officials should belch forth superfluity from the revenues of the monastery, while poor cloistered monks lack clothing, food, or other necessities, especially in bodily sickness, so that they are often compelled to turn back to the assistance of their parents.
