Concerning proctors. Rubric 5.

There are two kinds of proctors. Some are general, belonging to the whole chapter; it is their duty to see to it that the visitors carry out the office entrusted to them energetically and faithfully, indeed to compel them by censures if need be, and to report their negligence to those presiding, as above, chapter 13 and certain following chapters.

Others are special proctors, who are substituted for certain abbots unable to come to the chapter. Enough has been said above, in chapter 20 and in very many other places, such as chapters 4, 6, 11, 13, 17, and 18, about these proctors and the abbots substituting them: namely, how one proctor is not to undertake the cases of more than three abbots, except for the cases there expressed; and that they are not to be admitted unless an effective bodily oath has first been taken; and likewise many other matters that can be seen there. A form of procuration, for the sake of those ignorant of it, has also been inserted in the recess of the nineteenth chapter. It is as follows.

To the reverend fathers in Christ, the lord presidents of the sacred provincial chapter of the province of Mainz and diocese of Bamberg, of the black monks of the Order of Saint Benedict, to be held in the monastery N. of the diocese N. on the coming Sunday Iubilate, N., humble abbot of the monastery N. of the Order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz, offers whatever reverence and honor he can.

Reverend fathers and lords, since according to the praiseworthy institutes of the fathers a provincial chapter is coming to be held, to which I both ought and am bound to come in person, but because I am so hindered by such a cause that I cannot conveniently appear in person, therefore I constitute the reverend lord abbot N. as my envoy and true proctor, in the best form, with full mandate in my stead in the said provincial chapter,

Concerning visitors. Rubric 6.

Visitors of monasteries and visitors of visitors must be elected by those presiding in the provincial chapter, as above, chapter 16, and only from reformed houses, as above, chapter 13. They are bound to know each and every thing that has been done and ordained in every chapter from the first to the last, so that, by first observing most strictly in themselves the law that they wish and ought to impose on others, they may at length require it more effectively from others and bind them to it, and may be able and empowered to punish transgressors, as they ought, with fitting vengeance, under the strictest account to be rendered in the dread judgment to come.

After they have come from the chapter, these visitors must begin their visitations within a year, under penalty of thirty florins, as in chapter 4, or of three florins for each monastery to be visited, as in chapter 20, indeed also under sentence of excommunication to be imposed, as in chapter 8, unless they are excused by reason of hostility, illness, or some other legitimate cause, as in chapter 13. And they must faithfully complete the work under virtue of holy obedience, keeping God before their eyes, to whom they will render a strict account, so that before they leave the monasteries they effectively reform, as far as lies in them, whatever is in those houses, according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the canonical sanctions, and the statutes of the provincial chapter and of the order, especially in the three substantial matters, which are obedience, continence, and poverty. And where they do not find disciplines and ceremonies [unclear].
