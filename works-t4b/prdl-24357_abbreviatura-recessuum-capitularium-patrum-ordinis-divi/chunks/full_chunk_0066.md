On the preacher.

The one who is to preach the sermon should arrive before the presidents somewhat before the appointed day, and should submit the sermon itself, in the form in which it is to be delivered, in good time to the correction or alteration of the presidents.

If any of the aforesaid presidents is absent at this arrival.

If it should happen that any of the presidents is absent for any reason, the others shall treat and dispose all matters that are appropriate, with the power of summoning one of the fathers present if need be, until the one arrives who ought to have been present for this preliminary meeting.

On the death of a president.

Concerning the death of any president, or any other kind of deficiency, the bull of Pope Martin V, which is set down above at the beginning of this volume, is to be observed, with this declaration added in this chapter: that the authority of the former presidents lasts until the day of the opening of the chapter, exclusive; and on that day the authority of the new presidents begins.

That schoolboys should not take part in the monks’ choirs by singing.

Since the company of schoolboys and secular clerics in the divine offices forces corruption of maturity upon the minds of religious, the lord presidents decreed that those schoolboys and clerics are to be dismissed to their parishes. But if in some place they cannot be dismissed without notable scandal, the visitors of those monasteries, after hearing the reasons, shall decide how long and in what manner they ought to be tolerated.

In it it was ordained that serious resistance should be made to those who wish to transfer themselves from the order to the secular state or to the state of secular canons. And against them procurators are to be appointed in the Roman curia.

In difficult and important cases we will and ordain that first the fathers be consulted.

We will and ordain that, in important and difficult cases of the chapter and the order, henceforth during the time of the chapter itself the votes of each of the fathers present be examined in order; and each of them, according to God and his own conscience, shall speak with humility and reverence what he has judged better, when he has been asked.

On the multiplication of constitutions.

Considering that in the earlier chapters many useful statutes were made, from which little profit has so far been found to have followed, because of the negligence of very many, since it is superfluous to establish laws however celebrated if they are not duly entrusted to execution, and lest the multitude of statutes increase the material of transgression, we do not now wish to enact new ones, but we command each person to observe the old constitutions more diligently than hitherto.

The nineteenth provincial chapter, celebrated at Michelsberg for the first time, in the year of the Lord 1467.

In the year of the Lord 1467, in the fifteenth indiction, on the nineteenth day of the month of April, which was Jubilate Sunday, the nineteenth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Michelsberg at Bamberg. In it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Johannes of Bursfelde, Melchior of Augsburg, Heinrich of St. Michael in Hildesheim, and Johannes in Werden.
