LXI

In the year of the Lord 1435, in the tenth indiction, in the fifth year of the pontificate of lord Pope Eugenius IV, on the twenty-sixth day of the month of June, which was the Sunday after the Nativity of blessed John the Baptist, the ninth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of the Friars Preachers in Basel, for at that time the general council was being held there. Presiding in it were the venerable fathers and lords, the abbots Ortwin of St. Peter’s at Erfurt, Fridericus of St. Mark’s in Reichenau, Iohannes of St. George’s in Stein, and Heinricus in Faucibus, of the dioceses of Mainz, Constance, and Augsburg.

On the statutes of the earlier chapters.

First, we approve and commend the statutes of the other chapters, and we wish them, as expressed here, to be observed by all.

The statutes of the preceding chapters are repeated here.

In this chapter of the fathers, nearly all the statutes issued in the three preceding chapters were again repeated word for word: first, namely, the statute on those coming to chapter with communal life, and on the number of courses, in the same place, chapters 6, 7, and 8, as it has been set out above exactly. And there it was ordained that the treasurers themselves must provide for the refreshment during the three days of the provincial chapter. Likewise, on the power of the presiding lords, that each of them should have authority jointly and severally to proceed against rebels. On the visitors also, under penalty of excommunication imposed on them, that they faithfully complete their office within the year and make a report as soon as possible to one of the nearer presidents. And that they first visit and reform themselves and their own, and only then proceed to the reform of others. Likewise, that henceforth they are not to receive mendicants into our order, under penalty of excommunication, as above. Under the same penalty it was decreed, as above, that abbots are to seek out their fugitives and are not to give anyone permission to dwell or rule in parishes unless as is permitted above, chapters 7 and 8. Likewise, the statute was repeated as above concerning fugitive monks not to be received to obedience without letters of recommendation. All these things, which I have briefly reviewed, as they stand above in their form word for word, were again issued and enacted as though new.

On the penalty for the contumacious.

It was further decreed that the presidents, and each of them, should compel abbots and others who have not come to chapter in person nor sent lawful procurators with a sufficient mandate to pay double, and also to pay the contributions imposed by the chapter, by ecclesiastical censures according to constitution 12, with appeal removed.

We also command all visitors appointed and to be appointed that, in each monastery which they have visited, they leave a recess or charter of visitation.

On the observance of precepts.

We command all and each of the statutes and mandates written above to be inviolably observed by all, while preserving nevertheless whatever is to be added, decreed, or changed in the holy Council of Basel for the reform of our said order. In the matter of this reform, twelve prelates of the aforesaid order from the said province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg have already been appointed and elected in the said holy council.

The tenth provincial chapter was held in Nuremberg, in which the bull of the Council of Basel was accepted, in the year of the Lord 1439.

In the year of the Lord 1439, in the second indiction, in the ninth year of the pontificate of lord Pope Eugenius IV, on the twenty-sixth day of the month of April, which was Jubilate Sunday, the tenth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of St. Giles at Nuremberg, of the same order. Presiding in it were the venerable fathers and lords, the abbots Hartung of St. Peter’s at Erfurt, Georgius of St. Giles at Nuremberg, Nicolaus of St. Blaise, and Heinricus in Amerbach. In this chapter the bull of the Council of Basel concerning the reform of our order through the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg was presented and accepted.
