Concerning monks living in common and sleeping in one place.

We will that the brothers live in common according to the Rule, and likewise eat together in one refectory, in which no one at all shall eat meat. They shall maintain an infirmary according to the Rule and the abbot's disposition. All private little houses, moreover, shall be destroyed at the abbot's command, or assigned to some offices for the utility of the monastery. All shall sleep in one dormitory, in screened chambers, each in separate beds, according to the abbot's disposition.

Concerning the other statutes of the preceding chapters repeated in this chapter.

Furthermore, the constitutions and statutes decreed in the previous chapters were repeated precisely, word for word, in this one, as they are set forth above: namely, concerning the divine office; concerning the ringing for matins, prime, and vespers; likewise, that monasteries and conventual places that have fallen down and been destroyed be restored; and that a prudent old man be placed at the gate of each monastery. Likewise, that abbots and the other officials of monasteries make to the visitors a full and faithful accounting of receipts and expenditures. Likewise, that silence be kept most strictly in the four conventual places belonging to them. And that, concerning loans, leases, spoils, fiefs, and those to be sent to studies, and also concerning the preservation of the Eucharist and relics, the Benedictine constitution be observed. And that each abbot publish all the statutes of the present chapter to his subjects and cause them to be observed, and the other matters which are contained above in the first chapter.

The matters stated concerning nuns.

What has been said concerning monasteries of men we will also to be understood concerning monasteries of nuns, so far as concerns the articles applicable to them; and we command that they also remain under perpetual enclosure.

of the mandates.

We will, ordain, and command that all these our mandates written above be observed inviolably by all who are subject to our chapter, as each matter concerns each person. Nor by this do we restrict the statutes of our order or of the preceding chapters; rather, we will that they likewise be diligently and firmly fulfilled and observed by all, without any contradiction. Therefore let the visitors provide that all and each of the things mentioned above be diligently observed, as they are bound to render account on the day of their death and of the last judgment.

The fourth provincial chapter was celebrated in Selgenstadt in the year of the Lord 1422.

In the year of the Lord 1422, in the fifteenth indiction, on the third day of the month of May, in the fifth year of Pope Martin V, the fourth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter, martyrs, in Selgenstadt. In it sat the venerable fathers Albertus Hirsfeldensis, Georgius Castellensis, and Theodericus Amerbacensis, abbots of monasteries.

Concerning the statutes of this chapter.

Before reviewing the statutes issued in this sacred chapter, it is fitting briefly to set first those things decreed in previous chapters which were inserted here word for word by way of review. For the repetition of statutes inserted so often serves this purpose: that the more chapters in which something has been decreed, the greater authority it has.

First, this sacred assembly of fathers repeated the statute of the first chapter concerning deceased presidents: that one who succeeds someone in an abbacy also succeeds him in the presidency.

Likewise, that the visitors shall not bring more than six mounts with them, under the penalty contained above.

Likewise, concerning the ringing for matins, prime, and vespers, to be done for the space of one quarter of an hour, as above, c. 1.

And that the chapter of faults be kept daily, as in the same place.
