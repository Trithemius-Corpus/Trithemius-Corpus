They should not readily believe, even if belief is required; yet those things whose intention is unknown should be interpreted in the better sense, since we ought to presume good rather than evil. *De regulis iuris*, c. *Esto te*. And in such matters the intention of the one giving testimony is to be weighed rather than the words. For if someone sets forth true things from an evil intention, the intention makes the good act evil. 23, q. 5, *Cum minister*.

Let the visitors be cautious, and especially discreet, in examining the excesses of prelates, who, since they have to correct the sins of others, frequently incur the hatred of many and suffer plots. Extra, *De accusationibus*, c. *Qualiter*, II. In all things let them always act so that they do not lose the form of charity. For correction must be made according to each person’s rank and with the peace of the Church preserved. 23, q. 4, *Ecce*, and c. *Quisquis*, c. *Quidam*, c. *Cum quisque*, c. *Ipsa pietas*, c. *Quid faciet circa me*.

Likewise, correction must be made with the intention of healing, not of raging. 86 dist., *Odio*; 23, q. 4, *Ita plane circa me*. And not from hatred but from charity. Extra, *De accusationibus*, c. *Qualiter*, II; 24, q. 3, *Corripiantur*. And sometimes it should have more gentleness than severity. 45 dist., *Qui sincera*, and c. *Licet*, because one gently chastised shows reverence for the one who chastises. c. *Cum beatus Paulus*; 12, q. 2, *Fraternitas*; 24, q. 3, *Corripiantur*.

These and other testimonies of discretion, the mother of virtues, the visitors ought not to omit, so that they may proceed worthily and justly.

The Form of the Oath Which Visitors Once Used to Present to the Presidents. Chapter 6.

Formerly the presidents, not only in Mainz but also in other provinces, used to require from the visitors an oath concerning the diligent fulfillment of their office. In our times, however, this is not done, nor do I judge it useful or necessary, because a man whom the spur of his own conscience does not drive to act diligently is scarcely, or only rarely, compelled by another’s command. Yet I wished to insert here the form of the oath, so that when the oath is read, what is to be done may be considered.

The form of such an oath, which I found in old books, follows thus:

I, Brother N., abbot of such-and-such monastery, appointed as visitor of the diocese of N. with respect to the monasteries of our order by the reverend fathers in Christ, the lord presidents of the provincial chapter recently celebrated in the monastery of N., swear by these holy Gospels of God that I will carry out the office of visitation entrusted to me faithfully and diligently, according to my powers, in accordance with the Rule of Saint Benedict and the decrees of the holy fathers, setting aside love, hatred, favor, and every other disordered affection. So help me God and these holy Gospels. Those absent used to give this oath; those present, however, swore in chapter before the lord presidents. But neither now seems necessary.

On the Power and Authority of Visitors. Chapter 7.

They have it from the apostolic see, as in Extra, *De statu monachorum*, c. *In singulis*, where it is said: “Let religious and circumspect persons also be appointed in the same chapter, who, according to the form prescribed for them, shall take care to visit, in our stead, the several abbeys of that kingdom or province, not only of monks but also of nuns.” “In our stead,” it says, so that, when the visitor’s authority is considered, none of those to be visited may dare to rebel. Their authority is more fully noted by the Abbot on the same chapter and the final chapter of *De statu monachorum*.

Yet their power is limited, as in the same c. *In singulis*, because they cannot depose the rector of a place from his office without a special mandate. Therefore, if they see that he must be removed altogether, let them report this to his own bishop, so that he may see to removing him; but if he does not do so, let the visitors refer this to the judgment of the apostolic see. Those exempt, however, are to be reported directly to the lord pope. The visitors should also have monks corrected by the abbot of the place, as in the same title, c. *Ea quae*. But those whom they find contumacious and rebellious, according to the measure of the fault, let them strike with regular censure in the place of the lord pope, without [unclear].
