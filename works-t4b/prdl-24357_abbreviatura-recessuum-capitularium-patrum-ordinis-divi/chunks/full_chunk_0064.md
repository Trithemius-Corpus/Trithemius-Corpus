LXVII

The sixteenth provincial chapter, held at Erfurt for the third time, in the year of the Lord 1456.

In the year of the Lord 1456, on Jubilate Sunday, the sixteenth chapter was held at Saint Peter's in Erfurt. At it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Berthold of Saint Stephen's at Wuerzburg, John of Saint Michael's at Hildesheim, Ulrich in Blaubeuren, and Rupert of Saint James's at Mainz.

The election of future presidents and visitors belongs to the presidents.

We decree and declare that the election of presidents and visitors succeeding for the time belongs to the presidents, just as constitution 12 of Pope Benedict defines.

That secular persons are not to be allowed to be present at capitular acts.

We will that henceforth no secular person be admitted to capitular deliberations and proceedings; but when some act must be drawn up in public form, then notaries and witnesses who are honorable and worthy of trust are to be called in.

Concerning conformity of habit and tonsure among those coming to the chapter.

We also will and ordain that henceforth the prelates coming to the chapter, and the proctors of absent prelates, should strive for conformity of conduct, especially in habit and tonsure. And those who use birettas are not to wear them bare and openly with the hood of the cowl lowered, as some are accustomed to do, but are to place the hood of the cowl itself under the biretta for the sake of conformity, since this too seems to be a sign of humility. We will this to be firmly observed henceforth in every place, especially by the simple conventual brothers of our order, except during the divine offices.

That the names of abbots and proctors present in the chapter be inserted in the recess regularly.

We have also decreed that henceforth, in the recess of the chapter held for the time, the names of all fathers present and of the proctors of the absent be written down individually.

Concerning not requiring an oath in the act of visitation.

The visitors of the provincial chapter, in the act of their visitation, are not indiscreetly to constrain either prelates or subjects, by oath or otherwise, to disclose or publish hidden matters, especially criminal ones, unless the order of fraternal charity has been observed.

That provostries and priories be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter.

Some abbots who have provostries, priories, and deaneries subject to themselves or to their monasteries have, as we have learned, hitherto been negligent in visiting them. Therefore we decree that these same provostries, priories, and deaneries are henceforth to be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter. The abbots, however, to whom they are subject may, if they wish, be present at such visitation. And those so visited are bound for the time to pay only half the contribution.

Monks and nuns ought not to give themselves to disorderly feasting.

It has become known to us that among certain persons of our order there is an abuse of holding in turn, after dinner and also after supper, disorderly and excessive feasts and drinking bouts, mixing with these vain and scurrilous conversations. This abuse we abolish by the present statute, and we prohibit it from being done henceforth, commanding that those who are found transgressors in these matters be duly punished by the visitors with abstinence on bread and water, or otherwise according to the nature of the excess.

And because the aforesaid abuse is said to be fostered by another abuse, altogether more detestable, namely that in certain monasteries they usurp special revenues and have them distributed not through the cellarer, but through whomever they themselves wish, we likewise will that this abuse be wholly abolished, and that all things be brought into common use, and that through the cellarer the necessities be supplied to all according to the Rule.

Concerning the visitations of monasteries of nuns.

The monasteries of nuns of our holy order are to be visited by the visitors of the provincial chapter. These visitors, among other things, while consulting the interests of the same monasteries, should also strive to exclude from them secular girls who are there not for the sake of conversion, but at the request of their parents.
