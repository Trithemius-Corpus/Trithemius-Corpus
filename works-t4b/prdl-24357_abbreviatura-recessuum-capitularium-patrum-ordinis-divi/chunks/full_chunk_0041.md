Concerning the enclosure of monasteries.

They are to apply careful and due concern for the enclosure of the monasteries themselves; nor is it permitted for any religious, except certain officials, to go beyond the bounds of the immunity of his cloister without the special permission of his superior. Let all observe the fasts appointed both by the Church and by the rule.

Concerning the divine office.

Monks are to perform the divine office, by day and by night alike, reverently, as is fitting, and distinctly, at the proper hours. When it has been completed, they are to devote themselves to reading or to manual labors, according to the demands of the time and of their duties and under the direction of the rule of blessed Benedict. Books are to be distributed to the brothers at the appointed time. The beds of the brothers are to be inspected from time to time because of particular work.

Concerning holding the daily chapter of faults.

A chapter of faults is to be held daily; in it, after the readings of the martyrology and the rule have first been completed, they are to proceed to the exposition of the rule and to the examination and correction of faults.

Concerning the celebration of masses, confession, and communion of monks.

Let the abbot celebrate or hear mass every day. Monks who are priests and reside in monasteries are to prepare themselves to celebrate at least once each week. Those below the priesthood are to confess at least once each week and receive communion on the first Sunday of each month.

How the brothers are to provide for the sick and for guests.

They are to provide suitably for monks in food, drink, clothing, and other necessities, according to the determination of the rule. Suitable places are also to be assigned in the monasteries themselves, both for the sick and for guests arriving for a time; care and humanity are to be shown to them according to the disposition of the same rule. They are to bring guests to prayer, and a sacred reading is to be read to them for edification, and they are to wash their feet.

With due moderation, let the highest care be taken that the monasteries not be burdened with unbearable loads of debts; that their goods not be scattered or alienated uselessly and without cause; and that any alienated goods, if there are any, be diligently recovered.

Concerning the preservation of goods and privileges.

Abbots, presidents, or rectors are diligently to defend and preserve the rights of their monasteries. The fruits, revenues, and income of the same monasteries are to be converted to common uses and benefits. Books, vestments, crosses, chalices, and other vessels and ornaments of the treasury, privileges, rights, letters, and the common seals of the convent are to be kept under lock and in safe places.

Rectors of monasteries are ordered to render accounts to their visitors every year.

Prelates or rectors are to give full certification to their visitors, by whom they have been visited, concerning the state of their temporal affairs. But the prelates themselves and each of their officials are also to render a lawful account of their administrations to the convents every year. Abbots are to abstain altogether from superfluous horses, hounds, and birds, from hounds and hunting birds, and from other animals and beasts by which the inner man is defiled; both they themselves and their religious and household are to do so.

It is not permitted for monks to have weapons within the precincts of the monastery, nor for any religious to become co-fathers.

No religious person within the precincts of the monastery is to presume to have weapons, or to contract any co-fatherhood or co-motherhood in any way.

Concerning nuns and their enclosure.

Nevertheless, we will that the foregoing, insofar as it can concern abbesses and nuns, be extended to their monasteries that stand within the limits of your visitation, with perpetual enclosure to be established by you with apostolic authority, according to the decree Periculoso, book 6.
