And you, lord scribe, I duly and urgently require to record this act and those that follow with diligence, and to mark it among the acts of the chapter, with trustworthy witnesses brought in for these matters.

Immediately the notary shall require witnesses, two or three of whom are to be present at all acts, except at the final proclamation of the fathers. Likewise, the promoter of the order, with all hearing, by the authority of the lords president, shall say by way of mandate to all and singular, both those now present here and those arriving at whatever hour, in virtue of holy obedience, that no one in any way reveal the secrets of this provincial chapter to any secular person or to any person not subject to our aforesaid chapter, to the detriment, disgrace, or burden of the order and of its professed members.

By the same authority, too, it is commanded to all and singular that no one presume to speak in the chapter places and proceedings before the lords president or the officials of the chapter, appointed or to be appointed, unless license has been requested and obtained, and this while standing and with head uncovered, with humility, reverence, and submission. Or, if he wishes, let him present it in writing.

Likewise, by the same authority, it is enjoined on all and singular who are bound to attend this chapter that no one withdraw from the place of the chapter without license, but that he remain until its end, under the penalty for contumacy and for those not coming to the chapter.

Then the register of the monasteries shall be read by the promoter or the notary, and the absentees shall be noted with the pen by one of them, as is customary. When these have been noted, the promoter shall accuse the contumacy of the absentees in these or similar words, saying:

Reverend fathers, lords president, I accuse the contumacy of the absent prelates, who are bound by law to attend this sacred provincial chapter; and I request that a monition be decreed, to be executed on the doors of this monastery against those same contumacious persons, and against rebels toward the visitors, so that they may appear to show that they have obeyed the mandates of the chapter and have actually presented themselves to it; otherwise, that they may see themselves declared to have incurred the penalties written below.

When he has said this, let one of the presidents see to it.

We, the presidents, wishing to act more graciously and not to condemn anyone too quickly, shall await the absentees until the following day, and longer if need be; so that meanwhile the procuratorial mandates of the absentees may also be examined, to determine whether their absence ought deservedly to be excused, and whether their procurators themselves ought to be reckoned present. Moreover, we decree a monition against rebels.

After these things, if there is time, let them choose definitors from among the abbots, who can give counsel and help to the presidents in the business of the chapter. Likewise, let three or four from among the same abbots be deputed to examine the procuratorial mandates of the absentees after dinner, or at a set time, in the place deputed to which the procurators are to come. This proviso is added: that if any procuratorial mandates are found ambiguous, they shall refer their examination to the lords president. The form of a sufficient procuratorial mandate is set out in the acts of the chapter.

Then the president may admonish the visitors beforehand that, at the set time or hour which they shall have determined, they are to be ready to present to the lords president the accounts and summaries of their visitations, according to the constitutions of the preceding fathers.

And if the hour is late and nothing difficult now remains, they shall be licensed to go to table, one of the presidents saying in these or similar words:

Venerable fathers, since the hour has already grown rather late and it is time for dining, lest we delay you, weary as you are, any longer from refreshment, we now wish to put an end to these proceedings. Therefore let each man dispose himself for table according to the promoter’s ordering, with discipline; in silence and with thanksgiving he shall eat what is set before him.

The promoter of the order shall seat the fathers in the refectory according to the time of each man’s prelacy, so far as this can be done. First, indeed, the abbots shall sit, then the priors and provosts, after that the procurators of the absentees, and afterward the other simple brethren. Yet this order of sitting can rarely be arranged in this way, on account of the multitude of those sitting and each individual [unclear].
