And he should also command, under penalties and censures if need be, in part or in whole, concerning the observance of the matters inserted. At the end of the charter, moreover, he should command how often in the year it is to be read in the convent, and he should insert the condition of the monasteries in respect to the number of persons and the amount of revenues, or also of debts. Let the charter be sealed with the two seals of the visitors themselves, as testimony for each. And it should be noted that the abbot is bound to make an accounting to his visitors concerning all revenues, according to the statutes of the fathers, as in chapters 1 and 2, and as was established in many other places.

The visitors should have a copy of the charter transcribed by themselves or by the monks visited, not by a layman, who ought not to know the defects of the monks. And they should carry this copy with them, to be sent to the chapter. Let the visitors carefully take heed not to hand over to the abbot the notes of the depositions, lest an occasion for ill-will be given, however zealous he may be; but let them carry them with them, to be revealed to the presiding lords, or certainly, if they do not need them, let them burn them in the fire.

Concerning what should be received from those visited for travel expenses. Chapter 28.

Every abbot and convent visited is bound, according to the decrees of the fathers, to pay his visitors, when they visit, three Rhenish florins for travel expenses, without any contradiction, as in chapter 11 and the three immediately following. Hence in the same place it says as follows: The whole community of the fathers decreed that every abbot visited should be bound to pay the visitors three Rhenish florins for travel expenses, unless the visitors themselves, for some good consideration, wish to act more moderately. Thus there. But let the visitors see that they demand necessities in such a way that they do not hide greed under the appearance of need. For a procuration of this kind ought not to be burdensome, but moderate: 10, q. 3, Cavendum, and 2, c. se. But if they do not visit, they ought to demand nothing, 10, q. 1, Relata.

IVX

That visitors or their attendants should dare to accept any gift or money, offered in any way, from those visited. Chapter 29.

Because gifts make the giver and the receiver turn aside from justice, 11, q. 3, Pauper, and c. Qui rector, it is strictly forbidden to all visitors to accept any gift from those visited, or money offered in any way by anyone, whether by themselves or through their attendants. As is said in Constitution Benedictus XV, chapter De visitatoribus, where it has as follows: Visitors and their attendants should receive no money on an occasion of this kind or in any other way; nor should they themselves, or any one of their people, presume to receive any gift, whatever or of whatever sort it may be, from anyone who offers it, so that they may not seem to seek the things that are their own, but those of Jesus Christ. Hence we establish and ordain that all and each of the visitors of this kind, or their attendants, who, besides the expenses to be provided to them only in food, presume to demand or receive money, even from one willing to give it, or who themselves, or any one of their attendants, presume in any way to receive such gifts, shall be bound within a month to restore twice the amount they received to the church or monastery or other place of that same order or religion from which it was received. Otherwise from that time onward let such visitors, who delay beyond this period in restoring that double amount to them, know that they are suspended from office and benefice until they have made full satisfaction for such double payment to the churches, monasteries, or places thus burdened; no remission, liberality, or grace on the part of those who gave it being of any force for them in this matter. And nevertheless, if it is expedient, let the visitors thus suspended be compelled by the presidents to restore the aforesaid double amount, by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure and by other appropriate remedies, with the obstacle of appeal removed. Thus there.

Therefore let the visitors forbid their chaplains and attendants to accept anything from anyone, on account of visiting, during the time of visitation.
