And he may add the general depositions or matters seen, for admonition and correction. Then let the conversi, novices, and subdeacons, that is, those not yet constituted in sacred orders, be called to the mat, one after another, or two by two, or certainly those concerning whom such a deposition has been made that they ought deservedly to be proclaimed.

When the one called comes to the mat, let him bow moderately; and let the visitor tell him his faults. He should at once lie prostrate on the ground until he has been ordered to rise.

When those who are not members of chapter have been dealt with and have gone out, let the abbot first be called to the mat, then the prior and the others in order. But while the abbot stands in judgment before the visitors, all the other brothers, out of reverence, ought to stand uncovered until he has again been ordered to sit. And note that the abbot’s faults are to be stated here briefly, and the lesser ones, lest an occasion for contempt be given to the weak. But in private, or before a few of the seniors, let them tell him all necessary things, great together with small, etc.

But they shall impose penances at the discretion of the visitors, with discernment, according to the custom of the daily proclamation. But if anyone there comes to be punished more severely, let the visitors have this done through the abbot, as in Extra, De statu monachorum, Ea quae.

On Making the Final Exhortation. Chapter 25.

After all who are to be proclaimed have been proclaimed, and penance has been enjoined on each, let the visitor make a brief exhortation concerning regular observance, rebuking the general negligences of the brothers; and let him declare how such things are contrary to the rule and harmful to the soul, etc. And the rest in this manner, as seems expedient to them. On the Customary Absolution. Chapter 26.

When these things have been finished according to custom, let all humble themselves for absolution, bending their knees, and let them say, Confiteor, concluding: Pray for me. Let the visitor, standing, say:

May almighty God have mercy on you, through the intercession of blessed Mary and all the saints, and may he forgive you all your sins and lead you to eternal life. Amen.

By the authority of almighty God, and of the ecclesiastical keys granted in this matter to our order, we absolve you from ecclesiastical censures; and we restore you to the communion of the Church and to participation; and we dispense with you and rehabilitate you for the execution of the things to be done. In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. And let some psalm to be said be enjoined on each; for conversi, the Lord’s Prayer twice or three times.

When these things have been completed, the visitors may excuse their insufficiency before those visited, and commend themselves to their prayers, as is often done in these or similar words:

Most beloved brothers, we ask you to receive charitably those things that we have said or done here to all, or also in private to individuals; and do not think anything else of us than that we have done all things in charity. Ah, also, I beg you, pardon me for the things said by me; and if I have offended anyone with words, or if I did not speak as I ought, or otherwise than I ought, ascribe it to my lack of skill, not to my will, etc., and such things. And let him add, if there is time: Our help is in the name of the Lord, who made heaven and earth.

On Making the Charter of Visitation. Chapter 27.

According to the statutes of the fathers, when the visitation has been completed, the state of the monks visited must be set in order in a charter, in which the necessary articles are to be inserted, in which each one ought to amend himself: first concerning the abbot, then concerning the prior.
