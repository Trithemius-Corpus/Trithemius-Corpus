Concerning the consent of all. Concerning the old statutes. C. 9, at the end. With the consent of all the fathers residing in chapter, we approve and renew all and singular the decrees and statutes issued in the provincial chapters held up to now, insofar as they are not contrary to the reform.

Concerning the authority of the presidents. We wish that each of the presidents, severally, shall have full and complete power and authority to carry out execution and to proceed, according to the form and tenor of the constitutions of Lord Pope Benedict XII and the disposition of the common law, against all and singular persons of either sex who have presumed to oppose the visitors, or have not admitted them for visitation, or have hindered them in any way, or have been found contrary to the concluded reform, and who have harmed those monasteries in goods or in persons.

Let the presidents visit the royal monasteries personally, having taken the visitors with them. The holy chapter decreed that each of the presidents must personally visit the exempt and royal monasteries assigned to him, together with the visitors of those monasteries, or one of them. Let the same be done in other monasteries wherever and as often as it shall be necessary.

Concerning the visitors, as above. The statutes issued in the four preceding chapters were again set down in this form and inserted into the recess, concerning the visitors: that within the year they are to begin their office from the word of God, and are as soon as possible to report to the nearest president the condition of those monasteries visited by them; all of which is contained more fully above, chapters 6, 7, and following.

Concerning the bull of the Council of Basel. To the fathers residing in the holy chapter there was shown and read the lead-sealed bull of the Council of Basel concerning the reform of our order throughout the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg. Because of its length I have been unwilling to place it here, but it has been set down separately at folio 34. Given on the tenth day before the kalends of March, in the year of the Lord 1439. The fathers unanimously accept this bull.

With concordant and unanimous vote, no one dissenting, and with mature deliberation, we accept, and, insofar as lies in us, approve the bull of the holy general Council of Basel; and concerning the reform of the monasteries subject to our chapter, we will and command that it be observed in all our monasteries not yet more strictly reformed. Nevertheless, we command all and singular the prelates throughout the aforesaid province of our order that they should take care to reform those subject to them who have not yet been more strictly reformed, according to the same form.

That the bull of the aforesaid reform is to be read publicly in each monastery and in each quarter once in the convent. We also will and command that the form of the aforesaid reform, carefully checked word for word in every monastery, be kept in good and legible script, and that once in every quarter it be read publicly in the daily chapter; and that it be explained in the vernacular to the brothers by the president, or by whomever he shall order, lest anyone be able to excuse himself under pretext of ignorance.

Those coming to the provincial chapter shall live communally. Those coming to the chapter shall likewise take their meals during those three days in one refectory, and there shall be served to them two dishes of fish only, and one of eggs, or fried eggs, and two good pottages, with cheese and some fruits added.

The eleventh provincial chapter was held at Nuremberg for the second time, in the year of the Lord 1441.

In the year of the Lord 1441, on the last day of the month of April, which was the Sunday Misericordia Domini, the eleventh provincial chapter was held at St. Giles in Nuremberg. In it the venerable fathers, the abbots of St. Giles there, of St. Peter’s in Erfurt, of St. Blaise, and of Amorbach, presided.
