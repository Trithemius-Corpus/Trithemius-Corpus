When all these things have been completed, after the notary and witnesses have gone out, let the fathers proclaim themselves before the presidents, six or seven at a time, beginning with the juniors up to and including the presidents themselves, and let them receive the salutary penances imposed on them. Then let all read, except one president:

I confess to God, to blessed Mary, to all the saints, and to you, father, that I have sinned exceedingly, through my fault, by pride, thought, speech, deed, and omission. I beg you, pray for me.

And that president shall read the absolution as follows:

May almighty God, through the intercession of blessed Mary and of all the saints, have mercy on you, and forgive you all your sins, and bring you to eternal life. Amen.

By the authority of almighty God and of the ecclesiastical keys granted in this matter to our order, we absolve you from ecclesiastical censures, and restore you to the communion of the Church and to participation in the sacraments; and we dispense with you and rehabilitate you for the execution of acts to be done, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

And when some psalm has been imposed for satisfaction, let all rise; and the same president, kneeling before the other presidents, shall read, “I confess to God.” One of the presidents shall read over him the absolution as above, with the psalm.

And note that this absolution is one of jurisdiction, and that clause which many thought should be added ought not to be placed in it: “and from your sins.” For sacramental absolution, which must be preceded by a special confession of sins, has this clause. But it must be known that this

absolution avails only this much: namely, by absolving from censures, if any had been incurred through neglect of the statutes of the order and of the chapter. And just as sacramental absolution absolves from fault, so that of jurisdiction absolves from temporal and spiritual punishment.

When these things have been completed, an end shall be made for each. Yet it must be known that if some advantage or necessity is pressing, the lord presidents may prolong the chapter further, until everything is well and maturely determined. At the end, one of the presidents, if time permits, shall give the fathers a brief exhortation before the blessing, concerning these or similar matters.

Venerable fathers, since now, with God’s favor, we have celebrated this our provincial chapter, lest, as we depart from one another, we forget because of their multitude the things that we have heard, listen patiently to the brief admonition that I make. We exhort you first of all that each of you remember within himself what the Rule says: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd falls upon him, whatever lack of profit the father of the household shall have been able to find in his sheep,” etc. And let him add, exhorting as the Lord gives him, concerning the preservation of pure religion, the faithful carrying out of profession, peace and charity and goodwill toward all, etc. Finally, let them depart from their lodgings honorably, after paying what is owed, with the knowledge of the host, not secretly but openly, etc. And let the abbots publish the statutes of this chapter to each of their subjects, and have two Masses sung conventually, one for the living and the other for the deceased brothers of our order; and let them see that the other prayers enjoined are fulfilled, and that the recess is publicly read in their monasteries to the brothers in necessary matters.

When these things have been completed, let the blessing be given by the president, as is customary, while all bow moderately.

Save your servants, O my God, who hope in you.

The Lord be with you.

And with your spirit.
