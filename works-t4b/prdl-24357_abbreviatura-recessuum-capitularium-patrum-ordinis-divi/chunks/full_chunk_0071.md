Likewise, Johannes in Wiblingen, Udalricus of Mönch... [unclear], and Johannes of St. Egidius in Nuremberg.

*That the recesses of the provincial chapters from the time of the Council of Constance should be kept in all monasteries and read publicly in the convents.*

Lest the statutes and ordinances of the provincial chapters celebrated up to now become unknown to those who are bound to know them, we will and strictly command, under penalty of twenty Rhenish florins, that all and each of the fathers have all recesses of chapters of this kind, from the time of the Council of Constance up to the present, copied and kept in one volume, which they are to bring with them at the time when the chapter is to be celebrated; and that in their convents they see to it that these same recesses are read publicly every quarter. But this statute concerning the reading of the individual recesses would seem too burdensome, as it appears to me. I therefore think it should be understood of an abbreviation, as will be clear below.

*Concerning fugitives.*

And lest an opportunity be given to monks for wandering here and there, if they were received everywhere as guests, we command all our abbots, under penalty of thirty Rhenish florins, that wherever they find a monk of our province wandering, immediately, if convenience permits, they send him, at the expense of the same monastery, back to the monastery from which he had gone out, to his own abbot; and that abbot shall be bound to receive him when so sent, after paying the expenses to the sender, without objection.

And under the same penalty we forbid all our prelates to admit or lodge any monks or converts turning aside to their monasteries, if they have not first shown letters commendatory, or credentials as they are commonly called; unless perhaps it is for this reason, that they may hold them and send them to their own abbots.

*Concerning visitations.*

Moreover, because the whole force of regular observance consists in visitations, we therefore forbid all those subject to our chapter to presume in any way to hinder the visitors in their offices; rather, they shall receive and treat them honorably and reverently, and provide for their safe-conduct and for travel-money of three florins, according to the statutes of the preceding chapters,

*Concerning the form of visitation.*

And so that the visitors may be able to observe due order in their visitations, we will and strictly command, under penalty of ten florins, that they take care to fulfill their office according to the tenor of the form of visitation contained in the bull of the lord Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal legate, which he gave to the visitors of our province at that time. And note that this form, contained in the constitutions of the same cardinal concerning visitation, was inserted here word for word into the recess. Since I have placed it separately with the others on folio 42, I judged it superfluous to repeat it again at present in one volume.

*The twenty-fifth provincial chapter, celebrated at Augsburg for the second time, in the year of the Lord 1485.*

[unclear]

In the year of the Lord 1485, in the third indiction, on the Lord’s Day Jubilate, the twenty-fifth provincial chapter was celebrated for the second time at Saints Afra and Udalricus in Augsburg. In it presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Johannes of St. Egidius in Nuremberg, Johannes of St. Martin in Sponheim, Henricus of Saints Peter and Paul in Weissenburg, and Paulus of Blessed Mary in Elchingen. There the following statutes were made.

*That criminal and incorrigible persons should not be expelled but imprisoned.*

Although our blessed father Benedict, in the Rule, orders incorrigible monks to be expelled from the monastery, nevertheless we, considering the evident danger that would arise for many from this if it were put into practice, have thought that the statutes should be varied according to the variation of the times. Therefore we strictly forbid all our abbots and prelates to presume to expel or exclude from the monastery their monks, converts, or donates who are guilty of any crime whatever, since such expulsion of incorrigible persons would give them the opportunity to wander and to commit still graver offenses. Rather, they shall thrust such persons, offending so enormously, into prison, to be punished for a longer time according to the nature of the crime committed.

And two offices are to be held in the convents after the chapters have been celebrated.
