We decree and ordain that all and each of such visitors, or their attendants, who, beyond the expenses to be provided to them only in food, presume in any way to demand or receive money, even from one willing to give it, or who themselves, or any of their attendants, similarly presume to receive gifts, shall be bound within one month to restore double what they have received to the church, monastery, or other place of that same order or religion from which it was received. Otherwise, from that time on, such visitors who delay beyond that time in restoring the double amount to them should know that they are suspended from office and benefice, until they have made full satisfaction for this double amount to the churches, monasteries, or places thus burdened, no remission, generosity, or favor from those giving it availing them in this matter. And nevertheless, if it is expedient, those visitors thus suspended shall be compelled by the presidents of the said chapters, and by each of them, to restore the aforesaid double amount, by apostolic authority, through ecclesiastical censure and other suitable remedies, with the obstacle of appeal removed.

And because it proceeds from the virtue of prudence if the way is barred to scandals, we forbid the aforesaid visitors to presume to reveal to others those persons, or their names, who have disclosed any secrets to them in visitations, unless only when, because of such disclosures, it will be necessary to proceed to someone’s punishment or penalty. Otherwise, those who make such revelations are to be punished more or less severely, as the quality of the deed requires, according to the judgment of the same presidents.

There follow, from the decrees, various rubrics concerning the state of monks, which it is sometimes useful to have at hand in visitations.

The conduct of monks ought to be separated from all others, according to the etymology of the name, because he is called “singular.” 16, q. 1, c. 1.

Monks ought not to be in a crowd of the people; for they are called “singular” for this reason, that they ought to avoid

the multitude of people. 16, q. 1, Si cupis.

According to the etymology of the name, a monk is to lead a solitary life; let him be content with his own cloister, because just as a fish without water lacks life, so a monk lacks life without the monastery. Therefore let him sit solitary and be silent, because he is dead to the world but lives to God. Let him recognize his name: for monos in Greek is “one” in Latin; achos in Greek is “sad” in Latin. Thus monachus is so called, that is, one and sad. Therefore let him sit sad and attend to his office. 16, q. 1, Placuit communi.

Except in illness, no permission is granted to a monk either to taste or to take meat. But if anyone presumes to act against the rule, let him be subject to confinement and penance for a period of six months. De consecratione, dist. 5, Carnem.

Monks ought to be subject to their abbots with all obedience and devotion. 18, q. 2, Abbates.

No one ought to become a monk in hope of an abbacy. 16, q. 7, Per laicos. See, however, the fine distinction in the gloss in the same place.

A monk cannot make a testament. 19, q. 3, Quia.

When anyone has been made a monk, all the things he had are to be conferred on the monastery; nor can he afterwards hand them over to anyone. 19, q. 3, Praelatum.

A monk is not permitted to make vows without the consent of the abbot; but if he has made a vow, it will have to be broken. 20, q. 4, Monacho.

No one may receive or ordain a monk without the permission of his abbot. 20, q. 4, Monachum.

A monk should never be idle, because every idle person is in desires; rather he should always be engaged either in the offices or in manual labor fitting for his state. Let him love knowledge of the Scriptures, and he will not love the vices of the flesh. Let body and soul tend toward the Lord. Let his senses keep watch; and, not lying open to wandering thoughts, let him do some work, so that the devil may always find him occupied. And many other fine things Jerome says to Rusticus. De consecratione, dist. 5, Nunquam.
