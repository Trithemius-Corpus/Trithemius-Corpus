Likewise, let inquiry be made about the state of the temporal goods, what it is like and how each item is spent.

These and similar matters may be inquired into generally, as it shall seem expedient to the discreet visitors.

Inquiries concerning the divine office should also be made thus, or in a similar way.

First, at what time do they rise for Matins?

Likewise, whether they sing Matins and all the other Hours, or only some of them?

Likewise, at what time do they rise for Prime?

Likewise, at what time do they perform the other Hours?

Likewise, whether they discharge the Hours deliberately and religiously, as is fitting?

Likewise, how often the chapter of faults is held in the week?

Likewise, how faults are examined there?

Likewise, whether all diligently attend the work of God?

Likewise, whether the sacred vestments, the ecclesiastical ornaments, the books, chalices, and other things are handled cleanly?

Likewise, how many Masses are celebrated daily?

Likewise, whether they sing the high Mass every day?

Likewise, how often the brothers celebrate in the week?

Likewise, whether they always confess before Mass?

Likewise, whether the Rule is read to them and expounded according to the statutes of the fathers?

And many other things in this manner.

Questions concerning abbots, provosts, or priors, if they are the supreme rulers of monasteries. Greater diligence must be applied in inquiring about abbots and their way of life, since by their merit the condition of monasteries is often led in either direction. Many things indeed occur for inquiry, but, with a few of the greater matters indicated, let the visitors themselves investigate the rest.

First, therefore, let inquiry be made whether he has zeal for God and for souls, as befits a pastor?

Likewise, whether he diligently visits the choir, the divine services, and the conventual places?

Likewise, whether he presides in person over the chapter of faults, and how often?

Likewise, whether he corrects offenders without respect of persons?

Likewise, whether he shows himself to the brothers as exemplary and imitable in all good things?

Likewise, whether he often wanders outside the monastery without reasonable cause?

Likewise, whether he takes excessive riding retinues with him?

Likewise, whether he has associates who are suspect or harmful to the monastery?

Likewise, whether he has any suspect familiarities with women?

Likewise, whether he has been in any way marked by a charge of incontinence?

Likewise, whether he admits women into the monastery, and what kind?

Likewise, whether he diligently preserves the temporal goods?

Likewise, whether he is too intent on cares for temporal affairs?

Likewise, whether he provides the brothers with necessities according to the Rule?

Likewise, whether he burdens the monastery with debts?

Likewise, whether he alienates the immovable goods of the monastery?

Likewise, whether he uses the counsel of the convent in matters to be done according to the Rule, or not?

Likewise, if he has monasteries subject to him, whether he visits them?

Likewise, whether he has had the recesses of the chapter published in the convent?

Likewise, whether he has observed the charter of the most recent visitation?

Likewise, whether he duly observes the Rule in all things, as is fitting?

And many things similar to these may be inquired into according as there is need; to express them all here would be tedious.

Likewise, questions concerning the other officials and brothers.

As for the other officials, the questions must be formed according to need, namely:

Whether there is a prior; who he is, what kind of man, and of what importance?

Likewise, whether he is continually in the convent?

Likewise, whether he is for the convent in providing necessities?

Likewise, whether he is zealous for discipline according to the statutes of the fathers?

Likewise, whether he gives the brothers an example of good conduct?

Likewise, whether he corrects offenders according to the Rule?

Likewise, whether he diligently causes the other regular statutes to be observed?

Likewise, whether he is diligent in the office entrusted to him?

Concerning the cellarer: who he is and what kind of man?

Whether he diligently fulfills his office?

Whether he duly visits the divine services and conventual duties when he is otherwise free?

Whether he diligently disposes necessities for the brothers according to the Rule?

Whether he is humble, good, and exemplary before outsiders?

How often he renders an accounting to the abbots for receipts and expenditures?

Likewise, whether he reverently obeys the abbot's commands in all things?

Likewise, whether he is wasteful and a squanderer of the monastery's goods?

Whether he is too anxious about temporal affairs?

Likewise, whether he diligently observes the Rule in fasts, vigils, and so forth?

As for the other brothers, the visitors shall inquire according to what seems opportune to them: concerning mutual charity, peace, obedience, continence, private property, and so forth.

Where, however, the abbot is diligent in correcting, less inquiry will be made about the simple brothers.

Lastly, after all have been examined, let the abbot himself also be examined, in case perhaps he wishes to say anything. And the greater articles concerning which the brothers have made depositions should be put before him; let him be advised seriously to amend. But if there is something of such a kind that it ought not to pass without correction, and he denies it, the testimonies of those making deposition must be examined more carefully, until the truth appears more clearly. Nevertheless, let disturbances and scandals be guarded against.

Concerning the final proclamation of each one, after the examination has been begun according to custom in chapter. Chapter 24.

When it shall seem opportune to the visitors, let all be called together to the chapter place to continue the act of visitation. When they are seated, one of the visitors should say: “Give a blessing.” And when the response has been made, “Lord,” let him add:

Most beloved brothers, there now remains, as you know, the final execution of our visitation; and for carrying it out we ought to give thanks to God.
