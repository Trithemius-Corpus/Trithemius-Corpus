Yet for this reason we do not intend, as indeed we cannot, to derogate in any way from the other things contained in the aforesaid Rule, even if they are not now explained by us, or from the praiseworthy constitutions of the fathers and the stricter observances, so that the religious of the said order are bound to them as before.

First, just as the said Rule of Saint Benedict took very solicitous care concerning the divine work, we, following it, ordain that the divine office, both by day and by night, be celebrated devoutly, deliberately, with pauses, not racing through it, not cutting it short, at the proper hours, as is contained in the Rule of our most blessed father. And as soon as the signal has been heard, leaving everything that may be in their hands, they shall hasten to it with the greatest speed, yet with gravity, lest frivolity find encouragement, so that nothing is preferred to the work of God.

And so that they may not be able to excuse themselves on the pretext of ignorance because they did not hear the signal, we will that the recitation of matins, which is to begin at midnight or thereabout, of prime, and of vespers last at least more than a quarter of an hour.

But whoever has not come to the aforesaid office shall be punished without remission, as is contained in the Rule.

On Confession and the Celebration of Masses. Article 2.

We also will and ordain that the abbots and the other aforesaid members of the order confess, celebrate, and communicate as is contained in the constitutions of Pope Benedict XII concerning the celebration of masses.

And all those who, according to the law and the Benedictine sanctions, ought to have been established in sacred orders or in the order of priesthood shall be promoted to these within a year; and against those who are negligent the visitors shall proceed as they can by law.

XXXVII

On the Daily Chapter of Faults. Article 3.

We further will and ordain that every day, in places where there are six monks, and in other places where it is customary, after prime if only one mass is said, but if two are said, after the first-mentioned mass or at another customary hour, the daily chapter shall be held. In it, after the martyrology and the reading of the Rule, which is to be explained in the vernacular by the one presiding over that chapter, or by another whom he has ordered, before they proceed to other matters, the faults and excesses of the negligent or delinquent shall be punished.

On the Books and Ornaments of the Church. Article 4.

We will that every abbot or presiding officer, when he has come to his monastery, immediately within a month provide for a clean and proper vessel and place where the sacred Eucharist is to be kept; and also that the books, with the other ornaments concerning the divine office and the monastery, be preserved with diligent custody.

On the Form and Decency of Clothing and Tonsure. Article 5.

Since, through the decency of outward habit, the professed and novices of the said order ought to show moral integrity and the dignity of religion, we establish and ordain that all wear large and broad tonsures, so that the circle of them does not exceed the space or thickness of two transverse fingers. And let them carry themselves decently in garments, habits, and footwear, according to the Clementine Ne in agro and the constitution of the aforesaid Pope Benedict XII on the form and decency of garments: namely, that they wear tunics and garments nearest to the outer habit, of black or brown color according to the custom usually observed among them, round and reaching to the ankles, not slit nor marked by any other deformity, having broad sleeves extending as far as the wrist, in no way buttoned or tied; yet not so broad that their width extends beyond the wrist with the thumb raised, nor around the mouth of the elbow, as the abuse of certain persons has it, beyond what is proper.
