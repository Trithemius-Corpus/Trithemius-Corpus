XIX

Monks may neither make women their co-mothers, nor have familiarity with women: 18, question 2, Pervenit ad nos.

Monks are not permitted to live with nuns or to dine with them: 18, question 2, Diffiniuimus, and the two following chapters.

Monks are not permitted, at their own pleasure, to expel abbots or appoint others: 18, question 2, Nullam.

Women are forbidden to enter the monasteries of monks: 18, question 2, chapter Pervenit ad nos.

A monk is not permitted to possess property of his own: 12, question 1, Non dicatis, and chapter Quia tua; but all things ought to be common to them: chapter Scimus.

Incontinent monks are to be confined in the prisons of harsh penance: 27, question 1, Impudicas; 12, question 1, Non dicatis.

A monk is not permitted to receive anything without the permission of his prelate.

A congregation of monks, however holy it may seem, is rarely found without some admixture of bad men: distinction 47, Quantumlibet. And this is the saying of Augustine to Vincent the Donatist, where he also says: Just as I have scarcely found better men than those who have made progress in a monastery, so I have not found worse men than those who have failed in a monastery. Hence, I think, comes the saying in the last chapter of the Apocalypse: Let the just man still be justified, and let him who is in filth still become filthy. Thus there.

A monk who seeks to live delicately in religion, though he was poor in the world, is to be detested: 12, question 2, Gloria.

A monk has the office not of one who teaches, but of one who laments, who mourns either himself or the world, and who awaits the coming of the Lord with fear: 16, question 1, Monachus.

Monks who fail in religion are the worst; those who make progress are the best: distinction 47, Quantumlibet.

True monks and good religious men are to be honored before the rest: 16, question 1, Qui vere.

Monks are not to be received into a monastery for a price, nor are they to be permitted to have a private fund: De statu monachorum, chapter Monachi.

A monk who has property of his own is to be removed from communion at the altar; and if such a man is found at the point of death, he is not to receive burial among the brothers: in the same place, and chapter Super quodam, and chapter Cum ad monasterium.

Monks are bound to observe continual silence in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and cloister: in the same chapter Cum ad monasterium.

Healthy monks are not to feed on meat: in the same place, section In refectorio.

From the Summa of Brother Angelus, on religious.

Whether religious are bound to be perfect. The answer is that they are not, except with that perfection to which they have obligated themselves; but they are bound to tend toward perfection, according to Thomas, Secunda secundae, question 186. Contempt for perfection is contrary to this. Hence they are not liars, although outwardly they bear the state of perfection, because they do not assume it as professing themselves to be perfect, but as professing themselves to be tending toward perfection. Therefore they would indeed be liars if they recalled their mind from the intention of perfection, according to Astesanus, book 6, title 47.

On obedience, collected from the Decrees.

Obedience alone possesses the merit of faith and is rightly preferred to sacrifices, because through sacrifices another’s flesh is slain, but through obedience one’s own will is slain: 8, question 1, Sciendum.

Obedience ought to be promised in writing; this is from custom and from the precept of the Rule of Saint Benedict. Likewise, he who knows that he has been placed over others should not be vexed that someone has been made his prelate, but should himself render the obedience that he demands: chapter Qui suis.

It is often not expedient to obey commands: 11, question 3, Non semper. It is not always evil not to obey commands; when the lord commands things that are contrary to God, then he must not be obeyed. And the following chapter, and chapter Iulianus, and chapter Si is.

Superiors are to be obeyed promptly: distinction 93, Si inimicus.

God is to be obeyed principally: distinction 8, Quae contra.
