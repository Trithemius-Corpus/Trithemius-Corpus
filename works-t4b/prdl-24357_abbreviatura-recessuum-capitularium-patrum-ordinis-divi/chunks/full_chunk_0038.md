*That abbots and presidents of monasteries celebrate there on solemnities. Article 28.* We also will that the said abbots celebrate there on the principal solemnities and on others, according to the custom and practice hitherto laudably observed in the said monasteries.

*That monks not live with their parents. Article 29.* We forbid abbots to permit their religious to make a long stay and residence with their parents or other laypersons, or to be entangled in the services of such people. Indeed, if one prior could not live honorably and fittingly in his priory with at least one religious, let him be brought back to the monastery, according to the constitution of Pope Benedict XII and of the Council of Vienne which begins *Ne in agro*.

*Of beneficed monks to be sent to general studies.* But if any priors or beneficed monks wish to go to study, let the greatest care be taken that, under the appearance and shadow of study, they do not wish to wander about in cities. For this reason, when they are seen to be useless and neglectful of study, let them be sent back to the cloister or to their benefice, if, as has been said, they can live there. And the general prior of the monks in study should take diligent care over this matter; and we will that this prior be diligently visited and corrected by the provincial chapter within whose boundaries such a study is located.

*Of the chapter of faults of monks who are students. Article 31.* We will that the prior of the monks who are students of the said order hold his chapter every fifteen days, and there correct and punish, according to the form of the Rule, any student monks whenever they have behaved scandalously in life or dress. Moreover, he must inquire diligently into this and appoint trustworthy investigators from the order itself in the same study.

According to the form of the constitution of lord Benedict, Pope XII, no general prior shall continue in study except for one year. And we will and decree that the same prior be a formed bachelor in theology, or licensed in decrees, or a man of advanced and proven religious life. This prior shall also have authority to punish the contumacious and rebellious by bodily imprisonment, but with the consent of four or five notable religious present in the study, to be chosen by the religious of the said order studying there. Furthermore, with the consent of the aforesaid persons, when he sees any to be incorrigible and excessively scandalous, he may send them back to the cloister or to their own priory.

Item, we will and ordain that all religious coming to the study present themselves to the said prior within five days, and that he have all of them written down in one book. Nor shall they leave the study unless the cause of their return has first been made known to the said prior. If this prior sees that the cause is unjust on the abbot’s part, then let him take care diligently to inform the visitors of the provincial chapter, so that they may make provision concerning this.

*That monks not go on pilgrimage. Article 32.* We forbid that abbots permit their monks, under the pretext of some devotion to some saint, or of indulgences to be sought in some place, to go out from their places, especially beyond the boundaries of their diocese, unless perhaps to visit certain regions by whose example they can be edified, or which they can edify; but this rarely and with great caution. And no prior or beneficed monk shall remain outside his own diocese for five days unless he has obtained permission from his abbot. Otherwise the guilty shall be punished most sharply by the visitors of the provincial chapter.

*Of the increase of penalties. Article 33.* The aforesaid visitors shall impose the more serious penalties of the constitution *Ne in agro* against any religious so disorderly or forgetful of their profession.
