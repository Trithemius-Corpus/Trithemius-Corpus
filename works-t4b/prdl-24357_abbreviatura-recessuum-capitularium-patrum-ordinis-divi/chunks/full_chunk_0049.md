Visitors and those coming to the provincial chapter shall not have more than twelve mounts. We further will that no visitor shall dare to bring more than twelve mounts with him; and we will the same to be observed regarding those coming to the chapter. But if they do the contrary, they shall be bound to pay as much as they have spent in excess, to be deposited within a month in the common chest for the use of the aforesaid chapter. If they do not do this, they are to be condemned by the presidents in the next chapter to double the amount to be assessed, with no remission to avail them in this matter.

Concerning the appointment of presidents, if any one of them should happen to die before the chapter to be celebrated in the future.

We therefore refrain from naming the presidents for the chapter to be celebrated, so that if any one or more of them should happen to close his last day, his successor in the dignity of the abbacy shall likewise succeed to the presidency. We will the same to be understood concerning the preacher, the one who is to celebrate Mass, and each of the visitors of the whole aforesaid province and diocese.

Mendicants are not to be received into our order without apostolic authority or sufficient license.

It has come to our hearing that certain abbots, or other presidents in monasteries of the said order, have received and incorporated mendicants as monks, even without the license of their superiors; and, what is worse, afterward permit them to wander, to the detriment of their souls and to the scandal of very many. We forbid them to presume such things hereafter. Those whom they have already received they shall be bound, as soon as they can, either to have under apostolic authority or sufficient license, and others wandering outside the monasteries they shall recall to the monastery, at least within a year; otherwise, if they do not exercise diligence in this matter, they shall incur sentences of suspension.

That these statutes be published to all subject to the provincial chapter, and twice a year together with the constitutions of Benedict XII.

But that the aforesaid statutes may be better observed throughout all places of the order in the province and dioceses aforesaid, we ordain that within a year, or at least a month before the future chapter, every abbot shall be bound to hold and complete a general chapter, if he has any subjects outside his own monastery, in which the aforesaid statutes are to be published. We nevertheless will them to be read distinctly twice a year in the daily chapter, so that no one may be able to put forward the excuse of ignorance, together with the constitution of Benedict.

The second chapter was held at Mainz among the Augustinians, in the year of the Lord 1418.

In the year of the Lord 1418, in the eleventh indiction, on the first day of the month of September, in the pontificate of the lord Martin, pope, the fifth of that name, the second provincial chapter of Mainz was held in the monastery of the brothers, hermits of Saint Augustine; for it could not be held at Saint Alban's, where it had been appointed the preceding year, because of the transfer from the order that was then being carried out. In it the venerable fathers and lords, the abbots of Fulda, Kempten, Saint Ulrich at Augsburg, and Reinhardsbrunn, presided.

Concerning the statutes.

In this second provincial chapter few new statutes were issued among those issued in it; but all and each of the old statutes of the preceding chapter were approved and repeated word for word. It is not necessary to set them down here again, since they have once been set down above. Nevertheless, after these I shall briefly mention those things that were not noted or established above. For I judge that the new material should come first in this place.

Concerning the spoils of benefices.

We further ordain that abbots and others to whom, by custom, statute, or privilege, it belongs to receive the spoils of benefices subject to them shall, when receiving them, observe constitution 11. But if they exceed in this matter, they are to be gravely punished by the visitors, even apart from the penalties contained in that constitution, which the visitors may impose.
