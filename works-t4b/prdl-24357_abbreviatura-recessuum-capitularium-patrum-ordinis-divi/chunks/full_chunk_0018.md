11, question 3, Imperatores.

A monk ought to obey his own abbot more than the bishop in matters that pertain to the Rule: 18, question 2, Quam sit, § Nullus, and chapter Abbates; 9, question 3, Conquestus.

The commands of superiors are to be obeyed even if they themselves act otherwise, unless they have erred in the faith: 2, question 7, Sacerdotes.

Evil must never be done through obedience, but some good can sometimes be omitted because of obedience: Quod agit, 11, question 3, Quod generale.

Likewise, collected from the decrees concerning the correction of sinners.

Correction ought to be made from charity, according to the diversity of faults: 24, question 3, Corripiantur.

Correction ought to be made with the intention of healing, not of raging: distinction 86, Odio; 23, question 4, Ita plane circa me.

Correction ought to be mingled with kindness and severity: distinction 45, Disciplina; 12, question 2, Fraternitas; distinction 45, Omnis.

For excessive severity receives from those rebuked neither correction nor salvation: distinction 45, Cum beatus.

Many things about these matters are found scattered above in the form of visitation.

He who consents to sins is accursed before God: 11, question 3. He who consents to sinners and defends another who is offending will be accursed before God and before men, and he is to be corrected with a very severe rebuke. Hence also a certain most holy father says: If anyone defends a sinner, let him be restrained more sharply than the one who sinned. These are the words there of Isidore, and in the chapter Ita corporis and the two following chapters.

He who does not resist errors consents to them: distinction 83, Error. What he does not resist, he approves; and truth, when it is not defended, is oppressed. For to neglect to disturb the perverse when you can do so is nothing other than to foster them; nor is he free from the scruple of hidden fellowship who ceases to oppose manifest crime. This is the saying of Innocent. And in the following chapter, Pope Pius says:

For what does it profit him not to be polluted by his own error, if he grants consent to one who errs?

And in the following chapter Gregory says:

He seems to consent to one who errs who does not meet and cut away the things that ought to be corrected.

The faults of inferiors are imputed to the negligence of rulers: distinction 86.

The faults of lower orders are to be referred to no one more than to slothful and negligent rulers, who often nourish many plagues while they avoid applying a harsher medicine.

Men are to be loved; sins are to be held in hatred, in the following chapter.

Let them hate sins, not men. Let them correct the proud, tolerate the weak; and what it is necessary to punish more severely, let them strike with the mind not of one raging, but of one healing.

One must have pity on the man and be angry at the sinner: 23, question 4. When we say these two names, man and sinner, they are not said in vain. Because he is a sinner, correct him; because he is a man, have mercy on him. Nor will you wholly free the man unless you have pursued the sinner. This is Augustine on the Psalter.

XXI

The manner and form of celebrating a provincial chapter of the fathers of the order of Saint Benedict in the province of Mainz.

Since, in celebrating provincial chapters, many occupations are accustomed to confront the lord presidents, lest, when those who are experienced and practiced depart or are absent, those less experienced and unaccustomed to celebrating chapters of this kind should happen to be distracted by those very many occupations, I have taken care to send in writing, for the knowledge of those who follow, the present form, which in those very chapters that I have more often attended, when they were being celebrated, I saw and heard. Although in it we do not have all the things that are to be done in chapters, nevertheless many and more necessary things will be found. For I think this will be of profit both to those present, for the exercise of discipline, and to those to come, by the example and consideration of their predecessors.

That the presidents, visitors, and treasurers should arrive before the day of the chapter.

Therefore the past and future presidents, together with the visitors or treasurers, should come some days before the appointed day for celebrating the chapter, in the meantime also being supported by the expenses of the chapter, in order to discuss and advise one another in the matters that occur and must be done, as was ordained in the fifteenth chapter, celebrated for the second time at Seligenstadt in the year of the Lord 1454.

On the Saturday preceding the day of the chapter, let one citation be made of the absent, and of those who have not yet satisfied the assessment of the contribution. The promoter of the order, by command of the lord presidents, shall have this citation posted by the notary of the chapter on the doors of the monastery at vespers or compline. And he shall have the same done in two other churches if there is need. The citation may be made in this form:

By mandate of the reverend lord presidents of the black monks of the order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg, all and singular abbots, provosts, and priors of the order of the province...
