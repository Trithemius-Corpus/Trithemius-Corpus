LXV

In the year of the Lord 1451, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of May, which was Cantate Sunday, the fourteenth provincial chapter was celebrated at St. Stephen's in Wurzburg, with the most reverend father, lord Nicholas of Cusa, cardinal priest of the title of St. Peter ad Vincula and legate, present and presiding over that chapter. In it the venerable fathers, the lord abbots of St. Giles in Nuremberg, of St. Burkard in Wurzburg, of St. Peter in Erfurt, and of St. Godehard in Hildesheim, presided.

Concerning the old statutes repeated.

Besides those statutes which the aforesaid lord Nicholas, cardinal and legate, issued, the present sacred chapter established certain things, and repeated word for word several things issued in previous chapters, namely:

Concerning safe conduct and the visitors' travel expenses, as above, in the preceding chapter.

Likewise, that the names of deceased brothers are to be announced in the individual chapters, as above, chapter 12. And many other things which it would seem superfluous to insert here in order.

Concerning collations and drinkings not to be held in our monasteries.

It has come to our truthful hearing by report that in certain monasteries, even reformed ones, by a certain special gracious permission of the prelates, collations have customarily been held before compline. Certain brothers now contend that these are to be held daily in certain monasteries as if by right, taking them up on their own authority, to no small prejudice and loss of the purity of reform and of the peace of the same monasteries, with detractions, frivolities, reports of secular business, and also sometimes by extending even to extravagant disputes and at times in the presence of laymen. Therefore we forbid the same collations to be held henceforth without the special grace of those same prelates. We also will that the visitors be specially admonished concerning these things.

Besides these things nothing new was established in this chapter. But the lord cardinal issued certain constitutions for the visitors, some of which are contained here separately.

The fifteenth provincial chapter, celebrated a second time in Seligenstadt, was held in the year of the Lord 1454.

In the year of the Lord 1454, on the twelfth day of the month of May, which was Jubilate Sunday, the fifteenth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saints Marcellinus and Peter, martyrs, in Seligenstadt. In it the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Wolfram of Hirsau, Christian of St. Peter in Erfurt, Ulrich in Wiblingen, and Berthold of St. Stephen in Wurzburg, presided.

They approve the statutes of the lord cardinal.

Following in the footsteps of the lord cardinal and legate, with the sacred chapter approving, we command all and each of the statutes issued and ordained by him for the reform of our order in the immediately preceding provincial chapter celebrated in Wurzburg, and then unanimously accepted by the lord presidents and the whole chapter, to be strictly observed by all, under the penalties and censures contained and expressed in the letters of the said lord cardinal composed concerning this matter.

That the presidents, visitors, and treasurers should arrive before the day of the chapter.

We further will and ordain that, for provincial chapters to be celebrated from now on in the future, the lord presidents, visitors, and treasurers shall arrive on certain preceding days, while also being supported in the meantime at the chapter's expense, to handle the matters that arise and are necessary and suitable for the more useful and speedier celebration and dispatch of the chapter itself.

How visitors are to be received and treated.

We also strictly command all prelates and convents that, if they are required concerning this matter, they are to provide for their visitors safe conduct, at their own expense and according to their ability and knowledge, from the princes of the region and of that place or dominion as far as the next monastery to be visited. They are to receive and treat those visitors with all reverence and humility, charitably; they are diligently to observe their admonitions and ordinances and obey them.
