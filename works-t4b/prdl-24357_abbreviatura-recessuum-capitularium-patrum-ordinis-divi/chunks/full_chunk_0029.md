We therefore condemn them thus in these writings, and declare that they are to be compelled to pay by ecclesiastical censures, and we do compel them. We have assessed the amount of these monies at two Rhenish florins, to be paid irrevocably to our treasurers within this time and N., under penalty of excommunication, which, once that term has elapsed, they shall incur in these writings unless they have made satisfaction truly and effectively before the day fixed.

From these words let the proper form for the process be taken. Or, if the lord presidents wish to proceed more gently, then let the final clause be changed to this or a similar one: “We reserve to ourselves, or to our visitors, the assessment of these sums or shares of money.” The presidents can, however, first cite them to hear the assessment made, though this does not seem necessary, since the Benedictine constitution commits the matter simply to the lord presidents.

Then the promoter of the order shall accuse the rebels and say:

Reverend fathers, in the second place I accuse the contumacy of abbots A, B, and C, who do not appear, neither in person nor through lawful proctors, although they have been sufficiently awaited, and who do not show that they have obeyed the concluded reformation and withdrawn from their contumacy and rebellion. I ask that they be held, as indeed they are, contumacious; and, in their contumacy, I ask that the same men be declared to have incurred the sentence of excommunication contained in the processes of the past provincial chapters, and that appropriate letters be decreed upon this, with further penalties, censures, and sentences, even to the invocation of the aid of the secular arm.

Then the same president shall add: “We hold the aforesaid absentees to be contumacious, as indeed they are.” And he shall add this sentence:

The name of Christ having been invoked, keeping God alone before our eyes, by this our sentence, which we, residing in this place of presidency, issue in these writings with the counsel and assent of men learned in the law, we pronounce, decree, and declare:

that lords A, B, and C, abbots, and A, B, and C, conventuals, being contumaciously absent, because of their contumacy and manifest rebellion, in that they damnably refused in their chapters to receive and effectively enter upon the concluded, holy, and due regular observance, and, not withdrawing from this rebellion of theirs, did not appear, nor did they take care to allege lawful or reasonable causes why they were by no means bound to this, have incurred the sentence of excommunication according to the tenor of our processes in the preceding chapters, or in the last chapter celebrated at N.; and that, as such, they are to be publicly denounced, and are denounced. We decree by these presents that our necessary letters concerning this are to be issued, also under other censures and sentences, up to and including the invocation of the aid of the secular arm.

It should be noted that the lord presidents can proceed otherwise, if they please, against contumacious absentees: namely, in the recess they may state that within a fixed time they are to make terms with them, or with the visitors, or with others to whom they have given authority, a term being given for canonical monition. If they neglect this, once the fixed term has elapsed they shall be excommunicated by the fact itself, to be absolved by no one except by the presidents themselves or by the visitors. If this is done, then they should take care to bring such a process to their certain knowledge.

After the sentence has been issued, let the absentees be cited anew to hear and see the expenses assessed on the next day, in this or a similar form, and let it be posted under vespers:

By mandate of the reverend and venerable fathers, the lord presidents of the black monks of the order of Saint Benedict of the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg, all and singular abbots, provosts, priors, and all others who are bound, or think themselves bound, to have an interest in the same province and diocese are cited to appear on Wednesday after the Sunday Jubilate, at the sixth hour in the morning, in person or through their lawful proctors, in the monastery of N., before the aforesaid...
