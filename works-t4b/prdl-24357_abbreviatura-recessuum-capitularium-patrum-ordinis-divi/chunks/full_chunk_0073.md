Concerning those who do not admit the visitors.

It has come to our ears by certain and weighty report that some abbots and monks have not only refused to admit their visitors, but, what is more serious, have even shamefully driven them from their houses with reproach and indignation, in manifest contempt of the chapter and canons of the order. Therefore we exhort all and each of the prelates and professed members established anywhere in our province in the order not to presume to attempt such things henceforth, under the penalty more fully expressed in the statutes above; and we wish that penalty to be applied without remission against rebels of this sort by the visitors themselves or by the presidents. Rather, all who are to be visited should meet their visitors cheerfully and reverently, receive them humbly and gladly, and treat them humanely; and, if need be, they should also provide them at their own expense with safe conduct as far as another monastery to be visited by them.

Concerning the visitation documents.

It pleased the fathers that, unless contempt intervenes, in the visitation documents henceforth they should not place words of command or threat under censures, but rather exhortatory or entreating words, by which they may draw the minds of their subjects toward virtues out of love, unless, as we have said, contempt intervenes, or where the matter concerns the transgression of the three substantial vows, or some evident necessity elsewhere compels it.

The twenty-eighth provincial chapter was celebrated in the year of the Lord 1493.

In the year of our Lord 1493, on Jubilate Sunday, the twenty-eighth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul at Hirsau, of the order of Saint Benedict, in the diocese of Speyer. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots John in Sponheim, Conrad in Wiblingen, Bartholomew of Holy Cross in Werdea, and Andreas of Monks' Mount near Bamberg,

Concerning the confession of monks.

We will and ordain that every monk professed according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, in accordance with the Clementine constitution Ne in agro, De statu monachorum, must and is bound to make confession to his abbot at least once a month concerning matters that arise; and concerning reserved cases, to the abbot as often as he knows himself to have offended in such matters, under the penalties contained in the constitutions of the supreme pontiffs, general councils, or provincial chapters.

It was also established in the same chapter that the abridgment of the recess of the provincial chapters, after it has been printed and presented to the individual monasteries, should thereafter be read publicly at least once every three years.
