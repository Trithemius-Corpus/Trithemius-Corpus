Nicholas, by divine mercy cardinal legate of the holy Roman Church, of the title of Saint Peter ad Vincula. To all the faithful of Christ throughout the province of Mainz, etc. Concerning the enclosures of nuns.

Quoniam multorum, etc., section 1. We strictly command and order that no man, of whatever status, dignity, or preeminence, even if he is exempt, under penalty of excommunication latae sententiae, which we pronounce in these writings, shall henceforth, after the execution of this our mandate, presume in any way to enter any monastery of nuns in the city and province of Mainz, unless he has license from the ordinary, or from the person to whom this belongs, according to the provision of common law, and otherwise in cases permitted by law. But if anyone attempts to do the contrary, we will that he, by that very fact and without any further declaration, be avoided by all as excommunicate, both in divine services and outside them; and wherever he may be, for as long as he remains there, the strictest interdict shall be observed in that place.

Likewise, nuns are forbidden to leave their monasteries under penalty of excommunication. Under penalty of excommunication latae sententiae and of interdict, we command each and every nun of the aforesaid monasteries that, after notice of the present letters, they shall by no means presume to leave their monastery, even with permission granted by the abbess and prioress or by any other woman, except as common law permits; nor shall any one of them presume to do so. Rather, we will that anyone doing the contrary be excommunicate, and that the place wherever she may be be under interdict, without any further declaration.

And we command, under a like penalty latae sententiae, all chaplains and priests who have certain knowledge of this to publish that nun as excommunicate and that place where she may be as under interdict.

XLV

Constitutions of the provincial chapters of the Order of Saint Benedict throughout the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg.

Preface.

Since, for one who wishes to know the individual statutes of the order, namely what and of what kind they are that have been issued by our most devout predecessors, the fathers, in provincial chapters, it would be laborious to run through all the acts and recesses of those chapters because of their length; and yet, lest so great a labor impede the pious desire of knowing, since it is also full of tedium for each person to carry with him the larger volumes of the aforesaid recesses when chapters are to be celebrated according to custom, I undertake to gather those same statutes together, abbreviated into one work. And this is the order of this labor: I shall pass over the sanctions of the supreme pontiffs, and also the decrees of general councils made for the celebration of chapters of this sort, so that what must be done or judged in the remaining statutes may be recognized more easily. Then, with as much brevity and diligence as I can, I shall summarize the times, places, and statutes of the individual provincial chapters celebrated in our province from the time of the Council of Constance. I believe this labor will be useful enough for posterity, if they decide to add to these things what must be established. But if it pleases no one, which I rather fear, I nevertheless pray that I be pardoned, since I have sought to satisfy not my own will, but that of the fathers, who desired and requested this.

Innocent III, in the general council. That a provincial chapter is to be held every three years.

In each kingdom or province, preserving the right of the diocesan bishops, a common chapter of abbots and of priors who do not have their own abbots, and who have not been accustomed to celebrate such a chapter, shall be held every three years. All who have no canonical impediment shall come to it at one designated monastery.
