Moreover, they ought to be gentle, not cruel: 10, q. 3, *Cavendum*. For this office mature men should be chosen, and men experienced in many matters, not novices, masters before they have been disciples: 16, q. 1, *Si clericatus*; dist. 61, *Miserum*. Yet I do not mean by this that mature young men should not be preferred to dissolute old men, since men are deservedly called priests, that is, elders, by wisdom, not by age: dist. 84, *Porro*; provided that men gray-haired in character are appointed as visitors. What this gray hair is, Job 28 says: “Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom, and to depart from evil is understanding”: 26, q. 2, *Qui sine*. And prelates, because of their honor, are also called elders: dist. 21, *Cleros post me*.

Let the visitor be, through humility, a companion to the good; but toward the obstinate and those who despise discipline let him display the severity of his authority, because it is harmful to governance if prelates show excessive humility toward subjects: dist. 86, *Quando*. Yet this severity must be tempered with kindness: dist. 45, *Disciplina*, and c. *Sunt*. Therefore let him always appoint from among the fathers, if it can be done, the better men for this office, who know and are able to give counsel to individuals in their needs and cases, and who first do in their own communities what they attempt in those of others.

For thus our fathers said in chapter 5 of the sixth provincial chapter: “Visitors appointed by the sacred provincial chapter shall first and before all else visit and reform themselves and their own monasteries subject to them, lest, when they direct their eyes to the reform of others, the sick murmur and say, ‘Physician, heal yourself,’ Luke 4. Therefore let them begin first with themselves; then let them visit and reform the other monasteries committed to them, of both monks and nuns, with all the diligence they can.” To this pertains *De censibus*, *Romana*, book 6; and 3, q. 7, *Iudicet*, and the two following chapters; dist. 25, *Primum*; dist. 61, *In sacerdotibus*; dist. 83, *Nemo recte*.

No one rightly assumes the role of an admonisher unless he condemns errors by his own deeds and demonstrates love of innocence by his conduct. These are his words. Therefore let the visitors take care first to visit themselves, and so proceed to the correction of others. For it is written, Job 5: “Visiting your own appearance, you will not sin.” For he who neglects himself cannot amend another, as the Lord says in Luke 6: “If the blind lead the blind, both fall into the pit.”

III

Lastly, that visitors should have zeal for God and for souls. Chapter 4.

Finally, the visitors themselves must be zealous, in accordance with knowledge, for the salvation of those to be visited, so that they do not carry out their office negligently and merely in passing, lest, with the lazy servant, they be judged useless by the sentence of damnation. And let them have zeal for regular discipline and for the constitutions of the fathers, knowing without doubt that they will be abundantly rewarded for their labors by the giver of all good things. Let them carry out the office of visitation committed to them as if the care of all whom they visit belonged to them alone.

Yet their zeal should be discreet, lest, God forbid, if they correct sinners indiscreetly, they themselves incur a graver fault: 24, q. 3, *Non imperpetuum*. And where they find anything worthy of correction, let them correct it not from love of vengeance nor from hatred, but from zeal for justice: 23, q. 4, § last. And the just and holy are indignant against sinners not with hatred but with zeal for discipline: dist. 45, *Vera iusticia*. Nor should they be remiss where fault has deserved discipline, because if they let the faults of offenders go unpunished, they sin gravely: 11, q. 3, *Si is*; 23, q. 5, *Qui viciis*. And a prelate who does not correct crimes is called not a prelate but a shameless dog: dist. 83, *Nemo*. He bears the guilt of the doer who, when he is able to correct, neglects to amend: dist. 86, *Culpam*; dist. 83, *Error*. And not to correct the wicked but to foster them is to kill them: 23, q. 5, *Qui viciis*; dist. 45, *Sed illud*.
