Certain of the same statutes were also again inserted into this recess, which are found above word for word: namely, that the presidents, visitors, and preacher should arrive on the appointed day of the chapter, on Wednesday. Likewise, that if one of the presidents is absent at this arrival, the others, adding to themselves one of the priors if they wish, until he comes, may do everything with full power. Also the decree concerning apostolic indults not to be obtained or used, as is found in the preceding chapter. Similarly, that no one should be procurator for more than three persons; and that the procurators should be fully informed about each matter concerning the visitors, under the penalty stated there, and should transmit the recess to them within two months.

And that statute: that abbots and monks should wear suitable dress in every place. Also that one which forbids any abbot, for his own excuse, to dare to obtain letters of princes for the chapter. Likewise, that prelates should seek out their fugitives, and should henceforth grant no one a general letter of license. And that the visitors should visit provostships, priories, and convents of nuns, with their abbot able to be present if he wishes, as is found there. Also that those visited should pay their visitors three Rhenish florins for travel expenses. And concerning the penalty of three florins imposed on negligent visitors for any monastery not visited. Likewise also this: that the visitors should denounce rebels and disobedient persons to the nearest president, so that they may be punished. These and similar matters, which are contained above word for word, were here repeated again, with the following nevertheless newly added, as seemed good.

Novices are not to be admitted to profession before the completed year of probation.

Seeking as far as we can to forestall future dangers and to comply with regular and legal provision, we strictly forbid any prelate henceforth to receive a novice to profession before the year of his probation has been completed.

Concerning the habit of the lay brothers.

For the pleasing uniformity of all, lay brothers shall wear their cowls open in the front, in the manner of monks.

The twenty-second provincial chapter was celebrated at Michelsberg for the third time, in the year of the Lord 1476.

In the year of the Lord 1476, in the ninth indiction, on Jubilate Sunday, the twenty-second provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Michelsberg near Bamberg for the third time. In it the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Martin in Schwarzach, Hermann of St. James at Mainz, Henry in Blaubeuren, and Johannes of St. Giles in Nuremberg, presided.

The statutes of previous chapters are first approved.

Directing our attention to the things that were established in previous chapters and recesses, we confirm and approve all their constitutions and decrees, and we will that they be held and observed by all, just as if they were expressly inserted here word for word.

Concerning surreptitious apostolic indults not to be used, under penalty of excommunication.

Under penalty of excommunication we command that no one presume to use apostolic indults obtained surreptitiously, tending toward danger to souls and harm to regular observance, whether already obtained or perhaps to be obtained in the future without the knowledge of the presidents, as is also found there.

That absentees, with a procuratorial mandate, should pay four florins for the one whose procurator he is, under penalty of that person’s contumacy, without contradiction, to the chapter henceforth.

Concerning the other statutes placed here.

The statute concerning procurators was also renewed here again word for word: that they should transmit the recess of the chapter to those by whom they were sent, within two months after the chapter has been celebrated, under penalty of two florins if the procurator is an abbot, and of suspension from divine offices if he is a monk, as is found in chapter 20.
