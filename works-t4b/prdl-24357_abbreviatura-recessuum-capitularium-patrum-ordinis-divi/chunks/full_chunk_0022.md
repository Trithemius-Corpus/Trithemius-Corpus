The visitors themselves, moreover, should, in our stead, strike with regular censure those monks whom they find contumacious and rebellious, according to the measure of their fault and without preference of persons; they should not spare rebels because of their stubbornness or because of the power of their friends, but should cast out the diseased sheep lest it infect the healthy sheep.

But if abbots are found negligent in correcting, according to the visitors' mandate and the regular statutes, either themselves or their monks, let them be denounced and rebuked, and let them be punished publicly in the general chapter in such a way that their penalty may be an example to others. But if an abbot who is not exempt is found by the visitors to be excessively negligent and lax, they should report this without delay to the diocesan of the place; and through him let a faithful and prudent coadjutor be given to him until the general chapter. But if he is found to be a squanderer, or otherwise deserving removal, after this has been reported to the diocesan by the visitors, let him be removed from the governance of the abbey without the noise of legal proceedings; and in the meantime let a suitable administrator be provided for the monastery, to take care of temporal affairs until an abbot has been provided for that monastery.

But if perhaps the bishop is unwilling or neglects to fulfill these things, the visitors or the presidents of the general chapter should not delay reporting the bishop's default to the apostolic see. We order these same things to be done concerning exempt abbots by the visitors or the presidents of the general chapter, with their deposition reserved to the apostolic see: so that, once the abbot who appears fit to be removed has meanwhile been suspended from administration by the visitors or by the presidents in chapter, a suitable administrator may be appointed for the monastery. Their offenses, however, and the other matters that seem proper to be reported to the chapter, the presidents shall report to us through faithful and prudent messengers,

to whom expenses shall be sufficiently supplied from the common contribution of the abbots, according to each one's means.

The following visitors, moreover, shall carefully trace the footsteps of the former visitors, and shall report their negligences and offenses to the next general chapter, so that they may publicly bear the penalty due according to their fault. We command the same to be observed also concerning abbots presiding over the general chapter. And after a few words: We command all these things to be observed also in monasteries that do not have their own abbots but priors, and likewise in monasteries of nuns, insofar as the articles are suitable to abbesses and nuns.

From the constitutions of lord Pope Benedict XII, concerning provincial chapters, chapter 2.

We also decree and ordain that, in the order or religion of the said Black Monks, in each of the provinces assigned by us below, there shall be held, in a place to be assigned for this purpose, every three years, saving the right of the diocesan bishops, a common or provincial chapter of abbots and of priors who do not have their own abbots; and of other priors of cathedral churches of that order or religion, or of others greater in those churches after their bishops, by whatever name they are called; and of the other prelates who, as stated above, do not have their own abbots, whether exempt or not exempt, of the same order or religion. To this chapter, unless a just impediment prevents them, all the aforesaid abbots, priors, and other prelates shall assemble; and concerning the reformation of this order or religion and regular observance, they shall strive to carry out what is set forth in this constitution.
