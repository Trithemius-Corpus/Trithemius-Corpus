If it should happen that the visitors are robbed on the way while carrying out their office. Chapter 17.

Finally, if, which God forbid, visitors of this kind, while going to fulfill their office, should happen to be robbed on the way or suffer loss, the provincial chapter shall make satisfaction for such loss as is fitting, as was laid down in the fifth chapter held at Würzburg.

When the visitors first come to some monastery to be visited, how they are to begin. Chapter 18.

Therefore, when the visitors have come to visit some monastery, if it can be done, on that same evening they should summon the abbot and the convent, the monks and professed conversi, to the chapter room; or on the following morning, at the hour they have determined to be suitable. When these have assembled, they themselves should also enter. And when all have taken their seats, one of the visitors should say, “Bless.” When they answer, “The Lord,” he should add these or similar words:

“Venerable father and beloved religious brothers in Christ: if you have read the recess of the last chapter held at N., you have certainly understood that we, though insufficient, have been appointed as visitors for you. But if you have not read the recess, nevertheless we are prepared to fulfill the office enjoined upon us.

Therefore we have come for this reason: that, obedient to the precepts of the chapter according to our ability, we may visit you.

“And we do not doubt that you know that, in the order of visitation, three things are principally to be done. First, the visitors themselves must present themselves to those to be visited in the chapter room, make known their authority to visit, and first offer prayer according to the custom of the order, and an exhortation according to the character of the time and of the persons: De statu monachorum, Attendentes, in the Clementines, and in the constitution of the provincial chapter, and in the constitution Nimis carum.

“Second, there must be a diligent examination of those to be visited, and an inquiry into the conduct and state of both parties: 10, q. 1, Episcopum, and q. 3, Cavendum; De statu monachorum, chapter Eaque.

“Third, there must be a final conclusion and the due correction of the guilty: chapter In singulis.

“Therefore, since even in the smallest matters divine help must always be implored, as blessed Dionysius says in the fourth chapter of On the Divine Names, and since in spiritual matters especially one must begin from prayer, it is fitting that we too observe the commands of such teaching, and not pass over the praiseworthy custom of the order. Let us therefore pray.”

And, rising, let them read alternately the psalm Ad te levavi oculos meos:

“To you I have lifted up my eyes, you who dwell in the heavens. Behold, as the eyes of servants are on the hands of their masters, as the eyes of a maidservant are on the hands of her mistress, so are our eyes on the Lord our God, until he have mercy on us. Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us, for we are greatly filled with contempt. For our soul is greatly filled: a reproach to the prosperous and contempt to the proud. Glory be to the Father. As it was. Kyrie eleison. Christe eleison. Kyrie eleison. Our Father.”

According to the season, let them kneel. One of the visitors says:

“And lead us not into temptation. Save your servants. Send them help, O Lord, from the holy place. Be to us, O Lord, a tower of strength. O Lord God of hosts, convert us. O Lord, hear my prayer. The Lord be with you. And with your spirit.”
