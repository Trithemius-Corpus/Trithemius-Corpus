If he requires an oath contrary to the statutes of the fathers, he commits a fault of disobedience; if it is not to be required, why did the legate of the apostolic see, a cardinal and distinguished doctor, order it to be required? Can it both be required and not required? Clearly, I think so. Where it is necessary and useful, it can be required; nevertheless it ought not to be required lightly. And let him have at hand the form of such an oath, as I have also found in the older institutions of the fathers and have taken care to insert here, so that if it should be necessary there may be no need for a long inquiry.

The form of the oath and the manner of swearing, with the hand placed upon the text of the Gospel, beginning here:

O Brother M., I swear by these holy Gospels of God that concerning all matters about which I shall be questioned by you, or by any one of you, generally or specially, I wish to tell the truth; and also that all things which I know to require reform, and which are necessary for the full reform of this monastery, according to my knowledge, I will faithfully disclose to you. So help me God and these holy Gospels of God.

While saying this he shall place his hands upon the Gospel according to Luke.

At that time, while Jesus was speaking to the crowds, a certain woman, lifting up her voice, said to him: Blessed is the womb that bore you, and the breasts that nursed you. But he said: Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and keep it. Amen.

Of examination. Chapter 23.

When the visitors are prepared for the examination and inquiry and are seated in the place assigned, let them exercise great diligence in inquiries of this kind, asking into each necessary matter. And if anything worthy of correction has been disclosed, let it be marked down on a schedule by one of the visitors; let him also guard it carefully, lest it ever come into anyone else's hands. And concerning conspiracies, pacts, and oaths

let them inquire first, lest perhaps the abbot has presumed upon any such thing against the visitation. And if they find any, let them annul them, as in Extra, De accusationibus, chapter Olim.

Concerning general matters, the following, or at least some of them, can be asked.

First, let them ask about general matters as it seems good to them.

Whether the abbot has extorted an oath from the monks about keeping the truth silent, or has otherwise forbidden them to report anything.

Item, whether all are professed, and how.

Item, whether any have been received simoniacally. And if so, what has been done with the simoniacal goods.

Item, whether they observe true obedience to the abbot and to their superiors according to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

Item, whether any or all of them are proprietors.

Item, whether any are fornicators, and who.

Item, whether they are chaste, as befits religious.

Item, whether the healthy eat meat; whether all do, or some; where, when, and how often.

Item, whether they keep silence in the four prohibited places, and whether they do the same during the night hours or not.

Item, whether they observe the fasts of the Church and of the Rule, as they are bound to do.

Item, whether there is always reading at table for those who are eating.

Item, whether all eat together in one refectory.

Item, whether all sleep together in one place, and whether they are regularly clothed and girded.

Item, whether they use garments or bed-linens made of linen.

Item, whether they sleep on feather beds or mats.

Item, concerning habit and tonsure, they can be inspected and questioned.

Item, whether they continually wear the monastic habit.

Item, whether they go about outside the enclosure of the monastery without the permission of their superiors.
