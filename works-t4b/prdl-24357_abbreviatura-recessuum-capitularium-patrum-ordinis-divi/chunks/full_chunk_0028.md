For the pastor’s teaching is useful to all, but examples of virtue profit the weak and the untrained more. Examples are stronger than words, and it is better to teach by deed than by speech. We say these things, therefore, so that, shining forth in good morals, we may provide in all things a good example to those who see us. And to come briefly to the point, we exhort your fatherhoods that in every place and time, but especially in lodgings, you do good, scandalizing no one; for the suspicion of the weak is so readily turned toward worse things that the reputation of many is damaged by the scandal of one or a few, and even the praises of many scarcely know how to repair the infamy of a few.

If nothing more remains, let the fathers be dismissed to their lodgings, first advising them that on the following day all are to assemble for the Mass of the Dead. And let the notary, with the promoter of the order cooperating, write a warning against the absent in the manner of a citation, to be posted tomorrow, for greater abundance of caution, on the doors of the monastery. “Our help is in the name of the Lord.” “Who made heaven and earth.” And when these things have been said, let each go out to his lodging, except those who must remain.

Monday.

On the following day, at the appointed hour, let all assemble, and let them attend the Masses of the Dead, and whoever wishes may celebrate. When Mass has ended, let them assemble at the chapter place, and let him again read “Come, Holy Spirit,” with the prayers stated above. And let the president say, “Bless ye.” Let them answer, “The Lord.” Then, if anything was begun yesterday, let it be continued. And let the presidents entrust to the notary the writing of the chapter proceedings. After this, when the hour is suitable, let the presidents withdraw to some more private place and hear the visitors one by one concerning the office enjoined upon them, and concerning the condition of the monasteries that they visited, both in spiritual and in temporal matters, and whether in any places they were not admitted. And let them present copies of the letters

that they left in the monasteries visited by them. But if any visitors are not present, let them make a report of this kind to the fathers either in writing or through their proctors. If any abbots have been notably accused in any matter by their visitors before the presidents, let them be summoned, if it seems useful to the presidents, and let them be admonished to amend themselves henceforth in the matters charged.

Likewise, if the lord presidents cannot hear them personally, let them appoint three or four fathers to hear and receive from the treasurers themselves, in writing, an account of receipts and distributions; and when this has been reduced to writing, let them send it to be placed in the chapter chest. Then, when it is opportune, let the promoter again accuse the contumacy of those absent, and ask that they be condemned in expenses according to the constitutions of the order, under this form of words:

“Reverend fathers, today, for greater abundance of caution, I have caused another citation to be posted at the doors of the monastery against the abbots and other absent persons who are bound to attend this provincial chapter, so that they might still appear before your fatherhoods; otherwise they should know that they are bound to certain penalties. None of them has appeared or is appearing. Therefore, lest they derive advantage from their disobedience, and so that their penalty may become an example to the rest, I require and ask you, reverend lord presidents, that according to the force and form of the constitutions of Benedict XII, you deign to proceed against those absent persons, as contumacious, disobedient, and rebellious, according to the order of law, and to condemn them in expenses according to the named constitutions of the aforesaid Benedict.”
