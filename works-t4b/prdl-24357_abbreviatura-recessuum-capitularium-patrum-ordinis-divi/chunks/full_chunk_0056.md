Concerning the proctors of absentees also. Further, we moderate and mitigate the statute of the fifth chapter, which provides that no one may be proctor for more than three, decreeing that some religious person otherwise suitable may be proctor for four or even five abbots in cases of great necessity.

Let the visitors first reform themselves and their own, and only then visit others. Those appointed by the provincial chapter shall first and before all else visit and reform themselves, their monasteries, and those subject to them, lest, when they direct their eyes to the reformation of others, the sick too should murmur and say, “Physician, heal yourself.” Therefore let them begin first with themselves, and thus confidently visit and reform the monasteries entrusted to them, both of monks and of nuns.

It was further decreed concerning the report of the visitors to the presidents that the visitors, after completing the office of their visitation, shall make a faithful report in chapter to the lord presidents concerning the state of all the monasteries visited by them, both in spiritual and in temporal matters.

An oath is not to be extorted from those being visited. It also pleased us that, without demanding an oath to tell the truth, after first setting forth the word of God, the visitors themselves shall begin and complete their act according to the form prescribed for them in the sacred canons.

Likewise concerning the form of visitation. We also will and ordain that the visitors shall effectively reform, as far as they can, each and every monastery of either sex to be visited by them before they withdraw from the place being visited, according to the rule of our most blessed father Benedict, especially in the three essentials, which are obedience, continence, and poverty, without which no religious life can stand. And where they do not find sound disciplines and ceremonies, they shall establish new ones

Concerning the three essentials. We command all and each of the prelates of our province and those subject to the chapter that each of them, immediately after the notification of the present decrees, shall strive in his monastery to correct and reform himself and his monks according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, the canonical sanctions, and the statutes of the provincial chapter and of the order, especially in the three essentials, obedience, continence, and poverty; in eating and abstinence from meat; in divine worship and the other monastic disciplines, as far as he can, fearing that terrible and true sentence of our blessed father: let the abbot know that whatever the householder can find of less usefulness in the sheep falls under the fault of the shepherd.

The seventh provincial chapter was celebrated at the Monks’ Mount near Bamberg, in the year of the Lord 1429.

In the year of the Lord 1429, in the seventh indiction, in the twelfth year of the pontificate of lord Martin, pope the fifth, on the tenth day of the month of April, which was the Sunday Misericordia Domini, our seventh provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saint Michael of the Monks’ Mount near Bamberg. In it the venerable fathers, the lord abbots Georgius in the castle of Saint Peter at Erfurt, Heinrich of Saint Giles at Nuremberg, and Hugo in Alpirsbach, presided.

Concerning the approved statutes of the ancients. First, we approve and renew the old statutes, and we wish all of them, insofar as they were duly and lawfully issued, to be observed inviolably by those subject to our chapter.
