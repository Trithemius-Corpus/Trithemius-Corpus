Let those already more strictly reformed not wish, under pretext of this form, to relax themselves to a more lenient life. Yet we admonish and exhort in the Lord Jesus those who have thus far begun, in holy religion, the observance of a stricter life, that they persevere in good beginnings and make greater progress from day to day; nor, declining from their purpose under pretext of this norm, should they turn themselves toward more lenient things, since the constitution of this bull was issued as a beginning of regular life for those who up to now have been more negligent, until the good God grants them greater gifts.

The aforesaid bull also commands here that in each monastery it be kept, collated, in good and legible script, and that once in each quarter it be read publicly in the convent, as above in the preceding chapter.

Concerning the proctors of the visitors.

Furthermore, it pleased us, and we have decreed, that the appointed proctors of those absent should observe whether the visitors have fulfilled their visitations, and should report those who are negligent to the nearer presidents, so that, by ecclesiastical censure and other penalties if necessary, they may compel them to fulfill their office.

Let no one be proctor for more than three.

We also forbid anyone henceforth to dare to be proctor for more than three, except because of evident necessity, under a penalty to be imposed by the lord presidents.

Concerning the travel allowance of three florins to be paid to the visitors.

The whole community of the fathers decreed that each abbot who has been visited shall pay his visitors four Rhenish florins for travel allowance, unless the visitors themselves, for good reason, wish to receive less; on this point we burden their consciences.

Let prelates not absent themselves from chapter.

We also earnestly forbid, under the penalties contained in the Benedictina, that prelates who are obliged to come to the provincial chapter should presume henceforth to absent themselves from such a chapter.

We will and ordain that, when their office has expired, the presidents shall make a full and clear account to the new presidents concerning all and several receipts and expenditures on behalf of the chapter.

The twelfth provincial chapter, celebrated at Erfurt at St. Peter’s for the second time, in the year of the Lord 1444.

In the year of the Lord 1444, on the tenth day of the month of May, which was Cantate Sunday, the twelfth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of St. Peter at Erfurt. There presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots of St. Peter there, of St. Gall, of St. Giles at Nuremberg, and of St. Stephen at Würzburg. In it proctors were appointed who should accuse the negligence of the visitors before the presidents.

Concerning the old statutes here renewed and repeated.

In this sacred chapter, as also in the two immediately preceding ones, the bull of the Council of Basel concerning reform was accepted in the same words as above. And it was provided that it should be kept and read in each monastery four times a year, as above in the preceding chapter. And that brothers of the stricter life should not wish, under pretext of this bull, to descend to more lenient things, but should persevere by setting out better, as in the same place. The statute concerning the authority of the presidents over rebels, and concerning the negligence of the visitors to be investigated by proctors, is set down here in precisely the same words as it is found above in the same tenth chapter, with certain other matters.

Royal monasteries are to be visited by the presidents.

Royal monasteries are henceforth to be visited by one of the presidents, with the visitors of that diocese associated with him, as above in chapter ten.

Concerning the monasteries of nuns to be visited.

But also the monasteries of nuns in each diocese are to be visited by the visitors of that diocese; and each monastery visited shall pay its visitors, when they depart, three Rhenish florins for travel allowance.
