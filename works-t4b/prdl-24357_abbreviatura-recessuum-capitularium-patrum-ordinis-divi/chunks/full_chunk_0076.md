Likewise, in the chapter itself, when great and difficult matters are to be treated, the votes of each one must be carefully sought out; and each person must state his vote according to God and conscience, with humility and purity of intention, and must give the reason moving him.

Furthermore, it has been ordained how those present ought to conduct themselves there in their conversation: namely, that all, both abbots and the procurators of abbots, ought to strive for conformity of manners, especially in habit and tonsure; and no one is henceforth to use a bare biretta, but is to place the hood of the cowl itself under the biretta, as above, chapter 16. Likewise, during the three days over which the chapter continues, all ought to come together to one table, so that they may sit together and be refreshed together. There only two dishes of fish are to be served, one of eggs or fried foods, and two good pottages, with cheese or some fruits added, as above, chapter 7. The treasurers, however, ought to advance the expenses, and then collect them again from the contribution of the individual abbots, as above, chapter 9.

Likewise, there also the abbots and the procurators of abbots ought to present notices in which the names of the dead from the next preceding chapter are contained, so that in chapter they may be absolved, that is, by way of prayer, and prayers may be made for them. The form of these notices is as follows: Brother N., priest or levite, died on such a day in that place, etc., as above, chapter 4.

Concerning the presidents. Rubric Three.

The presidents who are to preside in the future chapter must, according to the ordinance of chapter 16, which was drawn from the constitutions of Benedict XII, be chosen by the presidents of the preceding chapter; and, according to the ordinance of chapter 13, only from among the reformed. Further, in that same future chapter over which they preside, they must choose, just as their predecessors did, all the officials of the chapter, namely the presidents, visitors, etc., and do the other things contained in the constitutions of the aforementioned Benedict XII. For they have authority in chapter to preside, to hear, inquire into, and discuss faults, and to impose penalties and penances according to the measure

of the faults; to compel negligent visitors, under ecclesiastical censures, to execute faithfully the office enjoined upon them; to determine, in certain cases, an increase of the subsidy or travel allowance that is owed to the visitors by the monasteries visited by them, beyond three florins; to make ordinances concerning brothers to be sent to study; and to license brothers who, with pious intention, desire to pass from an unreformed monastery to a reformed one.

Against those who do not appear in chapter, and against all who hinder, oppose, or refuse to admit reformations and visitations, or in any other way act contrary to them, and also against those who do not obey the visitors, and against those who harm monasteries in goods, persons, etc., they have authority to proceed by censures, their authority applying jointly and severally. They also have authority, and are bound, to visit exempt and royal monasteries in person together with the visitors. Likewise, they have authority from Martin V to change, for certain reasons, the place appointed for holding the chapter, even with a prorogation of the term. The same aforementioned pope also grants that, if anyone elected president is unwilling to accept the office or cannot conveniently be present, then the other presidents, with the consent of the greater part of those making up the chapter, or, if none of the presidents is present, the others assembled for the celebration of the chapter, may choose and appoint other suitable persons as presidents for those occasions.

Concerning the chest and the keepers of the chest. Rubric Four.

A common chest is recognized as always necessary for the benefit of the chapter, so that in it may be placed the monetary contributions, the penalties for contumacy or for any other fault of the abbots, and also the other things that are received, either by the visitors or by the treasurers, as pertaining to the necessity or benefit of the chapter. But it is also necessary that this chest be kept in a safe and suitable place under trustworthy custodians, who are called treasurers. They have full power to exact penalties, under censures, from those who have incurred penalties, and likewise to absolve any who may perhaps have been excommunicated on account of the aforesaid matters.
