Statute of lord Ludovicus, cardinal priest of the title of Saint Cecilia of the Roman Church, of Arles, legate de latere deputed to the parts of Germany by the sacred general synod of Basel, lawfully assembled in the Holy Spirit and representing the universal Church: that during the time of the chapter the divine offices may be celebrated even in a place under interdict; and that the fathers, presidents, and visitors may absolve persons subject to the chapter from all censures and penalties, confirm elections, confer the office of benediction, and crown nuns. And this statute has force when those to whom confirmation belongs, after being solemnly required a third time before a public notary and witnesses, with no reasonable cause preventing them, have maliciously refused or neglected to confirm the same election without charge, according to the decrees of the aforesaid synod. Likewise, if those to whom this belongs by right, having been similarly required, have refused or neglected to bless abbots or abbesses, to confer upon them the office of benediction, and to crown nuns, or also to bless them according to the accustomed manner, without charge and according to the decrees just mentioned. Done at Frankfurt, in the year of the Lord 1446.

A brief and summary recapitulation, or epilogue, of all the matters aforesaid: in what order and manner individuals ought to come together at the provincial chapter.

First rubric.

When any chapter is to be celebrated, those who are officials for the time being, namely the presidents, visitors, treasurers, and the one making the sermon there according to the ordinance of the seventeenth chapter, and certain others, ought to precede the chapter itself by four days, so that they may be present there on Wednesday and may more conveniently inform themselves, both about matters already transacted and about matters to be handled in their offices, and may at last be able to dispatch them more quickly. This should be understood of the presidents of each chapter, namely of the one to be celebrated as well as of the preceding one, as appears above, chapter 20. Concerning the one making the sermon, it has been ordained that his sermon first be examined, so that, if need be, it may be corrected and amended. The rest, however, whether abbots or procurators, ought to present themselves there, so that when the chapter is to be opened, no one may be found absent. Nor is the excuse of anyone to be admitted, except either of one who is ill, or who has deadly enmities, or certainly is so poor that he cannot conveniently present himself at the chapter without danger.
