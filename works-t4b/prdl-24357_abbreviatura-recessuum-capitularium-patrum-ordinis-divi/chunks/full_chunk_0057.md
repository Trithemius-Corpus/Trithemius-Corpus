And only two dishes of fish are to be served, one of eggs or fried foods, and two good soups, with cheese added, and some fruits if they can be had.

Concerning lords.

By the authority of the presidents we establish and ordain, with all the fathers present consenting, that each of the presidents shall have full power and authority, acting jointly and severally, to carry out execution and to proceed, according to the tenor of the Benedictine constitutions, against each and every person of either sex who opposes those visiting them, or does not admit them, or impedes them, or otherwise hinders them in any way; and also against those who offend monasteries in persons or goods in any way. And that they shall compel visitors to exact on behalf of the chapter, in accordance with the Benedictina, from the monasteries visited the contribution or the penalties for contumacy from abbots who have neglected to come to the chapter.

Visitors. Concerning the time for completing visitation.

The visitors shall faithfully complete within the year the office of visitation enjoined upon them, and shall make a report about it to the lord presidents as quickly as they can. Rebels are to be denounced by the visitors to one of the presidents.

It was decreed that, if any persons resist the visitors, they are to be denounced to the nearer president, so that by apostolic authority he may proceed against such rebels, and also against those who do not obey the visitors, by ecclesiastical censure according to the measure of their faults and rebellion.

Concerning certain statutes placed in the preceding chapter, which in this one were repeated word for word.

In the next preceding chapter certain statutes were issued, which in this one also were precisely repeated and inserted word for word into the recess, namely: that the visitors should first take care to reform themselves and their own; likewise that, after the word of God has been set forth, they should proceed without exacting an oath; and concerning the full reform to be introduced by them. Likewise, the mandate made to the prelates concerning the reform of themselves and their own. All these are found in the preceding chapter.

The eighth provincial chapter was celebrated at Saint Ulrich’s in Augsburg, in the year of the Lord 1432.

In the year of the Lord 1432, in the tenth indiction, in the second year of the pontificate of lord Eugene IV, pope, on the eleventh day of the month of May, which was Jubilate Sunday, the eighth provincial chapter was celebrated in the monastery of Saints Ulrich and Afra in the city of Augsburg. In it the venerable fathers presided: the lord abbots Ortwin of Saint Peter’s at Erfurt, Johannes in Stein, of the diocese of Constance, and Heinrich in Amorbach, of Mainz. In this chapter provision was made concerning persons to be sent for study.

The old statutes.

First, with common consent, they renew and approve what had been approved. We approve, ratify, and renew each and all the statutes issued in the preceding chapters, and we wish them to be inviolably observed by all subject to our chapter, so far as each one is concerned. These statutes, set down several times above, were here again set down.

Finally, in this chapter almost all the statutes of the two immediately preceding chapters were repeated in the same words and inserted into the recess. Therefore, to avoid tedium, it is enough to note them briefly. First, the statute that the fathers coming to the chapter should live together in the refectory throughout those three days, and concerning the number of dishes, as above, chapter 7. Likewise, the statute concerning visitors, that they should first reform themselves and their own; and that, beginning from the word of God, they should fulfill their office without exacting an oath, as above, chapters 6 and 7. A mandate was also given to the abbots that they should take care to reform themselves and their own in three especially substantial matters, concerning abstinence from meats and the other matters issued above in chapters 6 and 7; they are similarly set down here. A statute, moreover, concerning the authority of the lord presidents, as above, chapters 6 and 7, is similarly set down here in the same words.

that within the year they shall faithfully and effectively complete the office of visitation enjoined upon them, and shall make a report to the nearer president as quickly as they can.

That mendicants are not to be received into the order.

We forbid all abbots and prelates of our chapter, under penalty of excommunication, to receive anyone from the order of mendicants into obedience; and those already received, who are wandering about, they are to bring back to their monasteries as quickly as possible, as in chapters 2 and 3, and as has often been established in many other places.

Concerning fugitive monks.

Under the same penalty of excommunication we command all abbots and prelates subject to our provincial chapter to gather back their fugitive monks; nor henceforth are they to give anyone permission to stay in parishes or to govern them, unless those parishes are annexed to the monasteries.

What sort of persons abbots ought to be toward their subjects.

Just as the madness of wolves must be driven off by the barking of dogs and the shepherds’ staff, we command all abbots subject to the chapter of our province that, following the example of the good shepherd, Jesus Christ, whose place they are seen to hold in the monasteries, they should kindly foster and prudently defend their monks and subjects, not lording it over the clergy, but becoming an example to the flock.
