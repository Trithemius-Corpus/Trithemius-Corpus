On the propriety of outward habit. From chapter 1. Rubric 11.

We ordain and establish that all are to wear large and broad tonsures, so that the circle of them does not exceed the space of two transverse fingers; and that they conduct themselves properly in clothing, habits, and footwear, according to the Clementine Ne in agro and the constitution of the aforesaid Pope Benedict.

Concerning the form and propriety of garments: namely, that the brothers are to wear tunics and garments next beneath the outer habit, of black, brown, or white color, according to the custom usually observed among them; round, ankle-length, not slit, nor marked by any other deformity; having broad sleeves extending to the wrist, in no way buttoned or knotted, yet not so broad that their width above the wrist, with the thumb raised, can be stretched out. Nor should they extend around the elbow-opening, as is the abuse of certain persons, farther than so that the arm can easily be turned and inserted within.

They are to be content with almuces of skins in place of hoods. Yet with the abbot’s permission they may use decent hoods in places where they are accustomed to wear them, slit over the shoulders for a length of at least four transverse fingers.

For linings in garments they are not to wear sendal, or miniver, or other precious materials, but are to use only the skins of lambs or young lambs for the said linings, or at least other non-precious cloths; nor are the linings to appear in any way above the collar or at the ends of the garments or sleeves. Furthermore, when they have to go outside the monastery they may wear a frock, cowl, or cloak, and beneath the cloak, if they wear it, a cowl or scapular. The lay brothers are to use a cowl within the monastery, cut open on both sides, and outside the monastery; and the boys are to use a scapular with a hood attached to it both within and outside the monastery.

But because some use the aforesaid habits more ample and long, while others use them less so, for that reason we have thought fit to insert in the present statutes the breadth and length of the aforesaid garments, which we wish to be observed.

And first, beginning with the hood of the frock, we ordain that its breadth is not to reach the elbow-opening by the thickness of two transverse fingers, nor may it be narrower or so short that it does not extend as far as the middle of the elbow and of the upper bone near the shoulder. The said hood, however, is to extend behind at least as far as the belt, but may not extend beyond it by as much as four fingers. The length of the sleeves is to extend one cubit beyond the hand, and their width is not to exceed another cubit and a half. Yet below it is to be so broad that steps may easily be taken within it. And what has been said about the frock we wish also to be understood of the cowl, the sleeves being excepted, since it has none.

Within the monastery, in places where there are twelve monks, all are to use the frock. But in other places where there is not so large a congregation, they may use cowls; and if they wear these, no one is to use the frock, except only the president of the monastery and the first after him, whom we wish always to wear the frock. Yet in duties, namely in the divine office beneath the alb, and when riding beneath the cloak, they may use a scapular, which is to have at least the breadth of half a cubit.

They are not to use linen sheets and canopies, but are to wear either a woolen or a linen garment in place of the aforesaid shirts. And they are to wear boots or shoes, or high laced shoes, so that their height covers on the leg a space of four fingers above the ankle. If they have been negligent in any of the aforesaid matters, or if within three months after they have come to their monasteries, as stated above, they have not complied, then, beyond the penalties imposed by law, we wish them to be punished by their superior in religion, or, if they have no superior, by the aforesaid visitors, in such a way that it may serve as an example to the rest.
