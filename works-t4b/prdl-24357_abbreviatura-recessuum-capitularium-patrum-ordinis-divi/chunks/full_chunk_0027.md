And if the presidents wish to give any further admonition, let them do so, especially if there are any whose tonsure differs from the others, so that after lunch they may see to it that they are properly shaved. Then let the president conclude: “Our help is in the name of the Lord.” Let the others answer: “Who made heaven and earth.” And so let them go. But before they are dismissed, let all be admonished that at the first or second signal of the bell they are all to be present again in the chapter place; and, if it seems good to the presidents, let some penalty be imposed on those who arrive late.

After lunch.

When the bell has been rung and the fathers have assembled together, let them make the customary prayer, with the president beginning it, which ought to be done on bended knees before every capitular act, as follows:

“Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love, you who through diversity, etc. Alleluia, alleluia. Kyrie eleison. Kyrie eleison. Our Father.”

Let one of the presidents, adding to this, say: “And lead us not into temptation.” Let the others answer: “But deliver us.”

Versicle: “Save your servants.”
Response: “My God, who hope in you.”

Versicle: “Send them, O Lord, help from the sanctuary.”
Response: “And from Sion protect them.”

Versicle: “Be for us, O Lord, a tower of strength.”
Response: “Before the face of the enemy.”

Versicle: “O Lord God of hosts, convert us.”
Response: “And show us your face, and we shall be saved.”

Versicle: “O Lord, hear my prayer.”
Response: “And let my cry come to you.”

Versicle: “The Lord be with you.”

Let us pray.

“Stretch out, O Lord, over your faithful the right hand of heavenly help, that they may seek you with their whole heart, and may deserve to obtain the things they worthily ask.

“O Lord, we ask you to go before our petitions by inspiring them, and to follow them by helping them, so that all our action may always begin from you and, once begun, may be brought to completion through you.

“O God, who taught the hearts of the faithful by the illumination of the Holy Spirit, grant us in the same Spirit to know what is right, and always to rejoice in his consolation. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.”

These things are to be said by the president who gives the address, with the others answering. After this let the same president say: “Give the blessing.” Let the others answer: “The Lord.” And let him add:

“If any one of you, reverend fathers, wishes to put forward anything for the common good of the chapter, of the order, and of its persons, let him confidently set it forth with fear of the Lord, reverence, and humility, and we will give him a hearing with all patience. But if anything necessary has been proposed, let the presidents, together with the definitors, have mature deliberation as to what is to be done in each matter.”

The presidents are also sometimes accustomed to ask all the fathers in order, according to the requirement of the cases that arise: “If any fathers have complaints to put forward against anyone, which could be met by us, let them put them forward humbly and without disturbance, and in like manner we will patiently hear them.”

And then let him add afterward:

“Likewise, if there are any brothers sent on behalf of their communities with testimonial letters to put forward something against anyone, let them present the letters and explain the causes; we will also hear them patiently and kindly.”

After this, if there are any matters to be treated capitularly, let them be treated. And let one of the fathers be appointed to sing Mass on the next day for the brothers. Then a brief exhortation may be made to the fathers on how they ought to conduct themselves in lodgings and in every place, in this or a similar manner:

“Venerable fathers, as you heard when it was read today from the Rule of Saint Benedict, according to which we are bound to live: he who is appointed abbot over brothers ought to instruct them in holy doctrine and to go before them with examples of good works. Unless he shows both to his disciples, he will undoubtedly receive a fearful sentence in the strict examination of God’s judgment. For he is believed to act in Christ’s place in the monastery; therefore he must follow Christ himself.
