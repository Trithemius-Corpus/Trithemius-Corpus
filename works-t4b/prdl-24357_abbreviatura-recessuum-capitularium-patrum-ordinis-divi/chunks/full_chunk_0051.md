We most strictly forbid anyone who hereafter wishes to enter this religious order to promise any money for his reception, either by himself or through others, or otherwise to pledge that he will give anything, except those things that are offered at the altar by parents of their free will, without any agreement with the boy. And those received in this way, when the year of probation has elapsed, shall promise to live according to the Rule of Saint Benedict, and to fulfill and observe it according to the tenor of the vow of profession and promise.

On the celebration of Masses.

On the daily chapter of faults.

Furthermore, we will that, in accordance with the constitution of Pope Benedict XII, the daily chapter be observed, in which discipline and regular correction are to be treated; and there too they are to carry out all things just as the aforesaid Pope Benedict established.

That collapsed conventual buildings be repaired.

We also will, and most strictly command by virtue of holy obedience, that in monasteries in which perhaps refectories, infirmaries, or dormitories have collapsed or otherwise been destroyed, they be repaired; and those negligent persons to whom such repair belongs are to be gravely punished by the visitors.

That women not have entry into monasteries of monks.

We altogether forbid entry to the monasteries, choirs, and cloisters to the female sex, commanding that all doors of those monasteries of the said order be closed as the visitors shall have arranged.

We likewise establish concerning the same matter that in monasteries, or within their precincts, in the houses in which monks live, no one shall presume to keep women, even if they are mothers or sisters. Those who do the contrary are to be duly punished according to the fault.

That mendicants not be received into our order, and that those already received who wander be recalled to their monasteries.

We forbid mendicants hereafter to be received into our order; and those already received are to be recalled to their cloisters

and instructed and reformed according to the Rule and the statutes of our order.

On the cloister prior.

We will and strictly command, by virtue of holy obedience, that in each monastery there be appointed a provident and discreet prior, as suitable a man as can be found in the congregation of the monastery. Let him be such a man as is capable in work and useful in speech, able to exercise care of the cloister and governance in spiritual matters, who diligently observes all the aforesaid articles insofar as they concern him, causes them to be observed by all, and has vigilant and faithful care in spiritual matters concerning the discipline of the choir, dormitory, refectory, and the other things as described above. And if difficulty arises in any matter, let him refer it to the abbot, who is to examine and judge it.

On baths.

Because the use of baths is sometimes necessary for monks, we will that every abbot, or the one to whom it belongs by office, shall make and build a bathhouse in his monastery, so that the way of wandering away may be closed off to the monks.

On conspiracies, games of dice, and betrayals of the secrets of chapter.

Furthermore, we forbid all conspiracies against abbots and also against private persons, defamations of brothers, every betrayal of a secret of chapter or council, and every game of dice, knucklebones, chess, and other games and disorders by whatever name they are called.

That monks not become godfathers.

Furthermore, according to the ancient decrees of the holy fathers, we establish and forbid monks to become godfathers, as in De consecratione, distinction 4, Non licet abbati, and chapter 16, question 1, Placuit communi.

That the ornaments and books of divine worship be kept clean.

Furthermore, with this sacred chapter approving, it has been decreed and established that churches, oratories, altars, vessels, corporals, vestments, and other ecclesiastical ornaments be kept clean and bright. And provision is to be made in every way for breviaries, missals, and other books, vestments, chalices, and any ornaments necessary for divine worship.
