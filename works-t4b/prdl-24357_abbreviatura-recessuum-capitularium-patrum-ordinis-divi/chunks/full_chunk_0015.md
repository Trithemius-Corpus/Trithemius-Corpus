That those visited should provide their visitors with safe-conduct. Chapter 30.

If there is need, the abbots and convents that have been visited shall provide their visitors, at their own expense, with safe-conduct from one visited monastery to another to be visited at the visitors’ discretion, as has been established in many provincial chapters.

That visitors should not reveal to anyone the secrets of those visited. Chapter 31.

Let the visitors take care not to presume to reveal to anyone, especially to laypeople, matters entrusted to them, or even discovered by them, in visitations; nor indeed to their brothers or to any familiars whatever. For what each person does not wish to be done to himself, let him not do to another. For he who reveals secrets is fraudulent, 5, question 5, “He who walks.” And in the council of Pope Benedict XII, chapter 3, it says: “And because it proceeds from the virtue of prudence if a way is closed to scandals, we forbid the aforesaid visitors to presume to reveal to others those persons, or their names, who have revealed any secrets to them in visitations, except only when, on account of revelations of this kind, it will be necessary to proceed to the punishment or penalty of someone. But those who reveal such things otherwise are to be punished more or less severely, according as the quality of the deed shall require, at the discretion of the presidents.” This is found there.

This brief form of visitation, drawn from the writings and statutes of the fathers for the direction of myself and those like me when there is need, as best I could, I, brother Johannes, abbot in Sponheim in the diocese of Mainz, have written out and humbly offer to the lord presidents of the provincial chapter, to be examined and corrected. If they approve it as to be observed in visitations, I shall not be deprived of the fruit of my labor; but if they reject it, by subjecting myself patiently and humbly I shall be granted the reward of humility. In the year of the Lord 1490.

From the constitutions of Pope Benedict XII concerning visitors. Chapter 3.

Moreover, the visitors who, according to the aforesaid constitution of that general council, shall have been appointed in such provincial chapters, are to be suitable, diligent, and discreet in carrying out the office of their visitation; and they are to observe everything that is known to have been established in the aforesaid constitution of that council and in the other constitution of the said Honorius, our predecessor.

But if those visitors omit to carry these things out, or do so negligently, we will that by that very fact they incur the penalties incurred by those who do not come to the provincial chapter and have no canonical impediment.

And because we desire that the conduct of the aforesaid visitors proceed with complete sincerity, we decree that no one may be the visitor of the visitor appointed to him in the same year.

The aforesaid visitors, or their familiars, shall in no way remain more than two days in a cathedral church, monastic church, monastery, or other place of that order or religion to which they have chosen to turn aside for the sake of visitation, unless evident necessity or some great advantage of the church, monastery, or place of this kind should require a longer stay there by the visitors themselves or by their servants or familiars. But then they shall do this with the counsel of the elders of the aforesaid church, monastery, or place.

Nor shall the visitors or their familiars be burdensome and oppressive to those churches, monasteries, and places visited. They shall not seek sumptuous meals, but with thanksgiving shall receive what has been honestly and suitably provided for them.

And the said visitors and their familiars shall receive no money on this occasion or in any other way; nor shall they themselves, or any of their people, presume to ask for any gift, whatever it may be, or however it may be offered, so that they may not seem to seek the things that are their own, but those of Christ.
