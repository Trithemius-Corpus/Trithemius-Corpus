*How visitors are to be received and treated; on the time for visiting monasteries, to be fixed by the presidents. Chapter 11.*

The time is to be fixed for the visitors by the presidents themselves, as is said in Constitution 12 of Benedict, chapter 2. Nevertheless, we find it prescribed in the more ancient statutes of the fathers that, within a year from the time of the chapter that has been celebrated, they are to fulfill the office of the visitation committed to them. It belongs to bishops, however, to visit every year: 10, question 1, *Relata*, and chapter *Decrevimus*. Concerning the time of visitation, in chapter 7, celebrated at Bamberg, it was enacted as follows: “Let the visitors faithfully complete within a year the office of visitation enjoined upon them.” Thus there.

And how often ought visitation to be made? It seems that every year, as in Extra, *De statu monachorum*, *Attendentes*, in the Clementines. On this Franciscus de Zabarella frames the following question: “I ask,” he says, “concerning the point where it says that visitation is to be made every year: is it enough that it be made at the end of the first year and at the beginning of the second?” The gloss on whether that is enough refers to the note on the same title, chapter *In singulis*, where it is concluded that it is not, because in that way, in the case of chapter *In singulis*, it could be deferred up to six years, to the prejudice of the monastery, and it would not satisfy the law requiring that it be done from three years to three years. Stephen says the same, because visitation is made for correcting offenses already committed, not for offenses yet to be committed. Therefore, if the first was sufficient, the second will have nothing to correct. Paul says the same, because if this were done at the end of the first year and at the beginning of the second, there would not be one year between the first and the second, but two years. Hence it seems that they ought to stand at equal intervals, by the argument from *De consanguinitate*, *Quod dilectio*, as Paul thinks. This is precisely true with respect to chapter *In singulis*, because the text there says “from three years to three years.” Thus Franciscus de Zabarella on the said chapter.

From these things it seems one must say that the visitation ought to correspond to the chapter, so that as often as the chapter is celebrated, so often too the visitation is celebrated. We also have this in custom, that we regularly visit from three years to three years. This, however,

Can a visitation be repeated? The answer is yes, if there is a reasonable cause. On this matter, in chapter 17, celebrated at Nuremberg for the third time, the fathers decreed as follows: “We enact that after the visitors have once visited some monastery and received procuration from the same, if they judge, for the richer discipline of regular observance, that it is expedient, they may exercise the office of visitation again, as often as it seems useful to them, by authority of the chapter. They are also to be received as true visitors, yet without exaction of procuration.” Franciscus de Zabarella says the same in the said chapter *Attendentes*, question 10: that visitation is to be made as often as there is need. From all these things I conclude that the visitation must be completed in the first year from the time of the chapter that has been celebrated, and afterward may be repeated as often as there is need, but that procuration should not be received except within a three-year period.

*How visitors are to be received and treated in monasteries. Chapter 12.*

When, therefore, they come to some monastery for the sake of visitation, let them be received as visitors and be led to prayer; if they are not neighbors and often received, let their feet be washed, and let them be treated with charity. In conventual places, let the abbot of the house yield place and acknowledge them; let him not take it ill if he has others set over him: 2, question 6, *Qui scit*. Yet the abbot himself, in his usual manner, is to give the blessing everywhere, not the visitors. For this has been enacted in several chapters: that visitors are to be received with honor and reverence, and treated in all things with charity, as is fitting.
