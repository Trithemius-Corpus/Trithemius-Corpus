The thirteenth provincial chapter, held for the second time in the monastery of Petershausen at Constance, in the year 1447.

In the year of the Lord 1447, on the seventh day of the month of May, which was the Sunday Cantate, the fourteenth provincial chapter was held in the monastery of Petershausen at Constance. In it there presided the venerable fathers, the lord abbots of Petershausen there, of Saint Giles of Nuremberg, of Saint Stephen of Wuerzburg, and of Saint Gotthard of Hildesheim.

On old statutes here renewed and repeated.

First of all, it receives the oft-mentioned bull of the Council of Basel, and commands in the same words and form as above that it be held and read in every monastery. Several other statutes also were placed here in the recess, though they have been determined several times in the preceding chapters, namely concerning the authority of the lord presidents against rebels; that the visitors of each diocese should visit the monasteries of nuns; and concerning the negligence of visitors to be reported to the presidents by the procurators, along with many others that are fully contained above here and there.

That only the reformed are to be appointed to the offices of the provincial chapter.

As the Lord says in the Gospel: if the blind leads the blind, both fall into the ditch. We establish that, as far as can be done, this rule should be observed in the election of presidents and visitors: that the reformed be appointed to such offices, for one who has neglected first to reform himself can never well instruct others.

On the negligence of visitors.

And because we have learned by experience that some visitors appointed up to now have been negligent and have not taken care to fulfill the office enjoined upon them, therefore we will and command all visitors that they take care to fulfill the office of their visitation with all possible diligence.

Otherwise, according to the tenor of the Benedictine letter, we will and declare that they, and each of them, incur by the very fact the penalties that are incurred by those who do not come to the provincial chapter without having a canonical impediment.

Who are excused from the chapter.

It pleased the fathers that no one henceforth should put forward any excuse for not coming to the chapter except bodily illness, enmities, or evident and plainly established poverty. And only for these reasons are the procuratorial letters of absent abbots to be admitted in chapter.

On wandering monks not to be received.

Wandering monks without commendatory letters from their abbot are not to be kept or received as conventuals, nor are they to be hosted in any monastery beyond three days.

On a monk who has laid violent hands on an abbot.

If any monk, at the devil’s persuasion, has broken out into such audacity that he lays violent hands on an abbot or another brother, such a person is not to be accepted or kept in the province.

On one who wishes to pass over from an unreformed monastery.

If anyone, with pious intention, wishes to pass from an unreformed monastery to a monastery, he shall seek permission from his superior. If the superior refuses, he shall seek it from one of the presidents, who will give him permission to stay there for a time, or even to establish himself there, but with the consent of those receiving him.

On the safe-conduct and travel expenses of visitors.

Every abbot or prelate who has been visited, with the help of his convent, shall conduct his visitors as far as the next monastery to be visited by them. When they wish to depart, he shall not refuse to give them three Rhenish florins for the contribution or collection imposed by the chapter for relieving its burdens, unless the visitors themselves, for a pious reason, have decreed that less should be received.
