This citation, as has been said above, shall be affixed under vespers or compline to the doors of the monastery or church.

After dinner on Monday.

If the matters written above can be completed before dinner, well and good. If not, let them complete them in the vacant time, as shall seem appropriate to all the presidents. Likewise, let the recesses of the last chapter be read, and if any commissions were made to anyone, let the execution of them be examined. Then, if nothing remains, let the fathers be dismissed to their lodgings, to return tomorrow at the sixth hour at the ringing.

The third day.

On Tuesday morning let the bell be rung, and let a Mass of the blessed Virgin Mary be sung solemnly by one of the fathers appointed for it on the preceding evening, at which all the others are to be present. When it is finished, let them go to the chapter, and let the customary prayer be said, “Come, Holy Spirit,” etc. And let one of the presidents say, “Bless.” And when the answer has been given, “The Lord,” let him continue if anything was omitted yesterday. Likewise, afterward let attention be turned to the order, and if anything else is newly to be established, let it be inserted this day in the recess. Likewise, if any answers are to be given or written to princes or to others, let them be made and dispatched with the counsel of the fathers. Let some be appointed, if the treasurers are not present, who shall collect the expenses, namely three Rhenish florins from each abbot, on this day, marking down what has been collected on a schedule, lest someone perhaps, which God forbid, pretend that he has given what he has not given.

Likewise, let some be assigned to assist the notary, who know how to arrange the recess, so that by the evening after dinner it may be complete. Likewise, if the necessity of the order requires it, let a proportionate contribution be imposed on all monasteries, and let the cause and form be inserted in the recess, which all are bound to pay under the penalties to be expressed in the same, according to the constitutions of Benedict, pope XII. And it should be noted that the lord presidents, with the definitors, can impose a contribution on others according to the Benedictine constitution without their consent; nor, however, are they themselves free from such a contribution. For what they impose on others they themselves ought first to bear. That it is theirs to impose a contribution is shown clearly in the same constitutions, in these words in chapter five:

“We decree and ordain that in the same chapters, notwithstanding any contradiction or absence, for liberties, necessities, utilities, common expenses, and other customary matters, since a burden divided into parts is more lightly borne, collections, subsidies, or contributions are to be made, imposed, and assessed by apostolic authority, according to the judgment of the presidents or definitors, for such a provincial chapter.”

Meanwhile also, if it has seemed good to the lord presidents, let the common expenses of the chapter be assessed with the father of the house, as is customary.

After dinner.

When dinner has been completed, at the signal of the bell let them assemble at once, as above; and when the president has said, “Bless,” and the answer has been given, “The Lord,” let the chapter be continued. Then, at the command of the presidents, let the recess be read, which ought now to be complete. At its end it should contain the place and day of the future chapter, and the other things that ought customarily to be done. And it should be noted that no more than two years ought to elapse between the chapter that has been celebrated and the one to be celebrated, according to the constitution of Benedict.
