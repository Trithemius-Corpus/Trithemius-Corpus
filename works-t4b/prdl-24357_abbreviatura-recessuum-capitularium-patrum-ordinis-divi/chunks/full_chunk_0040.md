Let refreshment without grumbling suffice you. The eating of meat, except in the case of illness according to the permission of the Rule, and also the acceptance of any offered gift, even one not suspect, shall in no way be lawful for you.

The form of visitation and the manner of proceeding.

With gentleness preserve the censure of discipline, correcting crimes and excesses without any acceptance of persons; strive to extend the offices of charity and kindness to all. Take care to recall those at discord to concord, diligently exhorting prelates to solicitude and vigilance concerning the souls entrusted to them, and to consider how much more perfect they ought to be than others, to whom they have promised that they would teach the way of perfection; how grave a danger hangs over them, since in the strict examination of the last judgment they will be bound to render an exact account for each and all of those entrusted to them; and that all, both prelates and subjects, should fulfill their profession, solemnly made in the person of the prelate before God, who does not deceive, and the vows that their lips have uttered, without which it is impossible for them to be saved; likewise, how sweet is the yoke of the Lord and light his burden to all of good will.

But because among very many the conversations of exhortation make little progress unless the censure of coercion also comes in, therefore in the monasteries or places that you shall visit for the time being, after the customary conference of salutary exhortation has first been given, in order to complete the act of visitation, once a corporal oath has been received from all that they will speak the truth in their examination, you shall proceed according to the tenor of the commission delivered to you by our other letters, even through the coercion of the censures and penalties expressed therein, establishing, ordaining, and carrying out whatever will be opportune there for introducing and continuing regular observance and discipline.

Take particular care that the prelates of the monasteries that you shall visit are so instructed and imbued by the ministry of this visitation of yours that, in decent habit and tonsure,

in honesty of life, gravity of morals, the word of doctrine, and the example of holiness, they may preside over their subjects laudably and fruitfully, and correct the excesses of their subjects according to canonical and regular statutes. Let them recall all their fugitive and absent monks to their own monastery as soon as they are able, within a suitable term fixed for them by us for this purpose, except only those absent monks who are bound to the care of annexed parish churches, so that they may live there under regular discipline and observance.

Let all things be common to abbots, priors, and provosts, and indeed to one another, in fraternal charity.

Let no monk be permitted to use the damnable private ownership of things or goods acquired in any way whatever, but let all things be brought back to common use.

Concerning the vice of incontinence.

Against the incontinent, both prelates and subjects, let the strictness of a graver censure proceed, so that not only are public vices of incontinence punished or coerced by vengeance, but also every matter or occasion from which a suspicion of incontinence could arise is removed from the midst.

Concerning the three substantial elements of religion to be observed.

Let the three principal substantial elements of religion be observed most fully by all, so that no dispensation or contrary custom may avail anyone against them.

That monks should keep silence.

Let silence be observed in these places: in the oratory, dormitory, refectory, and cloister, and in the other places and at the customary times.

How the monks are to take refreshment.

Let the brothers take refreshment together under table reading, to be continued until the end of the meal. The eating of meat is to be most strictly forbidden to all, at every time and place, except only in a case permitted by the Rule.

How the brothers are to sleep.

Let all sleep together in one dormitory, as far as possibility permits, girded with cords or belts.
