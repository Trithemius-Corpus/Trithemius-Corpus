With regard to the enclosure of the monasteries, he shall apply due vigilance. Entrance into the precincts of the monastery is not to be open to women, even those not suspected. Nor is it lawful for any religious, except officials, to leave the precincts of his monastery without the special permission of his superior.

All who are healthy shall observe the fasts, both those instituted by the rule and those instituted by the Church. They shall perform the divine office, both by day and by night, fittingly and distinctly, as is proper, at the appropriate hours. When this has been completed, they shall apply themselves to reading or to manual labor, according to the requirement of the time and of their business, and according to the direction of the aforesaid rule.

Books are to be supplied to the brothers at the appointed time. They shall inspect each brother’s beds, on account of private possessions. He shall observe the chapter of faults daily; in it, after the readings of the martyrology and the rule have first been made, he shall proceed, if there is need, to the exposition of the rule and to the examination and correction of faults.

He shall also see to it that the brothers appointed for this do not neglect, at the proper times, confession, communion, and celebration. He shall provide diligently for the monks concerning food and drink according to the disposition of the rule. Suitable places in the monasteries themselves are also to be assigned both for sick brothers and, for the time being, for guests who arrive; to all of these care and humanity shall be shown according to the disposition of the aforesaid rule. They shall lead guests to prayer; a reading shall be read to them for edification, and he shall wash their feet.

With the utmost diligence he shall take care that monasteries are not burdened with unbearable loads of debts, and that their goods are not dispersed or alienated, in accordance with the sanctions of Benedict XII and other apostolic sanctions, and the oaths sworn by abbots at their confirmation and blessing; alienated goods, if there are any, they shall carefully recover.

Abbots, provosts, priors, or rectors shall diligently protect and preserve the rights of those monasteries. They shall use their fruits, revenues, and proceeds for the common benefits of the monasteries. And each year they shall give a lawful account of their revenues and of the state of their temporal goods. They shall abstain from superfluous and unbecoming riding-horses, and from hunting dogs and birds. Nor shall any religious within the precincts of the monastery

presume in any way to have weapons or to contract confraternity.

These and other things handed down by the holy fathers and by canonical and regular institutions, according as the character of the time and the condition of persons requires and as seems salutary to the visitors, they shall renew by establishing them in monasteries and places that have fallen away from regular observance, for the introduction of holy reform. And they shall take care to commit them to due execution, causing all and each of these things to be firmly observed by ecclesiastical censure, by our authority which, through the present letters, we commit to them.

Thus, at the instance of the reverend father in Christ, the lord abbot of the monastery at Saint Giles in Nuremberg, a prelate of the aforementioned order, whose wish he greatly desires to satisfy, Georg Stuchs of Sulzbach, citizen of Nuremberg, with his craft as leader and fortune as companion, brought to its preconceived and intended end the present volume: divided and arranged in brief form into the form of visiting the monasteries of both nuns and monks of the Order of Saint Benedict throughout the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg; into the manner of celebrating a provincial chapter of the same every three years; into an abridgment of the recess of each provincial chapter celebrated from the year of grace 1417 up to the present; into a summary of the privileges granted to the aforesaid order; and into an epilogue briefly expressing the foregoing matters.

In the year of the incarnation of the intelligible divine Word, from the Holy Spirit and from Mary ever virgin, 1493, on the twelfth day before the Kalends of the month of October. May the most blessed Benedict aid us in attaining the clear intellectual and most blessed vision of him. Amen.
