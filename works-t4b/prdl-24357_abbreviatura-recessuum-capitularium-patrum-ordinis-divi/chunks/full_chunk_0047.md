...of the outward habit, the professed and novices of the said order ought to show uprightness of conduct. We establish and ordain that all shall wear large and broad tonsures, so that the circle of them does not exceed the space or thickness of two transverse fingers; and that they shall dress decently in clothing, habits, and footwear, according to the Clementine constitution Ne in agro and the constitution of Benedict XII, On the Form and Decency of Dress. That is, they shall wear tunics and garments closest to the outer habit of black, brown, or white color, according to the custom usually observed among them: round and ankle-length, not split, nor marked by any other deformity, with wide sleeves extending to the fist, in no way buttoned or tied, yet not so wide that their breadth above the fist can be stretched out with the thumb raised. Nor shall they extend around the elbow-opening, as the abuse of certain persons has it, beyond the point where the arm could easily be turned and inserted.

Let them be content with fur almuces in place of hoods. Yet in proper places where they are accustomed to wear hoods, they may, with the abbot’s permission, use respectable hoods, split over the shoulders for at least the length of four transverse fingers.

They shall not wear sendal for linings in their garments, nor miniver, nor other costly materials; but they shall use lambskins or sheepskins for the said linings, or at least other non-costly cloths. The lining shall not appear in any way above the neck or on the hems of the garments or sleeves.

Concerning the habit of those travelling.

Furthermore, when they must go outside the monastery, they shall wear a frock, cowl, or cloak, and under the cloak, if they wear one, a cowl or scapular.

Concerning the habit of novices.

Novices, however, shall wear a cowl, inside and outside the monastery, fitted on both sides. And lay brothers shall use a scapular with a hood joined to it, closed, inside and outside the monastery.

Concerning the length of garments and the use of the hood of the frock.

...shall not reach the elbow-opening by the thickness of two transverse fingers, nor may it be narrower or so short that it does not extend as far as the middle of the elbow and of the upper bone near the shoulder-blade. The said hood, however, shall extend behind at least as far as the belt, and may not extend beyond it by more than four fingers. The length of the sleeves shall extend one cubit beyond the hand and no more. Their breadth shall not exceed another cubit and a half. Below, however, it shall be so broad that steps may easily be taken within it. And what has been said about the frock we wish to be understood of the cowl as well, except for the sleeves, since it has none.

Within the monastery, however, in places where there are twelve monks, all shall use the frock, except only the president in the monastery and the first after him, whom we wish always to wear the frock.

Yet during work and the divine office under the alb, and when riding under the cloak, they may wear only the scapular, which we wish to contain a breadth of at least half a cubit.

Likewise, they shall not use linen or hemp shirts, but shall wear either a woolen undergarment or a woolen garment in place of the said shirts. And they shall wear boots or shoes with straps, high enough that their width covers a space of four fingers of the shin above the ankle.

Monks living in common shall abstain from eating meat when healthy.

The brothers shall live in common and eat in the refectory, in which no one at all shall eat meat. And the visitors shall be diligent to see that the constitution of Pope Benedict XII on the eating of and abstinence from meats is inviolably observed. Sacred reading shall not be lacking at the table of the brothers while they eat; and the one who is to read for the whole week shall enter on Sunday with a blessing, as is contained in chapter 38 of the Rule.
