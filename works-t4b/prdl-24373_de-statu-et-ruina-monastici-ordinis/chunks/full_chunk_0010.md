For you have been made poor by wicked habits; by holy conduct you would be made rich again. Look around you at monasteries restored to regular observance, how, after extreme poverty, by the Lord’s generosity they are once more advancing toward their former wealth. And indeed this is quite just. For the Lord promised us in the Gospel, saying: First seek the kingdom of God and his justice, and all these things will be added to you. But you put aside the love of God, neglect his worship, give yourselves to games, feasts, drunkenness, and gluttony, and do not attend to the work of the Lord. You care nothing for regular observance; therefore, because spiritual things have been neglected, by the just judgment of God temporal things too have failed you. False, then, is the necessity which you invent, and insufficient the dispensation which you allege.

But perhaps you say: We are not sons of peasants, but of nobles; we are noble, delicate, and weak. We cannot keep the observance of servants, farmers, and shepherds; fasts, abstinence, vigils, disciplines, and the other exercises which it contains are unbearable for us, and we neither can nor ought to imitate them. What then? Are you not a monk because you are noble, because you are delicate, because you are weak? There is no way for you to defend yourself against Saint Benedict, who in the Rule made ample provision for every condition, every rank, every age, and who with the greatest discretion assists the delicate and the weak. Are you alone, from among the noble, tender, and delicate, to have professed the monastic order? Many most noble young men, sons of emperors, kings, dukes, and princes, have shone in our order at various times by holiness and learning under regular observance. They, nobler, more delicate, and more tender than you, yet keeping cenobitic discipline inviolate, will stand against you as a testimony, readily showing to those who love God what, full of pride, you cast away as unbearable.

Nor do I bid you imitate the observance which you despise as that of peasants, but the Rule which you promised, which you vowed, which you pledged yourself before God to keep. If anything in our way of life displeases you, you have the Rule: fashion your life according to its precepts. No one demands more from you; no one requires more. The exercises of monks which you condemn in observers of the Rule, the Rule itself teaches, the Rule itself commands. If you are unwilling to imitate them, you have condemned yourself by the sentence of your own mouth.

For conversion of morals according to the Rule of Saint Benedict enjoins on its professors fasts, abstinence, vigils, disciplines, obedience, patience, humility, poverty, and the other exercises of monastic discipline, which you judge hard and heavy; yet they belong so properly to the Rule that without them no one deserves to be called a monk. Tell me, I ask you, transgressor of your vow and of your order, tell me: do you also wish to live blessedly? Do you also hope that you can come to eternal life? You will answer: This question you ask is foolish. Who does not wish to live blessedly? Who does not hope to come to eternal life? Therefore I both desire to be blessed, and I trust that I can come to the heavenly homeland.

If, then, this is so, if you trust that you will receive the blessed life, why do you desert, spurn, and despise the way that leads to it? You err, wretched unhappy man; you are deceived. One does not come to the kingdom except through humility, nor to rest except through labor, through abstinence, through obedience, through poverty. For Scripture says: Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. But you, unhappy apostate, who reject the order, spurn humility, refuse obedience, hate poverty, and despise patience, with what rashness do you presume to such heights? If the mouth that lies kills the soul, unless you do penance you have no part in the kingdom of Christ and of God: you who lie not to man but to God, who violate the covenant, who deny faith, who withdraw the vow of religion made to God.

The dispensation of the Roman pontiff will not save you, I dare say, when you seek it by fraud, report it by lies, and extort it by importunate petitions. For the four things which we said make a due dispensation are clearly seen not to have come together at all in your frauds. For that transfer which you make from the holy order is neither useful nor necessary, and therefore it is unfitting and dishonorable. In it, indeed, will is put in place of necessity, pleasure in place of usefulness, carnality in place of fittingness, impiety in place of honor. Is this the proper occasion for your dispensation? Keep for yourself your little letters obtained by fraud; keep the instruments of denial and apostasy.
