For whatever you wish to say will be nothing, because, accused outwardly and inwardly by the testimony of your conscience, you will be judged worthy of condemnation. You seize what belongs to divine right, though you had been appointed guardian of it; therefore you will hear the Lord saying, “Pay what you owe.” That is: return the monks whom he entrusted to your faith; return the monastery which you received to govern; return faith, return the vow, return holiness, return gain, return merit, return honor. Pay what you owe.

I appointed you shepherd of my sheep, that you might preserve them in holy religion, teach them, instruct them, amend them, correct them when they sinned. But you hated discipline and cast my words behind you; if you saw a thief, you ran with him, and with adulterers you set your portion. Intent on the pleasures of the flesh, you did not guard my sheep; therefore pay what you owe. After monks have come from the world to religion, they have become subject to my right, and it is not lawful for them to return any further to the world. But you were appointed minister of my sheep, not their lord; nor was it lawful for you in any way to alienate, usurp, or plunder anything of what is mine by right. You were guardian of my goods, not their owner. What had been acquired you ought faithfully to have preserved; what had been scattered you ought faithfully to have sought out again. But you, forgetful of me, have squandered my inheritance and scattered the sheep; indeed, from sheep you have made beasts, from servants enemies, from confederates infidels. Now, therefore, pay what you owe.

I entrusted monks to you, whom you ought to have preserved in their holy purpose: to raise up those lying fallen, to care for the weak, to restore those failing, to kindle the sluggish with charity, to stir up the negligent, to rebuke sinners, to correct the disobedient, to restrain the dissolute, and to hold back the perverse from their evil pursuits. But you cared for none of the things that belonged to your office. Given over to greed, luxury, and gluttony, you not only permitted monks to return to the world, but, utterly forgetful of pastoral care, you became the leader and companion of their apostasy. Now therefore pay what you owe: restore what he entrusted to your faith, not to say your faithlessness. But because you will not be able to restore it, you will be cast into a dark prison, covered with the gloom of death; from there you will not come out until you pay the debt, which you will never pay. There not only

will you receive punishment for your own deeds, but also for all the sins of those whom, committed to your rule, you neglected to correct. For so Saint Benedict says in the Rule: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd will lie upon him if the head of the household can find any lack of profit in his sheep.”

Therefore, abbot, whoever you are, if you are wise, if you understand, if at last you believe in the future judgment, if you desire to escape the torments of hell, do not consent to the depraved desires of monks; do not agree to criminal dismemberments. What does pleasure in this very brief life profit you, for which you will be compelled to receive eternal punishments? Do not, I beg you, do not inflict so great an injury on Saint Benedict; do not take from him monasteries which not even all your parents built. Let it be enough for you that you are abbot, that you live from his patrimony. Do not desert the order; do not consent to diabolical suggestions; do not make your monks seculars; do not resign the monastery committed to you into the hands of outsiders. Recognize yourself as a man subject to death, of brief life upon the earth. Do not do so ruinous a thing; do not commit this crime; do not perpetrate so inestimable a sacrilege. Remain in the holy order, and do not desert your purpose, if you desire to come to eternal life.

For the Lord says: “No one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom of God.” Behold, you have put your hand to the plow; behold, with Lot you have gone out from Sodom. If you look back, if you corrupt your purpose, if you return to the world, you are not fit for the kingdom of God, and you will by no means deserve to receive it. But if you persevere in your renewal, if you keep a good will, if you have the charity of God in your heart, believe me: even if you have lived negligently in some things, you will find a merciful intercessor before God in the saint for whose love, though you could have done so, you refused to desert the order. Remember, I beseech you, the covenant of the Lord which, when you entered the order, you made with him; and do not be unfaithful to your redeemer. Do not break your vow; do not violate the pact by so criminal a perjury. Consider the pact, attend to the condition, and beware lest you prove false to God. Before you promised him, you were free; but after you promised, you were no longer free, but became the servant of God.
