“If such a monastery,” he says, “most blessed father, is transferred into a secular church, the divine worship will be greatly increased by it. It will make the princes obedient to the Roman Church, peace for the surrounding peoples, and will procure the greatest honor for the Christian name.” O deceitful tongue, O lying lip, O contriver of deceit, O inventor of fraud, O minister of iniquity, who tell false things for true, evil things for good, sacrilegious things for holy ones. Kings and princes were accustomed to build monasteries, not also to destroy them; you ought not mendaciously to have alleged them as patrons of your perfidy. For if they seek the praise of God, they never consent that monasteries be transferred away from the holy order. But if they wished to attend to their own advantages, they would know that they receive more profit from monasteries than from collegiate churches. Whence, then, comes such unheard-of presumption in you, that before the vicar of Christ you lie that a thing is pleasing to princes, when it is known to be contrary to them? Thus you act, thus you deceive the vicar of Christ, teacher of evil, deviser of deceit, inventor of falsehood, author of crime, craftsman of iniquity, you who live by fraud, who fortify yourself with impiety, who attack religion, who gape after profits, who are fed by pleasure, to whom everything depends on crime and nothing on the love of God.

Far be it from faithful princes that they should take away from Saint Benedict the monasteries which their devout forefathers once built for him. Woe to you, perverse monk, who presume to inflict so great a disgrace on the order that you destroy monasteries which neither you nor your parents built. Woe also to you who offer patronage to wicked matters against God and his saints, who entangle yourself in the sins of others, who sell your tongue, who for a paltry price incur the indignation of God and of all the saints. Woe to you, I say, advocate of reprobate monks, who do injury to God, who wickedly mock the works of his saints, who deny the right of equity, who urge monks by words and deeds to return to the world. O what will you answer to almighty God when an account of all your works is demanded from you?

When you are accused by Saint Benedict as the destroyer of his order, what then will you set against the accusation of the saints? What will you answer? How will you excuse yourself? Who will have a distinct oration before the judge for your deliverance? Whom will you then be able to have as advocate, you who are now the advocate of the wicked against God? Would that you were wise and understood; would that you provided for your last things and did not entangle yourself in the sins of others. But, inflamed by the torches of greed, you think only of the profits of this life, and show clearly that you have no understanding at all of future calamities. Therefore, just as birds are caught in a snare, so you will be caught, when you do not expect it, in your sins and in the sins of those whom you have made apostatize from the order. What a harsh sentence they will receive by whose counsel monks have despised their order.

You have lied that there is great benefit in the supplication, when this alone is your purpose: that you may be able shamefully to empty out the honor of the order. Is the worship of God increased in this way? Is this the peace promised to people in the surrounding region? You will never make bad monks into good canons. But one who makes men bad, what does he deserve except the reward of the bad? Those monks are bad who, despising the order, return to the world. For unless they were bad and sinful, they would by no means have deserted the holy order. No one hates the good except an evil man; no one withdraws from the holy purpose that he vowed except a perverse man. But one who presents himself as patron of the perverse in evil causes makes himself a participant in the sins of others.

To be a canon in the Church of God is good and holy, but to be a monk for the love of God belongs to greater perfection. A canon is permitted to pass over to the monks as to a higher rank, but a monk is forbidden to descend to the canons. Nor will a dispensation make lawful what has been obtained by false narratives. If there is necessity in the supplication, integrity in the intention of the petitioner, and honesty in the use of the dispensation, I boldly say that the pope can dispense with a monk so that he may become a canon, yet in such a way that he is not absolved without necessity from that by which he is known to be bound to God.
