The advocates of apostates set forth false causes as true; they invent necessity; they falsely allege usefulness; they pretend to an honorableness which they flee. The root and cause of all this evil is founded solely in pleasure and hatred of discipline. But when the stated cause is false, the dispensation does not free the petitioner but condemns him. The supreme pontiff, after hearing the statements of petitioners, has been accustomed to reply cautiously, saying: “If it is as is stated, let what is requested be done, in the manner and form that follows.” He replies cautiously, grants secretly, and dispenses under fixed limits. But when the monks have received bulls in that fashion, they do not remain within the limits of the dispensation, but transgress its measure, considering not what they have obtained but what they wished to obtain.

O wretched, senseless, unhappy men, why do you deceive your very selves? Why do you willingly destroy your souls? The intention of the one dispensing does not absolve you from what you promised to Saint Benedict. You usurp more for yourselves than the pope has granted; indeed, without license you assume the whole of what you illicitly desired to obtain. It is true what a certain man, still living, said about some of us, when by apostolic commission he had stripped them of the monastic habit: “Even if I have removed the habit from them, nevertheless, according to the tenor of the bull, they are still monks before God.”

Unhappy apostates, who will free you from the hand of the living God? Who will be able to defend you from the accusation of Saint Benedict? What will it profit you that here, without hindrance, you have been made canons out of monks, if in the last judgment of God you should happen to be numbered with the reprobate? Indeed, unless Saint Benedict, prince of monks, consents, no dispensation excuses you. Your transfer does not look to its proper end, and for that reason it will be inexcusable. Question your consciences, and consider with what intention you withdrew from the order. If for love of God, if from desire for the heavenly homeland, if for the salvation of souls, if for progress in virtues and for a holier state of life you took off the habit, God will approve the deed you have done. But in all these things your intention was contrary; therefore your transfer from the order is rightly judged alien to the will of God. For you cast off the habit so that you might live without law, without obedience, without humility, without

poverty. This was your first intention. The fear of God is not before your eyes. You think nothing of the salvation of souls; you understand nothing of eternal life; you desire to walk in the pleasure of the flesh alone. All the causes and reasons that you stated in your petition are fabricated and false. How, then, will the dispensation be legitimate? How will the authority of the supreme pontiff excuse you, when you did not fear to circumvent it by lying, fraud, deceit, and importunate prayers?

What should be thought about you is made clear when your conduct is examined. For in the Gospel the Lord says about ravening wolves: “By their fruits you will know them.” Never among you is a mind truly joyful in God seen; never can conscience be secure, but it is always anxious about its guilt, always troubled, since you have as inciter to despair the devil, by whose impulse you withdrew from the order. Hence it is that you are not at all concerned with the love of God and the salvation of souls, but, intent on earthly vanities and carnal desires, you think of nothing less than eternal life.

By what spirit this transfer was made in you, your way of life shows. For those who are led by the Spirit of God have crucified their flesh with its vices and desires. But you are not so. For as soon as you cast off the habit of religion, like wanton oxen freed from the yoke of subjection, you plunge yourselves into every disgrace as though licitly. You cast off every honorableness; you make a pact with vices and wage war with virtues. The charity of God is not in your hearts. You despise mercy and piety. You love vanity, and have become vain. You are evil to all good people, worse to monks, worst to yourselves.

No transfer has been made for the better when it has opened the way to vices. Deserters of the love of God and of the order, enemies of honorableness, adversaries of piety: if you love God, if you are good, if you love religion, why do you hate monks? And you especially hate those who persevere in the order from which you have fallen, because, when you now see them standing firmly in their vocation, you fear their testimony against you in judgment. Therefore all monks in holy observance will stand in the strict examination to bear testimony against you.
