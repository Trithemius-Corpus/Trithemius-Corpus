You hate the monks of your order because, as you know yourselves truly to be, so you judge others to be; and the good that you do not see in yourselves, you envy also in others. We do not marvel at your heart's madness against monks, when we consider the injury and insult that you have inflicted on God and on your holy lawgiver. For one who does not love God, who breaks a vow solemnly made to him, who casts off the habit of religion, is not surprisingly one who hates monks, his servants. Every apostate, indeed, is the adversary and despiser of his own order. But I beg, I exhort, I advise all of you who have deserted your order: abandon your fury, soften your rage, cease raging against monks, lest those whom you now pursue with hatred be witnesses and judges against you at the judgment. Do penance for this impiety of yours while you have time, lest sudden death seize you unprepared. I beg you, do not raise the horn of wickedness against Saint Benedict; do not wish by your transfers to destroy his venerable order; do not despise the opportunity for repentance, if you desire to reach eternal life. You have built no monastery for Saint Benedict. I beg and exhort you not to transfer to secular uses the things built for him by others. Remember how brief the present life is, how certain death is, and how severe the judge is; and do not place upon your necks so heavy a burden of sacrilege that you will never be able to make fitting restitution by any satisfaction. Do not stir up Saint Benedict as your adversary, if you desire to find the friendship of the other saints. For if one is offended, none of the rest will be your friend. Consider the end of those who long ago transferred themselves from the holy order, and fear likewise for your own. Where are the abbots and monks who once withdrew from the order and handed over the monasteries of holy father Benedict into the hands of secular men? They are either all dead or will die shortly. You also who are still living, who have withdrawn from the order after the example of those who went before, if you do not take precautions, will go to those whose imitators you are.

Would that you feared for yourselves from another's danger, and, while there is time, took up penance with your whole heart. I have heard from many with what great bitterness of mind, at the point of death, many transgressors of the order were constrained: those who had been made canons from monks, or even those who had been the authors of transfers of this sort. For they said that they grieved over no sin so much as over this one, that they knew they had given consent or help to the transfer from the order. If the transfer of monasteries from the order is good, why did those who had done it grieve when they were dying? But if it is evil, why do you do and undertake it? Let all who occupy monasteries of monks remember how harsh an anathema they are marked with by blessed Pope Gregory, those who do not fear to turn things consecrated to God in monasteries to alien uses. Let Thietgang, once archbishop of Trier, be an example to them: when he had received from Pope Adrian, in the city of Rome, the monastery of Saint Andrew in Maestricht, and had been warned a third time in a vision by that same apostle and by Saint Gregory not to occupy the dwellings of monks any longer, yet disregarded the command to withdraw, he died a harsh death with all his companions. Whether those who occupy the monasteries of monks are secure before God, we leave to their consciences. But a man who possesses another man's goods without that man's will is bound to restitution. Let the one who occupies the goods of some saint against his will consider for himself what he deserves. We have now used a sufficiently long discourse against the transgressors of our order; let us now come to what remains.

Chapter V.

How these dismemberments by apostate monks are to be resisted.

Although both my mind and the very necessity imposed upon us offer abundant material for inveighing against the destroyers of our order, nevertheless, since those who might be recalled to repentance by exhortations are not present, I think enough has been said to them. For those who so wickedly crush the honor of their own order are senseless; I judge that they must be resisted not with words only, but with deeds.
