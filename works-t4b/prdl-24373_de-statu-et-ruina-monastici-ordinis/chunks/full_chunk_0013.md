But if you corrupt your purpose, if you deny your vow, if you return to the world, God will not keep you in that rank in which he would previously have kept you, before you entered the monastery. For before your entrance into religion you were, as it were, of the Lord’s household, among his subjects and laborers. You were, I say, God’s creature; you were his servant, his slave; but you were not his friend, his officer, his intimate, his secretary. But after you became a monk, you promised fidelity to the Lord, that you would cling to him continually, that where the Lord is, there you too would be as his minister. He chose you as a friend and, so to speak, as guardian of his body, so that in his sight you might always minister to him and fulfill his will in all things. For this is what you promised.

Therefore, if you withdraw from him to whom you vowed yourself, if you are found unfaithful and a despiser of your vow, he will not have you in his household as before, before you became a monk, but certainly he will shun you as faithless, a prevaricator, perjured, and lying. For the monk who denies his vow denies Christ. For blessed Jerome says: I, who seem to be a monk, if I corrupt my purpose, have denied Christ. But you prevaricators, who, despising the sacred order, have been made canons from monks, you, I say, have not only corrupted your purpose, but, what is more criminal, have utterly destroyed it. Now, therefore, either you have denied Christ, or what Jerome says is false. But since what so great a man taught cannot be false, it remains that you are deniers of Christ, perjured and unfaithful.

Would that at least late, at last, you would undergo the remedies of repentance, you who stand in such great dangers. If he has denied Christ who violates the monastic purpose, who defrauds the whole order and all the saints of their due, what does he deserve? If he always burns who unjustly occupies the professions of men, of what punishment is he worthy who usurps for himself goods dedicated to God and the saints? If, according to ecclesiastical tradition, he sins mortally who unjustly claims for his own use another’s house or field, how great a crime is he guilty of who turns the house and possessions of God and the saints into dwellings for secular persons? And if a man is bound to make restitution to another man for things wrongly taken away, how much more are those who take monasteries away from God and Saint Benedict bound to restitution?

If a sin is not forgiven unless what has been taken away is restored, when will those be able to be absolved from guilt who turn the dwellings of God’s servants to alien uses? For monasteries once taken away will never be restored to the order. Woe to those who did not fear to make for themselves this testament, which they will not be able to revoke by any satisfaction whatsoever.

O monk, O abbot, you who revoke your vow solemnly made to God, who cast aside the habit of holy religion, who transfer monasteries consecrated to Saint Benedict into the hands of clerics: what will you answer when accused before almighty God? Perhaps you will say: You changed your vow for the better. Indeed, they do not know the difference between vows. To live as a canon is good and holy, if someone has been called by God to that state. But if someone has been called to the grade of monastic perfection, it will never be lawful for him to descend to the canonicate. For no one doubts that canons are inferior to monks.

Tell me, whoever you are who stand as a cooperator in the depravity of monks, who transfer them from their order, who defend unjust causes against God and Saint Benedict, who are regarded as the supporter and patron of their apostasy. Tell me, then: what do you think? Is it lawful for monks professed in a holy order to put off the habit and become secular canons? If it is lawful, why do you obtain a dispensation from the Roman pontiff at such great expense, labor, weariness, and through such importunate princes? For lawful things do not need dispensation. But if it is unlawful, who will make it lawful? A dispensation? I very much doubt it. Whether the pope can dispense in this matter or cannot is not for me to discuss, since it is agreed that he has the fullness of power. Far be it from me, therefore, to derogate even in the least from the authority of the supreme pontiff. For he is the vicar of Christ, and it is not lawful for a man to judge anything concerning him.

Are you therefore secure if he does what he seems able to do? Believe me, you are not secure, because you gave a false account of the reason for your transfer; you fraudulently deceived the vicar of Christ, because both the narrative was lying and the petition, not to say the extortion, was importunate. Finally, the bull of your transfer, carefully and cautiously written, does not absolve you from the order, nor take away the vow, nor remove religion, nor consent to your laying aside the monastic habit.
