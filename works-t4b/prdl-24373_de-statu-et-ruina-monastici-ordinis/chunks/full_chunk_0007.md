...the outcome, you who deny the holy purpose of religion, who break your vows, who despise the order, who violate your faith. Would that you weighed the brevity of this life; would that you feared the examination of future retribution; would that, pierced by saving teachings, you heeded the voice of the Lord calling you to repentance and saying: “I do not desire the death of the one who is dying, but that he turn back and live.” And through the prophet: “Return to the Lord your God, for he is merciful and loving, patient and of great mercy, and he revokes his sentence,” if only you would wash away your guilt with tears.

But you do not fear the judgment of God; you spurn his clemency; you despise the patience of his long-suffering. Therefore the Apostle says to each of you: “Do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each one according to his works.” He said that the judgment of God is just because it is late, because he delays long and greatly, so that the long patience of God may take counsel for you unto life. But how will you do penance, you who do not acknowledge guilt? You will grieve, but too late, when there is no longer a place for repentance, but only the repayment of vengeance. Then punishment will pay back the impious man and the sinner, when repentance for sin can no longer be of use.

O how such men will burn then; how wretched, how unhappy those who, for the briefest pleasure of this age, have made themselves deserters of God and rebels against the command of God, and have done the will of the devil, so that they must be tormented with him in inextinguishable fire. Would that these things clung in their hearts; would that those who desert their order, who break their vow, who so boldly lie both to God and to his saints, thought on these things. But we strive in vain. They in no way receive our words, who despise the words of God and of the Church and of Saint Benedict. Therefore this cause of the order must be carried on not with words, but with deeds.

Let us therefore arm ourselves, fathers, with all our strength, and let us prepare for the contest with an uncorrupted mind, devoted virtue, and firm constancy, against those who desire to destroy our order, who strip off the monk, who take away monasteries, who desert the rule, who stand as rebels against their visitors. Let us arm ourselves, I beseech you, fathers, with zeal for regular discipline and for the honor of the order, sparing no labors and no expenses, lest this pernicious plague, through our silence, gradually infect the whole order.

Never has so great a necessity lain upon our order; never has it been entangled in so many evils. Behold, monks strip off the habit; monasteries are transferred into dwellings of clerics; and while we are absorbed in private advantages, while we flee labors, while we spare expenses, while we take counsel for our own avarice, the order perishes, religion passes away, the worship of God vanishes. Already many monasteries, by the malice of the times, not to say by our own sloth, have been transferred away from the order; and unless we keep watch in another way, the rashness of wicked men threatens that greater losses will be inflicted on us. Indeed, where the audacity of fools is not immediately restrained, an occasion for presuming similar things is offered to the rest.

What shall I say of the monks whom we still have in the order only by habit and tonsure? And, lest we place the members before the head, what is more unhappy in this wretched time than deformed abbots? They care for nothing less than the rule; they pursue nothing more eagerly than lust, avarice, pleasure, pride, and the vanity of the world. Thus, when the head is infected, all the members of the body are corrupted. Now in monasteries where the rule is despised, where discipline is not observed, what else is seen but a kind of spring of all vices? No obedience, no poverty, no chastity is maintained among them for love of virtue; among them, to live without crime is itself a crime. They are corrupt men, perverse monks, artificers of deceit, authors of fraud, ministers of iniquity, friends of crime, who flee discipline, now despise observance, destroy the order, and confound unity.

And truly, fathers, unless you swiftly provide remedies for these evils, in a short time you will see the order diminished and will grieve over it. But perhaps you say: “Our vigilance has never been lacking; our diligence has never ceased. We have always opposed the rashness of the wicked; we have always tried to prevent the dismemberment of monasteries, and we have never accomplished anything. What do our labor, our efforts, our heavy expenses achieve, when princes and rulers of churches oppose themselves so powerfully?” I know, fathers, that what you say is true; I know what has been done, and I know it has been continuous. But I beseech you, do not be willing to take my words in a bad sense if I dare to respond to these objections. Your vigilance in the causes of the order is known to me; your labors are evident; the magnitude of your expenses is proven; the continuation always applied in these matters is known.
