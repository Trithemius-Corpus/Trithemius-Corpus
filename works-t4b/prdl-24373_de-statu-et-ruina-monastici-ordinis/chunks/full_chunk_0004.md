O with what beautiful grace you once flourished in the Church of God, when you brought forth men distinguished by every holiness and learning, and, as one younger, strong and steadfast, you used to drive away from yourself every dishonorable way of life. But now, as if wearied by old age and the oppression of many days, you have failed, and the strength of your youth has been taken away, so that your beauty is no longer in you. Where now is your zeal? Where is the comeliness of your face; where your honor, where the power once spread throughout the whole world? Where are your sons around your table, men most learned and most holy? Where is that study of letters, venerable to God and the angels, whose fervor once made you exalted above all orders? For they have failed, and you have been reduced almost to nothing; you have lost the beauty of your youth, and the strength of your adolescence has been scattered. To whom shall I compare you, or to whom shall I liken you, secluded daughter? To whom shall I make you equal and console you, mother of monks? Great as the sea is your crushing sorrow; who will heal you? O, if you had guarded against this ruin which you now experience, how blessed all nations would have called you. But you said in your abundance: I shall not be moved forever. Plenty has made you poor, and the abundance of temporal things has stripped you of all your adornment. You have brought forth perverse sons, disobedient, rebellious against holy institutions, who, hiding beneath the cloak of your honor, have stripped you of all your beauty. Alas, mother and virgin, you have grown old in a foreign land; you are counted with the dead; there is no appearance of your beauty. Rise now, blessed Benedict, most holy prince of monks, and see your order which you established, and your inheritance which you planted. Look upon the monks forgetful of your profession, immersed in earthly things, given over to pleasure, full of every outpouring of vices. You once gave them the best precepts for living; you commanded them to be free from the cares of secular affairs; you ordered their hearts to be stretched out toward the love of God alone. Rise, I say, most blessed father, and see the desolation of your order, which is confounded within by its own sons and scattered without by enemies. Monks despise the institutions of your piety; alien men seize the monasteries of your order. There is no one to defend the honor of your name, no

one to guard the beauty of your most holy order. Rise also, O great pope Gregory, who under anathema forbade places consecrated to God for monks to be transferred to the uses of secular men, and see monks expelled from the monasteries and worldly clerics thrust in. You, once the chief of all churches, used to devote the greatest care to monastic honor, taking anxious precaution that nothing be presumed in the sacred order beyond the rule of Benedict. Now behold the ways of monks, most reverend bishop, bare of all honor and full of every disgrace. See how monasteries dedicated to your order are handed into the hands of clerics, and those that once could be compared to angelic choirs now become brothels of prostitutes. Rise, most glorious emperor and unconquered defender of the Christian faith, Charles, glory of the Germans, and see the calamity of the monks, you who once made the order of Father Benedict glorious and, by your zeal, spread it through almost the whole world. See now that the monasteries which you once founded have all been exposed to the plundering of enemies. You who once venerated the monastic order as the camp of God, now have compassion, holy prince, on it as despised by all and stripped of its goods. You once used, with great care, to bring tepid abbots and monks back to the observance of regular discipline, and, once reformed, to defend them with great solicitude from every harassment by your own men or by anyone else. Rise also, Berno, abbot of the monastery of Cluny, with your successors, you who in ancient times magnificently restored the holy order as it was collapsing and brought it back everywhere to its former beauty. Rise now, I say, and see how the iniquity of monks abounds: fury rules, crime overflows, and pleasure makes almost all of them mad. The beauty of reform has been taken away; discipline has collapsed; zeal for holy conduct has perished; humility has breathed its last. Rise, William, most reverend father, abbot of the monastery of Hirsau, who renewed the Cluniac restoration in Germany after its lapse, who provided abundant statutes for monastic conduct, who by your precepts strengthened the limbs of the weak, who by teaching and holiness illumined it like a most brilliant star.
