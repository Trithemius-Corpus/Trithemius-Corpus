Now, therefore, the one from whom you have all these things knocks at your door; in the necessity of his order he asks for help; he desires to receive the least part of the very great things he entrusted to you. Why do you deny him his own goods? Why, when they are demanded from you, do you not immediately return what was entrusted to you? Divine Scripture gives this command: “If you see the naked, cover him, and do not despise your own flesh.” Behold, your order is naked; behold, your torn mother cries out; she asks alms, seeks what is hers, and looks for help. If there are any inward parts of pity in you, any sweetness in your heart, any love for your order, any love of God, do not despise your own flesh and do not scorn so great a public bond of kinship. Honor the one who first honored you. Give alms to the one who raised you up. Bestow a little upon the one who made you rich in many things. However much you give him, nothing at all will be diminished for you, provided only that you bring a good will. You will give a little and receive much; you will generously bestow earthly things and hope for heavenly ones.

Nor should it escape you that, as people commonly say, whatever is withheld from honor is reserved for disgrace. For the jurist of sentences says: “The treasury takes what Christ does not receive.” He is not free from the guilt of crime who sets greed before the order of piety. Those who despise the words of God: what wonder is it if they scorn ours? Though I may speak much, say much, and urge excellent things, I shall never bend by words those who rebel against contributing to the order. Indeed, unless your authority, O presiding lords, intervenes, the speech of one who warns labors in vain. It therefore belongs to your office to bend the rebellious; to you it is entrusted to humble the disobedient; ours is humbly to obey your sanctions. Therefore, if you desire to defend our order from the assaults of the depraved, take care first to recall the abbots to obedience, because unless unanimity is restored to the order, the impiety of its adversaries will easily prevail. For the Lord says in the Gospel: “Every kingdom divided against itself will be laid waste, and house falls upon house.” Division alone confounds the order.

How disobedient and rebellious abbots are to be compelled to aid the order. Chapter vii.

If judges lack the authority to restrain rebels, legislation will be made in vain. For we always strive after what is forbidden, and unless compelled, we resist commands. For where there is no fear of power, the condition of law is quickly despised. The preservation of law, therefore, is fear of authority. But where laxness of authority turns a blind eye, the vigor of the law wavers. For a statute, however holy or necessary, is quickly carried off into oblivion if there is no authority by which contempt may be punished. Take away power, and the commonwealth will be nothing other than a confusion full of sedition. For rustic savagery is not led by reason; it is not calmed by gentleness. Among our own men too, may God correct it, there are many abbots and monks rougher than rustics, more implacable than beasts, who are not bent by gentleness to the observance of the commandments of God and the rule, nor are they ever persuaded by exhortations, however holy. If the authority of those who preside does not oppose these men, they will never be led to any obedience to the chapter.

For this reason the supreme pontiffs, founders of this most sacred provincial chapter, established many suitable remedies for us, by which we can compel the abbots subject to the chapter to support the order. We have authority; we hesitate over power. And what is authority worth without power? If you wish, most honorable fathers, that I speak more plainly what I have wished to express figuratively about the power of authority, you will pardon me if I have noted your faintheartedness, not to say negligence. Behold, we have known 127 abbeys placed under our provincial chapter, but we do not receive from all of them the subsidies of the contribution that is due. Why is this? We have received the authority to demand, but we do not exercise the power. We receive what is offered voluntarily; we are afraid to compel the disobedient. For I am certain that many abbots of our province contribute nothing to the support of the order; indeed, if they are asked, they contradict us as though on the basis of prescription.
