Rise also, O most excellent fathers, authors of the various reforms, and see that your labors have now perished: your constitutions neglected, your exhortations despised, the protests of your solicitude held in contempt. By life and teaching you made the monastic order glorious, beloved by God, venerable to angels, holy to yourselves, and reverend to men. Where now are those merits? Where now are those labors of the saints?

Alas, venerable mother Religion, to what great calamity you have come. Those who once glorified you now despise you; those who honored you now hold you in contempt. You have abandoned the commands of your lawgiver; you have cast away the study of the Scriptures; therefore you have become contemptible. The sons whom you bore have done this evil to you. For as soon as monks began to savor earthly things, to neglect sacred reading, and to put inward things after outward, your excellent color was changed into filth. But what then? One who falls often sets himself to rise again; but you, once fallen, have never risen up completely.

What could Cluny, what Hirsau, what Melk, what the very recent Bursfelde, and whatever other reforms there may have been besides these, do for the whole order spread throughout the world? For although Cluny poured out the light of its integrity through all the provinces of the world, still it left many monasteries in various kingdoms in their former deformity. Indeed, from the time when our religion first fell, there has never been any general reform. O how easy is the descent to Avernus; how ready is the will to sin; how great, after a fall, is the difficulty of rising again to uprightness. All, so to speak, have fallen; few have risen from their deformity; many rot away in their pleasures.

I grieve over you, O flower of monastic beauty. I grieve, and very deeply indeed; and if I cannot help you otherwise, I pour out tears, hot and most bitter, for your contrition. But you too, O most reverend fathers, who love the honor of your order, who grieve over the contrition of our mystical Joseph, who desire to come to the eternal inheritance: attend to the state of our religion, look upon its desolation, think upon a remedy. See, wealthy monasteries are being snatched away in turn from the hands of Saint Benedict; see,

cloisters of monks are becoming dwellings of clerics, and monks are being transferred from bad pursuits to the worst. You err, O prudence of the world; you are deceived, O piety of the age. Never will you be able to make a good canon out of a bad monk.

But you also, transgressors, pay attention; apostates from the order, listen. What madness drives you to destroy for Saint Benedict monasteries that you did not build? Do you think yourselves free from damnation if, after casting off the monastic habit, you sin as clerics? In the strict examination, will a presumed license to sin save you? Perhaps in another habit you will be unknown among monks? We shall see whether God approves your transfer, when you are in that dreadful judgment, where no order but everlasting horror dwells. For what other dwelling he is to receive, who has become a worse canon from being a bad monk, I do not sufficiently understand.

Senseless men, enemies to your very selves, who will teach you to flee from the wrath to come? Who will be able, or dare, to defend your wicked cause against Saint Benedict in that dreadful examination? Whom will you have as advocate for your rashness? Saint Benedict, prince of monks, will stand as witness against you; he will accuse you and condemn you. Who will excuse you? Do not flatter yourself, wicked despiser of the regular vow; do not soothe your conscience. If you have Benedict as your adversary, you will find none of the saints as your friend. For whoever offends one citizen of the heavenly homeland [offends all]; where there is highest and blessed tranquillity, there too will be the indivisible unity of souls.

Who, then, will be able to deliver you from the hand of the Lord, if all the saints stand against you? Call upon those who now deceive you, who take from you the habit of the order, who lead you away with flattering words, who become collaborators in your apostasy, so that they may defend you from the tribulation that is at hand. For if the righteous will scarcely be saved, where will the apostate and destroyer of the holy order appear? In that day the justice of a saint will not free the wicked man, nor will it bring help to itself or to another. Rather, those very men whom you now win over to yourself with gifts and presents as collaborators in such presumption will then be witnesses of your unhappiness, and companions as well.
