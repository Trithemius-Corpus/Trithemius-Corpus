And indeed, to speak without envy, the holy religion of Benedict easily surpasses all orders, if one considers its dignity, its widely extended dominion throughout the world, its men distinguished in learning and holiness, and finally their multitude. For this venerable plant has produced eighteen supreme pontiffs, more than two hundred cardinals, sixteen hundred archbishops, and three thousand five hundred bishops. But if anyone calls this total of illustrious prelates excessive and brings it into doubt, let him turn over the chronicles and old deeds, and he will find a few named out of many. Yet no one should think that the former beauty of the order is to be measured by the present, since ours bears no equal comparison to that, when in our times monks do not shine, as once they did, either in knowledge of the Scriptures or in holiness of conduct. Perhaps even now you will find devout monks, though few, but simple men. Yet for the governance of souls in this time devotion is not enough, if knowledge of the Scriptures is lacking. But if our monks are neither devout nor learned, they are rightly judged unworthy of the governance of churches. Many, indeed, walk well as subjects; but if they are raised to the government of others, they have never remained in the tranquility of their former life. And, to say briefly what I think, neither are monks worthy of churches, nor churches of monks, in this time. The reason, however, is not hidden from the prudent; it will have to be brought into the light hereafter, in its own time. Now, if you please, let us inquire briefly what the present condition of our order is.

On the lamentable present condition of our order. Chapter III.

My eyes have failed because of tears; my bowels are truly troubled; my liver is poured out upon the earth over the destruction

of my order, since the humble little child has fainted within it, since, cast down without honor, it lies open to the hands of the enemy. Let no one despise me as I grieve; let no one accuse my sorrow. I do not seek earthly honors; I do not covet ecclesiastical dignities, but I groan that learning and holiness have fallen away from my order. What have we to do with vain honors, we who seek our life in heaven? Alas, the crown has fallen from our head; monastic devotion has grown lukewarm; charity has grown cold; the honor of regular discipline has very greatly failed. Where now in our order is the devotion once so abundant in its most sacred men? Where is the venerable erudition in every variety of the Scriptures? Where is the solicitude of abbots for the care of the Lord’s flock? We see many monasteries founded according to the rule of Saint Benedict, in which, apart from the name and the habit, we see no trace of regular discipline. Over this, fathers, take up lamentation; deplore the ruin of our most holy order, if perhaps God may turn to mercy and leave a blessing behind him. And you transgressors, who are the cause of these tears, return at last, even late, to your heart; return, and fear the indignation of your blessed lawgiver. Attend to the desolation of the order that you bring on by your evil; fear the wrath of the Almighty, which you provoke by your crimes. Woe to us. The city of our religion is being destroyed by enemies all around us; the house of discipline is being reduced to nothing; and no one is found to resist, no one to set himself as a wall for the house of God. We all seek the things that are our own; we all conceal the common necessity of the order, until little by little we too fail. Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, our houses to foreigners. Monasteries consecrated as dwellings for monks are now becoming stables for clerics and fortresses for fighting soldiers. What shall I say? I am ashamed to utter what men without conscience are not ashamed to perpetrate in the cloisters of monks, men who disturb the privacy of religious persons and turn houses of prayer into dwellings of merchants. I suffer with your calamities, O venerable religion of Benedict; I groan that you have been exposed to plunder by every passerby.
