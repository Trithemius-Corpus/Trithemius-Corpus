Therefore, if it can be done, if it seems good, let someone be chosen from the order, a man zealous, devout, learned, and experienced, who will fight no less with favor than with zeal; who will not prefer his own advantages to the public good; who will not flee from labors; who will attempt nothing by his own rashness contrary to the commission entrusted to him; and who, once appointed procurator of the order in the city, will always watch diligently over the aforesaid matter. I believe that, if such a man were available, he would forestall many dangers to the order. For it is very great foresight if the procurator in the city can denounce before the pope the false and fictitious things that perverse monks lie about as though they were true, for the sake of their own dismemberment. And this is not a work of great difficulty. For all the causes they allege are full of fear and falsehood. But when the vicar of Christ, who grants transfers of monks more from importunity than favor, sees that their allegations have been refuted by the diligence of our procurator, he will rejoice to have found an occasion for denying what he grants unwillingly. For he himself also fears the judgment of his Lord. Therefore, if we were vigilant, we would have him as defender and advocate of our order. Our negligence alone is accused; our stinginess alone is condemned.

Against those who uselessly consume the goods of monasteries and disdain to give help to the order even in a small measure.

Chapter VI.

But now I am pleased to turn the force of my words against those who do not deign to assist the holy order, even slightly, in such great necessities. These are worldly abbots, useless men, given over to pleasures, who, as Mantuan says, neglecting the worship of those above and scorning the command of the Thunderer, indulge Bacchus and serve Venus. They carry sacred vessels in gold; they build many altars with money; with gold they pour out wines; with gold their ceilings shine. They cover harlots with gems; they set banquets before dogs; enriched from the goods of the poor, made wealthy by plunder, puffed up by success, they laugh at those above and at Acheron alike. They have no care for the salvation of souls, no love of God, no reverence for sacred things, no practice of the virtues. These are the abbots rebellious against your chapter, who neglect statutes, spurn sanctions,

contemn the rule. From abject men they have been made great, but they impiously despise the order that honors them. Saint Benedict, ungrateful abbot, raised you up; he made you rich; he received you as a monk; he appointed you shepherd of his brothers. Why, then, do you not acknowledge the generosity of your lawgiver? Why do you not repay his benefits in return? What do you have that you did not receive from his rule? Who endowed the monastery entrusted to you? Was it not the devotion of princes and the alms of faithful peoples? Why, then, are you ungrateful for such great benefits? See, you think nothing less than of regular observance; you reject discipline; you scorn God; you despise his precepts; and you presume upon security. See, contrary to the rule you amuse yourself with the birds of the sky; see, contrary to the law of clerics, you hunt with unclean dogs; see, contrary to honor, you delight in the pleasures of the flesh; see what is most shameful: with the oath of chastity despised, you consume the substance of Saint Benedict with prostitutes and pimps. You are not afraid to spend thirty or forty gold coins in the bath, and yet to offer Saint Benedict even one coin, never. I lie if I have not known abbots who consumed more than fifty florins at the baths, among whom some are the first to resist the subsidies of the chapter. Others too, from among those who wish to be called observant, impatient of seclusion and quiet, unable to remain long in the monastery, judge every occasion suitable by which they can migrate here and there at great expense. What shall I say of destructive, curious, and superfluous things, which are no less liable to rebuke than to expense? Walls are adorned with paintings; pinnacles shine with gold; windows are marked with monuments. The poor cry at the door; the sacred order, pressed hard, asks for help; and no one deigns to assist. O abbot, servant of the belly, worshiper of vanity, enemy of piety, who shamefully pours out the substance of the monastery, who pours out wines with gold, who denies subsidy to your order: how will the account of your stewardship be rendered to Saint Benedict? If the rich feaster was buried in hell because he did not give Lazarus help from his own goods for God’s sake, where will the abbot be buried who denied to his own lord goods that belonged to another?
