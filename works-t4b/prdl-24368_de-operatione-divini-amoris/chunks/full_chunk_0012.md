Ninth, divine love effects an inestimable adhesion of union between God and the soul, when the spirit of the lover is dissolved, not by nature but by grace, into the abyss of divine contemplation, and, failing from itself, is wondrously transformed into participation in divinity. This adhesion of union in no way annihilates the human spirit, nor changes it into another essence; rather, while remaining what it is, it is carried above itself, and, while its first essential act remains, it is deprived of the exercise of the second. For, entering the new darkness of divine contemplation, it leaves itself and all things that have been created, or can be created, far behind; it ascends above every imagination, reason, or understanding, where, when it has failed from every action of a created or creatable being, exercising itself in God alone, it rests insensibly. For the mind of one who loves God must rise through affection to this experiential knowledge and, abandoning every creature, enter through the darkness to the Creator who makes darkness his hiding place. And first the mind itself leaves behind every operation of sense; then, in an unknown way, it rises affectively above the intellect; afterward, failing even from itself, it enters the divine darkness, in which, resting through ecstatic love, it is lulled to sleep. But to see God face to face as he is is granted to no soul, however holy, so long as it is contained in the prison of the flesh. For God says to Moses: No man shall see me and live. And that disciple whom Jesus loved says in a certain epistle: No one has ever seen God. Likewise holy Paul, contemplator of divine things, says: No human being has seen God, nor indeed can see him. For the true and immediate perception of God is promised to the lover in this life, but granted in the life to come. True love, however, raising the mind into that divine darkness, does not satisfy it completely, nor does it manifest the vision of God; but by the inward sweetness of divine delight it ineffably refreshes it, and in most secret ways breathes into the pilgrim a perceptible taste of heavenly felicity.

Tenth, the love of God rewards the soul that truly possesses it in the heavenly homeland with an incomparable gift, and honors it with outstanding prerogatives. For whoever in this life perseveringly loves the Lord Jesus with all his heart above all things, whoever keeps the exercises of holy love in fervor of spirit, when he departs from the body, with the holy angels coming to meet him, is immediately led into the bosom of most loving Jesus and honored with immortal glory. For love surpasses all virtues in faith, because no good work will have merit unless it has been rooted in the love of God. The more ardent a soul has been in the love of God, the greater it will be in glory. Truly, eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man, how great a sweetness of inward delight the Lord Jesus grants his lovers to taste beforehand in this life, and how great and inestimable a glory of eternal felicity he stores up for them. When speaking of invisible things, we are compelled to imitate painters; nor can we, stammering as best we can, express the condition and state of heavenly felicity except through words and likenesses sensibly known to us, which can in no way be compared with spiritual things or joys. For they depict God and the angels as composed in bodily form; we speak of things existing above human sense and understanding with words suited to our sense, although God and the angels have no body, and spiritual things do not admit likenesses of bodily or sensible things. Indeed, what and how great the joys of that heavenly homeland are, neither human reason can grasp nor any speech explain. The true inward lover of God, who prudently persists in the exercise of divine love, perceives many and most sweet things by tasting beforehand the sweetness from above; and although in this life he cannot attain to the fullness of the highest truth, nevertheless he is permitted to taste great things, hidden from others who do not love in this way. O how gladly, how joyfully, and how happily the soul of one who fervently loves the Lord Jesus goes out from the body; how gloriously it is exalted in heaven; with what ineffable joy it is inwardly suffused.
