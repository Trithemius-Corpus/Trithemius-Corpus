The oration of Lord Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, delivered at Erfurt in the annual chapter on the sixth day before the Kalends of September.

If it were permitted to me, without injury to obedience, most excellent fathers, to avoid this duty of speaking, I would try to excuse myself before you, with many words and in various ways; nor would I, an unskilled man among learned men, take upon myself so arduous an office of speaking, if I could have declined it without sin. For I neither can nor ought to teach men far more learned than myself; and I, a carnal man, blush to speak about the inward conversation of the spirit among very devout men. But since I am a man placed under authority, I am compelled to take up your command, and to consider not what I can do, but rather what I ought to do. Your authority commands me to speak, but my weakness enjoins silence upon me. Yet the authority of superiors must prevail, so that the weakness of one who obeys may be strengthened.

For I trust both in God and in your holy prayers, that humble obedience will supply to me what my inexperience takes away from me as I am about to speak. Therefore, fathers, assist me with your prayers before the merciful God; humbly ask his clemency to obtain the gifts of grace; and, turning as usual to the most reverend ark of piety, the Mother of God, say with me: Hail Mary.

¶ Hail Mary.

Since up to this point, most loving fathers, I have often given this synodal gathering a treatment of the formation of morals, at last it has seemed fitting, and no less honorable than useful, to say something briefly about the love of God, to which every regular institution ought to be ordered as to its final end. For whatever good, whatever holy, whatever just thing is done by us in holy conduct is all rightly referred to divine love, as to its most tranquil center. Inflamed by this desire, we have left the world; with this hope we have entered monastic solitude, so that, free and released from all cares of the age, without the hindrance of temporal things, we may be free for the contemplation of the love of God.
