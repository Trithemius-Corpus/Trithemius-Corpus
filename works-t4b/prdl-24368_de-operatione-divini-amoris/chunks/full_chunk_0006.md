He would scarcely contain himself; he would confound the enemies of the cross of Christ with a free voice; he would no longer fear to undergo any adversities of this world for the love of Jesus. O how many souls you have suddenly snatched from the danger of this age; how many sinners you have brought without hindrance into the joys of paradise. You made the holy angels of God stable in cleaving to their principle; from the beginning of the world you marked out the innocent; you chose the patriarchs; you ordained the prophets. Finally, you made the holy disciples of God despise the world; you wonderfully strengthened the martyrs; you purified confessors; you prescribed a rule of life for holy virgins. And why should I use many words? No one ever pleased God without you; no one, strengthened by your aid, ever displeased the highest and unchangeable good. Through you faith receives the reward of merit; through you religion receives increase; through you the rust of sin is consumed; through you alone every elect person is sanctified.

As a great fire consumes the few waters poured into it, so the immense power of divine love restrains carnal passions in a person. For whoever has truly conceived the love of God in his heart will burn with such desires and such affections toward God that, while the exercise of love continues, he feels no disordered affections of the flesh and desires nothing except God. And this is plainly a wonderful change of affections: one who previously could scarcely tame the passions of his flesh through many labors now, kindled by divine love, overcomes them in the silence of contemplation. But it is not granted to all to experience this, because it is reserved for few. For many think that their own conceptions, implanted in the senses, are divine love; and the higher they lift themselves through imagination, the more gravely they usually fall. For Scripture says: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” If you desire to ascend the holy mountain of divine love without danger, you must kill the beastly affections, because the soul is made one through love with that one principle of all things only when, stripped of every multitude of affections, it is lifted up alone and, raised above all imagination, reason, and understanding,

sees itself stripped bare of all things that can be spoken or thought, and ascends, free through affection, to the dwelling-place of love. Nor is it necessary here to instruct the true lover about the conditions of false love, since the true love of God brings with it so many most certain signs of itself that it does not allow the one who has it to doubt. And although a person does not know whether he is worthy of love or hatred, nevertheless I say confidently that one into whom almighty God deigns to pour the sweetness of his inward love will be able to know many things unknown to us.

Third, the love of God makes the one who has it despise all things and love absolutely nothing in this world. He is not drawn to honors and dignities; he delights in no riches or joys of the world; he always thinks of the Lord Jesus; he longs to come as quickly as possible to his sweetest embraces. Whatever is seen as delightful in the world is bitter to him; he delights in nothing other than in his beloved. For the soul, gathered together and united to itself, despises both itself and all things, so that without the hindrance of multiplicity it may stretch itself through love toward that one ineffable principle. O wondrous sweetness of divine love, how powerful is your fire, how sweet your bond, how blessed your experience. Whoever has you has all things; whoever does not have you has nothing. But no one can acquire you, O sweet love, who has wished to love anything in this world besides you. For you either dwell alone in the human heart, or you refuse to enter when not alone. Therefore whoever desires to have your fellowship within himself must despise all things except you, because insofar as he wishes to love anything besides you, to that extent he drives your sweetest love away from himself.

The soul cannot exist without love. For either it loves God and is truly blessed, or it relies on transitory things and is always wretched. No one can love God and the world at the same time, because he necessarily despises one or the other. But there is a great difference between the love of God and the desire of the present age. For the love of God makes a person always pure in conscience, glad in heart, honorable in conduct, outstanding in action, tranquil in mind. He covets nothing; his own condition is enough for him; he rests without ambition. But the love of the world stains its possessor in conscience with many crimes.
