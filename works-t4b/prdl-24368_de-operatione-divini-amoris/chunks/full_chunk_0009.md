And when the affect itself has now, through love, been admitted insensibly, without the intellect, alone and naked and not knowing itself, to that secret place of divine contemplation, there it sweetly comes to rest. Where, do you think, does the intellect remain meanwhile? It dwells inactive among lower things; once a teacher, now a pupil, it waits. It does not understand what is being done around the affect, because it has insensibly lost both itself and that affect. In this most secret silence of the mind, the soul through love, above all reason, imagination, and understanding, receives the ray of divine illumination, which neither the intellect itself understands nor the affect comprehends.

Happy, therefore, is the one who has deserved to receive true love of God from the very giver of love; who has learned to sit alone and to raise himself above himself. For if he perseveres prudently in the exercise of divine love, he will be illumined wondrously in his soul and will rejoice ineffably. We do not promise through love the vain knowledge of the world, but that knowledge which belongs to the flavorful knowledge of God. Yet we often read that even the knowledge of this world has been divinely infused into lovers of God, so that, when both this and that are granted, worldly prudence may be confounded by divine prudence. Nevertheless, the hand of the Lord is not shortened; rather, whatever will be necessary and useful for salvation to the lover, divine goodness will not deny to the one who asks. For the anointing of God will teach the lover good things.

Sixth, divine love raises the mind to heavenly things, as the Lord cries out in the holy Gospel: “Whoever serves me, let him follow me.” And again he says: “Father, I will that where I am, there my servants may also be.” The best servant of Jesus is the one who loves him above all things, who through love continually rests in his dwelling. Love raises the mind in three ways and calls it into a certain excess. For whenever desire is carried away in great devotion, the enflamed mind is raised above itself; in this elevation it is often kindled by such rays of inward love that,

utterly melted like wax, it is dissolved from its former state. For divine love makes the mind go out from itself and fly into the secret place of inestimable contemplation.

Second, through wonder the mind is led outside itself and raised to that highest summit of divine darkness. For love stirs up the desire to enjoy; the affect, so that it may enjoy, knocks through love; suddenly heavenly light presses itself upon the lover and, before the greatness of its beauty, turns the mind into wonder. For just as lightning, by its flashing, strikes back the eyes of those who look upon it, so that divine light above every intellect, when it slips into the mind, suddenly changes it into an excess of astonishment and wonder. And the more deeply the mind is cast into the depth of God through consideration of its own vileness, the more swiftly it is caught up and raised above itself through desire for the ineffable light.

Third, the mind is carried away into excess through the greatness of sweetness, when it is made drunk by the excessive delight of divine love. In these ways the love of God is accustomed to raise the mind from earthly things to heavenly ones, if it has found it burning purely, fervently, and sweetly through desire.

The ardent lover of Jesus Christ without interruption raises his mind to the one whom he loves; in various ways he reshapes his affections toward him. Whatever he does, whatever he undertakes, he always thinks of Jesus sitting on a throne not made by hands, and continually bears the memory of him in his breast. Now, when with the gaze of the mind he beholds him wondrously glorified in human substance, he humbly prostrates himself in spirit at his feet, pours out prayers with tears, and, melted in the bosom of Jesus, comes to rest. But then, passing beyond the humanity of the beloved, the whole mind directs itself toward the wondrous contemplation of his divinity; in this it enjoys such secret and thoroughly divine mystical splendors that, made external to itself, it seems not to be in the body. What this elevation of the mind, placed in the abyss of love, accomplishes through the depth of contemplation, only those understand who have received this gift from the mercy of the Lord.
