For when he has stipulated “with the whole heart,” he has left absolutely nothing to be assigned to another. Hence he also says elsewhere through the wise man: “Son, give me your heart.” O good God, what have you to do with my heart? Why do you ask my heart from me, you who gain no advantage from me? For you are always what you can be; my love cannot add anything to you, nor can the turning away of my mind take anything away. Yet you persist, you knock, and you say: “Son, give me your heart,” because where your treasure is, there your heart also will be. “I am the treasure of the soul; I am delight, I am love, I am the end, I am the beginning. Son, give me your heart, because every desire that is not fixed on me alone is held liable to mutability and eternal miseries. Give me your heart, so that my love may possess it entirely; let it admit absolutely no other besides me. Behold, I stand at the door of your heart and knock; why do you not open to me? I am the bridegroom of your wedding, O faithful soul, who rescued you with a mighty hand from tyrannical power and joined you to myself as beloved by my own blood. I am the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys; so that I might be able to take you to myself as my chosen bride, I was willing to undergo death. For through the transgression of your parents, polluted among adulterers, you set down your portion; you despised me, the guide of your youth; you had the devil, the impious corrupter of your virginity, as a most harsh exactor. But I, having compassion on you, like a giant ready to run his course, girded with human flesh, came into the midst; I undertook to fight a war against the tyrant; I poured out both my blood and my life for you; dying on the gibbet of the cross, I overcame the enemy. I, conquered by my exceeding love for you, in order to lead you, wandering far away, back to the delight of the heavenly homeland, put on human flesh from a virgin; visible and passible, I appeared on earth to human beings; for your salvation I submitted myself to many labors; I gave my tender body to be most cruelly scourged; I was willing to be crowned with thorns, spat upon, struck with blows, and afflicted with insults and mockery; half-dead, I carried my cross on my shoulders; horribly fixed to the wood by hands and feet, I stood. O who could express the anguish of my most innocent heart which I endured for you in that hour, when, full of wounds…”
