Often also the fervent lover of Jesus is lifted up toward Jesus by such great desires that he seems to himself no longer able to live; and, hidden in secret, he extends loving prayers to the beloved, and from the greatness of love sends forth his voice with tears: “O most loving Jesus, mediator of God and human beings, why do you not deliver me, placed as I am in this prison? Why do you leave me so long an exile in this misery? I do not desire to increase my merit by the delay of time; I do not look for a reward. You alone, O sweetest lover, are enough for my enjoyment, since you do not despise the spirit that loves you. Be my reward, you who have handed over to me, your unworthy creature, the fire of your love. O good Jesus, love raises hope; it strengthens confidence. Nevertheless, let your most holy will always be fulfilled in me, for although I vehemently desire to come to you, I do not refuse to remain in the flesh as long as you wish.”

In these ways, then, and by many more secret exercises, love raises the mind to heavenly things; through these it shows itself not idle, but as working many wondrous things in a human being.

Eighth, divine love makes the soul of its host, while still established in the flesh, known and familiar to the citizens of the heavenly fatherland, because whoever is loved by the king is venerated by the whole court. But our heavenly king loves the one who loves him, as he says in the holy Gospel: “He who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and will manifest myself to him.” Therefore the lover of Jesus is loved by him, and is honored by those holy spirits who are in the fatherland; and although a human being, according to his own understanding, cannot always understand this familiarity with the angels while in the flesh, nevertheless the mind itself can insensibly perceive wondrous things. O with what great charity and reverence, beyond every human understanding, the spirit of the lover of Jesus, released from the flesh, is received by the citizens above; with what great praises, altogether ineffable to a human being, he is honored, because when he enters, whom the emperor loves in a special way, the glad whole body of citizens runs to meet him. For when the spirit of one who loves Jesus goes out from the flesh, the citizens of heavenly blessedness, as one known and most dear to them,

receive him gladly as their fellow citizen, since they had known him while he was still placed in the flesh, lifted up so often by love to them in heaven. For the love of God prepares an open way for the soul to the sight of the highest emperor; it calls the citizens of the heavenly fatherland to meet the one who is passing over; it does not permit demons to draw near; it removes every obstacle. The spirit of a human being who loves Jesus above all things has as many familiar friends in heaven as he has had companions in the love of God. There the angelic spirits, confirmed in the highest charity of God, love most ardently the soul of a person who loves Jesus; they recognize their familiar fellow citizen, guard him while he remains in the flesh, and most gladly run to meet him as he departs. There the order of patriarchs attends the lover of God with wondrous favor; it rejoices with inestimable joy that a soul loving the divine majesty has arrived. There the holy prophets, foreknowers of future things, receive the soul gladly, taking up the spirit of the lover of God with inestimable joy. There the holy martyrs, clothed in white robes, come forward and lead the soul, fervent with divine love, to the feet of the Most High. There the most holy virgins will be present; with incredible exultation they will receive him and lead him, to be crowned with immortal beauty, to the throne of glory.

We read that very many saints in this life had the companionship of angels, and that these angels often came to meet them at their death, because wherever they see the love of their king, Jesus Christ, dwelling with sweet fervor, they come more frequently, as it were, to his dwelling place. For Jesus inhabits the heart of the one who loves him; he prepares a throne for himself in it; he drives out all the snares of evil affections round about; he establishes peace and concord in his kingdom. The holy angels also frequently render invisible service to the one whose rule over them they love. Therefore the soul blazing with the fire of divine love is familiarly known to the spirits above.
