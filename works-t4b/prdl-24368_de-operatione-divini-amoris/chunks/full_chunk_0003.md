And although the pain of my heart afflicted me beyond measure, nevertheless I gladly endured all things for love of you, so that I might rescue you from the power of the ancient enemy and join you to myself forever as my bride.

See, my dearest, at what price I redeemed you, I who did not hesitate to die for you by a most shameful death. What, O sweet beloved, what do you give back to your lover, or what do you repay? He does not demand gold and silver; he does not care for precious stones. In place of all these he requires faith and charity from you; he desires the dwelling-place of your love. Therefore give your Savior the lodging of your heart, he who opened heaven for you in his most holy blood. Open to him the chaste bridal chamber of your mind, so that he may grant you the secret of his sweetest love.

For the Lord Jesus nowhere goes forth empty-handed; the ineffable gifts of his grace accompany him. And although the little gifts that Jesus, king of heaven, brings with him are many and various, nevertheless they are all very precious; and among them one is most precious, to be purchased from him by the human heart alone. What is it? It is that very thing of which he said in the holy Gospel: “I came to cast fire upon the earth, and what do I want except that it burn?” The greatest and chief gift of Jesus is love, which indeed he pours into the saints and those who flee the vanities of the world, but denies to sinners who are carnally wise. No one understands this most secret gift of divine love unless he has first received it in hiddenness.

For truly Jesus, beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, is always ready for those who love him; he comes to meet those who meditate intently on his passion; he loves the simple and upright in heart; he does not admit curious lovers of the world into the sweetness of his love. Therefore I would rightly call those blessed who, having despised the business and cares of the world, desire to lie hidden in monasteries with themselves in the tranquility of a pure and simple mind; who, apart from God alone, love absolutely nothing in this world; who despise vain honors and the deceitful prosperity of the world; who, rooted in divine love, fear none of the adversities of this time. For the love of God loves nothing in this life except God himself; it fears nothing. For the one who loves, God is sufficient for all things;

outside him the soul requires nothing. The soul that truly loves the Lord Jesus above all things gladly remains alone with itself in secret. The lover of inward sweetness speaks in secret with his most loving Lord in words of burning love; he delights to remain alone with the Alone. He sits in secret, solitary but not idle, since love, which gathers all the powers of the soul to itself, in no way permits the lover to be idle. For Gregory, that lover of God, great among the great, spoke truly: “The love of God is never idle.” For it accomplishes great things, utterly incredible to those who do not love God.

And what does immortal love accomplish in mortal man? Great things, I say, and not to be disclosed to carnal people. The love of God, poured into the human heart, performs an operation that no one perceives unless he has experienced it. Alas for me, since if it belongs only to those experienced in the love of God to speak of this operation, my own lukewarmness imposes silence upon me. But since I have once begun, I shall speak of the operation of love, not what I have learned by experience in myself, but rather what I have received from experienced and devout men. Yet the more secret matters of divine love are not to be brought out in public, since only that person can understand them whom the very illumination of love has made skilled by long exercise. Therefore, honoring with due silence the mystical visions unknown to me, I shall pursue those things generally known to all, so that we who cannot attain those more inward things may at least strive with all our strength to imitate the more manifest ones.

And first, indeed, when the love of God is in a person, it works a wonderful contrition and a repentance for sins that is wonderfully effective. For Peter, the arch-apostle and lover of Jesus, says: “Charity covers a multitude of sins.” The love of God is a consuming fire, which Jesus, the most beloved bridegroom of souls, sent upon the earth, as the apostle also bears witness: “The charity of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.” Just as this material fire consumes the moisture set against it, if it has a more abundant blaze, so the more the love of God grows in a person’s heart, the more quickly it consumes the chaff of sin.
