I was able to obtain consent from him to release him. At last, with Christ inspiring him, he consented, though timidly, because he fears to serve nuns, who are changed very easily. He does not fear without reason, since, as experience has taught us, nuns have sometimes given their stewards a reward not praiseworthy for benefits received. A woman’s mind is found to be changed by the zeal of bitterness.

But with Christ granting it, I know that he will be useful to you. He is a Christian; he is a monk; he fears the Savior; and he is of good understanding. Therefore accustom yourselves to restrain feminine changeability, so that you remember to be constant: receive him with holy honor and sincere charity, laying aside every levity of womanly spirit, especially since, with the help of Christ the Redeemer, who cherishes charity, he can help you and restore the condition of temporal affairs in your monastery, which has collapsed. Farewell. From Spanheim, 17 Kalends of May, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ 1500.

When the person to whom the letters marked with the character of Tartys are sent has received them, being skilled in our art, let him observe the lord of the ascendant and do, most diligently, all and each of the things that must be done, and let him add the usual conjuration. And when he sees that the spirits sent are present, let him say these words to the principal one: Chabor mastotiel tuscuo reneas porean trismeny penatron. And when he has said these words with strong intention to the principal leader, he will at once approach calmly and reveal the secret.

CHAPTER XV.

Whose hour is called Quabron, and its supreme angel is called Serquanich, having under his command many princes, dukes, counts, and servants without number.

The third hour of the night is called Quabron. Its emperor and supreme angel is Serquanich, having under his command many princes, dukes, and counts in twelve orders, with a great continuous division of servants. For in the first order there are 10; in the second, 20; in the third, 30; in the fourth, 40; in the fifth, 50; in the sixth, 60; in the seventh, 70; in the eighth, 80; in the ninth, 90; in the tenth, 100; in the eleventh, 1,000; in the twelfth, 10,000. Here now we have named twelve of the more powerful spirits of Serquania. From their order, one, according to his turns, receives the dignity both of dukes and of counts. The remaining six never change their office, but are always dukes. But whoever wishes to know the names of more of them can easily attain his purpose through those whom we have named. Therefore, when you wish to work through these, observe the lord of the ascendant and write his character, with the other necessary things, on clean paper, and on its back this conjuration.
