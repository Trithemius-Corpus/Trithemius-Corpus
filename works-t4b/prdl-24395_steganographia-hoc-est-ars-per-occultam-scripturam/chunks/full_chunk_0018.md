CHAPTER IX.

WHOSE PRINCE IS CALLED MALGARAS, who dwells in the west, having under him 30 dukes for daytime operations and just as many for nighttime ones; their office is to announce the private secrets of friends.

Malgaras, the greatest prince of the west, is the inhabitant of the ninth mansion, which is in the equinoctial west. He has under him 30 dukes who preside over the day, and 30 others who preside over the night, together with their servants. The office of all these is to announce to friends the secrets and hidden matters of familiar friends. The dukes are good and most ready to obey the one who calls them, especially the daytime dukes, since they are more accustomed to converse among human beings. For the nighttime dukes, although they are good, nevertheless do not willingly converse with human beings, but avoid and detest the light exceedingly, wishing to lie hidden in darkness; and for this reason they often appear to the one who calls them through conjurations like bats. From all these we shall place twenty-four by their names in the table; twelve of them in the day, and just as many at night, are sufficient.

You now have, from the Malgaretic princes, twelve for daytime operations and just as many for nighttime ones, with their 640 servants. And note that Aroiz and Basiel are always solitary wanderers; Miniel, Basas, Aspor, and Delias can be called two by two, but the rest three by three. When you wish to have three, or two, or one, take care that you do not err in their names. Do as the table teaches you, without alteration, lest you fall into danger.

Song of the Conjuration.

MALGARAS ador chamteso buluetiny mareso bodyr Cadumir auiel casmyo tedy pleoryn viordi eare viorba, chameron vesy thuriel vl nauy, beuesy meuo chasmiron ty naor ernyso, chorny barmo caleuodyn batso thubra fol.

When the conjuration has been spoken, be a strong and steadfast man: those whom you have called will immediately appear to you visibly. But if those called from among the nighttime spirits do not come at once, do not on that account interrupt your work, but press them with the conjuration repeated until they obey. For they are somewhat slow and do not willingly come among human beings, as we said above.

Let it be a secret of the mind, which one does not wish to entrust either to a person or to letters.

Roth:

If, in matters that pertain to family business, money, a deposit, trust, or any affairs of human intercourse, you have a secret which you desire a friend far away to know, but it is of such a kind that it would undergo public examination with the greatest danger or loss to each of you, and for that reason it must be entrusted neither to letters nor to people: then, so that you may be secure and fear no one’s knowledge, turn yourself toward the west, call the duke, and entrust the hidden matter to him. He goes and faithfully carries everything through; if anything has been entrusted to him for you, he reports it.

Write any sort of letters you wish, in which you fear no one’s inspection.
