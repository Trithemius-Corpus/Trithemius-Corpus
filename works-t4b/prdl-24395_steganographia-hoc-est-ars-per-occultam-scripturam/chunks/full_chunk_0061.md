CHAPTER XIV.

WHOSE HOUR IS CALLED PANEZUR, AND WHOSE ANGEL, THE SUPREME COMMANDER, IS CALLED TARTYS, WHO HAS UNDER HIM MANY PRINCES, DUKES, COUNTS, AND SERVANTS.

The second hour of the night is called Panezur, and its angel is called Tartys. He has under his command many princes, dukes, counts, and subordinate servants, whom, when called, he is accustomed to send by their turns and ranks to the various services of those working in the hidden secrets of nature. Of these, twelve ranks have been assigned to our knowledge of Steganography, and they are sufficient for us for every operation. For in the first rank there are 10, in the second 20, in the third 30, in the fourth 40, in the fifth 50, in the sixth 60, in the seventh 70, in the eighth 80, in the ninth 90, in the tenth 100, in the eleventh 1000, and in the twelfth 100000.

Yet it rarely happens that an operation is carried out through all these spirits, because there is no need to be occupied with the higher ones, since the weaker ones will abundantly suffice for us in all matters, unless someone well instructed in the art and altogether perfect should desire to see the princes of all the aforesaid ranks and to know their names. Here we have named 6 of the dukes of Tartys, and 12 of the counts; also 1320 servants, numbered, distributed by their turns and ranks, who are sufficient for us for the experiment of the art in this hour. And note that, according to Solomon and Ratiel, all these dukes and princes of Tartys are benevolent and most ready for obedience; and in whatever form they are ordered to appear, they willingly assume it. Therefore, when you wish to work in this science, carefully observe the lord of the ascendant together with the other things that we prescribe; and write all the things that must be written, with this conjuration.

Conjuration.

Tartys chrybes faziel yrsoti haelnot dreor adeuar afy mearo Veny fatu pemerson.

Then, when all things have been most carefully completed and the conjuration read in due fashion, as is proper, the spirits called will soon be present, ready for your command.

Let the secret be whatever occurs to us, great and difficult.

The secret which we wish to communicate to a friend through the spirits may be whatever occurs to us according to the occasion and the variety of business, provided that it is not trivial, and such as can otherwise safely be entrusted.

Johannes Trithemius, to the nuns on the Mount of Saint Rupert, adorning the Catholic faith with shining virginity, sends the wish that they may continually merit eternal happiness by the most holy zeal of religion.

By the mercy of the eternal King, we prudently send to you the man long awaited, greatly experienced in temporal affairs, Johannes, native of Raunenstat, for a long time the reverend and zealous abbot of the monastery of Vilsdorf, who freely resigned that abbey. On account of him, as I had promised you, I went to the monastery of Lüneburg, where he was serving Christ, occupied in continual prayers and readings of the holy scriptures with the monks of that monastery; and I asked him to come down to your convent, to take up the care of temporal affairs, and to become procurator and cellarer, since the Abbot of Limburg, with his usual instability, had changed his mind.
