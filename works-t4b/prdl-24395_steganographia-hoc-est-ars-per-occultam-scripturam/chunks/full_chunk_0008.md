It is not necessary to summon all of them at once; a few suffice, and sometimes one can satisfy the operator, whether the operation is nocturnal or diurnal. For since they are well-disposed, quiet, and peaceful, they are more often solitary wanderers than tumultuous in a crowd. Therefore, when you wish to work through them and to communicate the secret of your mind through them to someone, however absent he may be, do this. Write on paper previously prepared in the proper manner, as you know, whatever narrative you wish and in whatever language, because it makes no difference, since he will perceive your mind not from the paper but from the Spirits. While writing, you shall turn yourself toward the subsolanus; when it is completed, say this conjuration:

Padiel aporsy mesarpon omeuas peludyn malpreaxo. Condusen, vlearo thersephi bayl merphon, paroys gebuly mailthomyon iltheartamarson acrimy lon peatha Casmy Chertiel, medony reabdo, lafontriaciel mal arti bulomeon abry pathulmon theoma pathormyn.

When you have completed these words, you will see two, or at least one, of the Spirits present most peacefully and ready to obey your will. To him you shall entrust your secret, and he will faithfully convey what you have commanded. But when the letters have come by messenger to the person to whom they are sent, if he is alone, well; if he is with others, let him withdraw apart if he can, or if he cannot, let him turn toward the subsolanus and say this conjuration in silence:

Padiel ariel vanerhon chio tarson phymatto merphon ampriscoledabarym, elfopihroy mesarpon amcorsy, paneryn atle pachungel thearan vtrul vt solubito beslontylas gomadyrn triamy mesarnothy.

When these words have been duly spoken, the Spirit sent will immediately appear, revealing to him perfectly, with no one else perceiving it, whatever you entrusted to be revealed to him, without deceit and without any danger. No one malicious and wicked will easily be able to work in this art; but the better and purer someone has been, the more willingly and with greater cheerfulness the Spirits obey him. And the more each person has kept this science hidden, the more easily he will operate. And note that this Padiel with his Spirits is appointed to announce secret notices for the correction, imprisonment, and punishment of wrongdoers, as we have shown in the example set below.

*THE SECRET OF THE INTENTION, Roth; WHICH IS TO BE ENTRUSTED NOT TO LETTERS BUT TO THE SPIRITS OF THE SECOND DWELLING.*

Someone has been denounced before a prince, and some crime of lese-majesty or any other offense is laid against him. The prince wishes him to be punished by his official, from whom he is far distant, but he does not wish to write this very thing to him, lest, if the letters are disclosed by any chance, the accused be warned and flee, or resist with the forces of friends joined to him, or attempt something sinister against the prince or the laws of the country. He writes this or any other narrative to his prefect, one which he would not fear to be read by everyone; but he entrusts the secret to the Spirit in the manner we have described above, who will faithfully tell his secret to the person to whom it is sent, provided that person has first been instructed in this art.

Sincere charity. I send you a prayer, composed rather beautifully and briefly for the devotion of penitents, which, in the fullness of my goodwill, I did not wish you alone to possess, and it is as follows. Free us, Jesus Christ, eternal savior of all good people, and forgive the offenses of the guilty; hear our groans, assuaging the storms of vices, and, renewing the oldness of conscience, lead us back to eternal paradise. Good Jesus, remit our sins to us who pray, since we have gravely offended and have often relapsed. Sweetest savior, heal our infirmities, we beseech you. Be kind to those who ask you, most pious Jesus, and hear us, your servants, as we supplicate. We are human beings, wrapped in constant miseries and plunged in the greatest storms. Deliver us, most victorious comforter of the afflicted, since our life, constantly buried in felicity, is corrupted. Have mercy at least, most merciful Jesus and savior; look upon the humility of those who love you, and do not allow us exiles to be submerged in the flood and foulness of vices, in the love of the world. Until now we have lain sluggish, and, bound by vices, we have slighted our salvation.
