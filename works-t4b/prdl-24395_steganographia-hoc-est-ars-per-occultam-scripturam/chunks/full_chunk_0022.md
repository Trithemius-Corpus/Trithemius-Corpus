Love God above all; continually devote yourselves to works of mercy with justice; flee impiety; cultivate righteousness; love the good; always speak praises to God; assist the poor with alms; obey the commandments of God. Do not abandon truth; honor the ministers of God in the churches; pay tithes; lodge travelers; free the innocent; feed the hungry; obey your superiors; love your neighbors honorably, doing injury to no one at all; cultivate the Christian religion most devoutly; abhor the vain, harmful, and superstitious customs of the gentiles. Keep the evangelical teachings and live according to the commandments of God. Flee the pleasures of the flesh, and show yourselves honorable and good; do not stain the purity of mind which you carefully guard. Remember, I beseech you, what Christ our Lord cried out in the holy Gospel: Blessed are the clean of heart, that is, those who preserve purity of soul in a muddy body for God’s sake. But let cleanness of heart not be lacking in continual and most humble prayers, since prayer purifies the mind, cools those who burn, and drives far away the temptations of the devil. Hence the holy Apostle Paul teaches you to persist in diligent and devout prayers, so that you may be able to overcome harmful temptations. We join you in marriage so that your family may be propagated and established by decree of the law, not so that you should overflow with harmful pleasures. For the pleasures of the flesh kill the soul and corrupt the most noble dignity of human substance, which belongs to things to come. Therefore, mindful of these things, know that you are wayfarers, destined to return to the homeland to which you have been called. The present life is very short, and the delight of the age comes most swiftly to an end. Therefore live in marriage in such a way that after death, when you are exalted, you may come to the kingdom of heaven. Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, published this on the day before the Ides of March, in the year of the nativity of our Lord Jesus Christ 1500.

When the person to whom these or any other letters are sent has received them, once he has recognized the sign of the great Usiel, let him do secretly what he knows from the art must be done. Then let him turn himself toward Subcircius and say the charm: Usiel afoyr paremon cruato madusyn sauepy mauayr realdo chameron ilco paneras thurmo peã elsoty fabelrusyn iltras charson frymasto chelmodyn. When this charm has been said, the spirit will show himself visibly and will disclose the things entrusted to him; and if anything has been entrusted to him by that man, he will report it to the sender.

CHAPTER XII.

THE SPIRIT WHOSE SUPREME COMMANDER, Cabariel, dwells toward that quarter of the world which is called Circius from the wind, and he has seventy subordinate princes by day, and as many by night.

The great commander Cabariel inhabits the twelfth mansion of the described world, which is toward that quarter from which the wind Circius blows, together with infinite spirits. Among them, fifty are appointed for the work of steganography by day, and as many by night at all times; they command many very powerful servants. The office of these spirits is to inform friends of secrets, reveal plots, and warn an absent person of those against whom he must especially be on guard. From all these we shall name a few.

You now have, out of the hundred princes of the great Cabariel, twenty, and they are sufficient for you at present. The first ten of them are appointed for operations by day, with 500 servants; and the other ten likewise for operations by night, with their 500 servants. And know that the spirits presiding over daytime operations are far more benevolent and readier to obey, so that it is often necessary to compel the nocturnal spirits themselves with a certain force, and more often to repeat the conjuration. They bring their servants with them when commanded; if they have not been commanded, they hide them. When you wish to operate through the ministry of these spirits, turn yourself toward that quarter which we have named, and, after first setting forth the things which customarily must be set forth, say the following mixed with a conjuration:

Cabariel onear chameron fruani parnaton so fiel bryosi nagreal fabelrontyn adiel thortay nosruau pena asesiel chusy.

When the charm has been completed, if it is during the day, the spirit called will be present at once, without delay. But if it is at night and the spirit whom you have called delays in coming, press him as often as needed by repeating the charm until he comes, for they are accustomed to hold it in great reverence.
