CHAPTER XIII.

WHOSE HOUR IS CALLED OMALHARIEN, AND WHOSE SUPREME ANGEL IS CALLED SABRATHAN, WHO HAS UNDER HIS COMMAND MANY PRINCES, DUKES, COUNTS, AND SERVANTS.

The first hour of the night is called Omalharien, and its angel is called Sabrathan, who has many other spirits under him, serving various magical operations according to their times and orders, as Hermes Solomon the Jew, most experienced in magical matters, attests. Of these, many are deputed to the operation of our art, by turns and according to their orders. For in the first order there are 10; in the second, 20; in the third, 30; in the fourth, 40; in the fifth, 500; in the sixth, 600; in the seventh, 70; in the eighth, 80; in the ninth, 90; in the tenth, 100. All these are sent into the ministry of secrets by their turns and hidden orders, of which we neither have full knowledge nor speak.

Here now we have, from the spirits of Sabrathan, 5 named dukes and 10 counts, who have 2,000 servants assigned to them by their turns and orders. Therefore, when you wish to operate through these, observe the lord of the ascendant, and write his character with the other necessary things on a clean sheet, and this conjuration on the back.

Conjuration.

Sabrathan odiel melros rhupis othiam elroz adiel methiel mear naturiel lahan irsoti brestion dreor chametson. Then, after the things customarily to be prefaced have first been set forth, as soon as you have spoken the prescribed conjuration, you will see the spirits called to be present. With them do whatever you wish according to the precepts of the art.

Let the secret be as desired.

It will be a great secret, and a difficult matter, one that would do harm if made public; and for that reason it must be entrusted neither to letters nor to a notary, but should come under the ministry of the spirits who preside over the hour in which you are operating. It is not safe to summon spirits in trivial matters; but where human ministries fail, theirs are to be sought in the art.

Let the tenor of the letter be as desired.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Brother Nicolaus Basselius, monk of Hirsau, with greeting, says that he should present this prayer.

Almighty God, exalted Creator of all things, who through Christ redeemed us Christians, gave Catholics the zeal of his love, and through the light of faith made known the remission of sins to believers. Moreover, he most generously gave his beloved Son, the gracious light, to free all of us believers from the power of the enclosed Tartarean king, and to destroy the dark prison and most cruel Zabulus. What, as Christians, can we worthily render for the passions of Christ, weak men, professing the Catholic faith without works of righteousness? Therefore, praying most devoutly to you, we praise our Redeemer with the highest praises, saying: praise be to you, eternal light, most kindly Savior of all, who redeemed us by your blood. Jesus Savior, look upon our frailty, and pour into our hearts the zeal of the Catholic faith. Holy is your zeal; it works compunction in the hearts of those seeking you, Christ most kindly Savior. O eternal light, sweetest Jesus, illumine the darkness of your servant with your accustomed goodness. Jesus, eternal Savior, save our souls; may you grant us eternal joys. Christ, hear your humble servants, most unworthy of all. Grant us, who humbly ask, a good zeal. Amen.

We wrote on the 18th day before the Kalends of May, in the year of the Lord 1500.
