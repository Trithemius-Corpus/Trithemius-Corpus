For just as a good and honorable lover can, for private or common good, at any time and as often as he wishes, safely, secretly, and without any suspicion on the part of any mortal, perfectly, fully, and most completely make known the secret of his will to another who knows this art, by letters passing through everyone’s hands, whether open or closed, so that no one, however learned or curious, can suspect anything about the sender’s secret, nor, even if he does suspect it, detect it, and can disclose and express it at any distance of places; so too any perverse, wanton, or malicious man, even if previously wholly ignorant of the Latin tongue, as soon as he has acquired this art, which, with me as teacher, you will be able to acquire in two days at the longest, will thereafter write letters in Latin, with fitting composition, open in any narrative, elegant and sufficiently adorned, to me or to another skilled in this art. In these letters he will wrap the secret of his will, accessible to me alone, in such mysteries by cabalistic power that it cannot in any way be penetrated by anyone, however studious or experienced, without the spirits of this art, about which I am going to speak.

Thereafter the learned and the unlearned, man and woman, boy and old man, good and bad, chaste and wanton, in Latin or in any language known to him, would write throughout every region of the world Latin, Greek, or barbarian letters free from all suspicion, in which they would present one thing openly to those who do not know the art, and another thing secretly to those who do know it. Nor, once this knowledge had been made public among the reprobate, would the faith contracted by sacrament between married persons thereafter remain so great: for a wife, though hitherto ignorant of the Latin tongue, now sufficiently taught through chaste, honorable, and most holy words of any language or idiom, could understand most fully the evil and impure mind and intention of an adulterous or fornicating lover, even while her husband carried the letters and praised them as excellent; and in the same way, as broadly and fully as she wished, she could most safely answer her desire to him in the same or other letters, in a beautiful and quite highly adorned sequence.
