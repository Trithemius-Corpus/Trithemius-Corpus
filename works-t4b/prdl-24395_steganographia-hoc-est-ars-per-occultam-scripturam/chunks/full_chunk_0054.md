Here now you have 10 named from among the dukes, and 600 counted from the order of servants. The dukes and counts are summoned by name, and they are sufficiently ready to obey the one who calls them in all matters that pertain to the ministry of this art. Therefore, when you wish to work through them, write on clean paper the characters of the lord of the ascendant of the twelfth house, together with the other things that are to be written according to custom; and on its back write this conjuration according to the precepts of the art, as you know.

*Conjuration.*

LAmedon mosco vrsoty tharuean: dayr Lays emel thebrean rasoty bamerson.

Then, after adding the things that are customarily to be added according to the art, say this same conjuration with strong intention, as is proper, and immediately the spirits summoned will appear visibly. Entrust the secret not to the count but to the duke, unless perhaps you have summoned a count without a duke. Although I do not deny that this can be done, nevertheless, according to the precept of the art, at least one duke must be summoned with as many counts as you wish.

*The subject of the secret, as you wish.*

Let the secret be of whatever kind you have for a friend, which you do not wish to be shared with anyone else. Therefore one must trust neither letters nor messengers, because fraud can intervene in either case. Summon as many of the aforesaid spirits as you wish; entrust the secret to the more powerful one, fearing nothing, because they are good and faithful in all things.

*Let the form of the letter be as you please.*

*Of Steganography*

through slowness, to discharge what you promised. It is not fitting for a good and honorable man, born of noble parents and ancestors, to violate a promise. He had promised to return them very diligently in a short time. It is a mark of fickleness to postpone a promise, as it were, to the Greek Kalends. Have I not waited long enough with great goodwill? To promise the greatest things and to repay the smallest is most shameful. Good faith adorns a soldier. What more serious fault is there than to deny the loan that was promised? Nor does it befit a Christian to be unwilling to observe an agreement. The constitution of Christian law commands one to keep a promise, to repay a loan, and willingly to do also for others what you desire to be done for you. I demand back my books, which I had lent to you when you begged for them. Set aside the longer excuses which you promise until the Greek Kalends. You keep my mind attentive with brief delays. Consider your honorable name and the noble family of the Bunans, and send me back my volumes. Farewell from Sponheim, April 2, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When you have received these letters, or any others of the kind, from the one who sent the spirit to you, once you have recognized the sign of Barquiel, observe in the same hour the lord of the ascendant and write its character, with the others, on a clean sheet; and you will secretly read the conjuration written on the back, as is proper. Immediately the spirit that has been sent will appear. As soon as you see him, say these words to him:

Mefarym burne theor alueas casuean cralti lierto aply charmoyS.

When these have been said, the spirit that has been sent, coming nearer, will faithfully reveal to you the secret entrusted to him. But see that you are strong, constant, and fearless in all things.

*Chapter VIII.*

*Whose hour is called Iafanym, and its angel is called Osmadiel, who has under his command 10 dukes and 100 counts. The number of servants is uncertain.*

The eighth hour of the day is called Iafanym, and its principal spirit is called Osmadiel, according to Picatrix in the magical writings. He has under his command 10 dukes, 100 counts, and innumerable servants assigned not only to the operation of our Steganography but also to many magical experiments.
