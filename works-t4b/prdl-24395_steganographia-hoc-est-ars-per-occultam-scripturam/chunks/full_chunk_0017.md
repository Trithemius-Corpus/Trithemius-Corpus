You have, then, from the thirty principal spirits of Masteriel twenty-four, with seven hundred and twenty servants. The first twelve of these preside over daytime operations, and the remaining twelve over nighttime ones. Therefore, when you wish to perform some operation in Steganography through them, turn yourself toward Favonius, and, after first doing the necessary things required by the art, call whichever one you wish from those named above, as is proper, and he will come without delay. The conjuration: Maseriel bulan lamodyn charnoty Carmephin iabru caresathroyn asulroy beuesy Cadumyn turiel bulan Seuear; almos lycadufel ernoty panier iethar care pheory bulan thorty paron Venio Fabelronthusy. When the spirit whom you have called appears, entrust your secret to him to be carried safely, hesitating in nothing at all, speaking silently to him, if you are in the midst of others, as you know.

Rothi

Let the secret of your mind be such.

You have secrets in the hidden sciences: philosophy, the magic of the stars, Cabala, arithmetic, or any arts whatsoever, which you desire a friend, absent near or far, to know; but they are such things as it is not safe to entrust either to letters or to messengers. Or certainly, if he is to send secrets to you, this must be pressed; or even to commend someone philosophizing, yet so that the others do not know. Call one or two of the spirits mentioned above; set out the secret. He undertakes the burden, will carry it to the friend, and will report back to you what was entrusted by him.

Kostli

Almighty, everlasting God, most just rewarder of the good, who wished your son to become a participant in our race, so that he might redeem us most wretched ones from diabolical envy; who by kindness alone, taking on the overflowing form of our nature incorrupt from the flower of the virginal womb, when the holy archangel Gabriel announced that the Virgin would conceive by your most blessed spirit, remained a perpetual Virgin, immaculate, more excellent than men and angelic spirits. The most holy and most reverend Virgin Mary, wholly without knowledge of male companionship, gave birth to the almighty king, God and man, bearing without pain, receiving without sorrow the crying God and man, always remaining immaculate, most chaste, most innocent, higher than men by the virtue of humility, full of charity, most worthy of every praise and to be venerated by all: the most blessed and always to be praised empress of the angels, most resplendent, noble, renowned, and truly most splendid. Your son admits her, with most holy favor, in all things whatsoever she has asked, and venerates her reverently as his mother, more excellent than all creatures. He is accustomed never to deny her anything she requests, but reverently consents to your most holy will, granting all things for her honor. She will stand for us on the last day, helping us who venerate the dear merits of the gracious Virgin. God, light of truth, we most devoutly pray: pour into us, O God, grace and mercy for all who call upon you and humbly ask forgiveness of their faults; and do not despise us wretched ones, but mercifully receive us, trusting in your goodness, for love of the Virgin Mary, who rejoices in God, and of all your elect. Amen. I, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, wrote this in the year of the Lord’s incarnation or nativity 1500, on the fourth day before the Ides of March.
