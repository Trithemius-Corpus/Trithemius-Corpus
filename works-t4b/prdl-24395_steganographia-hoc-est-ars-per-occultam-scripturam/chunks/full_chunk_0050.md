In this table we have named 4 from among the dukes, 8 from among the counts, and 1,730 counted from among their servants, who are sufficient for us for all the operations of this art in the third hour of the day. Therefore, when in this hour you wish to call a spirit to carry some message to a friend, let no fewer than at least two dukes, and the same number of counts with their servants, be called. But if you wish to call more or fewer, you may do so; nevertheless you will need to have a conjuration addressed to the universal emperor, without whose command the spirits do not violate their order. Therefore write on a new sheet the character of the lord of the ascendant and the others in the order of the planets, with the sign of the twelfth house, in the manner that the instruction of the art requires. On the back of the sheet write this conjuration.

Conjuration.

Vequaniel, odiel mefrij fotiel mear iamy otiel aslofian yrfothreotion drearij fabel merufin.

When the conjuration has been written, and when those things that are required by the art have been added beneath it, say it in a low voice, so that it is heard by no one; and immediately the spirits called will come. When you see them, entrust the secret to the superior one, whom you will easily recognize from his dress, because the one who is first among the other spirits always appears crowned.

Whatever the Secret May Be.

Whatever the secret may be that you have, and that must be entrusted to an absent friend, entrust it neither to letters, lest they be read by anyone, nor to a courier, lest by some occasion, or corrupted by a price or by fear, he betray you publicly. Call the spirit appointed to the hour, entrust the secret to him, fearing nothing: he will carry it faithfully.

STEGANOGRAPHY

He will form letters at will.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Arnold Bostius, Carmelite of Ghent, his fellow philosopher: greeting and charity.

Since I have known you as a lover of humane arts, a devout worshipper of the Christian faith, and a very great supporter of all philosophers, I gladly oblige your requests, moved by the wonderful pleasure of friendship. Behold, at your urging I send to you the book previously noted, concerning ecclesiastical writers and Catholic doctors, so that you may have something with which to delight your mind. But send back to me the codices of Hermes, the books of Dionysius on spirits, and the great work of Puenclus on winter [questions]. May Christ deign to adorn the eyes of our heart with the most fervent zeal of his love; and may he cause us to have love for heavenly goods, and, stripped of desires for the lowest things, may he teach us to despise vain honors, so that we may be able to penetrate the sublime matters of the art and also the great secrets of nature, and may have a continual desire for philosophy, to purify our affections, by which we may always adorn the Catholic faith with most honorable morals. Hold fast the great saying: you are a philosopher; give up everything and you will find it. Regard the hour of death as near, at which may you, prepared, meet what you attempt. Farewell. 3 Kal. Apr. 1500.

When you have received any letters sealed with the character of Vequaniel, write on a new schedule the figure of the lord of the ascendant and of the twelfth house, with the other things that are required for the art, and on its back the aforesaid conjuration; and immediately the spirit sent will appear visibly. As soon as you see him, say this verse.

Verse of the Conjuration.

Fameron aprois liernoti stadiuear diuiel sauean Lamersy.

When these words have been said, receive the word from the mouth of the spirit without fear, because he is sufficiently faithful. And take care that you do not operate in public before others.
