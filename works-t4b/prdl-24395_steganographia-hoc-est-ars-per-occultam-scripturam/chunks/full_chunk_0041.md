*Book One.*

...we have learned by the revelation of the aforesaid spirits, and have tested quite fully. Thus among the other spirits who favor this art there is one who has received the name Menadiel by reason of his office. He has under him 20 princes and dukes, 100 counts, and many servants, whose number is uncertain to me. All of them are faithful in commissions and excellent messengers in the great affairs of kings and princes; through them we shall work marvels.

R. R. R.

Larmol, Benodiel: 20, 10, 100; Barchiel, Nedriel

Draffiel, Charsiel: 20, 10, 100; Amasiel, Curafyn

Clamor, Samyel: 20, 10, 100; Barucho, Tharfon

We now have six of the dukes named, and the same number of the counts, and also 390 of the servants assigned to them by their turns and orders. They have this order among themselves: on the first turn two dukes and one count are called; on the second turn that count is called again, and now comes into the order of the dukes. On the third turn the one who had been the first duke of the first order becomes count, and so on in succession. Therefore, when you wish to perform something through the spirits of Menadiel, consider carefully which duke and which count must be called by you according to the time and hours; and, once the things to be set forth beforehand have been set forth, say the conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Menadiel marfoy peanos onael chamerusyn theor ianothy ofayr melros tudayr penotfyn fachul tarno roseuas peatham asiel morfoy maplear casmyron storeal marpenu nosayr pelno dan layr thubra elnodion carsephydrumos fabelmerusyn andu pean, purays calbyn nachirloes philuemy calaner.

When this conjuration has been duly completed, the called spirit will be present, namely first one count, two dukes, together with servants.

*Let whatever occurs be a secret.*

Whatever may occur according to the time or the variety of circumstances, let it be your secret for a male or female friend, something you do not wish to become common to anyone else. Call the spirit, entrust the business to him, and fear nothing; he is the most faithful of all, and will carry out well what has been entrusted to him.

You will be able to compose letters on these matters, of whatever sort you wish, at your pleasure.

Great and eternal are the future benefits of those who preserve innocence: that men rejoicing should be present amid angelic praises; that they should behold that exalted light, the splendor of eternal blessedness, and see the face of majesty in its own proper light. Attend, all of you, and wipe away with tears your exacted negligences; put off useless cares and harmful ones, and the deceptions of unstable things. O muddy men, shudder; at last seek the gain of souls. Be honorable in good morals and virtues; despise vanities; humbly call upon God; do not covet the world, which pours in bitterness. In the year 1509, on the Kalends of April. S.

When he to whom these letters are sent has received them, being already skilled in the art, let him, once he has recognized the character and sign of the supreme prince Menadiel, and after those things that are customarily to be set forth beforehand have been set forth, say the conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Menadyel murty chamero le dayr pean cathurmo phameron etsoti pray saruepo, fabel merij rean, charonietlas Meduse fayrlamerosyn alty merchahon.

When the charm has been spoken, the spirit sent will appear visibly to the one who calls him, and will faithfully and secretly report into his ear everything that he has among his commissions.

*Chapter XXIX.*

*Whose supreme prince is called Macariel, having under his command several dukes, princes, counts, and servants appointed to various ministries.*

Macariel also, one of the supreme spirits who preside over the operations of Steganography, is not to be omitted by us, along with his princes and servants, because he is quite useful, prompt, and faithful for whatever we may wish. He has 40 princes, of whom no fewer than four are ever sent into ministry, namely three dukes and one count, together with not a few servants. They observe this order among themselves: by their turns one of the dukes always holds the office of count, according to the four parts of the year.
