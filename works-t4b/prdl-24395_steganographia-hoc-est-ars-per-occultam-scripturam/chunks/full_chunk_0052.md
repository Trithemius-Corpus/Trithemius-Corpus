BOOK TWO.

CHAPTER V.

Whose hour is called Fealech, and whose spirit and supreme commander is called Sasquiel, having under his command 10 dukes, 100 counts, and a very great number of servants.

The fifth hour of the day is called Fealech, and its spirit is named Sasquiel, a great and powerful commander, who has under his command, among many others, 10 dukes and 100 counts, with their attendants, appointed to the service of diverse and especially secret arts and of this science of ours.

And it should be noted that the dukes are rarely called in the fifth hour for the operations of Steganography, because they are not always necessary, since the counts, together with the servants assigned to them, are abundantly sufficient for us for every operation. Yet if anyone should wish to see some of the dukes also, and to make use of their power, he may call one, two, or more; and they will come without delay, for they are sufficiently well disposed and ready to obey those who operate.

Of the dukes of Sasquiel we have the ones already named; of the counts, 10; and from the number of servants, 5550, whose service is sufficient for every operation of the present hour. Therefore, when you wish to work in Steganography in the fifth hour of the day through one of the aforesaid, write the character of the lord of the ascendant and of the twelfth house, together with the other things required for the art, on a clean sheet; and on its back write this conjuration, in the manner that you know.

The Conjuration.

Sasquiel adres rhetroleti rosiel emelto satu olmeniel istoti sauear naudiel liernoti chameson. When these things have been completed, and the other necessary things added, say the conjuration itself, as is proper; and immediately you will visibly see the spirit whom you have called present before you, to whom you may safely entrust the secret.

96 STEGANOGRAPHY: An Argument for Announcing a Secret.

Let there be any sort of secret of ours for a friend, which we want no one besides him to know; and let it be of such a kind that it is not safely entrusted either to letters or to messengers. Let us call one of the counts of Sasquiel named above; let us entrust the secret to him; let us add letters sealed with his character; and we shall be safe.

The Tenor of the Letter, as Desired.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to the religious brother Nicolaus Baselius of Durckheim, monk of Hirsau, greetings.

Good brother, you neglect holy obedience and humility; you undermine the constitutions of Benedict. By the report of all who have come successively, I have learned that you hate good zeal, despise the norm of religion and Catholic obedience, and occupy yourself with secular works and arts. Good brother, what is being done? You have disturbed us, and you are zealous for foolish and double-tongued men; you love hatred, bitterness, quarrels, and contentions; you hate goodness; you are double-tongued, contentious, and proud. Fool, what are you doing? The time is at hand to correct these negligences of yours more quickly, according to justice and zealously; and unless you hasten humbly to us, to break the zeal of bitterness with the lashes of humiliations, we are compelled by our office to be zealous against everything that is seditious toward the purity of faith and Catholic morals. Farewell from Sponheim, the day before the Kalends of April, in the year 1500.

When the person skilled in the art to whom the letters are sent has received them, after recognizing the character of Sasquiel and after the usual things have first been done and are to be done, let him say the above-mentioned conjuration; and immediately the spirit who was sent will appear and present himself visibly. When he has been seen, let him at once say these words: Fabelmerusyn aueual vearyan cralti penason. When the spirit has heard these words, he will at once approach and reveal the secret.
