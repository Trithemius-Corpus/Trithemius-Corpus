Song.

CAMESIEL aphroys chemeryn mear aposyn. Layr pean noema ouear ma fere calty caleuo thorteam chametonianoar pe lyn Layr, baduson iesy melios ionatiel delassar rodiu al meron fauean fabelion clumarsy preos throen benarys sauean demosyon laer-noty chamedonton. When these things have been duly completed, the general messenger of secrets whom you have called will be present, ready, faithful, and wholly secret. :-:

If I wish to warn a friend secretly about a bad man.

There is a certain man, under the appearance of honesty, a wicked babbler. Knowing that I have influence with a friend, he asks from me letters of recommendation to him. I cannot refuse, yet I do not wish my friend to be deceitfully deceived by him. I write the letters; I praise the vagabond in astonishing fashion. He reads them, exults, and promises himself that an open way of laying traps has been offered to him. I give him the letters; he goes away glad. I call the spirit and send him to my friend, so that he may warn him and disclose my secret.

I send letters of recommendation pleasing to the man who is being recommended.

Johannes Tritth, Abbot of Sponheim, to Johann Vigilius, lord Wackor, most distinguished doctor of both laws, sends greetings.

I send the bearer of the present letters to you, most beloved Vigilius, a man indeed adorned with every virtue, most learned in all writings, highly esteemed, and crowned with such elegance of manners that I scarcely remember ever having seen his equal with these eyes: an excellent mathematician, a most acute philosopher, to be compared with the most learned men, surpassing all the most practiced Teutons in wisdom and study. Therefore he has deservedly seemed to us worthy to be received with the praises of men of letters and of all good men, since he is noble and most learned, and can be useful to all in handing down various teachings about wondrous things. Farewell. From Sponheim, 15 Kalends of April, in the year 1500. +
