First, before we proceed to the operation, you should know that at least two spirits must always be called, together with their twenty attendants, without whom they proceed nowhere, although they do not always all appear visibly. But this need not be a concern, provided that through their invisible ministry you obtain what you intend. Therefore, when you wish to perform anything in Steganography through their ministry, turn yourself to the south-southwest, since all of them dwell there at all times with their prince Gediel; and do what you know must be done according to the proper order. This is the conjuration:

Gediel asiel modebar mopiel, casmoyn, rochamurenu proys nasaron atido casmear vearsy maludym velachain demosar otiel masdurym sodiuiel mestray seor amarlum, laueur pealo netus fabelron.

When these things have been completed, the two named princes who have been called will be present, good, kindly, and secure. You must not fear them, but confidently entrust to them whatever you wish, and they will faithfully fulfill your command. This sign will be red.

HAVE IN YOUR MIND SUCH A SECRET
as you do not dare to commit to letters.

I am bound to my prince because of many benefits promised to me. I have secretly learned of his adversaries’ plan, by treachery and betrayal, to seize the castle this night. I wish to warn my prince; I cannot do so by letters, since a search of travelers is being made by enemies on the road; I cannot do so by messengers, because I know that my danger is not to be entrusted to anyone at all. I shall call a spirit, entrust the secret, and send whatever letters I wish merely for form’s sake, so that the prince may know who is to be called; and from the number of the spirit, because otherwise without letters he would not know that a spirit had been sent by me, that one goes and announces it. The prince forestalls the seizure of the castle.

The highest nobility of the human race and the highest virtue is to love God purely, to seek his glory, and to adore him with rectitude of mind. For the humility of a pure heart will reign in the heavens; patience will exalt it. The mind alone of a naked man who fears and loves God will be established in true nobility. Love God, despise the world, flee the age of iniquities; love religion, brotherly charity, the bond of peace, and the unity of those who live uprightly. Christ admonishes in the Gospel that we ought not to be anxious over superfluous human cares. “Having only the necessities,” says the holy Apostle, “let us be content.”

Therefore let us have the love of God and zeal for justice, since purity of mind, simplicity of heart, disregard for earthly honor, cleansing of comeliness, and instruction in holy morals are truly solid riches. Let us be intent upon good works, lest vices overwhelm us. Let us live innocently in all uprightness, as though about to die tomorrow. Let us love God with the purest heart, carefully keeping his commandments; with humility let us mourn for our sins at all times, beseeching the most pious Savior to forgive us our vices. To defend the honor of his love and of the heart, to rebuke the vices of the wicked, to persist in prayers, to despise the glory of the world, and to devote oneself to readings are the treasures of Christians. For the vain glory of the world is soon to collapse; riches too are false, which, when we soon expire, we leave behind unwillingly. Justice alone is our happiness; in our humility consists our glorying and the possession of riches that will remain with us forever: remembrance of death, fear of judgment, and recollection of our sins.
