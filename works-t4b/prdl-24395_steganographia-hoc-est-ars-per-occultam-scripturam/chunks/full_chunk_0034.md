*BOOK ONE.*

*CHAPTER XXII.*

Whose prince is Buriel, nocturnal, a fleer from light, and dwelling in lakes and in the holes of the earth; and no operation is performed through him except at night.

After Geradiel follows Buriel, indeed a great prince, but perverse and wicked, who hates, persecutes, and detests all other spirits, especially princes; but he is no less hated by them. He fears and flees the light with all his leaders and servants, nor is he ever accustomed to come except at night, and this often with great horror and fear for the operator, especially if he is not perfected in the art, steadfast, and strong in spirit. And he often appears in the form of a serpent, having a maiden’s head, but a tail and whole body like a serpent, hissing terribly. When adjured in the proper way, he speaks in human words. He has under him leaders and princes, whom he is accustomed to send into service with their attendants and servants, whose multitude is almost innumerable. From these let us set down the names of some who may suffice for our operation.

In this description of the table we have twelve of Buriel’s princes, with 880 servants, who proceed with their individual leaders in their own order according to the twelve unequal hours of the night, which we call planetary hours, when they have been called by the operator in the proper way. The leader is accustomed to appear in the form that we have described; the servants, however, are often accustomed to appear like mimics or jesters, playing and leaping from on high to the earth with a wondrous illusion.
