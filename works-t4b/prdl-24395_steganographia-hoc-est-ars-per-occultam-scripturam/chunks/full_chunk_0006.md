THE SECOND BOOK.

And so that we may prove by example the things we have said, let us set out our intention in the vernacular language, which we shall then make known to a friend in the manner stated before.

LET THIS BE THE SECRET INTENTION OF THE MIND
of some prince, which he desires his absent vassal to know.

Roth.

Dear faithful one, we intend, for proper cause, to make war on those of Strasbourg and to fall upon them with our whole force on the next Wednesday after Vitus and Modestus. Therefore it is our earnest desire of you, since you are bound to us by oath and pledge and obligated to faithful service, that on that same day, well equipped according to your ability, you appear at four after midday near Strauffenburg in the forest. (Here the letters were placed thus.) You will find further instruction there; comply with it and do not fail to appear. We will acknowledge this graciously toward you, and keep this plan of ours with you in high secrecy, etc.

FOR MAKING THIS SECRET KNOWN

To the vassal, with the sender’s fuller intention, let it be written thus, or in another manner:

Roth.

Almighty, everlasting God, who created all things out of nothing, have mercy on us through the most bitter passion of your only-begotten Son as he died on the cross; he was conceived of the Holy Spirit without male intercourse and born of the most pure ever-Virgin Mary, and, handing himself over voluntarily to death for us, by his most holy blood, as he died, freed our souls from death. Hear us wretches, O most merciful Father, and through the outpouring of the most sacred blood of the same your sweetest Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, forgive us our sins and pour your grace into our hearts, so that, loving you in all things and above all things with sincere fervor, we may always obey your commandments with devotion and, continually advancing in mutual love, henceforth love nothing earthly and seek nothing perishable. For you are the Lord our God, who from the beginning created us in your image and likeness when we did not exist; and through your beloved

son redeemed us when we had perished; him, dead and buried for our sins, you raised from the dead on the third day, and showed him alive to his rejoicing disciples for forty days, to their great consolation. We believe, with pure and most firm confession, that he, true God and man, reigns with you in your uncircumscribable majesty without end, and that at the end of the world he will come again to judge the living and the dead, and will render to each one of us according to his works. Through him we ask you, O most merciful Father: hear us and have mercy on us now and in that terrible hour in which we are to be judged by your Son. Look upon us, most merciful God, for we are frail and pitiable sinners, bare of good works, who by sinning have offended your most holy Majesty in many things. But, turned by your mercy to penitence and humbly pleading for our sins, we ask that you turn your indignation away from us, and mercifully forgive us the crimes of the past, leading us, when this present exile is ended, to the delight of the heavenly homeland. Johannes Trithemius wrote this.

A simple and open narrative of this kind, or any other you wish, which everyone may read and understand and which contains no secret, which we have said is suitable for public view, you should write on the aforesaid paper. When the one whom you wish to know the secret intention has read the words, facing toward the East, let him turn his face to the East and say the conjuration which we set out above: Lamarton, anoyr; and at once he will certainly understand your secret intention, which you entrusted to the spirits.
