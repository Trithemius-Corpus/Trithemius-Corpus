From the forty princes of Usiel who preside over daytime operations, you have fourteen, with their servants numbering 400; and fourteen who preside over nighttime operations, with their 440 attendants. With these, placed opposite for each order, you see two signs, for testing and for precaution concerning treasure, as you know, so that it cannot be found by thieves. We use them for guarding treasure that has been found, while the spirit is being sent to a friend. Therefore, when you wish to operate in this business through spirits, if the matter is very great or pertains to a prince, call whichever dukes you wish from the order of the groups of four; if it is your own matter, from the groups of three; if private and small, from the groups of two; if mean and very minor, then let it be done at night in the order of the solitary ones only.

When these have been said facing Subcircius, in silence, as time and place permit, do the other things that the art itself requires, and entrust to the spirit, now appearing, the secret together with the seal of the profound mystery, doubting nothing; for all those dukes are good and faithful.

Let us suppose that you have such a secret of the mind, to be entrusted to absolutely no one.

You have found a very great treasure in the coffers of the dead, or in some other place in the earth, or you know for certain that it is secretly hidden. Alone you are not able to carry it away, and yet you trust no one among those dwelling nearby. You have a faithful friend, but he is far away, to whom you desire to reveal the mystery, but you trust neither letters nor messenger, lest by some mishap you fall into public exposure and danger, and lose the profit. Therefore you call the spirit. The spirit comes; he carries the message; he calls the friend. The friend comes, helps, and takes part.

Write the letter as you wish; there should be absolutely nothing in it to fear.

I am sending you the speech which I composed at the wedding of a certain female friend of mine. God, creator of all things and redeemer of the human race, and savior of all, lest the human race perish, also established the sacrament of marriage. For we read that he joined Eve as wife to the first man and said: Increase and multiply, establishing the law by which humanity was to be preserved. Afterward God, having become incarnate, deigned to sanctify weddings by a miracle. Those who serve the established order deserve well of God; but those who undertake chaste marriage out of zeal for offspring confer still more upon human beings. Therefore you who undertake honorable marriage I have rightly praised by the example of our Savior. For he, by miracles, and I, by words, shall duly praise the established and necessary sacrament of marriage. Although I am not married, I shall nevertheless rightly praise marriage. Indeed, if marriage were removed, the whole human race would fail and collapse. For by the use of marriages the human race exists, though ruinous, weak, and corruptible; yet without marriages it would not endure. But do this: let the fruitfulness of your marriage be adorned with morals and holy conduct, and put a stumbling block before no one. First of all, love God; continually devote yourselves to works of mercy together with justice; flee impiety; cultivate righteousness; love the good; always speak praises to God; assist the poor with alms; obey God’s commandments. Do not abandon truth; honor God’s ministers in the churches; pay tithes; show hospitality to pilgrims; free the innocent; feed the hungry; obey your elders...
