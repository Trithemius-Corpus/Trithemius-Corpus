CHAPTER XVIII.

WHOSE SUPREME PRINCE IS CALLED CASPIEL, whose mansion is toward the South, and who has under him many princes, dukes, and innumerable servants.

Toward the South dwells the great prince Caspiel, who has under his dominion 200 principal princes or dukes, and 400 counts or subdukes, with a vast multitude of servants, whose office is to announce generally all and individual things not contained above, toward the south. But since it is necessary in this art to know certain dukes by name, and since they are many, we shall take care to state from among the many the names of a few who will suffice us for everything.

You now have twelve of Caspiel’s more principal dukes, with counts, subdukes, and servants, 100,640 in number, through whom every general operation of announcing toward the south is carried out. But when you wish to announce some hidden matter to a friend dwelling toward the South, do what is required according to the art, and say the conjuration.

Conjuration of the Spirit.

Caspiel aloyr chameron noeres padyr diuiel prolsyn vear maduson cralnoti fruon phorsy latsonthon thiano pematson theor. Caueos adeueos friato briosi panyeldrubon madiel sayr fabeltrusyn gonear pean noty nabusran.

When the conjuration has been completed in the proper manner, you will see the one whom you have summoned present and ready to obey your commands in all things. All the dukes of this Caspiel are very benevolent and willing, but their subdukes are somewhat harsher; nevertheless they yield to conjurations, if the operator has been steadfast and undaunted, speaking to him sternly, as though with the greatest authority.

For none of them is so harsh that he does not become gentle when compelled and adjured by the force of authoritative power.

Let us posit any sort of secret which is to be entrusted to no one.

I have a matter with a friend which, if it should become public, would certainly give rise to great danger for both of us. All messengers are watched, so that letters may be read by those lying in wait for us. Therefore, so that we may be secure and our matter remain secret, I write letters that need fear no one’s inspection, and I send them to the aforementioned friend accompanied by an invisible spirit.

It does not matter what sort the letters are, provided they have been entrusted to a spirit.

Behold, mortals, the brevity and misery of the present life, and practice saving repentance while you have time. Woe to you, most senseless lovers of worldly glory, who rashly despise almighty God and eternal gladness. Why do you not love those heavenly banquets that have been promised to those who humbly seek God? For you, having cast aside humility, seek the very brief joys of the world; you do not love the excellence of eternal blessedness. You live in contentious zeal; you stain honor with wicked actions and do not know the praises of the Most High God. Day and night you devote yourselves to worldly actions and temporal gains; always intent on hunting after novelties, you sharpen your memory, but neglect to hear readings. You do not care for the time of grace granted to you, nor for the expectation of eternal blessedness. Behold, the Judge, terrible to all criminals, approaches. Behold, the stern Almighty Majesty hastens. Behold, the Last Judgment is established. Behold, eternal lamentations are set forth; everlasting goods will be withdrawn from us.

In the year 1515, April 6.

CHAPTER XIX.

WHOSE SUPREME PRINCE IS CALLED AMENADIEL, dwelling in the West, who has under him 300 dukes, but 500 counts and almost innumerable servants.

Amenadiel, emperor and supreme spirit of the West, has under his dominion 300 more powerful princes or dukes, no fewer than five hundred subdukes or counts, and servants, subservants, and attendants from among the innumerable, whose names I have found to be fewer than 30,000, besides other innumerable ones whose names I have not yet found. For these suffice for announcing all secrets to friends toward the West. But I shall state the names of the dukes who suffice.
