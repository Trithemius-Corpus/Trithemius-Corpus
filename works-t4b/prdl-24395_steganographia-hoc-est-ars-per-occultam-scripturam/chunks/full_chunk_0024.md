When the charm has been duly completed, and in silence, the spirit called will be present. Safely entrust the secret; he will carry out and report back what is to be entrusted. But if you see that those who preside over nocturnal operations are delaying, as they often do, compel them manfully by repeating the charm, fearing nothing; for they will be forced to reverence you as their master.

I have a secret of this sort, which I trust no one to carry.

I put the case. I have a certain noble and learned friend, whom I know is to be killed secretly by certain hired assassins. I do not dare warn him openly, because it is dangerous for me. In a familiar and more secret matter, I fear that letters may come into others’ hands. I do not presume to trust a man to speak it by mouth, because he who is with me today may perhaps stand against me tomorrow. And so I flee to the customary art, in which, whatever I entrust to it, I do not fear that it will become public, even if it should happen to be attempted by an enemy.

I send familiar letters to my friend, in which I fear no danger.

The ancients define the greatest ornament of nobility as justice and philosophy, and disgrace as impiety and ignorance. For what is more beautiful than to adorn with letters and virtues a noble lineage that bears arms? We find that very many most noble men, and many others serving as soldiers, have labored at this day and night, so that they might conquer barbarians with arms and instruct their own people in uprightness and letters. Following their example, most learned Bumavy, you do not cease to unfold humane letters and the good arts, adorned with arms and letters; and once the wisest ancient princes were accustomed not to shut up their wealth in their treasuries, but rather to apply it to literary use, judging the study of wisdom to be a most honorable support for those tending toward happiness, by which the blessedness of eternal reward is acquired. Indeed, knowledge of the scriptures makes princes venerable to all and especially to be feared; ignorance of the scriptures, called the burial of a living man, is accustomed to bring shame and dreadful calamity upon princes and nobles.

A man without letters is a two-footed ass, to be buried alive, indeed buried more than once. Especially nobles and those governing the commonwealth ought to be wise and notable for knowledge of letters, ignorance of which brings in tyranny. Love of letters also commends to perpetuity the illustrious deeds of princes and nobles. For we see that the ancient philosophers obtained remembrance and achieved immortality of name among all people forever more by letters than by arms. For letters, more than arms, made our ancients and princes glorious, excellent, and wise, and granted the titles of praise to their memory with immortality. Add that for Christians literary study provides the highest and most honorable help for flying up to heaven. For from the scriptures we begin to contemplate the flashing and honorable majesty of divine illuminations, otherwise inaccessible to us. Therefore, Henry, most learned knight, favor letters in such a way that, adorned with uprightness, you may always live. Farewell, from Spanheim, on the day of March, in the year 1500.

After the friend skilled in the art has received these or any other letters, and has recognized the sign of Raysiel, after the customary preliminaries have been performed, let him turn toward the North, and let him use this charm for the spirit.

Charm of Conjuration.

chulthusamedon veplusandly tusan axeyr asslon

When these things have been said, the spirit sent will immediately appear visibly, and will disclose each and every thing that I have given as a command to his ear. And I wish you to know also that some spirits who preside over nocturnal operations do not come gladly to the operation at night, unless they are compelled by the greatest solemn oaths and conjurations; yet after they have come and have been sent to the friend, they are accustomed to appear quickly and to obey the one who calls, unless perhaps he himself, deceived on some occasion, has neglected something prescribed in the art.
