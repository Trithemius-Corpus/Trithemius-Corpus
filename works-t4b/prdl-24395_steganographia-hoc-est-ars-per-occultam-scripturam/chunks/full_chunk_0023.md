*Steganography*

You have a faithful friend, whom you favor as yourself, but he is absent far away; and you know that, when he is about to return, ambushes are to be set for him on the road by someone more powerful, or in some other place or manner. You wish to warn him, since he knows nothing; but great danger would threaten you, the warner, if it became public. Therefore it must neither be entrusted to letters, lest they be read by someone, nor confided to any man, lest it happen to be revealed. Therefore, so that you may remain safe and your friend be warned, you call the spirit and entrust the secret: it is done.

You send familiar letters to your friend, or a devout prayer, such as may bear being seen.

I had lent you my codex on the deeds of the Lombards and Bede’s volume *On Times*, which I ask you to send back. For it befits a wise man, and especially a Christian, to keep everywhere the faith he has promised. To forget benefits, according to divine and our human ordinances, is held to be a very great crime. Do not incur so sinful a guilt. I sent you my volumes so that you might restore them at some time. But I gave you the Greek books freely; in consideration of them, you ought rightly to show yourself grateful to us and more faithful in conduct. Farewell, from Spanheim, on the Ides of March, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When your friend has received these letters, or any others sent by you, and has recognized the sign of Cabariel, let him turn himself toward that quarter of the world from which the Circius wind comes forth; and, after first performing the customary preliminaries, let him read this prayer in silence.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Cabariel afiar paremon chiltan amedyn sayr pemadon chulty mouayr sauepor peatha mal frimaston dayr pean cothuruo fabelrusyn elsoty chelmodyn. When this song has been said toward the Circius, the angel will at once appear visibly, disclosing to him the secret entrusted; and he will report to you if that man has entrusted anything.

*Book One.*

The thirteenth dwelling is in the north; its spirit and supreme emperor is Raysiel, who has under his dominion 50 dukes who preside over the daytime operations, with many servants, and the same number who preside over the nighttime operations with their attendants. Their office, in those causes and betrayals that pertain to death, is to warn the operator’s friends and announce the secret. The dukes who preside over daytime operations readily obey the one who calls them, and they come cheerful, pleasant, and most ready for service; but those who preside over nighttime operations are somewhat insolent, because they greatly hate and detest the light, nor do they easily obey, especially novices less proven in the art, whom they frequently laugh at to their face, unless they are so constrained by the operator’s conjurations that they cannot prevail in resisting. For they fear the conjuration greatly, and, as though in one respect, they allow themselves to be conjured more than the daytime spirits; and yet they obey unwillingly. But now, from the number of all these, let us set down the names of a few and the number of servants, as many as are sufficient for us for the customary operation.
