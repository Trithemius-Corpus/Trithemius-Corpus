From the dukes we have five, and from the counts ten, already named in the table; they suffice for us for every operation of this hour. But when you wish to work through them in Steganography, write on a clean slip, with the customary characters and conjurations, and first, after adding what must be added below, speak the written conjuration itself, which is as follows:

*Conjuration*

O Smodael aneor ersoty neas, hays layr Caphrayn thelreas me arpenary.

When this song of conjuration has been spoken, the spirits called will be present, ready and obedient for everything. Entrust the secret to a duke, not to a count, unless the duke is absent, because it must always be entrusted to the more principal one among those who come.

*Subject of the Secret at Will*

Let us have any secret whatever, which we wish to write to a friend far absent, and of such a kind that we do not wish it to come to the knowledge of anyone else, as can often happen. Therefore, lest either the letter-carrier reveal the mystery for any reason, or the letter come into foreign hands, let us work, and through the spirit we shall be secure.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, sends greetings to Lord Conrad, Abbot of Saint Stephen at Wuerzburg.

A rumor has gone out about the abbey of Limburg, that it has dismissed Brother Johannes Feilsdorf, a faithful man and especially skilled in temporal affairs,

and has introduced new monks from Pantimorum and appointed them its officers. I am greatly astonished at this useless deed. Yet when I recall the custom of the men of Limburg, I do not know what to say. Indeed I see men ignorant of temporal affairs, who dismissed a faithful man because he rebuked their actions. They ought to have given thanks to our Savior Jesus Christ for the fidelity of so great a brother, who could have disclosed their necessities. Wretches, why do you reject a good and necessary helper? What shall I say? I do not praise the departure of so useful a brother, one most skilled in temporal affairs, who would very quickly have assisted the men of Limburg. I grieve greatly that the monks of Limburg have so lost their sense, who dismissed an excellent man and appointed as stewards monks who are worth absolutely nothing in temporal affairs. Macharius, abbot, it did not befit a man to act so. First you praised him; now you have rejected a man very necessary to you, to be put after no one. I confess that, if Christ had given him to me, as befits a Christian I would now give the greatest thanks and would take care that he never departed. But you, ungrateful to Christ, preferred to dismiss a faithful man, wearied by your counsel, lest, with your affairs abandoned, there should seem to be hope of recovery. From Sponheim, 3 Nones of April, 1500.

When these letters, or any others, have been received by the one to whom they are sent, marked with the sign of Osmadael, let the person skilled in the art, after first setting out what must be set out beforehand, say the prescribed conjuration; and soon the spirit sent will appear visibly. When you have seen him, say: Menason aproysy elmano thulneas assierto mauear veneas cralnoti permason. When these words have been spoken, the messenger spirit will disclose the secret to you.
