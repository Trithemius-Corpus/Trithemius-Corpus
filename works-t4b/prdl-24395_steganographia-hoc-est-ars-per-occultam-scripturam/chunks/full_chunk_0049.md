CHAPTER II.

THE SECOND HOUR OF THE DAY IS CALLED CEVORYM. Its prince and supreme spirit is Anael, who has under his rule 10 dukes, counts, centurions, and 100 presidents. There is no fixed number of servants and subservants.

The presiding ruler and principal emperor of the second hour of the day is called Anael. Under his rule he has, assigned to the service of this art of Steganography, 10 dukes, counts, centurions, and presidents over all his spirits, 100 in number. The number of servants is still not certain to me.

The second hour of the day is called Cevorym, in which changeable operations are performed, amazing to all human beings. All the spirits of this emperor are sufficiently benevolent, pleasant, cheerful, and most ready to obey the operator in the art, provided that he is skilled in the art, strong in mind, constant, and understanding well.

R. R. R.
Menarchos Orphiel Quosiel 10 100 G. n.
Archiel Cursiel Ermaziel 10 100 G. n.
Chardiel Elmoym Granyel 10 100 G. n.

In this table we have placed 3 of Anael’s dukes, 6 of the counts, and 330 from the number of servants, who are sufficient for us for every operation of Steganography in this second hour of the day, when they are sent through their orders.

When the conjuration has been written, do the other things which you know must be done according to the art. Then say the song in silence, and at once you will see the spirit present, cheerful, pleasant, and benevolent toward all things; to him you may securely entrust the secret.

Let us set down any secret whatever.

Let the secret be any matter that occurs at the time, which seems neither useful to commit to letters nor to be entrusted to a messenger. From the spirits of Anael let one duke and one count be summoned, or one or the other of them, as you please. Let the secret be entrusted to the more principal spirit in the proper manner, as is required according to the precept of the art, and he will carry it faithfully.

Let the tenor of the letter be familiar.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Wilhelm Veldicus, Canon of Saint Augustine in the valley of Franconia, his fellow philosopher, greetings.

I would rejoice to answer your letters, as Christ may make me rejoice; and I would send the volumes you requested, if at last I had them. For I have been zealous with a good zeal for the very great and very weighty hidden investigators of nature, the zeal which the most ancient philosophers possessed. Yet I give you thanks beyond what mortals can repay, my sweetest brother, since you have kept the bond of mutual affection inviolate. For in this familiar matter, which you seem to have managed most diligently with grave modesty, I have plainly recognized this; I praise the deed, and I give thanks that will never die. And so I would very much like to comply with your requests, if I had what you ask for, as Christ knows. I recently saw various Latin translations of Ficino; I have read many most brilliant volumes of Pico della Mirandola, that most excellent man.

I saw Hippolytus in Greek; whether he has been translated at all, I do not know. The highest Truth, Christ, to whom no shadows conceal anything, knows that from my heart I desire to comply with your request. I beg you, be zealous for virtue with the zeal of righteousness; despise praises and honors that will perish. Consider true praise to be the imitation of Christ going before us. See to it, I beg you, that as a priest you may exist in works, and love Christ for all the time in which you live. A true lover of Christ burns with zeal for justice. True repose of the soul, knowledge of the savior, and pious observance of Catholic tradition prepare the eternal glory of all. May glorious Jesus Christ, giver of all good things, grant you the love of eternity, making you a Catholic priest of Christians. Farewell. 4 Kalends of April, in the year 1500.

When the person to whom the letters have been sent has received them, being skilled in the art, after first performing the things that are generally customarily done beforehand, let him arrange the lord of the ascendant with the others that are customarily placed on the little chart, and let him say the prescribed conjuration. When it has been said, the spirit who was sent will appear visibly; turning to him, let him say fearlessly: Fabelmerufyn passoriel liertos ryneas melchus thyrmo nydran vear padroys. When this song has been completed, the spirit will at once approach gently and will faithfully disclose the entrusted mystery.

But everyone who is going to operate in this art must take care, both the one who sends the spirit and the one to whom he has been destined, not to presume to perform anything in public or among others who are not skilled in this art, because all the hourly spirits love secrecy and detest publicity; and they would not depart without danger.

CHAPTER III.

THE THIRD HOUR IS CALLED DANZUR, AND its supreme emperor-spirit is Vequaniel, who has under his dominion 20 dukes and 200 counts. The number of servants is uncertain. Their office is general for all things; and they are sufficiently ready and benevolent in all matters whatsoever have been entrusted to them, faithful and secure; nor are they unwilling in obedience. But when summoned in public, they do not depart without harm or danger to the one summoning them.
