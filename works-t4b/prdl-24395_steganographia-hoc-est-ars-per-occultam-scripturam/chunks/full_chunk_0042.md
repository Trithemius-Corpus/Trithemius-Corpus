We now have named 12 from the princes of Macariel, with 400 servants counted according to their turns and orders. Therefore, when you wish to work through them in Steganography, take care that you call no fewer than at least four from those named above. After the customary preliminaries have been performed, say this Conjuration.

*Song.*

Macariel myrno chamerosy purmy marefyn amos peanam olradu, chaber ianoes fabelron deatfy chadon vlyfes Almos; rutiel pedaron deabry madero neas lamer o deatfy, thubra dorpilto melrofyne draor chalmea near, parmon deatfy charon alnodiel parfaradean, maroy reneas charfo gnole, melrofin te dranso casmar ebrolet. Landtys masfayr therafonte noel amalan.

When this song has been said, the spirits called will be present, appearing in diverse form, and frequently indeed with a maidenly head, while their body and tail end in the form of a dragon, winding and unwinding themselves in a fourfold order.

*Let there be a secret for a prince.*

The prefect of a king or prince, appointed in a land or province, has learned by a most secret report that the enemy shortly intends to break into the province. He wishes to inform the prince, but cannot do so by messengers, because they are to be tortured by the adversaries on the way so that they may betray the secret; nor by letters, since all will be opened by them. Therefore he calls the spirit, entrusts the secret, and composes other letters.

*Let us compose letters conveying nothing useful.*

and also Bernard's letter to the Templars. We all know that the glorious name of Catholic sanctity was given to Tertullian of old among our people, and we know that he, a noble light of sacred faith, did not remain the least. He was outstanding in his actions, a glorious teacher of divine law, a most devoted defender of our liberty. Although he is censured in some matters, he nevertheless wrote honorable volumes in expounding our faith. He had a wife zealous for our religion and a lover of it, noble in humility and innocence, glorious in chastity. Some praiseworthy writings of his correct instruction survive, sweet and overflowing with much doctrine; brief letters, not useless, honorably and graciously exhorting us to the pursuit of eternal glory. 8 Kalends of April. In the year 1500. Johannes Trithemius wrote this.

When the person to whom these or any other letters are sent has received them, being already skilled in the art of Steganography, and has recognized the sign of Macariel, let him do what is customarily to be done, adding the Song.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Macariel osayr chamerose chulti pesano dayr fameron cathurmo pean ersoty lamedon souapor casrea mafyr. Ianos thatfia, peathan non acri pean etion matramy.

When the song has duly been completed, the spirit sent will appear visible to him alone.

*Chapter XXX.*

*Whose supreme emperor is called Uriel, having under his dominion 10 dukes, and 100 counts or subdukes; the number of servants is uncertain.*

Nor is it fitting for us to omit Uriel, a great prince, whom we know, with experience teaching us, to hold a place not the least in usefulness among the patrons of this art. Under his dominion and command he has 10 dukes and princes appointed for this art of Steganography; counts or subdukes, who always accompany those dukes, each in his own order, 100; many servants, of whom I still do not know the certain number.

R. R. R. a. c.
