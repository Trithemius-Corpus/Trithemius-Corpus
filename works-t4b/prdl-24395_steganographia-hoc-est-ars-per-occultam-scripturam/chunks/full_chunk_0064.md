*Book Two.* 115

The secret, whatever occurs to you at the time, which you wish someone absent to know in this fourth hour of the night, you may safely entrust to the spirits, provided it is of such a kind that you cannot safely disclose it to him either by letters or by messengers. For those things that we can disclose without danger either by letters or by messengers we must by no means disclose through the ministry of spirits. Whoever, however, attempts to summon spirits for trivial matters and secrets that can be disclosed without danger by letters or messengers will without doubt incur loss and inconvenience, together with danger.

Let the tenor of the letter be as desired.

To the venerable father in Christ, the steward of the Limburgers, the religious lord called Johannes Westdorffen, Johannes Ovilis, humble abbot of Sponheim, sends the joys of heaven.

The nuns of Saint Rupert long most eagerly for you as their governor, and with humble goodwill ask you to undertake the administration of the temporal affairs of that monastery. To serve the devout virgins of Christ is praiseworthy and meritorious. You have as the excellent visitor of the spiritual affairs of that named monastery, embracing you with the greatest honor, a man grave in character, adorned with virtues, cautious in counsel, beloved by all, of good reputation for the conduct of monastic religion, and notable among all people, whose life is deservedly most pleasing to Christ: the excellent abbot of the monastery of Saint John. He is most learned, most studious, and most skilled in the writings both of Christians and of gentiles, and he far and notably surpasses the wisdom of all people. He knows very well how to govern the flock entrusted to him, and he is a zealous guardian of the virgins of Christ, whose enclosure he preserves most firmly. Do not be afraid to serve under him such honorable nuns, for he knows how to govern the brides of Christ according to the monastic law with the best discretion, and by virtue and zeal for the Christian faith he will gladly assist you in necessity. Since you have as a familiar friend so illustrious and kindly a pastor, I ask that you gladly take up the helm of government of such religious virgins. The spouse, guardian, and most kindly rewarder of the nuns will repay your labor and theirs with eternal joys.

When the person to whom they are sent, skilled in the art, has received the letters marked with the character of Iesicha, let him first observe the lord of the ascendant of that hour with the whole figure of the heavens, and let him write it on a clean sheet, and on its back the prescribed conjuration. When, after the preliminaries have been performed, he has said it, and has seen the spirits sent to him standing present before him, let him say to the more powerful one: chamerusin aphroys aslotiel mean reneas veat tryamo craltipenaſon. When these things have duly been said to the principal spirit, he himself will come nearer and will reveal in that person’s ear the secret entrusted to him.

*Chapter 17.*

*Whose hour is called Sanayfar, and its supreme angel and emperor is called Abasdarbon, who has under his dominion many dukes, counts, and servants.*

The fifth hour of the night is called Sanayfar, whose first angel and supreme emperor is called Abasdarbon. He has under his dominion many princes, dukes, counts, and ministers, distributed in twelve orders and turns like the preceding ones; he is accustomed to send them into the service of various arts and experiments for operators who have been duly instructed. In the first order there are 10, in the sixth 30, in the fourth 40, in the fifth 50, in the sixth 60, in the eighth 80, in the ninth 90, in the tenth 100, in the eleventh 10000. And through these every operation of steganography is carried out in the fifth hour of the night.
