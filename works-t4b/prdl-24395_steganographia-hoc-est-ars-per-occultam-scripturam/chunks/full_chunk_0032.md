Conjuration Song.

Demoriel onear daburfoy Cohyne chamerson ymeor pean o-

When the song has been completed, the spirit, ready for all things, obedient, and fully prepared, will appear visibly to you. To him you may safely entrust the secret to be carried.

Suppose I have such a secret, or any other whatever, to communicate.

I have a close friend, at this time far away, to whom I have a very secret matter, which cannot safely be entrusted to anyone at all, not even to letters. Therefore, so that the secret may remain hidden, I always like to call some spirit from the art who is familiar to me. I entrust the secret: he comes, goes, acts faithfully, and carries to my friend what I have entrusted; we are both safe. He himself also brings back to me from my friend a secret reply, which he tells to no other mortal. But so that the friend himself may understand that the spirit has been sent to him, it is necessary that letters of any sort, fearing nothing, be devised, in which he may recognize the sign of the spirit sent. For the spirit does not appear unless compelled.

Let us first send whatever letters we please, such as need fear the sight of no one.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, to Jakob Wimpfeling of Sélestat, theologian, poet, and most eloquent orator, greetings.

I should very much like, Jakob Wimpfeling, for you at last, even if late, to satisfy your promise and my expectation of you. You know what you promised, and you are not ignorant of what I expect. I do not wish you to become a monk, but to live with monks. Hasten; let us live together. Behold, the hour of our passing is at hand, greatly to be feared. Why then do we delay? Why, unhappy men, do we neglect the brief and wretched condition of our sojourn

Let us prepare ourselves for death, since it hastens terribly enough, calls all the reprobate, spares no one, reveres no one; it denies a hearing to those who pray; it despises the tears of the [unclear]; it scorns the threats of princes and destroys their kingdoms, names, and glory; it brings to nothing the hateful pride of tyrants before all. Therefore, brother, diligently considering the benefits bestowed by the Redeemer, very useful to us, recompensed by many things, correcting what is ours, shining continually with good morals, so that by penance and tears we may be able to appease the strict examination of the judge hastening to the seat of judgment, who will show himself terrible to the [unclear] and joyful to the elect. Farewell, from Sponheim, the fifteenth day before the Kalends of April, in the year of the Lord 1500.

When the song has been completed, as is prescribed in the art, the spirit sent will be present and will appear visibly to you alone, and to absolutely no other; and he will speak the secret into your ear.

CHAPTER XXI.

Whose supreme emperor and prince is called Geradiel, dwelling in no quarter of the world, but wandering and accustomed to be present everywhere.

Besides those emperors, princes, and dukes of spirits whom we have named in the preceding chapters, who remain in fixed places throughout the visible world, there are also many others assigned: wandering, unstable, and remaining nowhere fixed. The ancient wise men and magi called these aerial, that is, unstable, because they fly in the air like flies, without order, without dwelling, and without restraint.
