*Of the Steganography*

The fifteenth mansion toward the northern region is inhabited by Armadiel, a great emperor, who has under his command many dukes and princes with their servants. From these, one hundred have been appointed over daytime and nighttime operations indifferently, so that all alike preside over our nighttime and daytime actions in Steganography, according to the will of the operator. Their office is to announce the most secret hidden matters of the operator to princes and great men, with the greatest fidelity and mystery. From these we shall name some of the more important ones, who will suffice for our intention.

You now have from the spirits of Armadiel fifteen in number, with their 1,260 subordinate servants, who, according to the division of the twenty-four hours into six parts, are accustomed to come with their dukes, when called, to the operator, if he wishes it, preserving their order in coming. Therefore, when you wish to perform something in Steganography through them, you must most diligently observe the division of time according to this art, without which you will make no progress at all. When this has been understood as it ought, turn yourself toward the North, doing what the art requires.

for the sake of example, such a secret as must be entrusted to no one.

I have a certain secret for my prince which must be communicated to absolutely no one. I do not dare commit it to letters, lest through negligence or forgetfulness they be scattered and read; much less do I wish to announce it through a man, lest it happen in some way to be revealed through him to others also. Therefore it pleases me to test the mystery of this art, through which my secret will remain hidden at all times.

Let us write letters, of whatever sort we please, which need fear no one’s sight.

To the Most Serene Prince, Lord Philip, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of Bavaria, Arch-Steward of the Holy Roman Empire, Prince Elector, Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, with prayers, offers his service.

Most illustrious prince, with humble submission I offer my service. The kindness of your renowned dukedom commanded me to commit to writing certain very hidden matters which had been set forth in words. I would rejoice, not without devout exultation, if powers equal to the will were present: to distribute the most honorable benefit shown, and a more suitable place, and to offer the service owed by grace. The novelty of the work, which must be undertaken without the path of a predecessor, restrains the weakness of my heart; I know myself to be far unequal to it. For it will have an immense and great labor, and it casts a very great dullness upon my efforts. Yet I shall test my powers, in obedience to Your Grace, without doubt approaching a most kindly judge, if the possibility suited to completion has not attended my good will. There, with most abundant humanity, I perceive that a glorious and most munificent prince, lover and defender of letters, speaks; under his most humane protection the University of Heidelberg, adorned with many most learned and most erudite men, most fresh in talent, surpasses all the schools throughout Germany. And so, most renowned and most humane Prince, if I have or am anything, I know it all to be bound to your kindness; and I shall always take care to offer it with humble submission most readily.
