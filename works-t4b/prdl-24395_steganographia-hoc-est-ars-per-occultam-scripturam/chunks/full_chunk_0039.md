BOOK ONE.

CHAP. XXVI.

Whose supreme emperor is called Icosiel, having under him 100 leaders or princes, 300 counts or subjects; the number of his servants is not yet certain to me.

Icosiel, among aerial spirits, is one of the more powerful, a great and mighty emperor in the secrets of Steganography, having under him 100 princes of ducal rank, and 300 counts. I have not yet found the fixed number of his servants. He is called Icosiel because he readily dwells in houses among human beings. For after you have once duly conjured any one of his leaders and called him to you with his counts, if you wish him to remain with you all the days of your life, appoint for him one secret and hidden place in your house, and firmly command him, under conjuration, to remain there; and he will obey you cheerfully, and will always be ready at your command.

Here, out of the 100 leaders of Icosiel, we have 15 named, and 50 sub-leaders, and 2,150 servants, who, with their leaders, according to the order appointed for them in the 24 hours of day and night, are accustomed to come when called by the operator into the service of Steganography. They are fit and willing for everything you may wish, provided you are perfectly instructed in the art, steadfast, and fearless in the operation; for they come in the form and likeness of serpents, having maiden heads. Therefore, when you wish to operate through any one of them, do the things that are to be done according to the art, and add this conjuration:

Conjuration.

Icosiel aphorsi chamersyn thulneas ianotiel menear peanos erasnotiel medursan matory fabelron ersonial cathurnos laernoty

noty besraym alphayr lamedonti nael cabelron. When the conjuration has been duly completed, the spirits called will be present without delay, to whom you will be able safely to entrust your secret for your friend.

Make the letters to be sent as you please. Let the secret be whatever you wish.

I have a friend far away, skilled in the art of Steganography, to whom I have a great secret and mystery which must not be entrusted to anyone, even by letters, nor disclosed. Therefore, so that all things may remain secret, I send as messenger a spirit who will reveal the secret to my friend and keep my business secret.

Let us amend our life, humbling ourselves before the supreme ruler of the universe. Humility gains heaven, destroys the harmful temptations of demons, breaks down the pernicious swelling of the mind, and powerfully drives out all fires of vices. The path of humility deserves to receive a crown and to possess a noble kingdom. I have set the path for you: innocence, uprightness of conduct. By guarding it continually, you will not sin. This alone is the way for us to heap up the merit of blessedness: that, giving thanks to God, we may fear him with a most upright heart, fleeing the tumult of the world; that we may never grow weary of the practice of humility, while seeking the life above. Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, wrote this on 10 Kalends of April, 1500.

When the one to whom they are sent, being skilled in the art, has received the letters, once he has recognized the sign of the prince, let him do what he knows must be done; then let him say the conjuration.

Conjuration.

Icosiel osayr penarizo chulti meradym phrael melchusy dayr pean cathurmo fabelron erfoti chamerus an iltham pedaly fuer melrossn crymarsy phroylon. When this conjuration has been completed, the spirit will be present to the one calling, revealing the secret.
