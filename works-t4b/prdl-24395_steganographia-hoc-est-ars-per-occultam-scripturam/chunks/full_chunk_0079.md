Then, in the same second table, we have arranged the motion of Saturn in each of the twelve signs, for all the quarters of day and night, down to the minutes, so that, once we know in any hour the degree of the ascendant in which Saturn himself is, his punctual root can be clearly disclosed. Once, however, the calculation of some period has been completed, a period which I do not know whether our age can ever reach, the table must be begun again from the head of its beginning. In the third table we have reduced the sevenfold conjunction of Saturn with the other planets, which is unique, to the proportion of a point; through this all 120 conjunctions are easily reduced to the point. In the same table the pure motion of all the planets is known through the punctual division, progression, and elongation of the beginning of each, from the point in any motion, if the mean motion has first been perfectly obtained. For without knowledge of the mean motion, it is impossible for anyone to be able to approach the practice of this science. Again, even if you understand the mean motion and likewise the true motion most perfectly, it will profit you nothing for knowledge of this science unless you understand completely the three prescribed tables for all the points of the divisions in the motions of the planets, taken equally from unequal degrees, through each minute. But now let us proceed to the operation.

When all these things which we have said, and with God’s help shall say, have been known and perfectly understood, then, when you wish to operate in this most profound speculation, you must first know the rising, exaltation, and setting of all the stars of the eighth sphere through which the operation is performed, and how far each is distant from another. The stars through which we operate for announcing any secret whatever to an absent person, whether by words or by writings and without a human messenger, are seven hundred in number. Their names, places, risings, settings, elevations, distances, approaches, and recessions we have described in a special book; it would be too painstaking to repeat them here by inserting them again. Therefore, with the star observed in all respects as is proper, and with the point of the ascendant known in which Saturn is at that same hour, if the operation is performed through him, consider most diligently the quality of his motion,

whether it is direct or retrograde, pure or mixed, proper or joined with other impediments of aspects, distinct or confused. Then preserve on a clean sheet the point of the beginning of the motion in the same sign, both of Saturn himself and of the other planets conjoined with him, most subtly and properly calculated from the common tables and from those which we have set forth; and carefully observe how far it has progressed, how far it has been elevated, and how far depressed. You will do the same for the other planets conjoined with Saturn, or with that planet through which the operation is thus performed. In like manner observe most diligently by what aspect the planets behold one another, whether trine, square, conjunction, or sextile, because in these lies the greatest force of this operation. Likewise, before all things, observe in which quarter Saturn is, not only with respect to day or night, but also with respect to the sign in which he is, and with respect to the years from the beginning of the world, when he was first created in his own house; for all these things are necessary. And note that when Orifiel, the angel of Saturn, rules the world in his order, that rule will last 354 years and four months. Then every operation of this most profound science which pertains to the works of Saturn, and which is performed through him, is easy and will attain a clear effect without great labor. And the same must be observed concerning the other planets. Therefore whoever, once the principles of this art are known, wishes to operate through it without difficulty should operate through that planet whose principal angel is found by easy calculation to be governing the sphere at that time.

Therefore, when all things have been calculated most diligently and you are about to proceed to the operation, observe which angel of Saturn presides over that quarter of the day or night, and write his name together with the name of the star through which you wish to operate, which it is necessary for you to see. At any time, however, you will be able to see the stars necessary to you by the art which we have handed down, whether by day or by night. Then write on the same small sheet the other things which you know to be most secret, with the ink prepared for this according to our custom, as you know. Afterward place the very note which you have written before you upon the table, and write on another sheet the conception of your mind, what you wish the absent person to know; and roll these two notes together with one another, and place them in a higher place before you upon the table or desk.
