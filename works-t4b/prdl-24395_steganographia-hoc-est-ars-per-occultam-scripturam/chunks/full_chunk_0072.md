*THE SECOND BOOK.*

Our Lord Jesus Christ, the most gentle savior of our souls, promised us the happiness of eternal consolation, if we always observe his commandments with a pure heart. Therefore, dearest brother, carefully keep the commandments of God and of the holy rule, because unless you preserve inviolate until death all the things that almighty God has commanded you, you will perish by eternal death. For this reason I have handed down to you the form and law of religious and holy conduct. Take care, then, diligently, lest through the desire of love you give your heart to any mortal creature to possess, because no one comes to those highest joys unless he despises the world for God’s sake. Therefore again through this letter of mine I exhort you, as a dearest friend, to observe for yourself the admonitions I have made. For I consider myself happy when I have written something good, especially at this time, for edification concerning the manner of living well, by which you may always become better. For this reason I now write to you again for your good, because I fear your danger no less than my own. But I pray: always be watchful, advancing daily in the fear of God, living innocently, harming no one, and persevering entirely in a holy purpose. Do not put your trust in time, fortune, or age, because they are nothing, unless we are. Truly I love you sincerely in Christ Jesus, praying that he himself may arrange that I also am beloved by you, and that although I am absent from you in body, I may always be present in spirit. Therefore, since I continually remember you in my prayers, I earnestly beg you not to forget me. Farewell. The twelfth day before the Kalends of May, in the year 1500.

*CHAPTER XXIV.*

*WHOSE HOUR IS CALLED XEPHAN, AND*
*its supreme angel and emperor is called Sarandiel, having under him princes, leaders, counts, and many servants.*

The twelfth hour of the night, which borders on dawn, is called Xephan, and its angel is named Sarandiel. He has under his command many spirits, good and bad: princes, leaders, counts, and ministerials, who are appointed to various operations, the good to good things and the bad to bad things. These princes, as Hermes the Hebrew, who is called Solomon, says in the fourth book *On the Offices of Spirits*, often appear in the form in which the Devil is read to have appeared to Eve, having one beautiful head like that of a maiden, with hair hanging down on both sides,

*The Constitution for Announcing a Secret.*

When you see the spirits standing before you in a strange form, let them not frighten you, because they are good and benevolent, harming no one; and, lest they terrify anyone, they almost always hide their monstrous form, appearing with a very beautiful face. Entrust securely whatever you wish your friend to know in the hour of the night.
