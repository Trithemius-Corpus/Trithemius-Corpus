*Book One.*

Drobos 10   Curmas 10   80   40
Narmiel 10  Drapios 10  60   60
Frasmiel 10 Hermon 10   40   80
Brymiel 10  Aldrusy 10  20   100

Here we have the named dukes with their counts and sub-servants, whose service is sufficient for us for the completion of this art of ours for the present. And note that the first in order are dukes and princes. The second are counts; and they have between them two orders, which you must know above all else. Those who are in the first order, both dukes and counts, whenever they are called, always appear monstrous, namely with a maiden’s head, and with body and tail in the form of a serpent. Those who are in the second order are accustomed to appear in a likeness familiar and usual to us. Nor are fewer accustomed to come than one duke and one count following him in each order. When, however, you wish to operate through these spirits, first do what you know must be done according to the art; afterward add this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Vriel marfoys lamedonti noes, chameron, anducharpean phusciel arsmony tuerchoy iam ersyn nairiel penos raseon loes vear fabelruso cralty layr parlis meraij mea; thubra aslotiel dubyr reanu nauosti masliel pedonyto chemarphin.

When the conjuration has been completed, the spirits called will be present, each in his own order, ready and cheerful to obey in all things. :Q

Let the secret be very great.

Let there be for me some secret concerning a prince or a friend, the disclosure of which would bring danger to me and no small loss to him. Therefore, so that everything may remain secret between me and him, I entrust the secret not to a person, nor to letters, but to a spirit.

Let the letters be at pleasure, fearing nothing.

Begin, most beloved brothers, to amend your negligences, constantly wiping away past sins with tears, and asking the Savior of all the living for the remaining time. Hear, O Lord, the groans of those who constantly weep; grant pardon for vices, lover of the innocent.

After he to whom the letters are sent has received these or any other letters, marked at the end with the sign of the prince Vriel, let him, being skilled in the art, first do what he knows must be done according to the art; and let him say the conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Vriel Aflan pemason cosayr chameron, cathurm omerosyn ersoti chalmon sauepo Meduse reanalermosyn.

When the song has been spoken, the invisible spirit who has been sent will appear in the form customary to him, and will faithfully and most secretly reveal everything entrusted to him by the operator or sender, so that none of those sitting around can perceive or hear anything; and the secret will always remain hidden.

*Chap. XXI.*

*Whose supreme prince is called Bydiel, having under his dominion 20 dukes, and 200 counts; the number of servants is uncertain: they are very many.*

There still remains one of the supreme spirits appointed to us for the operation of steganography: last indeed in order, but among the first in dignity. By reason of his office he is called Bydiel, having under his command 20 dukes, 200 counts or subdukes, and very many attendants and servants. These dukes with their counts have a fixed order among themselves; and whenever they have been called by the operator, two dukes come with 20 counts, and they appear in human form, peaceful and ready for all things.
