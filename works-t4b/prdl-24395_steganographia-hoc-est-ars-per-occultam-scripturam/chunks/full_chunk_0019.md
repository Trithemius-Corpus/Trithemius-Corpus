*Steganographia*

...I could offer consolation for his misfortunes. But since I have been drained by heavy losses, as is clearer to you than daylight, I cannot satisfy my zeal as I would wish. But you, who are a kinsman and abound in worldly goods, should show yourself generous to a friend, and, as you have been accustomed, show yourself a humane patron of the necessities of friends. Therefore I ask you to lend him two hundred florins for a fixed term, because he is placed in the greatest necessity, as he will explain to you. I trust that he will restore everything in full when the term appointed for him has come. If you do this, I shall give thanks that will never die, and I shall stand by you in weightier matters. Act so that we may prove our sincere love in actual effect. I wish you to know that what you do for him is done for me; and no less will you bind him most gratefully to yourself, believe this firmly, and when the term has elapsed he will restore everything most completely. For he has been accustomed to keep his promises inviolate and to show himself steadfast toward all men.

Moreover, since you delight to hear new reports, I should like to write something. I have trained a puppy, if that is what it is rightly called by the Greek word, flourishing with such learning, discipline, and memory of all things that to some it seems to have cast off the beast and put on the human being. It understands Greek and Latin words and knows how to beg pardon for its faults. Whatever I command, it does; and when I nod by gestures, it reveals human secrets. You would see it imbued with such discipline of manners that you would judge it more learned than many men. When ordered to open a window, it opens it; and, surpassing nature by intelligence, when forbidden it puts the food back. It carries a burden placed upon it to be shown. It loves itself as the zealous defender of me, plays excellently, and does wondrous things altogether incredible to those unaccustomed to them. Farewell. Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of St. Martin in Sponheim, wrote this on 3 Ides of March, in the year 1500.

When the friend to whom these or any other letters are sent has received them, he understands the sign to belong to the prince of the west. Therefore, after first performing the preliminaries that are customary, he turns himself toward the west and calls the spirit, adjuring the invisible messenger that he become visible and tell no one.

*Song of the Spirit*

MALGARADAS apro chameron asoty methroes Zamedo
sogreal paredon adre Caphoron onatyr tirno beofy. Cha
meron phorty mellon tedrumarsy dumaso duise Casmiel elthurn-
peson alproys fabelronty Sturno panalmonador.

When the song has been completed, the spirit himself who was called will be present in his customary manner, faithfully revealing to you what he has received in his commission. Through him you will be able to send back to your friend whatever secret thing you wish him to know.

*Book One*

*Chapter X*

*Whose supreme prince is called Dorothiel, dwelling in the tenth mansion, which is called Chorus from the wind; and he has under him many dukes and servants.*

The tenth mansion is called Chorus from the wind. In it dwells the great prince Dorothiel, who has under his power and dominion forty dukes who preside over diurnal operations, and as many who preside over nocturnal operations, together with servants and attendants almost infinite in number. Their office is to announce all secret matters that pertain to spiritual business, to ecclesiastical gifts, benefices, prelacies, dignities, and the like. In the operation of Steganography, however, there is no need to bring forward the names of all Dorothiel’s dukes; rather, a few from each order are sufficient. We shall set out the names of the rest in their place with the others. And consider the table concerning these.
