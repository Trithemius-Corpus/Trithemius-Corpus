*Steganographia*

The counts, however, together with their servants, have been specially appointed to the ministry of this art. They are also more often sent to it without the presence of the dukes. Since they are from the highest order among spirits, they are accustomed to show themselves somewhat proud and rebellious toward those who call them, nor do they easily obey anyone, unless he is most skilled in the art of Steganography, without labor and danger. For they are insolent and very ready for illusions and mockeries of men, and they deride and jeer especially at those whom they have found to be less skilled in the art of Steganography. But those whom they judge to be bold, steadfast, and experienced in this art itself, they revere and fear, and they always obey their commands most promptly, with trembling and reverence. Therefore we shall now report the names of those of their counts who are necessary to us in operations of Steganography at this hour.

We now have four of the dukes of great Samael, and the same number of counts, and 4,440 servants from the threefold order. Their ministry is abundantly sufficient for us for every operation of the art of Steganography as regards the first hour of the day.

Therefore, when you wish to work in the art in the first hour of the day, first write on a small sheet the character of the ascendant lord, then that of the moon; afterward those of all the remaining planets according to the customary order, and at the end the character of the sign ascending at that same hour. And when you have completed this, write this conjuration on the back of the same sheet in silence, saying nothing.

Conjuration.

Samael affuar onayr myico layt madiel cuhiel naniel nabruys satiel atharbiel nadian naslon, ranyalcoha pe marson.

When this conjuration has been written on the sheet, as stated above, clearly and distinctly, you will prepare the letters to be sent to the one whom you wish to know the secret, in whatever form pleases you,

because it makes no difference in what form the letters to be sent have been composed, provided they contain no secret in themselves. Then, when the letters too have been written, call one or two of the counts according to the order, if you are working many times in this same first hour of the day, and say the conjuration prescribed from the same sheet, with the strongest intention, fearing nothing. When it has been duly completed, the spirits called will at once appear to you visibly, in a familiar form and one customary to them, calm, benevolent, and most ready for every service that arises. When they have appeared, entrust your secret to the principal one among them, saying: Ameniel, by the hidden power of this conjuration I command you to go as quickly as possible to Albertus Goelet of Ravensburg, prefect in Kreuznach, and to tell him in my name thus and thus. Having said these things, add the secret word, which must not be written; and immediately the spirit will fly away. Send the letters, sealed with the character of Samael, as is customary, by a messenger; they should contain no secret and should fear no one’s inspection.

Let the secret be whatever occurs:

Let it be a great secret, and an arduous one, whatever occurs for the occasion, and of such a kind that it ought to be communicated to no one: it must not be entrusted either to letters or to couriers. For in this art, especially through the spirits of this second volume, one must not work for small and trivial causes that can otherwise be announced by letters.

You will compose the letters concerning these matters as you please.

The majesty of the Catholic faith, to be firmly preserved by all men, has promised the happiness of eternal life to those who serve Christ. Hold the Catholic Christian faith; glorify the Almighty, fleeing the turmoil of vanity. No one of wicked deeds will have the blessed life. Let the faithful man follow Christ on the path of virtues. Integrity is adorned by good works, and the Catholic name is preserved by Christian faithfulness. For a wicked man clouds over the faith. Christ hates the rash, and loves Christians who practice Catholic works and revere tranquility. Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, published this on the Kalends of April, in the year of the Lord 1500.
