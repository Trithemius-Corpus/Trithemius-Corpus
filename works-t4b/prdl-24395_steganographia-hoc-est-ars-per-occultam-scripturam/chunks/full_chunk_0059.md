BOOK TWO. 109

CHAPTER XII.

CONCERNING THE SPIRIT WHOSE GREAT EMPEROR HAS UNDER HIS dominion many princes, dukes, counts, and servants. The hour is called Nahalon, and its spirit is Beratiel.

THE twelfth hour of the day is called Nahalon, and its supreme angel-emperor is called Beratiel. According to Solomon the Jew, he has under his command almost innumerable spirits appointed for various operations. From all these, different spirits in different ranks are assigned to the operation of Steganography. In the first rank there are dukes and counts, 10; in the second, 20; in the third, 30; in the fourth, 40; in the fifth, 500; in the sixth, 600; in the seventh, 700; in the eighth, 800; in the ninth, 900; in the tenth, 100; in the eleventh, 200; in the twelfth, 300. All these, by their turns and ranks, with an infinite multitude of servants, are assigned to the operations of this art; from them an intelligent and perfected operator in the art will be able to summon whichever and however many he wishes in the twelfth hour of the day. All the princes, dukes, and counts of this hour, according to the opinion of Raziel, assume forms at the operator’s pleasure.

Here, then, we have fifteen named from among the spirits of Berathiel, of whom five, through as many ranks, are dukes, and ten are counts. Joined to them are 1,100 servants for various operations in the secrets of magic. Therefore, when you wish to operate through one of them in the last hour of the day, observe the lord of the ascendant of that hour and the house of the twelfth; write the character of each, together with the other necessary things, on a clean little sheet, and on its back this conjuration.

STEGANOGRAPHY

Conjuration.

Berathiel Odiel irsoti rodu dreor ranezo melros ethiel aty nodiel hayres penafon.

Then, after all the usual preliminaries that must be set beforehand have been set beforehand, say this conjuration in the proper manner, and immediately the spirits summoned will appear. Then entrust the secret to the chief one, according to the instruction of the art, and he will obey faithfully in all things.

An argument for any secret whatsoever, as desired, that presents itself.

We have a friend far away; we know that a danger threatens him, of which he is unaware. We wish to warn him, but for certain reasons we do not dare do so openly. Therefore, according to the hour, we summon the spirits appointed for this operation and safely entrust the secret to them.

Letters, composed as desired, we seal with the spirit’s sign.

No one who boasts of temporal things is at all to be called virtuous; and one who loves transitory things cannot be a lover of Justice, for Catholic integrity detests a person who gapes after desires. For the holy religion of the Christian faith forbids love of the world to its people, since whoever loves worldly joys loses heavenly happiness. A Catholic who does not live in a Catholic manner is shown to have denied Christ; and one who loves temporal things is not said to be a good Catholic. Whoever loves the Creator seeks the joys of spiritual things. The lover of Christ despises all the pleasures of the world and does not care for unstable glory and riches. But the lover of the world hates virtues and cares nothing for future joys. Alas, what are you doing, Christians, intent on vain things? You think things that are not honorable, and you do things contrary to religion. Do not, wretches, I beseech you, do not love the soon-perishing vanities of this world. Take care rather to love Christ, that he may receive us into the heavenly kingdoms.

When one skilled in the art has received this prescribed little prayer, or any others, sealed with the character of Berathiel, let him diligently do everything prescribed in the art, and let him say the conjuration. And when he has seen that the spirits, who will come without delay, are present, let him say to the chief one, who walks crowned: Famerusyn melysno alny vemoby dreary drymes charsony.
