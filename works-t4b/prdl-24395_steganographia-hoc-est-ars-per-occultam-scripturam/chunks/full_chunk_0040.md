*Chapter XXVII.*

*Whose supreme spirit and prince is called Soleuiel, having under his dominion 200 principal dukes, 200 subdukes and counts, and innumerable servants.*

Among those spirits who gladly associate with men skilled in the art of Steganography and possessed of practiced habits, there is one named Soleuiel, not the least among the princes of this art. He has under him 200 principal dukes, and likewise 200 subdukes or counts. Among themselves they unfailingly observe this order: those who are dukes this year are counts the following year, and by alternating turns counts are made from dukes, and likewise dukes from counts. As for the servants, I have not yet found a fixed number; they too keep their ranks among themselves according to the arrangement of the hours.

R. R. R.

Inachiel Nadrusiel Axosiel 20 20 20 280 200
Praxeel Cobusiel Charoel 20 20 26 200 200
Morucha Amriel Mursiel 20 29 20 200 209
Almodar Prasiel Penador 20 20 30 200 260

Here you have twelve of Soleuiel’s princes, of whom the first six are dukes this year, and the remaining six counts. The following year these will be dukes and those counts, and so thereafter, always in alternating turns. I have gathered the number of sub-servants as 1840, although there are more whom I have not yet been able to count. But when you wish to work through these spirits, be constant and brave in spirit, and do not be terrified by their appearance when you see them appear in the form of serpents with a maiden’s head. Then do what must be done according to the art; after these preliminaries, add this conjuration.

*Song of Conjuration.*

Soleuel marfoy chamensyn oniel dabry pean veat, lasmyn cialmoty pedaros drumes, pean veat chameton loes madur noty basray erxo nadrus peliel thabren thyso ianothin veat petasyloes pean nothyr fabelton bauesy deameron est hiran pumelon meor dabrion crimorsiel peny veat nameroy lyernoti praisoner. When the song has duly been completed, the spirits called will be present in their accustomed form; entrust the secret to them.

*Let the secret be set down at will.*

I judge should be warned about certain imminent dangers. But I fear public disclosure, since from another’s danger I would make my own. So that I may avoid this, I entrust the secret neither to letters nor to men, but to a spirit whom I know will be most faithful in all things.

*Let us compose the letter at will.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim of the Order of Saint Benedict, sends greetings to the Reverend Father Lord Rutgerus Sycambro, archbishop of Narvia.

Nicolaus, the most reverend and most studious bishop of Irbursen, will explain to you that the general governor of your ecclesiastical affairs has handled the matter badly: he subjected the vicar of our lord King Desiderius, a kindly man, to chains, and killed him, tortured by hunger. We daily expect King Desiderius, the enemy, to break into the diocese. But we entreat you: come quickly to our aid, so that you may not fall into the enemy’s hands. Farewell. 9 Kalends of April, M.D. 3.

When the person to whom these letters are sent has received them, provided that he has previously been imbued with the general art, let him do what must be done, adding this conjuration with steadfast spirit and fearing nothing.

*Conjuration.*

Soleiel curtiel chamereyn faty pemalros dayr ianothy cathurmo parmoy iotran laudon frascu penoy ilthon fabelmerusyn. When the song has been spoken, the spirit sent by the operator will appear visibly, faithfully revealing everything entrusted to him; and if he wishes to send anything back to the operator, let him entrust it to the same spirit.
