*Book Two*

...the services may abundantly suffice for every operator. Yet, lest we seem to omit anything that can contribute to the perfection of this art, we have resolved to join a second book to the first.

Since, according to the tradition of certain wise men, individual spirits are assigned to each hour of day and night for various operations and diverse, wondrous effects, it has pleased us to imitate only their order, not their superstition: so that we may take from it, without injury to the Christian name, what conduces to our purpose, while we pass over the rest with contempt, as full of magical arts and contrary to our faith. Therefore, taking up the principal spirits assigned to the several hours, both of day and of night, according to the secret teaching of Solomon, surnamed Hermes, in his volume *On Magic*, as supreme emperors of the art, together with the other officers necessary to us for the proposed operation, we shall proceed. And we shall give both method and instruction to all students of this art, by which they may at any hour, with safer counsel, make known to friends the secret and hidden conception of their minds.

For each prince presides over his spirits, dukes, counts, and servants as a watcher. At his command, when called by us with the lawful song, they come; they carry the secrets entrusted to them, and show themselves obedient to us and ready in all things. But since I am again going to make mention of spirits, I think I must once more say beforehand: nothing frivolous is contained in this our art; nothing contrary to evangelical tradition or the Catholic faith; nothing at all superstitious is taught. For all the things that we have said in the preceding volume, or shall say in the following ones, rest upon natural, lawful, and honorable principles. Only the mystery veiled by foreign usages, and the words wrapped in the names of spirits, require an educated reader. For we use the service of a spirit to veil a secret which, if made public, would harm the wicked. And lest anyone fail through error in reckoning the hours, let him know that we have taken the hours of the planets both in the day and in the night.

*Chapter I*

*Samael, the supreme emperor of the first hour, presides over the first hour of the day. He has under his command several dukes, counts, and servants, whom he sends, when called in this hour only, to an operator experienced in Steganography.*

The spirit and emperor of the first hour of the day, which takes its beginning at sunrise, is called Samael. He has under his command very many dukes, counts, and sub-servants, who, when called by an operator in Steganography, come in this hour alone. They carry the secret that is entrusted to them, but they utterly refuse to come outside the hour. And note that the dukes and first princes of Samael are rarely sent by him for the service of this science, since their office is to stir up magical and nocturnal illusions among human beings.
