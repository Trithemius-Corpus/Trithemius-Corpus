And after the master of this art has explained to his disciple the conjuration, order, and sequence of all the spirits, let him disclose to him the most secret mysteries of this knowledge, which have not been written and must not be written. Under the oath already sworn, he shall forbid him to reveal them to anyone as long as he lives, except to worthy persons sworn in the art, nor shall he write them down in any manner or for any reason. Then let him teach him the natures, names, places, numbers, offices, and properties of all the Spirits appointed to this art: how, when, and where one must work through them, and the remaining secrets that pertain to the perfection of this art, concerning which nothing is to be written.

PREFACE.

After, with the aid of our Lord Jesus Christ, savior of faithful souls, we completed the first two books of our art, in which I have described our Steganography as best I could, we must join this third book to them, and add things still more wonderful to wonderful aids. I found in a certain book by an ancient philosopher, who is called Menastor, that it is possible by a certain art to make known the thought of our mind to a friend, however far absent, within twenty-four hours, without words, without books, and without a messenger, most perfectly, most fully, and most secretly.

Since I was driven by an extremely strong desire to know, I began to wish to test the truth of these sayings; and after many labors I finally found the knowledge itself, proved by experience. The secret of this art is very great and wrapped in such obscurities that it is not easily accessible to anyone. For Menastor enclosed the mystery in exceedingly obscure and few words, and used such powers that it might remain secret, so that after him no one has yet been found who has dared to make his discourse clearer by any instruction. But I, fearing lest it come into the hands of the unskilled, or finally be burned in the fire, since I have now long tested it by many proofs, have wished to commit it to writing in rather clearer letters, so that, with God’s help, it may become somewhat accessible to learned men and to those most devoted to magical studies; and yet at all times remain hidden from unskilled Rapophagi, and in no way become known to their dull understanding.
