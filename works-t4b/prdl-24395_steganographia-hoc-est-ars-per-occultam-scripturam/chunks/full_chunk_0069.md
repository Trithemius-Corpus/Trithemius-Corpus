*Book Two.*

*Chapter XXI.*

*Whose hour is called Zeschar, and whose spirit is called Pamiel, having many princes under him.*

The ninth hour of the night is called Zeschar, and its angel, the supreme emperor, is called Pamyel. Like those preceding, he has under his command many other spirits, good and evil: princes, dukes, counts, and ministering spirits assigned by their turns and orders. In the first order there are 10, in the second 20, in the third 30, in the fourth 40, in the fifth 50, in the sixth 60, in the seventh 70, in the eighth 80, in the ninth 90, in the tenth 100, in the eleventh 1,000, in the twelfth 100,000. But in the operations of Steganography we do not need all these, since a few are enough for us.

Here now we have eighteen princes from the spirits of Pamiel, who in their turns are sometimes dukes, sometimes counts. Anyone who does not know their orders will labor in vain in this art. They have 1,320 ministers assigned under them by the turns and moments of the hour, with whom they perform marvelous effects. Therefore, when you wish to operate through them in Steganography in the ninth hour of the night, observe the lord of the ascendant of that same hour and the whole figure of the heaven; write everything on clean paper, and on the back of that paper write this conjuration.

*Conjuration.*

Pamyel lyraz lafan mauelo ureothis thirmoan erfoti layr pornis theoti moar azas, penason. Then, when all the things prescribed in the art have been completed, say the conjuration itself, taking the greatest care not to omit anything required for the perfection of the operation.

When the conjuration has been spoken, the named spirits will immediately appear visibly in the form that you ordered them to put on. For all those whom we have named are benevolent, good, ready, and willingly obedient to one who operates rightly, and faithful in the things entrusted to them.

*The Argument of Secrets.*

Whatever secret occurs to us, which otherwise we cannot safely make known to a friend either by letters or by messengers, we entrust to the first among the spirits appearing to us, and we are secure. The more powerful one among the others is distinguished by his dress and crown.

*Let the letters be formed at will.*

Howl, mortals, you who now laugh, and who in many ways have most ungratefully despised the kindness of our glorious redeemer by very grave sins and dreadful crimes. Behold, the Turk, the deadly enemy of Christians, will move his army from Hungary and will slaughter men like beasts. The savage one has crossed the Danube, inflamed as if by fire with zeal for impiety. Therefore mourn, most wretched people, destined to die in a dreadful slaughter; you will die in bitter zeal, because your offenses will overtake you. You have despised the laws of God, your most kind redeemer. We have heard how cruelly the Turk has invaded Hungary, Bohemia, and Poland: he has destroyed very strong cities; women and virgins consecrated to God, keeping chastity in zeal for holiness, he has most shamefully violated, horribly slaughtering Catholics of every kind, ranging everywhere like cruel beasts. You will feel his horrible cruelty in a short time: for he will suddenly trouble Germany with his most warlike army, unless a general defense by the princes resists him without delay. Therefore, groaning, take up humility; amend your ways; pray to God with tears that he may grant you remission of your offenses and free us from the enemies who thirst greatly for our blood. I wrote this on the thirteenth day before the Kalends of May, in the year of the Lord 1500. Johannes Trithemius.

When the person to whom they are sent, skilled in the art, receives either these letters or any others sealed with the sign of Pamiel, let him observe and write down the lord of the ascendant and the whole figure of the heaven, as we have instructed, on paper.
