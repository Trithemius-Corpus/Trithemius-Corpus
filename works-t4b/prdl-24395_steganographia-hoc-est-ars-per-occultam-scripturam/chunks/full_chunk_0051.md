*Book Two*

The fourth hour of the day is called Elechym; its supreme angel is called Vathmiel, who has under him, appointed for this art of Steganography, ten very notable dukes and one hundred counts, while the number of servants is infinite. They have the office of announcing generally all secrets, and they are very good, well-disposed, and obedient in all things whatsoever they are commanded. Yet, as lovers of secrecy, they greatly detest the commotion of people. Therefore beware that, when you are among others who are not skilled in the art, you do not attempt to work anything through them, because, when conjured, they would not readily approach, and they would not depart without danger to you. Keep everything that we prescribe to you, and you will make great progress in this science.

R. R. R. R.

Ammyel Emarfiel Itermiel 10 100 100 100
Larmiel Permiel Thurox 10 100 100 100
Marsiel Queriel Vanfiel 10 100 100 100
Ormyel Strubiel Zafniel 10 100 100 100
Zardiel Diniel Hermiel 10 100 100 100

In this table we have five from among Vathmiel’s dukes, ten from among the counts, and 1,550 in the number of servants, whose service is sufficient for us for every operation of Steganography at the fourth hour. Therefore, when you wish to operate in this hour, write on new paper the character of the lord of the ascendant and of the twelfth house, with the rest, and on its back this conjuration, as is proper.

*Conjuration*

Vathmiel adres rhearefo raser rheotij venofi fayr fatiel cafa irfoti verotiel does ro fabel merufin. Then, after you have first done those things that customarily must be done beforehand, say the same conjuration in a low voice, and immediately the summoned spirits will be present: at least one duke and one count, with their servants, ready to obey your commands.

*Procedure for a Secret Messenger*

Let there be any secret, great and very hidden, which you wish absolutely no one to know except one person. Summon as many spirits appointed to the hour as you wish, and entrust the secret to the chief one. Write letters,

in whatever form you wish, in which nothing secret is contained and which need fear no one’s inspection at all.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, to Rutger Vencaius the Sicambrian, Canon of the Order of the divine father Augustine of the monastery of Damentis, greetings.

Those matters which he proposed to me to be solved, with Christ’s help I have resolved. Whether well, you who proposed them must decide. I shall briefly report to you what happened, or rather a summary. A certain brother recently reported to me, and also a friend, not the least of men, that a young man had been raised from the dead at the invocation of Christ, reporting wondrous and dreadful things among the dead, and narrating the torments of wicked Christians, namely of those who by acting perversely crucified Christ. They are especially guilty of this: that, forgetful of the most gracious benefit of redemption, they always remained ungrateful to Christ. He said that they suffer horrible things, those who were known to be ungrateful for his benefits by not living well, and who, blinded by sin, neglected future joys while pursuing the pleasures of the world; and that, in the bitterest way for the negligences they had committed, they cried out: “Deservedly we endure these things, we who were always ungrateful to the redeemer of the human race. We were Christians in name, Gentiles in action. Wretched as we are, we always sought earthly consolations; therefore we receive the due recompense of eternal damnation. Alas, most unhappy that we are, why did we offend Christ? Why did we despise the time granted to us?”

The resurrected young man reported many other things to us, which I am now wholly unable to write, being especially hindered by serious business. Therefore, brother, let us zealously imitate the footsteps of Christ, always giving thanks to our most gentle Savior, and praying that he may pardon the guilt we have committed. Farewell. 3 Kalends of April.

When the one skilled in the art has received the letters sent to him, let him observe the lord of the ascendant of that same hour and the sign of the twelfth house, and let him write their characters, with the rest, on paper, together with the other things required for the operation. Then let him say the conjuration written above, and, once the spirit has been seen, these words: Camerusin aproysiliertto thulnear venean maueas fabelron. When these have been said, the spirit that was sent will approach and will faithfully and without danger reveal the secret entrusted to him.
