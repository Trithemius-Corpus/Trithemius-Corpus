*Chapter 24.*

*Whose prince is called Pyrichiel, having under him many dukes, princes, counts, and servants, whose number is uncertain.*

Pyrichiel, a certain supreme prince of spirits, does not seem to us to be altogether rejected from our art, because he is said to be quite benevolent.

He does not have under him dukes or princes in the manner of the others, but only counts and subordinate servants, whose number is uncertain. There are some who say that he has dukes and princes under him; but they seem to us not to understand his property and custom sufficiently, since they have imagined counts and servants to be dukes. The name Pyrichiel derives its word from fire, because he is accustomed often to be around fire.

When these things have been completed, you will see the spirits whom you have called present; and the principal one is always accustomed to appear in the form of a serpent, having a beautiful head like that of a maiden, with hair spread out. There follows:

*Let us posit any secret whatever.*

I have a secret for a friend absent far away, which I cannot safely send by letters, lest they be read if intercepted, or else seized on the road. I have less confidence in entrusting it to a man to carry, since I know that loyalty among men changes with fortune. Therefore, so that everything may be safe, I call upon a spirit, a friend of secrecy. He comes at once when the song has been heard; he faithfully carries what has been entrusted to him.

*Let us compose letters as one pleases.*

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, to Jakob Draconius, canon of the Premonstratensian order, greetings and charity.

There is with us a distinguished mathematician and an excellent rhetorician, continually zealous in the pursuit of a most tranquil life. He has with him almost countless books and an inspectable treasury of various copies: a pleasant and benevolent teacher of upright and good life and of honorable fellowship, most expert in resolving doubts. Great matters are being expounded; his name is Orophanius, burgrave of the kingdom of Bosnia. Farewell, the 12th day before the Kalends of April.

When the person to whom they are sent has received these or any similar letters, signed with Pyrichiel’s character, being skilled in the art of Steganography, let him do, as he knows, what must be done according to the art; then, from the consolation, let him say this conjuration.
