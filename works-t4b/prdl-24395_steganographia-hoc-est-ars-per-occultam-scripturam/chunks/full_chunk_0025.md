*Steganographia*

The fourteenth dwelling is toward the North, distant from the beginning of the North by 21 degrees and a little more. Its supreme emperor is Symiel, who has under him ten dukes, who preside over daytime operations, with their many servants. Many dukes preside over nighttime operations, whose definite number I could not discover; they too have many servants under them. The office of these is to announce among friends the most secret hidden matters, which are to be disclosed to no human being forever. But so that, if necessary, you may be able to operate through them, I shall explain to you ten from the daytime spirits and the same number from the nighttime ones; they will suffice for the present.

You now have ten princes named for daytime actions, with many subservient attendants, and ten also for nighttime operations, with many substitutes; through them you will be able to operate securely and without danger, provided that you are well instructed in Steganography. For whoever, not well instructed in the operation of this art, presumes to approach its precepts will either accomplish nothing or will scarcely escape danger. For the princes do not obey everyone. But when you wish to operate with this very knowledge, after the things that must duly be done beforehand have first been done, turn yourself toward the North, and, in the manner I have handed down to you, conjure the duke so that he may come by this charm.

Charm to the Spirits.

Let us set down as an example any secret whatever, to be entrusted to absolutely no one.

You have some very secret business for a friend, concerning both you and him, whose disclosure would bring either lasting harm or disgrace upon you and him. Certainly it is not safely entrusted to letters, lest they be read by someone, nor committed to a messenger, who would divulge it to many. You will use our art; thus you too will be safe, and your friend will know the secret. For this purpose let us compose whatever little letter seems suitable, in no respect at all suspicious.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, to the excellent young man Jacobus Trithemius, his dearest brother: greetings.

Devoting yourself to letters, preserve humility of heart. Good morals adorn young men and make them more distinguished than nobles. Knowledge of letters adorns a young man who loves holiness; without the morals of justice, knowledge will lack honor. A youth immersed in vanities and vices, a despiser of wisdom, does not love true wisdom, which you should remember must be sought through virtues. The instruction of youths to be educated is adorned by morals, since honor is conferred upon the heavenly arts, and unfruitful knowledge will be tortured with eternal torments. He advances in the knowledge of virtues and letters; both fail when stained by vice. Spurn pleasures, so that by experience you may understand the truth of wisdom. Farewell.

The person receiving these letters, being skilled in the art of Steganography, as soon as he sees the character of the prince Symiel, understands by comprehension what he must do; and, after the things that must first be done have been done, turning toward the North, he says the conjuration:

Conjuration.

Symiel marlos chameron pyrcohi pean fruary fabelronti gaelto siargoti melastor hialbra penor olesy Aiulbrani ordu Casmeron omer vemabon. Once the charm has been completed, the spirit sent appears and faithfully performs the office entrusted to him.
