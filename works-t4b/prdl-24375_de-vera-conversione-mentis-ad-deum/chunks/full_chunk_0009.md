For union between God and the mind cannot come to be unless, after all things have been cast aside that are wont either to stain the affection or to confound the intellect, the mind itself is joined through love: pure to the most pure, clean to the most clean, one to the One, and alone to the Alone.

Now, man, approach the deep heart; and after such cultivation of your soul, whereby through the study of letters and the various traditions of teachers, from youth to old age and advanced age, you have labored for a little knowledge, at last cast the seed of divine sweetness into the field of your heart, so that someday you may receive the fruit of most holy love, without which no one will arrive at the enjoyment of the unchangeable good. The studies of letters are indeed beautiful; to devote labor to received theology is pleasant; to have knowledge of law, astronomy, poetry, music, rhetoric, arithmetic, philosophy, and the other arts is certainly delightful. Yet one must not grow old in them in such a way, not linger, not consume all the time of life; rather, from these one must ascend by the ladder of love to the highest beauty, in which the true blessedness of the soul infinitely transcends every created beauty.

Otherwise, O learned men, what does it profit to cultivate the field of the mind laboriously through the daily study of letters, as though by plowing, if either, barren, it is not sown with divine seed, or the slender fruit of inward love is choked among the thorns of riches and the briars of pleasures? If we believe the proverb, or rather, if with open eyes we see what experience teaches us, integrity of life is rarer among the learned: the brighter they are in intellect through acquired knowledge, the fouler they are in affection. In their person, Saint Augustine, having heard of the conversion of Saint Anthony and moved by just grief, cried out: “The unlearned rise up and seize heaven, and we, with our doctrines, serving flesh and blood, descend to hell.” Turn back to the heart, O wise men; leave vain cares; purify your souls from every carnal affection; drive out hatred together with love; cleanse your minds of all things that can impede the sweet divine influx,

so that you may be able truly to love God. To his surpassing enjoyment the soul can in no way be admitted after the present life if, while set in the flesh, it has not loved him above all things. Therefore, so that a gradual ascent toward true conversion may be prepared for the mind, since no one suddenly becomes supreme, we shall erect, in a few words, a ladder distinguished by seven steps. By it the mind itself, ascending in some measure to the knowledge of the highest good and meanwhile persevering steadfastly in holy exercises of divine love, may deserve to obtain sweetness.

The first step, then, is the purification of the soul itself from every affection for corruptible things, actions, and desires, so that it may be clean, quiet, tranquil, released from cares, empty of evil desires, free from every carnal love, and united to itself as far as possible in its innate beauty. For the soul’s natural beauty, formed by the highest beauty, is made foul by mixture with the body it has assumed, just as ugliness befalls gold if it is covered over with clods of earth. If someone shakes these off, pure gold at once remains; and it is now beautiful when it has been separated from foreign matter, content, that is, in its purity, with its own beauty.

In the same way, when the soul has been separated from those desires that it conceived in itself through too much commerce with the body, and has been freed from the other disturbances, with the stains now erased that bodily familiarity had burned into it through long habit, and thus left alone to itself, it has without doubt purged away every ugliness previously absorbed from an alien nature. And when the beauty it had lost has been found again, it then ascends the first, and the more difficult, step of divine knowledge itself. For this reason the oracles of the Scriptures rightly prophesy that a soul not purified in the present life from the sins contracted in the flesh is tormented after death among the lower regions, lying in filth. For an impure soul, because of its depravity, very much like filthy swine in body, takes delight in filth and dirt.

What else is true temperance, by which alone the monastic religion is especially adorned, than not to indulge bodily pleasures, but to flee far from them as the most impure diseases of the soul?
