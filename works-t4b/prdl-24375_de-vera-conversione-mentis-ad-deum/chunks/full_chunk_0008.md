Therefore his beauty too is lovable above all beauty, for he created every beauty; and since he is both its beginning and its end, he does not participate in it, but, existing beyond comparison and immeasurably above every posited thing in his own beauty, goodness, truth, and eternity, he transcends it.

¶ But now you will have to consider further how the mind itself ought to conform itself to its own principle; by what inquiry it must return to that principle after the enticements of pleasures; what necessity or what usefulness is imposed on those who return; and finally what is the manner of purging the affection from the lowest and carnal passions, as it seeks the highest and unchangeable good, so that, free and released from desires opposed to reason and to the rectitude of the mind, it may put on the upward passage of love. For our soul itself, made in the image of God, as Scripture bears witness, is always stirred by the natural love implanted in it, and desires to have recourse to the fountain of its beauty and to be united to its own principle, which reason has most excellently persuaded it must love; and the nature of loving, surpassing every appearance, consists in the single object of blessedness itself. Whatever you show it by referring it to appearance is not enough, since in that very thing it always seeks something else that formed it. For reason teaches that everything which has form, and form and appearance itself, is something measured, and for that reason is neither universal, nor self-sufficient, nor beautiful by its own nature, but rather mixed, not pure, not simple, and made by another. Therefore these things must indeed be beautiful, but measured. But the highest good itself, which is truly beautiful, or rather above beauty, is circumscribed by the limit of no measure. Certainly it is not formed; likewise it is not appearance, nor something participating in any of those things that are known to us or to anyone in the world. Therefore that which is first lovable and exists first utterly surpasses every appearance; and there the beauty of intelligible beauty, above every understanding, and its comeliness, is the highest goodness itself, free and absolute from all things that are and that are not, always existing, the living principle of all things, the highest and unchangeable good of the rational soul, everywhere and always present.

When this first shines upon a soul now serene and purified from the lowest things, it kindles a wondrous love, when, that is, the soul, transcending and casting off in the mind all forms and intentions, not only imaginable but also intellectual, then through love of the infinite good despises itself and rejoices with ineffable joy in all things that either can be spoken or thought of God. This indeed the soul can sometimes attain not only after the present life, but even in this life, when, purified from the lowest things and freely restored to itself and united, it is borne upward through the desire of love, while its own affectionate movement toward God remains, so that, utterly forgetful of all things that are in the world, and even not knowing itself, it rejoices with inexpressible joy. Those who have experienced these things know them; if time permitted, their virtues could be cast in the face of those who, since they are carnal and devoted to vices, with a veil spread over their heart, dream that the mystical theology of the saints and the contemplative life have no truth in them, and judge that they are fashioned, as they themselves are accustomed to say, in the opinion of an unlearned man, according to the quality of affections. Of them the holy apostle said: The animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit, for it is foolishness to him, and he cannot understand, because it is examined spiritually. And holy Jude the apostle says: These are they who separate themselves, animal men, not having the Spirit. Unhappy are those who immerse themselves in earthly cares and the pleasures of the flesh, who hunt after the vain knowledge of this world together with false honors; just as their mind, always occupied with the lowest things, never knows how to shake itself free from sensible things, so it does not rise up to the sweetness of divine love. You are mistaken, O mortals, and very greatly mistaken indeed, who, whether intent on vain studies, or sunk in the cares of the world, or oppressed by pleasures of the flesh or evil passions, think that you either know or love God.
