For as Saint Dionysius the Areopagite, disciple of Paul, says in the Mystical Theology: that divine majesty, existing far beyond every affirmation and negation, according to our intellect, is neither soul, nor sense, nor imagination, nor opinion, nor reason, nor understanding; nor does it have reason or understanding; nor is it spoken or understood. Nor is it number, nor order, nor greatness, nor smallness, nor equality, nor likeness, nor unlikeness. Nor does it stand, nor is it moved, nor does it enact rest. Nor does it have power, nor is it power, nor is it light, nor life, nor substance, nor age, nor time. Nor is there any spiritual touching of it; nor is it knowledge, nor truth, nor kingdom, nor wisdom. Nor is it one, nor unity, nor deity or goodness; nor is it spirit, insofar as we ourselves can know. Nor is there a denomination of Son or of Father; nor is it any other thing among those that are known to us or to anyone in the world. Nor is it anything of those things that are, nor of those that are not. Nor do those things that are know the highest good itself as it is. For there is no speech of it, nor name, nor knowledge; nor is it darkness, nor light, nor error, nor truth; nor is there any affirmation at all of it, nor any negation. But when we make affirmations or negations of those things that come after it, we neither affirm nor remove it itself. For that highest ineffable good, the perfect and singular cause of all things, is above every affirmation; and its excellence is above every negation, simply and absolutely free from all things that can be thought, understood, or spoken by us or by anyone in the world, and beyond all things. can conceive in the mind, grasp with the intellect, or utter with the mouth is not God. But what he is, we can in no way know, understand, or say. Therefore, since that highest good, God, the immutable principle of all things, is absolutely none of those things that we understand or can understand, and since we can understand only by the service of the senses, which feed on the mass of bodily objects, while everything spiritual is farthest removed from those senses: whoever still clings to the senses, whoever, blinded by the passions of his flesh, sinks the desiring power of the soul into the chains of evil affections, cannot possibly either ever perceive this gift of mystical illumination himself or profitably persuade others about it. For only the soul of a clean heart, lifted up from the lowest things not by intellect but by affection, can now, while established in the body, sweetly see God by loving, in a mirror and in an enigma. And when at last, released from the flesh, free and absolved from every disordered affection, it has returned to its principle, then, more united to God through the grace of love, it will know him perfectly, as the Son of God promised us, saying: “Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God.” But now, proceeding to the examination of the senses, we shall see how far their consideration is from the knowledge of God.

¶ That great prince of theologians, Saint John the Evangelist, at the beginning of his theology, beautifully concludes concerning the highest good with affirmations that seem nearer: “In him was life, and the life was the light of men; and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it.” Come now, man, you who trust in sensible things, who have learned to exercise the mind for understanding from nowhere but through sensible things; come, I say, and question your senses themselves, so that they, who report to you countless other things, may teach what the light is that shines in the darkness and cannot be comprehended.
