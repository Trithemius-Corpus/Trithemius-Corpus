If anyone, fathers, thinks that I am about to deliver this oration on the conversion of the mind either because I desire novelties or because I am moved by empty little glory, he is greatly mistaken. For the very profession of our religion, instituted by most holy men, is nothing other than a certain path of our own soul, which freely, tranquilly, and without restraint desires, through the exercise of holy love, so long as it lives in the flesh, to turn itself back to its own principle and to be perpetually united with it.

For this reason, moved freely according to the force of our institution, we have left behind the world and all things that are in the world. We have resigned ourselves, all that is ours, and our own very will into the hands of another, for the love of God, in the presence of all the saints, for no other cause than this: that our soul, having nothing in this world to which it may cling with delight, may always be tranquil, quiet, free, and truly released from all things seen on earth, and may be able to raise itself, without impediment from anything, to the love of the highest good by daily, and indeed most holy, exercises.

For a certain writer says in sacred matters:

Those at rest can in some measure search the law,
If hearts, free from the world’s noise, are vacant.

And again, concerning the contemplation of heavenly things, which alone is the wisdom of Christians, he says:

Wisdom fills a mind tranquil and free from cares,
And will be the guest of a peaceful breast.

Since these things are so, among all the matters which the consideration of our salvation is wont to bring before us, there is nothing about which we ought deservedly to be admonished more frequently and more earnestly than this: that, remembering daily as far as we can the brevity of the present life and the terrible necessity of our approaching departure, we should always carefully draw our soul away from all earthly things. For it will never be able to be united with the highest principle unless, released and free from all other things, it has first been united with itself.

But for persuading men of these things no one is fit, no one is sufficient, no one is rightly ordered, except the one whom divine love, by the singular gift of its grace, has made experienced; so much so that whoever confounds his understanding through sensible things, or disturbs his affection by the passions of the flesh, is no more disposed to the contemplation of eternal things than an owl is to the light, or a blind man to the knowledge of colors. Into the mystery of this matter, to the mountain burning

with divine light, Moses, who was to become leader of the people, did not dare to approach until, after he had taken off the coverings of his feet, by which worldly affections are signified, he was admitted naked and released to the contemplation of divine things. And that great prophet Isaiah, when he had seen the mystical vision of almighty God sitting upon a lofty throne, trembled, mindful of his own frailty, and did not consent to thrust himself further into divine spectacles until one of the seraphim, flying to him, had cleansed the fearful man’s mouth with a burning coal.

And we, fathers, whenever we are about to speak or hear about the highest and unchangeable good, should lay aside the shoes of worldly affections, fearing that which God threatens against vicious men who dare to scrutinize his majesty, saying: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” Let us also raise our mind upward through the desire of inward love, so that, touched by the coal of divine fervor, it may be made worthy to be filled with the mystical inspection of divine spectacles.

For I say to you, who have gathered in this lecture hall of an unlearned man, that no one can truly love God without some prior knowledge of him; and that knowledge, the maker of love, comes to no one who has not first been purged in affection from all hatred or love of those things that are in the world and that follow the movement of the flesh. Knowledge of the highest good causes love in us, and love in turn perfects knowledge. For the Son of God also said to the Father concerning believers: “This is eternal life: that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent.” Therefore from knowledge arises love, and from love arises for us the everlasting enjoyment of the highest good.

That highest and unchangeable good, which we call God, from whom every good in heaven and on earth is and is named, incomparably far transcends both our understanding and the angelic understanding, since indeed he is above every position.
