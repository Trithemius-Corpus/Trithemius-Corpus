Fortitude, however, is to fear none of the things that are transitory, nor even death itself, which is nothing but a certain separation of the soul from the body; and the mind does not fear it when it desires to be alone and to be happily united forever with its own beginning. Moreover, what else is magnanimity than contempt for mortal things for the sake of divine love? And prudence is understanding that turns away from lower things and raises the soul to higher things. When the soul has been instructed in these teachings and already purified from the filth of vices, reason and beauty become united to it, so that thereafter it yields to no pleasures of the flesh opposed to the highest principle. Wonderfully restored to beauty and understanding, it advances, illuminated by the ray of divine splendor.

The second step is knowledge of the works of God. When the mind considers their beauty with admiration in many and diverse forms, it cannot be that it does not at some time lift itself upward, with the desire of love, to the author of so great a beauty. Hence the holy Apostle also says to the Romans that the invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, are seen, being understood through the things that have been made. For all the beauty of the world has been formed by the supreme Beauty, and by its variety it invites us to knowledge of him, to such a degree that nothing is more foolish than one whom so great a variety of beautiful things does not kindle to contemplation of the supreme Beauty. What else, too, does the very sequence of the Scriptures suggest to us than that that highest, unchangeable good, God, in the beginning created heaven and earth, and all things contained within the compass of that heaven; and that the miracles and wonders made by him, of which there is no number, by their own cry compel us, as if by hands, to knowledge of the highest truth, unless we are harder than stones?

The third step is contemplation of the order by which lower works are brought back step by step to higher ones. For since the supercelestial world, and also the celestial world, is most ordered, the sublunary world too is necessarily ordered; and since in it things lower than man are ordered, human things are much more so. Therefore all individual things that happen to human beings are justly dispensed by God, although the cause of them may be hidden from us. For the highest providence

governs the world by so beautiful and so manifest an order that no clearer proof of its goodness and wisdom need be sought by us from elsewhere than in what the ordered structure of the universe itself proclaims to us.

The fourth step is a certain proportional comparison, relating itself from the order of lower works to the divine order. For just as the beauty that is the author of all beauty must be infinite, wholly sufficient in itself, and in need of nothing, so the ineffable good, surpassing all things, by whose most peaceful will ordering all things it has clothed the universe with varied beauty, always remains in its majesty in a wondrous and incomprehensible order.

The fifth step is negation, by which you separate from the highest principle of all things everything that you conceive, just as Saint Dionysius taught in the Mystical Theology, and as we spoke in the earlier part of this discourse of ours. For since that highest and incomprehensible majesty, the cause of all things, is above every positive assertion of those things that we or anyone else in the world can know, understand, or say, it rejects every image, likeness, form, or species; and therefore our mind rises nearer to it by negation than by affirmation.

The sixth step is humble prayer to God, that he, the father of the intellectual world, may make you pure and actually intellectual. For this purpose you must remember the benefits and free gifts that the goodness of almighty God has generously granted to you, though you did not deserve them, and above all the benefit of redemption, which alone, when recalled with attentive use and familiarity, is sufficient to soften the mind to deep compunction.

The seventh and last step of this ladder is this: when, by the gift of the good itself, you have already become an intellectual world, then, further stirred by love of that good itself, you are transformed from the intellectual state into the good that is above intellect. There the quiet seat of holy contemplation unites the holy soul through love with its own beginning. Although it remains in its own essence, yet, withdrawn from all alien things, it becomes one with God by grace.
