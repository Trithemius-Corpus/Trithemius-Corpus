Woe to you, and again woe, who, immersed in earthly cares or pleasures, do not rise up to the love of the highest good; who, with your heart turned away from the highest principle, are monks and Christians in name alone. I say to you: unless you are converted to repentance, you will have no share with the lovers in the kingdom of God. Wondrous, indeed astounding, is your madness, which has prevailed so far in blinding you that, although you always live rebellious and opposed to God, you nevertheless so boldly expect from him the kingdom of heaven, as though you were just.

And how can it happen that a soul joined to the body and subjected there to excessive fellow-feeling, a soul that does not admit into itself the ray of divine love, should at last, when separated, go joyfully to God, whom it did not love when it departed? O wretched and unhappy ones, how shamefully you deceive yourselves, who seek your consolations anywhere other than from God. It is impossible for a soul departing from the body to reach its own principle, which is God, joyful and blessed, if, while placed in the body, it did not love God himself above all things. For pure love, free from and detached from all else, makes union between God and the soul; and when this has been neglected by a person throughout his whole life, it must be believed that it can never, or only rarely, be grasped at the point of death. Therefore live, mortals, while you live, bound over to love, always attending to the end of life; the less it is known to you, the more it is to be feared.

Come now, soul, search out where he is who created the whole world with such great beauty, who bestowed upon us so excellent a life, who promised bright and eternal dwellings in heaven to those who love him. You see beauty itself flashing in all things and in varied forms. It is beautiful to dwell in it; yet it is more beautiful and more necessary for the mind, already present in the beautiful, to investigate where these things come from and whence they are made beautiful. It is indeed necessary that the very author of beautiful things be none of them

at all; otherwise he will be some part of the things he made, which reason does not admit. Therefore the principle is God himself, above all things, who made all things, whom no intellect can comprehend, because he is above every intellect, and because he created every intellect. He is worshiped in spirit and truth; he is seen by the love of a purest affection, invisible, always remaining immutable, making not himself but us, by our affection, be changed according to the image of his goodness.

Since he has made all things and can do all things, the ineffable principle of all things is not circumscribable, but immeasurable, incomprehensible, including all things, filling all things, containing all things. And because he is immeasurable, he has no magnitude, since magnitude has place only in external things. Indeed, the author of magnitude must not be a participant in magnitude. When he is called great, there is no quantity in him; rather, his greatness is that nothing is more powerful than he, and nothing more sublime, and that there is nothing in heaven or on earth that can be compared to him.

Moreover, when we say that God himself reigns through all ages of ages and everywhere throughout the universe, this does not signify measurement in him, nor again any vast immensity; otherwise, by what means would he measure the rest? Nor is it permissible to fashion in him any figure or form, since that incomprehensible divinity and immeasurable majesty of beauty far exceeds, by infinite comparisons and into immensity, everything that can be fashioned, thought, comprehended, or said; nor is he any of the things that mortal man has ever understood or could understand.

About this the prophet Isaiah rightly exclaimed: Truly you are a hidden God, the God of Israel, the Savior, who make darkness your hiding place and dwell in inaccessible light; by whose wisdom every human being has been made foolish. O immense beauty of the divine majesty, to be tasted only by loving affection, desirable to the mind above all things, of which we can receive no figure or form, to be desired and loved by us with all our strength; whose love is immeasurable, and whom to know and love is eternal blessedness. For this love is not finite, since even the beloved himself is not finite, but his love is infinite, always in eternal happiness.
