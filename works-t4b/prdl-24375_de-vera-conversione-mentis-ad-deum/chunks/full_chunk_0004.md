Have you heard what light is? Show me, if you know, what material light is, so that through it an ascent may be prepared for me to the spiritual light which I have sought in all things. Hearing will answer you, saying: I am airy; be content if I report airy sounds to you. I have not heard light. But if I have ever heard anything about it, it was not light itself, but air moved by the tongue, very far removed from the truth of light, which began and passed away. Such is not the incomprehensible light.

Smell will say: I indeed am not so luminous; I am of vapor; learn vapors from me. Why do you seek from me things foreign to me? If light is not vapor, it has nothing to do with me. Taste, when questioned about light, will answer: I dwell among liquids. If the light you seek is not a liquid, either sweet or bitter, or at least mixed, judgment of it does not pertain to me. Touch will answer: Do not force from me what I cannot comprehend. I am corporeal; I report corporeal things. Seek from others; I do not know what light is.

Turn at last, finally, to the shining eyes; by the light in which, above the other senses, indeed in which alone, they delight, call them to witness. Let them indicate to reason what light is. Sight will at once answer: I am a shining spirit, and I am a spiritual splendor; when questioned, I shall gladly set forth my proper offices. Light is a certain spiritual, sudden, and very broad emanation from bodies, without detriment to their own nature: namely, an emanation of a certain brightness from transparent things, but of color from the opposites of these, and of quantity, figure, and motion from all things.

Now gather every kind of color into one genus. What will this whole be, except a certain many-colored light, or light made opaque in the more solid and darker matter of earth? Separate out the earth mixed with it. What will remain, except a certain quality, indeed the brightness and act of the transparent, just as color is the act of the opaque? Color indeed is opaque light; light, however, is clear color, indeed a certain flower and vigor of a transparent body and of colors, as if one-colored in act and many-colored in power. What is this that sight has reported to you about light? Is this the light that it described? I have never heard anything defined more obscurely. In what way can it happen that nothing is more obscure than light, than which nothing is clearer,

when through it all things are clear? See what sense contributes toward the incomprehensible light, when it cannot rightly comprehend even the material light in which it is and is moved. Therefore, despising the blind tradition of the senses, let us ascend from here to the lofty watchtower of the mind, to see whether perhaps the mind itself can intimate to us what the light is which is said to shine in darkness.

O mind, you who measure all things rightly, tell me: is perhaps the light that shines in darkness God himself, than whom nothing is clearer and nothing more obscure? Nothing is clearer than that God exists, and that he is supremely powerful, wise, and good; for whatever has been made by him proclaims this to our ears, so that no one is deafer than the one who does not hear so great a sound and din of all things. On the other hand, nothing is more obscure than what God is, so that nothing is more darkened than the one who thinks this is perfectly clear to him.

But the mind, illuminated by the light of the catholic faith, and raised through the knowledge of intellect to the cognition of heavenly things, will answer: God is the Father of lights, with whom there is no change by which he might be extinguished or diminished, nor any shadow of turning by which he might in turn enter night or suffer any defect. Again it will say that God is the light in which there are no shadows, the form in which there is nothing formless, and also the beauty in which there is nothing deformed.

But what are these things to him who is above all things and transcends every affirmation infinitely far: who made those things that are out of nothing; uncreated, eternal, immovable, incomprehensible, always existing; who is none of the things that he made, he who made all things? Therefore, mind eager for heavenly things, abandon the senses, pass beyond reason, and, withdrawing even yourself into the affection of the heart, transcend yourself. And even if not for others, at least for yourself do not be slow to acquire by love a taste of the highest and immutable good, leaving behind you all images and every likeness, so that by freely, absolutely, and purely going beyond yourself and beyond all things, with all things removed and, released from all things, naked and alone, you may be able to fly up to the supersubstantial ray of the divine darkness. Meanwhile lead your intellect captive; indeed, leave it buried.
