Hence it is that many who are devoted to profane arts labor to summon demons to a circle, to a mirror, or to any other receptacles by dreadful conjurations, and obtain no effect: because either God’s command stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious person who has not been devoted to him by homage.

For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is clear that none is perpetrated: a witch raging in spirit and of depraved will, the cooperation of a familiar devil, and, before all else, divine permission. For if one of these does not concur, she will not be able to perpetrate witchcraft. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not meet the will of the wicked woman, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch were lacking, the devil would by no means bring witchcraft to completion. For the devil uses the will of the raging witch as a craftsman uses an instrument for working, without which he could not inflict witchcraft.

And just as demons differ from one another in power and in sharpness of subtlety, so the power of witches is not one and the same in all, but differs in individuals according as they are joined by demons of subtler or grosser nature. For there are many kinds of demons, and they stand apart from one another in fixed ranks by reason of the places into which, when they fell at the beginning, they were thrust down.

The first kind of demons is called fiery. It roams about the higher air, and is never plunged down to lower regions before the day of judgment; rather, remaining continually beneath the lunar regions, they have no intercourse with human beings on earth. That they remain beneath the moon is attested by Saint Augustine in the work On the Christian Combat, saying thus: Demons do not dwell in the heaven where the sun and stars are, but all move about beneath the lunar region. Moved by this, as I think, Apuleius said that demons are fiery animals, because Aristotle affirmed

--- Page 106 ---

that in burning furnaces certain small animals have rather often been seen flying about, fitted with little wings, which pass their whole life in fire, since they arise with fire and are extinguished with it, and, vanishing, return to their own place whence they had come. They have no association with witches, since, because they dwell in fire, on account of their subtlety they cannot put on a gross or aerial body.

The second kind is called aerial, which wanders in the air and dwells near us. These indeed can descend to the lower regions and, having assumed bodies from the denser air, sometimes appear visibly to human beings. With God permitting, they quite often disturb the air, stir up thunder and storms, and all together conspire for the destruction of the human race. After the manner of human beings they are moved by the affections of passions, especially pride and envy, and are touched by continual disturbances; and they are neither clothed in a customary body, nor do they remain in one place. Nor do they all have one form,

but many, and these they change rather often according to the variety of the affections by which they are either summoned by witches’ charms or driven by disturbance to do harm. For they have an altogether violent and furious disposition, full of agitation; hence, when they have been greatly injured and disturbed, they contrive sudden ambushes for the most part, and while they carry out their attacks, they partly wish to remain hidden and partly inflict violence.

Witches supported by the cooperation of these demons are the more powerful for practicing witchcraft, the higher the order of the demon they have obtained as their associate. In the Life of Saint Antony, the blessed Athanasius said that the air is full of demons, which Mercury, called Thrice-Greatest, had said before: namely, that no part of the world is without the presence of demons. The holy bishop Ambrose also says: The world is full of holy powers, because it is full of wickednesses. Hence the Platonists have reported that demons flying in the air, in the manner of very dense fiery snow, are visible to those who desire it.
