Questionum:
They say that they can constrain malignant demons, so that they appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal, or in some other receptacle, and answer distinctly to all questions asked. All of them indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found whom the malignant spirits obey in this way, even among the wicked, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it, lest human curiosity, prone to novelties, should despise the religion of Christ and in time of need seek help from demons. But all those whom malignant spirits, summoned by the conjurations of necromancers, obey, have without doubt passed into their fellowship through an explicit or implicit pact. Yet the greater number are the others, whom the malignant spirits disdain to obey, although they are never afraid at any time to busy themselves in the profane arts of necromancers. And because in our own times especially iniquity has begun to abound, and the charity of many has grown cold, these accursed and most deceitful men have gained too much strength; and although

Believe me, Caesar: this most deceitful race of men is very harmful to your sacred empire, and therefore must be utterly exterminated no less than the source of witches. They promise great things, and they lie about everything. For indeed all the things promised in their books are empty and false, and they give believers nothing other than tricks, frauds, and most subtle deceptions. But if at any time they seem to display what they have promised, it is manifest to everyone that this happens only through the cooperation of demons, from whose fellowship one must flee no less than from the most savage torments of hell. For demons very often come to these necromantic operations, and, in order to keep their invokers stubborn in the treachery they have begun, grant them what they ask if they can. Sometimes, however, when invoked by conjurations, they disdain to appear, and nevertheless they have led astray the minds of those invoking them.

Questionum. 83
There is also another class of people, no less foolish than superstitious: namely, those who do not wish to have any commerce with demons, and nevertheless use, in empty operations, things appropriated to demons, and for that reason wholly unlawful. For they think that great and hidden power inheres in characters, and therefore they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But because this is not the time to recount their madness, let us pursue our purpose. Why good and just people do not obtain command over demons must be stated briefly.

Just as likeness of wills makes wicked and impious people equal to demons, so unlikeness of wills renders the good and just wholly opposed to them. Between opposites and dissimilar things there can be no familiarity; rather, continual resistance and hatred prevail. Nor do you, glorious Caesar, since you are a man, the great governor and prince of men, admit anyone into familiarity with you who is wholly opposed and unlike you in morals, and who continually resists your will. But because good and upright men always oppose the most depraved will of demons, they can in no way pass into fellowship with those with whom they neither wish nor consent to be familiar; rather, they flee and detest their encounter with all their strength. And therefore they do not obtain that fictive command over them of which we have spoken. For nowhere is familiarity secure when no likeness of wills has preceded it; nor can there be concord between opposites, nor friendship stand firm between those of unlike will.
