Medicinal Powder

Of anise,
caraway,
ammi,
parsley seed,
spikenard, of each ʒ s.

Of red coral;
of pearls, or unpierced pearls, ʒ v.

Of white ginger,
bitter-sweet (Chamepyt),
leaves of hay;
of burnt tartar, of each ʒ v.

Of mace,
cubebs, of each ʒ ij.

Of cloves, ʒ vij.

Let a powder be made according to art.

Its dose is ʒ s., to be taken morning and evening, in broth or wine, for the first month. In the second month, only in the morning; in the third month, in the morning three times a week; and so thereafter it is to be continued for life. For it is effective against many illnesses and is a most tested

and efficacious powder. It strengthens the stomach, purges the brain, clears the eyes and sight, sharpens the memory, consumes bad and superfluous humors throughout the whole body without danger, soothes and opens constriction in the chest, drives away nausea, prevents excessive fatness, preserves from epilepsy and apoplexy, dissolves the material of stone without pain, and does not permit it to harden into a stone; it does not allow chiragra, podagra, or sciatica to prevail. When Paulus Scalichius wished to give this powder for use to the Duke of Prussia, whose councillor he was at that time, the prince’s physicians forbade it; from this there arose a dispute among them, in which the case for and against that powder was fought out in writings. That dispute is extant in Scalichius’s own Philosophical Satires.
