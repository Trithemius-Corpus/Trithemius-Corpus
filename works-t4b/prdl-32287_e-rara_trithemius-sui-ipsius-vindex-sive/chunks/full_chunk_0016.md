Apologetic Defense

I can make the conception of my mind known to one who knows my art, at any distance whatever, at a hundred miles or more, safely, without words, without writings, without signs, through any messenger. If he is seized on the road, if he is questioned even under the harshest tortures, he can disclose nothing about my message, because he knows absolutely nothing about it. Whatever happens, my message will always remain hidden, nor could all the men of the whole world, if they were gathered together, track it down by natural power, etc.

I can also indicate my will to one sitting in prison, if he knows the art, even though I am far away, however closely he is guarded, even if he were sitting three miles underground. And all these things, as broadly and universally as I wish, whenever and as often as I wish, I can do naturally, without any superstition or aid of any spirits whatsoever. They are marvelous things, I admit; but hear still more marvelous things.

The fourth book contains many astonishing experiments, but purely natural ones. Namely, I can indicate my mind to one who knows my art while eating or sitting with others, without words, without nods, as broadly as I wish, even while speaking, preaching, playing the organ, or singing, without any hindrance to whatever other action is being performed. Thus, while preaching good and holy things, I may indicate, not by words, not by signs, not by nods, to anyone who knows the art, as fully as I wish, whatever I wish, even with my eyes closed. And the fourth book contains many other secrets that are not to be brought into public.

All who hear these things marvel at them; among them are many noble and most learned men, who suppose that these things are either impossible or wholly supernatural.

Apologetic Defense

To them I answer, as I have answered many, that many things are possible by nature which, to those ignorant of the powers of nature, seem impossible, or even supernatural. And behold, I speak before my God, who knows all things: the marvels I have foretold are far more excellent, deeper, and greater than I can write or you can believe, and yet all are purely natural, without any deception, without superstition, without magical art, without the invocation or service of any spirits whatsoever.

I have said these things so that, if perhaps some rumor should ever reach you that I either know or can do marvelous things, you would not, I say, think me a magician, but a philosopher. For what happened to Albertus Magnus, that most profound investigator of natural things, namely that because of the marvels he performed by the hidden power of nature he was regarded by the common people as a magician, I am certain can happen to me in the same way.

Behold, before God, because I do not lie: I have taught these things to no one yet, although many have often asked me, promising great things, except one prince.

I am writing to that prince, whom I made more certain of the possibility of the art by plain proof. Nor is it fitting that anyone should know these things except princes; otherwise many evils would be done through them: betrayals, deceptions, fornications, and whatever else wicked men might wish. But those who use the art well could accomplish many good things for the commonwealth through it. Moreover, I can teach all these things in every language of the whole world, even one I have never heard. I wished to tell you these things, Bostius, so that you would not think me idle, etc.

But, Trithemius continues, before this letter of mine had reached Ghent, Bostius departed this life to Christ during the Easter season. The prior of that place received the letter, opened it, read it, and, astonished, as I suppose, by the novelty of the promises, shared it with many others who asked to read and copy it.
