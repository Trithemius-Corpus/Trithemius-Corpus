Therefore temptation is the greatest test of a person, as sacred Scripture bears witness: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the trial of tribulation tests righteous people.” Patience is easy in prosperity, because no adversities strike the mind. But the tribulations of the righteous are many, and from all of them our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the guarding of humility, so that a faithful and righteous person may always fear God and never rely on his own strength or righteousness. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: “They are not in the labor of men, and they will not be scourged with men; therefore pride has held them fast. They are covered with their iniquity and impiety; their iniquity has come forth, as it were, from fatness.” It is the mercy of God that he sometimes permits good and righteous people to be shaken by adversities, when he knows that they cannot be humbled in prosperity. For tribulation has made many humble whom favorable circumstances would have made proud. Finally, the fearful imagining of acts of sorcery makes a good person fear

But no proud person, presuming on his own powers, should think himself safe from the sorceries of demons, because the person whom God’s protection abandons when he is swollen with pride undoubtedly lies open to the sorceries of demons.

The fourth reason is that there may be a distinction between the good and the evil. For Scripture says in the Acts of the Apostles concerning good people beloved by God: “Through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.” And holy Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: “All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution.” Likewise the most wise King Solomon says in Proverbs: “The Lord corrects the one whom he loves, and, like a father, takes pleasure in his son.” It is a most glorious thing for a Christian to endure adversities patiently for the love of God, whether they are brought upon him by wicked people or by demons. to harm good and righteous people, not for their confusion, as the Petrones suppose, but rather for the very great and certain consolation of the soul. Indeed, when a righteous person lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeps his commandments with all diligence, and nevertheless is afflicted by sorcerers, whether in body, in possessions, or in both, that person is truly blessed: for when he has been tested he will receive the crown of life that God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the Catholic Church of Christians, and, receiving evils in this world, patiently endures them for the love of God, has a sure sign that he perseveres in the grace of God and will be saved. Therefore we read this written concerning the wicked and reprobate: “Why do the impious live? They have been lifted up and strengthened with riches. Their houses are secure and peaceful, and the rod of God is not upon them. They spend their days amid good things, and in a moment they descend to hell.”

For it is a manifest sign of damnation if a person lives here badly in sins and vices, contrary to God, and is prospered in the goods of fortune and in bodily health.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and righteous people to be afflicted by the wicked and reprobate is for the complete purification of the mind. For, as Scripture says: “There is no righteous person on earth who does good and does not sin.” But since God lets no sin pass unpunished, in his mercy he has ordained temporal penalties for righteous people for their offenses, but eternal penalties in his justice for the impious and reprobate. Therefore in his mercy he changes the eternal penalty for the penitent into a temporal one, which they must necessarily suffer either in this world or after death in purgatory. But because the torments of purgatory are dreadful and very severe, merciful God is accustomed to purge in this life, in various ways, the penitents whom he loves, through the inconveniences of different tribulations in body and possessions.
