The fourth reason for divine permission is that the Creator’s justice may be declared against those who have sinned and have not done penance; for if all did good and no one sinned, God’s justice would have nothing to punish. Therefore, so that justice may have a place, the creature sins by divine permission. For the Lord is just, and he will render to each according to the works of his own will.

The fifth reason is the dignity of the human condition: for if God did not permit man to sin, he would seem worse off than the brute beasts, which, with no violence driving them, freely use the motions of nature. For although brute beasts lack reason, nevertheless they have a free motion of nature which they follow; by comparison with them man would seem of a lower condition if he did not have free choice of will. But God permitted man a free will, over which neither kings nor princes rule.

The sixth reason is the praise of the merit of constancy in human virtue, when we praise a just man for this reason: because, although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, with the grace of God helping him, remained in the good constancy of his will.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits demons to do such great works of harmful magic through the agency of witches is this: that those who, having despised truth, take delight in falsehood may be seduced by the devil, the inventor of falsehood, and be utterly deceived. For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who disdain to follow the saving teachings of Christ, should all be deceived by demons and plunged down to hell, there to be punished forever. They seek aid from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have adored. For it is just that one who, abandoning truth by his own will, clings to falsehood should be hurled by the devil into destruction.

whose power and strength is over every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits great things to the devil and denies certain small things, he makes manifest in all things how weak the enemy of the human race is, and how subject to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing by himself; rather, all the things he does, he does with God permitting them. Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God permits works of harmful magic to be done, is justice, by which he preserves, in inviolable order, the liberty which he once bestowed upon the creature from the beginning. But because the power of every creature dependent on its Creator is restrained and limited, it is clear that neither the devil can do everything he wishes, nor a man, however depraved in will. The will indeed is free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For indeed, if demons could do everything they wished, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would overthrow everything. Satan did not dare to tempt him any further than had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if, at their own discretion, they could always use a free power to harm. Before the Lord’s Passion, however, they used greater freedom of power than afterward; but when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, from then on their power was greatly changed, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of the world will be cast out.” For the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints greatly restricted the boldness of demons, so that they can rightly be called instigators of evils, but by no means drivers. Nor would the devil gain strength against us unless he received his strength from our sins.
