We call the third kind of demons terrestrial, whom we do not at all doubt to have fallen from heaven to earth according to their deserts. Concerning these, the most holy bishop and martyr of Christ, Theophorus, once Ignatius, wrote thus in a certain letter to the Ephesians: Nothing is better than to have peace, in which every weapon of the aerial and terrestrial spirits alike is made void. Of these demons, some dwell in woods and groves and set snares for hunters; others live in open fields and make travelers wander astray at night; some remain in hidden places and caves; the rest, less furious and disturbed than the others, take delight in dwelling with human beings in obscurity.

They are not led by one passion, but by various ones, since some are less depraved than others, although all are full of evil disturbances. Of these, some delight in frightening human beings only with illusions; others wish, by predictions of future things, to draw their hearers into admiration of themselves. Some, however, labor with the greatest effort to terrify, injure, or kill human beings who are disturbed by irrational fury or raging with melancholy; we have learned by experience that this has often happened. Hence Saint John Chrysostom says in the third book On Providence, to the monk Stagirius: More powerful for doing harm than every diabolical action is the greatness of sorrow, because whomever the demon overcomes, he overcomes through sorrow. If you remove it, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon.

This kind of demon is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy of their passions and the levity by which they delight in frightening many people rather than being subject to the command of one little woman. Yet at times, as they promise to the insane, they dwell in glass, crystal, or a mirror, and, stirred up by charms, they give answers to women; and if anyone who is coming has spoken ill of them, they announce it beforehand on the road.

And this kind of witch sometimes differs greatly from the rest, since what the others make infirm by sorcery, these women promise to cure. But because for the most part they repel sorcery by sorcery, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, since, immersing itself in waters, it dwells around lakes and rivers. It is full of anger, disturbed, restless, and deceitful; it stirs up storms at sea and, sinking ships into the deep, takes life from many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, they more often appear in the female sex, and are seen more rarely in the male, because they frequent moist places and follow a softer course of life, nor can they easily assume a common form by the habit of the others.

Hence antiquity long ago named the Naiads and Nereids, nymphs of the waters, not in the masculine sex but in the feminine. Among our people, moreover, they are called by the common term aquatic women, that is, Wasserfrauen. But those who live in drier and somewhat arid places, whenever they appear bodily to human beings, transform themselves visibly into men. They also assume the forms of various beasts, according as they are touched by various passions. Holy angels, however, since they never vary in passion, always appear uniformly in male form. For we nowhere read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast, but always in male appearance. Yet demons have very often been seen around rivers and springs in the appearance of women, sometimes dancing in choruses, sometimes loosening their hair in the manner of women, and at times speaking with human beings and practicing various deceptions. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have dealings with acts of sorcery.
