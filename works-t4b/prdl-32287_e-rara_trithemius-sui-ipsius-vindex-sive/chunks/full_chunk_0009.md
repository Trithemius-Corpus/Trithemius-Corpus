Socrates in Plato gives the warning that the mysteries of sacred things must be veiled, so that they may be kept apart from the unworthiness of the common crowd. Elsewhere Plato advises that it is altogether unworthy and foul for the mysteries of hidden things to be opened to the people, since those things are handled with polluted hands by the human race, though it is lawful for them to be treated only by persons initiated into the sacred rites of philosophy. Nor do the Hebrews neglect this in treating the sacred laws.

And, to say a little more about the signs of the ancients, they certainly used marks, strokes, and single letters in place of writing, both to write more briefly and to depart from the common form of writing. Thus they signified civem Romanum by C R, oportere by O P, duntaxat by D T, ab urbe condita by A B V C, and de isto lapide invenies aurum by D I L I A. This obscurity of words and sentences may be seen in almost all branches of knowledge. In books of law the ancients denoted calumniae causa by the two letters K K, dolum malum by D M, and iudex esto by I E. They also signified a condemned man by the letter K, but an acquitted man by A. Mathematicians too, especially astrologers, retain these remnants of obscurity and figures. For they depict the planets, and likewise their Zodiac, or twelve celestial signs, with individual characters; for example, the Moon from its waxing and waning horns, Gemini from their embrace, Sagittarius from an arrow, etc. So too they mark their aspects, triplicities, and configurations of the eighth sphere. Thus * signifies a hexagon; □ a square; △ a triangle. The conjunctions of the planets, moreover, are expressed by such a sign, and oppositions by this mark.

The chemical art also uses such signs, so that chemists may describe simples obscurely. For chemists mark sal ammoniac with the sign Δ, arsenic with ∞.

Likewise musicians have devised various characters and notes to denote time and measure, as may plainly be seen in their books. And it is certainly remarkable that by musical notes of this kind the thoughts of the mind can be signified secretly to others.

In the military art too, to pass over the special stratagems of our own men and the various methods of signifying secretly, there are distinctive signs among the ancients. For in the short lists in which the names of soldiers were contained, there was a proper usage by which commanders could examine the number of soldiers, how many of them survived and how many had fallen in disasters. For those who survived were marked with T, tau, at the beginning of the little verse; those who had fallen, however, with o, theta.

I pass over the most learned poets, who, devoting their labor to composing fables, by the skillful invention of a single narrative sequence handed down one thing to the unlearned and another to learned men.

Therefore, since, as is clear from what has been said, almost all branches of knowledge have adopted characters proper to themselves, by which the materials of the sciences might be transmitted, if not more briefly, at least more secretly, Trithemius too not undeservedly handed down his precepts in such a way that they would not be accessible to everyone, especially since understanding them was not even advantageous for everyone.
