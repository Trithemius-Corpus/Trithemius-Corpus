It is therefore no wonder, most excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to obey depraved and perverse human beings, whom, as we have said, malevolence has made like themselves and destructiveness toward the human race has made to be in agreement with them. But let it be far from every Christian to wish to command demons through a wicked likeness of will, since if anyone in this life has begun to have fellowship with malignant spirits, in the life to come he will burn with them without end.

But why are good and just people unable to command the holy angels of God, into whose likeness they have passed by good will? I think that, from what has been said above, the solution of this question would easily be given, if the difference of intellects were recalled to mind. Questions. 87

ON THE POWER OF WITCHES,
Question Six.

The sixth question of your most invincible Majesty was: whence comes this so great power in witches, who can accomplish so many, varied, and wondrous things even in a single hour, things such and so great that none of the good could accomplish them in his whole life?

If we wish to answer this question briefly, we must necessarily say that all power is from the Lord God, without whom no creature can do anything. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: Without me you can do nothing. Hence it is manifest that all power of doing anything is granted to human beings by the Lord God, and from nowhere else, whether people do good or do evil. Thus the most holy bishop Augustine says: The will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily forms and motions, and nothing comes to be in this most extensive

Hence there is no doubt, Caesar, that that power of little witches, by which they produce wondrous and horrendous effects with the cooperation of demons, is granted by the Lord God, whose will no one can resist, and without whose influence a human being cannot even move his hand. For the holy doctors conclude that neither demons nor witches can do anything, whether to harm a human being or a beast of burden, unless almighty God wills, consents, and grants the power that they may do what they have already determined by a perverse will. Unless God consents and wills, no creature can do anything. Otherwise, if something could happen when he did not will and consent to it, it would necessarily follow that God was utterly weak, since he could not prohibit what, against his will, he would be forced to permit.
