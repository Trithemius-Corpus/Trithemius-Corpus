For the edification of monks, a notable work which he entitled On the Threefold Region of Claustral Persons, three books.

He also wrote certain other smaller works, whose titles he did not indicate to me.

These, Nicholas, dearest brother, are the Trithemian works which, during the twenty-three years in which he was abbot, he labored over at Sponheim for the honor of God, amid cares and the continual occupation of household affairs: reading or writing day and night, whenever he was able to steal leisure for himself. During the years in which he was in charge, he brought into the library of Sponheim a very great treasury of books in every faculty, not only by the art of printing but also of manuscripts. For when I was with him for the space of one year to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, he counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eighty had been handsomely written with the pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his own mouth that he had spent more than two thousand florins on books,

which he had not taken from the substance of the monastery, which had always been very meager, but, since he was known and dear to many princes, he spent for the most part on books the gifts with which he was honored by them. For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books, beyond all whom I know living in the flesh today. It is said by many, moreover, that his successor, who is now abbot at Sponheim, has alienated very many volumes; whether this is true, I certainly do not know in the least. In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had arisen between the kingdom and Philip, count palatine, Trithemius, fearing the enemy, withdrew with his library and the possessions of the monastery to Kreuznach, the nearest town, and I likewise returned to my own affairs.
