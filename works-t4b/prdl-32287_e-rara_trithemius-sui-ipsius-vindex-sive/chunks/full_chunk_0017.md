Of all those who rashly judged matters of mine unknown to them, the most rash and most unjust appraiser of things at last burst forth openly: Carolus Bouillus, a Picard by nationality. Hence, since he did not ask for the key of understanding, he deserved to hear or perceive nothing of the things contained in that lucubration of ours.

After this, having returned to France, he repaid us evil for good and shamefully violated the covenant of Christian friendship. For when he was asked by Lord Germain de Ganay, a mutual friend, later Bishop of Orleans, what he had found and seen with me at Spanheim, he wrote back falsehoods for truths and lies for benefits, condemning and rashly judging things that he was in no way able to comprehend with his understanding. I firmly affirm, I speak truthfully, and I confidently swear upon my soul that I have never had any commerce with demons, or with depraved and pernicious magical or necromantic arts; but that all and every thing which I have either written or promised that I would write is pure, sound, natural, and in no respect at all contrary to the Christian faith. Indeed, as far as I am concerned, I would rather placate my rivals by silence than irritate them by letters, or provoke them to madness by writings. For I judged it better to condemn all these wondrous inventions to perpetual silence than to incur, even by the false estimation of men, the mark of pernicious magical or necromantic superstition. Let the Steganography therefore lie hidden in darkness, and let it not become common to the lying Bouillian company, which is accustomed to judge matters unknown to it and to corrupt the reputation of a good man by the mere lust of depravity.
