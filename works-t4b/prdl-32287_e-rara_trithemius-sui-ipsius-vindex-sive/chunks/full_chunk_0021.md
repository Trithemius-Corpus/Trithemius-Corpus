ON THE REPROBATE AND WITCHES.

The fifth question.

The fifth question of your most serene Highness was this: Why can impious, evil, and reprobate people in matters of faith, such as those women whom we commonly call witches, command malignant spirits, when good men and the most zealous observers of the Christian faith can command neither good spirits nor always malignant ones?

I remember that I formerly answered this question as follows. In all the works of the divine majesty, the sequence and order of beauty belong to an inviolable equity, by which each thing, fittingly disposed, persists in the rank it has received. For, without its proper order, the freedom of will that the Creator of all gave to human beings is driven by no violence of power so as not always to remain in the order that the first human being received from the beginning of his creation. Therefore no one among mortals is good unless, assisted by the counsel of his own will, he is made so through the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil unless, by his own choice, he is deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin.

--- Page 085 ---

Fragment

By uprightness of will, however, we are made like the good spirits, if we prove the piety of the faith that we have in Christ by holy and necessary works. And just as the perfection of a good will makes good men equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious makes them like demons. Hence, the more evil and perverse people have been corrupted by the malice of their own will, the more they become like demons in disposition. But likeness introduces the familiarity of friendship, and from this there gradually arises the confidence of the lesser toward the greater, and command, whether it is real or feigned by trickery. Thus, if there seems to be any command of wicked people over demons, it arises from the magnitude of their depravity, namely when their disposition is so corrupted that, by a reprobate will, they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons associating with them.

Of the Questions

For not all demons are equal in malice or intelligence, but one is much craftier than another in swiftness of understanding, and far worse in corrupted affection. For those who, having fallen from the lofty seats, are more closely joined to the earth and inhabit caverns and caves are certainly duller than the rest, even to such a degree that they fear swords and rods and seem to be moved by the threats of people who terrify them, as one may observe in those whom we call demoniacs. But demons who dwell farther removed from the earth are of a subtler nature and more rarely meet with human beings. Yet the more people full of malice advance in perversity of will, the more like demons they become; and once admitted by them into familiarity, they thereafter become bold and receive command over the demons whose familiarity they use, not by violent power but by feigned simulation.

Apologetic Defense

For demons, in order to keep those people whom they have once received into their submission willing participants in their depravity, gladly subject themselves to them as though moved by a simulated domination; and what they do of their own accord, they pretend to do as if compelled to obedience. For no one obtains true command over demons except the person whom faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says concerning those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.” Therefore evil people command demons because malevolence has made them like them, likeness has made them familiar with them, and familiarity has made them their own and subject to them.
