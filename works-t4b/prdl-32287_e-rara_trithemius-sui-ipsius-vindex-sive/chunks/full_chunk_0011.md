Certainly I would believe that I could more easily extricate myself from labyrinthine windings, advances, and retreats than, without the Key, disentangle myself from steganographic intricacies. Do you not think that, just as in the earlier books, so also in this book he made use of mysteries? Can it not be that by “motion” he meant the operation itself, and by the space of twenty-four hours the twenty-four letters, or alphabet? Therefore the words must be taken not as they sound, but in a mystical sense; nor should one believe that here a physical agent acts at a distance without an intervening medium, which certainly seems to be the case to those who do not know and do not understand the words.

For I pass over the fact that it is not necessary for the agent always to be present to the patient according to its own substance, or by contact of the subject, but that it suffices if it is present to the patient virtually, or by contact of power. I say nothing about sympathies and antipathies. But, to say more, reader, do you believe that it is contrary to physical reason that the thoughts of the mind can be made known, without words, without books, and without a messenger, to an absent friend? What reasons would those be? I myself will show you by experience that, through the art which I have drawn from Trithemius, I can make known to a friend sitting at my table or standing by whatever he wishes, and that through thirty, fifty, or a hundred words, indeed through words fixed and prescribed by you; yet to that friend, whoever he may be, I shall neither speak a word nor send a messenger, and, if you wish, I shall not even look at him; and this physically and naturally.
