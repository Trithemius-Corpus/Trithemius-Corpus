Apologetic Defense

Lest honey produce vomiting in those who are nauseated, or truth hatred in those who are indignant. Every time must be valued according to its own merits; and swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor laden with precious gems. All things are untimely when they do not agree with the customs and the age of the mortals upon whom they are thrust. There have been many very noble talents of men in unfitting times, and many new things are found almost daily in times not their own.

What profit did the wondrous invention of nautical art bring to the excellent sail-maker Bartholomaeus under Pope Innocent VIII, which in a certain little book he offered for sale at one hundred thousand ducats, when neither the supreme Pontiff himself, nor the Venetians, nor the Genoese, to whom he proposed selling his matter, deigned to come to terms with him even for a modest price? For he promised that for thirty months, by his art, he could keep a ship unharmed in the midst of the sea, repair it when broken, and without danger recover whatever had been cast into the deep. But no one cared.

Nor does it escape me that many, with Naso, have perished by their own genius. It is therefore better to set a limit to knowledge than, without profit, to expose oneself to the hatred and detraction of many, to whom it is more familiar to despise any knowledge whatever than to have learned it. These are the words of the Polygraphic Author concerning the uses of Polygraphy and Steganography.

But come forward at last, Trithemius, come forward, and as avenger yourself defend your cause from so great a stain and so atrocious an injury. The most reverend and most learned man Johannes Trithemius, while still alive and before he gave the often-named work of Steganography to the public light, began to be so bitten by the teeth of certain envious, or rather ignorant, men, especially a certain Bovillus, that he even published an apologetic work in his own defense (would that it were at hand, for it would contribute not a little to this Apologetic Defense), and resolved to commit that Steganographic work entirely to darkness. Trithemius narrates the whole matter thus in the Polygraphic preface

which may God forbid, the whole world will marvel at; the title of its first book is STEGANOGRAPHY. There will be four books, and each will be divided into at least one hundred chapters. I began this work at the urging of a certain great prince, whom there is no need to name. You ask what this work teaches? It teaches the greatest things, astonishing and incredible to all who do not know them, things that have never been heard from the beginning of the world. For the first book contains more than one hundred ways of secretly writing whatever you wish, without any suspicion, without transposition of letters, without any fear, so that there is no man in the world who by natural industry could know, or even suspect, what is contained in my letters, except the one who knows the art from me, or from the one whom I have taught. The words are plain and familiar, lacking every suspicion, but no one without the art will ever be able to perceive my intention, however learned he may be; and it is a truly astonishing thing.
