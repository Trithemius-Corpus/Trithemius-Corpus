Apologetic Defense

There, at rest, he writes various works; when asked, he recently sent me these titles of them:

To Maximilian Caesar he published an admirable work, which he entitled Polygraphia, in six books.

To the same, the key to that same Polygraphia, one book.

Replies to eight questions of the same Caesar, one book.

To the same, On the Seven Intelligences Moving the Spheres, one book.

To Joachim, Margrave of Brandenburg and Prince-Elector, Various Questions, Orations, and Propositions, twenty books.

To the same, On Foreknowledges Permitted to a Christian and Not Permitted, two books.

To the same he wrote that admirable and laborious work of Steganography, in eight books, which that reckless Bovillus, not understanding it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely.

To the same prince, Letters, one book.

To the same, Against Malefactors and All Arts and Superstitions Forbidden by the Church, five books.

To the same, On the Causes and Cure of the Falling Sickness and of Malefactors, three books.

To Jacob Trithemius, his brother, An Itinerary of His Life, two books.

To Burkhard of Horneck, physician, On the Property of Nuns, one book.

He wrote a History, or Annals, On the Origin, Kings, and Deeds of the People of the Franks and Germans, three large volumes, beginning the narrative from King Marcomer, who in the four hundred thirty-third year before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine, down to the thirty-eighth and ninth year of Maximilian Caesar.

To the same, On the Dukes and Bishops of the Franks of Wurzburg, one book.

A Chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, Saint James at Wurzburg, one book.

Steganography of Trithemius

On the transfer of the same man from Mainz into Saxony, one book.

To the same, The Life of Saint Maximinus the Bishop, one book.

To Johann, Count Palatine of Bavaria, Duke, and Count in Sponheim, residing at Simmern, On the Origin, Kings, and Dukes of the Bavarians, together with the Counts Palatine and the Counts of Sponheim, four books.

He has in hand a laborious work On Demons and Profane, Magical, and Superstitious Arts, which will have twelve books; in them he denounces, confounds, and overturns all the ravages of the diabolical arts.

Letters from Wurzburg, which he wrote in that abbey, four books.

He also wrote several other smaller works suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as:

A Course, a Rosary, a Missal, an Office, and various prayers, together with his own prose sequences on Saint Anne, grandmother of our Savior.
