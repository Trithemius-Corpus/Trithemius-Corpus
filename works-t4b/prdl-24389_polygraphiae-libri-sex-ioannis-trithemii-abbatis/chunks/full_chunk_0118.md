HOW WE MAY ALSO WRITE ANY SECRETS SAFELY WITH NUMBERS AND IN MANY OTHER VARIOUS WAYS.

These things are childish, I admit, since anyone, according to his own capacity and judgment, could devise many more attractive alphabets than we have recorded in this volume. Therefore we knowingly omit many things which, for the same reason, we judge to be childish, common, and of little utility. We fear that someday we shall be ashamed to have applied our mind to these trifles while more useful matters were left aside. But I pray that readers will pardon me, since my own will indeed drew me back from these childish studies, not to say studies at least partly vain; yet the insistence of certain friends compelled me to write them, friends whom I could not refuse without injury to the bond between us. Nevertheless, persuaded by their entreaty, I have also decided to join the following chapter to those already set forth, and to put numbers in place of letters. For the ancients devised various and manifold ways of writing by means of number-figures. Accordingly, persuaded by the prayers of friends, we have placed at the end of this sixth book a few methods out of many, by which a path lies open to the knowledge of others, even for those who do not know them. For if anyone takes delight in knowing these things, once they are known he will easily be able to ascend to higher things, and to vary the methods discovered by manifold alternation according to his own judgment. As a beginning for this matter, we have set down a table under an orchema, which, when the reading is varied in the form of a cross, will contain within itself ninety-six alphabets.
