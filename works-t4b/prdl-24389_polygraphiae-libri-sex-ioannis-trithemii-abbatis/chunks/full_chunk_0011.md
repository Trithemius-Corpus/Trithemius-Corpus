*A Small Index or Summary of the things contained in the sixth book*

*In* the sixth, which is the final book of our *Polygraphy*, after introducing a mental narration, we have set out in order the minute details of almost all the preceding signs and degrees, and, with arithmetical distributions, have drawn together the functions of the letters into the service of cabalistic operation. One thing lies hidden here in the bark, and another is found in the common operation of the signs. For just as the spirit in the blood gives life to the human body, so the vigor hidden in the art commends the operation. Therefore, for anyone to whom knowledge of this invention is necessary, let him open the eyes of his understanding, so that he may comprehend what the property of each operation is. For we desire all things to be open to good men in such a way that, if it were possible, they would never become manifest to the wicked. A cause compelled us to adapt the letters of the Latins to numbers, lest, if we were too obscure, the intention of our mind should be clear to no one. Yet this novelty, so that it would not be made public without reasonable cause, had to be hidden mystically under unfamiliar signs and marks of numbering. For the nocturnal winds are always stronger than the diurnal, and just as night intercepts day in its own order, so light shudders at darkness. What nature denies, no one dares successfully. We ask the reader not to despise this laborious work of ours before he understands it, but, so that he may be a judge without rashness, let him prudently remove the bark, so that he may come through to the hidden kernel. The work is good and natural, and can be turned to good use or bad, according as the desire of each person’s will is moved. The sword is a good instrument, and it too is turned to good use whenever it is carried for defense without injury to one’s neighbor. But if it is extended unjustly to the injury of anyone, a good thing is loosened into evil through abuse. Therefore, so that the good may have something by which they may prudently provide for their affairs, the *Polygraphy* is written by us; and so that an opportunity for wrongdoing may not be furnished to the depraved, the kernel is hidden in the bark under various enigmas. The man who is good does not abuse a good thing for evil; rather, if he can, he converts evil into good.

*What the causes and at the same time the uses of this work of ours, the Polygraphy, are.*

Indeed we undertook the labor of the present work, both rare and difficult, for no other reason than to provide for the usefulness of those in need. I say those in need not of food, not of drink, but of aid in preserving mysteries without danger. We judge, therefore, that it will be worth the effort if we briefly note the uses and advantages that can come to those who use these inventions of ours. For indeed, since we have no business in our personal affairs such that we need these teachings for concealing secrets, we have certainly labored for those whose command had to be obeyed. The first use is that, once the understanding of this work has been completed, it gives an easy method by which you will safely, secretly, and without any suspicion on the part of any observers make known the secret of your mind to an industrious person who knows the art, as widely as there may be need, over any distance of places governed by speech.

What lies hidden in these things, the work of *Steganography* draws out, which requires neither letters nor a messenger who knows the secrets he carries. It would be otherwise in matters that admit the light, according to the common custom of all our people. Do not take the matter otherwise than it is. The second use is that, by the service of this work, in the space of a short time you will teach a man ignorant of the Latin language, provided only that he knows how to read and write letters in his mother tongue, to write in the Latin language as you wish, ornamentedly or elegantly, and to conceal and hide all the secret mysteries of his will, and to announce them safely, secretly, and without suspicion to anyone he pleases who knows the art, as widely as he wishes. Again I warn you beforehand not to understand us as having promised otherwise than we promise.
