Third figure of the expansion of the straight table.

[a letter table follows]

There are also in this expanded figure of the straight table five alphabets distributed in order, as is seen. In the first of them, by the rule of substitution, m is taken for a; in the second, n; in the third, o; in the fourth, p; in the fifth, q. Their number can be enlarged in many ways, as was said above.

Continuation of the expansion.

Fourth figure of the expansion of the straight table.

[a letter table follows]

This is the fourth figure of the expansion of the table of the second substitution, and under five alphabets in straight order it comprises up to and including the twentieth. Translation in these is carried out just as in the others, for many purposes, which anyone who wishes may use very securely in secret matters.

Continuation of the straight table.

Fifth figure of the expansion of the straight table.

[a letter table follows]

This is the fifth and last figure of the expansion of the straight table, containing in itself three complete alphabets; and it completes in itself the twenty-third number. If you add the usual alphabet to it, they will rise according to the number of the twenty-four letters. The remaining two transfer the letter into the third, and are set down as an example.

Expansion of the reversed table:

First figure of the expansion of the reversed table.

[a letter table follows]

This is the first figure of the expansion of the reversed table. Since it proceeds in opposition to the straight table, it is rightly called irregular. For, by alternating the alphabets in order, it commits an error backward from the twelfth, neither less nor more. Since the rule produced this, it did not seem right to alter it.
