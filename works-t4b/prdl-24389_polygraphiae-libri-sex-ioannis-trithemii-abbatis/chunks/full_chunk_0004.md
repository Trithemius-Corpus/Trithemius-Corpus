To Laurentius, bishop of Wurzburg and duke of Eastern Franconia, he wrote an epitome or breviary of those same three volumes of histories, three books. To the same man, On the Dukes and Bishops of the Franks of Wurzburg, one book. On the miracles shown at the memorial of our Lady Saint Mary at Dettelbach, two books. On the miracles of the same Lady shown in the nettle-field at Heilbronn, six books. An Apology against Carolus Bovillus, who slandered what he did not understand, one book. On the Origin and Kings of the Franks, one book. A Chronicle of his own monastery, over which he now presides, Saint James at Wurzburg, one book. To Albert, of the margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, The Life of the divine Rabanus, archbishop, three books. On the translation of the same man from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To the same man, The Life of Saint Maximus, bishop, one book. To Johann, count palatine, duke of Bavaria, and count in Sponheim, resident at Simmern, On the Origin, Kings, and Dukes of the Bavarians, together with the Counts Palatine and the Counts of Sponheim, four books. He has in hand a laborious work On Demons and Profane, Maleficent, and Superstitious Arts, which will have twelve books; in these he casts out, confounds, and overturns all the tricks of the diabolical arts. Letters from Wurzburg, which he wrote in that abbey, four books.

He also wrote many other smaller works, suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as offices, a rosary, the office of the Mass, and various prayers, together with prose sequences on Saint Anne, grandmother of our Savior. He also wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on Saint Mary Magdalene; a rosary on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord; a rosary on Saint Peter the Apostle; a rosary on Saint John the Evangelist. At the request of Johann, archbishop of Trier, he wrote a sequence on Anne, which begins, “Jesus, King of the heavens,” to the melody “Ave praeclara”; another, to be sung on her feast, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day”; a third, for the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, beginning, “Let us exult in honor”; a fourth, on Saint Hildegard the virgin, which begins thus, “Praise be to you, Lord, King of eternal glory”; a fifth on Saint

Rupert, duke of the people of Bingen, which begins, “Let them rejoice on this day”; a sixth on Saint Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, which begins thus, “Let us sing, illustrious men”; a seventh on Saint Rabanus, archbishop; an eighth on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus, “Let us adore Jesus Christ; let us mourn him crucified”; a ninth on the compassion of Blessed Mary ever virgin, which begins thus, “Come, let us weep with pious mind, sharing the compassion of the blessed Virgin Mary,” to the melody, namely, “Ave praeclara”; a tenth on Saint Joachim, father of our Lady, which begins thus, “Let us adore the Trinity, let us praise together the Unity, of Father, Son, and Spirit”; an eleventh on the good angel proper to each person; a twelfth for the proper Mass of each priest; a thirteenth on Saint Maximus, bishop of Mainz; a fourteenth on Saint Mary the Virgin, Christ’s hostess; a fifteenth on Saint Mary Magdalene; a sixteenth on Saint Mary of Egypt; a seventeenth on Saint James the Greater, apostle; an eighteenth on Saint Andrew the Apostle; a nineteenth on Saint Benedict, abbot; a twentieth on All Saints. And for each of the named commemorations and saints he composed individual new offices of the Mass, conforming to the divine Scriptures.

He also wrote a little work of meditations and prayers on the Lord’s incarnation, nativity, life, passion, and death, suitable for devotion, two books. The Mirror of Heavenly Doctrine, of no small service to compunction of mind, one book, which begins thus, “The beginning of human salvation.” He also composed many most devout meditations and prayers on our Lord Jesus Christ, on his most holy mother the Virgin Mary, and on various saints, in beautiful variety, along with many other large works, which all devout posterity will see and admire. These, my dearest Nicolaus, are the volumes of our abbot Trithemius, which for nearly thirty years he has written continuously in night study, apart from those still unfinished and in hand, under the hammer, unknown to me. Farewell, from my house at Durlach, on the fifth day of the month of October, in the year of our salvation 1515. Johannes Durlachius.

From the letter of Johannes Durlachius, his disciple, to Nicolaus Hamerius of Emel.
