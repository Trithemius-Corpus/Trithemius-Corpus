What we promised at the beginning of the third book of our Polygraphia, for obtaining the shadow and likeness of a new gloss before those unskilled in it, we have fulfilled by way of example, with a sufficient saving of labor. We have entrusted the night, safely serving hidden matters, to be led by the shadows of paraphrases, and we have inserted the numbers in the order of four lines throughout, adapted for the use of those who wish to employ them. The places are degrees of minutiae, having three thousand one hundred fifty fixed positions, although, if you wish, there remain also infinitely many moments of the hours for concealing, in every nation and language of the world, whatever secret matter may occur. The circle, arising linearly from a point, whenever the night exceeds the image of light, you will be able to reduce as much as you wish and as much as will be needed to the path by which you have proceeded. If much of the night remains, reduce the dragon's tail to its original caudal point at the head. But the binary is made altogether certain from the marks and signs of unity. He who hungers much must labor much, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is there need of great labor to lead wherever you wish that which advances of its own accord. Here, therefore, we set the end to the third book of our Polygraphia, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell. From Peapoli, on the seventh day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ the Savior one thousand five hundred eight, in the eleventh indiction.
