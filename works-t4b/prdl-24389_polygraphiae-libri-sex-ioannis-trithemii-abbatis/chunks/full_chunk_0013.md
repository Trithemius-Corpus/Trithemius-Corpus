PREFACE TO THE EXPLANATION OF THE WHOLE WORK OF THE POLYGRAPHY OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF PEAPOLIS, THAT IS, WURZBURG, AT ST. JAMES.

No one who reads these things, as I trust, will judge me either proud or superfluous because I myself have commented on the Polygraphy recently composed by me, especially if he has been able to weigh exactly what reason induced us to speak obscurely. For whatever, if made public, can do harm had reasonably to be concealed in mystical speech. The subject that we treat in the Polygraphy is, for the benefit of the commonwealth among good men, very good, useful, and honorable. For this reason, persuaded by a rational cause, we wrote it, so that by its service we might make good men safe and secure from the investigation of the wicked when they communicate their secrets to one another. But lest an opportunity be given to the wicked and perverse, who very often misuse even good inventions, to deceive the simpler and those less cautious in judging matters, we handed down in it mystical, that is, hidden, methods of writing, and covered the truth lying hidden in the kernel with certain coverings of figures. Yet we do not in the least doubt that any industrious man, eager for these matters, will easily grasp everything if he applies even moderate persistence in searching it out. For the things I have handed down are not so difficult, nor wrapped in such great mysteries, that they cannot be fully penetrated by a studious man. Thus everywhere we have tempered the riddles of our words, so that they are easily accessible to learned and studious men, and nevertheless remain inaccessible to sluggish dwellers in darkness. But after it pleased me to offer the work of Polygraphy itself,

dedicated, to the most serene and most unconquered Caesar of the Romans, Maximilian, ever august Emperor of the world, I judged that it would be worthwhile to make it clear and open with brief and few interpretations, lest the mind of his sacred majesty, which is daily stretched by many affairs and cares, be drawn back from understanding it by the obscurity of the mysteries. For, unless my opinion deceives me, the work will be the more pleasing and useful to his majesty, the shorter and clearer it is. First, therefore, with the Lord’s aid, we shall briefly explain the meaning of our purpose, which is contained in the prologue wrapped in various enigmas; then, book by book through the Polygraphy, we shall concisely unfold, to the strength of our ability, the methods of working, rules, cautions, and distinctions, in order, that remain necessary. All these things, so that they may more quickly become accessible to those who desire to know them, we shall show one after another through examples, and we shall be as brief as the cause and reason permit.

THE KEY OF THE EXPLANATION OF THE POLYGRAPHY OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF PEAPOLIS.

Exposition on the Prologue.

Our whole intention in the entire work of the Polygraphy is concerned with this proposition: that we should hand down fixed methods of writing secretly, by which kings and princes, nobles and commoners, priests and laymen, learned and unlearned alike, may secretly, safely, and without any suspicion of a hidden secret, communicate to one another and to any friends, even those far distant from one another, by the service of letters, whatever conception of their minds they need to communicate, and as often as there is need.
