JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF THE MONASTERY OF SAINT JAMES THE GREATER, APOSTLE, at Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the order of Saint Benedict: the fourth book of the Polygraphia begins.

The order of reason required that the fourth book of our Polygraphia be made unlike the preceding books in quality, invention, and ease, so that those who wish to share in the secrets of our matters may attain this not by one way, but by various and diverse ways. We have extended the pica, comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that night, always joined by the minute to the monad, may in any order be recognized in the light of day. Here no disagreement is found between unity and the binary; nor will one who wishes to perform the art need to have the instrument of the art at hand, since what succeeds the first without an intermediary shows the hidden mystery. Hence a prudent man, understanding and experienced in the science, will satisfy the operation at every time and place. The binary receives from unity, whence it produces the ternary. The recognition of this cryptology is easy at first sight, after the moon has been illuminated by the sun, even by a single point. And yet it may happen that the knower becomes skilled without the guiding hand of a teacher. I think, however, that this is to be understood only in this book, and in none of the preceding ones. Concerning those that follow, judgment will be given in its proper place. Among all the cabalistic methods of secrets that we have given up to the present, the procedure of this fourth book, easier than the rest, can be extended to infinity. Therefore it is enough that I have handed down to those desiring fuller things both the form and the reason of the example.

The light of understanding easily penetrates all these things. I wrote at Peapolis, on the ninth day of March, in the year of the Lord 1508.
