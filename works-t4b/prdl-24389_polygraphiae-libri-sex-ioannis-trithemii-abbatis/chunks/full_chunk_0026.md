Then, with the membrane placed underneath the holes, you will write that sentence, and likewise on another and another; finally fill the spaces of the first membrane, completing and deleting sentences until the sense is coherent. You will complete this second sentence on the second membrane, so that the words and sentences seem to cohere; on the third also you will fit it in such a way that, with the first letters left unharmed, the whole sentence, the number and size of the words, stand firm and retain a certain neatness. When these things have been done, place the pattern which you have cut over an equal membrane, and at the edges of the holes mark very fine points as far as the letters which you wish to write extend. Then take the third membrane, and write the letter with continuous words, in a proper order of spaces and also of the size of the letters, so that the first sentence and its words are contained within the boundaries of the points. Thus no suspicion of fraud then remains; the other person, when he receives it and places the pattern over it, at once understands the thought of your mind. Nor, although the labor is not light, can anything equal to this be found for communicating one's intention to friends in dangerous times.

Marcus Tullius.) The opinion is that Cicero devised single and special characters for individual Latin words, with which, if necessity demanded, he wrote a speech, however long, both with very few signs and in the greatest secrecy. And in this way, they report, the speech of Cato, that the conspirators should be put to death, was preserved and committed to writing, when Cicero, as consul, appointed very swift writers to record speeches with notes of this kind. That the ancients also followed a certain similar method for writing words quickly and compendiously, so that they often signified a word by a single letter, is attested by Valerius Probus, Petrus Crinitus, Angelus Decembrius, and also the very ancient monuments collected with the greatest diligence by Petrus Appianus and Bartholomaeus Amantius. But this technique of writing will be treated more carefully below.

I find these things in Bede, On the Reckoning of Times. When you say one, bending the little finger on the left hand, you will set it against the middle joint of the palm. When you say two, you will place the second finger from the little finger, bent, in the same place. When you say three, you will similarly bend down the third. When you say four, you will likewise raise the little finger. When you say five, you will similarly raise the second from the little finger. When you say six, you will nevertheless raise the third as well, with only the middle finger, which is called the medicinal finger, fixed in the middle of the palm. When you say seven, with the rest raised, you will place only the little finger over the root of the palm. Next to it, when you say eight, place the ring finger. When you say nine, set the middle finger opposite it. When you say ten, you will fix the nail of the index finger in the middle joint of the thumb. When you say twenty, you will put the tip of the thumb between the middle joints of the index and middle fingers. When you say thirty, you will join the nails of the index finger and thumb in a gentle embrace. When you say forty, you will draw the inner parts of the thumb over the side or back of the index finger, with only those two held upright. When you say fifty, you will bend the thumb, curved like the Greek letter called gamma, toward the palm at the outer joint. When you say sixty, with the thumb curved as above, you will carefully encircle it from the front with the index finger bent around it. When you say seventy, you will fill the index finger, bent around as above, with the thumb stretched lengthwise, with only its nail raised across the middle joint of the index finger. When you say eighty, you will make the index finger bent around as above with the thumb held lengthwise on the left hand; two hundred on the right, in the same way as ten on the left, with it fixed in. But one hundred on the right is made in the same way as ten on the left. Likewise, one thousand on the right is as one on the left. Two thousand on the right, as two on the left. Three thousand on the right, with the fingers only raised toward the neck. When you say twenty thousand, you will place the same hand, widely spread out, upon the chest. When you say thirty thousand, with the same hand prone but upright, you will put the thumb against the cartilage of the middle of the chest.
