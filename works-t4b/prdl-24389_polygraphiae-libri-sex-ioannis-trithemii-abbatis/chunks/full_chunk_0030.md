But in what way it might be possible for all things to be transferred to the use of signs is not part of the present undertaking, but belongs to steganography. The very learned man Johannes Trithemius, in a certain letter to Bostius, recounts the contents of that work in these words, etc.

“I now have in hand a great work which, if ever it is published, which may it not be, the whole world will marvel at; the title of its first book is Steganographia. There will be four books, each divided into at least one hundred chapters. I began this work at the urging of a certain great prince, whom there is no need to name. You ask what this work teaches? It teaches very great things, astonishing and incredible to all who do not know them, things that have never been heard of since the beginning of the world. For the first book contains more than one hundred ways of writing secretly whatever you wish, without any suspicion, without transposition of letters, without any fear, so that there is no person in the world who by natural ingenuity could know or even suspect what is contained in my letters, except one who knows the art from me or from someone whom I have taught. The words are plain and familiar, lacking all suspicion; but no one will ever be able to perceive my intention without the art, however learned he may be, and it is a truly astonishing thing. The second book will contain things much more marvelous, namely by means of it, in this art, I can make the conception of my mind known to one who knows my art,

at any distance whatever, at a hundred miles or more, safely, without words, without writings, without signs, through any messenger whatever. If he is captured on the way, even if questioned under the harshest tortures, he can disclose nothing about my message, because he knows absolutely nothing about it. Whatever may happen, my message will always remain hidden, nor could all the people of the whole world, if they were gathered together, track it down by natural power. I can also do this without a messenger whenever I wish. I can also indicate my will to one sitting in prison who knows the art, even when I am far away, however much he is guarded, even if he were sitting three miles beneath the earth. And all these things I can do most broadly and universally, whenever and as often as I wish, naturally, without any superstition or aid of any spirits whatever. They are marvelous things, I admit; but hear things more marvelous.

“The third book teaches an art by which I can teach an uneducated man who knows only his mother tongue, and who has never known a word of the Latin language, within two hours to write, read, and understand Latin rather elegantly and eloquently, as much as he wishes, so that whoever sees his letters will praise the words and understand that they are composed in Latin. The fourth book contains many astonishing experiments, but purely natural ones. That is, I can indicate my mind to one who knows the art whenever I wish, even while speaking, preaching, playing the organ, or singing, without impediment to any other action, so that in preaching good and holy things I may indicate, not by words, not by signs, to anyone who knows the art, most broadly whatever I wish, even with eyes closed. And the fourth book will contain many other secrets, which are not to be brought before the public. All who hear these things marvel at them; among them are many noble and very learned men, who judge that these things are either impossible or wholly supernatural. To them, and to you, I reply what I have replied to many: that many things are naturally possible which, to those ignorant of the powers of nature, seem impossible or even supernatural. And behold, I speak before my God, who knows all things, that the marvelous things I have set forth are much more excellent, deeper, and greater than I can write or you can believe; and yet all are purely natural, without any deception, without superstition, without magical art, without invocation or service of any spirits whatever. I have said these things so that, if perhaps at some time a rumor should reach you that I either know or can do marvelous things, you would not, I say, think me a magician, but a philosopher.
