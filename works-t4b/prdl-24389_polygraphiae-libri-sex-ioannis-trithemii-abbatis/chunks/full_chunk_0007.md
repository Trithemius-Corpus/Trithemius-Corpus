At the urging of the aforementioned prince and lord elector, Philip, Count Palatine and Duke of Bavaria, I, Johannes Trithemius, now abbot of the monastery of Saint James at Würzburg, but then of Saint Martin the bishop at Sponheim in the diocese of Mainz, began to write eight books of mysteries in steganography. While I was already continuing my night work on the third book of the same work, something happened which, if I can do so briefly, I am about to relate.

In the year of the Lord’s nativity 1499, in the second Roman indiction, Arnold Bostius, a Carmelite of the town of Ghent, asked me by letter what new work I was bringing forth and with what studies I was occupied. On the day after Palm Sunday, through merchants, I wrote back to him as to a friend, saying that I had under the hammer a new and marvelous work of steganography, and I disclosed in order what was contained in each of the books. But before my letter had reached Ghent, Bostius, during the days of Easter, passed from life to Christ. The prior of that place received the letter, opened it, read it, and, astonished, as I think, by the novelty of the promises, shared it, at the request of many others, to be read and copied. From this it happened that my letter itself, within a short time published through almost all Germany and Gaul and rather often copied by many people, turned even many very learned men to amazement and wonder, to such a degree that they were uncertain what they ought truly to think about me.

Among these there were some who steadfastly declared that my promises in the said letter were frivolous, impossible, and false, and had been shamelessly devised by me for the sake of empty glory. Others, however, said: “This abbot Trithemius promises great and marvelous things; if he can do them, he can do them only by the ministry of demons, since they seem to far exceed the bounds of nature. But if he cannot, who will doubt that he is a liar and rightly to be refuted by all good and learned men?” But let both charges be far from me, since I promised natural things.
