But since none of these things induced me to write, it seemed enough to have given those who desire it an opportunity and material to add more polished things to my discoveries, and to adorn art with art.

INDICATION OF THE THINGS THAT ARE CONTAINED IN THE THIRD BOOK OF THE POLYGRAPHIA.

In the third book of the Polygraphia, having crossed by rational counsel the boundaries of our prescribed nation, we are led to foreign things; and, though placed essentially in the midst of the Franks, we wander farther abroad through the images and likenesses of barbarians. For with almost four thousand mythological paraphrases set in order by art, we show an exceedingly easy way for artificial unity, through which it will securely communicate symbolic notions to its binary at all times. Here the binary is harder than unity; and unless our philologist has become sharp-sighted through prudence, he will make the binary contrary to the monad. For only the ternary, which proceeds from unity and the binary, as a palindrome connects each within itself. But if the usefulness of necessary reasoning is extended beyond the limits of the proposed matter, then the paraphrases are to be counted by minutes from the beginning of the day as often as needed, until the minutes establish the degree in which night is equal to day. The face of this image knows perpetual silence, and does not fear the deceptions of the Bacuceans. It is extended in whatever abundance you wish, and is varied without number.

SMALL INDEX, OR PINAX, OF THE THINGS THAT ARE TREATED IN THE FOURTH BOOK.

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia we place much trust in the binary; after all obstacles have been removed, we have brought it back to unity at first glance, without the labor of difficulty. Hence in this place we use a circle in great matters, a line or point in the smallest. Nor does it matter if night surpasses day, or if light does not agree with darkness, because a circle receives no end, and fullness does not admit emptiness. For they are contraries, and whenever they succeed one another, one is corrupted by the other.

A singular mystery useful to the binary is handed down in this fourth book of the Polygraphia, when the airy wind itself, the composer of the basis, is never designated in anything other than the binary. But in that part the lightness of owls is always suspect to us in difficult matters. The construction of this matter makes paraphrases out of infinite atoms.

INDEX, OR PINAX, OF THE THINGS THAT ARE TREATED IN THE FIFTH BOOK.

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have inscribed in two tables, in order, the letters of the Latins, which we Germans also use; one of these is called the straight table, and the other is named the reversed table. Under these we have placed windy orchemata, so that what we seek with labor in foreign things, we may see without difficulty in our own. For besides those things which have been set down by us openly, many more secrets still lie hidden in the inner parts; through understanding these, a path to greater things is opened for the operator, though they are by no means set before everyone. We have also subjected letters to numbers, by which hidden things may be made manifest according to our will. Hence, through the knowing operation of this book, all messages are safely handed over to the Cabalists, although the mysteries are not free from suspicion of the Bacuceans. For every form of metathesis of signs generates suspicion in the one who looks upon it, although it brings no harm to the owls. But you who have attained the understanding of this work will easily penetrate every metathesis of letters among our people and among the Latins.
