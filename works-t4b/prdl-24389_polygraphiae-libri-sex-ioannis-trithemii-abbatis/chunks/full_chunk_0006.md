Or Namur: Germany had no greater man. To each prefect of the kingdom he assigned, by his art, individual owls. The Northmen, having gone out from the territories of Gothia, when they once cruelly laid waste Gaul, summoned owls from Greece and prepared a safe hiding place for their secrets, which today is known to few.

Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the Northmen’s deed, described many inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made an obscure path clear for mysteries. He was the first to commend artrology to the memory of posterity, and he invented dactylogy. There were some who distinguished small particulars by visible accidents; by their prudence night was turned into day, and without the service of owls light shone in darkness. Concerning these things, the illustrious count palatine, prince-elector, once an eager inquirer into our Steganography, received from us a manifest proof of the method.

Others, multiplying small particulars by regulated division, after the manner of barbarians, overturned the figures of nature, making secret in darkness the dances of shadows coming to birth; and where the secret nature of counsel required it, they supplied cabala to the absent through diapyric devices.

In these matters there is great labor and a long exercise of the mind over many years, in which no one suddenly becomes expert for use. Necessity sustains everything. There were also some whom celestial harmony taught to compose foreign words; for those who understood them their use was twofold, since one suspended the sense, the other raised it into an ethereal mind. God can do all things. Many tried to seek light in shadow without light, but they could not at all, because the fire lies buried in the ashes. Some practiced painters of masks distinguished one wind into two exactly: one of them, a misophos, always lurks in darkness; the other, a lover of light, a philosophos, hates darkness, loves light, and guards the secret with an owl. Again, as Bede bears witness, there were some whose purpose, advancing to broader things, bound what is invisible to the eyes with visible things, and compelled many plants, and no less stones and shrubs, which lack voices, to speak with a human tongue.

Why should I bring in brute animals, when all things obey the art? This secret is rightly preferred to all inventions up to now; through it the hidden things of mysteries receive strength

and power. Yet reason commands that what is great be hidden under what is small, and that pearls by no means be entrusted to swine. Nothing is brought back from nothing. The known multitude of visible things is great; but invisible things remain remote from us. If the latter are prudently fitted to the former, the enlightened mind of a human being will know and behold marvelous things. But to treat more fully of these matters is not part of the present purpose; since they are greater and weightier, they require another work and another time.

Matthias, the most powerful king of Hungary and a hammer of the Turks harder than adamant, who in our age fought many victorious battles against the enemies of the cross of Christ, discovered many things by marvelous talent and art. He adorned owls with periphrastic devices, held his mysteries secret with manifold variety, and by manifest industry destroyed the counsels of his enemies. He preferred wisdom to gold; therefore he always triumphed as a glorious victor.

In a wise man there is a sound mind in a well-governed body; driven upward by desire for a higher understanding, when through the likeness of the binary you return from the ternary into unity, thereafter you will easily rise to the denary. Pythagoras of Samos, that philosopher, admonished his disciples not to forget the quaternary, in which the first perfection of numbering is comprised. For thus we count: one, two, three, four. Stay your mind; for you have ten. The denary is the head of perfection. What are eleven, except ten and one? What is twenty? Twice ten. What is forty? Four times ten. You have a hundred, and you have by no means departed from the quaternary. And what is a hundred, except ten times ten? Deep mysteries lie hidden in these things, and they are manifest to few.

For love of brevity we shall pass over many in silence, who, aiding nature by art, applied prudence to secrets, and what seemed impossible to many who did not know, brought by shrewd study into easy use. A cultivated mind can do great things, and whoever adheres to God becomes, in understanding, higher than the world. Many are the mysteries of kings, and their counsels are full of cares; in them trust and fidelity are required. Rare fidelity harms the one who believes, and doubt, even when present, often admits it. Therefore it would be worth the effort for all princes to be philosophers, and endowed with singular wisdom, just, gentle, benevolent, and distinguished for munificence, so that they themselves would wish no harm to others
