TABLE, OR
INDEX OF THE LUCUBRATIONS
OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, FIRST ABBOT OF SPANHEIM
and afterward of Saint James of Würzburg.

Johannes Trithemius, abbot, was born in the village of Trittenheim, in the diocese of Trier, on the banks of the river Moselle, on the first day of the month of February, at the eleventh hour and the thirtieth minute after midday, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1462, in the tenth indiction of the Romans: his father was Johannes de Heidenberg, and his mother was called Elisabeth de Longuico. This was the same year, namely, in which the city of Mainz, in the dissension of two pontiffs, was captured, lost its liberty, and found servitude, many of its citizens having been driven into exile; in the fourth current year of Pope Pius, the second of that name, and the twenty-second of the emperor Frederick III. In the year of the Lord 1482, however, in the monastery of Spanheim in the diocese of Mainz, on the first day of February, the very day on which he had once been born, he laid aside secular dress and there took up the monastic habit under the rule of the divine father Benedict, according to the observance newly introduced. In the following year, which was the eighty-third above the four-hundredth and the thousandth, when Abbot Johannes de Colenhusen had been transferred by canonical postulation to Seligenstadt, Johannes Trithemius, by the common election of the brothers, was promoted and ordained abbot of the aforementioned monastery of Spanheim on the fourth day before the Kalends of July, and presided for twenty-three years, one month, and nineteen days. When these had passed, not without many labors, provoked by the rashness of certain rivals, he accepted the abbey of Saint James of Würzburg, which was being offered to him, and of his own will resigned Spanheim, full of useless cares. In both places he wrote many little works, from which he recently recorded for me in his own hand, on a sheet, the following.

Since you, as a friend, ask me for their titles, I gladly comply with your wishes, so that from consideration of such great labors by our common friend and teacher you may hold his memory more closely. First, then, I shall record the titles of those lucubrations which he laboriously composed while he was abbot at Spanheim. First he wrote the constitutions of his order which were made in the provincial chapters by command of the presidents: one book. On the Visitation of Monks: one book. On the Manner of Holding a Provincial Chapter: one book. On the Rule of Saint Benedict: two books. Homilies and Exhortatory Sermons to His Monks: two books, which he dedicated to his predecessor. On the Temptations of Cloistered Persons and Their Remedies: two books. To Nicolaus, Priest of Mernica, on the Priestly Life: one book. Against the Private Property of Monks: one book. A Penthic Work, that is, a mournful work On the Ruin of His Order: one book. On the Illustrious Men of His Order: four books. Praises of the Order of the Carmelite Brothers: two books. To Gerlacus, Abbot of Deutz, Praises of Manual Scribes: one book. To Johannes Dalburgius, Bishop of Worms, On Ecclesiastical Writers: one book. On the Praises of Saint Anne, Grandmother of the Lord: one book. Chronicle or Annals of the Monastery of Hirsau: two large volumes. Chronicle of the Succession of the Dukes of Bavaria and the Counts Palatine, to Philip, Count Palatine and Elector: one book. Life of Saint Irmina, nun and first abbess in Oeren at Trier: one book. On the Misery of Cloistered Prelates: two books. Praises of Saint Andrew the Apostle: one book. Praises of the Monastic Life: one book. Praises of True Penitence: one book.
