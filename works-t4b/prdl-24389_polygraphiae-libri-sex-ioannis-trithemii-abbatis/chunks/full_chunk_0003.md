And from the Greek language he translated into Latin Maximus the abbot, On the Incarnation of the Lord; also the anathemas of Saint Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria, promulgated against heretics in the third synod of Ephesus; and certain letters of that same synod. He also wrote for the edification of monks a notable work, which he entitled On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Men, three books. He also wrote certain other smaller works, whose titles he did not indicate to me.

These, most loving brother Nicolaus, are the Trithemian works which, during the twenty-three years in which he was abbot, he composed at Sponheim for the honor of God, amid cares and the continual management of household affairs, reading or writing day and night whenever he was able to steal some leisure for himself. During the years in which he presided he brought into the Sponheim library a very great treasure of books in every field, not only printed by the art of printing but also manuscripts. For when I was with him for the space of one year, to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, I counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eight hundred had been suitably written by pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his own mouth that he had spent more than two thousand florins on books, which he had not taken from the substance of the monastery, which was always very meager, but since he was known to and beloved by many princes,

he spent, for the most part, on books the gifts with which he was honored by them. For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books above all those whom I know living today in the flesh. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot at Sponheim, has alienated many volumes; whether this is true, I certainly do not at all know.

In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had broken out between the kingdom and Philip, count palatine, Trithemius, from fear of the enemy, withdrew with his library and the monastery’s goods to Kreuznach, the nearest town, and I likewise returned to my own affairs. But in October of the year 1506, Trithemius himself, stirred by the injury done by certain men, accepted the abbacy of Saint James at Würzburg and resigned that of Sponheim. There, in quiet, he is writing various works, whose titles, at my request, he has lately sent on to me.

To Maximilian Caesar he published a marvelous work, which he entitled Polygraphy, six books. To the same, the Key of the same Polygraphy, one book. Answers to Eight Questions of the same Caesar, one book. To the same, On the Seven Intelligences Moving the Spheres, one book. To Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg and prince elector, two books of various questions, orations, and propositions. To the same, On Prognostications Lawful and Not Lawful for a Christian, two books. To the same he wrote that marvelous and laborious work of Steganography, eight books, which that rash Bovillus, not understanding it, censured most ignorantly and most falsely. To the same prince, a book of letters, one book. To the same, Against Malefactors and All Arts and Superstitions Prohibited by the Church, five books. To the same, On the Causes and Cure of the Falling Sickness and of Maleficences, three books. To Jacob Trithemius his brother, An Itinerary of His Life, two books. To Burkhard von Horneck, physician, On the Peculium of Nuns, one book.
