NOW FOLLOW THE RULES or cautions to be observed in the operation of this book.

I. The first rule or caution advises you that you should write down, as carefully as you can, on a small sheet the secret of your mind that you wish to communicate to an absent friend who knows the art and has the book of Polygraphy, whether in the Latin language or in German, or in any other language you wish that can be expressed in Roman letters. Then place the sheet so written before you, so that for each single letter of the secret you may be able to fit a corresponding word. For, as we have shown in this practice of writing, a whole word is taken. We gave an example a little earlier in what precedes.

II. The second caution instructs you, as has been said, not to take more than one word at a time from any alphabet in a single narrative of public Latin speech; otherwise you would both confuse the open meaning and wholly draw away the friend to whom you had written from understanding the mystery.

III. Third, one must take the greatest care not to skip over any alphabet through negligence without taking from it one word suited to the secret. Likewise one must provide that, whenever one or more words occur outside the sequence of the alphabet, you take either the whole upper line or the lower one, and not both.

IIII. Fourth, care must be taken that whenever the discourse of the open speech has been brought to a complete sentence, the next sentence should take its beginning from a larger, thickened letter, according to the form of the preceding example, in which, according to the sense of the Latin discourse, we have taken large letters at the beginning and small letters in the rest, in the common manner. When the secret of your mind is lengthy, so that it exceeds the narrative of the open speech, if much remains, begin again from the beginning; if little remains, let the discourse be recalled at will from any place whatever.
