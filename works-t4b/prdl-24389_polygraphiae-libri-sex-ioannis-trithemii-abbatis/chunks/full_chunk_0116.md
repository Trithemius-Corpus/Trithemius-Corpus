This Hichus, a Frank, came with Marcomer from the borders of Scythia to the mouths of the Rhine in Germany, and he is said to have invented these characters as a supplement to the German language; they also seem to agree with it well enough in many respects.

Pharamundus also, king of the Franks in Germany, the forty-third after Marcomer, who had led the people in, died in the year of the Lord's nativity 426. When he had sent his scouts through Gaul for the purpose of invasion, he ordered them to write repeatedly in such characters as these:

From Latin letters, too, many alphabets are made, when they are either reduced to number according to the model that we handed down in the preceding fifth book, or, otherwise, in imitation of the Norsemen, they provide a very secure means, with Greek letters also among our own, of announcing whatever we wish. Let us next set the letters themselves, changed in an unusual way, according to the number and order of our ordinary alphabet, and we shall have a secret and mystical manner of writing, quite beautiful and elegant, confirmed by usage.
