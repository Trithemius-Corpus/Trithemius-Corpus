If you ask whence I have these things, which no one else knows, listen. I learned these things not from a man nor through a man, but through a revelation of I know not whom. For when in the present year I was at one time considering what new thing I might be able to discover that would be hidden from everyone, I began to consider whether I could devise those wonderful things I have described. After long imagining, when I had utterly despaired of it as an impossible matter, I put myself to sleep that night, laughing to myself at my own folly, since I had attempted to seek impossible things. That same night someone stood by me, saying, “The things you have thought are not vain, O Trithemius, although they are impossible to you, and although neither you nor anyone with you will be able to discover them.” And I said to him: “If, then, they are possible, tell me, I beg you, how they may be done.” And, opening his mouth, he taught me about each of them in order, showing how the things over which I had thought in vain for many days could easily be done. Behold, before God, since I do not lie but speak the truth, I have not yet taught these things to anyone, although many, promising great rewards, have often asked, except for one prince to whom I am writing, whom by a clear proof I have made more certain of the possibility of the art. Nor is it fitting for others to know these things than princes; otherwise many evils would be done through them: betrayals, deceptions, fornications, and whatever else evil men might wish. But those who use the art well could accomplish many good things for the republic through it. Moreover, I can teach all these things in every language of the whole world, even one I have never heard. These things, Bostius, I have wished to tell you for this reason, lest you think me idle.”

Thus far the words of Trithemius. Finally, there are other enormous advantages in these books of the Polygraphia, which we shall discuss in our books On the Method of Teaching and Learning; for however these things may appear, they have more in reserve than they promise on the surface. Concerning the Steganographia, in whose first books hidden matters lie concealed, with God’s will we shall treat separately.
