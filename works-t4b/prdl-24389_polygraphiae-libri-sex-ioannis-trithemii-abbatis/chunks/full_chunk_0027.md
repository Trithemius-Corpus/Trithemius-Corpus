You will place [the thumb] on the navel. When you say sixty thousand, with the same hand turned downward you will grasp the upper thigh from above. When you say seventy thousand, you will place the same hand upon the thigh. When you say eighty thousand, you will place the same hand, turned downward, upon the thigh. When you say ninety thousand, with the same hand you will take hold of the loins, with the thumb turned toward the groin. But from one hundred and two hundred thousand up to nine hundred thousand, you will complete them in the same order on the right side of the body. And when you say ten times one hundred thousand, you will intertwine both hands with the fingers interlaced. And so much for expressing numbers by the gesture of the fingers.

To these the same author soon adds: A certain manual speech can also be practiced from the very computation which I have set out, both for exercising the wit and for the sake of playing a game. By it, when the letters are expressed one by one, a person may convey to another who also knows this device, even if he is positioned far away, words composed of those same letters, so that they may be read and understood; or he may signify necessary things more secretly by these visible indications; or he may deceive the unskilled as if by divination. The order of this game or speech is as follows. When you wish to indicate the first letter of the alphabet, hold one with the hand; for the second, two; for the third, three; and so the rest in order. For example, if you wish to warn a friend placed among ambushers to act cautiously, show three and one, and twenty, and ten and nine, and five fingers; for the letters of this order signify, “act cautiously.”

Thus far the example of dactylogy according to Bede, which is also varied in many other ways. For suppose, as has also been said before, that the mark of unity signifies the letter a, and so on in order. Let one be placed on the thumb of the left hand, two on the index finger, three on the middle finger, four on the ring finger, or the third from the thumb, and five on the little finger. Likewise, on the right hand let two be understood on the thumb, three on the index finger, four on the middle finger, five on the third from the thumb, and six on the little finger. With these things so established, let us observe which numbers arise from the addition, multiplication, or division of which numbers among themselves. For example, twelve arises from the addition of one to eleven, two to ten, three to nine, four to eight, six to six, and seven to five. In multiplication, however, twelve is produced from the leading of four into three, or of two into six. In

division we have twelve if we divide twenty-four by two. Moreover, when the fingers alone are raised, this signifies addition; but when we touch one with another, multiplication. But if we bend one and raise the other, division must be understood. Therefore, wishing to show the letter m, which occupies the twelfth place, I indicate twelve either by addition, or by multiplication, or by division. First, with the two little fingers of both hands raised together with the thumb of the left hand, for the thumb signifies one, and one of the other fingers designates five, the other six; but five, six, and one make twelve, which number shows the letter m.

By multiplication it is done in this way: let us touch the third finger from the thumb of the left hand with the index finger of the right hand, since three multiplied by four makes twelve; or let us touch the little finger of the right hand with the index finger of the left, since likewise twice six yields twelve. It could also be done in another way, namely by the contact of the index finger of the right hand and the middle finger of the left, which show six. These, again, by the contact of both thumbs, or of the index finger of the right hand and the thumb of the left, signify multiplication of the former number by two; but twice six makes twelve.

By division, thus: first we shall indicate twenty-four, the division of which number by two gives twelve. Division by two is represented by the gesture of which I spoke above, namely if two thumbs are shown, one raised and the other bent; likewise the thumb and little finger of the left hand, and the index finger of the right hand bent. For they signify the division of six by three, that is, two, by which, when twenty-four is divided, they show twelve. So much for dactylogy, or the speech of the fingers, which can also be handled otherwise in countless ways, by assigning vowels, consonants, semivowels, double letters, and liquids to fixed places, and by treating such matters as each person shall see fit. Although these things may appear ridiculous, nevertheless they have very great use if they are transferred by a certain analogy to other matters. Moreover, there are some things of which the author makes mention partly in the exposition of this passage and partly near the end; but since they pertain to the treatment of steganography, let them be deferred for so long, until that point has been reached.
