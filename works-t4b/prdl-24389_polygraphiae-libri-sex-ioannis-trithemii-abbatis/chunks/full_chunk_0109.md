In the present orchema a multiple alphabet is handed down by transposition and leap, both according to the rule of order and according to the position of the letters. All these things will suffice as an example for those who cannot be content with the examples already set down, so that they may have a method and form for discovering the remaining ones, since they can be varied without number.

JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS,
ABBOT PEPOLITANUS,
greets the reader.

Since all the letters of the Greeks and the Hebrews are numerical, I have often wondered that the curious Latin learning of the ancients did not also make its own letters serve the reckoning of numbers. The Hebrews, by doubling certain letters, established twenty-eight letter-signs, sufficient for every number. The Greeks, however, relieved this lack of letters by adding four characters, with similar foresight. Since we, therefore, have twenty-four letters, why do we not also make ours numerical, since we can likewise find four doubled forms? Let us give four as an example, and there will be enough for every number: m and final ij; likewise r and final t; likewise long s and final or closed s; likewise closed v and open u. When we join these to the twenty-four usual letters, we have twenty-eight characters, sufficient for all numbers. Nor will it be a matter of great labor to grasp this method of numbering for those who already know the letters, if they have once committed it to memory. For this invention of ours will be useful to many, since in a few letters it comprises many thousands in number, all of which can be discerned and understood at first glance, without any calculation, more clearly than daylight. Many people, especially those less experienced in the art of calculation, think that this cannot be done so easily by means of algorismic figures, particularly in large numbers. For as soon as you have looked with your eye at a number, however large, you will be able both to understand it fully and to express it without circumlocution or division of speech. So that this may be done easily, it is fitting that we set down the letters themselves reduced to number. Written on 16 March, in the year 1508.
