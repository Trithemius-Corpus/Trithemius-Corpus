...despisers of him and of his commands, or those who disturbed the Christian commonwealth by notorious crimes, or recalled or solicited the people to paganism, should without delay, by imperial and royal authority and without penalty, be hanged with a noose, or otherwise killed as they could be. And so that this institution might remain perpetually unshaken, he gave those same men power to appoint others also, suitable under certain conditions, who, enjoying the aforesaid authority, might exercise without penalty the office of inquiry and of death against the guilty. Finally, he prescribed for them secret laws, hidden signs, and at the same time the form of an oath, by which they might proceed justly in judging and punishing, and, known to one another, remain hidden from others, and preserve forever in greater secrecy the necessary judgment in the Saxon land. They also used certain alphabets among themselves for a time, though these at last fell entirely out of use. Yet the office of this kind of inquisition remains down to the present time; its ministers are commonly called Feimeri.

Although these characters may not appear sufficiently beautiful and elegant, nevertheless it pleased me to join them to the new inventions of my own written above, for the honor and reverence of antiquity, since there is no one, even a little industrious, who cannot devise more beautiful ones for himself at his own discretion. Yet continued practice in writing will be able, with easy compensation, to correct this misshapen crookedness, and frequent use will make it pleasing by habit.

If anyone wishes to fashion a new alphabet for himself at his own discretion, let him know that two things above all must be observed, though I do not always find them preserved in the preceding examples. The first is that he should form characters that are easy to draw, and the simpler they are, the better they will be. The second is that he should arrange the individual letters so that, when joined into words, they present a beautiful and elegant appearance. I add a third thing, not promised: if you want the writing to remain secret, make some letters of the alphabet double after the manner of the Hebrews.
