The Sixth

--- Page 556 ---

With God’s mercy we have brought the sixth and last book of our Polygraphy to its end. In it, as we conclude our discourse with the wandering stars, we give the reader warning on certain points. First, whenever he uses the alphabet of any sign or planet, he should prudently add the character or mark of the same, for a double advantage. For by this means excessive labor will be removed from the friendly reader, and the mystery will remain secret, since anyone ignorant of the art will think that a table written in numbers and marked beforehand with the characters of signs or planets belongs to the reckoning of astronomy, and will suspect no hidden mystery at all. For this reason we have taken up the official matter for the letters not from one, but from a distance, so that we might give no one a mark of suspicion. Nevertheless prudence must be used by writers, since an astute reader touched by suspicion of something hidden, if he detects the function of one number, will easily calculate the whole alphabet.

Second, remember, whoever desires to be a shrewd practitioner of this matter, that whenever a letter is doubled in any word, as often happens, it should be written only once and marked above with a point. For example, I wish to write Ioannes from the alphabet of Aries: I do not duplicate 18, which is n, but write thus: 14. 16. 7. 18. 10. 23.; or abbas: 6. 7. 6. 23. Ioannes, abbas, so that every mark of suspicion may be removed. In similar cases one must proceed with similar caution, because unless the operator is endowed with diligence and knows how to discover greater things from small ones, he will not be fit for these least matters of ours to be disclosed to him. Not all things are to be communicated indiscriminately to everyone, especially those whose use can be turned to good and to evil.

Moved reasonably by that consideration, we have openly handed down many things in these six books of Polygraphy to those wishing to know, but we have allowed far more secret things to lie hidden beneath what is manifest. Finally, we have written a key that explains the whole work, showing how it is to be understood in each metaphor; yet, by the counsel of certain persons, we have ordered it to remain hidden at home, so that studious persons may not lack material for exercising their memory, and so that the rejected may be deprived of the occasion for knowing things they are not willing to use well. In every passage many mysteries lie hidden, and the greater the things you discover from these, the greater still are those that remain to be discovered. A path is prepared for beginners, on which, for those setting out,

it will not be difficult in the course of time to discover many things, both small and very great. Here a man who did not know how will learn to write Latin, and prudently to wrap all the mysteries of his will in a foreign speech. But in order that the unlearned man may be able not only to understand his own mysteries that he has wrapped up, but also plain speech, of which until now he has always been ignorant, the matter is not one of great labor, if the paraphrast has fully grasped our intention in this volume. For beneath the bark lie the kernels of our Steganography, buried for a just reason, lest they be open to unworthy babblers. In short, it was worthwhile that one work should not prejudice the other, but that its own right should be distributed to each inviolate.

We, wearied after a long voyage, here cast anchor in the sea under the name of JESUS CHRIST, Savior of the faithful, on the twenty-first day of the month of March, in the year of the Lord’s nativity 1508, the eleventh indiction of the Romans, at Pæapolis, a city of the eastern Franks, which they also call Würzburg and Herbipolis.

END OF THE POLYGRAPHY OF JOHANNES Trithemius, Abbot at Saint James in Würzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict.

CORRECTION.

The fair reader must be warned that the order of the books of this Polygraphy is above all to be observed, lest the division of the signature that occurred in the first alphabet perhaps produce an error. There, indeed, the Key of the Polygraphy ends at c; after the remaining books, however, the Fifth Book begins again at d.
