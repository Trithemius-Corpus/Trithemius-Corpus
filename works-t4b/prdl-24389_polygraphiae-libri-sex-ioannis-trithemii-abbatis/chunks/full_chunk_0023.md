Let us also set down an example of this alphabet, to prove that a secret can lie hidden in it:

Quogozozugi, yfarz din, uc, qdcquxua, rigidiuosi, aqquirix tz, fti, idra, yfocsaqufg, uc, coddotacuoz, ofogui, iuvg, ci, hidioz.

This writing contains in itself the following meaning: “Know, my friend, that the king has decreed in council that you are to be killed. Therefore flee as quickly as possible into Narragonia, away from his presence, lest you perish.” This too is a tortuous mode of writing and is subject to no regular rule, since at the writer’s pleasure it can be varied and changed by chance transposition as much as anyone wishes.

Next it should be noted that at the end of the sixth book of the Polygraphia we handed down another way of writing by numerical signs, which people commonly call ciphers. Under its covering you will safely write to an absent friend whatever you wish, and no one unskilled in the art will be able either to discover or to suspect that a secret lies hidden, especially if the rules we gave concerning these matters are observed with careful diligence. This is that the paper should be formed in the manner of an astronomical table, as though it contained something pertaining to the reckoning of the stars: namely, so that characters of the signs or planets are inserted, in order that anyone into whose hands it may perhaps fall by chance, led on by the likeness of a table, will think that no secret lies hidden in it, but will suppose that everything pertains to some astronomical calculation.

We have arranged twelve alphabets according to the number of the twelve signs of the Zodiac, assigning to each one the number twenty-four in place of letters. The first of these, Aries, begins from one and ends at twenty-four. Yet we advise that in great and difficult matters one should avoid beginning from one, because this way of writing can easily be suspected on account of that very thing. But whoever wishes to use his natural industry prudently will discover subtler things from ours, and what we have handed down not fully polished he will easily make better.

In the numerical orchema great secrets lie hidden; if anyone properly understands it, he will penetrate many mysteries of numbers. For we had neither the time nor the reason on this occasion to pour everything out into public view, but in our judgment what we have done will have been enough for the instruction of those to whom our inventions will one day be pleasing, so that they may have many ways not only of secretly writing whatever they wish

to absent sharers in the art, but also one way of bringing forth many better and subtler ones, according to the capacity of each person’s understanding. Likewise, since the orchemata of numbers are sufficiently open and clear in their proper places, and their tables have also been set out, it was not necessary here to repeat what has already been said, lest excessive prolixity create weariness for the reader. Nor did I judge it necessary to set down an example of secret writing by means of numerical figures, since there is no difficulty in knowing the method for all who possess even moderate intelligence.

With similar ease the very ancient signs of reckoning that we have brought back into the light will be able to be understood and grasped; by them the knowledge of numbers is extended to infinity. For whenever a small stroke is placed above a smaller number, a thousandfold value is indicated. Thus x with a stroke above it means ten thousand; C, one hundred thousand; M, a thousand thousand; X, ten thousand thousand; .M., a thousand thousand thousand; and so consequently as far as you may wish to proceed in numbering, without limit.

THE END OF THE KEY TO THE POLYGRAPHIA of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Peapoli, to Emperor Maximilian.
