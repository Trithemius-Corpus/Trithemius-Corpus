Fifteenth, note that all these things that we have handed down as discoveries in the Polygraphy are certain small introductions in comparison with those things that still remain to be discovered and written in this same faculty. We pass over these in silence, lest we seem to boast in vain. For we have given to minds fit for invention an occasion for discovering greater things than we have either written or perhaps been able to discover. Let him who can add, for the completion of this invention, those things that he knows to be useful; but he who cannot should not rashly judge what he does not understand.

An explanation of those things which seemed more obscure in the second book of the Polygraphy.

In the second book of the Polygraphy, apart from the order and sequence of the words, nothing new is first handed down; and therefore one and the same method of working will apply in both books, and all the things that we said were to be observed in the first, we admonish must also be observed diligently in the second. In the narration alone a difference of discourse is clearly seen to have arisen, since the former is composed concerning our Lord, the Son of God, while this one is raised to his mother, the ever-untouched Virgin. In this narration of fictive speech is contained this brief secret sentence of a friend warning a friend of danger: "Do not come to court, because the king is angry with you." You see how easy, safe, and secure this method is for writing and announcing to a friend who understands it whatever you wish. In all cases, however, you should know that care must be taken, as was said before, not to take more than one word from any alphabet. Whenever you have brought the narration of the invented speech to the end of the book, while the secret of your mind has not yet been completed, begin again from the beginning, and do this as often as necessary. Nevertheless, each person will be able, at his own discretion, to change these words of ours, improve them, and add more, provided that he avoids a consonance that breaks out disgracefully in the sounds.

Explanation of the fourth book of our Polygraphy.

In the fourth book of the Polygraphy, by a similar method, we have placed words invented by us under alphabetical order, but in one thing alone we deliberately differ from the preceding book. For in the preceding book the words contain in themselves no property other than that they represent the letters placed before them at the head. But in the fourth book, which we now have in hand, each word contains its own meaning, and nevertheless also designates the letters placed before it. They have within what they display without, because the second letter of each word always serves the hidden mystery, as we shall make clear in the following example.

Ecorach hanasar dulay semalas, chorion suriel anephor schabaras husarat uiimalo tropon tumelech amasar.
