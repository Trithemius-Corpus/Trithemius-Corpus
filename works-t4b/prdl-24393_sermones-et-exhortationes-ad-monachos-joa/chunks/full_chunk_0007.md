For the Lord in the Gospel did not proclaim every poor person blessed and to be granted the kingdom of heaven, but, as you know, he announced that only the poor in spirit are blessed. Contempt for riches is not sufficient for salvation, unless the one who renounces gold also strives with all his strength, through patience and humility, constantly to imitate the Savior. For it is easier to despise the world than to despise and deny oneself.

Therefore whoever wishes to imitate the Son of God and attain evangelical perfection must become a despiser not only of the world and temporal things, but also of his own self. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: “Anyone who has not renounced all that he possesses cannot be my disciple.” And lest we should think this sufficient for perfection, he speaks again more openly concerning contempt of oneself, saying: “If anyone wishes to follow me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his life will lose it; but whoever loses his life for my sake and the Gospel’s will save it.” Christ was made obedient to the Father even unto death. If you wish to imitate him as you have promised, brothers, it is necessary that you deny yourselves to yourselves. For he cannot be a monk who has not in every respect renounced his own will entirely.

You who before God and all his saints have promised to live according to the monastic Rule may no longer claim your own will for yourselves, since what has once been consecrated to God must not afterwards be called back to human uses. For you serve as soldiers not for yourselves but for Christ the King; therefore neither to will nor not to will remains in your power. For you are dead, as holy Theophilus says, and your life is hidden with Christ in God; but when Christ, your life, appears, then you also will appear with him in glory. If you desire to rejoice with him without end, you must now in this present time serve as vigorous soldiers for love of him. For no one will be crowned with Christ except the one who, striving lawfully, has persevered even to the end, since he cannot conquer who does not know how to persevere.

On the Progress of Spiritual Warfare: Second Homily

As often, most beloved brothers, as the memory of that event comes back to mind, when you appointed me, unworthy as I was, as abbot over you, though I protested and resisted, so often I am wholly shaken within myself by the bitterness of my heart, and even unwillingly I am compelled to shed undeserved tears. For whether this was done not because of my merits but because of yours, I do not know. May almighty God grant that this appointment of me was not made because of my demerits or yours. Why did you wish it so, brothers? You believed me, young, unlearned, and by no means fit for the soldiery which we profess, to be rich in God; so that now it is altogether necessary for me at the same time both to teach nothing and to learn, since neither perfection of life commends me as a teacher, nor does consideration of age yet grant authority to the necessity of my office.

In these three years I have sufficiently learned by experience in how many miseries I am entangled, while I myself alone am compelled to bear the weaknesses of you all, and I, unhappy man, am supported by no one. Who will deliver me from the multitude of these vanities into which I have been called back, I who entered the monastery in order to flee beneath all the care and anxiety even of the present life? Since, however, it is necessary to bear the burden placed upon my shoulders, I ask you, brothers, earnestly stretch out to me the helping hands of your prayers, so that in your midst, by the Lord’s gift, I may deserve to conduct myself in such a way that I do not depart from the condition of spiritual warfare which the Rule has prescribed for us.

For we are professors of the same warfare, nor does my office alter the vow by which, together with all the sheep, it has made me, as shepherd, bound by solemn profession in the enclosure of this life. Therefore, when I speak to you about the spiritual warfare which, by God’s mercy, we have professed, my intention is, as far as the Lord Jesus will deign to grant, to shake both myself out of the feathers of sloth and fear into the fervor of holy newness, and all of you, according to my strength, to stir up to the custody of the military order.

For we are frail and weak, and we do not long remain stable in holy readiness unless by daily exercises we continually renew the fervor of the spirit. For by reason of my office I am bound to set before you in common those things that can conduce to your salvation; it belongs to you to listen patiently in all things to me as I speak, as the shepherd of your souls. For although, as a young man, I am not fit to teach so great a multitude of elders, nevertheless, since you appointed me shepherd over you, I am compelled, under the necessity of salvation, to feed the flock which I have undertaken to govern.

What is mine to do I gladly do, and will do according to my strength as long as I live in this anxious care of pastoral charge: young as I am, I will teach you elders, and without interruption I will show how you ought to serve as soldiers for him into whose arms you have sworn. For the dreadful threat of our lawgiver does not escape me, when he says: “Let the abbot know that whatever lack of profit the father of the household may find in the sheep is laid upon the fault of the shepherd.” And what follows concerns you, my brothers, if, while I preach and admonish, contempt for the word of God arises. Again he says that the shepherd will be free only if every diligence has been bestowed on a disobedient flock, and every care has been applied to their diseased actions; then their shepherd, absolved in the judgment of the Lord, may say with the prophet to the Lord: “I have not hidden your justice in my heart; I have spoken your truth and your salvation. But they, despising, have spurned me.” And then at last, for the disobedient sheep entrusted to his care, death itself, prevailing over them, will be their punishment.

Here no covering of excuse is left; no place of escape is granted; rather, the danger of both parties is publicly described. For I, holding the place of abbot, if I have not announced to you the justice of God, if I have not corrected you when you sin according to the Rule, have denied my office, and whatever is found less than sanctified in you will be imputed wholly to me. It is mine, by reason of the pastoral office, to say what is expedient for the salvation of your souls; yours, however, is humbly to hear what I have pronounced in the name of the Savior, and, as far as each one is able, faithfully to fulfill it with a great heart.

You have heard my danger if I do not cry out; you have also heard yours if you despise me as I preach the word of God to you. Therefore, so that by the Savior’s mercy we may all come through safely, let each one of us diligently pursue what is prescribed for him in this spiritual warfare, taking the greatest care that we not, having become deaf hearers of the truth, lie to the Lord. Let us diligently remember the vow which we made, and let us preserve inviolate until death the holy order which we profess. Let us hear our king speaking in the Gospel to his soldiers: “If anyone serves me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also my servant will be.”

The ministers of the eternal king are priests and Levites, and all cloistered monks, bound by a double reason to great holiness, since we have at the same time received the order of clerics and profess the Rule of monastic purity. Therefore let us follow our commander, whom we serve, along the harsh road of all the virtues; and let us arm ourselves with right faith, firm hope, and the most fervent love of God and neighbor, in which the perfection of all holiness is comprehended. For whoever is girded with the defense of charity is never overcome by the adversary. For in charity the warfare of Christ truly grows sweet, and when its fervor has begun to burn in the soldier’s heart, he fears no adversities.

For charity, as the divine Gregory says, is never idle, but accomplishes great things if it is true; but if it refuses to work, it was not perfect charity. Therefore, cloistered soldiers, you who promised that you would serve the eternal king forever, cast away now the works of darkness, and put on charity toward God and neighbor, which is the strongest armor of salvation for all. If anyone has been clothed in it, he will fear no labors, however hard, and no opposition of this world. He will not fear hunger, thirst, persecutions by men, insults, mockeries, prisons, chains, or any hard and adverse things whatever; but everyone who has once perfectly tasted the charity of God will be unconquered against all things.
