Know, my brothers, that there is no sin so small that the justice of almighty God lets it go unpunished. Therefore, since there is none of us who has not offended in many things, arm yourselves, I beg you, with patience and humility, so that as often as you are corrected for your sins, you may bear it with all gentleness. For in this time of free choice you can make satisfaction for many debts with comparatively slight expiation, whereas after death in purgatory the soul will be tormented for a long time and most severely even for small faults. Indeed, voluntary satisfaction has great value; what is done from necessity wins less favor. Altogether, brothers, it is fitting and necessary for souls to be cleansed of sin, if they are to reign in heaven and rejoice with Christ. But the soul that has neglected the purging of its sins in the present life will be punished more heavily in the life to come.

Let none of you say, my dearest brothers, what many ignorant people are accustomed to say: “It is enough for me if my soul undergoes the pains of purgatory.” Believe me, brothers: whoever has said this word from the heart does not consider what purgatory will be. For he does not know what he is asking who desires purgatory. Just as a man is foolish who, when he could pay his debt as a free man, scorns to do so and willingly goes to prison, so he deserves to be judged the most foolish of all men who, when he can make satisfaction to God for his sins in this life, puts it off to the future. The penance of this life is fruitful, and voluntary satisfaction is also greatly meritorious; after death, however, there will be no place for repentance, but each one will then receive according to what he has done in his body, whether good or evil.

Think on these things, brothers, while you live in health, lest, overtaken by the command of death, you finally grieve too late. Let not doubtful tomorrow await today’s salvation; and he who can pay his debt with grace while at liberty should not allow himself to be dragged to prison with torture. As much as each person can throughout his whole life, let him take care to make satisfaction for his sins, because the spirit returns secure to the Lord if, while still existing in the body, it is daily purified by the fire of divine love. You have here a place in which the daily expiation of public offenses is carried out; if you kept true and humble patience in your soul, you would undoubtedly obtain the certain remission of them all. As for secret and hidden sins, not only does the Rule admonish you what you ought to do, but daily sacramental confession also instructs you in all things.

For we are accustomed daily, each one devoutly, to offer the Son of God at the altar; and none of us approaches that most holy mystery unless he has first, according to custom, made confession, because the greatest purity is required where, through the Mass, the salvation of the whole world is renewed. In the midst of these things, most beloved brothers, who among us would not make daily progress in the love of God, unless he shamefully and entirely laid aside every desire for progress? For I dare to say that a monk who does not make progress in this holy congregation is plainly proved to have utterly forgotten his own salvation.

Believe me, brothers: God will save no one against his will. If you neglect your own progress, it will not be imputed to him, but to you. For God is the intellectual light that enlightens every man coming into this world, as the high-flying John says; but those who do not receive the light necessarily remain in darkness. He who turns his mind away from the faith that is in the Son of God remains in darkness; and if he does not do the works of light, faith will profit him nothing for salvation. Outside the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ there is no salvation, and whoever thinks otherwise is not a Christian. O man, do not let a foolish notion of piety seduce you into believing that anyone outside the Christian faith has salvation unto eternal life. For Christ, the only Son of God, is the sole Savior of the world; whoever does not believe in him, the wrath of God remains upon him, and he will be condemned with the devil.

But you, my brothers, who already believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, I beg you henceforth to live in such a way that, Christian in name and deed, you may be able to come to his fellowship, who lives and reigns with God the Father, in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, throughout all ages.
