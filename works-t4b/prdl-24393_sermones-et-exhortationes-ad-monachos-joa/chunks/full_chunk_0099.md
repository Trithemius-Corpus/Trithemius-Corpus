On Continence and True Chastity.

Nothing is more beautiful than chastity: it makes clean what was conceived from unclean seed; it also makes a member of the household out of an enemy, and an earthly human being into an angel. Chastity is the most beautiful of all virtues; when preserved worthily for love of Christ even unto death, it is rewarded with an inestimable prize. Chastity is nearest to the fellowship of angels, the highest medicine of the Christian mind. Chastity is the lovable household companion of Jesus Christ, the eternal king, a wondrous beginning of future incorruption. Chastity is the fullness of heavenly grace, through which every corruption of nature is restored and renewed. Chastity is the most powerful of all virtues; through it, in fragile flesh, nature conquers in a wondrous contest. Chastity imitates the angelic life, and even in the midst of mud is not touched by any sprinkling of stain. Blessed is the person who has deserved to receive the true gift of chastity from the Lord God, and to keep it inviolate unto death in Christian humility.

Chaste is the one who, for love of Jesus Christ, pure in mind and body, abstains from every defilement of the flesh; who, by the speech of the Holy Spirit, sweetly burns in the charity of God; who desires absolutely nothing in this world, but always longs to be and to live with Christ. Chaste is the one who, mindful of his profession of spiritual warfare, subdues the motions of the flesh by reason, and strongly restrains at once, from their beginning, all the little sparks of budding lust. Chaste is the one who, as soon as a carnal thought begins to knock at the mind, implores divine help with tears, and does not cease from prayers until he senses that the fire of burning desire has been utterly extinguished. Chaste is the one who flees every occasion and touch of lust; who makes a covenant with his eyes so that he does not even think of a woman; and who sets a guard over his senses, lest any foul or immodest things be thrust upon them. Chaste is the one who avoids the company of the wanton and always turns his eyes away, lest he behold vanities. Chaste is the one who detests the presence of all misery and who at all times holds his own frailty suspect, with fear and watchfulness. Truly chaste is the one who, in mind and body, imitates angelic nature and admits no corruption in holy conduct.

Chastity is a gift of grace, and not a work of nature or human industry. Chastity makes a humble monk most pleasing to God, since one who lives in mortal flesh without corruption is a companion of the angels. Whoever has attained chastity is always truly humble, and whoever understands its nature does not boast in himself over its possession, but in the Lord who gave it. For no one is chaste by his own strength, since fragile man is not sufficient to conquer nature unless he who created nature and all things grants it. As often, therefore, as nature is conquered through chastity, God manifestly works together with man. Chastity itself has, in those subject to it, a difficult beginning, until the movement of unlawful thought is brought into contempt. Chastity overcomes fornication by fleeing rather than by struggling against it, since lust is girded about with so many subtle weapons that it must be avoided before it gathers strength.

Therefore, whenever anyone’s mind is touched by the motions of lust, let divine help be invoked without delay. Let no deliberate space be given to unclean thought, but as soon as it begins to be felt, let it be brought into contempt by the remedy of forgetfulness. No conversations at all are to be held with the fraudulent enemy who is always laying snares; nor are counsels of the flesh to be discussed with unclean thoughts. For unless you crush the serpent’s head, it slips away; unless you at once break the motions of lust from the beginning, it is strengthened. Hence the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, teaches that one must fight against any vices, but commands fornication alone to be fled. We carry about with us the materials of victory against ourselves; therefore he wills that we flee the enemy.

Chastity is nourished by fasting; it is shipwrecked by gluttony. Therefore, if you love chastity, love fasting, so that it may have necessary nourishment. One who is chaste follows the industry of the bee; and one who is truly a monk, as though crowned with all flowers, is adorned with all virtues. Not everyone who is ignorant of lusts is chaste, but the one who, in pure and clean flesh, for the love of Christ alone, also strives for holiness of mind. Otherwise, what does purity of dwelling profit, if the inhabitant is foul and unclean? Blind paganism once had its virgins, sacrificing in their garments; but because they did not offer the modesty of the body to the true God, nor did they preserve right faith in the mind, they descended into the depths of hell with the demons whom they worshiped. For chaste is the one who, for love of the Creator, restrains the flesh from every lust, and, holy in mind through virtues, offers to God himself a soul adorned with all virtues.

Outside the faith of Jesus Christ today there is no true chastity, nor any that is meritorious for eternal salvation. Although you live chastely, O monk, pure and holy, and as long as the spirit remains in the body, do not presume to have confidence in chastity from clay. The food of chastity is fasting and abstinence, and, in every action, the support of wholesome continence. But when you have attained all things, do not trust in yourself, because the one whom we know to have eaten nothing fell from heaven. Mortification of the body is both very necessary and very useful for preserving the beauty of chastity; but however much you have advanced in either, do not trust in yourself. Instead, always watchful, hoping in the Lord’s help, be afraid. For unless you keep yourself continually and most earnestly in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overthrown. He runs in vain who presumes to guard chastity by his own strength. For unless the Lord causes the dwelling of chastity to be built, the one who builds the wall of abstinence labors in vain. And unless you become, with all your strength, a co-worker with divine grace, you will by no means obtain the beauty of chastity. For God will not make you chaste without your will, nor will you attain this great gift unless he, through his mercy, comes to the aid of your weakness.

Therefore it will be your part, O monk, to offer the desire for chastity with good will, and, in humble confidence, to await its sure increase from the Lord. Apply fasting, and the Lord’s help will not be lacking. Chastity is a gift of grace, not of nature; and to live in the flesh as though beyond the flesh belongs to angelic strength, not human. Nevertheless, it is not difficult for a person of good will to preserve chastity, especially one who is separated from the tumult of worldly people and lives solitary in the midst of monks. A monk, too, who enters a monastery of good reform while young and innocent, if he keeps his mind continually occupied in the fear of the Lord, is less assailed by lust. For the experience of lust is pernicious, since, as someone truly said, it seeks to conquer souls, not to satisfy the veins. But a monk who is a lover of chastity can easily extinguish the heat of lust if, fleeing idleness and fullness, he chooses to occupy himself continually in spiritual exercise. For among all vices none assaults chastity more greatly or more dangerously than fullness and idleness. If a monk overcomes these two evils, he will easily preserve chastity.

Hence the most holy princes of monks, wishing to cut away the causes of lust, established for us a spare and meager diet according to a fixed measure, by which the body is sustained for necessity and is not fattened for lust. They also decreed that scantiness of sleep was useful for monks, because immoderate rest stretches the flesh no less than gluttony. But they wished idleness, as the most pernicious plague of the soul, to be far from cloistered persons, dividing the time most wisely so that a monk would never be found idle. Hear these things, brothers, and grasp them in your mind, you who carry a great treasure in a fragile little body. Always flee idleness; always do something good, so that whenever the tempter, the devil, approaches, he may find you usefully occupied. For truly, one who is idle lies open to the snares of the tempter; but one who is occupied in good work has closed every entrance to snares. Therefore the mind must be occupied with continual exercise, so that it is never found idle. Blessed is that monk who occupies his mind at all times in good exercises and never sleeps in the sleep of torpor and sloth. Blessed is the spiritual soldier of Christ who always keeps watch in the fear of the Lord and immediately restrains, from the beginning, all the disordered motions of the flesh.

For it is not enough to guard true chastity unharmed if someone plays with unclean thoughts as though deliberating with them. It belongs to strong men to crush the first motions of wicked thoughts, because where delay creates danger, one must fight strongly at once. There must always be vigilance against the adversary of our salvation, who is never conquered more quickly than if he is choked off immediately from the beginning. Continual exercise is needed, brothers, because a mind always occupied in holy studies is like a very high tower of strength, in which the soldier of Jesus, set as a most watchful guard, fears no hostile snares. Therefore I beseech you, my dearest brothers, do not consume time in idleness, lest you increase in yourselves the material of vices, you who are not ignorant of the wiles of the malignant spirit. Preserve continence in all things, and apply temperance, the guide of virtues, to all your works. Always consider how you ought to conduct yourselves in all religious holiness before the Lord [unclear].
