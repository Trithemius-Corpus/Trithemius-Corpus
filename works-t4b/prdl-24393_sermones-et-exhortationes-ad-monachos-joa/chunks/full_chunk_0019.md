On the reading and study of the divine Scriptures.

A man polluted by the filth of vices is forbidden to it. Thus, indeed, it is read in Exodus: “Let no one go up onto the mountain, nor let anyone touch its borders. Everyone who touches the mountain shall surely die. If a hand touches it, he shall be overwhelmed with stones. Whether it is beast or man, he shall not live.” A man touches the mountain and dies whenever someone, swollen with secular learning, takes offense at the simplicity of the divine Scriptures. A beast touches the mountain and is buried with stones whenever a carnal man, sunk in the frenzy of the passions and not purified in soul, advances to the study of sacred Scripture.

Therefore, if you wish to cultivate the readings of the divine Scriptures to your salvation, if by reading you desire to come to true knowledge, take care first to put away every swelling of pride and rashness of presumption; and then cleanse the chamber of your heart from every uncleanness of carnal pleasures. For God, as canonical Scripture says, resists the proud but gives grace to the humble. No one proud, puffed up, arrogant, and rash, no one carnal in soul, lustful or restless, is considered fit for the study of sacred Scripture, but only one humble in divine matters, devout in mind, meek, modest, chaste, and tranquil in body and mind.

Hence Prosper says in an epigram:

The commands of God cannot be kept unless the mind is at rest.
For religious study to be carried on, the quarrels of the malicious must be cut away.
Those who are at peace can write and read the law,
if their hearts, free from the tumult of the world, are at leisure.
Thus the mind, intent on pious studies, finds its battles;
divine love conquers carnal love.
By sacred knowledge this temple of the Lord grows,
and silently a house is built that will not fall.

Therefore proceed to the study of the Scriptures with devoted minds and with a heart pierced in the love of God and humbled in true contrition, because the Lord will not enlighten you unless he has first made you clean from every iniquity. For, as Scripture itself says, perverse thoughts separate from God, and tested virtue corrects the foolish, since wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sins; for the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee deceit and will withdraw from thoughts that are without understanding. And so we must often pray to the Lord with holy David: “Create a clean heart in me, O God, and renew a right spirit within my inward parts.” For no one reads and understands the sacred Scriptures of God who does not turn to the Lord with his whole heart through repentance. And so, if you wish both to retain the divine utterances and to understand them to your salvation, take the greatest care first to keep yourselves, as far as you can, clean from every pollution of sin.

Do not let the mad folly of certain monks seduce you, who try to excuse their ignorance with a fine proverb, saying: “Why do we need knowledge of the Scriptures, since we have no office of preaching? Let us rather embrace the counsel of Wisdom, which says in the book of Proverbs: ‘He who walks simply walks confidently.’ It is enough for us to live simply, since the servant who knew the will of his lord and did not do it will be beaten with many blows, but he who did not know and did things worthy of blows will be beaten with few.”

O foolish and utterly ruined men, who despise saving knowledge and love ignorance of the divine Scriptures more than understanding. Will chosen ignorance make you liable to fewer blows? Or will you, when you sin before God, be innocent for this reason, that you are willingly ignorant of the commandments of God? You will have to be tormented with a double punishment: one, because you despise learning the commandments of God; the other, because you do not keep his word. What you plead is not simplicity, but impiety hidden under that name. And since, according to the prophet, “blessed are those who keep the testimonies of the Lord,” who can doubt that those are accursed who neither seek God nor attend to his Scriptures, but rather, with hardened mind, despise them?

Hear what Solomon says to you in Proverbs: “Where the soul is without knowledge, it is not good,” and “the foolishness of a man trips up his steps.” Of you also, O most foolish dwellers in darkness, that saying of Saint Jerome is to be understood: ignorance is no longer excusable where one knows what it is that one does not know. But you must take greater care lest you at last be counted with those who said to God: “Depart from us; we do not want the knowledge of your ways.”

You understand Solomon’s saying badly if you think that “simply” is said of ignorance. He who walks simply, without any deception of his neighbor, walks confidently in keeping the commandments of God; but he who corrupts his ways will not be able to lie hidden, but will be made manifest. Hence again he says: “The lips of the just instruct many.”
