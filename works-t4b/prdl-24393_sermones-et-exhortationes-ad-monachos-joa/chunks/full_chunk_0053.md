When it is green, by its muddiness it stains the beauty of the surrounding meadows; so, when a monk gives attention to the tales of secular people, he defiles the purity of his conscience. Therefore, before you set your foot out of the monastery, fortify yourself with holy prayers; and before you go out, do not neglect especially to commend the road to the Lord. Then carry out what the lawgiver commanded, and entrust yourself to be protected by the prayers both of the abbot and of all the brothers.

Prescribe for yourself the canon of the strictness of regular observance, under the steady remembrance of which you may guard your mind from every distraction of the vanities that meet you. Never go out of the monastery without the abbot’s blessing, because, since you ought to be dead and buried to this world in your conduct, it will not be lawful for you thereafter to do anything without the command of your superior. Recognize that you are a soldier of King Jesus Christ; you must follow the whole emperor, without whom there is no salvation. He, fastened to the cross, was taken down not by his own hands but by those of others; if you desire salvation, your will must necessarily be governed by the abbot’s command. For the monk who goes anywhere out of the monastery without the abbot’s permission is like a little sheep in darkness, which trembles when the house burns with fire.

But the true monk of Christ, even when he is outside the monastery in body, remains in the cloister of the mind; and he is no less alone when with many than he has been accustomed to be solitary in his cell. The lover of Christ thinks of Jesus even in the midst of a crowd; and he who is a true worker of virtue keeps his heart firm in the fear of God in every place. For even if in body he is not in the monastery, nevertheless, through the vigor of his mind, he always stands fixed and steadfast, as if alone in the cloister. The spirit is not polluted by the body’s motion, when the solitude of the mind is kept secure.

You must live on the road, O monk, with the same holiness with which you are commanded to conduct yourself in the monastery, and you must walk with a certain greater caution, lest you scandalize anyone by a more lax example, where there seem to be as many judges of monks as there are observers present. The eyes of all secular people look upon monks, and in their works they look for the holiness which they hear them profess. A monk gives more offense by one reckless word than a man given to the world does by ten thousand. For however frivolous and buffoonish a worldly man may seem, nevertheless he has not learned to tolerate calmly the insolence of monks, since with rustic simplicity he believes all cloistered men to be not as they are, but as they ought to be. Hence among them an evil word or deed is charged to a monk for condemnation, whereas it is imputed to a worldly man for pardon.

This is not undeserved. For a monk who has professed the holy and approved canon is bound to direct his whole effort toward perfection, and in word and deed to surpass by far the conduct of secular men in all purity of mind and body. For a light or idle word, which is granted to secular people for pardon, is judged for cloistered men to be destruction.

Gold is more precious than all metals and is more greatly desired by mortals; and in nothing is a stain or corruption of falsity more quickly detected. And a defect which is discerned in gold with judicial severity is overlooked in the other metals because of the baseness of their nature. Gold delights the eyes with its beauty, and by its virtue strengthens the innermost parts of life. Gold, tested in the fire, is not diminished; and a true monk of God is not changed from the purpose of holiness in the tribulation of persecution. A small stain on a golden vessel greatly displeases in the sight of a powerful man; if an earthen vessel is crooked, scarcely anyone detests it. When a monk offends in small matters, a great outcry of many is raised against him, because his way of life is compared to gold. The canonical life of monks is a golden mirror. Gold in the midst of mud receives no stain upon its nature, but preserves all its sweetness inviolate.

So too the true lover of Christ, such as he has learned to conduct himself in the secrecy of his little cell, such also he stands among men when sent onto the road, because he walks pure in mind as well as body before the Lord. For if he is a true monk, he is not polluted by the dust of another’s way of life, nor is he changed from the rectitude of his holy purpose by anyone’s presence or conversation. Amid the tumultuous talk of worldly people he thinks of other things, and the dissolute conduct of others does not delight his mind. He who is single in heart is not divided even in the midst of those making an uproar. Therefore, monk, you who desire to be good, gather your mind back to yourself, and before you leave your cell for the journey, dispose your heart. Let the fear of the Lord always be the companion of your road.
