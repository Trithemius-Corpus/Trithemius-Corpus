Homily 10. Fol. XXIII.

We gird these, however, when we keep our bodies pure and unstained in mind from every form of lust. The virtue of holy chastity is of great merit before God, and, because of the greatness of the struggle, is to be endowed with a special reward in the appointed recompense, as the Lord says through Isaiah the prophet: Thus says the Lord to the eunuchs: Those who keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that I have willed, and hold fast my covenant, I will give them in my house and within my walls a place and a name better than sons and daughters. I will give them an everlasting name, which shall not perish.

And concerning these the Lord says in the holy Gospel: For there are eunuchs who were so born from their mother's womb; and there are eunuchs who were made so by men; and there are eunuchs who have made themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. He who can receive this, let him receive it.

But why did he say, He who can receive this, let him receive it? I think that this statement about eunuchs, who castrate themselves for love of the kingdom of heaven, should not be referred to the cutting away of the genital members, as many, in their exultation, have strayed from the truth of the Gospel, but rather to the mastery of the mind, by which chastity is preserved whole and conceived in chaste love. For they castrate themselves for the kingdom of God, who, for love of God, keep true chastity of mind and body inviolate. And just as no one is easily castrated bodily without the greatest torment of pain, so no one can attain the perfection of chastity without the greatest labor of zeal. And certainly no one is chaste by his own strength, no one modest by his own merits. It is God who gives the good will, and he also joins strength to the will so that it can prevail.

But because it does not belong to many to remain chaste, he leaves each person to his own judgment, saying: He who can receive this, let him receive it. Cutting off the members does not make the mind chaste, since the root of lust is within, and requires something more than the amputation of the shameful parts. But if the mind is chaste and whole, it will easily restrain the insolence of the members. For nothing is stronger than a mind ruling with a pure heart and sweetly loving above all things.

Think now, most beloved brothers, on the future beauty of the breast, and the labor of chastity will not burden you. For to the great height of the breast, to the surpassing joys, the infancy of labor leads. Therefore whoever flees the labor of the contest will never attain the height of honor. Now is the time in which, laboring, we resist the devil; but the eternal age will come very soon, in which, reigning without end, we shall rejoice with Christ.

Let the body be bound fast by discreet abstinence; let it submit to the command of the mind; and let the concupiscence of the flesh be broken by holy exercises, so that it may always obey the counsel of reason. If at any time it begins to swell with movements against salvation, let it also be restrained with blows, because it is better humbly to endure the bruises of wounds than, through the indulgence of the flesh, to cast the soul down into hell.

For it is said in Scripture: Whoever delicately nourishes his servant from childhood will afterward feel him stubborn. The flesh is the servant of the mind, and ought always to obey the command of reason. But if the flesh has been nourished too licentiously, already accustomed to pleasures, it will refuse to bear the rule of the spirit. For a servant is made insolent toward his master whenever he is delicately nourished by him through habit. And it is difficult to bring a rebellious and insolent servant back to humble obedience after he has long been accustomed to kick back.

For it is written: A servant cannot be instructed by words, because he understands what is said and scorns to answer. Therefore the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: Fodder and rod and burden for the ass; bread and discipline and work for the servant. Put him to work under discipline and he seeks rest; slacken your hand with him, and he seeks freedom. Yoke and strap bend a stiff neck, and constant tasks bow a servant down.

If we begin to transfer these testimonies of the Scriptures from the literal to the moral sense, everything that is said about the servant and the ass we may rightly understand as applying to the sluggish and delicate flesh. For truly the flesh is the servant of the soul, and it always rises up against its master unless it is wearied every day by discipline and work. Whoever keeps it delicate at all times in idleness and pleasures will necessarily find it insolent against spiritual promptings, because, while the flesh is lifted up in pride, the spirit, languishing in idleness and grown lukewarm, is weakened. Therefore, unless you continually humble this servant of yours, namely the flesh, O brother monk, by work and abstinence; unless you place upon its unlawful habits the bridle of continence and sobriety, you will go wholly after it as it runs ahead into the abyss.
