Book XVI.

For food is not fruitfully withdrawn from the body unless the mind is called back from every iniquity. The divine Jerome likewise says: What profit is it for the body to be made thin by abstinence, if the soul swells up with pride? What virtue is there in not drinking wine, and yet becoming drunk with anger, hatred, and the other poisonous cups of the soul?

Therefore let none of you promise himself holiness from abstinence from foods alone, since whatever is required for true penitence is also judged necessary for fasting. For fasts profit toward salvation for those who are contrite for their sins in a true purpose of amendment, and who fervently devote themselves to good and holy works. Hence all who fast from food and drink must also abstain from sins, so that the mind, adorned with the beauty of virtues, may sweetly grow warm in the love of Christ and not grow lukewarm in the progress of inner conversion. For the virtue of abstinence is nothing if it has not been adorned by works of charity.

Therefore hear my counsel, monk, if you wish to make your fast fruitful before God. First cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin, so that no desire for evil may intervene, so that the mind may become fervent in the exercise of divine love and be praiseworthily adorned with the other virtues. Through the remedy of contrition and confession wash away past sins, and by prayers, while standing with tears, strengthen your mind against all temptations with God’s help. Toward God, as much as you can, be fervent in the exercise of love; toward yourself, be at peace in a pure conscience; toward your brother, be rightly ordered in true charity. Always be outstanding in humility, firm in constancy and obedience, pure in chastity of mind and body, gentle in meekness, a monk in action, and continually kindled with desire for perfection. If you commend your abstinence with this kind of cleanness and purity, you will fast rightly.

Diligently exercise your mind in meditation on the passion of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, so that no day passes on which, with thanksgiving, you do not at least once run through his life and death in devout meditation. A Christian is, I will not say ungrateful, but without devotion, who is so immersed either in temporal affairs or occupied with the pleasures of the flesh that he does not, at least once each day, remember with thanksgiving the most bitter passion of his Redeemer. But the monk who forgets the benefits of our Lord Jesus Christ, who does not once each day recall his passion with thanksgiving, who does not every day meditate on the law of the Lord or on the Gospel, is deservedly considered the most ungrateful of all men. Nothing is more powerful than that prayer which is offered to God the Father in the union and power of the Lord’s passion.

Exceedingly justly is that spirit to be condemned which, redeemed by the most precious blood of Christ, is led astray by the pleasures of its flesh, so that it does not remember God’s many benefits toward it. For there was no reason why the Son of God should become man except that he wished to justify us sinners: born in the world for us, suffering in the world for us, and cruelly dead on the wood for our crimes. He, the just one, came into the world to justify us who were sinners; the merciful one came to the ungodly, so that from him we might learn mercy, humility, and patience. The physician came to the sick, life to the dead, compassion to the despairing; it was not our merits that drew him down from heaven into the world, but our sins.

It is fitting, therefore, brothers, that we celebrate with all devotion this Lenten fast, dedicated to his most holy passion, since he first deigned to do such great things for our salvation, and, in order to snatch us from the destruction of death, did not hesitate to give himself over to death for us. Let us do, for the love of Christ, whatever good we can; let us endure, in view of his passion and death, every adversity that happens to us in this world, since the Lord Jesus, who willed from charity to suffer for us, has shown himself truly most worthy that we should love him with our whole heart. For if, brother, you acknowledge yourself a sinner, Christ saves you by his death. Contemplate him hanging on the cross as is fitting, and you will be healed from every bite of sin. For nothing is found in this world more effective for cleansing and healing the wounds of conscience than meditation on the wounds of Christ. For the most precious blood of the Lord, poured out, has become a sufficient medicine for sin.
