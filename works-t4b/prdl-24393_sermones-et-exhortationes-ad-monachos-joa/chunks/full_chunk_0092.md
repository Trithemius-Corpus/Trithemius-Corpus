Homily XIX. Fol. XLVII.

At all times, with all your strength and as much as you can, flee all pride and display, because unless you greatly humble your souls, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven. Walk always before the Lord Jesus in the simplicity of your heart, having a pure conscience in all things, lest your humility become bestial; for it contributes nothing to salvation for sinners to acknowledge themselves publicly and, by persevering in evil, not to amend their fault. For the true servant of Christ sanctifies his humility with good works, and amends all the guilt of his past life through continual repentance. Daily he recalls to memory the evils he remembers having committed; he groans deeply that he has offended the Lord. He laments greatly, and so bewails his past offenses that he does not commit further things that must be lamented.

The monk who is truly humble follows the Lord Jesus with a pure conscience, and fears nothing in this world more than offending the teacher whom he imitates. Whatever he does and wherever he goes, he always thinks of the Lord Jesus as present, and by continual meditation considers how he may please him alone. For this is truly Christian humility and humble Christianity: to wash away sin in true compunction, and to preserve the conscience in all purity at all times, not only from fear of punishments but rather from love of Christ.

The true humility of monks continually does whatever good it can for the honor of God, and does not think of doing anything that it knows displeases Christ. The true humility of monks neglects none of the things God has commanded, and always flees as deadly the things he has forbidden. The penitent monk, humble in the compunction of tears and serving the Lord in the fervor of charity, is made a companion of the holy angels, a friend to the citizens of heaven, and very fearsome to demons. For demons fear and flee nothing more than the humility of the servants of Jesus Christ, because, since they themselves are most proud by their own vicious will, they are most violently tormented by humble devotion. Where true humility is present, demons cannot remain; for as ice is dissolved by fire, so malignant spirits are scattered by the presence of a humble monk.

O humility, venerable mother and preserver of all virtues: by your power the kingdom is conferred on the humble; by your glory eternal happiness is granted to the meek. Without your guidance no one comes to heaven; whoever follows you does not fear hell. Whoever gathers works of virtue without you is like one who carries dust into the wind. You are the seasoning of all good works, for just as food in the mouth of the eater is not savory without salt, so without your company no one will be established as a friend of God.

Humility made the Son of God man; humility will make the servants of Christ equal to the holy angels. An angel, through pride and by his own choice, was made a devil; and the Christian man, through humility, becomes an angel. Humility opens heaven to the meek servants of God, and pride plunges haughty men down to hell. Humility makes friends of God, and pride makes companions of demons. Humility enlightens the mind toward true wisdom, and pride blinds the understanding of the one it possesses. Humility purges the person it possesses from all disordered affections, and pride fills its worshipper with all crimes and filthiness.

Humility makes its lover a servant of God; pride, however, makes him a slave of the devil. Humility makes a man wise in the law of the Lord; pride, however, renders a man foolish, far from the knowledge of the Most High. Humility introduces the poor man into fellowship with God; pride makes those rich in their own minds belong to the devil. Humility is the mother and nurse of all virtues, and pride is the source of all vices. Humility, by means of charity, covers a multitude of sins; pride alone brings in the fullness of all vices.

Humility makes the good works of monks most pleasing to God; pride makes the efforts of the haughty useless to the Creator. Whatever humility asks from the Lord, it receives; but almighty God resists the prayers of the proud. Humility always has a devout confidence in the mercies of the Lord, with cheerfulness of mind; pride, however, with the goad of despair, generates disturbance in a person. Humility always has joy of spirit in the love of Christ; but destructive fear always occupies pride. Humility, strong in good works, neglects none of the spiritual exercises; pride despises all pursuits of salvation. Humility justifies sinners; pride condemns its followers. Humility makes a person most like God; pride makes him alien from the grace of God.
