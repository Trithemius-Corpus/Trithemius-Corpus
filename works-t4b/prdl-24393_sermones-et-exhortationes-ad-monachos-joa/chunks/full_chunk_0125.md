Sermon III. Fol. LXIII.

He bears continual contrition in his heart; and, always compunct in spirit, praying and groaning to the Lord with tears, he seeks pardon and mercy. Nor does he pray only for his own sins, but also for the sins of the whole world. The greatest remedy against exaltation of mind is the remembrance of one's own faults.

The third sign of humility is that he desires to keep all the good works he does privately with himself, and hides his own progress out of zeal for humility. For the man who is humble does not vaunt himself emptily, nor does he seek praise from men, since he hopes for the glory of eternal happiness. Whoever among you considers himself humble and a true monk should not despise, for God's sake, this sign of profound submission and abasement.

The fourth sign of true humility is that, voluntarily descending into the abyss of contempt for himself, he judges all his fellow brothers, even those openly sinning, better than himself before God. Mindful of his own evils and forgetful of his good deeds, he wisely considers them; but passing over others' evils in silence, he makes known at fitting times the good things they have done. This is the condition of true humility: always to admire and proclaim the good works of others, and prudently to depreciate and hide one's own for the avoidance of pride.

The fifth sign of humility is obtained by the man who is quiet in his own simplicity and does not speak of others' defects, unless he is compelled either by reason of office or by the command of charity. Yet prudent humility preserves moderation in correction, so that the man whom it cannot make better by correcting him more severely it strives to admonish gently and meekly. It also sometimes wisely passes over rebukes, and waits for and observes a more opportune time.

The sixth sign of humility is shown when a monk, out of love of humility, is slow and not ready to accuse and reprehend the sins of others. For he who is truly humble always considers his own weakness, and silently weighs in secret how many evils he himself has committed against God's justice. Hence he greatly fears to reprehend the deeds of others, because he recognizes that he is a sinner and therefore unworthy to judge others. We except the prelates and rulers of monks from this rule of humility, insofar as we know that they are obliged to correct those subject to them.

The seventh sign of true humility is that he gladly receives corrections and amendments for his faults and negligences, and is not embittered by any resentment against the one who reproves him. Whenever the humble man is corrected by anyone, he rejoices in spirit with the prophet, saying: Let the just man correct me in mercy, and he shall rebuke me; but let not the oil of the sinner fatten my head. But Scripture says of the proud: They have hated the one who reproves in the gate. The man who is truly humble loves the one who corrects him, but flees the flatterer.

The eighth sign of true humility is clearly shown when, once corrected, he does not defend, deny, palliate, or excuse his fault, but confesses it with simple shamefastness. When a truly humble monk is corrected, he does not murmur; he does not protest that he has exceeded the measure of fairness; but immediately, falling to the ground before the judge, he cries out most meekly: I have sinned; I confess my fault; I beg pardon; I promise amendment.

The ninth sign of monastic humility makes those who have it gentle, sweet, and modest in speech, and not clamorous in voice. A monk whom you see clamorous and headlong in speaking you will judge not humble, but proud and rash. The humble man, however, speaks modestly and almost under silence, and is always found readier to listen than to speak before others. It is shameful for a monk to raise his voice too much while speaking, since, being crucified to the world, he is commanded to love silence.

The tenth sign of perfect humility is possessed by the man who is accustomed to interpret everything he hears about his neighbor in the better part. For our Savior says in the holy Gospel: Do not judge, and you will not be judged. And again: With what measure you have measured, it will be measured back to you. The holy apostle also says: Do not judge before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both illuminate the hidden things of darkness and make manifest the counsels of hearts. Who are you, who judge another's servant? To his own lord he stands or falls, and not to you.

The eleventh sign of holy humility is that he despises no man in his heart on account of any offenses whatever, but sets all before himself by the merit of holiness. He also considers all who sin publicly to be better than himself, and therefore condemns no one. He always thinks within his heart about what evil he himself has done, and humbly weighs that the good deeds of others outweigh his own. Therefore he spurns no one, depreciates no one, contemns no one, since he desires all to be saved in the Lord.
