*On the sleep and wakefulness of monks.*

Not shouting, but the intention of one fervent in the love of God, deserves to be heard. Therefore flee drowsiness and torpor of mind as a deadly poison, because sleep and idleness are the spring of all vices. A watchful eye is the servant of inward devotion, and the monk who truly loves Jesus Christ guards himself from every excess. He who is watchful sharpens the mind with understanding; but the monk who sleeps much shamefully blinds his soul. A monk watchful in prayer and fasting is fearsome to the devil; but one who is sated with food and sleep is not permitted to rest by the spirit of fornication. For wakefulness devoted to prayers breaks the madness of lust and powerfully extinguishes the fire of desire; it purifies the inmost heart, enlightens the mind, and inflames the affection toward the love of heavenly things. Wakefulness is the mother, nurse, and guardian of good thoughts, the sure victory over the temptations of the ancient enemy, the flight of phantasms, the death of vices, and the life and fountain of all virtues. Sleep, however, is for many the companion and fosterer of lust, the robber of human life, the image of death, the procurer of oblivion, the partner of sloth. Immoderate sleep corrupts the stomach, harms the brain, and dissolves both body and soul. A monk frequently given over to sleep despises wisdom, prostitutes chastity, neglects the salvation of the soul, loses precious time, kills the mind, and nourishes vices. Therefore, whichever of you, most beloved brothers, desires to come to the rest of heavenly felicity, let him forcefully repel from himself all torpor and sloth of mind; let him learn first to conquer himself, so that thereafter he may be able to overcome the devil, the enemy of his salvation. For unless you first exterminate the evil that is in you, when will you overcome that which comes from outside? Learn to command your passions, and with the Lord’s help you will easily overcome everything that happens outwardly. Cleanse your mind from every disordered desire, so that you may truly love Jesus alone above all things, with whom you hope to rejoice forever. But no one will reign with Christ in heaven who does not imitate him with holy conduct while living on earth, as sacred Scripture says: He who says that he remains in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked. For the Lord Jesus, who by his death freed us from the destruction of eternal damnation, himself voluntarily became mediator of God and men; he himself is the way by which the path of life must be continued for us; he himself is the norm and rule of saving conduct for Christians. No one comes to God except through Christ; no one will see the Father except through the Son. Therefore, if you wish to come to salvation, you must pass through the Savior. For he himself said in the holy Gospel: No one comes to the Father except through me. And that other saying: I am the door; if anyone enters through me, he will find the pasture of eternal life. And again: I am the way, the truth, and the life. Through this way, I pray you, my brothers, always walk by pious and holy conduct; fervently love this truth with a pure heart; hasten ardently toward this life, which alone is lasting. Let the teaching of Christ never depart from your hearts, but remain in memory at all times, since he refuses to become his true imitator who has not been exercised day and night in meditation on his words. Therefore, always watchful in prayers, live with such purity of mind that, when Jesus Christ our Lord calls you in the hour of death, you may deserve to hear him with joy: he who lives and reigns with God the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, one almighty God, forever and ever. Amen.

*On patience and meekness. Homily XVIII.*

Among all professors of the Christian faith there is today no class of men for whom the virtue of patience seems more necessary than for the company of monks.
