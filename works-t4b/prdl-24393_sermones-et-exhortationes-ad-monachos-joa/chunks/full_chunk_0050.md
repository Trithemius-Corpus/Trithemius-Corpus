His mouth will meditate folly, and there is no equal around him. He will be hateful to those near him and to his neighbors, and no faithful man will pursue his friendship. Even if he takes on the hypocritical pretense of simulation, his foolishness will draw others into aversion. He hates peace, loves discord, and will be caught in the sloth of his mind; and there will be no one to grieve with him. He will be crushed by a double affliction, because he did not permit peace to those who had it. The devil has made a nest in his tongue, and Satan has had his dwelling in his mind. Therefore there is no love of the brothers in his heart, because he sows discord between the shepherd and the sheep. He boasts that he is a friend of God, and accepts no one as his equal in regular observance, but he does not understand the very great humility of the Canaanite monk. He does not open his mouth to words of spiritual edification, and from his heart nothing proceeds except vanity. All day long, dissolved in mind and wandering in body, he runs about in the monastery; he gives himself to useless and wholly idle talk; he thinks nothing of the salvation of his soul; he cares for his belly as his god. Guard your soul from his speech, and do not set your foot in the path of his words.

Whoever you are who desire to show yourself a monk and minister of the cross, whoever you are who truly love the Lord Jesus with your whole heart: be cautious and watchful in all your works, and always flee the company of those in whose life nothing is taught and nothing heavenly is seen. Be a companion and associate of humble monks who follow perfection; and gladly admit as preceptor and master the one whom you have known to be grounded in the fear of God. Walk gladly with those who love discipline and purity of mind, and do not be like a man who yields when shaken by every wind; but strengthen your soul in a good purpose, lest you be taken by the counsel of the wicked. For it is best to establish the heart by grace, and neither to be able to deceive nor to wish to deceive. Have as your friend a brother of discipline; and the one by whose word you can be corrected from evil and instructed toward good by example, love and venerate as a father. Have no commerce of familiarity with a restless brother, lest at some time, encouraged by his evil example, you fall into the rebellion of the proud.

Let not a wrathful monk, full of murmuring, be at your side when he stubbornly kicks back against his superiors, nor ever choose him as a friend. Whoever you see violating the canonical fast without the elder’s blessing, flee him as an enemy of salvation; and flee the one who, overcome by talkativeness, without any cause disturbs peace and stirs up quarrels. Flee a rebellious monk, disobedient to elders, as from the face of a snake, because he seethes full of the poison of malice and in arrogance of mind has lost his soul. He who does not obey the command of an elder is perverse; and he who, when corrected, is not humbled is an apostate. Let the one who detracts from and speaks against his brother not be in conversation with you, because he is far worse than a thief.

Whoever you see wholly solicitous in regular observance, love him as a father and venerate him as a most loving preceptor. Keep confidently to the familiarity of the one whose friendship always makes you better in the love of God. With all zeal imitate good men, and follow the manners of those who live in conformity with monastic perfection. Have as your friend a brother of good will, but turn your ears away from the one who is hard-hearted. Let the one who is light in mind and dissolved in conduct be to you like a man who scatters a hedge in a field. Do not be the companion of one who despises the rule and silence, who does not distinguish places or times, who without the prior’s blessing multiplies words everywhere.

Avoid the monk who is given to wine and drink, who too often exceeds the monastery’s canonical measure, as an encouragement of lust and a spring of all vices. The monk who, by the counsel of his own head, strives against the elder’s command is near perdition and hastens breathlessly into the danger of his soul. Do not share in the works of a stubborn monk, and do not have the friendship of one who, laughing at his own rashness, speaks against his superiors in deeds. Be continually in the midst of those who are disciplined, and let your soul gladly walk with the humble. For whoever joins himself to proud men will never be exalted.

He who raises his voice when speaking and cries aloud with a straying voice cannot hide the folly of his heart, and he will boast in his vanity. The friendship of good men is the firm salvation of the soul, and in the company of the wicked the innocence of the simple is corrupted. The company of those whose fear of God has confirmed the examples of good works is best. Nothing is more precious than a friend who fears God, and nothing more dangerous than a companion who has an evil will. A brother who fears God with his whole heart will be a wall of strength for his friend; and whoever is joined with such a man in soundness of mind is sanctified at all times by the blessing of the Spirit of God. He who walks among those who fear God is upheld, lest he fail, as though by a very strong wall around him.

From his words the servant of the Most High will be known, and works of holiness mark out the good monk. No shameful, scurrilous, or idle word comes from the mouth of one who fears the Lord; and he who is a true lover of Jesus does nothing that offends goodness. In the mouth of the monk who loves God Christ always resounds, and he who is a true worshiper of God demonstrates the word of justice both in his mouth and in his work. He who fears God is the best counselor, and he who walks according to the canon is a prudent judge of thoughts. An elder who fears God, and has been proved and tested for a long time in regular observance, is a director of young men; and one who has no stain on his garment is a good instructor of novices. Whoever is an understanding teacher and interpreter of the divine Scriptures, guarding his soul in humility, receive all the words of his mouth gladly as if they were golden, because they have the marrow and solidity of future eternal happiness. He who is truly learned has compassion on the weak; and he who loves Jesus exhorts the wavering soul to strength.

He who walks with a talkative man will not escape contention, and he who delights in lying will never be a friend of wisdom. He who is constantly occupied with external things will not make progress, and boasts without cause that he is a monk. The purpose of holiness is quickly corrupted when a monk more frequently wanders outside his cell with desire. He who leaves the secrecy of his cell without the elder’s command does not strive toward the perfection of a holy purpose. Beautiful is the company of good men and of those who fear God; and one who has grown accustomed to live with them will not feel confusion.

The heart of a young monk is a clean tablet, on which the conduct of elders is inscribed, never to be erased.
