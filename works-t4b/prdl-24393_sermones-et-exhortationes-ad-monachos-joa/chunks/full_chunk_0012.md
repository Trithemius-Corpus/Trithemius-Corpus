Homily III.

...for a crown, lest he be overcome, he abstains from everything, so that naked and free he may run the more readily. Therefore, if you desire to walk the way of God in true purity, if you wish to conquer the enemy in the arena of combat, cast away from yourself all the garments of cares; lay aside earthly hair and all that belongs to carnal pleasure. Whoever, with hair and clothing cast away from him, has learned to run naked brings nothing with him except his bare body by which he can be held by the adversary. For he who runs naked is stripped of every solicitude for things surrounding him, and thinks of nothing but victory; nor meanwhile does he care what is happening among wife or children. Nothing recalls him from his course; the swiftness of his companion does not frighten him away from winning the prize. If he contends among many, he hastens to the prize as though alone, panting as though he were certain.

Imitate him, soldier of Christ, in spiritual combat. Contemplate your Savior hanging naked on the cross, so that you may be able to overthrow your adversaries all the more easily, the less they see you possessing anything of worldly vanity. Therefore, naked, light, and unencumbered, fight on every side; naked and freed from all things, fly to heaven. Naked you came into this world; naked you will return to the earth. Thinking carefully on these things, my brothers, run in such a way that you may so obtain eternal things with all the saints. Amen. End of the homily.

What Is the Armor of Spiritual Warfare. Homily IV.

Most beloved brothers, today we have heard the holy Apostle writing in the epistle to the Ephesians: Put on the armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil, because our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against spiritual wickedness in the heavenly places. Therefore take up the armor of God, that you may be able to resist on the evil day, and, having been made perfect in all things, to stand.

He then adds what the armor of God is: Stand, therefore, with your loins girded in truth, and clothed in the breastplate of justice, and your feet shod in preparation for the gospel of peace. In all things taking up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one. Take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, praying at every time in the Spirit with all prayer and supplication, and in this very thing watching with all persistence and supplication for all the saints.

Likewise he says to the Thessalonians: But we who are of the day, let us be sober, clothed with the breastplate of faith and charity, and with the helmet, the hope of salvation. These are the arms, brothers, with which the soldiers of Christ, when clothed, defeat the fierce adversary. These are the badges of monastic virtue, without which no one attains victory over vices.

The equipment of human warfare, which most mortals pursue with excessive zeal, is the helmet, shield, breastplate, sword, dagger, cuirass, belt, lance, and many other things of this kind, with which men about to go forth to war are most carefully armed; and unless they have been most diligently protected over the whole body, they are certainly judged too unfit for battle. The arms of our spiritual warfare are faith, hope, charity, patience, humility, obedience, chastity, together with the holy virtues of the soul enumerated by our blessed father Benedict in the fourth chapter of the Rule, where he makes beautiful mention of the instruments of good works.

And would that, my brothers, we were as careful and diligent for these spiritual arms of our warfare as worldly men are seen to be solicitous for their corruptible armor. But this is what I say with too much sorrow: since we shamefully cast away the arms of salvation with which we ought to defeat all our adversaries, what remains except that we should fall before our enemy in the contest?

But perhaps you say: If Christian faith, as the Apostle testifies, belongs to the armor of spiritual warfare, we have certainly never cast it away. For we firmly believe whatever holy mother Church has set before us to be believed, and in that profession of faith we steadfastly intend to remain even to death. So be it. But what does Saint James say in his epistle? What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith but does not have works? Surely faith cannot save him? For just as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead. Therefore, whenever Scripture speaks of faith, it must not be understood as common and dead faith, but as faith formed by good works and illumined by the sweetness of divine love. For no one will be saved through faith alone.
