May they so weaken the body that they do not lift the mind into pride, lest the exercise of humility become material for vices. Fasting detests idleness; it does not love verbosity; it cherishes secrecy; it devotes itself to prayers and tears. Fasting, as Saint Ambrose bears witness, is the death of guilt, the destruction of sins, the remedy of salvation, the root of grace, the foundation of chastity. By this step Moses, the just man, came to God; by this gift Elijah, before he died, ascended in a chariot into heaven.

Those who walk in the Spirit love fasting, and those who profess chastity must necessarily flee an abundance of delicacies. For hunger is a friend to virginity and an enemy to lust. Satiety, however, betrays chastity and nourishes lust. Fasting extinguishes the burning arrows of the devil; persistence in prayer restrains them; and the continuation of vigils in the love of Christ repels all the snares of demons.

Hence Saint Leo the pope says in a certain sermon: What can be more effective than fasting, by whose observance we draw near to God and, resisting the devil, overcome his enticing snares? For fasting has always been food for virtue, and abstinence the nourishment of holy chastity. From abstinence proceed chaste thoughts; it gives birth to reasonable desires and modest counsels; and through voluntary afflictions the flesh dies to its concupiscences, while the spirit is renewed in virtues.

Therefore, my brothers, let us love fasting for the love of virtue, because of him who did not refuse to undergo death for us; and let us abstain not only from unlawful things, but also, as far as can be done, from permitted things, so that we who have offended in many ways against the commandments of salvation may, in recompense of satisfaction, restrain ourselves henceforth from every pleasure. Let us fast from every sin; let us take no food of malice; let us devour no banquets of pleasure; nor let us become drunk with the wine of luxury. Let us fast from all depravity; let us abstain from evil works; let us not speak harmful things; and let us always beware of noxious and perverse thoughts that separate us from God.

Let us always keep watch most earnestly in prayers to God, knowing that he whom we call upon in our prayers is everywhere most present to us. Let his praise always be in our mouth; and when we cannot praise his magnificence in speech, let us adore him in the heart with the desires of a most chaste love. For then our fasts are pleasing to him, when they have been adorned with holy works and continual prayers. For the prayer of the just man, as sacred Scripture bears witness, pierces the heavens, and it becomes the more effective the more it is found adorned with charity and fasting. Let us join to it, as it were, two wings, fasting and charity, and it will obtain for us from the Lord whatever we ask that is conducive to salvation.

On the Lenten Fast of Monks. Homily XVI.

Because, by the bounty of our Lord Jesus Christ, most dear brothers, we have begun the Lenten fast, reason requires that we say something briefly about its observance. For although you have many writings of the holy fathers concerning this most sacred exercise of fasts, nevertheless, because I have found by experience that my sermons among you are not wholly fruitless, I gladly share with your edification what the Lord has granted.

For, as blessed Jerome bears witness, the action of the living voice has within it a certain hidden operation, which in the Greek tongue is more elegantly called energy; and instruction spoken from the teacher’s mouth is stronger for persuasion. But I, set apart among you in the name of pastor alone, confess that I am young, unlearned, and lacking the experience and discretion necessary for the governance of souls. Hence it happens that I speak with a certain blush and shame: a young man among elders, ignorant among the learned, a disciple among masters.

Therefore I beg that none of you despise the simplicity of my speech; let no one take it badly that I speak, since, even if I am not fit for speaking, nevertheless by reason of the pastoral care committed to me I confess before God that I am bound by the duty of admonishing you. Your charity gives me boldness to speak, and the more ready you show yourselves to listen, the more you provoke me to speak. Nor will the labor be heavy for me, however great it may seem, by which the salvation of your souls is advanced.

Assist me with your prayers, so that the Lord Jesus may speak in my mouth, and may grant strength to my little talent for bringing forth into the open whatever can produce in you an increase of holy fervor.
