The demons also believe, and tremble. What then? Where are the works by which your faith is shown to have been formed in Christ? Faith is idle and dead when it is not commended by holy and necessary works. We believe in Christ: that is good, if we follow Christ’s examples. For if we do not follow Christ in works of piety, we flatter ourselves in vain about faith alone. You, I say, believe; the demons believe. Your faith, most Christian beloved, is a faith of love, not the faith of the devil. You believe rightly, if you love God with your whole heart and with all your strength. But if you do not truly love God, you have an idle and dead faith. The proof of love, as Gregory bears witness, is the showing of work in deed. For thus the Lord speaks in the Gospel: If anyone loves me, he will keep my word.

This, then, is true faith: ordered according to Christ’s teaching by good and holy works. If, by faith and at the same time by work, you believe in the Lord Jesus, surely you hold the shield of salvation in your hands; with it, by the Lord’s own gift, you will easily overcome all adversaries. Of this armor it is written: The saints through faith conquered kingdoms, worked justice, and obtained the promise. John likewise says: Everything that is born of God conquers the world; and this is the victory that conquers the world: our faith. And Peter, the prince of the apostles, admonishes us thus, saying: Brothers, be sober and keep watch, because your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour; resist him, strong in faith.

True faith and confidence in our Lord Jesus Christ is an invincible and most powerful armor, which none of the adversaries has ever been able to resist, as it is written in the book of Paralipomenon. King Jehoshaphat said to his army: Believe in the Lord your God, and you will be secure. In Ecclesiasticus also we read thus: You who fear the Lord, believe in him, and your reward will not be made void. And again: In your work believe from the faith of your soul, for this is the keeping of the commandments. Whoever believes God attends to the commandments; and whoever trusts in him will not be diminished.

Now therefore take care, dearest brothers, and with the highest zeal always strive that you may be able to hold in your hands this shield of faith, formed by holy works, against all assaults of your enemies, because one who is fortified by this impregnable shield is never conquered by the adversary. Confirming all this, Augustine too says from the words of the Lord: There are no greater riches, no treasures, no honors; no substance of this world is greater than the catholic faith. It saves sinful men, illumines the blind, heals the sick, baptizes catechumens, justifies the faithful, restores the penitent, increases the righteous, crowns martyrs, preserves virgins and widows and the married in chaste modesty, ordains clerics, consecrates priests, and places believers in the eternal inheritance with the holy angels.

Just as no appearance of beauty at all is visible in the root of a tree, and yet whatever beauty or grace there is in the tree proceeds from it, so too from the humility of faith whatever merit, whatever blessedness the soul is going to receive proceeds wholly from the foundation of faith. For, as the apostle says, whatever is not from faith is sin, because without faith it is impossible to please God. For the one who comes to God must believe that he exists, and that he is the rewarder of those who seek him. The more anyone loves, the more abundantly he advances in faith. This faith without charity is like a useless missile and a shield without a strap. O treasure more opulent than all things: faith, which, once formed in the mind of a human being, compares mortal things with immortal, conquers demons and all kingdoms, and bravely overcomes hard adversities.

Faith is the foundation of all virtues; without it no spiritual building stands. Faith gives light to the mind, and is the chief instrument of spiritual warfare. For without the shield of faith the devil is not conquered, nor is the impulse of wanton flesh overcome. Therefore let us consider, brothers, how necessary faith formed by charity is for our salvation, since in this warfare neither contest nor victory is granted to us, nor is confidence preserved, without it. For faith is the shield by which we most easily avert the hostile attacks on our salvation, standing beneath the yoke of divine love. For blessed Pope Gregory says: Then we are truly faithful if we fulfill in deeds what we promise in words. In this armor, then, faith is the shield of all good works, joining our acts to us.
