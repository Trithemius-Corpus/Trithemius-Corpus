On the recognition of spiritual grace.

...they despise all the consolations of the present life. The soul that has God present within itself through love despises all comforts of the flesh; it desires to come to its Lord through hard and bitter things. It does not love the riches of this world; it spurns transitory honors and dignities; it resolutely contemns the pleasures of the flesh. It meditates always on the law of the Lord and never forgets the most bitter Passion of Jesus.

Who among you, brothers, is there who does these things, who detests all the little consolations of this age, who thinks of nothing else, seeks nothing, desires nothing, except the charity of God? Whoever has obtained this sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, if he perseveres to the end of life, will be saved.

The eighth indication of a good course, and the true sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, we receive when we humbly conform our wills in all things to the divine ordinances. This degree of spiritual progress separates the monk from all cares of the world and calls him wholly to inward things. For his will is peacefully subjected, by the affection of the mind, to the divine will, and is separated far from all desires that war against the spirit.

Whoever has received this gift of the grace of the Holy Spirit should first give thanks with humility, and should not vainly exalt himself because of the gift; rather, the more he has advanced, the more he should learn to humble himself in all things. Often, however, a man deserves to lose the grace of God because he neglects to preserve it in the vessel of true humility. Therefore almighty God sometimes withdraws for a short time from those making progress the grace of sensible devotion, so that humility may persevere in the mind and the fervor of desire may burn more strongly for seeking further. But since there are many causes in human beings for which the grace of God is sometimes withdrawn, it will be worthwhile for them to be stated in order.

First, then, the grace of God is withdrawn from a person because of sin, when the conscience is stained by the ugliness of vices. For our God, as Scripture says, is jealous: either he alone is loved, or he is not loved with any companion. Therefore, as often as the mind turns back in disordered desire either to itself or to anything else, it is alienated and removed from its source, which is God; and so it deserves to lose the grace it had, because by consenting to sin it was unwilling to keep that grace inviolate.

Hence the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, exhorts us not to receive the grace of God in vain. For it is then received fruitlessly and in vain when the mind of the recipient is made unworthy by the filth of vices. The little vessel into which we receive the grace of God is our conscience. When it is cleansed from vices and adorned with holy virtues, it becomes a worthy dwelling of the Holy Spirit and produces great progress in us. But if the vessel of our conscience has been defiled by the corruption of sin, immediately the Spirit of God withdraws with all the gifts of grace.

Is not our Lord Jesus Christ the lover and faithful bridegroom of the soul, which he purchased for himself by the outpouring of his own blood? With what face, with what rashness, then, can you, O man, despise so great a lover and savior of yours, and love anything else in this world? God, who is your creator, loves you supremely and by his death has freed you from everlasting destruction. What will you repay him, O mortal man, from whom you have received such great benefits? He asks nothing of you, seeks nothing, demands nothing, except gratitude, affection, and charity. Guard innocence and purity of conscience, lest you lose the impulse of grace.

The second cause, indeed, on account of which grace is withdrawn from a person is called the pride of ingratitude. God, as Scripture testifies, resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. By the just judgment of God, the grace of the Holy Spirit is lost whenever the one who has it is lifted up into pride. For everyone who humbles himself will be exalted, and he who exalts himself will be humbled. Hence almighty God again says: Upon whom will my Spirit rest, except upon the humble and quiet, and the one who trembles at my words? The more anyone has humbled himself in true purity of conscience for the love of Christ, the more fit he will be to receive the grace of the Holy Spirit. But as often as the mind is raised up in pride, the spirit of devotion is deservedly taken away, as from an unclean vessel.

Therefore, my brothers, let no one grow proud over the grace of the Holy Spirit that he has received; let no one vainly exalt himself, because unless we are humble we shall by no means be able to share in the mercies of the Lord. For, as the holy lover of Christ, Paul, says: he who thinks himself to be something when he is nothing deceives himself.
