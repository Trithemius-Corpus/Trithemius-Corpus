Ottomar Luscinius of Strasbourg, on the homilies of Johannes Trithemius to monks: a decastich

If for anyone it is not enough, Christ, to follow
Your gentle laws of justice, unless he also recalls the monks:
Serapion, Paul, and Anthony, who long ago explored the path,
A path rough with countless hard places;
How each strove to show, beneath a harsh cloak, a body tormented,
And did not allow down to be in his bed;
How fasts drive out the swollen pride of the soul,
And brief, half-sleeping rest sustains the limbs:
These men, silent, taught this by their deeds; but fitter than they is
Trithemius, who is strong in tongue and hand.

With the grace and privilege of the Imperial Majesty.

Maximilian, by divine clemency favoring him elected Emperor of the Romans, ever Augustus, and king of Germany, Hungary, Dalmatia, Croatia, etc., archduke of Austria, duke of Burgundy and Brabant, and count palatine, etc., to the honorable and devout man beloved by us, Johannes Trithemius, abbot of St. James the Greater at Wurzburg: imperial grace and every good thing.

Since you have humbly explained to us that you have composed various editions of both old and new authors, gathered, not without great labor, vigils, and diligence, and that you desired with all your mind that they be brought forth into the light; and since our beloved master Johannes Haselberg of Constance, beloved also of the Holy Roman Empire, has offered with a most ready mind to be the minister of this undertaking, and has therefore procured at great expense the forms by which he may print the aforesaid works: therefore, wishing provision to be made for the indemnity of the said Master Johannes and for the adornment of your honor, lest your works, composed by you with great labors and vigils, lie hidden as if covered with rust, but rather shine forth more and more from day to day, we have therefore given and granted, and by the tenor of the present letters do give and grant, to the aforesaid Master Johannes Haselberg of Constance full power and authority of every kind.

We decree that he may print, and sell after printing, wherever he pleases, whatever among the treatises and little works has been issued by you, the aforesaid Trithemius, abbot of St. James the Greater at Wurzburg, and has been reviewed and approved by the honorable and faithful man beloved by us, Conrad Peutinger, doctor and our counselor. And we forbid all and singular typographers and printers of books, of whatever condition, under the penalty written below, commanding most strictly that, for ten years after such works or similar ones have been published by the said Master Johannes, they not dare or presume to print, publish, or publish what has been printed by others, buy, or sell them throughout the whole Roman Empire and in our hereditary lands.

We further command all and singular our beloved faithful subjects and those of the Holy Roman Empire, of whatever status, condition, dignity, and office, whether ecclesiastical or secular, under penalty of our indignation and the gravest indignation of the Holy Roman Empire, and of ten marks of pure gold, to be applied, as often as the contrary has been done, one part to our treasury and the other part to the aforesaid Master Johannes Haselberg, that they assist the same Master Johannes of Constance with their favor and aid in their places and lands, and not permit the said works and treatises to be printed, or those printed elsewhere to be sold. Rather, they are immediately to declare in our name that all who presume to do anything against this our grace and prohibition have incurred the sentence of this indignation of ours and of the Holy Roman Empire, carrying out our express will in this matter.

In testimony whereof, by these letters furnished with the impression of our seal on the back. Given in our town of Innsbruck on the tenth day of the month of November, in the year of the Lord 1513, in the twenty-ninth year of our Roman reign and the twenty-fifth of our Hungarian reign.

By the king personally.

At the command of the lord emperor himself.

N. Ziegler, and the chancery, through Paul Oberstein.
