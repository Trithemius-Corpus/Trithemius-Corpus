*On fleeing the company of the wicked: Homily eleven.*

...makes progress; those whom evil habit corrupts from the beginning of their training. Nothing, however, is worse than evil habit. He is the monk who neglects to find anything new in all the days of his life, and passes his time in sloth. For Scripture says: A young man walking according to his own way, even when he is old, will not depart from it. Therefore I say to you, venerable elders, I beseech you, I warn you, and with all the urgency I can, by the mercy of God, I ask you: from now on live in such a way, conduct yourselves in such a way, walk in such a way, that your manners may befit the years of old age, and that you, who have already advanced far into age, may offer an example of holiness for the training of the younger men. Without doubt whatever fault is found in the morals of the younger men is charged to you. For the younger men look to you; therefore you ought to go before them in word and deed; you ought to show yourselves to them as an example of complete perfection, so that, when the fullness of every perfect virtue is seen in you, they may be compelled, as though by a certain impulse of reverence, to imitate your holiness. For the conduct of elders gives the younger men an example of living, just as the proverb says: What the elders have sung, the young men have learned.

But you younger men, show reverence with humility to your elders, considering their gray hair; and those whom you know to be more observant in keeping regular discipline, rejoice to have them as teachers by keeping their company. Carefully avoid those who either walk restlessly or observe the purity of regular discipline too negligently. Not men who are called elders are more worthy of imitation by the young; years do not make a monk venerable, but good morals, together with prudence in the love of God, establish him as worthy of reverence. Therefore, whoever among you, dearest brothers, desires to be a true lover of God, whoever longs to make progress in holy monastic conduct, let him always flee the company of undisciplined brothers, as he has professed; and let him seek the companionship of those who support his purpose in the good. For this reason it says in the Psalm: With the holy you will be holy, and with the innocent man you will be innocent, and with the chosen you will be chosen, and with the perverse you will be perverted.

For evil company has harmed many, and one who delights to dwell with the reprobate will not be free from the contagion of sin. Indeed, everyone who lives among the wicked and reprobate must necessarily have the integrity of his life damaged, at least somewhat, by their perversity. For either he does not correct them and becomes like them and evil, or he will hate them and be an enemy to many, and for that reason iniquity necessarily follows. But if, overcome by the multitude of the wicked, he has once begun to delight in their company, he is already corrupted in three ways, and little by little he will be corrupted further. If, however, he detests the works of the wicked, he will suffer wearisome inconvenience, and his desire for progress toward the better will be slower in proportion as he is more troubled by the company of the perverse.

Yet I give thanks to almighty God for you, because even if not all of you are as fervent in the love of God as I would wish, nevertheless, as I trust, you all live far removed from the stain of sins and from the rashness of pride. And my exhortations and fatherly admonitions, which I make to you daily as I am bound to do, you receive willingly, although not all of you yet show them perfectly in deed. But I hope in the Lord Jesus that he will complete in you the good which he has begun, so that my joy may be fulfilled, and I may see you all burning with one purpose of zeal and one fire of holy fervor, delighting and being exercised in the workshop of divine love.

But since some have not yet taken up the fullness of the amendment I desire, I am compelled by just necessity to admonish those who are making progress, that, rising up with a manly spirit, they should flee the wicked societies of the sluggish. For as yet the fire of divine love is small in their hearts; as yet the fire of holy fervor burns new and weak, and unless it is nourished by daily kindling, it will easily suffer loss. Therefore again and again I warn you who have begun to receive the fervor of inward love: continue steadfastly with the spirit by which, inspired by the Lord, you are directed; and those whom you find opposed to you in this holy journey, whether by deed or speech, avoid as deadly poison. For just as water extinguishes the fire onto which it is poured, so the beginning of a holy purpose in novices is harmed by the examples of the lukewarm.

Beware of conversation with those men who, as I see, are negligent in observing the commandments of God and monastic purity, because, since they are stirred by carnal passions, out of the abundance of the heart they speak nothing but vain and carnal things. For wicked conversations corrupt the good morals of the young. All their pursuits are empty, and their words are useless, indeed pestiferous; being carnally minded, they do not know the exercises of divine love. For just as one diseased sheep gradually corrupts the whole flock, so one slothful and perverse monk in a congregation often becomes the cause of many transgressions. As, according to holy Christopher, a little leaven that remains corrupts the whole mass of flour with which it is mixed by its smell, so young and new monks who associate with lukewarm and slothful elders are imbued with their depraved morals and never thereafter are restored to the integrity of a holier way of life.

Therefore I say to you elders: be a pattern of living for the young by good works and chaste and always modest speech, so that whenever they are corrected for their faults, they may not defend themselves by the examples of their elders. For it is utterly shameful for elders to be culpable in those things for which the younger men are deservedly rebuked. I beseech you, elders, live in such a way that before you the younger men may presume to say or do nothing that seems reprehensible. Let the wicked fear you as judges, not as men acting in the place of patrons, because as many as sin by following your examples are reckoned to have perished through your instruction. Let there be among you no one lukewarm or slothful, because desires kill the lazy man, and vanity destroys every idle monk. Let there be among you no verbose man, no wanderer, no gadabout, no murmurer or restless man, because whoever delights in such things and pursuits destroys himself by evil action and kills his brother by his examples.

Let there be among you no mocker of the simple, no despiser of the humble, no rash and censorious man, because one who is ready to mock will be consumed in his own folly and will not be glorious. He opens his mouth against the humble, always seeking quarrels, and rejoices that he is talkative about the deeds of others. He will not prosper in his counsel; and if he is raised to some honor, he will quickly fall into the depths. His pride is hateful to God, because, though he is foolish and senseless, he boasts that he is learned and wise. Soon his foolishness will be laid bare, and grave punishment will seize him inwardly.
