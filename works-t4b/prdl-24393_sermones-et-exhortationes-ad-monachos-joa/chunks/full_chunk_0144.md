On the knowledge of spiritual grace.

Third, if you do not wish to lose the grace of inward compunction, you must love the pursuit of solitude. For divine Scripture says: Upon whom will my spirit rest, except upon the humble and devout? And likewise it says elsewhere: In silence and in hope shall be your strength. For the grace of compunction is generally lost quickly when one does not love the leisure of solitude. Therefore let the monk remain gladly shut up in his cell with himself, if he wishes to possess the grace of God.

Fourth, one must take the greatest care not to despise in one’s heart any persons, however evil, because the grace of compunction is withdrawn by the just judgment of God whenever one does not guard against rash judgment of one’s neighbor. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned.

Fifth, the monk who does not wish to lose the grace of inward devotion should see to it that he exercises himself in the cultivation of all the virtues. Let him always keep vigilant in the fear of God; let him occupy himself in the exercise of divine love; let him be constantly intent on good works; and let him remain devout in the memory of the Lord’s passion, so far as he can, at all times. For diligent meditation on Christ’s passion strengthens human beings against all the vices and preserves the grace of compunction.

Sixth, be constant in holy prayers, because it is not enough to keep the grace of compunction if one interrupts the practice of prayer. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: Watch and pray, that you may not enter into temptation. Divine help is near in your calling upon him. Whoever neglects to pray loses grace.

But if you lose the grace of devotion in any way, you will quickly recover it by the following remedies. First, consider that without the grace of God you cannot make progress toward salvation. Know that in all good things you are about to do you most greatly need his mercy. Therefore recognize that you are unworthy of the grace of God for doing good, although you are unworthy because of the multitude of your lost years. Call upon him while he is near, and ask that every time which he grants you free from the crowd of exterior occupations may be spent usefully and fruitfully. The grace of God is necessary for you in doing good; it is acquired by breadth of charity, preserved by humility, and recovered by the practice of saving penance. It is with you when you have long been with him in good will. Therefore you will be able to recover the lost grace of devotion whenever you subject yourself to God in true humility.

Second, for the recovery of the lost grace of compunction, it seems necessary that you lift yourself inwardly against yourself and conform your will in all things to the divine ordering. Since we seek God’s will in the prayer of compunction, it is fitting that in that very prayer we should always be in agreement with him by the mind and affection of a willing heart.

Third, whoever desires to recover the lost grace of devotion must strongly condemn every pleasure of this world for love of God. For it cannot happen that he receives the spirit of inward compunction when he fills the dwelling of his heart with earthly joys and pleasures. Therefore you must cast aside every consolation of the present life if you wish to have the spirit of divine love and the charisms that follow it.

Fourth, you are commanded to withdraw yourself from the vain cares and occupations of this world, and to occupy your mind delightfully at all times in the service of God. For, as the Lord says, no one can serve God and mammon; and whoever truly desires the familiar presence of God in the truth of the heart must necessarily despise all occupations of the world.

Fifth, we advise that you remember always to occupy your heart in meditation on Christ’s passion, because there is no other preparation more suitable for recovering the grace of compunction, nor any manner of prayer more effective for hoping for whatever is sought from the Lord.

Sixth, you should strive to cleanse your conscience daily through true penance; and whenever you have fallen into any fault, you should soon repent with a contrite heart. If you take care to keep these things with a good will, then, with God taking pity, you will recover the lost grace of compunction.

On the nine degrees of ascent.

Sermon VII.

We shall hasten, very dear brothers, to set before you the nine degrees by which the ascent of human beings into heaven is made. And although one is higher than another, nevertheless each is directed toward the kingdom of heaven and distinguishes the merits of those who ascend according to their merit. Therefore attend most diligently to what is said here, and consider in which degree of spiritual progress you stand. All the saints, whoever have come to the sight of God, have ascended by the degrees of this ladder. The more anyone has advanced in the love of God, the higher he has already ascended. The greatest degree, because it is lowest, is the first, and it is more remote from the sublimity of the heavenly fatherland. Those placed in it are said to withdraw from vices and to begin to make progress in the fear of God; neglecting venial sins, they withdraw from mortal sins. Indeed, they flee the heavier sins, being raised to a certain exercise of the virtues; yet through the weakness of a bad habit they do not attend to the smaller sins. Hence they often fall, through frailty, into the pit of mortal sin, because they have not guarded against venial sins as occasions of graver ones.
