Homily XXV. Fol. LVII.

Act now, my brothers, so that you may have consciences pure from every crime; and if anyone feels himself burdened in some fault, let him return without delay through true penitence to necessary purity. For unless you have consciences clean and good at all times, you live as apostates, not as monks.

On Continual Preparation for Death. Homily XXV.

The highest philosophy for cloistered religious, my dearest brothers, is continual meditation on death. Hence a certain wise man, considering the usefulness of this philosophy, admonished every mortal, saying: “Remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.” Meditation on death draws people back from the pleasures of the flesh and brings contempt for all corruptible things. Meditation on death despises present things through voluntary poverty and drives the mind toward desire for future things. Meditation on death banishes pride far away and plants true humility in the heart of the one meditating. Meditation on death makes the monk solicitous about the observance of regular discipline and never permits him to be idle. Meditation on death cuts away all the vanities of the world and preserves the mind in the highest temperance. Meditation on death makes one despise the vain and transitory honors of the world and think of nothing except the future judgment of God. Meditation on death does not fear the poverty of this life, because it longs with its whole desire for everlasting riches. Meditation on death most patiently tolerates all the adversities of this world for the love of Christ, because it hopes for nothing except the heavenly reward. Meditation on death makes the monk humble and obedient in all the commandments of righteousness. Meditation on death makes the monk quiet and stable in the congregation, and well concordant with all in Christ. Meditation on death makes the monk quiet and peaceable, and tranquil without any disturbance of mind. Meditation on death drives out all dissoluteness and frivolity, and preserves the monk mature and wholesomely sorrowful in all things. Meditation on death makes the monk solitary and continually devout in holy prayers. Meditation on death hastens penitence and brings true compunction. Meditation on death withdraws desire from every vanity and keeps the conscience serene and most pure. Meditation on death makes a person despise all the consolations of this world and prepare daily for departure. Meditation on death teaches one to flee idleness and makes a person keep watch continually in the fear of the Lord. Meditation on death does injury to no one, because it always fears the strict accounting of judgment. Meditation on death softens anger and all disordered motions, because when a person thinks that he will soon die, nothing external disturbs the mind. Meditation on death makes the heart contrite and humble, because intent remembrance of future things permits no one to be proud. Continual meditation on death makes departure safe at the last, because it has long made the one who has meditated prepared and ready. Meditation on death especially befits cloistered religious and monks, whom renunciation of all earthly things and regular profession itself have already made dead and buried to the world. Meditation on death makes the monk run prudently in the course of spiritual warfare and daily renew the purpose of holy fervor. Continual meditation on death is very useful to cloistered religious, because it powerfully represses the vices of the flesh and grants no entrance to perverse thoughts of the mind. For the holy bishop Augustine says: Fear of future death necessarily shakes the mind and, like a nail of the flesh, fastens all the motions of pride to the wood of the cross. And the holy champion of the Church, Jerome, says: Nothing will benefit you so much for temperance in all things as frequent thought of the shortness of life; its end is certain, but nothing is more uncertain than its hour. Therefore meditation on death corrects all intemperance and sets in order the character of the one meditating, stable in beautiful variety. Meditation on death gives a person true wisdom and perfects the highest philosophy among cloistered religious. For Plato too held the opinion that the whole life of wise men is a meditation on death, because we are all born on this condition, that we should depart again from here through death.
