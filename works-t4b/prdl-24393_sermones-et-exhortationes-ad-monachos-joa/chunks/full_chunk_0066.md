He disgracefully expelled our first parents from the kingdom of paradise and innocence, who did not devour a bird, a fish, a calf, or a pig, so that you might judge food to be the fault, but presumed against God’s commandment to take a very lowly apple, so that you may know that it is not the food but the appetite that is liable to the vice of sin. For it can often happen that a person takes more splendid foods without fault while he regulates his appetite, and that another takes meaner foods not without guilt of conscience, when he exceeds the measure of temperance through immoderation. Therefore let none of you consider himself safe from the vice of gluttony on the grounds that he eats neither lavish nor delicate foods, since not the food but the appetite for food is held at fault. For it is quite possible for one who eats beans and vegetables to contract the guilt of sin, and for another who takes the most delicate birds as food not to sin. Hence one of the holy fathers says: “A belly filled with any foods produces seedbeds of lust; nor can a mind, choked by the weight of foods, avail to govern the rudders of discretion.” For not only overindulgence in wine customarily makes the mind drunk, but also excess of every food makes it unsteady.
