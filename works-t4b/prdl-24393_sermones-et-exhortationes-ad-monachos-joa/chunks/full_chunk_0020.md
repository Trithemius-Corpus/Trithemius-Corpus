Homily III. Fol. XI.

...words; but those who are unlearned will die in poverty of heart. What is more pitiable than an unlearned priest, who, even if he does not have the office of preaching, is nevertheless bound by reason of the order he has received to knowledge of the Scriptures?

For God wills every person to fulfill his holy will; no one who is ignorant of it can do this. First, therefore, the Lord commands that you learn what is pleasing to him; second, that you do it. How, then, will you think ignorance of the law excusable, through which you have first become transgressors of the will of God?

Why do you think almighty God handed over to human beings the volumes of the Scriptures? Was it so that they might be read and understood by them, or so that people might despise reading them? Surely the Scriptures of God’s commandments were given to mortals first to be read, and afterward to be fulfilled in deed. But you, who with deliberate mind wish to be ignorant of the Scriptures: how can you do his will, which they disclose? Or how will you be able to be doers of the law, when you have not first been hearers? For it can happen that someone who knows the will of God either observes it or neglects it; but it cannot happen that someone who is ignorant of it fulfills it. Indeed, someone will more easily fail to do the good he knows than carry out in deed what he does not know. No one does the good he does not know.

Saint Augustine says of the ignorant in a certain place: Not everyone who is ignorant is free from fault. That ignorant person can be excused from punishment who did not find what he might learn. But those cannot be forgiven who, having persons from whom they might learn, did not apply themselves to instruction.

Saint Leo the pope also says this: If ignorance seems intolerable in laymen, how much more in those who are placed over others; nor is it worthy of excuse or pardon.

Therefore, brothers, ignorance of the divine Scriptures does not make you excused before God, but doubly guilty, since you wish to be wholly ignorant of the will of God so that it need not be done by you. For a person ought not be ignorant of the divine will, since he is placed in the world for this: that through illumination of understanding and purity of affection he may merit the eternal enjoyment of God’s vision. And so knowledge of God, and also of oneself, is necessary for every mortal unto salvation; no one will find it without knowledge of the Scriptures. For just as from knowledge of oneself there comes to human beings fear of God, and from knowledge of him love is born, so from ignorance of oneself comes pride, and from neglected knowledge of God come contempt and despair of salvation.

Nothing is more unhappy than an unlearned monk who either neglects or despises the study of the holy Scriptures, because he can never stand pure within himself in true tranquility of heart, but is compelled by his own restlessness, contrary to the integrity of monastic life, to occupy his mind with outward and worldly things. We see among you some who are ignorant of the Scriptures, unstable in mind, undisciplined and wandering; their ignorance is the more impure, the less they are inclined toward discipline. It is shameful indeed not to know what one is ordered to know; more shameful not to learn; but most shameful of all to despise it.

And there are among you some who do not know saving knowledge; there are also some who neglect to learn. What if I add that there are still others who utterly despise the knowledge of the divine Scriptures? And yet I am not lying: you know that what I say is true. For some of you have bitterly opposed my studies, even to the point of envy, quibbling that they would rather have a plowman than an orator as their superior. Have they not also tried to draw the visitators over to their opinion, wishing to lead me [unclear] into renunciation of the Scriptures, so that they might make me a little rustic and a keeper of pigs rather than of souls? But those who hinder the divine [unclear] strive in vain; at God’s urging I have resolved, as long as I live, to set all the honors and riches of the world after the study of the Scriptures.

I am your abbot for as long as he who called me wills it. If one of the two must be done, I would rather give up the abbacy than renounce the holy study of the Scriptures. For I know truly that nothing more wholesome can happen in this life to a cloistered man than if he begins truly to love the divine Scriptures. Whoever among you wishes to follow this instruction of mine will have me alike as guide and companion on the way. But whoever despises following me as I learn will nevertheless be compelled, even unwillingly, to hear me as I teach.

I have said these things against those who, savoring earthly and perishable things, cast away from themselves the fear of the Lord; who, ignorant of almost everything, despise learning. I know the face of my flock, and I believe I am not wholly ignorant of the several inclinations of your hearts. Therefore I will not cease to exhort you to the holy studies of the Scriptures, knowing that a lover of sacred reading cannot be a worker of carnal vices.
