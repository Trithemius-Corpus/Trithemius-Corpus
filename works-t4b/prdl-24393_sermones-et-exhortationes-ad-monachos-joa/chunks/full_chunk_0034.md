Let us therefore pray with the highest fervor of spirit, and let us live with as much purity of conscience as we can, since that prayer is perfect and pleasing to God in which both the cause and the tongue cry out, whose deed and speech agree with the petition, whose life and thought bring support to the prayers.

For without the justice of truth every prayer will be fruitless, since we know that God does not hear sinners, and that salvation is far from sinners, as Scripture says. Whoever turns his ears away so as not to hear the laws, his prayer will be abominable. The holy prophet also says in the psalm: If I have regarded iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me. For just as no medicine is effective in a wound unless the iron is first drawn out, so the prayer of one in whose mind deceit still remains, or hatred in his breast, is of no effect.

But in order that God may hear your prayer, first of all conscience must be cleansed from every stain of mortal sin, and the mind must undertake a true purpose of amendment. To pray much and at the same time offend in many things: what is this other than to mock God? For prayer is hindered in two ways so that it is not heard by the Lord: one, when the person praying still commits evil by sinning; the other, if for God’s sake he does not forgive his debtor the debt.

You therefore, who desire to have the fruit of your prayers, who wish to come with the penitents to God’s mercy, take care that you do nothing evil against God’s will, and diligently see to it that you truly forgive from the heart everything committed against you by human beings. For you know that the Lord said: Forgive, and you shall be forgiven. But after you have cleansed your conscience from evil and forgiven every offense to your neighbor, you will then be fit to pray, if in the fervor of a right purpose you have been ordered toward God.

Now then approach the Lord confidently for prayer; prostrate yourself humbly in his sight, and pray with purity of heart and fervor of spirit. Do not cease from prayer as long as you live in the flesh. Let your prayer be pure, simple, clear, and plain; let it be full of seriousness, weight, and dignity, adorned with true humility. Do not ask for anything in prayer that is contrary to the salvation of the soul, nor should you beg too importunately for a temporal thing, since it is shameless to ask God for riches, which furnish the greatest occasion for pride and often defile Christian purity.

It is not shameless if you ask for daily bread, since it is one thing to ask for matter from which you may become proud, and another to ask for the necessary food by which you may live. Do not justify yourself while praying, lest, like the Pharisee, you go out condemned; nor ask anything from the Lord as though owed to your merits. Rather humble yourself in all things, so that you may deserve to receive grace. Remember the humility of the publican, and pray with such great humility that with him you may find forgiveness of sins and justification of the soul. For God’s grace is richer than the prayer of the suppliant, and he grants us more when we pray than we ask.

You will not pray with a loud cry of the voice to him who knows the hidden things of the heart, but in fervor of spirit and with the inward cry of the mind you will strike the ears of the divine majesty. Nor should you prolong the length of words before him who is Spirit, because God will be appeased not by much speaking but by the pure mind of the one praying. At the time of prayer cast away from yourself all malice and disturbance of heart, and, as we often say, forgive if you have anything against your neighbor. For unless for love of the Savior you forgive your neighbor every debt, you will obtain nothing from the Lord by your prayers.

There is said to be a certain kind of serpent which, when it wishes to go into the water to drink, first vomits out all its venom. You too, monk, imitate this serpent in this respect: vomit out all the venom of wrath and bitterness, forgiving your brother the hundred denarii, so that the Lord may forgive you the ten thousand talents. As you desire the Lord to be toward you, so you also should show yourself toward your fellow servant.

Call upon the Lord with your whole heart and you will find him. Let your prayer be burning and most pure, so that your mind may be joined to him in his most holy love. Strive for this throughout your whole life, that at all times you may offer pure prayer to the Lord God. Never cease from prayer, nor despair of God’s mercy. Even what he is going to grant, he sometimes delays; he does not take it away. He does not act with fickleness, nor is he easily moved in his sentence; what he has promised is fixed. If you pray insistently, you will see a miracle. Remember that you stand under the gaze of God, who understands all the secrets of hearts and knows the hidden things of the mind. Therefore stand watchfully in his sight, who has known all things, and pray with as much fervor of spirit as you can,

That One Must Pray Without Interruption.

so that you may deserve to obtain mercy. For by holy prayer the conscience of the penitent is cleansed; and whoever raises his heart to the Lord in true purity, his prayer does not return empty. O, dearest brothers, if you knew how pleasing to God are the prayers of a pure mind, and how much usefulness they confer upon the soul, you would allow no time to pass without prayers.

For if our prayer is pure and burning in fervor of spirit, it is a most faithful messenger to God; it speaks to God on our behalf, and while it prays it obtains everything necessary for our salvation. Therefore I admonish you and exhort you in true charity to pray without interruption and never cease from the fervor of the Holy Spirit. Let there be no time empty for you, or idle in sluggishness of mind. In the fear of the Lord, I beg you, always be watchful and solicitous, so that you may be able bravely to conquer and overcome the temptations that threaten.

As soon as foul thoughts begin to strike the mind, run back to the practice of prayer, and they will be wholly unable to prevail against you. For prayer is a great fortification of the soul; in its presence all the schemes of the opposing side fail. Through the purest prayers all useful things are granted to us by God, and all adversities are driven far away. By pure prayers the mind is powerfully kindled in the love of God, and whatever slight stain comes upon it is cleansed by praying.

By holy and continual prayers the world is conquered; the plagues of the soul are healed by devout petitions; by tears and weeping the devil is overcome; and he for whom a thousand cohorts are not enough for battle is weakened by the prayer of a single poor man. Just as wax melts and dissolves before the face of fire, so at the fervor of pure prayer all the power of demons, crushed, is enfeebled.

If a foul thought has been sent into your mind by demons, immediately, with the sign of the cross impressed upon your forehead, say: Save me, Jesus Christ, mediator of God and human beings, and drive away from me all the wickedness of the ancient enemy through your most innocent passion. What can be shorter than this prayer, or what more powerful? For as soon as it has been uttered with fervor of mind against a demon, whether present or by suggestion, every scheme of iniquity withdraws.

For the power of pure prayer is very great, since it both penetrates heaven and by its steadfastness defeats the powers of the air. Prayer illumines the mind, purifies the affection, obtains forgiveness of sins, and strengthens the spirit in the love of God against all adversaries. Nothing so raises the mind from earthly things to the love of heavenly things; nothing so quickly and powerfully destroys the pleasures of the flesh; nothing so illumines the understanding; nothing, too, so greatly confounds the adversary of truth as pure prayer, which is poured out to God with the fervor of divine love and adorned by the testimony of a good conscience.

If you desire to be terrible to demons, if you wish to be a friend of God, keep your conscience pure and never interrupt spiritual prayer. For devout prayer can do all things if it is accompanied by uprightness of faith and purity of life. Prayer puts demons to flight, enfeebles the strength of malignant spirits, heals the soul languishing in tepidity, and confirms the weak and infirm spirit in the fear of God.

Pure prayer makes one near to God, and the one whom it had received as a servant it makes to be God’s friend. Pure prayer, fervent in the love of God, penetrates heaven and wonderfully illumines the understanding of the one praying; it closes hell, opens paradise, and leads in the souls of the faithful. For Truth does not lie, who in the holy Gospel promises, saying: Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and it will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives, and whoever seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened.

Who among you has ever turned to the Lord with his whole heart and prayed, and has not been heard? Or who has called upon the Lord, who cast down Amalek trusting in his own strength, not by fighting with iron but by praying with holy prayers? For the continual petition of the just person has great power, and the prayer of one who humbles himself penetrates the clouds. Elijah was a man subject to suffering like us, and he prayed in prayer that it might not rain upon the earth, and it did not rain for three years and six months. And again he prayed, and heaven gave rain, and the earth gave its fruit.

By prayer and tears King Hezekiah obtained fifteen years of longer life, and by his prayers changed the prophet’s sentence. By prayers holy men became renowned for miracles, and by supplicating the Lord they recalled the dead to life.
