On the true obedience of monks.

For the monk who, for God's sake, of his own free will binds himself to perpetual obedience in religion, if he lives rightly according to his vow, certainly undertakes the next thing to martyrdom. Obedience, then, is martyrdom without the shedding of blood; and voluntarily to subject oneself to a man for God's sake is a great torment of the flesh. The soul that is firmly loved in humility and obedience will without doubt attain the crowns of martyrdom. It is a hard work and an exceedingly laborious contest for a free man to despise himself, and to resign himself wholly, and his own will, forever under the command and power of another. But let us look with faithful eyes upon the reward of this contest, and we shall reckon all the hard and rough things of this life to be like a certain childish game, which already is striving toward blessedness and afterwards will appear as nothing. For, as the apostle, lover of Christ, says: "The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which shall be revealed in us." Therefore the crown that once gave the saints martyrdom for Christ, true humble obedience will prepare today for the lovers of Christ.

For the monk who is truly humble and obedient for Christ dies in secret without fear, since one who has served faithfully under the command of his superior is freed from every demand of accounting. For he who lives at the nod of his superior and walks in all the commandments in humility can commit nothing that should be carried before the judge. He is defended by the testimony of the abbot, under whose direction he lived rightly without complaint.

Consider, brothers, the law of obedience according to which you promised to live, and know that what had been voluntary was made necessary when you solemnly consented in the profession of monastic consecration. It is no longer lawful for you to withdraw from your holy purpose, nor can you revoke the vow once uttered. If you do everything according to the holy Gospel of Christ and the rule of father Benedict that you vowed, let there be no doubt of your salvation: you will be blessed, and you will receive from the Lord the reward due to the saints. But if, forgetful of your promises, you violate the vow of holy purity made to God; if, after spiritual consecration, you live according to the flesh, then your condition is now such by your own law that you are most certainly to be condemned by the Lord, since no one under Christ the judge of all can mock God, to whom you consecrated yourselves.

For it is much better to promise nothing than to violate a vow. Therefore the loss of your salvation is set in this one thing: that what you once promised to God according to the rule of Saint Benedict, you should not persevere in until death in all purity of mind and body. For a Christian man who has not made the vow of monastic profession can be saved by living according to the Gospel; but one who has once become a monk will not be saved unless he has observed until death all the things required for monastic life.

Listen then, O monk, to the word of Benedict to the end. For if you live as a monk, O monk, you will be saved; but if you live otherwise, you will not be saved, but will receive your place among apostate spirits, because you have mockingly despised the God whose servant you are. Now therefore call yourself back to yourself; examine carefully the perfection of your whole life, how you have fulfilled the vows that you promised and assumed. For the third fearful sentence of first examination now constrains not only the conscience but also the judgments of others. Always think on that soul in which, for the sake of the outward monastery, the first entrance was made, so that whenever you find yourself growing sluggish from the fervor of holiness, you may be able to renew in your mind the purpose of your first beginning. And so you will be able to be a holy and good monk, if in all things and always everywhere you keep obedience and humility.

The monk's true obedience gathers all the virtues into itself, and humility preserves them gathered. Forget yourself, and be ready to obey the command of your superior; and however far you advance in the arena of holy struggle, do not lift up your soul in any estimate of perfection. For what are our righteousnesses before God, and what good can we do that he has not given? For God, who is all in all, is himself sufficient and alone the perfection of our goods; he has no need of ours, since he is the highest and unchangeable good. In comparison with him, all our justifications are less than nothing. Therefore the monk placed under an abbot, truly obedient in humility, walks securely; and if he perseveres to the end, fighting and conquering, he will be gloriously rewarded.

Your humility of obedience truly delights me, brothers, when you present yourselves as faithful to the almighty Lord; nor in this matter do I regard myself as set over you, but as your companion, and the humble, though unworthy, servant of you all. Therefore, in the things that are yours and pertain to your salvation, receive me as speaking and enjoining not my own private and personal precept, but rather the common precept of the Lord of all.
