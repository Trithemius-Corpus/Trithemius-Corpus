For I say what is attested as true: no prayers are more effective than those which are offered to God the Father with remembrance of the Lord’s passion and with gratitude.

Fourth, what we have judged necessary for you for regular observance is that you keep watch over the custody of the outward senses, because no one can sufficiently guard the city of his heart if he scorns to close its gates. Love silence, and flee every disturbance of this world. Apply holy prudence to everything you do, and love the secrecy of quiet. For a monk who loves public and wandering excursions has never been a true lover of observance.

Fifth, we admonish you to flee idleness, destructive to the soul, because this pestilential poison nourishes the gravest vices and always begets unclean thoughts. Therefore whoever, as a lover of virtues, loves his own salvation; whoever desires to reach eternal happiness: let him flee idleness as from the face of the devil, if he wishes to avoid sin.

Sixth, for purity of regular observance, by the necessity of salvation, it is required that the renunciation of property be kept inviolate by all until death. For a monk who has private property prepares for himself a road to hell. But whoever receives and spends money in the monastery should be a public minister; no private person should dare to presume this. The possession of riches is not fitting for monks, who are commanded to imitate the Lord Jesus in poverty of spirit. Let us hear the holy apostle of Christ saying: “Having food and clothing, with these let us be content.” By God’s mercy we provide you with necessities, not as much as the desire of disordered pleasure demands, but as much as nature, content with little, requires. Therefore, just as it is not lawful for you to possess anything without the abbot’s consent, so, since we supply everything, nothing is lacking that is necessary.

Seventh, what makes a monk is humble obedience to commands. For he cannot be a true monk who scorns obedience and refuses to his superiors the humble subjection which he promised. This disease of disobedience arises from elation and pride of mind, just as pus is produced from a bad ulcer. But what does Scripture say? “Pride is hateful before God and men,” and is the beginning of every transgression. The warfare of monks is the imitation of Christ, who obeyed the Father even unto death.

The eighth place is adorned by chastity, a most beautiful virtue; if it is scorned, it is now inevitable that the whole integrity of regular observance suffer shipwreck. For a monk, by necessity of his salvation, is commanded to live chastely until death and always to abstain from every venereal pollution. For these are the three principal vows of monastic life; to live contrary to their observance is certain damnation for monks. Indeed, the profession of monks, under sentence of eternal damnation, commands them to live without property, under obedience to a pastor, and in perpetual chastity. If one of these three is removed, monastic integrity is violated, and transgression condemns the one who scorns it.

In the ninth place, therefore, we admonish every person dedicated to God always to beware of his own will as of a venomous serpent, because unless he has it suspect at all times, he easily falls into the pit of damnation. Attend to the Rule, which does not permit monks power over either their own body or their own will. No one sins unwillingly. If you have perfectly submitted your will in all things to God and to your abbot, you will easily avoid sin.

The tenth thing that can preserve in you, my dearest brothers, the integrity of regular observance is that in all adversities and oppositions you have most steadfast patience for the love of God. For patience is a great virtue, which by another name we call endurance, without which no one will come forth the victor over his temptations. Therefore the Lord said in the holy Gospel: “In your patience you will possess your souls.” What is patience except to bear calmly both the injuries done by one’s own people and all other adversities, out of reverence and love for God? Believe me, brothers; I speak the judgment of the apostle: patience is very necessary for you, so that, doing the will of God, you may receive the promise. Let the Lord Jesus be for you the living example of patience, whom you should follow with all your strength.

Eleventh, my brothers, take care that your hearts not be weighed down with gluttony, because a monk should not eat and drink to satiety, but, for love of abstinence, should always stop with a certain appetite and hunger. For the wise man says: “He who loves feasts will be in poverty, and he who loves wine and fat things will not become rich.” Love fasting; cherish abstinence; preserve continence and frugality in all things. These are the weapons of cloistered persons; these are the insignia of the spiritual warfare of monks.
