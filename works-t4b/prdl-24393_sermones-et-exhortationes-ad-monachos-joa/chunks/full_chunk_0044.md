Homily 10. Fol. xxiii.

let us diligently recall, saying: You who have been weaned from milk, who have been drawn away from the breast, expect tribulation upon tribulation, hope upon hope, yet a little while because of the malice of lips, because of the malicious tongue. Those weaned and drawn away from the breasts are men advanced in age, whose part it is to undergo tribulation upon tribulation for God, so that they may deserve to receive hope upon hope, as sacred Scripture says: But we glory also in our tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, and patience trial, and trial hope. And hope does not confound, because the charity of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us. For through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. Through temptations we become known to ourselves and discover whether we truly fear God. Hence the Lord speaks through Moses to the people, saying: The Lord your God tests you, so that it may be known whether you love him with all your heart and with all your soul.

This world, most beloved brothers, must be compared to a furnace, in which the fire of temptation and adversity continually burns, and through which we must necessarily pass. In this furnace of tribulation the chaff is burned, but gold and silver are cleansed from every stain; the chaff is turned to ashes, while the gold is reserved for the adornment and splendor of the royal palace. The world, I say, is the furnace; the fire is temptation or tribulation; the gold is the righteous man; the chaff is the useless and sinful man; and the artisan who purifies the gold is almighty God himself, who favors our progress and, through grace, cooperates toward the merit of virtue.

Therefore let us do what God wills; what he imposes, let us bear patiently for love of him, since the true lover of Christ says: The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that will be revealed in us. Hence Saint Jerome says: Who would not wish to be troubled? Who would not desire to be cursed, and to deserve to be praised by the voice of Christ and rewarded with an abundant heavenly recompense? Would that, for the Lord’s name and for justice, the whole crowd of unbelievers might persecute and trouble me. Would that this whole world might rise up to my reproach, provided only that I might deserve to be praised by Christ and hasten toward the reward he has promised. And the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men.

Therefore, whoever of you, O my brothers, desires to reach the kingdom of heaven, whoever above all things is concerned to please God, let him learn willingly to undergo the tribulations of temptations for love of him, and gladly to endure whatever adverse thing occurs. For there is none of us who does not need daily purification, none who does not often fail at every moment. Therefore, if we love our own salvation, if we ask that our offenses be forgiven us and beg to be cleansed from every stain of heart, let us patiently endure whatever adversity befalls us, since the evil of tribulation, when patiently endured for the love of God, purifies souls more than the good works that one now performs in this world by his own will.

Great material for suffering has been left to us in this place: lack presses us heavily, poverty weighs us down, and debt owed to others constrains us. What adversity do we not experience in this monastery? What storms and tempests do we not suffer, brothers? By what inconveniences are we not worn down? None of you can be ignorant how utterly desolate all the goods of this cenobium were when regular observance was introduced fifteen years ago. But the tribulation of want as a companion rages more severely against me, since I am obliged to provide you with the necessities of life, and often I do not sufficiently know from where I shall give them. Yet your patience consoles my spirit, and the hope that you have humbly conceived in the Lord Savior, so that we do not doubt that mercy will come in due time; and I hear him saying to me through Isaiah the prophet: Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.

Let us therefore gladly have patience in the poverty that we now suffer according to God’s ordinance, so that after death we may deserve to come to the riches of eternity. For the more we have been afflicted in this our pilgrimage with imperfections, poverty, want, labors, and tribulations, the greater rewards we shall obtain in the future recompense, when the just Lord bestows them. Learn, my brothers, to endure patiently whatever almighty God has foreordained for us, since Saint Pope Gregory says: Faith in almighty God is then certain when adversity is endured for a good work, and in this world the scourge of God is received with joy, so that a more beautiful reward may be preserved in the future recompense of the saints.

Again he also says: Those whom the adversity of temporal life humbles take joy from the hope of an everlasting inheritance; for divine dispensation would not restrain them, for their instruction, under the rule of discipline, if it did not perceive that they were to be saved forever. Afflicted, we lose earthly things; but by humbly sustaining affliction we multiply heavenly goods for ourselves. For the present life is a road by which we hasten toward our homeland, and therefore we are often touched in it by disturbances, lest we suppose our homeland to be here.

Therefore let the monk rejoice in his adversity; let the tempted man, to whom the world has been crucified, be glad. Let him trust that he belongs to the fellowship of God, who with a pure conscience always does good and receives the opposite in tribulation. For it is a very true sign of divine mercy if a Christian man is struck by various adversities in this world, while he serves God with a pure conscience. Some whom God sees to be able to sin greatly, he forestalls with bodily weakness so that they may not sin, and mercifully scourges them for their salvation, seeing that it is more useful for their souls to be broken by illnesses unto salvation than to live healthy unto damnation. He wills others to be poor, whom he has foreknown to be unable to use riches for salvation.

For many people have been cast down by the wealth and delights of this world; and that unexpected enemy has struck them more readily because, from long habit of ease, he found them negligent. Hence holy men, when they trust that they are making great progress by prosperity in virtues, rejoice also to be exercised by temptations through a certain moderation of heavenly dispensation, because they guard the glory of the virtues they have received all the more strongly when, shaken by the assault of temptation, they recognize their own weakness more humbly. The way of tribulation is the shortest road to Christ, and a person is found on it more quickly than in tranquility. For the holy bishop Ambrose says: Many seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and there are those who seek him amid persecutions and quickly find him. In the dangers of his faithful ones he is present, as he speaks of the just man in the psalm, saying: I am with him in tribulation; I will rescue him and glorify him.

Let purity of conscience guard us in adversity; let the integrity of life always be preserved inviolate; and whatever adversity comes will undoubtedly be for us toward the merit of salvation. The ancient enemy, by fighting, can overthrow no one except the one whom he has first found to have fallen away from himself. He seeks those made sluggish by idleness; he undermines the unwary soldier; and he most easily casts down the one whom he finds stripped of arms.

Therefore, my brothers, always keep watch in holy prayers; always be intent on good works, so that whenever the tempter approaches, he may never find you idle. Remember the words of our Savior Jesus Christ, saying in the holy Gospel: Let your loins be girded and lamps burning in your hands, and you yourselves like men waiting for their lord to return from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may open to him at once. He keeps watch who, standing firm in the fear of the Lord, never dozes in mental sluggishness. He keeps watch who preserves his soul, by the tireless constancy of holy purpose, against any adversities of this world. He keeps watch who never ceases to advance daily in the love of the Lord.

He carries a lamp in his hands who adorns his holy purpose with fitting works. He also holds a lamp in his hands who, cleansed in mind and body, shines for his neighbor through works of holy devotion. Hence the holy lover of Christ, Paul, says in the epistle: I beseech you, dearly beloved, by the mercy of God, that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God. He who chastens his body through the pursuits of holy abstinence and brings it into servitude to the spirit has consecrated a living sacrifice to the Lord. But whoever has already learned to keep his heart clean and pure from every pollution of sin has undoubtedly offered a holy sacrifice to the Lord. And whoever perseveres in the purpose of holiness to the end has without doubt offered a pleasing sacrifice.
