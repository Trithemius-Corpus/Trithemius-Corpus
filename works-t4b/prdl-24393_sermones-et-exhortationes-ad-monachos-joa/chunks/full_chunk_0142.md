*On the knowledge of spiritual grace.*

... they despise the comforts of the present life. The soul that has God present within itself through love despises all consolations of the flesh; it longs to come to its Lord through hard and bitter things. It does not love the riches of this world; it spurns passing honors and dignities; it firmly contemns the pleasures of the flesh. It always meditates on the law of the Lord and never forgets the most bitter passion of Jesus Christ. Who among you, brothers, does these things: who detests all the little consolations of this age; who thinks of nothing else, seeks nothing else, desires nothing else than the charity of God? Whoever has obtained this sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, if he perseveres to the end of life, will be saved.

We receive the eighth indication of a good course, and the true sign of the presence of the Holy Spirit, when we humbly conform our wills in all things to the divine ordinances. This degree of spiritual progress separates the monk from all cares of the world and calls him wholly to inward things. For his will is subjected, by the affection of a peaceful mind, to the divine will, and it is far separated from all desires that war against the spirit. Whoever has received this gift of the grace of the Holy Spirit should first give thanks with humility, and should not vainly exalt himself because of the gift; rather, the more he has advanced, the more he should learn to humble himself in all things. For a man often deserves to lose the grace of God because he neglects to keep it in the box of true humility. Therefore almighty God sometimes withdraws the grace of sensible devotion from those making progress for a short time, so that humility may persevere in the mind and the fervor of desire may burn more ardently to seek more. But since there are many causes in people for which the grace of God is sometimes withdrawn, it will be worthwhile to state them in order.

First, the grace of God is withdrawn from a person because of sin, when the conscience is stained by the foulness of vices. For our God, as Scripture says, is jealous: either he is loved alone, or he is not loved with any companion. Therefore, whenever the mind turns back by desiring either itself or something else in a disordered way, it is removed, alienated from its source, which is God. Hence it deserves to lose the grace it had, because by consenting to sin it refused to keep it inviolate. Therefore the holy apostle of Christ, Paul, exhorts us not to receive the grace of God in vain; for it is indeed received fruitlessly and in vain when the mind of the recipient is stained by the filth of vices. The little vessel into which we receive the grace of God is our conscience; when it is purged of vices and adorned with holy virtues, it becomes a worthy dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and generates great progress in us. But if the vessel of our conscience has been defiled by the rottenness of sin, immediately the Spirit of God departs with all the gifts of grace.

Is not our Lord Jesus Christ the lover and faithful spouse of the soul, which he purchased for himself by the pouring out of his own blood? With what face, with what rashness can you, O man, despise this lover and your savior, and love anything else in this world? God loves you supremely: he is your creator, and by his death he freed you from everlasting destruction. What will you repay him, O mortal man, from whom you have received such great benefits? He asks nothing from you, seeks nothing, demands nothing, except gratitude, affection, and charity. Keep innocence and purity of conscience, lest you lose the prompting of grace.

The second cause for which grace is withdrawn from a person is called the pride of ingratitude. For God, as Scripture testifies, resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. By the just judgment of God, the grace of the Holy Spirit is lost whenever one is lifted into pride by what he possesses. For everyone who humbles himself will be exalted, and whoever exalts himself will be humbled. Hence almighty God again says: “Upon whom shall my Spirit rest, if not upon the humble and quiet, and the one who trembles at my words?” The more anyone humbles himself for the love of Christ in true purity of conscience, the more fit he will be to receive the grace of the Holy Spirit. But whenever the mind rises into pride, spiritual devotion is deservedly taken away from the unclean vessel. Therefore first, my brothers, let no one grow proud of the grace of the Holy Spirit which he has received; let no one vainly exalt himself, because unless we have been humble, we will by no means be able to share in divine inspirations. As the holy Christ-loving Paul says: “He who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, deceives himself.”

Third, the grace of devotion is withdrawn from a person because of laziness and acedia, when the mind is hardly exercised at all in good works. For, as Scripture says, idleness is the enemy of the soul, and everyone who follows idleness will be in want. The wise man also says: “Desires kill the lazy man; his hands have refused to work at anything.” Therefore, just as fire is extinguished without fuel, so the spirit of devotion grows weak without the continual exercise of charity. Let no one, then, presume upon his own powers in this life; let no one settle down securely in his former way of life, because, I say, as long as someone can be made better from worse, he can also be made worse from better.
