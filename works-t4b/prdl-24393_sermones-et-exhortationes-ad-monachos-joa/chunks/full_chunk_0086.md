Homily XVIII. Fol. XLIII.

You have been called by God: yet, given over to the pleasures of the flesh, you think of Christ less than anything. You have been invited to love the Lord Jesus above all things: yet you walk according to the evil desires of your heart. You show yourself ungrateful to your redeemer, to whom you were obliged to live; and, submerged in the torpor and all the holy exercises of spiritual discipline, you despise them in pride.

And what will you answer on that day of judgment to the judge, who will say to you, most ungrateful as you are: I, who am Lord of all things, for your sake humbled myself, taking the form of a servant, and was found in appearance as a man. For you I poured out my blood; for you I endured scourges; for you I underwent a death exceedingly bitter by my own free will. I, O monk, loved you with all my heart. I redeemed you from perpetual death by my blood. I called you to the heavenly kingdom and to love, if you had kept my commandments. But you have lived in opposition to my precepts; and what is more grievous, although you had been sworn to my words, you were not afraid to present yourself against me, your Lord, as an apostate and impious traitor. Now therefore render an account of your stewardship.

What then will you answer, O lazy and idle monk, who now spend this acceptable time so fruitlessly, and despise living in true monastic purity according to the rule? What, I ask, will you answer? Will you deny the works of depravity that you have committed, and falsely claim the good works you now scorn to do, so that by a lie you may circumvent the judge and thus escape the punishment due to your deserts? But you must not fail to know that all the things you have done, said, or thought throughout your whole life, good and evil, are manifest in the sight of the judge, and nothing can be hidden from him, who alone knows all things. For all things are written in the book of God, and your conscience, reading against you all the evil you have done, will give open testimony, so that not even the smallest thought will be able to lie hidden. For the Scripture of the prophets says to God: Since even human thought will confess to you.

You are to be judged before the tribunal of the all-knowing one, before whose terrible judgment every creature will stand with very great fear and trembling. There no excuse at all is admitted, but each person will receive, according to his deserts, a sentence without appeal. Tens of thousands and hundreds of thousands of holy spirits will stand by, and do you think you can deceive the judge with a lie?

But hear my counsel: rise from your destructive idleness, wake up at last, and by true penitence return to the mercy of God and, while there is time, to justice. Do not put it off from day to day, because it is exceedingly dangerous to remain in sins and to refuse the remedy of salvation that is offered. Remember that you will die, and that when you do not expect it you will be called to judgment. Consider, I beg you, how uncertain is the length of time for living, how it slips away, and how quickly all mortal things are corrupted. Nothing among human things is stable, nothing secure, nothing firm; but all things that we perceive are corruptible and change with time.

Consider also where our brothers Valentine, Martin, and John now are, who in the present year were living with us, healthy, safe, and joyful; they were speaking with us, eating, drinking, and working, thinking less than anything that they would die this year. Behold, when they did not suppose it, death came and seized them, and brought their souls to the tribunal of the dread judgment, which rendered to each one according to the works of his hands. Behold, now learn by the example of your brothers that you too will die, and perhaps you will pass from this world much sooner than you can estimate.

Look to your own interest while you can. Do penance in the time of your health, when the judge promises mercy, and do not defer today’s good into the uncertainty of tomorrow. Rise up even now, late though it is, from the sloth of your negligent past way of life, in which until now you have shamefully rotted away, and cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin. Wipe away by labors the evil you have committed, and from now on live innocently, as befits a monk. Take up a perfect purpose of amendment, and understand that the kingdom of heaven is laid up not for those who begin the good, but for those who persevere to the end.

I know also that heaven is not reached by idleness and torpor, but by great labor, by the strongest voluntary effort to make progress, and by the tireless practice of spiritual exercise. Therefore take care to serve and please the Lord in truth. Always remain within yourself, fearful in awe, and prepare your soul for restoration. One must resist disordered movements and wicked desires of the flesh.
