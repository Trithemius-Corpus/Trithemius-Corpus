Therefore watch at all times, praying that you may be accounted worthy to escape all the evils that are to come, and to stand with confidence before the sight of the Son of God, who judges all things. For, as the divinely inspired Christophilus bears witness, we shall all stand before the tribunal of Christ, so that each may receive what belongs to his own body, according to what he has done, whether good or evil.

Blessed is that monk whom the Lord finds watching in good works: who has prepared himself for the marriage-feast of the heavenly Lamb in true purity of heart; who will not fear to go forth to meet the Judge. Let that memorable saying of Saint Jerome on the Gospel according to Matthew always be in your hearts: “Whether I eat or drink, or do anything else, that voice always seems to sound in my ears: Rise, you dead, and come to judgment.” Whenever I think of that day of future examination, I tremble in my whole heart and body. Therefore, brothers, if there is any joy in the present life, it must be so practiced that the bitterness of that dreadful judgment never recedes from memory.

O that day, great, terrible, exceedingly dreadful, and very bitter, on which the sun and moon will be darkened, not by a lessening of their light but by comparison with his majesty; where every argument of excuse will cease; where all things will be manifest, and nothing will be able to lie hidden from the Judge. For he who knows the hidden thoughts of minds will then judge openly; he is not anticipated by favor, nor bent by mercy; he is not corrupted by offerings and gifts; he will not be softened by prayers, nor thereafter appeased by any satisfaction.

Placed in this life, therefore, brothers, let each one of you do as much as he can for his soul, because repentance for sins made here alone is useful and fruitful, so long as there is a place for pardon and the sentence of justice is still deferred. He who postpones doing good and practicing repentance until death is very near to damnation, because the good that a man neglects while healthy and sound he rarely restores when sick and already about to die. For Saint Pope Gregory says: Whoever does not fear to put off the fruit of repentance until the hour of death is himself a witness against himself that true repentance was never in his mind. Now, therefore, turn yourselves to our Lord with your whole heart, doing penance before him; and so live, I beg you, that when you must depart from here, you may not fear to appear before his face.

Fol. XVII.

That One Must Pray Without Intermission. Homily IX.

Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, brothers, wishing in the Gospel according to Luke that we be constant in prayers, admonished us in these words: “It is necessary always to pray and never to fail.” This counsel of the Lord befits monks above all mortals, and all who are cloistered in any religious order whatsoever, whose every action and whole life, indeed whatever they do, ought either to be prayer or at least conformed to prayer.

For all who profess the monastic soldiery are called spiritual, not without reason, so that by the very name they may be reminded to despise the pleasures of the flesh for love of God and to walk in the ardor of the spirit through true charity. For the holy apostle of Christ says: “Those who are in the flesh cannot please God,” and, “If you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” Knowing, however, that without continual prayer the fervor of the spirit cannot long subsist in us, he again says to the Thessalonians: “Rejoice always; pray without intermission; in all things give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for all of you.”

The Savior taught us in the Gospel what the manner of praying is, saying: “But you, when you pray, enter into your chamber, and, having shut the door, pray to your Father in secret; and your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you.” And in another place he said to the Samaritan woman: “Woman, believe me, that the hour is coming. The Father seeks such people to worship him. God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.”

It is clear and manifest from Christ’s teaching that prayer is of the heart rather than a lengthy speech of the mouth, because, since God is spirit, he must be worshiped in no other way than with the inward fervor of the spirit. For prayer of the heart in the fervor of the spirit has great power; but the multiplication of words without attention of the mind [unclear].
