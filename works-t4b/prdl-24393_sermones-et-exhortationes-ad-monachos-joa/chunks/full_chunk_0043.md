...let us recall all the words of him who says: “You who have been weaned from milk, you who have been drawn away from the breast, expect tribulation upon tribulation, hope upon hope, yet a little while, because of the malice of lips, because of the evil tongue.” Those weaned and drawn away from the breast are men advanced in age, whose part it is to take up tribulation upon tribulation for God, so that they may deserve to gain hope upon hope, as sacred Scripture says: “But we glory also in our tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, and patience testing, and testing hope. And hope does not confound, because the love of God has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.” For through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God.

For by temptations we become known to ourselves, and we learn whether we truly fear God. Hence the Lord speaks through Moses to the people, saying: “The Lord your God tests you, that he may know whether you love him with all your heart and with all your soul.” This world, dearest brothers, must be compared to a furnace, in which the fire of temptation and adversity continually burns, and through which we must necessarily pass. In this furnace of tribulation, straw is burned up, but gold and silver are cleansed from every stain. Straw is turned to ash; gold is reserved for the adornment and beauty of the royal palace.

The furnace, I say, is the world; the fire is temptation or tribulation; the gold is righteous people; the straw is the unrighteous and sinners; the craftsman who purifies the gold is almighty God himself, who favors our progress and cooperates by grace toward the merit of virtue. Therefore let us do what God wills; let us patiently bear for love of him what he lays upon us, because, as the holy lover of Christ says, “the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that shall be revealed in us.”

Hence Saint Jerome says: “Who would not wish to be troubled? Who would not desire to be cursed, so that he might deserve to be praised by Christ’s voice and rewarded with a heavenly and abundant recompense? Would that, for the name and righteousness of my Lord, the whole crowd of unbelievers might persecute and trouble me. Would that this whole world might rise up to my reproach, provided only that I may deserve to be praised by Christ and to hasten toward the reward of his promise.”

And the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation tests righteous men.” Therefore, whichever of you, my brothers, desires to reach the kingdom of heaven, whoever takes care above all things to please God, let him willingly learn to take up, for love of him, the tribulations of temptations, and joyfully endure whatever adversity may befall. For there is none of us who does not need daily purification, none who does not fail almost at every moment.

Therefore, if we love our own salvation, if we demand that our offenses be forgiven us and that we be cleansed from every stain of heart, let us patiently bear whatever adversity may happen to us, because the evils of tribulation, which the soul patiently tolerates for love of God, purify it more than the good works it now performs of its own accord in this world.

In this place a great matter for suffering has been left to us, whom want presses heavily, poverty weighs down, and the debt of borrowed money also constrains. What adversity do we not experience in this monastery? What squalls and storms do we not suffer, brothers? By what hardships are we not shaken? None of you can be ignorant how desolate all the goods of this monastery were when, fifteen years ago, regular observance was introduced. But the tribulation of common want rages more severely against me, since I must provide you with the necessities of life, and very often I do not sufficiently know from where I may repay.

Yet your patience consoles my spirit, and the hope that you have conceived in the Lord, the savior of the humble, so that I do not doubt that his mercy will come to our aid in time. And I hear him saying to me through Isaiah the prophet: “Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of poverty.” I will gladly have patience in the poverty that, with God so ordering it, we now suffer, so that after death we may deserve to come to the riches of eternity. For the more, in this pilgrimage of ours, we have been afflicted by persecutions, poverty, want, labors, and tribulations, the greater rewards we shall obtain in the future recompense, with the rewarder of the righteous granting them.

Therefore, my brothers, endure patiently with me whatever almighty God has preordained that we suffer, because, as Saint Pope Gregory says, confidence in almighty God is then certain...
