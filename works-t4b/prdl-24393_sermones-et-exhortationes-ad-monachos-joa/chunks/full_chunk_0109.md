Let each of you, as much as he can, strive with the whole strength of his mind to love the Lord his God above all things, because love of God alone is what will open for you the gate of paradise. For the more ardently you now love God, the greater the glory in which you will be received in the heavenly homeland.

Happy is that monk in whose conscience, apart from the love of Christ, there remains no imagining of any other occupation; who, waking, thinks of nothing but Christ; sleeping, dreams of Christ; living and dying, sighs most steadfastly after Christ. O how well and securely he dies who has always been diligent in the exercise of divine love, and at all times zealous in observing the commandments of God. For love of God alone is admitted into the kingdom of heaven; and the more fervent the mind has been in loving, the greater its reward will be in the kingdom of heaven. He who loves much receives much; and he who now loves less will also then receive less in glory. Therefore let each person, as much as he can, love with his whole heart and with his whole soul and with all his strength, because our God is not to be loved lukewarmly or lightly, but fervently, wisely, and sweetly.

The love of God grows and is increased by spiritual exercises, just as a burning fire spreads when wood is supplied. For truly, if the wood is taken away, the fire is also extinguished; if spiritual exercises themselves are interrupted, charity is soon shipwrecked. True love of God is never idle, but at every time is delightfully occupied in those things that nourish and preserve charity. The lover of his beloved God never forgets him, but always meditates on his presence and beauty; and now, in most devout prayers, he speaks to Jesus; now, in the holy Scriptures, he marvels at him speaking to himself. The true lover of Christ meditates day and night on the law of the Lord, and does with the greatest diligence all the things that he understands to be pleasing to God.

O how sweetly he burns whom God has truly set aflame; how pleasantly he glows whose mind the flame of the Holy Spirit kindles. Blessed is that monk who, full of divine love, wholly withdraws himself from earthly vanities and rests in the unity of heavenly desire. O if only we had love toward God as strong, whole, and vehement as mad and foolish lovers have toward their corruptible mistresses, from desire for whom they are often shamefully driven mad, and fear no scandals of this world. But the love of God is stronger than all things, if it has truly been rooted in us. Whoever truly loves the Lord God always keeps his most bitter passion in his memory, because nothing so produces perfect love of the Lord God in the heart, nothing so truly brings on compunction, as the never-interrupted recollection of God’s benefits. Therefore let the written sequence of Christ’s passion always be in your memory, and let this word never depart from your mouth: O crucified Jesus, my love, by the power of your most innocent death have mercy on me and make me your faithful servant.

*On Serenity of Conscience.*

*Homily 24.*

Most beloved brothers, we must keep watch with great solicitude, and must take care with continual meditation that we always guard our conscience, whose testimony we cannot escape, clean from every contagion of sin and unstained by intimate custody. Nothing is more beautiful, nothing more pleasant, nothing finally more precious than a good conscience, when the mind has in itself a manifest testimony of rectitude; it suffers no remorse of destructive unrest. An upright and good conscience is an incredible exultation of the mind and a sure consolation in every adversity. The monk who has obtained the testimony of a good conscience neither refuses to live nor fears the coming of future death. A good conscience is always glad, always joyful, always fearless, and truly tranquil in the peace of Jesus Christ. In a good conscience there is always peace, piety, goodness, light, virtue, honor, joy, gladness, sweetness, and delight without dissipation. Conscience is the treasury of the soul, because whatever is placed in it is reserved as provision for the life to come. A good conscience is the best treasure; if anyone has neglected to have it, he must be judged the most wretched and unhappy of all men. A good conscience is the dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit and the earthly paradise of all grace.
