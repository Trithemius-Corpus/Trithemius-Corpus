Therefore holy Christophilus says: “Brothers, we are debtors, not to the flesh so as to live according to the flesh. For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the flesh, you will live.” Therefore he fights rightly for the Lord who gives to each what is his.

Next, we are commanded to have our feet shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that the good which we ourselves have come to know by the Lord’s generosity we may also gladly share with others. For our law, brothers, according to which we are commanded to serve the Lord as soldiers, is the gospel of Jesus Christ; to it all the rules and constitutions of cloistered persons are ordered. For our monastic constitutions are not the gospel, but the statutes of monks were made on account of the gospel. A Christian can indeed be saved without being a monk, but a monk cannot be saved unless he has been a Christian.

Let certain excessively superstitious and foolish cloistered persons be ashamed, who value their rules and statutes more than the gospel of Christ; who observe the traditions of men even to prisons and chains, but do not heed the constitutions of God and of the holy universal Church. To these the words of our Savior, spoken to the Jews according to Matthew, properly apply: “Why do you also transgress the commandment of God because of your tradition?” The evangelical teaching must be preferred by far to all the constitutions of the whole world. After the gospel, however, the statutes of the universal Church hold the first place of authority; without them the constitutions or rules of monks are not compared in dignity. Let us take care to live according to evangelical purity, and we shall be perfect in the monastic way of life of the fathers.

Let our feet always be shod in preparation for the gospel of peace, so that we may mortify in ourselves the desires of the flesh and, through love of God and neighbor, have peace with all. For without peace and the concord of brotherhood, mortification of the flesh is of no benefit, as Saint Jerome says: Whoever afflicts the body but abandons concord does indeed praise God on the drum, but does not praise him in the choir. Hence Solomon says: Better is a dry morsel with joy than a house full of sacrifices with strife. And again he says: It is an honor for a man to separate himself from contentions, but all fools are mingled with insults.

Saint Christophilus also, lover of peace, admonishes us, saying: “Pursue peace with all, and holiness, without which no one will see God.” Hence also the prophet Zechariah says: “Love peace and truth, says the Lord almighty.” And our Savior says in the holy gospel according to Mark: “Have salt in yourselves, and have peace among yourselves.” “There is no peace for the wicked,” says the Lord.

But he is truly a soldier of Christ who has peace with God, with his neighbor, and in himself. A man is said to have peace with God when, moved by good will and turned toward him through the continual desire of love, despising transitory things, he directs the gaze of his mind to things above and most diligently keeps everything that the Lord has commanded. For when the Son of God was born in the flesh, the holy angels sang glory to the Lord, who sits above all things in the highest, and announced peace to people of good will. But wicked and unjust people cannot have this peace with God, because they have turned their wills away from the Lord.

He has peace with his neighbor who does injury to no one, who when injured does not return vengeance, but, overcoming evil with good, never abandons brotherly charity. If someone among his neighbors murmurs unjustly against such a person, the soldier of Christ has not for that reason abandoned peace, because perhaps another, without just cause, has begun unjust murmuring. A just man, who has peace with God and desires to harm none of his neighbors, will rightly be called peaceable, even if someone disturbed rises up unjustly against him. For the just man says: “With those who hated peace, I was peaceable; when I spoke to them, they attacked me without cause.”

Hence Saint Augustine says: Among true worshipers of God, even wars themselves are peaceful, because they are waged not from greed or cruelty but from zeal for peace, so that the evil may be restrained and the good relieved. He has peace in himself who, illumined by purity and holiness, lives secure in his own conscience without error, whom anxious conscience does not gnaw, nor does unjust or wicked thought disturb. Hence Augustine again says: Peace is serenity of mind, tranquillity of soul, simplicity of heart, the bond of love, the fellowship of charity. For it removes enmities and rivalries; it checks wars.
