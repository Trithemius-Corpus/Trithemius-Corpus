For unless the mind has first been blinded, the will will not yield to the demon. The spirit of fornication first darkens the mind gravely; then, until it has hurled it into sin, it fights against one already blinded. But after it has cast the monk into the mire of unspeakable crime, it sets two obstacles in his way so that he may not rise again to penance. For either it subtly tries to lighten the gravity of the sin, so that he does not fear a great punishment, or it brings back to the fallen man’s memory the severity of judgment, so that he falls into the pit of despair. By these snares the sinful soul is caught; and the longer it lies in the depth of misery, the more hardened it becomes.

Flee idleness, O cloistered men, and above all avoid gluttony and the satiety of the belly, because the monk who fills his belly with food and drink provides material for the demon of fornication to do harm. Put on true contrition of heart, and restrain illicit movements of the flesh through tears of prayer. For no one is able rightly to conquer his body who has not first taken up the goad of contrition. But he cannot attain the grace of holy contrition who has despised perfectly to deny himself. Yet whoever has perfectly denied himself for the love of Christ, already overcoming the impulse of the flesh, will more easily obtain contrition. For one who is deadened in will desires nothing carnal, nothing base. But who among us, brothers, would dare to say that he has attained this grace of mortification? Who among us does not feel himself weak? We run in the stadium of spiritual warfare, and in daily combat we fight against vices. We have received no assurance of peace, even if at times we have peace. The more familiar to us the weakness of the flesh is, the harsher are the battles of chastity. Therefore we must keep greatest watchfulness when we carry about a familiar enemy and endure, as it were, a domestic instigator of evil.

Therefore as long as we live in this mortality, let us always fear the strongest enemies of chastity. Let us avoid everything that excites lust; let us flee as much as we can from sloth and idleness. For satiety of bread and idleness are truly the strongest enemies of chastity; unless they are driven far away by abstinence and by the work of salutary exercise, they threaten chastity with imminent shipwreck. Guard your hearts with watchful keeping, O dearest ones, and always keep watch in mind against your adversary. Arm yourselves with the breastplate of perfect faith in Jesus Christ, so that in work and belief you may be true Christians. Always be of one mind in brotherly charity, and in the observance of true regular discipline be concordant without pretense. Love fasting and continence; meditate without interruption. Flee idleness as a pernicious plague of the soul, and exercise yourselves continually in holy works. Love silence, and do not flee monastic solitude, if you desire to obtain true purity of mind and body. Keep continual guard over your eyes, ears, and speech, lest sin enter the soul through the windows of the body. Flee conversations with women, and avoid even the touch of holy women as you would a burning fire. No one safely looks willingly on what brings the sting of death to the covetous.

Always behold our Lord Jesus Christ dying for you with the faithful eyes of the mind through love; be occupied every day in his saving evangelical doctrine; give humble thanks for his passion and death alike. If you do these things with persevering continuance, in true simplicity of heart, until death, we trust that you will be rewarded in the company of the saints.

*On the brotherly charity of monks. All things, XXII.*

Let us hear, my dearest brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ commanding in the holy Gospel: Whatever you wish people to do to you, do the same also to them. This is indeed a brief but solid commendation of brotherly charity, without which no one can ascend to the love of God. For no one can love God who is convicted of not loving his fellow Christian. Hence the blessed apostle John says: Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer; and you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him. Therefore if anyone says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar. For this reason Paul, the lover of Christ, exhorts us, saying: Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ.
