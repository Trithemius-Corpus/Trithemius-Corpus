On the reading and study of the divine scriptures: Homily Four.

Since all scripture, divinely inspired, as Saint Chrysostom says, is useful for teaching or for arguing, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness, so that the man of God may be perfect, instructed for every good work, we ought freely to devote ourselves to the reading of the divine scriptures, and to direct every pursuit of secular literature in which we have sweated at all toward the sharp point of this contemplation. For in the scriptures everything that saving knowledge requires is found set out in the greatest abundance, so that whatever you do not find there is in no way necessary for salvation. Indeed the majesty of the divine scriptures far surpasses all the knowledge and teaching of the whole world, which the Holy Spirit poured into the men of God so that it might be written, and which, so that it may be understood, he imparts by his most glorious grace to diligent readers.

Turn over this scripture, my brothers, with purity of mind and fervor of twin charity, for whoever truly loves God spends all the time he can find in sacred readings before his eyes. Let the frenzied conduct, so to speak, of carnal lovers move you to the wholesome study of the scriptures: when they receive letters and tokens from their mistresses, they do not cease to read them; they read them again and again, kiss them, and, as a greater sign of love, hang them either from the neck or over the breast, so that the dear memory of the one they desire may never depart from their mind. Consider, brothers, the madness of carnal lovers, and blush at your own sluggishness in the love of God.

Which of you is affected toward the holy scriptures of God with as great a love of heart as a blind lover is toward the love letters, foul and most empty, of one little prostitute? Mad with love for his infamous beloved, he follows with repeated kisses the letters and little gifts sent because of the absence of the one he desires. Out of excessive love he examines every single stroke again and again; in everything he does he always thinks of the absent woman; in his love he hastens to come into her sight. O if we, who are called Christian monks, who serve as soldiers for the supreme and immortal king, Christ Jesus, whom we are commanded to love above all things and are ordered to love nothing in this world except him, if, I say, we pursued our Creator with as great a love as carnally minded men cherish and love their corruptible and mortal mistresses!

Holy scripture, when read, makes known to us what God says, reveals his will to us, instructs us what we ought to do, and lays open what we must avoid. Through these writings the Almighty speaks with us, and, invisible, consoles us by informing us with his teaching, as the holy apostle says: Whatever things were written were written for our instruction, so that through patience and the consolation of the scriptures we might have hope. Hence the true soldier of Christ, and especially the cloistered monk, who loves God with his whole heart and strives to please him alone, embraces the divine scriptures, and above all the holy Gospel, with affection, and is continually occupied in meditating on its reading. But whoever neglects the readings of the divine scriptures plainly shows that he is not a true lover of God. For scripture itself says: He who turns away his ear so that he does not hear the laws, even his prayer will be detestable. Blessed, however, are those who keep the testimonies of the Lord; with their whole heart they seek him.

Therefore, my brothers, if you truly love the Lord Jesus with your whole heart, if as true soldiers you desire to please him above all things, if finally you desire to advance toward the perfection of the monastic life, you ought always to occupy yourselves with fervent affection in the reading of the divine scriptures. How useful the reading of the scriptures is, blessed Jerome shows when he says: Love knowledge of the scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh. For after God nothing in this life is taken up more sweetly, nothing is felt more pleasantly, nothing separates the mind more powerfully from love of this world, nothing so strengthens the soldier of Christ against all the temptations of adversaries, nothing so disposes and confirms a monk for every good work, as frequent reading and ardent study of the scriptures.

Indeed, if through the whole breadth of scripture we read and with studious devotion recall to memory the benefits that the Almighty has bestowed on the human race, they will provide us the greatest matter and occasion by which, burning ever more in the love of God, we may make progress. For what else does the whole sequence of the divine scriptures contain than a certain right order of the good things that God either has already bestowed upon human beings or has promised that he will yet give? Or what is there that can so strengthen us for enduring evils and render us voluntarily patient as if we read in the Gospel that the Son of God suffered innocently for us, and after that read of the victories of the holy martyrs?

For sacred scripture contains the things necessary for us to saving knowledge, and it admonishes us what, as soldiers in this pilgrimage, we ought usefully to seek, and also what we ought to avoid. Hence Saint Jerome again says: Our riches are to meditate on the law of God day and night. Let us therefore prepare strength for ourselves; let us take up perfect vigor, so that amid the darkness of errors and the confusion of night the light of the knowledge of Christ may appear, and days and nights may have a protecting light, so that, with scripture as guide, we may be able to come to the holy land.

For what is better than this learning, what sweeter than this discipline? What is sweeter than our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the true salvation and sweetness of faithful souls? Let Christ be our banquet; let sacred reading be our food and drink. For what can be the life of a monk or priest without knowledge of the scriptures? We know Christ not by sight, not by hearing, not by touch, but by reading the scriptures; and the more we advance by reading toward true knowledge, the more we delight in the holy scriptures. For what is sweeter than to know God’s will and not to be ignorant of the rewards of his promises? For, as Jerome says, the field of the divine word is most rich: it has every delight within itself, and whatever you wish is born from it, just as the Jews hand down that when the manna was eaten in the desert, it tasted in the eater’s mouth according to his will and desire.

Therefore, my brothers, read the divine scriptures more often. Through them you can plainly know the will of God and arm yourselves most strongly against the snares of the devil. For if, according to the apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, then whoever does not know the scriptures does not know the power and wisdom of God; and how will he know Christ? Ignorance of the scriptures is ignorance of Christ; and whoever does not know Christ will not belong to his fellowship, as Saint Chrysostom says: For if anyone is ignorant, surely he too will be ignored. Moreover, the more constant a person has been in the study of the divine scriptures, the greater understanding and sweetness he draws from reading them.

But it especially befits you, brothers, to turn over the divine scriptures daily by reading and learning, since abundant time, free from occupations, is granted to you, and a place quite suitable by the rarity of those who come upon you is offered. Therefore I exhort and admonish you, if you love your salvation, always to have divine readings in your hands, so that all the time that, after the worship of prayer, is allowed you without labor may be spent on the study of sacred scripture. Let no day pass, my brothers, in which a monk serving Christ as a soldier does not read something from the sacred volumes that may make him better in his holy purpose. Rather, let him follow the counsel of Saint Jerome, who says: Let the divine scriptures always be in your hands, and let them be constantly turned over in your mind; nor should you think it enough to store God’s commandments in memory unless you also strive to fulfill them in works.

For God requires two things from us, my brothers. First, he wants us to know his most holy will; second, that once known we should do it. But we cannot know his commands if we are ignorant of the knowledge of the divine scriptures. Nor does a person do the will of God if he is ignorant of it; nor has he known it if he has not learned it. Learn, therefore, what the good and pleasing will of God is, so that you may do it; do it once known, so that you may understand it more fully. For no one understands the will of God better than the one who, once he has known it, does it out of love. And so, if you desire to become a most learned interpreter of the divine scriptures, learn to begin the study of knowledge not from words but in holy works. For then you will grasp the rich knowledge of sacred reading, if you keep your heart clean from every sin and, pierced in the love of God, constantly turn over holy scripture with compunction. For the knowledge of the divine scriptures is a high mountain on which Moses speaks with the Lord, to which the filth of vices [continues].
