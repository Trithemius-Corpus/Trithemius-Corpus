On the daily renewal of holy purpose.

If the fire of divine love is now burning in you, apply the necessary fuel, lest through your lukewarmness the little sparks of holy fervor should ever be felt to have been wholly extinguished. Believe me, brothers, for experience has taught me this: you will more easily preserve in yourselves the burning fire of divine fervor by exercise than kindle it again after it has been extinguished.

Therefore whoever has now received love into himself, whoever has received heavenly charity in his heart, should take the greatest care not to fall back into the negligences of his former life. Rather, he should apply the necessary fuel of spiritual exercises, so that, as he makes progress, he may preserve the fervor of God within himself. Let him beware not only of great sins but also of small negligences, and, as far as he can, of the smallest ones; for whoever despises small things falls little by little, and whoever does not always fear danger will soon suffer loss in spiritual fervor and purpose. Scripture says: “Unless you hold yourself steadily in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown.” Blessed, then, is the man who is always fearful, who does not presume upon the virtues he has received, who judges for certain that he is failing whenever he has not understood himself to have advanced at every moment toward higher things.

But truly, brothers, I also greatly fear, unless the appearance of certain works deceives me, that the fire of holy divine fervor is not present in you, since you seem to care little for the true pursuits of monks. Your conduct confirms this, as none of you can fail to know. Yet it remains for me to admonish those who seem to have made too little progress thus far, since the hand of the Lord has not been shortened so that it cannot save penitents. I trust in the mercy of the living God that, because of my charity toward you and the exercises of piety, he will at some time grant those among you who do not walk according to the purity of the rule to come to their senses and to love more ardently the pursuits of holy fervor.

What is this, brothers, that you do not live monastically in the monastery, and, though you have professed a spiritual manner of life, you walk according to the flesh? Do you not know how dangerous it is for a monk to live in a worldly way in a cenobium, and to violate by lukewarm conduct the vow of holy purity which we have all made? Know, I beg you, that the last things of all cloistered persons lie near the extremes: the good and zealous lovers of their own salvation receive in heaven the rewards not of ordinary men but of saints; and those who conduct themselves badly or lukewarmly in the monastic religious life are punished not with a moderate or common torment, but with the most grievous torment of demons. The higher the state in which someone sins, the heavier and more serious will be the punishment of the offender in it.

Therefore, if anyone among you has already learned to walk the way of divine love with fervor of spirit, he must go forward, so that he may make greater progress each day. But whoever still lies in the disgrace of sheer sloth should strive to rise again, since just as punishment is promised to those who lie fallen, so the crown of victory is denied to those who do not persevere. Beware, then, lest destructive lukewarmness overthrow the mind. It is exceedingly dangerous for cloistered persons, because it is most troublesome and full of sorrows, and plainly very near to hell. From its abyss a monk who has once fallen into it rarely rises again.

And alas, how many fainthearted cloistered persons are seen today, slack and idle, who frequently collapse under the burden of spiritual warfare and rarely rise again to the true fervor of spirit. Their condition is truly pitiable: they have the name of being alive, and yet they are dead. These are the ones whom neither the memory of death could bring to true compunction of heart, nor the recollection of Gehenna set before them could soften. As for monks of this kind, afterward, when they have remained thus sluggish in acedia, they are either overcome by bad habit and collapse, or they are in a certain sense in hell, so that they never fully come back to the light of the Lord’s mercies, nor reach the freedom of spirit which alone is nearest to the kingdom of heaven and makes the Lord’s yoke sweet and his burden light.

These evils befall them because their will has not yet been purified from carnal passions, nor do they will the good as they know it, being enticed and drawn away by their own desire. For they love the corrupt consolations of the flesh; and even if they sometimes interrupt them, they never entirely break them off. Hence it is that they very rarely direct their affections toward God; and if at times they are moved to compunction for an hour, they do not persevere long. Instead, wearied in spirit by laziness and inertia, they suddenly abandon, out of sadness and melancholy, the good they had begun. A mind tossed by these storms cannot be filled with the fervor of divine love.
