Third, if you do not wish to lose the grace of inward compunction, strive to love the quiet of solitude. For divine Scripture says: “Upon whom shall my spirit rest, unless upon the humble and quiet one?” And again in another place it says: “In silence and in hope shall your strength be.” For the grace of inward compunction is quickly lost when the secrecy of solitude is not loved. Therefore let the monk who wishes to possess the grace of God learn gladly to remain with himself in his cell.

Fourth, one must take the utmost care that you do not despise any person in your heart, however bad he may be, because the grace of compunction is withdrawn by the just judgment of God whenever a rash judgment concerning one’s neighbor is not avoided. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “Do not judge, and you will not be judged; do not condemn, and you will not be condemned.”

Fifth, the monk who does not wish to lose the grace of inward devotion should strive to exercise himself in the practice of all the virtues. Let him always keep watch in the fear of the Lord, always occupy himself in the exercise of divine love, be continually diligent in good works, and at all times, as much as he can, be devoted to the memory of the Lord’s passion. For attentive meditation on Christ’s passion strengthens a person against all vices and preserves the grace of compunction.

Sixth, be constant in holy prayers, because one who neglects the practice of prayer cannot sufficiently guard the grace of compunction. For the Savior says in the holy Gospel: “Watch and pray, that you may not enter into temptation.” The Lord’s help is ready for those who call upon him; but the person who neglects to pray loses grace. But if you have lost the grace of devotion in any way, you will quickly recover it by the following six remedies.

First, consider that without the grace of God you cannot make progress toward salvation, and that in all the good things you are going to do you have the greatest need of his mercy. Therefore recognize that you are unworthy of the grace of God for doing good, and know that, because of the multitude of your sins, you are unworthy. Call upon him, because he is near; and make every time that is granted to you as free from the common exercises of the brethren pass usefully and fruitfully. The grace of God is necessary for you to do good; it is acquired by the breadth of charity, guarded by humility, and recovered by the practice of saving penitence. The Lord is with you as long as you are with him in good will. But in order that you may be able to recover the lost grace of devotion, you must submit yourself to God in true humility.

Second, for the recovery of the lost grace of compunction it seems necessary that you do not in anything raise yourself against the Lord, but conform your will in all things to the divine ordinance. We say in the Lord’s Prayer, “Your will be done”; it is fitting that we always willingly agree with that prayer in mind and affection.

Third, whoever desires to recover the lost grace of devotion must strongly despise every pleasure of this world for the love of God. For it cannot happen that one recovers the spirit of inward compunction while he opens the dwelling place of his heart to earthly joys and pleasures. Therefore you must cast away every consolation of the present life, if you desire to have the divine spirit of love with his gifts.

Fourth, you are commanded to withdraw yourself from the vain cares and occupations of this world on account of your purpose, and at all times to occupy your mind delightfully in the service of God. For, as the Lord says, no one can serve God and mammon; but whoever desires to serve the Lord in truth of heart must despise all the occupations of the world.

Fifth, we advise that you remember always to occupy your heart in meditation on Christ’s passion, because there is no shorter way for recovering the delay of compunction, nor any manner of prayer more effective for obtaining from the Lord whatever we ask.

Sixth, strive daily to cleanse your conscience through true penitence; and whenever you have fallen into any fault, immediately recover yourself through contrition. If you take care to observe these things with good will, you will recover, by the Lord’s mercy, the lost grace of compunction.

On the Nine Degrees of Ascent

Sermon VII

Beloved brothers, we describe for you the ladder of the nine degrees by which the human spirit ascends into heaven. And although one is higher than another, nevertheless all and each direct one to the kingdom of God, and they are distinguished from one another according to the merits of those who ascend. Therefore attend diligently to what we are about to say, and consider how much progress you have made in spiritual advancement. All who come to the kingdom of God have ascended by the degrees of this ladder. The more each person has advanced in the love of God, the higher he has already ascended.
