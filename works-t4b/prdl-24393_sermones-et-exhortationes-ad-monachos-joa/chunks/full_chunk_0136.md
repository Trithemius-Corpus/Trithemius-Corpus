*Sermon V.*

Sixthly, it strongly draws the mind back from sin if, by habit, it always remains occupied in meditation on the Lord’s Passion. Who will be able to devote his efforts to the pleasures of the flesh, if he has considered Jesus Christ, the ruler of the world, hanging for him on the cross? The death of Christ freed the world from eternal death; whoever carries this benefit with him in memory will easily abstain from every wickedness of the vices. Finally, if anyone considers, with the whole attention of his mind, that God is present to him in every place, knowing and understanding all things, he will without doubt always abhor every sin. For God is everywhere with us, not only present, but truly most inward and most present; no secret escapes him, nothing remains hidden from him, but everything lies plainly manifest to him. He searches the hearts of all; he knows the most secret thoughts, since he alone has true knowledge of all things. For who among mortals, in the presence of so great a majesty, which he believes sees all things, would dare without the greatest shame, if he weighs who and how great he is who sees? Therefore, whenever some wicked thought troubles the mind, whenever the disordered motions of the flesh disturb the soul, think of the Lord God, who is the avenger of evils, as present; recall to memory the bitterness of the Passion of Jesus Christ; devoutly implore the aid of heavenly grace. He is harder than stone whom these things, diligently considered, have not been able to call back from the intention of sinning.

The seventh thing that calls the mind of the studious monk back from the desire of sinning is that, by continual reading, he should go through the passions of the holy martyrs. For indeed, whoever often recalls to memory the unconquered constancy of the holy martyrs of Christ cannot fail to become stronger and more robust in resisting sins. For this reason the brave deeds of the saints were committed to writing: so that, whenever we who are weak read such things, we may rise up to imitate those who have gone before. Do not look, brothers, to those who with proud rashness deny credence to the histories of the saints, and think that the glorious things narrated about the most blessed friends of Christ are fables. For the virtues and good qualities that they do not find in themselves, they scorn to believe that the saints of God possessed. For there are many, especially among those who think themselves great because of their learning, who reject the histories of the holy fathers far too rashly; and since they themselves cannot enter upon the arduous path of virtue, they invent with windy loquacity that no one, or only a few, could have done so. Whatever is read before them about the lives of the saints, whether lofty or great, they cry out that it is invented and fabulous; and, being themselves placed in the darkness of vices, they judge that those whose histories they mock went before them just as they themselves live. But the prudent and devout monk will not be moved by the sloth of the proud, since he well understands that an effective power of exhortation is present in the histories of the saints. For, to confess meanwhile what I feel about myself, I am moved more toward the devotion of inward fervor by the life of one saint than by many curious sermons, because, as the most holy pope Leo says, examples are more powerful than words, and it is more to teach by deed than by voice. But just as not all things please all people, so not all things are suited to all; we advise each person to read more frequently and more zealously that by whose reading his mind is stirred to greater perfection.

The eighth thing that most powerfully draws a man back from sin is charity, concerning which the holy apostle says: Charity is patient, is kind; it does not act perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, does not seek the things that are its own, is not provoked, does not think evil, does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth; it bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Charity never falls away. He who loves God flees sin; but he who is a servant of crimes assuredly does not love God. A lover of God does not commit sin; and he who truly loves his Lord and always beholds him as present does not work iniquity. God is the highest and unchangeable good; for love of him sin must both be fled and detested, since one who fears to sin only from fear of punishments does not ascend to the heights of charity. For a slave does not sin through fear of the rod; but a son withdraws from evil out of love for his father, lest he offend his father’s heart. Let us avoid sin, my brothers, and live in the sight of the Lord with as much purity of soul as we can, since just as deadly poison, once taken, kills the body, so sin kills the soul of the offender. Hence the wise man admonishes us in Ecclesiasticus, saying: Flee sins as from the face of a serpent, because if you come near to them, they will receive you.
