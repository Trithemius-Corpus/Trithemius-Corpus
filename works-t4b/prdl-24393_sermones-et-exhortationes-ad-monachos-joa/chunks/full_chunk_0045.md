Again he also says: “God’s dispensation often gives joy from temptation, when it humbles by the adversity of temporal life those whom it sees are to be saved forever, if it did not preserve them by correction under the rule of discipline.” When we are afflicted, we lose earthly goods; but by humbly enduring affliction we multiply heavenly goods for ourselves. For the present life is a road by which we are journeying toward our fatherland; and for this reason we are often touched in it by disturbances, lest we suppose that our fatherland is here.

Therefore let the monk rejoice in his adversity, if, as a tempered servant, to whom the world has been crucified, he trusts that he will attain the fellowship of God: he who, with a pure conscience, always does good, and receives its opposite in tribulation. For it is an evident sign of divine mercy if a Christian man passes through various adversities in this world, while serving God with a pure conscience and living in true humility without offense. For the merciful God frequently strikes in various ways in this world those whom he loves, for whom he has prepared eternal rewards in heaven. But those whom he has foreknown cannot be saved without sin, nor be overtaken by sin through weakness of the body, he mercifully scourges for their salvation, seeing that it is more useful for their souls to be broken by bonds unto salvation than to live unharmed unto condemnation. He wills others to be poor, whom he has foreknown could not come to salvation with riches. For the delights and wealth of this world would have furnished many with the cause of eternal damnation.

Hence Saint Pope Gregory again says: “Many have been laid low by their own previous good fortune; prolonged peace has rendered many conquered, and the unexpected enemy struck them down more grievously because he found them negligent through long use of quiet.” Therefore holy men, when they consider that they are making much progress through the prosperity of virtues, rejoice that, by a certain moderation of supernal dispensation, they are also exercised by temptations; for they guard the accepted glory of virtues the more robustly, the more, shaken by the impulse of temptation, they more humbly acknowledge their own weakness.

Thus the road of tribulation is a shorter path to Christ, and he is found in the heart more quickly than in tranquillity. Saint Bishop Ambrose says: “There are many who seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and there are some who seek him amid persecutions and quickly find him.” In the dangers of his faithful he is present, just as he speaks of the righteous man in the Psalm, saying: “I am with him in tribulation; I will rescue him and glorify him.” Therefore let purity of conscience be guarded in adversities; let the inviolate integrity of life always be preserved, and whatever adversity comes will undoubtedly be for us unto the merit of salvation.

The ancient enemy cannot overcome anyone by fighting unless he finds that the man has first failed from within himself. He openly seeks to attack; he trips the soldier in the plain, and he casts down most easily the one whom he finds stripped of arms. Therefore, my brothers, always keep watch in holy prayers; always persist in good works, so that when the tempter approaches he may never find you idle. Remember the words of our Savior, who says in the holy Gospel: “Let your loins be girded and lamps burning in your hands, and be like men waiting for their lord when he returns from the wedding, so that when he comes and knocks, they may open to him immediately.”

He keeps watch who, solidly established in the fear of the Lord, never sleeps in the torpor of the mind. He keeps watch who preserves his soul against every adversity of this world through the indefatigable constancy of a holy purpose. He keeps watch who does not cease to advance daily in love of the Lord. He carries a lamp in his hands who adorns his purpose of holiness with worthy works. Likewise he holds a lamp in his hands who, purified in mind and body, shines before his neighbor by works of holy devotion.

Hence Saint Paul, the lover of Christ, says in the epistle: “I beseech you, dearly beloved, by the mercy of God, that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God.” Whoever, chastising his body through the practices of holy abstinence, brings it into the service of the spirit, consecrates a living sacrifice to the Lord. Whoever preserves the heart clean and pure from every pollution of sin, has already without doubt offered a holy sacrifice to the Lord.
