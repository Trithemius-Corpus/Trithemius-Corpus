On the Daily Renewal of the Holy Purpose.

As rust consumes iron, and a garment is gnawed by worms, so idleness weakens the mind; and virtue of the soul, long interrupted, gradually flows away and is utterly uprooted. Therefore flee idleness, and overcome sloth.

But since we cannot remain firmly and without change of mind in one state of divine love, it is necessary for us, lest we entirely fall away, to renew the holy purpose every day. Thus, as often as through the negligence of human weakness we begin, however slightly, to grow torpid from spiritual fervor, so often, examining ourselves by the memory of future things and by contemplation of the Lord’s Passion, we should immediately recall our conscience to its former purpose of advancing.

But we must take the greatest care not to defer this holy effort of advancement until tomorrow, because it has often been harmful to put off a good that could have been done. Hence the wise man says in Ecclesiasticus: “Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day. For his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you.” Augustine also says: “Why do you defer the good until tomorrow, saying, ‘Life will still be long’? For who knows whether it will be long? And if it is not good, what profit is there that it will be long?”

Without a good life, the conduct of a monk should be called death rather than life. Therefore the remedies of a more corrected life must not be deferred by any delays, lest the time of conversion perish through tardiness, when no delay for repentance is any longer granted. For he who promised pardon to the penitent did not promise tomorrow. No one who fears God delays being converted to repentance, nor extends today’s salvation into tomorrow.

But every person who knows that he is established in lukewarmness of mind, and who defers amendment of life from day to day, is deceived by hope of a longer life; for if he knew he would die tomorrow, he would without doubt change his purpose today. Hope of a longer life has miserably deceived many who, while they daily put off the amendment of their way of life, suddenly fall into the depth of death.

Who among you, brothers, does not resolve in his mind to assume the purpose of a more amended life before he dies? For I believe that each one of you intends to exercise still greater devotion toward God before he falls into the danger of death. And why do you wait so long for that day? Why do you defer from day to day? Behold, you who now live in health and do not give yourselves to the spiritual exercises of divine love when you are able, while the Lord patiently waits: the last day will come for each one of you, and it is already hastening, on which, when the angel of death presses hard, the power whose effect you have delayed so long is taken away from you.

Therefore again and again I ask you, brothers, and with all the diligence I can I admonish you: turn to the Lord our God with your whole heart while you live, sound and unharmed, and do not further defer your will, lest you lose even the power granted to you. Think and often think again that we are not citizens of this world, but strangers and pilgrims; nor do we have here an abiding city, but we seek the one to come, and we hasten daily toward death, even without knowing it.

On the Love of Quiet and Solitude.

Homily VI.

Holy Scripture does not plainly reveal how many intellects were created from the beginning. But it is known from the Fathers that the work of the angels is the work of God, and it is counted among the works of the sixth day. There are, however, some who write that the angels were created long before this visible world; and just as we cannot prove this by the testimony of the divine Scriptures, so neither do we rashly attack it. For there are many weighty and holy men who have placed the creation of the angels long before the beginning of the world.

But the angelic intellect was made from the very beginning in the act of all knowable things, and it knew all that it could know; afterward it admitted nothing toward its perfection. For, created by the Lord in its perfection, it required no increase by which it might understand more. Since, without any intermediary, it is joined to God, it was made a pure intellect by nature, nor does it need exercise for understanding after our manner, since its being admits no corruption. And so, adhering to its object through the affection of love, it immediately and inwardly loves God, whom it understands to be the center of its blessedness.
