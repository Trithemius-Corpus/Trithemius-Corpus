Homily XIII. Fol. XXX.

...that you should preserve patience; the other, that you should willingly receive our exhortation, such as it is. For the first point, our holy lawgivers, wishing you to be prepared, prescribe that the brave deeds of the martyrs, whose imitators you ought to become, be read; and then, so that you may be teachable in mind, they commanded that you be subjected to the discipline of the Rule. Thus, so that in imitation of the holy martyrs you may be brave and patient under necessary corrections, the account of their passion is read here in your ears, in order that, your minds prepared for saving instruction, a reading from the Rule which you profess may be recited to you. The holy martyrs of Christ encourage you by their example to patience; blessed Benedict also admonishes you by his teaching that you be ready even for injury.

Therefore let none of you, most beloved brothers, refuse in this place humbly to receive penance each day for his negligences; let no one disdain it. This is the public place for expiating sin, in which the severity of judgment is so tempered that the kindness of discretion is not violated. For I wish to be the physician of your souls, not their destroyer. Therefore come securely to the examination of our judgment, so that, concerning negligences committed openly, you may humbly proclaim them in simplicity. I do not wish to burden you beyond the Rule by transferring the most secret offenses of conscience from sacramental confession to the public examination of faults, which we know certain persons have done, to the grave detriment of the souls of their subjects. For thus our lawgiver Benedict says in the Rule: “But if the cause of the sin has lain hidden in the soul, let him disclose it only to the abbot or to spiritual elders, who know how to heal their own wounds and those of others, and not to uncover or publish them.”

There are two places in the monastery for the expiation of sin: one for those sins that are manifest, and another for those that are hidden. Manifest sins are to be expiated publicly before all in the daily proclamation of faults; but hidden sins, and those whose manifestation would scandalize the weak, are to be referred to confession. For a sin that is manifest must also be punished publicly; and one who has offended in secret must also be corrected in secret. There are certain rulers of cloistered persons who do not fear to bring the hidden sins of their subjects to public examination; in my judgment they do not carry out the rights of their office with sufficient prudence, since they close off the way of salvation to their subjects and bind the free act of confession under the bond of ill will. How dangerous this is, no one with even moderate wisdom fails to understand. For the holy founder rebukes this rashness in dealing with the passions when he says to monks: “The abbot must exercise very great solicitude, and with all sagacity and industry take care not to lose any of the sheep entrusted to him. Let him know that he has undertaken the care of weak souls, not tyranny over healthy ones.”

We have appointed for you four spiritual counselors from among the elders, so that if any of you has been overtaken by some hidden temptation and has conceived shame before us, he may be able to have secure recourse to them for his consolation. Yet so far we have found no one in the whole congregation whom we have observed to have withdrawn trust from us, whether in confession or outside it. For all of you know and plainly see that I seek nothing more in you, desire nothing more, than your salvation and the daily increase of spiritual progress. You are my crown, you are my good, you are my joy, because I count myself happy when I know that you have made progress in the love of God.

You have obtained a worthy opportunity for progress, dearest brothers, since the place in which we serve the Lord is remote from every disturbance, and we do not cease to advance you toward better things by daily admonition. Whenever one of you falls into some sin through human frailty, he is not permitted to remain long in dejection of spirit, since one is proclaimed by another in true fraternal charity each day for his negligences. This is the workshop of salvation, in which the mind is fed by the food of heavenly teaching, and the soul, by public sanctification, is daily cleansed from the stains of offenses. This is a place of purgation on earth, in which the monk is discreetly corrected for his negligences; and by humble patience, for the love of Christ, he is daily offered in a long martyrdom. It is my part, brothers, in this arrangement of faults, to correct discreetly those who are proclaimed; it is yours to suffer all things most patiently.
