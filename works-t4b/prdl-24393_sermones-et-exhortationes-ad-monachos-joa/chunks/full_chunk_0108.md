Let each of you strive, as much as he can, to love the Lord his God above all things with the whole strength of his mind, because love in God alone is what will open for you the gate of paradise. For the more ardently you now love God, the greater the glory with which you will be received in the heavenly homeland.

Happy is that monk in whose conscience, besides the love of Christ, no image of any occupation resides; who, waking, thinks of nothing but Christ; sleeping, dreams of Christ; living and dying, sighs most steadfastly for Christ. O how well and securely he dies who has always been diligent in the exercise of divine love, and at all times zealous in observing the commandments of God. For only the love of God is admitted into the kingdom of heaven; and the more fervent the mind has been in loving, the more blessed it will become in glory. He who loves much receives much; and he who now loves less will then also receive less in glory. Therefore, as much as each one can, let him love with his whole heart, with his whole soul, and with his whole strength, because our God is not to be loved tepidly or sluggishly, but fervently, wisely, and sweetly.

Now the love of God grows and is increased by spiritual exercises, just as a burning fire spreads by the addition of wood. For if the wood is withdrawn, the fire is extinguished; and if spiritual exercises are interrupted, charity is soon shipwrecked. But true love of God is never idle; rather, at every time it is delightfully occupied in those things that nourish and preserve charity. For the lover of God never forgets his beloved, but always meditates on his presence and beauty; and, in his prayers, most devoutly marvels at Jesus speaking to him. The true lover of Christ meditates day and night on the law of the Lord, and does with the greatest diligence everything he understands to be pleasing to God.

O how sweetly he burns whom the love of God has truly inflamed; how pleasantly he glows, whose mind the flame of the Holy Spirit has set on fire. Blessed is that monk who, full of divine love, withdraws himself wholly from earthly vanities and rests in the shadow of heavenly desire. O if only we had a love toward God as strong, whole, and vehement as mad and foolish lovers have for their corruptible mistresses; out of desire for them, empty men often become disgracefully insane and fear no scandals of this world. But the love of God is stronger than all things, if it has truly been rooted in us.

Whoever truly loves the Lord God always keeps in his memory his most bitter passion, because nothing causes the love of God so perfectly in the human heart, nothing so induces true compunction, as the never-interrupted remembrance of God’s benefits. Therefore let the written sequence of Christ’s passion always be in your memory, and let this word never depart from your mouth: O crucified Jesus, my love, by the power of your most innocent death, have mercy on me, and make me your faithful servant.

*On Serenity of Conscience.*

*Homily XXIV.*

With great solicitude, dearest brothers, we must keep watch, and with continual meditation take care that we always preserve our conscience, whose testimony we cannot escape, clean and unstained from every contagion of sin. For there is nothing more beautiful, nothing more pleasant, nothing finally more precious than a good conscience; when the mind has within itself a manifest testimony of rectitude, it suffers no gnawing of destructive unrest. An upright and good conscience is an incredible exultation of the mind and a sure consolation in every adversity. The monk who has obtained the testimony of a good conscience neither refuses to live nor fears the coming of future death.

A good conscience is always joyful, always glad, always fearless, and truly tranquil in the peace of Jesus Christ. In a good conscience there is always peace, piety, goodness, light, virtue, honor, joy, gladness, sweetness, and delight without dissipation. Conscience is the storehouse of the soul, since whatever is placed in it is reserved for the provision of the life to come. A good conscience is the best treasure; if anyone neglects to have it, he must be judged the most wretched and unhappy of all people. A good conscience is the dwelling place of the Holy Spirit and the earthly paradise of all graces.
