Homily 1, fol. II.

In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Here begins the first book of expositions to the monks, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim and afterward of Wurzburg: On the Spiritual Warfare of Monks.

First Homily.

Beloved brothers, Christian converts, and especially monks, must be subject to the various calamities of this world and, like gold in the furnace, be more fully refined and proved by tribulations. For monks belong to the Christian warfare, since they pass beyond the ordinary rule of living in a higher way, and obey not only the Lord’s precepts but also his counsels. It belongs to monks utterly to despise divine honors and all pleasures of the present world for love of God, and, having overcome carnal desires, to devote themselves continually to God alone. For “monk” is so called as though “solitary,” from oneness; and by the very title of his name he is admonished to contempt of the world. Therefore the office of monks is to be purified from all contagion in the solitude of the world, and to love Christ sweetly above all things.

For Christ is the goal of monks, toward which regular discipline is directed; whoever refuses to imitate him is not even a monk, nor indeed a Christian. Therefore, my brothers, we ought to know that whoever does not follow the Lord and Savior in true patience and humility gains nothing for salvation from being called Christian or monk. For he himself says in the holy Gospel: “Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my Father who is in heaven: he shall enter into the kingdom of heaven.” And how great the will of God is, the holy Apostle discloses, saying: “This is the will of God, your sanctification.” But since the necessary fulfillment of this precept of sanctification demands from us great tolerance of evils in all things and humility, and since no one is holy by his own strength, we must, my brothers, keep constant consideration of our vocation within ourselves, lest, growing numb through most destructive idleness, we be overcome in the soldier’s contest by the enemy, who, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking the incautious and languishing man whom he may devour.

For we are soldiers of God against the adversary, fighting a threefold battle: namely, against the flesh, the world, and the devil. The war is two-edged, and the struggle very hard against the one whom nature has made familiar by long endurance. Therefore, dearly beloved brothers, let us be armed with every kind of virtue, since he who is adorned with the splendor of good works is not easily conquered by the adversary. First let us arm ourselves with the most steadfast faith of the holy and undivided Trinity; its devout and sincere confession by mind and mouth drives far away all the wicked contrivances and deceits of demons, and, defeating the greatest enemy, overthrows him and utterly weakens him. Then let us take hold of the most fervent love of God and neighbor, which is fearful to demons above all things, especially if it has been rooted in true Christian humility. Let us not place confidence in ourselves, because he is called blessed who is always fearful; but he who is hard in mind will fall into evil.

Let us learn our leader, Christ, who says in the Gospel: “I am the way, the truth, and the life, because no one comes to the Father except through me.” Therefore, brothers, attend carefully to what we read in the holy Scriptures concerning our Savior, because whoever says that he remains in Christ, that is, in the way of truth, should strive to walk just as he walked. But it was necessary that he first suffer the reproach of the cross and so enter into his glory, by which we, his soldiers, unworthy though we are, are admonished that it is necessary for us to enter the kingdom of God through many tribulations. Therefore the Lord himself, who reigns, admonished us in the Gospel, saying: “Whoever wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow me.” Behold, Christ is set before us as the way to eternal life; although he is Lord of the heavenly and worldly powers, for our sake he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, and in that form, willingly and not by compulsion, bearing our infirmities, he was obedient to the Father even unto death, death on a cross. And so, if it was necessary for the Lord of the whole universe first to suffer, it certainly cannot be that we, his imitators, wicked and most vile sinners, ought not to suffer many things.
