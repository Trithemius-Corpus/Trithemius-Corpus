*All Things. XVIII. Folio XLIII.*

You have been called to the observance of God’s commandments; and you, given over to the pleasures of the flesh, think of nothing less than Christ. You have been invited to love the Lord Jesus above all things, and you walk according to the evil desires of your heart, showing yourself ungrateful to your Redeemer. You, who ought to have lived by the spirit, savor earthly things, and, sunk in pride, despise all the holy exercises of spiritual conduct.

And what will you answer on that day of judgment to the Judge, who will say to you, most ungrateful one: I, who am Lord of all, humbled myself for your sake, taking the form of a servant and being found in appearance as a man; I poured out my blood for you; for you I endured scourges; and for you, by my own willing choice, I underwent a death exceedingly bitter. I, O monk, loved you with my whole heart; I redeemed you by my blood from perpetual death; I called you in love to the heavenly kingdom, if you had kept my commandments. But you lived in opposition to my precepts, and, what is worse, although you had been sworn upon my words, you were not afraid to show yourself an apostate and impious traitor against me, your Lord. Now therefore render an account of your stewardship.

What then will you answer, O lazy and idle monk, who now consume this acceptable time so fruitlessly, and despise living in true monastic purity according to the rule? What, I say, will you answer? Will you deny the works of depravity that you have committed, intending deceitfully to show good works that you now refuse to perform, so that by a lie you may circumvent the Judge and thus escape the punishment due to your demerits? But you must not be ignorant that all things you have done, said, or thought throughout your whole life, good and evil, are manifest in the sight of the Judge, and nothing can be hidden from him, who alone knows all things. For all things are written in the book of God, and your conscience will bear open testimony against you concerning all your evil deeds, so that not even the least thought will be able to remain hidden. For the scripture of the prophets says to God: “Because the thought of man shall confess to you.” You are before the tribunal of the Judge who knows all things, before whose terrible judgment every creature will stand with very great fear and trembling; there no excuse at all is admitted, but each shall receive the sentence due to his merits, with appeal set aside. Thousands of thousands and ten hundred thousand holy spirits will attend him; and do you trust that you can deceive the Judge by a lie?

But hear my counsel, and, rising from your ruinous sloth, at last, while there is time, return to righteousness. Do not put it off from day to day, because it is exceedingly dangerous for one remaining in sins to be confident, and to reject the remedy of salvation that is offered. Remember that you are going to die, and when you do not expect it you will be called to judgment. Consider, I beg you, how uncertain the time of life is, how it slips away, and how quickly all mortal things are corrupted. Nothing among human things is stable, nothing fixed, nothing firm; but all things that we see are corruptible and change with time.

Consider also where now our brothers Valentius, Martin, and John are, who in the present year, while living with us, were healthy, unharmed, and glad; they spoke with us, ate, drank, and labored together with us, thinking of nothing less than that they would die this year. Behold, when they did not expect it, death came and seized them, and set their souls before the tribunal of the fearful Judge, who rendered to each according to the works of his hands. Behold, learn now by the example of your brothers that you too are going to die, and perhaps you will pass from this world much sooner than you can estimate.

Come to your senses, wretched man, while you still can; do penance in the time of your health, when the Judge promises mercy, and do not put off today’s good into the uncertain tomorrow. Rise up, even now, late though it is, at last from the sloth of your too negligent past way of life, in which you have hitherto shamefully rotted; and cleanse your conscience from every stain of sin; wipe away with tears the evil that you have committed; and from now on live innocently, as befits a monk. Take up the perfect resolution of amendment, and understand that the kingdom of heaven is promised not to those who begin the good, but to those who persevere unto the end. Know also that heaven is not reached by sloth and torpor, but by great labor, by the very great effort of a will to make progress, and by unwearied zeal for spiritual exercise.
