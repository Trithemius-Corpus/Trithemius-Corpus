*On the Custody and Origin of Thoughts*

The holy fear of the Lord never leaves the mind of a wise man; rather, like a strong and watchful fighter who always stands at the side of his prince, so it guards the mistress of the heart. It turns its ears away from every scurrility of words, and closes its eyes lest they see evil. It always walks according to the twelfth degree of humility, with its gaze fixed on the ground, praying to the Lord with the prophet: “Turn my eyes away, lest they see vanity; in your way give me life, O Lord.”

Thus I admonish you, brothers: direct your senses in the fear of the Lord, so that you may always be able to keep the dwelling of the heart clean and inviolate. Remember the saying of Scripture: “Be clean, you who bear the vessels of the Lord.” Consider, I pray, with what great cleanness and purity of mind you ought to shine, you who not only by reason of the priestly order bear the vessels of the Lord, but, what is more, are yourselves also vessels of the Lord. For the holy Christ-loving Paul says: “Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ?” And again he says: “Or do you not know that your members are the temple of the Holy Spirit, who is in you, whom you have from God, and that you are not your own?” And elsewhere: “For the temple of God is holy, which you are.” And concerning this same Paul, in the Acts of the Apostles the Lord said to Ananias: “Go, for this man is a vessel of election for me.”

The monk who exercises the priestly order in the Church of God is bound by a twofold reason to the observance of most holy purity. For Christ’s soldier must, because of the vow he has made to God, be pure, holy, and continually tireless in good works; and by reason of the priestly ministry, circumspect and most diligently watchful. For it is written: “Cursed is the man who does the work of God negligently.” You are ministers of almighty God, who daily sacrifice his Son at the altar; his work is entrusted to your hands, and therefore great purity of mind and body is required in your way of life.

Therefore, so that chaste purity of mind may remain, the instability of thought must be restrained. The senses, through which the integrity of the mind is corrupted, must be guarded at all times. For you are dead to the world and have now begun to live for Christ. It is not lawful for you to think anything except what is holy, nor is it fitting for you, as ministers of God, to love anything earthly. For he whom you serve, O monks, is God; he to whom you minister, my brothers, is almighty. Live pure, holy, and innocent lives, because it is not fitting for you to be occupied with the business of the world, you who have been called to the warfare of God. Let the love of God and purity of heart grow in you with time, so that the longer you live in this world, the holier you may become.

Always turn your sight away from every evil, so that you may be able to remain without failure in good thoughts. Always raise the eyes of your mind to heaven, and do not occupy the sight of the flesh with perishable things. Always close your hearing also against every useless, vain, and foolish word, and always think with dread upon the future judgment of God. Do not pollute your hands by unlawful and shameful contact with any things, so that you may be able to sacrifice the Son of God with a clean body and a pure conscience.

In food and drink it is not for monks to distinguish flavors, nor is it fitting for Christ’s soldiers to love bodily delights. Nature requires few things, and rustic foods by which it may be nourished; but appetite, unless it is restrained by the command of reason, is carried off toward unlawful things, by which the simplicity of nature is violated. Flee excess in all things, and preserve regular temperance in your manner of living.

Let none of you patiently listen to someone disparaging his brother or speaking ill of his neighbor. Let each one of you restrain his tongue from useless speech, and let a good monk blush to speak ill of Christian people. Let that threat of the Lord in the holy Gospel never depart from your memory, where he says: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. A good man brings forth good things from a good treasure, and an evil man brings forth evil things from an evil treasure. But I say to you that every idle word that men have spoken, they will render an account of it on the day of judgment.”

O sentence greatly to be feared! If an account is demanded for idle speech, what do detraction, scurrility, and foul speech deserve? Or if a useless word is judged liable to the reckoning of a strict examination, what will he answer who so readily speaks what is evil and destructive? An idle word is every utterance of human beings that has been brought forth without just usefulness and lacks the reason of lawful necessity. Therefore, brothers, consider your words, and let no one speak a word that does not pertain to the matter, or that lacks just usefulness or necessary reason.
