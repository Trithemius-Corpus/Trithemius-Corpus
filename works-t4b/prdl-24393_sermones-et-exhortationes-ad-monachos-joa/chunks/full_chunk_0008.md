*Second Homily.*

Fol. V.

...and all of you, according to your strength, toward the keeping of the military order. For we are frail and weak, and we have not long stood firm in our holy purpose, unless by daily exercises we continually renew the fervor of the spirit. For by reason of my office I am constrained to set before you in common those things that can conduce to your salvation; it belongs to you to listen patiently in all things to me as I speak, as the shepherd of your souls. For although I, a young man, am not fit to teach so great a multitude of elders, nevertheless, because I call myself shepherd over you, who have been committed to me, I must under necessity feed the flock that I have undertaken to govern.

What is mine to do, I willingly both do and shall do according to my strength, so long as I remain vigorous in this solicitude of pastoral care. Though young, I shall teach you elders, and I shall show you without interruption how you ought to serve as soldiers for him in whose army you have sworn. For the dreadful warning of our lawgiver does not escape me, when he says: “Let the abbot know that whatever the father of the household finds lacking in usefulness among the sheep falls upon the shepherd’s fault.”

And what follows concerns you, if, while I preach and admonish, the word of God is treated with contempt. “Only then,” he says again, “will he be free, if all diligence has been applied by the shepherd to the restless or disobedient flock, and all care has been shown for their diseased actions; then their shepherd, absolved in the judgment of the Lord, may say with the prophet to the Lord: ‘I have not hidden your righteousness in my heart; I have spoken your truth and your salvation; but they, despising it, have scorned me.’ And then at last for the disobedient sheep under his care, death itself shall be their prevailing punishment.”

Here no veil of excuse is left; no place is offered for escape, but the danger of both parties is openly described. For if I, holding the place of abbot, do not announce to you the righteousness of God, if I do not correct you when you sin according to the Rule, I have denied my office, and it will be imputed to me as a crime whatever is found lacking in holiness among you. Therefore, by reason of the pastoral office, it is mine to say what is expedient for the salvation of your souls; yours, however, is humbly to receive what I have proclaimed in the name of the Savior, and faithfully to fulfill it with a great heart, each one as far as he is able.

You have heard my danger if I do not cry out; you have also heard yours if you despise me when I preach the word of God to you. Therefore, so that by the mercy of the Savior we may all escape safely, let each one of us earnestly pursue what is commanded him in this spiritual warfare, taking the utmost care that we do not become deaf hearers of the truth and be measured accordingly. Let us very carefully remember what we have vowed, and let us preserve inviolate until death the holy order that we profess.

Let us hear our king saying in the Gospel to his soldiers: “If anyone serves me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also my servant shall be.” We are servants of the eternal king, priests and enclosed monks, and by a twofold reason we are bound to great holiness, since we have at once received the order of clerics and profess the rule of monastic purity. Let us therefore follow our commander, for whom we serve, along the path of the openness of all virtues, arming ourselves with right faith, firm hope, and the most fervent love of God and neighbor, in which the perfection of all holiness is comprehended. For whoever is girded with the defense of charity is never overcome by the adversary. For in charity truly Christ’s sweet service consists; and when it begins to burn in the heart of his soldier, it fears no adversities. For charity, as Gregory says, is never idle; rather, if it is true, it does great works. But if it refuses to work, it was not perfect charity.

Therefore, enclosed soldiers, you who have promised that you will serve the eternal king forever, cast away now the works of darkness, and put on the charity of God and neighbor, which is the strongest armor of salvation for all. If anyone has been clothed in it, he will fear no labors, however hard, nor any contrary things of this world. He does not fear hunger, thirst, persecutions for the Lord, reproach, mockeries, prisons, chains, or whatever hard and adverse things may come; rather, everyone who has once perfectly tasted the charity of God will remain unconquered against whatever raging things of the world.
