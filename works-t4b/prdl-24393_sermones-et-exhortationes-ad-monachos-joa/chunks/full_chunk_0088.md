Homily XVIII. Folio XLV.

...is known as patience. By this, the more each person has been instructed in the law of the Lord and in the knowledge of the teaching of rational nature, the more patient he will be in adversities. What if I speak more boldly, lover of truth: adversities profit us more than prosperous things, if we are instructed and are not forgetful observers of our own condition.

The third degree of patience, certainly the highest and most perfect of the remaining ones, is deservedly granted to those who not only bear contrary things calmly, when need arises, for the love of God and the benefit of the brethren, but who, inflamed by a more fervent spirit of charity, even of their own accord offer themselves and hasten eagerly to receive insults and injuries. And this kind of patience is divided in two, just as a twofold account of the martyrdom of the saints is found. For some, although they could have remained hidden while persecution of believers was raging, because the charity of Christ burned in them, with no one compelling them but by their own free will, presented themselves before the tribunals of the persecutors, and publicly confessing Jesus Christ, the redeemer of the world, received those torments for themselves with joy. Others, though they did not present themselves of their own accord, nevertheless gladly received the martyrdom offered to them, always ready to endure for the faith of Christ whatever the cruelty of persecutors could have brought against them.

The most radiant confessors are rightly seen to hold this degree; and although the cruelty of the impious did not take them away by persecuting them, they in no way lost the glory of martyrdom. For those who crucified their flesh in this world for the name of Christ endured both a voluntary and a lasting martyrdom; through abstinence and humility they were made glorious, remaining constant in soul and most patient, and always most ready to receive bodily martyrdom.

Therefore, O monks, take care to be imitators of virtues, so that, since executioners who put the body to death for Christ are now lacking, by fighting against the desires of the flesh and by patiently enduring adversities for the love of God, you may show yourselves martyrs in mind and spirit. In time of peace the best kind of martyrdom among the saints is to afflict the flesh by abstinence for the name of God, to desire nothing in this world, always to advance in good works, and, if any evil happens, to bear it in all things gladly and patiently with cheerfulness of mind. Henceforth, dearest brothers, live by this rule of patience, if you desire to reign with Christ forever. Bear one another’s works, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ: whatever each person desires to be done for himself, let him also cheerfully bestow upon his brother.

On the True Humility of Monks. Homily Nineteen.

The judgment of all the teachers, dearest brothers, is that the highest and chief virtue of monks is none other than humility, because it is itself the mother and nurse of all virtues. For humility makes the monk; pride destroys and corrupts him. Hence the Lord says through Isaiah: “To whom shall I look, except to the poor little one, contrite in spirit, who trembles at my words?” For God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble; and the beginning of every sin is pride, and without true humility no one can obtain salvation.

Since this is so, it is necessary that I, a brother crucified to you, exhort you without interruption to flee pride and always preserve deep humility in all your works. For no good work is believed to please the Lord God if it is presented without the seasoning of humility. Humility is the most wholesome medicine of faithful souls, which restores all the swelling wounds of a spirit that fears compunction, corrects what is depraved, and removes what grows up excessively.

O humility, mother and parent of all virtues: you strengthen patience, stir up compunction, illumine charity, adorn chastity, make prayer fruitful, and commend fasting and abstinence. O holy humility, truly beloved of God: through penance you justify the impious; you preserve the one who has been set in the purpose of holiness; you powerfully open heaven to those who love you; you close hell to penitents. O venerable humility, exceedingly pleasing to God: you compelled the Son of the eternal Father to descend from heaven into the womb of Mary, the most chaste and perpetual Virgin; you made him appear visible to us in the world; you placed him, wrapped in poor cloths, in a manger, so that he might adorn us in heaven with the ornaments of perpetual happiness.
