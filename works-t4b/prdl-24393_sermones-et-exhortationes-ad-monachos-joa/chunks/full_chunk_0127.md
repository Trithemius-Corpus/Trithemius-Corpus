Sermon M. Fol. LXV.

...he longs to put on. He willingly uses cheaper garments, and takes very great delight in those things which others despise. Beautiful and ornately arranged garments do not befit a monk, but the cheapest ones, by which he may merely defend the body from the harm of inclement weather. For all fastidiousness is forbidden to cloistered persons, who, by reason of the profession they have made before God, are bound to contempt of the world. Humility flees lofty things; it seeks what is lowly.

The twenty-third sign of monastic humility shows a monk voluntarily given over to servile duties and services for the brothers, and ready for all things whatsoever he has been ordered to do. For true humility obeys even inferiors with the readiest will, and is never indignant against one who commands. As much as he is able, he desires to serve all for the love of Christ, because he faithfully loves God with his whole heart. For whatever service he renders to the brothers, he firmly believes it all to have been shown to Christ.

The twenty-fourth sign of humility a monk then obtains in truth, when for the love of God he delights in taking care of the lower offices in the monastery, which the rest despise. We read that many once ascended to this grade of true humility, and we do not doubt that they were of great merit before God. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: Everyone who humbles himself will be exalted; for God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. The humble monk imitates Christ; the proud one, the devil.

The twenty-fifth sign of monastic humility is, in any gathering of people, always to seek the lower place for sitting, and not to prefer oneself to an equal or an inferior. It is not for monks to contend proudly over preeminence of place, nor does it befit a soldier of Christ to wish to surpass others in honor. For the true monk, who loves humility, is content with the lowest place and desires to excel no one.

The twenty-sixth sign of humility belongs especially to him who, though learned and educated, because of the humble abasement of himself, hides all doctrine and knowledge. For the monk who is truly humbled is always found readier to listen than to speak. For the divine priest Jerome says: The monk has the office not of one teaching but of one mourning, who either laments himself or the world, and fearfully awaits the coming of the Lord. The arrogance of monks is hateful both to God and to men, and is always liable to sin.

The twenty-seventh sign of humility is perfectly attained by the one who, out of love for regular discipline, delights more in being silent than in speaking. The humble lover of Christ loves silence, and, lest he offend in speech, is accustomed to speak very little. Humility is rarely found among the talkative, and he who is quick to speak casts down his own soul. A loquacious and garrulous monk is not free from pride, and the fool multiplies words. Whoever desires, in the spiritual contest, to overcome his adversary through humility must not neglect to restrain his tongue from much speaking and from every idle word. It is written in the book of Proverbs that in much speaking sin will not be lacking.

Whoever has obtained the twenty-eighth sign of perfect humility will avoid laughter and all disordered gestures of those who guffaw. Mourning, not laughter, especially befits a monk, as one who is dead to all the levities of the world. But the wise man says: The fool raises his voice in laughter; but the wise man will scarcely laugh silently.

The twenty-ninth sign of perfect humility takes its beginning from the fear of the Lord, for whose honor, as much as he is able, he flees and detests every sin. For everyone who does not fear to sin was not truly humble. But the monk who follows Christ according to his strength in true humility is accustomed to flee every sin, and, as much as he can, to exercise himself in the pursuit of virtues. Humility confesses its sin, and as often as it has offended even in the least things, it does not rest until it has amended everything through true penance. He who is truly humble is always found zealous to preserve cleanness of conscience. For the nourishment of humility is charity, which does not permit the one who has it to be idle.

The thirtieth sign of true humility shows a monk confirmed in the purpose of holiness, and makes him subject to all the brothers, even the younger ones. Whatever the pastor commands, he obeys simply; he never debates the commands of a senior, nor asks the reason why he orders it so. If impossible things are commanded, he is ready in spirit; for any hard and arduous things whatsoever, he is prepared without contradiction. He is directed by the counsel of his pastor: he believes him, trusts him, obeys him, and willingly is governed by his command.
