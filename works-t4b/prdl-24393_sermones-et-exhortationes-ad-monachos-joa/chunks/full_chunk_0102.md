Homily XXI. Fol. LI.

For unless the mind has first been blinded, the will does not yield to the demon. The spirit of fornication first gravely darkens the mind; afterward it fights until it has hurled it, blinded, into sin. But afterward, when it has cast the monk down into the mire of unspeakable crime, it obstructs him in two ways, lest he rise again to repentance. For either it subtly tries to make light of the gravity of the sin, so that he may not fear a great penalty, or it brings the severity of judgment back to the fallen man’s memory, so that he may fall into the pit of despair. By these snares the sinful soul is caught; and the longer it has lain in the depth of misery, the more hardened it becomes.

Flee idleness, O cloistered men, and avoid gluttony and the excess of the belly with the greatest care, because the monk who stuffs his belly with food and drink provides the demon of fornication with matter for doing harm. Put on true contrition of heart, and restrain the illicit motions of the flesh through tears of prayer. For no one is able rightly to conquer his body who has not first taken up the goad of contrition. But he cannot attain the grace of holy contrition who has not first learned perfectly to despise and deny himself. Whoever, however, has perfectly denied himself for the love of Christ, already overcoming the assaults of the flesh, will more easily obtain the grace of contrition. For he who has been mortified in will desires nothing carnal, nothing base.

But who among us, brothers, would dare say that he has reached this grace of mortification? Which of us does not know himself to be weak? We run in the arena of spiritual warfare, and in daily combat we fight against vices. We have received no assurance of peace, even if at times we have peace. The more familiar to us is the weakness of the flesh, the harder are the battles of chastity. Here we must be especially watchful, since we carry about a familiar enemy and endure, as it were, a domestic instigator of evil. Therefore, so long as we live in this mortality, let us always fear the corruptor of chastity. Let us beware of everything that arouses lust, and as much as we can, let us flee sloth and idleness. For fullness of bread and idleness are the strongest enemies of true chastity; unless they have been driven far away by abstinence and the labor of salutary exercise, they threaten immediate shipwreck to chastity.

Keep your hearts with every guard, O my dearest brothers, and always keep watch in spirit against your adversary, who never sleeps. Arm yourselves with the breastplate of perfect faith in Jesus Christ, so that in deed and belief you may be true Christians. Always be of one mind in brotherly charity, and be united without pretense in the true observance of regular discipline. Love fasting and continence; pray without ceasing. Flee idleness as the pernicious plague of the soul, and continually exercise yourselves in holy works. Love silence, and do not flee monastic solitude, if you desire to obtain true purity of mind and body. Keep continual guard over your eyes, ears, and speech, lest death enter the soul through the windows. Flee conversations with women, and avoid even the touch of holy women as the burning of fire. No one safely looks willingly upon what brings the sting of death to the desirous man. Always behold with faithful eyes of the mind, through love, our Lord Jesus Christ dying for you on the cross; occupy yourselves daily in a salutary way with his evangelical teaching; give humble thanks for his passion and likewise for his death. If you do these things with continued perseverance in true simplicity of heart until death, we trust that you will be rewarded with the fellowship of the saints.

On Brotherly Charity among Monks.
Homily XXIII.

Let us hear, my dearest brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ commanding in the holy Gospel: Whatever you wish men to do to you, do the same also to them. This is indeed a brief but solid admonition of brotherly charity, without which no one can ascend to the love of God. For no one can love God who is shown not to love his fellow Christian. Hence the blessed apostle John says: Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that every murderer does not have eternal life abiding in him. Hence, if anyone has said, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar. Therefore Paul, the holy lover of Christ, exhorts us, saying: Bear one another’s burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ. And Peter, prince of the apostles, admonished us thus: Before all things, he says, have continual mutual charity among yourselves, because charity covers a multitude of sins.
