Homily III. Fol. IX.

It restrains anger; it treads down the proud; it loves the humble; it calms those at odds; it reconciles enemies. It is gentle to all, burdensome to none, troublesome to none, oppressive to none. It does not know how to be exalted; it does not know how to be puffed up. Whoever has received it, let him hold it; whoever has lost it, let him seek it again; whoever has let it slip away, let him search for it, because whoever is not found in it is disowned by the Father, disinherited by the Son, and no less made alien from the Holy Spirit. He can never come to the inheritance of God who has refused to observe the testament of peace.

For the place of God is in peace, but the dwelling of the devil is in anger and disturbance. Therefore every person who does not have this threefold peace in his heart, who does not offer it in his speech, and who does not show it in his works, is not to be called either a monk or a Christian. Hence the Lord says in the Gospel: “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God.” If those who make peace are called sons of God, beyond doubt those who throw peace into confusion are sons of Satan.

Therefore, my dearest brothers, have among yourselves that peace which the world cannot give, which is the peace of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, of which the prophet Isaiah said: “And the work of justice shall be peace; and the service of justice, silence and security forever.” For God is the Lord of peace and love, and so whoever violates peace and brotherly love shows that he is not a true lover of God. Let the true peace of Christ be in your hearts, and you will fear none of this world’s adversities. Let there also be true peace among you, not painted peace, because Christ desires peace not from the mouth but from the heart, and wants one’s neighbor to be greeted with affection rather than with lips. Therefore if any one of you has saddened his brother in anything, let him make satisfaction to him as soon as possible before sunset; and take care, all of you, that the sun not set upon your anger. For anger grown old begets hatred and strips the soul of every adornment of virtue. Therefore, monk, if you have sinned against your brother, do penance at once before him and, falling at his knees, beg forgiveness.

“And the sword,” he says, “of the Spirit, which is the word of God,” through every prayer and entreaty, we soldiers of Christ must always hold in our hands. The word of God, which is contained in the holy Scriptures, is a victorious defense against all attacks of demons, as we are warned by the example of our Savior, who, when tempted by the devil, with calm gentleness hurled in his face the testimonies of the divine Scriptures, saying: “It is written: Man lives not by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God.”

And you, monk, serving as a soldier for Christ, whenever you are tempted by the devil, immediately imprint on your forehead the sign of the holy cross, and say with Saint John Chrysostom in a certain homily: “I renounce you, Satan, with all your iniquities, and I join myself to you, Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, because it is written: The soul that has sinned, that one shall die.” This is the sword of the Spirit, sharpest on both sides; if you brandish it in your hands against the enemy from the very beginning of temptation, with God’s protection you will easily overcome all his devices and ambushes. For Scripture says: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

And so, rising early in the morning, at once make that protestation which I have described against the adversary of your salvation; and neither the devil nor any perverse person will be able to harm you by incantation or by malefice, whether that be spaticum [unclear] or something else. Do this same thing when you are about to sleep at night, and as often as you perceive any temptation of the malignant spirit rising up in your thought. Thus you will be safe in all your ways, and the devil will flee far from you and will not dare come nearer to you, unless your own sloth first overthrows you, so that either you cast away the sword or at once commit some mortal sin.

For Scripture says: “Those who do iniquity and sin are enemies of their own soul.” Solomon also says in Proverbs: “His own iniquities seize the impious man, and he is bound by the cords of his sins. He shall die because he did not have discipline, and in the multitude of his folly he shall be deceived.” Therefore, dearest brothers, if you wish to dwell securely under the protection of almighty God, flee sin, because sin separates us from the Lord’s help, just as purity and cleanness of conscience join us to God, as the prophet Isaiah says: “Behold, the hand of the Lord is not shortened, so that it cannot save; nor is his ear made heavy, so that it cannot hear. But your iniquities have divided between you and your God, and your sins have hidden his face from you, so that he would not hear.” Jeremiah also says: “Our iniquities have turned these things away, and our sins have kept good

On the reading and study of the divine Scriptures: Homily IV.

Since all Scripture, divinely inspired, as the holy Christ-lover says, is useful for teaching, for arguing, for correcting, and for instructing in justice, so that the man of God may be perfect, furnished for every good work, we ought gladly to turn our effort to the reading of the divine Scriptures, and to direct whatever study of secular literature we once sweated over to the sharp edge of this contemplation. For in the divine Scriptures whatever saving knowledge requires is found expressed most abundantly, so that what you do not find there is by no means necessary for salvation.

Indeed, the majesty of the divine Scriptures far surpasses all the knowledge and teaching of the whole world. The Holy Spirit infused it into men of God so that it might be written, and, so that it may be understood, he grants the grace of his illumination to devoted readers. My brothers, turn this Scripture over diligently with purity of mind and the fervor of twofold charity, because whoever truly loves God spends every time that he can find free for leisure in sacred reading.

Let the frenzied acts, so to speak, of fleshly lovers be incentives for you, I pray, toward the wholesome study of the Scriptures: when they receive letters and tokens from their mistresses, they do not cease to read them, and they read them again and again and kiss them; and, as a greater sign of love, they either hang them about their neck or place them on their breast, so that the cherished memory of the one they desire may never depart from their mind. Consider, brothers, the madness of fleshly people in love, and blush at your own lukewarmness concerning the love of God.

Which of you is affected toward the holy Scriptures of God with as much love of heart as a blind lover, mad with desire for the filthy and utterly vain love letters of one little prostitute, is inflamed with affection? He, because of love for the absent woman whom he desires, protects her letters and little gifts with frequent kisses; he examines each individual stroke again and again, scrutinizing it with excessive love; in everything he does he always thinks of the absent woman; anxiously he hastens to come into her sight.

O if we who are called Christian monks, who serve as soldiers for the supreme and immortal King, Christ Jesus, who are commanded to love our God above all things and are instructed to love nothing in this world besides him, if, I say, we pursued our Creator with as much love as fleshly-minded people honor and love their corruptible and mortal girlfriends. Holy Scripture, when read daily, makes God known to us; it reveals his will to us; it instructs us what we ought to do and discloses what we ought to avoid. Through it the Almighty speaks with us, and, forming us by an invisible teaching, consoles us, as the holy Apostle says: “Whatever things have been written were written for our instruction, so that through patience and the consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope.”

Therefore the true soldier of Christ, and especially the cloistered man, who loves God with his whole heart and desires to please him alone, embraces the divine Scriptures, and especially the holy Gospel, with affection, and is constantly occupied in meditating on its reading. But whoever neglects the reading of the divine Scriptures plainly shows that he is not a true lover of God. For what does Scripture itself say? “Whoever turns his ear away so that he does not hear the laws, his prayer will be execrable.” But “blessed are those who search the testimonies of the Lord; with their whole heart they seek him.”

And so, my brothers, if you truly love the Lord Jesus with your whole heart, if as true soldiers you desire above all things to please him, if finally you wish to advance toward the perfection of monastic life, you ought always to occupy yourselves with fervent affection in the reading of the divine Scriptures. How useful the reading of the Scriptures is, blessed Jerome indicates when he says: “Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” For after God nothing in this life is taken in more pleasantly, nothing is felt more sweetly, nothing separates the mind more powerfully from love of this world, nothing strengthens the soldier of Christ so much against all temptations of adversaries, nothing disposes and confirms the monk so greatly for every good work, as frequent reading and ardent study of the Scriptures.
