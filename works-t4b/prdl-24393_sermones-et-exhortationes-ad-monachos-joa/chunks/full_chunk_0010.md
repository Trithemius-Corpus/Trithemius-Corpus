I shall love you; I shall cherish you; in the midst of my saints I shall establish you glorious forever.

Hear me now, my servant, who for reverence and honor of my name have bound yourself in the strictest warfare of monks. With a great and perfect heart follow the footprints of my steps, as that blessed preceptor of yours, once my friend and yours, taught you, and strive with all your efforts to endure injuries. Let the enticements of the flesh not deceive you, O valiant soldier. Let the vain and destructive commerce of this world not seduce your mind, dedicated to me. Never yield to the exceedingly deceitful suggestions of unclean spirits, who rage with all their power for your ruin, but remember that your life in this world is most wretched and brief, and that death stands before the doors, which no mortal has ever been able to escape or outlive. Always keep in memory those last things which you must necessarily undergo; and because the pleasure of the flesh passes by in the swiftest flight, take up dread and contempt for this sojourn which must soon be ended.

Just as brief labor for me in the world, borne with patience and charity, prepares eternal happiness for my soldiers, so the desires and pleasures of the flesh, which suddenly pass away, sink the soul of the sinful master down to hell, to be tormented without end. Therefore, O monk who minister to me and follow me along the difficult path of regular observance, let all your desires be directed toward me. Give me your whole heart if you desire happiness. Seek me alone if you love your own life and salvation. When you have loved me, you are tranquil and holy; when you have followed me, you are blessed and glorious without end.

What, my brothers, would you do if it happened to you to hear these words which we have spoken with your own ears, as uttered by our king and savior? What, I ask, would you do? I certainly believe that great compunction would arise in your hearts if you heard Christ speaking in person. But we, who bear Christ’s place in the monastery, speak the words which he has given, and you do not attend. We proclaim to you the contest necessary for salvation, and you show no signs of compunction.

If you do not know what the cause is, I shall say something useful; now, I pray, understand. I see some of you shudder at sacred readings, devoted to vanities, and care for nothing less than the perfection of spiritual warfare. Their god is their belly, and their glory is in confusion. Do you think, vilest idlers, who savor nothing spiritual, that Christ will come again from heaven in order to speak to you the things which he entrusted to his preachers to say? Have you not heard Paul saying: Christ rising again from the dead now dies no more; death shall no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God.

Once Christ lived on earth; once he appeared visibly among mortals; once he preached the way of eternal salvation. He will not add a second time what he entrusted to faithful pastors and vicars over his household, who are to teach the ignorant. Wishing to make their authority clear, he said in the Gospel: He who hears you hears me, and he who despises you despises me. Therefore hear those whom Christ has appointed over you as rulers, to whom he has entrusted the administration of spiritual wheat. In the holy Gospel he said: Who, do you think, is the faithful and wise steward, whom the lord has set over his household, to give them their measure of wheat in due season? Blessed is that servant whom his lord, when he comes, shall find so doing.

Therefore we exhort you, brothers, in the Lord Jesus, and with whatever diligence we can we beg you: do not consume your days in vanity, but consider the extreme brevity of this life, and bear fruits worthy of penance while the opportunity is granted to you, lest, if you neglect to repent, the time be cut off. For you know what divine Scripture says to us: The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night. Do not, I pray, put off the good, because none of you knows when he is to die. Rather, attend to that true saying which has lately been put in verse: For this reason, do not postpone yourself to future hours; he who is not fit today will be less fit tomorrow.

Let no brother promise himself a long life, but rather let each one think that death is near at hand, and therefore let him live so prepared as if he were to depart from the body tomorrow. For the hope of a longer life has deceived many, and, holding them back from the fervor of their holy purpose, has suddenly sunk them down to hell. Therefore perfect your warfare, which after not much time must necessarily be ended, in such a way that you may not fear to appear before your king when he comes. Always be fearful, because you do not have here an abiding city, nor can you remain long in this mortal life; death will soon come and compel you to depart.

Whoever is a true monk and desires to be a faithful soldier of Christ despises all the delights of this world. He always thinks of his Lord as present to him, and, having cast aside all impediments and every little burden, he takes care above all how he may please him. He should not be anxious in seeking the necessities of life, since the providence of his pastor arranges all these things for those subject to him. Let the monk’s whole care be to keep himself clean from this world, so that, free and released from all things, he may seek nothing except God and think of nothing except Jesus crucified for us.

O happy soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised, if he remains faithful, prudent, and watchful in the love of God to the end. Certainly nothing is happier than the good monk, to whom the Son of God promises himself as reward; yet nothing is more laborious than he who, placed in the contest of battle, daily risks his salvation. Nothing is stronger than the monk who conquers the world together with the devil; and nothing weaker than the one who is overcome by his own flesh.

Therefore, O monk, you must always keep watch against the hidden enemy of your salvation. He is never more easily conquered than when he is kept far from the entrance of the mind. Therefore, if you desire to escape victorious against him, do not allow him to move the devices of his deceit and fraud nearer to you. Guard your senses first of all, lest death enter through the windows; and beware especially the ambushes of your enemy when he has allowed you for a time to be secure in your devotion. Never forget the warning of the wise man, who says: Unless you hold yourself constantly in the fear of the Lord, your house, that is, your conscience, will quickly be overthrown. Therefore Solomon said in Proverbs: Blessed is the man who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil.

For while the monk lives presumptuously in security, he is without doubt heading toward destruction. The soldier of Christ must therefore always be prepared against his adversaries, so that as long as he lives in this mortal body he may not trust that he has perfectly overcome them. For the flesh always desires against the spirit; and lest the soul ever be overcome, it is necessary to fight as long as life continues. Whenever you have conquered your invisible enemy, he rises up again and gathers greater forces against you, so that, if he cannot obtain victory by strength, he may at least bring on despair by persistence.

But you, most valiant soldier of Christ, take care not to fail in the contest of this very brief struggle. Take care not to lay down the arms of necessary defense before the battle is completed at the end. For the Savior says: He who perseveres to the end will be saved. Do not give rest to your soul as long as it has been granted to you to remain in this miserable pilgrimage, but always do some good, lest the devil ever find you idle. Always remember the warning of Scripture, which says: Whatever your hand can do, do it earnestly, because neither work nor reason nor wisdom nor knowledge will exist in hell, where you are hastening.

There is no one who will live forever, but we all die, and like waters we sink into the earth, which will not return again to their former state. Then there will no longer be time for doing good toward the merit of eternal happiness; but whatever a person has sown here and now in due season, that he will then receive as reward. Therefore holy Scripture says again: In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand cease. And Saint Paul, admonishing Timothy, says: Labor as a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No one serving God as a soldier entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has proved himself. For also, of those who compete in the contest, no one will be crowned unless he has competed lawfully. Everyone who contends in the contest abstains from all things. In another place the same lover of Christ says: I want you to be without anxiety.
