*On the Progress of Spiritual Warfare.*

Let no one, brothers, promise himself a long life; rather, let each one think that death is impending, and let him live so prepared, as if he were to depart tomorrow. For hope of a longer life has deceived many, and by holding them back from the fervor of their holy purpose has suddenly plunged them down to hell. Therefore pass through this life of yours, which must necessarily end before long, in such a way that you do not fear to appear before your king when he comes. Always be mindful that you do not have here a lasting city, and that you cannot remain long in this mortal life; but soon the day will come which will compel you to emigrate.

Whoever is a true monk and desires to be a faithful soldier of Christ despises all the delights of this world, always thinks of the Lord as present to him, and, casting away from himself every impediment and burden, takes care above all how he may be able to please him. He ought not to be anxious in seeking the necessities of life, since the providence of the shepherd disposes all these things for those subject to him. Let the whole care of the monk be to keep himself clean from this age, so that, free and released from everything, he seeks nothing except God, thinks of nothing except Jesus crucified for us.

O happy soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised, if, faithful, watchful, and vigilant in the love of God, he remains to the end. Certainly nothing is happier than the good monk, to whom the Son of God promises himself as a reward; nothing also more laborious, for, set in the arena of battle, he is in danger each day concerning his salvation; and nothing is stronger than the monk who conquers the world and the devil together; nothing also weaker than he who is overcome by his own flesh.

Therefore, O monk, you must always keep watch against the hidden enemy of your salvation, who is more easily overcome if he is kept far from the entrance of the mind. Hence, if you desire to come away victorious against him, you must learn beforehand the tricks of his deceit and fraud. Guard your senses at the first entrance, lest presently he enter through the windows; and beware especially the ambushes of your enemy, when he has allowed you for a time to be secure in your devotion. Never forget the admonition of the wise man, who says: Unless you hold yourself steadily in the fear of the Lord, your house will quickly be overthrown. Therefore Solomon said in Proverbs: Blessed is the man who is always fearful; but he who is hard of mind will fall into evil. For as soon as a monk has presumed upon security, without doubt he tends toward destruction.

Therefore the soldier of Christ must always be prepared against his adversaries, so that as long as he lives in this mortal body he does not trust that he has perfectly overcome them. For the flesh always lusts against the spirit; and lest the soul ever be overcome, so long as one happens to live, it is necessary to fight. As often as you overcome the invisible enemy, he rises again and contends with you with greater savagery, so that, if he cannot obtain victory by strength, he may at least bring on despair by persistence. But you, most valiant soldier of Christ, beware lest you fail in the arena of this very fierce contest; beware lest, before the battle is completed at the end, you lay down the arms of your necessary struggle. For the Savior says: He who perseveres to the end will be saved.

Do not give rest to your soul as long as it has been granted you to remain in this miserable pilgrimage, but always do some good thing, lest the devil ever find you idle. Always remember the admonition of Scripture, which says: Whatever your hand is able to do, do it earnestly, because there will be no work, nor reason, nor necessity, nor knowledge among the dead, where you are hastening. There is no one who lives forever, but we all die, and like water we shall flow down into the earth, and shall not return again to our former state. Then there will be no more time for working good toward the merit of eternal happiness; but whatever you, O man, have sown here now in time, that you will receive as your reward. Therefore holy Scripture again says: In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening let not your hand cease. And Saint Paul, admonishing Timothy, says this: Labor like a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular business, so that he may please the one to whom he has approved himself. For also among those who compete in the arena, no one will be crowned unless he has competed lawfully. But everyone who contends in the arena abstains from all things. In another place, too, the same lover of Christ says: I want you to be without anxiety.

You therefore, soldier of Christ, who contend in the arena of daily battle so that at last you may appear glorious forever before your God, imitate the perishable gymnosophist, who, competing in the arena for a perishable crown, abstains from all things so that he may not be defeated and may run more swiftly, naked and free. And so, if you desire to walk the way of God in true purity, if you wish to conquer the enemy in the arena of battle, cast away from yourself all garments of carnal pleasures. For whoever, with hair and clothing cast off from himself, has learned to run naked, carries around nothing besides his unencumbered body by which he can be held by the adversary. For he who runs naked has stripped himself of every anxiety over the things clamoring around him, and thinks of nothing other than victory; nor meanwhile does he care what is being done at home with wife or children. Nothing present recalls him from the course; the agility of his companions does not deter him; among many he contends as if alone, hastening breathless to the prize as if certain.

Imitate him, soldier of Christ, in the spiritual contest; contemplate the naked Savior hanging naked on the cross, so that you may be able to lay low your adversary all the more easily the less he sees that you possess anything of the vanities of the world. Naked, therefore, light, and unencumbered, fight on every side; naked and released from all things, fly into heaven. Naked you came into this world; naked you will return to the earth. Thinking diligently on these things, my brothers, run in such a way that you may obtain eternal life with all the saints. Amen.

The homily ends.

What Is the Armor of Spiritual Warfare. Homily III.

Most dear brothers, today we have heard the holy Apostle writing in his epistle to the Ephesians: Put on the armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the snares of the devil; for our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of these darknesses, against the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavens. Therefore take up the armor of God, that you may be able to resist on the evil day, and in all things stand perfect. He then adds what the armor of God is: Stand, therefore, having girded your loins in truth, and having put on the breastplate of justice, and having shod your feet in preparation for the Gospel of peace; in all things taking up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the fiery darts of the wicked one; and take up the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God; praying at every time in the Spirit through every prayer and supplication, and in the same keeping watch with all perseverance and supplication for all the saints.

To the Thessalonians likewise he says: But we who are of the day, let us be sober, putting on the breastplate of faith and charity, and as a helmet the hope of salvation. These are the arms, brothers, with which the soldiers of Christ, clothed, wage war against the savage adversary; these are the insignia of the virtues of monks, without which no one obtains victory over vices. The instruments of human warfare, which most mortals pursue with excessive zeal, are helmet, shield, breastplate, sword, dagger, cuirass, belt, lance, and many other things of this kind, with which men preparing to go to war are most carefully armed; and unless they have been most diligently equipped in their whole body, they are certainly judged less fit for fighting.

But the arms of our spiritual warfare are faith, hope, charity, patience, humility, obedience, chastity, together with the holy virtues of the soul enumerated one by one by our blessed teacher in the fourth chapter, where he makes beautiful mention of the instruments of good works. And would that, my brothers, we were as careful and diligent about these spiritual arms of our warfare as we see worldly men to be anxious about their corruptible armor. But now, what I say with too much grief, since we shamefully cast aside the arms of salvation with which we ought to fight down all our adversaries, what else remains except that we fall before our enemy in the contest?

But perhaps you will say: If Christian faith, as the Apostle testifies, belongs to the arms of spiritual warfare, we certainly intend to remain in it. But suppose this is so. What, however, does Saint James say in his epistle? What will it profit, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith, but does not have works? Will faith be able to save him? For just as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead. Therefore, whenever Scripture speaks of faith, it must always be understood not as ordinary and dead faith, but as faith formed by good works and illumined by the sweetness of divine love.
