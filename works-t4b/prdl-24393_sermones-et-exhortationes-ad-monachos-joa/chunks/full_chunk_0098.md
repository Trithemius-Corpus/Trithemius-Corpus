Homily XXI.
Fol. L.

... for love of him, unworthy men have set me, an unworthy man, over you as abbot. For my part, I shall not be troublesome to you if you run well; but if anyone strays from the way of truth, I am compelled, even unwillingly, to oppose him with all my strength.

Let the monk always think about his entrance into the monastery, and never forget the first purpose of his profession. In all your works, O monk, always remember that you are not long-lived upon the earth, but will shortly depart from this life, and perhaps will die when you do not expect it. Therefore stand by the purposes of inviolate holiness, and always be watchful in good works, so that when the Lord comes and calls you, he may find you prepared. Remember that you are a pilgrim on earth and have here no lasting city; but just as a traveler hastens toward the end of his journey, so you hasten swiftly toward the thing to which you draw nearer every day without interruption. Therefore, while you are healthy, put your house and the confidence of your soul in order, so that soon you may not be afraid; for rarely does he die well who is not prepared as one who is to die each day. O how secure a monk departs from this life, who, keeping his conscience in all purity, has lived at all times, without rebuke, according to the integrity of the rule of our most holy lawgiver, even unto death.

Let your mind continually hold in memory our Lord Jesus Christ as if present, indeed as never absent; and let no hour pass in which you do not think with thanksgiving upon him suffering for you. Stand in humility and true obedience, and no adversities will ever prevail against you. Just as trees, deep-rooted and sending great roots into the earth, are not easily torn from their place, so the monk who possesses charity and obedience in humility, together with the other virtues, is by no means overcome by demons. Obedience, brothers, is a great virtue, which brings the other virtues into the mind and, through the humility of service, preserves them once brought in. May our Lord Jesus Christ himself, who for us became obedient unto the death of the cross, enlighten your hearts by his grace and sweetly incline your minds at all times to the observance of the regular life: he who lives and reigns with God the Father in the power of the Holy Spirit, God, for all ages of ages. Amen.

On Continence and True Chastity: Homily XXI.

My very dear brothers, I have obtained from God a slender grace, by which, as your pastor, I may be found worthy and fit at once in life and in speech. For I know, nor does it escape me, how dangerous a thing the governance of souls is for a young man and beginner such as I am, when one who has not yet learned to direct and govern himself according to the norm of justice presumes to undertake the instruction of others. I blush to open my mouth among you for teaching, since in a short time and before maturity I am compelled to be a teacher of discipline. Yet since this has pleased you, and you wished to have me, a young man, as master, pardon me, for necessity compels me to fulfill, as best I can, the office committed to me. Help me with your prayers, I beg you, brothers, so that the mercy of God may supply the strength that the rashness of age denies.

For I desire you to walk before God in all purity of mind and body, so that you may please him to whom you handed yourselves over from the beginning. As leader of the journey you have undertaken toward heaven, find our Lord Jesus Christ, who, born of Mary ever most modest virgin, perpetually preserved the most perfect chastity of mind and body. Follow his commandments, in whose service you were consecrated, so that you may preserve not only in the body the continence commanded to you, but also in a mind brighter than sapphire and carbuncle guard perpetual chastity. For chastity is a most beautiful virtue, especially fitting for cloistered men; and when, under the yoke of reason, the impulse of every lust boils up, chastity is the ordered and conquered habit of the vices within the bodily fervors of honor. An incontinent and wanton monk, who is seduced by the spiritual lust of the flesh, is accounted to have withdrawn entirely from God and gone over to the demons.

There are also three things without whose observance no one should be reckoned firmly among the monks. The first gift is the obedience of humble and true subjection; the second, the renunciation of all property, as the Rule commands; the third, the unsullied keeping of perpetual purity and chastity. Whatever monk violates any of these has corrupted the apostasy of his order. A monk is lifted up by supports, strength, and spiritual work; and if one of these has been taken away, then, without doubt, he is blemished. Chastity adorns monks, indeed everyone, and leads them to the highest glory of the angels.
