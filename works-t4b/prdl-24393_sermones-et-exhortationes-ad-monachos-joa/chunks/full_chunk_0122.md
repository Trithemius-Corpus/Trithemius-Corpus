I give thanks to you, most sweet Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for all your labors, journeys, and weariness; for hunger, thirst, cold, nakedness, poverty, and want; and for the necessities, injuries, insults, mockeries, and slanders that you endured throughout your whole life for us, willingly and not under compulsion. And as often as you moved your hand to work, your foot to walk, or opened your mouth to speak, so often I bless, praise, glorify, and humbly adore you; and with all the powers of my heart I always give thanks to you, mediator of God and men, most sweet Jesus Christ.

I give thanks to you, Lord Jesus Christ, most merciful king of the heavens, for your most holy teaching, for the establishment of your faith and of the evangelical sacraments, for the calling of the nations, for the justification of sinners, and for all your wonderful works and powers, and for all your benefits, which you have bestowed either on me particularly or on all the rest together with me generally.

I give thanks to you, Lord Jesus Christ, most kind savior, for the charity that you had toward the human race, and for your whole most bitter passion and all the sufferings that you endured for us wretched sinners. I give thanks to you, Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you for the grief and sorrow of your most innocent heart, which you endured for us at the time of your impending passion, when you said to your disciples who followed you: “My soul is sorrowful even unto death.” I beseech you, by this sorrow of your heart, O God, have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death; and remove from me all melancholy and disordered grief, granting me at all times a salutary sorrow for my sins.

I give thanks to you for the sadness of your most holy heart, when, placed in agony, you prayed to the Father three times that he would take from you the chalice of the passion; and, having poured out sweat under wondrous anguish, you resigned yourself wholly to his will for us. I give thanks to you, O king of heaven and earth, for your captivity, for the insults, injuries, and mockeries that you endured throughout the whole time of your passion for the salvation of us all.

I give thanks to you, Lord Jesus; I praise, bless, and adore you bound to the column, for the very harsh scourging of your most tender body, for the outpouring of your most sacred blood, and for the immense pain of your wounds. I adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, crowned with thorns, and I give thanks to you for the piercings and pains of your most holy head, for the blows, spittle, and all the mockeries cruelly inflicted on your majesty by the impious. I adore you, Christ Jesus, Son of the living God, bearing the very heavy weight of your cross. I adore you stripped of your garments before the multitude. I adore you stretched out by your hands and feet with iron nails upon the cross, and fastened to it with exceeding cruelty.

I adore you, Jesus Christ, eternal king, hanging and dying on the cross for me; and I give thanks for the pains of your wounds; I give thanks for the shedding of your most precious blood; I give thanks for your most innocent death, which you voluntarily endured for our sins and the sins of the whole world. I give thanks to your boundless goodness because you called me, your servant, to the faith; because you caused me to be baptized in the name of the holy Trinity; and because from the vanity of the world you deigned to call me to serve you in true tranquility of mind in this holy congregation.

I beseech you by your most holy name, O most sweet Jesus, mediator of God and men; I beseech you by the pain of your wounds and by the merits of your most bitter passion; I beseech you by the shedding of your most holy blood and by the power of your most innocent death: grant me pardon for all my sins; pour into my heart the sweetness of your most fervent love; give me perseverance with the advancement of all virtues; and have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death, guarding me today and at all times, in your mercy, loving Jesus, from every sin and from danger of mind and body, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Which of you, brothers, is unable to say this brief prayer once each day, or a similar one if he has a better? This prayer is not long, nor wrapped in foreign expressions, but brief and plain, gathering together the life and passion of Christ in a concise summary. A Christian person, and especially one enclosed in the cloister, is exceedingly ungrateful to God if he does not at least once a day recall the Lord’s passion and devoutly render acts of thanksgiving. But if this prayer seems too long to any of you, in the eighth homily of our Exhortations, recently delivered to you, we gave a shorter one, to which anyone wearied may have recourse.
