The nourishment of chastity is fasting and abstinence, and devout prayer is the firm support of salutary continence. But when you have done everything, do not look to yourself, for one fell from heaven who, as we know, ate nothing. Mortification of the body is very necessary and useful for preserving the beauty of chastity; but however much you have advanced in both, do not trust yourself, but always watch, hope in the Lord's help, and fear. For unless you keep yourself continually and most earnestly in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will soon be overturned.

He runs in vain who presumes to guard chastity by his own strength. Unless the Lord builds the dwelling of chastity, he labors in vain who constructs the wall of abstinence. And unless you have cooperated with all your strength with divine grace, you will in no way obtain the beauty of chastity. For God does not make you chaste without your will, nor will you attain this great gift unless he comes to the aid of your weakness through his mercy.

Therefore, O monk, offer the desire for chastity with a good will, and with humble confidence await sure increase from the Lord. Apply effort, and the Lord's help will not be lacking. To live chastely in the flesh belongs to angelic strength, not human strength. Nevertheless, it is not difficult for a person of good will to preserve chastity, especially one who is separated from the useless company of secular people and lives among monks. A monk, too, who enters a monastery of good reform while young and innocent, if he continually occupies his mind in the fear of the Lord, is less attacked by lust.

For experience of lust is ruinous, since, as someone truly said, Venus wishes to conquer souls, not to satisfy them. But a monk who is a zealous lover of his own salvation can easily extinguish the heat of lust if, fleeing idleness and satiety, he wishes continually to occupy himself in spiritual exercise. Among all vices, nothing attacks chastity more dangerously than satiety and idleness. If a monk overcomes these two evils, he will easily guard chastity.

For this reason the most holy leaders of monks, wishing to cut off the causes of lust, established for us a frugal diet in fixed measure, so that the body might be sustained for necessity and not unchastely stirred to lust. They also decreed restraint in sleep as useful for monks, because excessive rest nourishes the flesh no less than gluttony. They wished idleness, as a most deadly plague of the soul, to be far from cloistered men, most wisely arranging the time so that a monk would never be found idle.

Hear these things, brothers, and grasp them in your mind, you who carry a great treasure in a fragile little body. Flee idleness; always do some good work, so that whenever the tempting devil approaches, he may find you usefully occupied. For the one who is idle lies open to the snares of temptation; but the one occupied in good work has closed every entrance to those snares. Therefore the soul must be occupied in continual exercise, so that it is never found idle.

Blessed is that monk who at all times occupies his mind in good exercises, and never dozes in the sleep of torpor and sloth. Blessed is the spiritual soldier of Christ who always keeps watch in the fear of the Lord and restrains every disordered movement of the flesh immediately at its beginning. For no one who knowingly plays with unclean thoughts is able to guard true chastity. The first movements of wicked thoughts must be resisted, because where delay creates danger, one must fight strongly at once.

One must always keep watch against the adversary of our salvation, who is never defeated more surely than when he is choked off immediately at the beginning. Continual exercise is needed, brothers, because the mind always occupied in holy studies is like a very high tower of strength; stationed there, the most watchful soldier of Jesus Christ fears no hostile ambushes.

Therefore I beseech you, my dearest brothers, do not spend time in idleness, lest you open within yourselves the materials of vices, which the crafty evil spirit knows well. In all your works apply temperance, the guardian in all things and the guide of virtues. Always consider how you ought to walk before the Lord in all religion and holiness.
