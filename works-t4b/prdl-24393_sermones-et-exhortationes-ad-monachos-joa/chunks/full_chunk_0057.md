Desiring you to be prepared for the first of these, our holy lawgivers ordered the brave deeds of the martyrs, whose imitators you ought to become, to be read; and then, so that you might be teachable in spirit, they commanded you to submit your necks to the yoke of the Rules. And so, that you may be strong in imitation of the holy martyrs and patient under necessary rebukes, the account of their passion is read here in your ears, so that, with your minds prepared for salutary doctrine, the reading from the Rule which you profess may be recited to you at the same time. The holy martyrs of Christ strengthen you by their example toward patience; and the blessed Benedict admonishes you by his teaching that you too should be prepared for injury.

Therefore let none of you, most beloved brothers, refuse in this place humbly to receive penance each day for his negligences; let no one disdain it. This place is the public expiation of sin, in which the severity of judgment is so tempered that the mercy of discretion is not violated. For I wish to be the physician of your souls, not their destroyer. Therefore approach the examination of our judgment without fear, so that, when you are charged with negligences committed openly, you may humbly proclaim them. I do not wish to burden you beyond the Rule, so as to transfer the most secret offenses of conscience from sacramental confession to the public examination of faults, which we know some have done to the grave harm of the souls of their subjects. For our lawgiver Benedict speaks thus in the Rule: “But if the cause of the sin is hidden in the soul, let him disclose it only to the abbot or to spiritual elders, who know how to heal their own wounds and those of others, and not to reveal or make them public.”

There are two places in the monastery for the expiation of sin: one for those sins that are manifest, and the other for those that are hidden. For manifest sins are to be expiated publicly before all in the daily proclamation of faults; hidden sins, however, and those whose disclosure would scandalize the weak, are to be referred to confession. For when anyone’s sin is manifest, it must also be punished publicly; and one who has offended in secret must also be corrected in secret.
