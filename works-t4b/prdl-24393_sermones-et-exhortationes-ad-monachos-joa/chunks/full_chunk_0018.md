For what else does the whole series of the divine Scriptures contain than a certain orderly text of good things, which God has either already bestowed on human beings or promised that he would give? Or what is there that can strengthen us for the endurance of evils and make us willingly patient, as when in the Gospel we read of the innocent one suffering for us, and afterward the victories of the holy martyrs? For sacred Scripture contains the things that are necessary and saving for our sufficiency, and it warns us what, as soldiers in this pilgrimage, we ought usefully to seek and also what to avoid.

Hence blessed Jerome again says: Our riches are to meditate on the law of God day and night. Therefore let us prepare solitude for ourselves; let us take up perfect strength, so that amid the darkness of errors and the confusions of night and day the light of Christ may appear, and so that nights may have light like a powerful lamp, that by Scripture as our guide we may be able to reach the holy land. What is better than this instruction? What is sweeter than this discipline? What is more pleasant than the dominion of our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the true salvation and sweetness of faithful souls? Let Christ be our food; let sacred reading be our nourishment and drink.

For what can the life of a monk or priest be without knowledge of the Scriptures? And we come to know this not by hearing idle tales, but by reading the Scriptures; and the more we advance by reading toward true knowledge, the more we delight in the holy Scriptures. For what is sweeter than to know the will of God and not to be ignorant of the value of his promises? For, as Jerome says, the field of the divine word is most fertile: it has every delight within itself, and whatever you wish is born from it, just as the Jews hand down that when manna was eaten in the desert, it tasted in the mouth of the eater according to his will and desire.

Therefore, my brothers, read the Scriptures often; through them you can plainly know the will of God and arm yourselves most strongly against the snares of the devil. For if, according to the apostle Paul, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, whoever does not know the Scriptures does not know the power and wisdom of God; and how will he know Christ? For ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ, and whoever does not know Christ will not belong to his fellowship, as the holy Christ-lover says, because if anyone is ignorant, he will surely be ignored.

Moreover, the more constant anyone has been in the study of the divine Scriptures, the greater understanding and sweetness he receives from reading them. But it is especially fitting for you, brothers, to turn over the divine Scriptures daily by reading and meditating upon them, since abundant time is granted you free from occupations, and a place sufficiently suitable is offered, with the rarity of those who come by. Therefore I exhort and admonish you, if you love your salvation, always to have divine readings in your hands, so that all the time permitted to you without labor after the service of prayer may be spent on the study of sacred Scripture. For this reason I think no brother of mine a monk, as a soldier of Christ, unless he reads something in the sacred volumes that may make him better in his holy purpose.

Rather, let him follow the counsel of blessed Jerome, who says: Let the divine Scriptures always be in your hands; let them be continually turned over in your mind. Nor should you think it sufficient to store the commandments of God in memory, unless you strive also to fulfill them in works. For God requires two things of us, my brothers. First, he wishes us to know his most holy will; second, that when we know it we should do it. But we cannot know his commandments if we are ignorant of the knowledge of the divine Scriptures. Nor does anyone do the will of God who is ignorant of it; nor will he know it who has not learned it. Learn, therefore, what the pleasing will of God is, so that you may do it; do what you have learned, so that you may understand more fully. For no one understands the will of God better than he who, knowing it, also does it out of love.

And so, if you desire to become most learned interpreters of the divine Scriptures, show the zeal for knowledge not in words but in holy works. For then you will grasp the fruitful knowledge of sacred reading, if you have kept the world of your heart from every sin and, groaning in the love of God, pricked by holy compunction, have continually turned over Scripture. For knowledge of the divine Scriptures is a high mountain on which Moses speaks with the Lord, and a person polluted by the filth of vices is forbidden to approach it.
