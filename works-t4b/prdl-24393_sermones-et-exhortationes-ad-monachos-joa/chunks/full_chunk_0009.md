*On the Progress of Spiritual Warfare*

Whatever things there have been in the world pass away. The true lover of God rejoices in tribulations, is glad in adversities, and exults in persecutions for the sake of justice. He follows his king, seated in heaven; there he dwells by the desire of his soul, where he does not doubt that his Savior reigns.

Whatever pleasure and delights are perceived in the world, if you fervently burn in true charity for Christ, it begets in you, as though from very useful dung, a hatred of them; for he loves nothing earthly, nothing transitory, nothing perishable, but only him to whom he has devoted himself by the single intention of his mind. For the true soldier of Christ thinks nothing of the affairs of the world; he cares not for the flesh but for the mind, since he is not ignorant of what is written: “No one serving as a soldier for God entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please him to whom he has approved himself.” Indeed, whoever serves an earthly emperor always obeys his commands in all things; at once, without contradiction, they go at the order of their superior. All follow one leader, and whatever he commands in the king’s name, the whole army obeys without contradiction. You also, soldiers of the king of the ages, who in his place and by his command have appointed me your leader, hear the command of your emperor Jesus Christ through my mouth, and do what he commands us in the holy Gospel. Hear what he commands, and consider how you ought to serve as soldiers in the observance of regular discipline. “He who ministers to me,” he says, “let him follow me.”

Listen, cloistered monk, bearing the duties of military service; pay attention. Christ, to whom you serve as soldier, speaks to you; the heavenly emperor, whom you serve at the altar, addresses you. If you wish to be my minister, you must follow the virtue of patience and humility which you have learned from me also. “For I have given you an example, my soldiers, that just as I have done, you also should do likewise.” Charity, monk, compelled me to be born as a man of a virgin in the world; let that same charity keep you always lowly and humble in the cloister for love of me. The work of human salvation which the Father commanded me, I strove for your sake to accomplish always with unceasing meditation; and if you love me, take care to preserve inviolate, even to death, the holy order of monastic life which you have undertaken for your sins. I was always subject to my heavenly Father in all things according to his will, obedient even to the most bitter death of the cross; you also, if you wish to come to me, obey your abbot, appointed in my place, humbly in all things and always according to the Rule.

I never transgressed in anything the command that my Father gave me; and unless you keep inviolate, even to death, the three principal vows of the monastic institution, you are by no means to be counted among my ministers and servants. Though I was rich, for your salvation I became poor and needy; but unless you contemplate my loves and give up all things, you cannot be my disciple. For your sake I endured want, hunger, thirst, hardship, cold, and continual labors in the world; unless you follow me in these, according to your Rule, as far as you are able, patiently and voluntarily, you will certainly not be my minister, my soldier, or my monk. In your patience you shall possess your soul.

I desired nothing in this world, had nothing, loved nothing earthly, but as poor, humble, and wretched I always subjected myself in all things to the perfect will of my Father, receptive of injuries and blows. But you, cloistered monk, who profess the monastic Rule under an abbot or prior, if henceforth you follow the desires of your flesh, if you possess gold or silver of your own in the monastery, if you love anything besides me, if you despise the commands of your superior, if you do not serve me perseveringly in humility, know for certain that I shall not regard you as a soldier, nor count you a monk, a minister, or a disciple, but I shall judge you a fugitive, an apostate, faithless, lying, derisive, and wickedly unfaithful; and when, with my betrayer Judas and the angels sitting in judgment, I am the judge forever, I shall condemn you without mercy.

What your lips vowed to me is no longer yours but mine. If, however, you usurp for yourself what is mine, you do not wish to be my monk and faithful servant, but a thief and a wicked robber, impious and unjust; and do not doubt that when I come I shall judge you as such. But if you faithfully persevere in the holy purpose even to death; if, under the authority of my vicar whom I have appointed for you as father, you preserve inviolate the solemn will by which you consecrated yourself to me; if you observe obedience, poverty, and chastity with humility for love of my name until the end; if you always strive to please me alone in all your actions; if you take care to follow me without interruption along the arduous road of the virtues: then know, and know with undoubting certainty, that I shall have you as a most beloved friend made from a servant, as a soldier to be crowned with me in my kingdom. I shall love you; I shall cherish you; I shall set you forever glorious in the midst of my saints.

Now hear me, my servant, who out of reverence and honor for my name have enrolled yourself in the military service of monks: with a great and perfect heart, follow the footprints of my feet, just as that teacher of yours, once blessed and your friend, taught you and strove with all his efforts to imitate. Let not the enticements of the flesh deceive you, brave soldier; let not the vain and pernicious dealings of this world seduce your soul, which is dedicated to me. Never yield to the temptations of the unclean spirit, which no mortal has ever been able to escape. Let those last things which you must necessarily undergo always remain in your memory; and from their swift passage, and from the pleasure of the flesh which passes away, take up for yourself the necessary dread and contempt of the future things of this sojourn.

Just as a brief labor undertaken for me in the world with patience and charity prepares eternal happiness for my soldiers, so the desires and pleasures of the flesh, which pass away suddenly, plunge the soul of the sinful man into hell, to be tormented without end. Therefore let all your desires be directed to me, monk, you who minister to me and who undertake to follow me along the arduous road of regular observance. Give me your heart if you desire happiness; seek me alone if you love your own life and salvation. When you love God, you are tranquil and holy; when you have followed him, you are blessed and glorious without end.

What would you do, my brothers, if it happened that you heard these words which we have spoken from the mouth of our Savior and king as he spoke? What, I ask, would you do? I certainly believe there would be great compunction in your hearts if you heard Christ speaking personally in this way.

But we, bearing Christ’s place in the monastery, speak the words which he has given, and you do not attend. We testify to you about the necessary arena of salvation, and you show no signs of compunction. If you do not know why these things have been said, I now beg you to understand. I see that some of you, given over to vanities, shrink from sacred readings and care for nothing less than the perfection of spiritual warfare; their god is their belly, and their glory is in their shame. Do you think, most worthless knaves, who have no taste for anything spiritual, that Christ will come again from heaven to speak to you those things which he committed to his preachers to say? Or have you not heard Paul saying: “Christ, rising from the dead, now dies no more; death shall no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died, he died to sin once; but in that he lives, he lives to God.”

Once Christ was seen on earth; once he appeared visibly among mortals; once he preached the way of eternal salvation; he will not add a second time. He established pastors and vicars over his household to teach the ignorant. Wishing to make their authority known, he said in the Gospel: “He who hears you hears me, and he who despises you despises me.” Therefore hear those whom Christ has set over you as rulers, to whom he has committed the distribution of spiritual wheat, as he said in the holy Gospel: “Who, do you think, is the faithful and prudent steward, whom the lord has appointed over his household, to give them wheat in due season?” Therefore, brothers, bear fruit worthy of repentance while it is granted to you at the opportune time, lest the time be cut off if you neglect to repent. For you know what divine Scripture says to us: “The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night.” I beg you, beware of putting off the good, because none of you knows when he is going to die. Rather, attend to that true saying of the wanton poet Naso, when he gives this warning: “But hasten, and do not put yourself off to the hours that are to come; he who is not ready today will be less ready tomorrow.” No one [unclear].
