This is the far-off region of beastliness and unlikeness, into which that prodigal son went away, and where he squandered the portion of his inheritance luxuriously with prostitutes. In this region carnal citizens dwell; concerning them the holy Apostle says: The animal man does not perceive the things that are of God. In this dark region, covered with the gloom of death, no spiritual good dwells. In it there reigns not peace but beastly discord, as the prophet says: There is no peace for the wicked, says my Lord.

Concerning those who, being near to this lowest region, often fall back into it through inconstancy of mind.

Although between the first region, that of the wicked, and the second, that of the good, there is properly no middle place, nevertheless there are some who often move between the two, when from weakness they are at one time lukewarm, and at another time, from contrition of mind, appear to be devout. These are weak people. Although they are not submerged in the lowest region, nevertheless through negligence they are very near to it, and indeed frequently fall into it through bad custom, because they do not withdraw from the affections of the flesh as far as they can.

Concerning these people Bernard says in a certain sermon: Pernicious lukewarmness, because it is dangerous, because it is most troublesome and full of misery and sorrow, and plainly very near to hell, is rightly reckoned the shadow of death. Indeed one finds people who are fainthearted and slack, failing beneath the burden, needing rod and spurs. Their joy is weak, their sadness fainthearted; their compunction is brief and rare. Their thought is with animal things; their way of life is lukewarm; their obedience is without devotion, their speech without circumspection, their prayer without the intention of the heart, their reading without edification. Such people, finally, as we see, are scarcely restrained by fear of Gehenna, held back by shame, bridled by reason, or checked by discipline. But if people of this sort remain so for a long time under the burden, they are either crushed and succumb, or in some manner they are in hell, so that they never fully breathe into the light of the Lord’s mercies, nor into the liberty of the Spirit, which alone makes the yoke sweet and the burden light. perilous lukewarmness flows, because the will has not yet been fully purified, nor do they will the good as they know it, being drawn away and enticed by depraved desire. For they love in their flesh their earthly little consolations, whether in word, or in signs, or in deeds, or in some other thing; and if they interrupt these sometimes, nevertheless they do not break them off completely. Hence it is that they rarely direct their affections toward God, and their compunction is not continual but only for an hour.

But a mind subject to these distractions cannot be filled with divine visitations; and the more it is emptied of those distractions, the more fully it will be filled with these visitations. If much, much; if little, little. Or perhaps, if you approve the statement more, those visitations will never be able to be mingled with these distractions forever. For the Spirit and the flesh, fire and lukewarmness, do not dwell together in one dwelling, especially since lukewarmness itself is accustomed to provoke vomiting in the Lord.

Therefore whoever desires to flee from the midst of Babylon, whoever longs to come to the region of the Spirit, must always take care to drive away sluggishness of mind from himself, and to burn continually in holy purpose through the fire of divine love. Nothing harms those who wish to serve God so much as lukewarmness of mind. It always arises at the beginning from a certain small negligence; but unless it is immediately attended to, it increases wonderfully by degrees.

Moreover, one placed near the lowest region always falls back into it, because the dregs of his former carnal way of life still subtly occupy his will, and with an easy occasion disturb the weak fervor of devotion. But no one can rise again from the depth of hell with a lukewarm will; no one can overcome custom without a contrary custom, because unless a certain force is applied, the habit of sinning will always strengthen vice.

Rightly, then, this lowest region is called hell, since by a certain likeness of disturbance and aversion it is compared to hell. After this life there are three dwellings of souls, which receive their citizens emigrating from the three regions of this world. The first dwelling is called hell, and this is compared to the lowest region.
