Each considers his own companion better than the rest, and one loves the other, deceived not spiritually but carnally, since those who do not lift their affections up to God love nothing other than the consolations of the flesh. But when you see a monk seeking friendship with a weak and infirm brother, you must judge him not a servant of God but a lover of the flesh. For one who truly occupies his whole heart in the love of God cares nothing for human friendship. The love of Jesus is sufficient for him in all things; beyond him he requires nothing more. But one who seeks outward consolations of the mind shows by his action that he does not love God with his whole heart. The love of God loves solitude, hates the tumult of men, and does not require conversations with friends.

O good Jesus, how sweet is your love, since it pierces the heart of the one who loves you. Seeing you, he desires to see you; having you, his soul longs to embrace you. Truly, one who loves you forgets himself; he meditates on nothing but the sweetest pleasingness of you. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves you; nothing is found anywhere that the soul desires apart from you. O with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is tormented; with how abundant tears the face is watered in secret, because as often as it thinks of the beloved’s face presiding in heaven, so often it laments itself as desolate on earth.

Meanwhile the mind, languishing with love, often tries to say something about its most beloved Jesus; it also strives to provoke others to his love. But as soon as it begins to utter the sweetest name of Jesus, unwillingly, because of the greatness of love, it falls silent. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, but is felt with sweetness in the secret of the mind. I know one who said: “My love is crucified.” Who is your love, O servant of God? Why do you weep? Why do you groan? Ignatius: “My love is crucified, and shall I not mourn? My Lord is dead, and shall I be able to hold back my tears?” Do not weep, O faithful soul, for though your love has died to redeem you from death, he has already risen from the dead to crown you in heaven. Be faithful unto death, and he will give you the crown of life.

On the Mode and Degrees of Divine Love, Containing the Conclusion of This Second Region

Since no one suddenly becomes supreme, nor can anyone by his own powers suddenly ascend to that highest summit of divine love, I now think it useful to note briefly the certain modes or degrees themselves of loving God. For moving the human heart to divine love, in my judgment the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is more effective than all other things, provided that it has been pondered in a person’s mind purely, sweetly, fervently, perseveringly, and diligently. Truly, one who often recalls to memory the life, passion, and death of our Savior, with compassion and thanksgiving, especially according to its principal articles, prepares a swift ascent of the heart to divine love. But we shall suitably treat this most holy occupation of the mind around the Lord’s passion in the second principal part of this work, where we shall set out more fully the manner of spiritual exercise. As for the account of what is said in this place, I find three modes of loving God, which I shall briefly explain in order.

The first mode commands that you love sweetly the one who loves you, and join your whole self to him gently, so that your heart loves nothing outside God and requires nothing from elsewhere. For since God is wholly all, he alone desires to possess the whole heart of the one who loves him, and he drives it away from displeasing thoughts. Therefore, if you wish to love sweetly, love totally. For the love of God makes complete either the whole person or nothing. Let meditation on the passion of Jesus Christ our Savior possess you wholly, until love grows sweetly strong in you. You must despise everything in yourself that flatters the flesh, if you desire to come to the love of God. For no one can love God sweetly in this life while he is convicted of loving anything in the world. Indeed the love of the world is utterly contrary to divine love, since where one is admitted into the inner chambers of the heart, the other is forbidden to be present.
