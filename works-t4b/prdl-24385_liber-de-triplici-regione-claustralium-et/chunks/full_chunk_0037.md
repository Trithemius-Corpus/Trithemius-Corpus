Therefore it is necessary that each monk diligently examine himself and, according to the ordinance of the Church, cleanse himself at once from the faults he has committed. But the person who, on examining his conscience, finds it clean from mortal sin should offer humble praises to God, and should not neglect to subject himself to the following examination.

The second thing, likewise necessary for a good man, is to intend God principally in all works, and to order all things according to his powers to God’s praise and honor. But if, because the multitude and variety of works impede him, a person cannot always actually keep God before his eyes, then at least by habitual intention he should conform his desire of living according to God’s most pleasing will.

The third is that in all things he obey God, holy mother Church, and his superiors, and not depart from the path of right and enlightened reason, since whoever despises this threefold obedience has denied the faith. It is therefore necessary to show an obedience ordered by right reason equally to these three, according to their established principles, so that the monk’s life may remain blameless in his actions, without fear.

These three things, possessed with humble sincerity, make a good man and show the state of a conscience that hopes well in the Lord. But a person who lacks one of them is not perfectly good, even if he seems to be good. If, however, repenting of his sins, he is afterward able to fulfill these three things, he is then made good and pleasing to God, however bad he may have been before. Moreover, so that from a good man a good religious or spiritual person may be born, three other things are required according to the aforesaid John of Kastl.

The first thing required for making a good religious is that purity of heart be cleansed from every emulation or incitement of the flesh through the practice of discipline. This purity is never better acquired than when the world is despised together with all its affairs.
