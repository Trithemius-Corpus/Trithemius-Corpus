If, however, by these exercises concerning God’s benefits we wish to kindle our heart to the love of God, we ought to consider six conditions in the Lord’s gifts themselves. First, consider that God’s gift is wholly noble; second, that it is especially fruitful and useful to you; third, that it is varied, manifold, and very often repeated. These three consider the gift as ordered in itself; the two that follow regard the person of the giver; the last notes the condition of the receiver. Fourth, then, consider the great affection of the giver toward you; fifth, his inestimable dignity; sixth, that you, receiving great things from the greatest, are vile and contemptible, and unworthy of all things. From these considerations of benefits the love of God is accustomed to rise up in the mind of the devout person, and, advancing by certain steps, to lift the spirit to contemplation.

On the Knowledge of Oneself.

The fourth root producing divine love is called knowledge of oneself, very useful if it is referred to the preceding roots. Indeed, if by reflection we bring back to mind how many things so great a Lord has done for us, who are so little, we shall recognize that we are unworthy, vile, and altogether undeserving of all things. Four things ought to lead a person to knowledge of himself; and from consideration of these it is especially fitting for us to love God.

First, therefore, let the faithful person consider the beginning of his creation, and he will find that, when he did not exist, he was created from God’s goodness alone. Who could have asked God, when no one existed? That highest and utterly ineffable good created you, O man, in his own image when you did not exist; from goodness alone he made you a participant in his goodness. What did you merit when you did not exist? Or what advantage could your condition bring to almighty God, who, always subsisting unchangeably in himself, admits no accident? Nothing is new to God; nothing can be old to the highest and unchangeable good. He is always whatever he can be,

because neither does the best become better, nor the greatest become greater. God created you by his goodness; your condition was not necessary to him, but advantageous to you alone. Whoever considers this goodness of God with attentive meditation rightly praises his creator.

Second, lest you be lifted up into pride, O man, consider the vileness of your condition: you were made from the slime of the earth, and after a little while, consumed by worms, you will return into the dust of the earth. Why, then, are you proud, earth and ashes? Your conception has guilt, your birth misery, your life punishment, your dissolution the distress of death. For man, born of woman, coming by a brief road of time, is filled with many miseries; and then he is most miserable when, in the flesh, he does not recognize himself as miserable.

Third, consider the origin of your conception, and you will find your soul stained with the foulness of original sin. For we are born children of wrath, but through the grace of baptism in the blood of Christ Jesus we are cleansed from the original stain. By this consideration we rightly love our Savior, by whose mercy we have been cleansed from the original stain.

Fourth, faithful soul, consider how excessively foul and vile you are whenever you stain your garments in the act of sin, since you have shown yourself an adulteress to Christ your spouse, who joined you to himself through his blood when you were utterly immaculate. What are you doing, O wretched one? Why do you desert your most loving spouse, or why, foolish one, do you withdraw from your source? Know that you have been placed in the flesh, not so that you may yield to its vicious and concupiscent desires, but rather that, prone as they are, you may resist their rule. For as often as, by dissembling, you have handed over your body, so to speak, to the disordered motions of the flesh, which are contrary to divine rectitude, so often, turned away from the highest good, you show yourself an adulteress to your spouse. Beware, beware, O soul redeemed by the blood of Christ, lest the love of the flesh deceive you; for just as the first man, deceived by love of the woman who was his companion, fell into death, so any soul, following the pleasures of its flesh, shamefully withdraws from its source, which is God. But alas, wretched one, how far you have fallen! You have dwelt in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been counted among those descending into hell.
