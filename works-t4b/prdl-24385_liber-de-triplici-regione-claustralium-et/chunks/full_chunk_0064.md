Let him examine himself carefully by considering, and let him test whether the compunction of devotion still burns in his mind. If he finds that it is still present with fervor, let him strive to kindle it further with holy desires, and to summon himself to higher things. But if he senses that he has grown lukewarm in affection or distracted by thoughts, then, at once reproving himself, let him say these or similar words: Woe to me, wretched man, who praise my God so negligently. Behold, my Lord, who suffered most bitterly for me through the whole night, had no rest; and I could not watch one hour with him. Help me, Lord my God, so that I may be able to sing psalms to you wisely.

Finally, under each psalm, let him fittingly stir himself by some burning words toward the compunction of fervor, so that through mental exhortation he may be more fully inflamed in affection, saying: Would that, Lord God, I might love you above all things with my whole heart, and cling continually to you alone. O good Jesus, kindle my heart in your most holy love. My love has been crucified, and what am I doing upon the earth? O Jesus, my sweet love, possess my heart, for you are the treasure of my heart, my king and my God.

After Matins.

When the matutinal office has been completed, and confessions have been made or heard, he should not neglect to read at once the Hour of the Holy Cross. When it has been completed, let him add this little prayer.

Prayer.

O sweetest chosen Lord Jesus, through the pain of your wounds, through the outpouring of your most holy blood, and through the power of your most innocent death, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death. Amen.

Then let him say:

To the holy and undivided Trinity, to the crucified humanity of Jesus Christ our Lord, to the most blessed Mary ever virgin, and to Saint Anne, her venerable mother, with all the saints, be everlasting praise and glory for endless ages. Amen.
