At the elevation of the most holy body of Christ, following the example of St. Thomas Aquinas, he may say attentively to Christ: “You are the King of glory, O Christ; you are the everlasting Son of the Father,” through to the end of that hymn.

After the elevation, let the devout cenobite turn himself to the Lord Jesus, desiring from his inmost heart to have the consolation of so great a mystery; and let him prepare his affection as though he were about to receive communion. For as often as a devout lover of God desires with fervor of heart to receive the body of Christ, he believes that he has received the power of the sacrament. The Lord Jesus said to a certain soul dear to him: “If a man, fervent in my love, desired to receive my body a thousand times in a day, if it were possible, he would truly obtain before me, by the power of that same body of mine, his desire in his soul as often as he desired it.” Therefore, with this desire conceived in the mind of one who loves Jesus, let the cenobite pray to the Lord in this or a similar manner.

Prayer of the one ministering.

Would that, most loving Lord Jesus, I were so well and fervently disposed that it were permitted me now to receive your most holy body in this venerable sacrament. Would that it were permitted me to repeat this sacred communion several times in a day. O my sweetness, O sole delight of my heart, O my hope, Lord Jesus, you know the ardor of my mind toward you, and all my desire is before you. O you, my whole good, grant me, by your goodness, to receive now and at all times in my soul the power and grace of this most holy sacrament, so that I may be able to remain continually in you.

The diligent preparation of the priest who is about to celebrate.

--- Page 154 ---

When a priest is about to approach to offer the sacrifice of this most holy sacrament of the body and blood, he should first examine himself diligently and most cautiously determine of which region he is a citizen. If he still finds himself a dweller at the boundary of the region, he should not approach the altar unless he first strives to cleanse himself through contrition and confession and a firm resolution to amend. But if he finds himself a citizen of the second region, if he discovers that his powers have been reformed and ordered toward the cenobitic end, he can readily dispose himself for celebration, since he has learned, by daily kindling his affection beforehand, to raise himself more often on high through desire. For divine goodness quickly enters the affection of a person that is stained by no prior desire.

Therefore, one who wishes to perform this most holy priestly office with devotion and fervor of divine love should first set forth some ardent prayers by which he may be able to stir himself to fuller compunction. For devotion is rarely granted at the altar if the mind has not been prepared for fervor beforehand. Therefore, our fathers, desiring each of us to advance in his own free devotion, did not wish to occupy us before access to the altar with many psalms, as some do; not because anyone should therefore approach without recollection, but so that each person might more freely devote himself in private to exercises or prayers more fitting to his own devotion.

Accordingly, wishing to provide for those celebrating an elevation of the mind to the Lord Jesus before access to the altar, we have set down the following prayer from that which is ascribed to St. Ambrose of Milan; we believe it quite effective in the merit of the Lord’s passion. Therefore, the devout cenobite who is about to approach the altar, if he has leisure and freedom, may use it, or set forth a similar prayer, or prepare himself in whatever ways he wishes.

Prayer before access.

Highest priest and true pontiff, Jesus Christ, who offered yourself to God the Father as a pure, holy, and immaculate victim on the altar of the cross for us miserable sinners; who also gave us your flesh to eat and your blood to drink, and established that mystery by the power of the Holy Spirit, saying, “As often as you do these things, you shall do them in memory of me”: I beg you, by the sorrow of your wounds and by the pouring out of your most precious blood.
