In this most holy freedom divine wisdom is learned; the sweetness of divine goodness is tasted with inestimable delight; the mind, made most free, is rapt above itself; resting in the silence of divine darkness, it is lulled to sleep.

And this mystical wisdom is said to consist in a mind that has been raised up and purified in God, and is hindered by no obstacle of any kind from its effective progress. Ascending in this region, the mind kindled by love utterly leaves behind thoughts and images; it becomes solitary, naked, and most free. For thoughts, because of the office of memory, are compelled to remain in the middle region, nor can they in any way approach the supreme region of the spirit, which is above every human understanding. For one ascends here by love through affection and desire alone; far from here the phantasms of thoughts are driven away. For as soon as the mind begins to ascend by thoughts, it is driven back and falls into the middle region. Yet the ascent itself is begun through the operation of the intellect; when that soon fails, it is completed through affection. For the intellect excites desire in the love of the highest good and commends its beauty as much as it can; but when the affection, thus aroused, begins to ascend by love to the contemplation of so great a beauty, the intellect fails in amazement and falls silent. But the mind, unable to endure the turning back of the intellect, alone and naked, most free from every image of thought, enters the dark mountain shining above it; it contemplates the beloved, not indeed face to face, but in an enigma; it fails before the greatness of love; rapt away, it rests in silence.

We read that once Moses, leader of the Israelite people, called by God, ascended the dark mountain alone; Scripture records that he forbade the people access to the mountain under threat of death. The Lord appeared in the darkness, and through Moses himself gave the law to the people. For thus it is written in Exodus: “All Mount Sinai was smoking, because the Lord had descended upon it in fire, and its smoke went up as from a furnace. The whole mountain was terrible, and the sound of the trumpet gradually grew louder

and was drawn out at greater length. But all the people saw the torches and the mountain smoking, and, terrified and shaken with fear, they stood far away, saying to Moses: ‘You speak to us, and we will listen; let not the Lord speak to us, lest perhaps we die.’”

All these things are full of mysteries; if they are examined with diligent consideration, they will magnificently commend this supreme region of the spirit. Mount Sinai signifies the height of this region; the smoky darkness represents the object of contemplation; Moses expresses the affection burning in love; the people show the crowd of thoughts and phantasms. Moses alone, the leader of the people, is allowed to ascend the mountain and enter the darkness, because the affection inflamed by divine love alone is raised to the summit of highest contemplation. But the people are forbidden, under threat of death, to approach the mountain, because no intellect, no thought, no imagination or reasoning can in any way penetrate the sanctuary of the divine majesty. The people stand far from the mountain, terrified with fear, because when the mind enters alone by the affection of love into the abyss of contemplation, every crowd of thoughts, utterly failing, is disturbed. Here the intellect does not dare to approach its own ascending affection, but, standing far off, shaken with fear in itself, is compelled to remain. Therefore, yielding in amazement before majesty, it says to the Lord with the prophet: “Truly you are a hidden God.” But when Moses is on the summit of the mountain, he is wrapped in darkness; he does not grasp God by the light of the intellect, but passes into contemplation of him by love and desire alone. Moses, and not the Lord, speaks to the frightened people, because the affection, purified, enters the dwelling-place of love, through which the powers of the spirit are illumined.
