What a putrid corpse is wont to do in the sight of human beings, sin does in its own way, incomparably, in the sight of the angels. But carnal monks despise the honor of God, and therefore they do not notice the shamefulness of sin. Those who commit mortal sins almost as if licitly neglect to amend venial ones. For Scripture says: “He who neglects the smallest things will gradually fall.” Hence Bernard’s saying admonishes us: “Let no one, brothers, say in his heart: ‘These things are slight; I do not care to correct them. It is no great matter if I remain in these venial and very small sins.’ This, brothers, is impenitence; this is the unforgivable blasphemy against the Holy Spirit. For no one can avoid greater sins if he has scorned to amend lesser ones.” Nothing is worse than carelessness of mind.

Second, carnal monks, lovers of the lowest region, are too disordered for penitence, because they do not notice the contagion of sin. For just as a healthy person is very often infected by a contagious disease, so a person is inclined from one sin into another. For one sin is always said to dispose a person to another, unless it is amended as quickly as possible through contrition. For the prophet says: “Jerusalem sinned a sin; therefore she has been made unstable.” And elsewhere: “He who is in filth, let him be filthy still.” We have shown sufficiently the contagion of this disease in the degrees of pride: for just as we have said that one always arises from another, so we perceive that through carelessness a person becomes liable to slip from one sin into another.

Third, carnal monks, disordered for penitence, contract a most wretched sickness through the habit of sin, when they fall into a pernicious blindness of mind. Every vice, when often committed, is held cheap, and through habit is judged lighter. Just as grace illuminates the mind, so the frequency of sin blinds reason. For it is written: “Blind the heart of this people and make their ears heavy, so that seeing they may not see and hearing they may not understand, lest they be converted and I heal them, says the Lord.” For when the impious man has come into the depth of evils, he is contemptuous. No sin is so great that through habit it does not seem slight and small.

Fourth, disordered cloisterers, blinded by the habit of evil,

do not come to the grace of true compunction. They approach confession only superficially, from habit; they do not abandon their depraved will; they do not truly take up the purpose of amendment. Woe to those who, buried in the habit of sin, do not fear the censure of a strict examination; who do not withdraw from blindness of mind through the remedy of contrition. For just as now they despise the long-suffering of God’s goodness, so in that terrible hour they will undergo the sentence of eternal death. What benefit can confession of the mouth have where there is no true contrition of the heart? Without contrition there is no saving confession, nor can there be contrition without the purpose of amendment. The sign of contrition is to be unwilling to sin any more.

Fifth, the disordered citizens of the lowest region, made despisers of almighty God through the habit of sinning, always fall into graver dangers of soul, since they always approach badly the most sacred mysteries of the body and blood of the Lord. They make a superficial confession from habit; they allow great beasts of vices to run about in their consciences. Then indeed, as if allied with hell, they do not fear God; they despise the paths of amendment of life; they think themselves well ordered; and they do not care with how many vices they are inwardly filled. Sometimes, sensibly affected by a fantastic devotion, they think the Holy Spirit is now present, and declare themselves beloved by God and holy. They also impudently thrust themselves upon the divine mysteries, and, not discerning the body and blood of the Lord, receive them to their own judgment and condemnation.

Finally, such people, communicating unworthily, are more gravely hardened, because, while they consider themselves very secure and think they are living well, they do not take care duly to cleanse their conscience from vices. Thus Judas the apostate, the evil of betrayal which earlier, as someone says, he feared to carry out against the Savior, after the morsel, when the devil had entered into him, did not fear to fulfill boldly and without shame. O how many citizens of this lowest region are carnal monks, lifted up, proud, envious, murmuring, and entangled in various sins, whose conscience is rarely or never made clean through true contrition, yet who do not fear to approach the most holy altar almost every day.
