In this region he mercifully receives us when we return with the prodigal son; in his love he rushes into our embrace; he clothes us with the first robe; he grants to those who love him the garment of charity. Why, then, do we neglect to return as quickly as possible to the most indulgent Father, whom we see ready for our embrace even after contempt? Wretched as we are, we have withdrawn by the perversity of our own will; we have squandered the portion of our inheritance with the harlots of carnal affections; we have been shamefully stripped of every beauty and paternal ornament. Why do we not return to the Father, whom we know to be ineffably merciful? Let us return at least, I beg, after our fault, since we have despised avoiding ruin. Return, return, wretched soul; return, gravely captive in affections; return, mortified by sorrows. Why do you delay? Why do you linger? Return, faithful soul, to the guide of your youth’s covenant; return to your former husband; go back to the most loving Father, who awaits you with the desire of love. For behold, he awaits you patiently, and through the prophet Isaiah he cries to you: “Return to me, for I have redeemed you. I have blotted out your iniquities like a cloud, and your sins like a mist.” What is this, O soul, that you are in the land of enemies? You have grown old in a foreign land; you have been defiled with the dead; you have been counted with those who go down into hell. Remember that almighty God created you from nothing; consider that he formed you in his image and likeness; remember whence you have fallen; see where you have gone, trampled under the feet of unclean affections among swine. Your Creator set you from the beginning in this supreme region of the spirit; he entrusted to you the natural exercise of his love; he also granted you the hope of life that will remain forever. But you have abandoned the fountain of wisdom; you have gone out into the lowest region of unlikeness; you have squandered the promised goods of the inheritance. But return, even late at last, to the exercise of penitence in the middle region, until, purified from all your iniquities, you may merit to be led back to the pleasantness of the supreme region.

There the most indulgent Father will joyfully receive you as you return with reverence; he will clothe you with the recovered first robe of innocence; and he will give you the ring of most holy covenant on your hands, and the shoes of reverent mortification of evil affections on your feet, so that, adorned with these ornaments like a faithful bride, you may cling to him with vigor and by no means withdraw from his love once begun. Finally, for your fattened food he will kill the calf; he himself will be both server and feast; through the greatness of his love he will give his flesh as food and his own blood as drink. There the Spirit of the holy Lord will be present and will fill you with his mystical gifts. Then, exulting in the Lord, you will sing: “The fruit of Jesus is sweet to my throat, for his spirit is sweeter than honey.”

Thus this supreme region of the spirit is magnificent in its leading-back, for it leads back to the pleasantness of the heavenly homeland those citizens whom it receives into its dwelling. And we unfailingly recognize the citizens of this most holy region from the fruits of the Spirit, of which we know the holy Apostle spoke: “The fruit of the Spirit is charity, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, long-suffering, gentleness, faith, modesty, continence, chastity.” These fruits the citizens of this third region bring forth, because, filled with God and united to him, they walk always joyful in the Lord and most fervent in charity. For whatever happens to a just man dwelling on this mountain of love will not sadden him. For a soul always peaceful and tranquil in the love of God is lifted up by no prosperities and disturbed by no adversities.

That this supreme region of the spirit is pleasant and most true in treasuring up.

Article VI.

Sixth, this most beautiful region of the spirit is called pleasant and most true in treasuring up, because whatever is acquired through the exercise of good work it possesses in the simplicity of the spirit. The soul’s treasure is the love of God.
