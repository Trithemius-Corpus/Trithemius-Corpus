Then the general preparation for fervor in prayer, set out above before Matins, should be observed; and the conscience should be cleansed if anything has been committed against its purity. Nevertheless, if the mind has been raised up in contemplation of divine love and continual desire, it will have less need of the smaller exercises.

At the second signal.

When the second signal has sounded, both for Prime and for the remaining hours of the day, the manner set out above for Matins should be observed, as much as the time allowed for prayer permits each person. But when our monk enters or leaves the church, he should not neglect to show reverence to God before the altars and the crucifix, but, humbly bowing, should say the customary appropriate prayers. For a modest bowing of the body is understood as the outward sign of an inner person well ordered toward God.

In the chapter room after Prime.

When the devout monk enters the chapter room after Prime, he should remember to give thanks to God for the victory of the saints and martyrs, whom he knows to have fought for the Lord in the greatest fervor of spirit, despising the torments of their bodies, by the testimony of death. Therefore, when you hear the brave deeds of the saints read, pray to the Lord that, through their merits, he may deign to forgive you your sins, and mercifully strengthen you to bear the yoke of monastic discipline by obtaining their fellowship. And this desire is set before you to take up, when, after their passions have been read, divine help is invoked three times by all. At the beginning of the day we also do this: that, strengthened for patience by the examples of the saints, we may fear no adversities throughout the whole day. But when you hear that some martyr bravely stood firm in the contest of battle, you will pray to him with these or similar words.

Prayer.

Holy soldier of the eternal King, I ask you, by the love of our Lord Jesus Christ, who made you overcome the torments of the flesh and the snares of this world: pray for me, that I may deserve to come as quickly as possible to your fellowship.

If the name of a confessor, or of a virgin, or of a saint of another condition, has been recited, the devout lover of God will easily vary a prayer to any saint extemporaneously, according to the ardor of his own affection. For affective prayers are effective above all others.

At the reading of the Rule.

When the reader recites, as customary, a single passage from the Rule, the devout monk should say in his heart: Help me, most sweet Lord Jesus Christ, that according to this standard of your religious servant Benedict I may be able to live, and to remain faithfully at all times in your most holy service.

Then let him consider that, just as the Rule is read daily for the progress of the good and devout, so it is recited to the lukewarm, the bad, or the negligent, that they may be without excuse, to the increase of their damnation. Finally, let him know that he is bound to a faithful effort in all the things that the Rule contains, just as the wording of his profession has it. For thus he says in profession: I promise obedience and conversion of my morals, according to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

When the names of the dead are recited.

But when, after the reading of the Rule, the names of the dead have been recited, the monk should pray as follows, no less from charity than from debt, for these and other faithful departed, committed and not committed to him.

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, by the pain of your wounds, by the pouring out of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on these souls, and on all those committed to me in prayer, and on all the faithful departed, and grant them eternal rest. Indeed, it is a great work of mercy to intercede before the merciful Judge for the souls placed in the prison of purgatory, and to come to their aid out of charity through the remembrance of the Lord’s Passion. For I firmly believe, and trust with pious presumption, that the prayer which is poured out for the dead from charity alone returns doubled into the bosom of the one who prays.
