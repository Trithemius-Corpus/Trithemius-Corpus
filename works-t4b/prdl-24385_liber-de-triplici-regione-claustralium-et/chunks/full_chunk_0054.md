No one understands the sweetness of this theology unless he receives it; no one receives it unless, out of all things, he seeks it alone through the sighs of love. The psalmist sought this when he prayed to the Lord: “Would that my ways were directed to keep your justifications at all times.” For the holy man was carried upward by such movements of desire toward obedience to the divine will that, in the same Psalm 118, he scarcely spoke any verse in which he did not place a word concerning the divine equity or judgment.

The exercise of this purgation begins in the second region and is consummated in this one. For no small labor is required for the affection to be purged from the desires of the world. But when the exercise of spiritual reformation has been undertaken, the mind gradually makes progress in love; it longs to ascend to this region by fixed steps; it hastens through desire toward the invisible school of this theology; sighing with continual groans, it knocks at the door of divine mercy.

But the mind is not admitted into the secrecy of this mystical wisdom unless, after a complete purgation of affection, it is stripped of every creature, unites itself to itself, and withdraws farther from every reasoning or imagining of thinkable things. For unless it has withdrawn from every creature, it does not approach the Creator, who is above all things. Therefore, in this school of divine instruction, the Holy Spirit is believed to preside over the learners; he teaches a person to love nothing in this world and to conform himself in all things to the will of God. Happy is he who has deserved to be the disciple of this master; happy is he who has entered this school through the desire of love, for in mystical silence he receives wonderful things that God has hidden from those who do not love.

That this supreme region of the spirit is founded on simplicity.

Article 4.

Fourth, this most holy region of the spirit is said to be founded on simplicity, in which the mind is united not by essence

--- Page 120 ---

but by the love of adhesion to its own unchangeable principle. For, as we have already said above, God is that supreme good, the One, to which our mind cannot draw near unless, having stripped off every multiplicity that belongs to attraction, imagination, and understanding, it strives to present itself entirely one within itself.

For the mind itself naturally seeks to reach the supreme good; but the more it is divided in its operation through multiplicity, the less fit it is found to be for coming to him. Multiplicity is opposed to unity, and the more the mind has turned itself toward many things, the more it withdraws from unity. One, two, three, four: they are ten. You withdraw from one when you are multiplied in number. Return to one if you desire to have the principle. For the One is the principle of all things; if you add anything to it in your mind, you at once withdraw from it. When you say “one,” you say “principle.” If you say “two,” you have already left the principle behind.

Therefore, so that you may always be able to contemplate your principle, take care at all times, stripped of all multiplicity, to make yourself one. For since all goods are from the first good, and every love is borne toward some good, being naturally kindled by that same good, our love cannot be touched by any goods and rest in the final end unless it strives finally to return, naked and pure, to its own principle. For union is made solely by the power of the One itself; and for no other reason do we say that things more similar to one another are more easily united than because we see that they are one in quality.

Therefore the mind is believed to be able to be united with the supreme principle of things, the One, only when, stripped of every multiplicity and united also to itself, it is lifted up to God by most fervent love. Just as no union occurs between things diverse in quality, so there can be no adhesion between God and a mind divided within itself. For union of one with one occurs through likeness of quality; and there will be adhesion with God for a mind simplified and stripped of all things through loving affection.

Thus the supreme region of the spirit is founded on simplicity; in it the mind of the purified person is raised up to God by the most fervent desire of love, and by grace is united to its unchangeable principle. Intellect and affection are two.
