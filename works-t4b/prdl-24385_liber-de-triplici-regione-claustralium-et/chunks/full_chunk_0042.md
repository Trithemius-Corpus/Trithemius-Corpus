On Meditation on the Benefits of God

The third root of divine fear and love is fixed in meditation on the benefits of God, through which one comes to the grace of devotion. Indeed, the beginning of devotion is placed in meditation on the benefits of God, from whose remembrance love is generated. The middle stage, as one advances in that meditation, desires nothing other than to please God above all things, since it knows that it has been forestalled by him with many benefits, without any merit of its own. The end, with burning desire, loves God above all things with the pure fervor of the mind, and longs to cling constantly to him alone without any intermediary. And although the most merciful God has bestowed many things specially on each one of us, nevertheless, because we write these our synthemata not for one person but for many, we must briefly proclaim not the particular benefits assigned to individuals, but the general benefits granted to all. Therefore we include the chief ones under the number seven, so that throughout the seven days of the week, or even at the canonical hours themselves, we may have material for daily meditation at hand. But if anyone already dwells in higher things through the elevation of the mind into God, let him not require these matters, which are set forth as for beginners. But he who, because of the weakness of his mind, cannot ascend to that supreme summit of contemplation should strive to exercise himself more fervently in these meditations on the benefits of God, so that, while he diligently continues in humbler things, purified and made stronger he may ascend to higher things. Therefore let the devout cenobite consider what he has received from the Lord God, so that he may be able to show himself grateful. Let him render to God what he has received, if he longs to be good. their adornments; and those whom he made intellectual beings according to his image, he has also mercifully called to the heavenly kingdoms. What more could he have given us, he who, when we were not, created us? He gave himself. But what can we render to our Creator, since by merit we are sinners and weak? Let us render what we are; let us render what we owe: faithful service for the body that we have received from the Lord, and sincere love for the soul by which we were created according to the image of God. Thus the prophet admonishes us in the psalm: “Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice before him with trembling.” Serve faithfully; love reverently. On the part of the body, offer faithful service to your Creator; on the part of the soul, love him with all your strength. Serve him because he is powerful and great as Lord; love him because he is good. His power is shown in your creation; his goodness is declared in your restoration. Since he created you, when you did not exist, by his power, he deserves faithful service; since he restored you, fallen as you were, by his goodness, he deserves the affection of sincere love. Indeed, he is our Father through love; he is Lord through incomprehensible power.

Second, he gave us faith with the sacraments, for just as he made us without our will, so he called us to faith without our merit. For Christ says to his disciples in the holy Gospel: “You did not choose me, but I chose you.” Through faith the beginning of the Christian soul is brought back to him; the sacraments renew it. Therefore, incorporated into Christ through the faith and sacraments that he has given, we are bound to cling to him perpetually and to remain continually in his holy covenant. For in baptism we renounced the devil and all his pomps; we promised that we would cling perpetually to Christ, as our sponsors in the faith lifted us from the sacred font on our behalf and placed us before the Lord in the faith of our parents. Therefore let us always say from the heart with the prophet: “It is good for me to cling to God, to place my hope in the Lord God.”

Third, almighty God gave us the craft of righteousness and the teachings of truth. For these we are bound to our Creator in faithful execution and perpetual service without deceit.
