And, when he is soon dying, the holy angels too are believed to run to meet him as one familiarly known, and to present the soul of Jesus' minister with joy before his sight. For thus the Son prays to the Father in the holy Gospel: Father, I will that where I am, there my minister also may be.

How great, indeed, is the condescension of divine tenderness in this venerable sacrament, because, as the same Hugh says, God seems to do nothing less in it, when each day he deigns to descend from heaven upon the altar for our salvation, than when, descending from heaven, he assumed human nature and became man. And just as our Lord Jesus Christ bestowed on us this venerable sacrament, in memory of his most bitter passion, as a sign of the greatest love, so he loves more fully and singularly those who dispose themselves with humble devotion and love to receive it or to minister it to those receiving it; and when they petition him in faith through it, he comes to their aid in every need.

For Jesus, presiding in heaven, will not be hard toward us when we ask with full faith, since he was so gentle and gracious to those who sought mercy while he walked on earth. Who ever came to his feet and was rejected? What sick person sought healing whom he did not cure while walking among human beings? Therefore the Lord Jesus will never be able to deny mercy to us when we ask in faith, for even while still mortal he was so merciful to those who prayed to him. For he says: All things are possible to one who believes.

Therefore, one who is about to minister at Mass should before all else strive to purge himself from every evil temptation and disordered affection. As far as he can, let him draw himself back to himself through the desire of Jesus' love, so that, purified, he may be able to assist the Lord, and to pray effectively both for himself and for the whole Church, the living and the dead. Standing, then, during Mass behind the priest, at the proper time let him pray in these or similar words:

[unclear] which you endured: I beseech you by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death; I beseech you by the holy mystery of your holy body and blood, at whose sacrifice to you I stand as an unprofitable minister: have mercy on me and purify my heart from every illicit affection, so that I may be able to minister worthily to you. Grant me true contrition for every sin and for my own sins, and pardon; the grace of holy compunction; continual remembrance of your passion; pure devotion of heart; a fountain of tears; purity of serene conscience; and perpetual fervor of your most holy love.

Grant me, I beseech you, most merciful lover of humankind, through the pain of your wounds, a firm purpose set in holy fervor; also true contempt for all worldly things; continual desire for the heavenly homeland; and certain progress in holy religion. Grant me, sweetest Jesus, constancy in adversity, patience in tribulation, discipline in conduct, and true humility always and everywhere in mind. Make me, I pray, a participant in this most holy sacrifice of your body and blood, so that my mind may feel the sweetness of your suffering, may burn sweetly in your love, and may not inwardly withdraw from you. Amen.

Let him also pray for his shepherd; let him pray for the whole congregation, that the Lord may mercifully direct him in his will, and, preserving this congregation under regular observance, defend it from every adversity.

Moreover, let him pray for the universal Church; let him pray for the faithful and the unbelieving, that almighty God may keep believers in the faith and convert unbelievers to the catholic faith. Let him also pray for the conversion of useful persons to the order, through whom both his own monastery may advance in both conditions and the whole observance of the order may grow for the better.

Let him not forget the faithful souls in the pains of purgatory, awaiting the mercy of the Creator; but, when praying for them, let him know that after a short while he will pass over to them, where the prayer now poured out will return into the bosom of the one who prays.

But let the devout minister of Christ take care that, through any occasion of negligence, he not neglect anything of those things that belong to his office...
