Third, for one who wishes to stand devoutly before the Lord God in prayers, it is necessary to raise the mind with fervor of spirit to heavenly things. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “God is spirit, and those who adore him must adore in spirit and in truth.” The most devout speaker of God, Bernard, also says: “The little recruit of Christ must be taught in his prayer to lift his heart upward, to pray spiritually, to withdraw from bodily images, and to think nothing bodily.”

But since it is not granted to everyone to be able to raise the mind above the intellect to the invisible God, provision must also be made for the weak by an easier mode of elevation. Hence he also says: “For a weak person meditating or praying, the image of the Lord’s nativity, humanity, passion, or resurrection is more safely set before him; in it the mind may be stirred to tears of compunction, so that the weak soul, which knows only bodily things, may have things by which it may be drawn, according to the measure of its capacity, toward divine things. For truly, while through faith it does not separate God from man in the form of the mediator, it reaches God in the man. For our Savior, the mediator of God and men, Jesus Christ, is God and man.”

Therefore let the devout monk say that he often turns over the first root of divine knowledge, so that through love he may be able to raise himself higher. For the more perfectly he knows God, the more ardently he loves him; and the more he loves, the better he praises. For praise is the first offspring of love.

But so that you may always be able freely to raise your heart in prayer to the Lord God, learn to love nothing in this world in any singular way. For one who is attached to no thing at all wanders less often. For nothing so unburdens the heart and makes it free; nothing makes one converse so freely and without constraint before the Lord; and nothing so quickly and lightly gathers a person back to inward things after various external tumults as being attached, in all things and circumstances, by no private affection to anything, but conversing humbly in all things, resigned to God and to one’s superiors. Besides this, whatever has been enjoined upon one so disposed for the glory of God and the preservation of regular discipline, the monk takes up all of it with humble obedience and sincerity, whether it is agreeable or troublesome. And this firmness of soul, despising both prosperity and adversity, wonderfully

To the second signal.

At the sounding of the second signal, the monk, now sufficiently recollected inwardly, should leave his cell with gravity, always blessing the Lord in his heart. With the prudent virgins he should hasten to meet the bridegroom, and he should consider that he hears the signal of the great king. Leaving the cell, let him say:

Protect, O Lord, your people by the sign of the holy cross from all snares of every enemy, so that we may render you pleasing service, and our sacrifice may be made acceptable to you. Direct us, O Lord, in the way of peace, to the glory of your eternal name.

Entering the church, let him say:

Blessed are those who dwell in your house, O Lord; they will praise you forever and ever. Cleanse me, O Lord, from every defilement of mind and body, so that I may deserve to praise and bless you in your house forever.

Passing before the crucifix, let him say:

I beseech you, most loving Lord Jesus, by all the bitterness of your most holy passion which you endured for me, and especially when your most noble soul went forth from your body: have mercy on my soul at its departure. Amen.

At the holy water:

Take away from me all my iniquities, so that with a holy and pure mind I may deserve to enter the holy places. You shall sprinkle me, O Lord, with hyssop, etc.

Before the image of the blessed Virgin Mary:

O blooming rose, beautiful mother of the Lord, O gentle virgin, O most fruitful vine, brighter than the dawn, pray for us continually.

Passing before the altar, let him say:

Glory to you, O Lord, who were born of a virgin, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, forever and ever.
