Then “Glory to God in the highest” is said in the singular number; in it we confess the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit to be one sole true God in the Trinity of persons and the unity of substance.

Accordingly, during that angelic hymn itself the monk ought to be very devout and, as I might put it, intent upon each single word. In it the gift of peace is promised to people of good will, and God is humbly adored by us in various phrases.

At The Collects.

When the collects or prayers are uttered to God by the priest, the monk, standing in choir, should think of nothing else, but, listening to the priest’s words, should present his own desire to God in agreement with them.

At The Epistle.

When the epistle is read, you ought to attend diligently to everything and, with the greatest fervor of spirit, give thanks to God, because, after the Jewish people had been deserted, he mercifully chose to call you and your forebears from Gentile error to his faith.

At The Gradual.

During the gradual, alleluia, sequence, or tract, consider what a long desire the holy fathers had, who before Christ’s incarnation longed for his coming. Then turn over in your mind with how many labors and injuries your Savior was wearied for you in this world for thirty-three years, he who willingly gave himself over to death for your salvation. Then remember the long and perilous exile of your soul in the flesh, so that, by this meditation on your present misery, you may be able the more fully to kindle your affection toward divine love.

During The Gospel.

During the holy Gospel, with mind lifted up to the Lord, rejoice and give thanks, because the merciful Father willed that you be born of Christian parents, and because he made you a sharer in his holy and most sweet teaching.

During The Creed.

When the symbol of the catholic faith is sung, consider all its words and mysteries with humble fervor of spirit. Hasten at once to gather yourself wholly back into yourself,

so that you may be able to give thanks to him who called you to the faith without any merit of your own, and to raise yourself up to him through the love of the Spirit. Think, if you can, indeed as much as you can, how great, exalted, and immense is the Son of God, who willed to be incarnate for you, and what hard and bitter things he endured for you.

At The Offertory.

At the offertory, join your mind with the host that is to be consecrated in the offering, and consider what is being done there mystically.

For the host is round and white. In its whiteness are set forth the purity, innocence, and suffering humanity of our Savior, to be offered to God the Father for us against all our impurities; in its roundness, his immense divinity is offered to us as the reward set before us. Therefore say to the Lord:

Prayer.

I have no worthy gift to offer you, almighty God; I hand over myself to you as a sacrifice. Therefore receive, holy Father, in union with the most holy offering of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, me as an eternal gift to you and as a perpetual servant. And give me the continual fire of your holy love, so that I may constantly render to you a service pleasing to you. Through the same Christ.

Prayer.

I offer to you, almighty God, the life, passion, and death of the same our Lord Jesus Christ, for my sins, for the holy Roman and universal Church, for the supreme pontiff, for our bishop, for our abbot, for all my brothers, for the union of our observance, for our whole order wherever it may be, for the salvation of all the faithful, for the conversion of unbelievers, and for the redemption of the souls of the faithful existing in the pains of purgatory. Receive, I beseech you, holy Father, this offering of mine in union with that charity with which you received the same our Lord Jesus Christ dying on the cross for us; and may it be pleasing to you.
