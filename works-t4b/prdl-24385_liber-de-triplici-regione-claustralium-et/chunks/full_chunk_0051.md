An unskilled and cold man ought not to speak about the secrets of divine contemplation, one who still remains with the weak in the valleys. For only he speaks fruitfully about profound matters who is illumined by the ray of a double spirit. Nevertheless, since I am a man placed under obedience to the annual chapter, I am compelled to take up the command of that synod and to complete, according to my powers, the work set before me. Therefore I enter the third region of cloistered souls under the guidance of the venerable father, Lord Johannes, the devout abbot of Bursfeld, so that, by reducing as far as I can to my own manner of speaking what he described in scholastic and truthful language, I may appear to have obeyed the chapter.

Here begins the third region of the soul, which is called spiritual, in which the highest tranquility of mind is possessed.

The third, or highest, region of the soul is called spiritual; in it the highest repose and tranquility of mind is found. This region is compared to the empyrean heaven, in which there is the true repose of all and the true happiness of souls. The spirit standing in this region leaves behind the subrational part and, lifted up by love into God, ascends marvelously. For whatever it does in this most holy region is judged wholly divine. Here the human spirit, rapt above itself, receives the divine inpouring; and, lulled to rest in the darkness of divine contemplation and withdrawn from all creatures, it sweetly rests. In this region almighty God directs the spirit contemplating him; and, when the intellectual powers of the soul have been simplified, recalled from every phantasm into the inner chamber, and gathered together according to their objective tendencies, he strips the mind of all multiplicity, leads it back to himself as the one principle, and also makes it one in himself, not by nature but by gracious participation. The citizens of this highest region are truly spiritual people, who, despising all things and affections of the world, love nothing in this world except God, desire nothing inordinately, but, absorbed by inward affection by

the divine Spirit, thirst vehemently for God himself and burn with an insatiable desire for his love. For they are driven in God by such great fervor of love that, lifted above themselves, they leave behind nearly all intellectual operations; and, rapt into the abyss of divine contemplation, they admit absolutely nothing except God. There, existing insensibly through the grace of love, they become one with God, while the distinctness of their natures is preserved, by the gift of participation. For the holy apostle of Jesus Christ says: “He who clings to the Lord is one spirit.” And again he says: “Those who are led by the Spirit of God are the sons of God.” The same apostle also shows himself to be a citizen of this most holy region when he cries out to the Philippians: “Our conversation is in heaven.”

For as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord; but we are sweetly led, as though through sleep, to our homeland when in mind and spirit we have our conversation in heavenly things. I have said “through sleep” not properly, but by a remote comparison. For in sleep we pass through images of things intelligible to us, with the species preserved in the imagination; in contemplation, beyond all imagination, we are lifted up to God by pure mind and spirit. And therefore this highest region of the soul is called spiritual, from which all religious receive the name of spirituality. Woe to us. We are called spiritual from this most blessed region, and are named religious lovers of God, although we have never climbed the summit of this mountain through the height of contemplation, but, always weak with the weak, lie on the bed of carnal desires. Why do we bear a holy name, we who are not holy?

Let us therefore strive with all our strength, through the continual exercise of the middle region, to ascend to the height of this highest summit, if we truly desire to obtain the name of spiritual life. In this region charity holds dominion, wisdom the consulship; in it nothing is loved except God, nothing is done inordinately. This most holy region, moreover, belongs to the empyrean heaven, because those whom it spiritually receives as citizens it brings to the joys of heaven.
