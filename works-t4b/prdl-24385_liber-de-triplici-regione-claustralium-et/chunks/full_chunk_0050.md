No one fully understands what sweetness, what delight, and what joy of mind the love of God produces in the heart of the one who loves, unless he has despised all the things and events of the world and has loved God alone for a long time. The heart of one who loves God loves none of those things that are in the world; it despises the riches, honors, and favors of the age; it does not fear to undergo any adversities for the love of its Lord. The love of God is never idle; it always desires to return to its beginning; it refuses to suffer no labors in this world. Therefore let the person who does not wish to become slothful love.

One’s neighbor also is to be loved sweetly for God’s sake, and is not to be offended in any way against God or justice. Let each person always show himself to his brother, in words and deeds, such as he expects others to be toward himself. For he truly loves his neighbor who exposes his possessions, body, and soul for him. Christ taught this; the Son of God did this for us. Indeed, whoever says that he truly believes in Christ ought himself to walk just as he walked. He does not truly love Christ who does not keep his commandments. For he says: “This is my commandment, that you love one another, as I have loved you.” We are members of one head, Jesus Christ; and we then believe in him when we observe his commandment concerning love toward God and toward neighbor. Therefore let us love our neighbor, because he is a member with us in Christ, so that just as one member in the body suffers with another, so a brother may be humbly helped by a brother for the love of Christ.

The second mode commands you to love wisely, and in divine love to seek nothing other than God. Stretch out your affection toward that Word which was in the beginning with God; raise your mind above everything that has been created, because unless you leave behind yourself and all things that are in the world, you will not be able to come to him whom you love. Finally, if you desire to love God wisely, do not think yourself to be anything; for where a person reckons himself to be something, he already loves unwisely.

The third mode commands that you love bravely, and that you never fear to expose body and soul for the love of God.

--- Page 110 ---

The lover of God is not brave if he is afraid to undergo death for him. For love is strong as death. One’s neighbor is loved wisely if not the person but the vice is hated. And he is loved bravely if every injury is forgiven from a spirit of doing good to that same person.

Here ends the second region.

Sebastian Hoffmann in Eger, 1548.

Preface to the third treatise of the first part, which describes the supreme region of the spirit of cloistered persons.

At last, with the Lord’s help, as I am about to write of the supreme region of the spirit, I am at once restrained by fear and shame: for, being small, I fear to speak of the greatest matters, and I am deeply ashamed to teach things in which I am not sufficiently experienced. For what ability, however practiced, could recognize the state of a mind resting sweetly in God through love? Or what iron pen, even of the most eloquent orator, could explain the secret sweetness of inward contemplation? Indeed, the exercise of this third region surpasses human understanding for the most part: in it the mind of the purified person, through the fervor of divine love, is carried above itself and, while its natural essence remains, is absorbed in the abyss of divine contemplation, lulled with wondrous sweetness. It is so overwhelmed by the great sweetness of the inward majesty that it seems able neither to understand nor to express its own state. Once the operation of the senses has clearly been left behind, it also passes wholly beyond the intellect; and, as though abandoning itself still more, without image, reasoning, or understanding, it is raised by affection above itself to that supreme summit of the divine darkness. But when it has entirely failed from itself, it does not act but rests; nor, when returned to itself, can it understand what it has undergone, or reveal to anyone the manner of its elevation. For this elevation of the mind into God is so pure and so sublime that not only can the inexperienced not understand it, but even the experienced person cannot fully explain in words what it is like. But what shall I be able to say about the mountain of divine darkness, which I have not yet deserved to enter? For the well of inward contemplation is deep, and I am weak: I have brought neither a jar suited to these waters, nor have I prepared steps of ascent suitable for such heights. Polished words do not befit a fool, nor is it fitting for an unskilled and cold man to speak of the secrets of divine contemplation.
