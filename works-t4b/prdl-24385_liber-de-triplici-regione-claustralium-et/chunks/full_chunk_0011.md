What then will you answer to the supreme shepherd, useless rector, when not only the blood of one slain by you cries out against you, but also the account of those who have made too little progress demands an answer? See, now you walk in the worst security, despising God’s examination; you think everything that pleases you in the body is permitted to you, and you do not consider to whom you will one day give account for all things. Soon the Lord of all will come, before whom you do not fear to show yourself an unfaithful servant, and whose honor you do not fear to arrogate to yourself. Unless meanwhile you do penance, unless you amend your life for the better, you will burn without end with the demons in hell.

Seventh, it happens that a pastor is disordered toward his subjects if, neglecting the common usefulness, he seeks his own advantage with a vicious intention; if, presiding in a monastery, he serves not God but his own interests, while he rejoices that he is prelate in order to obtain the pernicious freedoms of the flesh, and fraudulently provides for his own future from the goods of the monastery. This disorder is seen especially in those who, on any pretext, withdraw from office, to whom, as the reward of their stewardship, as though they had been bishops, an annual pension from the goods of the monastery is assigned. This is not the manner of those who seek God; this is not the way of the old monks. When you withdraw from pastoral office for God’s sake, withdraw simply. Do not demand anything for food and clothing, if you do not wish to sin with proprietors. You are a monk; you were a monk before you became abbot; you will remain a monk after the office. Why then do you demand a pension, since you know that you are a monk? Just as someone commits the vice of simony by buying prelacies, so by demanding a pension for resigning an abbacy one sins against the Rule. What then? Do we condemn those who, withdrawing from the office of pastoral care without fault, have received annual provisions from the goods of the monastery? Far from it. We do not forbid necessities; we deny superfluities. Yet one who is a true monk and lover of God is content, even after the abbacy, with the regular provision.

In What Way the Monks, Citizens of the Lowest Region, Are Disordered toward One Another

Article Four

--- Page 032 ---

The monks and carnal men of the lowest region are disordered toward one another in many and various ways. Since they are stirred up by animal passions like beasts and do not direct themselves toward the right judgment of reason, it is no wonder if they pervert the order of brotherly conversation. For a troubled conscience is always savage and presumptuous, and an evil mind never has the joys of peace, but is continually shaken inwardly by sorrow, tortured within by the everlasting fires of its crimes. And so the monks, citizens of the dark region, blinded by evil and disordered affections, do not consider the end of the monastic profession; they love nothing except the comfort of the present life; they neglect their own salvation; they do not rise up to the love of eternity.

First, then, they are greatly disordered toward one another when they set aside the intellectual regard that should be held for the brothers; when they do not notice the image of God in a human being; when they do not consider the communion of grace and glory infused into human beings and to be granted in the future; nor do they take care to show one another the love of brotherly fellowship. They love one another like horse and mule, harmonious in bodily form, because they love whatever is pleasing to sensuality, and do not admit whatever accords with reason. One is loved by another not because he is a human being, but because he is like the one who loves him in the affection of sensuality. For the man of flesh loves in a person not virtues, not religion, not finally nature itself, but flesh, blood, and the quality of the body. Therefore, since the love of carnal people is carnal, it is easily changeable by occasion. For citizens of this lowest region venerate those whose manners are like their own, and carnal monks always embrace carnal monks with affection. What God has loved from eternity, the disordered cenobite persecutes; and the one whom he despises because of his faults, this man venerates as holy.

Second, cloistered men are disordered toward one another by reason of dissimilarity, when either the seniors, stirred up by harshness and pride, despise their juniors, or the juniors stubbornly oppose their seniors.
