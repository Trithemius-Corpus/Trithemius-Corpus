Yet all that time during which something of the mountain remained, however very long it might be, would seem as nothing in comparison with eternity. What is eternal admits no imagining of an end, nor any hope of completion or limit.

O then, how foolish, how senseless, how insane is the person who casts aside this consideration of eternity, and uselessly spends the acceptable time granted to him for meriting eternal goods. The hour will come, and now it is already hastening near, in which the wretched man, when he can no longer do better, will learn what eternity is. But you, my monk, who desire to be faithful, cast away from yourself every anxiety of the world; set all your concern on the love of God. Do nothing, say nothing, think nothing except what you know can lead you to the love of your Creator, or pertains to the edification of others or to the simple necessity of the body. Separate, I beg you, precious things from the lowly, so that you may be able to delight in eternal goods. Let no anxiety of the flesh draw you away from the inner tranquillity of mind; let no pleasure corrupt you. Strive, as far as you can, always to persist in prayers with devotion, and again to lift yourself more frequently through the affection of love.

On Always Having Zeal for God.

The seventh root of the fear of God is called zeal; in this the monk is commanded to exercise himself in no small measure. For no one calmly tolerates injuries against his lord, if he truly loves him. Therefore you, monk, who desire to be a faithful servant of Jesus Christ, do not neglect his zeal within yourself. When you see an injury against God, do not dissemble; for whoever does not at once resist those who disparage his lord is shown to love him less. Let people know you as such a lover of God and enemy of vices that they will not dare to speak evil in your presence, nor presume to do any evil thing while you look on.

Be conspicuous for uprightness of soul, bright with the merit of your life, reverend in zeal. The upright will love you as a servant of God; the wicked will fear your face as that of a judge. Let no one presume to do anything in your sight that is alien to good morals, since all will have known that you cannot tolerate injury against God. Thus Phinehas the priest merited an everlasting covenant when, by zealously defending the law, he powerfully appeased the Lord’s wrath.

If you see order perishing in the monastery, if your brothers have begun in any way to corrupt regular observance, do not, if you are a servant of God, be silent about their vices. Rather, set yourself bravely on behalf of the house of the Lord; exercise the zeal of God as much as you can; destroy the counsels of the malicious; resist the rashness of the proud. If you are a true lover of God, by your very face alone you will scatter depraved gatherings. For no one will lay bare his plan before you if he suspects your zeal to be against it. But if the wicked do not fear your presence when they act badly, then surely they recognize no zeal of God in you.

Therefore, in order to show yourself a true servant of Christ, you ought willingly to forgive your brothers, for the love of God, injuries inflicted on yourself, and to pray to God for those who injure you. But injuries against the Lord, since it is not yours to forgive them without his will, you should rather avenge out of zeal for uprightness. Let love of blood-kinship yield in these matters; let the bond of friendship withdraw far away. Zeal for God must be preferred to all things. For friendship with a person must be observed in such a way that it is never preferred to zeal for God.

Woe to monks and abbots who so boldly cast aside zeal for God, who put human familiarities before the divine command, who dissemble the vices of subjects with foolish compassion, who observe the traditions of the holy Rule neither in themselves nor in others. And what shall I say? We perceive that among cloistered monks obedience to commands is despised, zeal for uprightness is spurned, observance of the Rule is contemned.
