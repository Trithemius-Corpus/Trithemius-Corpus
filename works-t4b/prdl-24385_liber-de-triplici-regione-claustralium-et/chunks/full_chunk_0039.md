O what shall I, a little worm, indeed nothing in comparison with my maker, repay to the supreme and incomprehensible majesty, who by his power created me when I did not exist, and, when I had perished, being made man for my sake, redeemed me by dying on the cross? What shall I repay you, mediator of God and men, Jesus, savior of the world, who, though you are God, exalted and inestimable, were made man for my most vile sake and willed to undergo things so base and unworthy? Behold, my heart speaks to you; to you all my inward parts are bowed, because no voice can express what you grant your faithful to feel inwardly in the heart concerning your most innocent passion.

Fourth, in the work of our justification, the kindly patience of God is praiseworthy, because he not only mercifully bears with sinners, but also calls those who are to be justified to repentance with promises of eternal joys. Who could sufficiently praise this most indulgent clemency of the Father, who so mercifully justifies sinners returning to himself, grants after a fall the grace of meriting, arranges many occasions for living well, supplies his sacraments to the penitent as certain channels of grace, and finally pours the Holy Spirit himself into his faithful for compunction, instruction, consolation, and thanksgiving, making friends and sons out of enemies? He does not consider what sort of sinner a person was before; he seeks only that henceforth he remain faithful to him. Great is the clemency of divine compassion, which so mercifully receives the penitent into pardon, and promises them great rewards even after guilt.

Fifth, the profound justice of God is praiseworthy in the damnation of the reprobate, because he allows no evil to pass unpunished, depriving the sinner of the good that, by sinning, the fool does not fear to lose. Finally, to speak in our own manner, the supreme purity of God is shown praiseworthy in this, that he abominates every sin and vice like a putrid corpse. We also recognize that his wisdom is praiseworthy in the damnation of the impious, since, drawing good things from evil,

he compels those who, by sinning against justice, were unwilling to be subject in guilt, to serve him in punishment.

Sixth, the abundant munificence of God is praiseworthy in the glorification of the blessed, since for very few merits he rewards his elect with so ineffable a recompense, and gives them so immortal a gift with the holy angels.

Seventh, the incomprehensible excellence of God in himself is praiseworthy, for he orders all things according to his providence and in himself, most tranquil, remains immovable by everlasting motion or time. The seventh contemplation of this consideration pertains to the sabbath of eternal happiness, in which there is no longer the laborious activity of earthly life, but the blessedness of a happy, eternal dwelling and rest. For truly, just as God the creator of all things is read to have completed and rested from all his work on the seventh day, so not labor but eternal rest will be imposed upon the just in the heavenly sabbath. Therefore, if we desire happily to come to those heavenly mansions, let us rest in God alone, in whom the rational creature is perfected by resting; and, once perfected through the adhesion of love, it is gladdened with the Lord in ineffable union and joy. Now let pure devotion seek nothing outside God for praising God, since God himself, omnipotent, perfect, and most wise, is sufficient to all as the matter of his own praise, and exceeds every praise by his infinite perfections. For Scripture says: “Praise the Lord as much as you are able; he will still surpass it.” For he is greater than all praise, and therefore he is to be praised by us most humbly rather in himself and for his own sake than from creatures.

From the foregoing considerations there arises concerning God a manifold and varied affection of devotion, through which a more worthy effect of prayer is also produced according to the desire of the affection. For when meditation of the heart is taken up concerning the majesty or power of God, reverent admiration is brought forth. But when we begin to meditate on his ineffable wisdom, we accuse ourselves within ourselves of our foolish imperfection. Thus, in turn, from consideration of the mercy of God there arises hope of pardon.
