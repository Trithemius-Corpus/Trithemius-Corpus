The more, however, anyone placed in a monastery has fallen away from this effort to make progress, the farther he withdraws from the purpose of a monk.

How the carnal monks, citizens of this lowest region, are disordered in many articles.

Although the description of this lowest region applies generally to all carnal men who abide in sins and crimes with the moral beasts, nevertheless, so that we may tend to our own affairs, we intend to speak of lukewarm and carnal cloisterers, doing no injury at all to the good and devout. For always and in all things we wish the conduct of good cloisterers to remain untouched and unharmed whenever we discuss the evil pursuits of the wicked. Therefore we rebuke evil cloisterers and corrupt morals, while everywhere honoring and praising the good, as is fitting. And what else but evil can we say about the evil, in whom we find no good things to speak of? They live disorderly in a holy order; they despise the traditions of the elders; they do not admit words of salvation. Indeed, so great is the disorder of the monks of the lowest region that, in my judgment, it would be easier to convert a publican than to recall a disorderly monk to repentance. We comprehend this disorder of carnal monks in eight articles, although we are not unaware that they are disordered in many other matters.

First, the beast-like monks, citizens of this lowest region, are disordered toward God; next, toward their own prelate; third, the prelate toward his subjects; fourth, the members toward one another; again, fifth, they are disordered toward themselves; then toward the goods of the monastery; seventh, toward contempt of the world; and finally toward repentance for sin. These are the principal disorders of the citizens of the lowest region; these are the beast-like affections of carnal monks, who dwell in the lowest things in mind and body, and do not hasten to make progress toward the highest things. Therefore I shall take care to set out briefly, in order, these disordered conditions of the lowest region, and to demonstrate as best I can how far carnal monks are from God. Ps. 118.v. For, as the prophet says, salvation is far from sinners.

How the citizens of this lowest region are disordered toward God. Article 1.

Therefore the carnal monks of this lowest region, its mad inhabitants, are in many ways disordered toward God. They do not feel the sweetness of his love; they admit no compunction of divine fear; beast-like and swollen in their affections, they are at all times unclean in their carnal pleasures.

And first, their disorder toward God takes its origin from ignorance: for, being given over to animal passions, they do not care to have God in their knowledge, nor do they consent to think in their mind about those things that lead to fear of him; instead, blinded by evil desires, they become vain in their thoughts. But what does Scripture say? “He who is ignorant shall be ignored.” They do not wish to understand so that they may act well; they do not fear before the face of the strict judge; without repentance they constantly persist in evil. Indeed, after the manner of beasts they do not fear to rot in their pleasures; they pay no heed to the patience of God calling them to repentance; they seek nothing except the comforts of their own flesh. At all times they walk in the greatest sins without any fear; they stubbornly refuse to know the ways of the Lord. Of the future judgment they think either nothing or little; they do not fear the punishments prepared in hell for sinners; the grace of compunction never pierces their hearts, since the love of God does not inflame their minds. They have no concern for God, and no dread of death. They obey the belly more than God; at all times they desire to live according to the flesh; and while they shamefully neglect their own salvation, they descend to hell with the demons. Indeed, those who, forgetful of God, badly keep the vow of the holy monastic way of life do not reach the rewards of heavenly life to be granted to the just.

Second, carnal monks are disordered toward God by a foolish profession: for they promise to live according to the rule, yet they do not truly care to fulfill this. And what is more disordered than to promise a holy life to the one God and then remain in the filth of one’s former life? Whoever persists in a monastery otherwise than the monastic rule teaches is a mockery of almighty God, not a monk.
