I know and truly acknowledge, and I confess this to your goodness, that I am not worthy to approach so great a mystery, because of my many sins and my many negligences. But I also know, and with all my heart believe and confess with my mouth, that since you are almighty, you can make me worthy, you who alone justify the ungodly, and from the unclean can make the clean. Therefore, by your omnipotence I beseech you: grant to me, your unworthy servant, true contrition for my sins, pure devotion, and most fervent compunction in your love, so that I may be able to offer this most holy sacrifice to you with fear and reverence, with purity of heart and a fountain of sweet tears, with spiritual gladness and heavenly joy, with humility and the fervor of charity. O most sweet Lord Jesus, grant me in this hour your mercy, so that my mind may feel the sweetness of your most blessed presence, the devotion of the holy angels around me, and a peaceful lifting-up into you.

Take away from me, Lord Jesus, all my iniquities, and graciously kindle within me the fire of your love. Do not be angry with me, O Jesus, ineffable good, nor restrain yourself from me because of the multitude of my sins. For I pour out my prayers before your face not in my own justifications, but in your many mercies. Take from me, Lord Jesus, a stony and hard heart, and give me a heart of flesh and compunction, which may fear you, love you, cherish you above all things, delight in you alone, follow you, and enjoy you forever.

Most merciful Lord Jesus, I beseech you by the most holy mystery of your body and blood, by which in your holy Church we are daily fed and given drink, washed and sanctified, and made participants in your one supreme divinity: give me your holy virtues, adorned with which I may be able to approach your altar with a good conscience, so that these heavenly sacraments may become for me salvation and life. For you said with your holy and most blessed mouth: “The bread that I shall give is my flesh, for the life of the world. If anyone eats of this bread, he shall live forever.” O most sweet bread, heal the palate of my heart, so that I may taste the sweetness of your love.

Heal, Lord, my whole heart from every sickness, so that it may taste no sweetness besides you, seek no love besides you, and love no consolation besides you. O most holy Jesus, bread of life and understanding, having in you every delight and every sweetness of fragrance, you who always refresh us from yourself and never fail in yourself: let my heart, I beseech you, eat you, and let my soul be filled with the sweetness of your savor. O holy bread, O living bread, beautiful bread, pure bread, who came down from heaven and gives life to the world: come into my heart and cleanse me from every defilement of mind and spirit. Enter into my soul; heal and sanctify me at all times. Be my protection and the continual salvation of my body and soul. Drive away from me the enemies who lie in wait for me, so that, fortified by the sacrament of your body and blood, I may advance through you on the right path under regular discipline, and after this life may come joyfully to your kingdom.

Excuse for the length.

If this prayer seems too long to anyone, let him read whatever part he wishes; for this reason we have divided it into short paragraphs, to remove weariness. But the one who wishes to read neither the whole nor a part complains of its length in vain, since he will be shown willing to pray neither a long prayer nor a short one.

Meditation before approaching the altar.

Then, by meditation on the Lord’s passion, or at least on one article of it, or on several if the opportunity of time is present, the mind of a devout person is pierced with wonderful effect, if he has the habit of lively meditation. I would call meditation on the Lord’s passion savory when a person, purified by true mortification, as soon as he begins to turn over in thought the Lord’s passion, is dissolved into tears without violence, from love alone, and from the excessive fervor of desire is prepared as though for a certain ecstasy of mind. Whoever receives this gift from the Lord will never be able to prepare himself better for celebrating than if, before ascending the altar, he always passes through with tears at least one article of the Lord’s passion in meditation. For since the celebration of Mass was instituted by our Redeemer himself in memory of the Lord’s passion, a person never prepares himself better and more fruitfully for it than if he burns more fervently through meditation on that most bitter passion.

The elevation of the priest’s mind to God during the celebration.

With how great reverence, devotion, and purity of mind we ought to stand before the almighty Lord at the altar, what speech could express, or what understanding could comprehend and penetrate? For there the true body and true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are consecrated in an ineffable manner; there the Lord of majesty, truly present, is adored; there the army of invisible angels, standing before its king, rejoices together; there the whole heavenly court adores the Lord and man Jesus. Therefore the greatest purity of mind, the greatest reverence, and humility are needed when we must stand before so great a majesty, lest the Lord Jesus, who searches the reins and hearts, be able to find in us anything that offends him. Let all worldly anxieties yield and go far away from here; let all thoughts withdraw; here let nothing be thought except what pertains to the consummation of so great a mystery; let nothing be turned over in the mind except what befits the presence of so great a majesty and glory. For here the work is not one of human power, but the miracle of divine majesty is performed: the Son of God is sacrificed to the Father; the body of our Redeemer is truly received; the mystery of our redemption is celebrated. Therefore the priest standing at the altar should remember that he stands before the invisible majesty, and should take care that he think of nothing else.
