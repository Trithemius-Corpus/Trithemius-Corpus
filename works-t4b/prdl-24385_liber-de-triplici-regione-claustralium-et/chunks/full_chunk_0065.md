Let him examine himself by considering carefully, and let him test whether the compunction of devotion is still fervent in his mind. If he finds that fervor is still present, let him strive to kindle it further with holy desires, and to call himself onward to higher things. But if he senses that he has grown lukewarm in affection or distracted by thoughts, then at once, rebuking himself, let him say these or similar words: Woe to me, miserable man, who praise my God so negligently. Behold, my Lord, suffering most bitterly for me throughout the whole night, had no rest, and I could not watch one hour with him. Help me, O loving God, that I may be able to sing psalms to you wisely.

Finally, under any psalm whatever, he ought at a suitable moment to stir himself to the fervor of compunction by some ardent words, so that by mental exhortation he may kindle his affection more fully. For example: O my God, that I might love you with my whole heart above all things, and cling continually to you alone. O good Jesus, kindle my heart in your most holy love. My love has been crucified, and what am I doing upon the earth? O sweet Jesus, my love, possess my heart, for you are the treasure of my heart, my refuge and my God.
