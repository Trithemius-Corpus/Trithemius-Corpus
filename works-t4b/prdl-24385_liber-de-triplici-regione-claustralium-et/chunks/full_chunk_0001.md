Letter of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the little work On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons.

To the reverend fathers in Christ, the lord presidents, definitors, and the other abbots of the annual chapter of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the Bursfeld union and observance: Brother Johannes Trithemius, called abbot at Sponheim, of the same order and congregation, in the diocese of Mainz, offers reverence and charity.

I have obeyed your commands, most venerable fathers, and, as you instructed me, I undertook to correct the little work on the threefold region of cloistered persons together with the spiritual exercise of monks, recently edited by the venerable father Lord Johannes, abbot of Bursfelde. And indeed, if your authority had not compelled me, I would never have dared to set my hand to the distinguished work of so great a father, who, it is agreed, is far more learned and holier than I, and wrote it excellently. For what was I to correct, where everything is found most approved? That good and devout man wrote no otherwise than as he wished and as was fitting.

Nevertheless, in order to satisfy both your command and the prayers of the author himself, I rendered the whole little work in briefer, though not fewer, sentences, and, while remaining faithful to its sense, I brought it as far as I could into my own manner and style of writing. I preserved the order and matter of the earlier volume, but entirely changed its form and manner of expression. In almost every place I added many things that seemed useful to me; I removed a few; in individual passages I altered the words, not the meaning. Your command gave me the courage to do this, and the insistent request of the aforesaid father fully persuaded me. For you wished me to make a new work out of old material, to add whatever seemed suitable to me, to cut away superfluities, and to alter what needed changing. I did what I could; I obeyed your commands; according to the powers of my intellect I completed, by rewriting, the work assigned to me.

--- Page 011 ---
Now therefore, most beloved fathers, receive a work that is not mine but yours, not composed by me but rather rewritten. If the labor pleases you, praise the talent of the aforesaid father; but if, because of its worthlessness, it displeases you, correct my imperfection. I had excellent material for spiritual edification from another's resources, but through my inexperience I have built an inept structure. If I have been made a fool, you compelled me. Therefore, whether the work pleases or displeases, it has been easier for me, by writing ineptly, to take up your command obediently than, against obedience, to keep silence. Yet I trust, not in my own merits but in another's, that a work brought forth by charity will displease no one who loves God.

I pray that your fatherhoods may remain forever blessed in Christ. From Sponheim, the eighth day before the Kalends of September, in the year of the Lord's incarnation 1497.
