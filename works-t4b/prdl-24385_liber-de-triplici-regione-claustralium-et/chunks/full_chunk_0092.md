After Compline

After Compline, when his strength has been recollected, let the devout cenobite enter alone into the secrecy of his own heart, and leave outside all cares and anxieties of the world, so that, free and released from all things, he may deserve to speak with the Lord Jesus through the compunction of desire, and to be sweetly united to him through the fire of love. Therefore, having recalled the faults of the day summarily to the memory of examination, let him pray thus to the Lord.

Prayer.

O most merciful Lord Jesus Christ, who for our redemption willed to undergo a most shameful death, grant me, I beseech you, through the pain of your wounds, and through the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death, pardon for all my sins, so that, with my conscience cleansed, I may henceforth cling to you continually through the desire of love, make daily progress in holy religion, and humbly submit myself in all things to your most holy will.

To the Son.

O Lord Jesus Christ, I commend my body and soul to you this night and at every time, in the power and union of that love with which, while hanging on the cross, you commended your beloved mother, the Virgin Mary, to the disciple. In that love, I beseech you, guard me from all snares of the ancient serpent, suppress the illicit movements of the flesh, drive far away foul and evil dreams, and repel nocturnal phantasms, so that I may sleep secure in your peace, and, with my hours duly ordered, rise cheerfully to your praises. Amen.

To the Father.

O holy Father, almighty and eternal God, who gently received us into your grace in the passion of your only-begotten Son,

gracefully receive, I beseech you, all the good works that by your gift I have performed this day, in words, deeds, or thoughts, in the power and union of that charity and love with which you received that same Son of yours hanging and dying on the cross for us. To you, holy Father, in the faith and union of that charity, I commend myself this night and at every time, with which your Son, our Lord, when already about to die, commended his spirit to you on the cross.

To Saint Mary.

Holy and immaculate Virgin Mary, Mother of God, I ask you, through the bitter passion and death of your most sweet Son, do not reject me, a miserable sinner, because of my unclean conscience; but remember that you are the mother of mercy, and pray for me to the God whom you bore, that he may mercifully forgive me my sins, make me a fervent lover of himself, guard me this night from all snares and evils, and after the death of the flesh lead my soul to eternal life. To your protection, most blessed Virgin Mary, I commit myself this night and at every time, in the power and union of that committal by which your beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, when already about to die on the cross, commended you to John his disciple.

On the Memory of the Lord’s Passion.

Since the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is the gate of our salvation, through which the kingdom of heaven is opened to us, we ought to exercise ourselves in it as frequently as we can with devotion, because we shall easily obtain the effect of our prayers if, through the love of compassion, we adore the Lord in the power of his wounds. Therefore, faithful cenobite, with all the diligence I can, I admonish you, exhort you, and ask you, mindful of the benefits of God, that you neglect no day at all on which you do not give thanks to your Savior for the bitterness of his

passion and death. And if, for any reason, you cannot do this at greater length, at least do not refuse to show yourself grateful to your Redeemer with a single word. Thus the Lord Jesus himself revealed to those of his lovers to whom he wished: that nothing is more pleasing to him, and nothing more useful to us, than to recall with thanksgiving the memory of his passion and death, and to admonish others to do the same. Receive, therefore, this shortened word, lest a more lengthy prayer make you weary.

With knees bent to the ground and hands joined together, say:

I give thanks to you, most sweet Lord Jesus, for all the wounds, pains, and insults of your most innocent passion and death. I ask you, through that same death of yours, have mercy on me now and in the hour of my death.

With a compassionate heart I give thanks, O Lord Jesus Christ, for your most bitter passion and your most innocent death. I ask you, have mercy on me.

I praise, bless, and adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, King of heaven and earth, and I give thanks to you for your holy passion, by which you freed miserable me from perpetual death. I ask you, through the power of that same passion of yours, make me a sharer forever in your most innocent death.

Conclusion of this compendium by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.

And so that I may not be more lengthy than is fitting, here I set an end to the compendium. For the venerable assembly of abbots of our annual chapter commanded me to avoid prolixity, lest lukewarm and worldly cloisterers claim weariness as an excuse. If the Lord Jesus gives me a life free from cares, as I have always desired, I shall try to satisfy both my own intention and another’s request. For I have resolved, for certain virgins dedicated to Christ who asked for it, to hand down a rule by which any devout cenobite may exercise himself in the Lord’s Passion.
