You should first spend some time thinking of God or meditating on God, so that in holy thoughts you may be able to fall asleep sweetly. For whatever the occupations of one waking have been, such also will be the repose of one sleeping, and such too will be his dreams. For a mind burning with divine love will be unable, whether sleeping or waking, to forget its beloved, as the bride says in a certain way in the Canticles: “I sleep, and my heart keeps watch.”

At Vespers. Consideration VII.

When the first signal for Vespers has been given, and when, as customary, “Blessed be the name of the Lord” has been said, give thanks to almighty God, who allowed others to suffer in secular business, as is usually permitted, and who willed you to be free from those things for serving him. Then recollect yourself inwardly, or meditate as much as you can on the Lord’s passion, until the second signal sounds and you go to choir. For in my judgment there is no meditation better for the compunction of beginners and of those making progress, and for the devotion of the mind, than that which takes its beginning from the passion of the Lord Jesus Christ and pierces devout affections.

At the Vigils of the Dead.

When the vigil office after Vespers has been completed, you will be able to say the hours of the Holy Cross, and afterward to think somewhat about the immeasurable charity of God, with which he loves the souls of the faithful. Then, as much as you can, consider in what great desolation those holy souls established in purgatory are placed; how great the pain is for a spirit separated from the body and desiring to return to the center of its own beginning, not to be able to behold the face of its Creator through friendly enjoyment, but to be forced to wait in punishments and torments most grievous to it. For so great is the desolation, sadness, and sorrow of a soul separated, placed in punishments, and desiring to come to God, that no kind of torments known to us in this world could be compared with its sorrow. For in our manner of speaking there is no comparison between bodily sorrow and desolation and the state of a separated spirit, placed in longing for return to its own beginning.

Therefore, as much as you can, have compassion for the desolation of the faithful souls groaning in the prison of purgatory, and pour out diligent prayers to the Lord for them. For although, according to his justice, the almighty and merciful Father defers them from the kingdom, nevertheless he loves them with inestimable charity. Therefore whatever good you now do by praying for them, you offer as a pleasing sacrifice to almighty God. And when you come there, you will receive multiplied whatever you have done here in charity for them. For Scripture says: “It is a holy and wholesome thing to pray for the dead, that they may be loosed from sins.”

Passing through the cemetery or cloister. Pray for the dead.

When you are passing through the cemetery, cloister, or another burial-place of the dead, do not forget your brothers and all the faithful departed; but, moved by charity, have compassion for them, and be eager to assist them before the Lord in this or a similar manner.

Prayer.

Depart, faithful souls; rest in holy peace. May he who redeemed you by his precious blood deign to free you from punishments and lead you to eternal joys, where, blessed, you may rejoice and exult forever.

Another.

May God the Father, who created you, bless you. May Jesus Christ, who redeemed you by his most precious blood, bless you. May the Holy Spirit, who by his kindness and gift preserved you from eternal damnation, bless you. Amen.

Read the following prayer often.

I beseech you, sweetest Lord Jesus, Son of the living God, through the pain of your wounds, through the shedding of your most holy blood, and through the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on the souls of all the faithful departed existing in the pains of purgatory, and give them the dwelling-place of eternal light with all your saints. Amen.
