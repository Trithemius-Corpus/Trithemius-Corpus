After Vespers until Compline. Consideration VII.

After Vespers, or the Vigils of the Dead, return to your cell with the customary prayers and bows, and there, on bent knees, give thanks to your most merciful preserver, because he has not only mercifully rescued you from the vanities of the world, but has also guarded you in the order from many evils and dangers. Then recall how many bodily and spiritual goods your kindly Father has shown to you, though you deserved nothing; and, what is greatest, how he has promised himself as a reward to you if you faithfully persevere in his love. Be grateful, and always watchful in prayer, so that you may burn sweetly in the love of God.

When the signal has been given for supper, prepare yourself humbly to go to it, and observe what we noted above concerning dinner. After supper you may return to your cell and devote yourself to prayer or meditation until the signal is rung for collation.

At collation.

After supper, when you have heard the signal for spiritual collation, hasten to it with gravity, and hear the last reading of the day with fervor of spirit. For the word of holy exhortation that has been heard last will cling more tenaciously to the mind throughout the whole night. But if it is a fast day, collation is customarily observed in the refectory while drinking. Therefore be attentive to sacred reading in every place, because when one willingly receives the word of holy exhortation with attention, it cannot happen otherwise than that the mind is often lifted up.

At Compline.

Going to Compline, pray to God that he may always make you despise all worldly and carnal things, and grant you to remain continually in his most holy love, so that, when the course of the present life is completed, you may deserve to enter the temple of God not made by hands, and to rejoice forever with all the saints in God. But at the end of Compline, when you hear the signal for the angelic salutation being rung,

After Compline.

When Compline is finished, return at once to your cell, and, after the customary prayers have first been said, take care to prepare yourself most diligently for the following recollection of mind.

On recollection after Compline. Consideration IX.

Since we cannot remain wholly unmoved in one holy state of mind, it is necessary for us often to renew our holy purpose, so that as often as during the day, wandering through the byways of sins, we depart from the one principle of our soul, the supreme God, so often through contrition and compunction we may return to that firmly resolved amendment. Hence our holy fathers established for us that we should devote ourselves to recollection after Compline and examine ourselves concerning the negligences committed during the day, so that, by the continual exercise of recollection, we may renew toward fervor of spirit the purpose of holiness which we know to be sufficiently weak and infirm. Therefore our devout cenobite, wishing to recollect himself fruitfully and with useful continuance, must examine himself chiefly in five things in order.

First, in recollection.

Rendering an account.

The rendering of an account must be observed, by which the cenobite is ordered to bring back before the eyes of his mind, for chastisement, whatever he remembers having committed during the day by failing negligently, badly, or less well. Therefore, turned toward God, with hands clasped, let him lie before his bed and speak in this or a similar manner:

I give you thanks, almighty and merciful God, for all your benefits, which you have deigned to show to me, an unworthy sinner, deserving absolutely nothing, from my youth up to this hour.

For I, who have deserved nothing good, have received many and innumerable gifts of your goodness. Would that I could give thanks worthily to you, the highest and unchangeable good; would that in your holy service I could praise you with a pure heart and clean conscience. But alas, with weakness, concupiscence, and evil habit prevailing, I, who ought to have clung to your most holy love with a pure heart and conscience, have offended you again and again with many negligences and faults; and as often as, according to the inward inspirations of your goodness, I have tried to rise to fervor of spirit, alas, so often I grieve that I have fallen again.

You know, good Jesus, into how many faults, how many negligences, how many guilty acts I have fallen today alone; how many vain, how many useless, how many harmful thoughts I have admitted in so few hours; how many useless and harmful words I have uttered; how many negligences and offenses I have perpetrated in my actions today.

Woe to me, wretched man, who could not persevere even for one very short day in your most holy service without impurity of heart and negligence, who today have committed so many faults and various imperfections. To your goodness, O most gentle God, I confess all my sins into which I have fallen from my earliest age; before your feet, O sweetest Jesus, I place all the negligences that I have committed today. I ask pardon, which I am not worthy to receive; I ask for grace, which I confess I have so often received in vain. O most loving mediator between God and men, Jesus Christ, do not despise me, a wretched sinner, whom you willed to purchase with your own blood. I confess to you all the negligences into which I have fallen in any way today, and the faults that I have committed.

Here the cenobite, prostrate in mind before the Lord, should recall to memory the more serious negligences, and, humbly asking pardon, lament his imperfection. Especially let him consider whether he has offended anyone by word or deed; whether he has said anything against a brother; whether he has committed anything against charity and reverence; or whether he has knowingly saddened anyone unjustly.
