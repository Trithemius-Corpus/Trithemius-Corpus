On the meditation of the aid of our restoration in the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Today: ir.

The second root of the fear and love of God is placed in meditation on our redemption, which is generated through diligent exercise in the life, conduct, teaching, and passion of our Lord Jesus Christ. Among all meditations we believe this one to be more useful to us, since we know that our salvation and the sum of eternal happiness are contained in it. And note that the Lord’s passion, with respect to its proceeding, was unjust; with respect to its manner, shameful; with respect to its pain, bitter; and with respect to its effect, fruitful. Therefore it must always be held in memory.

Note 1.

First, with respect to its proceeding it was unjust, because, born of a virgin, he neither contracted sin nor committed it, nor was deceit found in his mouth. Death is inflicted on us for sin, but he could not die justly, because without any sin he was always righteous and innocent. Finally, he was killed contrary to human law, if a sentence of death is brought against a person neither confessed nor convicted of a crime. For he was accused before Pilate the governor on three counts: that, calling himself king, he forbade tribute to Caesar; third, that he made himself the Son of God. On the first two the Gospel speaks, and the accusation is shown to be false. For concerning the first, the contrary was plainly established, since Christ said that his kingdom was not earthly. Nor did the falsity of the second escape Pilate, because he knew that Jesus had commanded that the things that were Caesar’s should be given to Caesar. Asked by the governor about the third, he was silent, because Pilate was unworthy to hear of his divine generation

or even of his human birth. His passion was also contrary to custom, because it accepted no delay of time. He is not put in prison according to custom; nothing is deliberated in his case. Instead, once captured, he is immediately handed over to the judge to be subjected to death; scourged, spat upon, and afflicted with many injuries, wounds, and mockeries, he is nailed to the cross. Third, it was contrary to the law of nature and brought about from the obstinate hatred of the heart. For nature forbids raging against a dead man and shudders at tearing a hidden body with new wounds. For our Redeemer, dead on the cross, is pierced with a lance, and, contrary to the law of nature, once deceased he is wounded by the impious.

Second, the passion of Christ is said to have been shameful with respect to its manner, for the six causes that follow. First, because of the paltry price, since blood of incomparable worth is sold by a disciple for thirty silver coins, for which no one would deign to sell his donkey. Second, because of the shameful service, because, full of wounds and half-dead, he is compelled to carry his cross, which is found to have been done to no robber. Third, because of the unworthy place, since he is crucified outside the city in the place of Calvary, where robbers and wicked murderers are punished for their deserts. Fourth, because of the shameful punishment, since he is suspended naked on the cross, a penalty owed only to robbers and criminal men. Indeed, as Scripture says, the Redeemer of the world was condemned by a most shameful death. Fifth, because of the shameful mockery, since he is suspended naked on the cross before the whole multitude and is blasphemed by everyone as though foolish and insane. Concerning this the psalmist says: “All day long my shame is before me.” Sixth, because of the shameful company, since, counted among criminals, he is suspended in the midst of robbers. What are you doing now, O Christian soul? Behold, your Savior hangs in the middle between robbers, and you have not feared to sit first in council.
