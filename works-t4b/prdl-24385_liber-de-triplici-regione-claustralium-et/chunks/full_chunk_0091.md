After None.

After None, be mindful of sweetest Jesus dying on the cross; and, going forward in spirit to his knees with tears, give thanks with as much devotion as you can and pray.

Prayer.

O Lord Jesus Christ, King of heaven and earth, I behold you dying on the cross for me, a wretch, and I humbly give you thanks for your holy Passion and most innocent death. O most loving Savior, what shall I render to you for all that you have rendered to me? Behold, I offer myself to you; I long to dwell continually in your most holy wounds. I ask you, by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on me, as you know how; pierce my flesh with fear of you, and suppress in me all disordered motions, so that, inflamed with the fervor of your most holy love, I may love you with a pure heart, confess you with a praiseworthy mouth, and follow you, according to my strength, by humble conduct. I beseech you, most merciful Lord Jesus, by that anguish of your most holy heart which you endured on the cross when your most holy soul went out from your body: have mercy on my soul at its departure.

The first hour after dinner.

At the first hour after dinner, unless you are occupied with higher exercises, you shall pray to the Lord in this or a similar way, to obtain knowledge of your own frailty.

Prayer.

--- Page 196 ---

Lord God, who, out of your great goodness, created me in my first parent from the slime of the earth, so that, mindful of my lowly condition, I might glorify you, my Creator, in true humility: grant me, I beseech you, truly to know my frailty at all times, so that, while I consider how vile I am and how often I am nothing, and what I rightly deserve, I may be worthy to please you by your gift. Through Christ.

The second hour.

At the second hour after midday, let our devout cenobite recall distinctly to memory the gravity of his sins, by which, especially while living in holy religion, he has sinned against the Rule; and, turned toward the Lord, let him pray thus.

Prayer.

Almighty, eternal God, who have deigned, by the Incarnation and death of your only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, to give life again, from your mercy alone, to us wretches who were dead in sins: grant me, by the power of his most innocent Passion, forgiveness of all my sins, so that henceforth I may be worthy to serve you with a pure conscience. Through the same our Lord.

The third hour.

At the third hour after dinner, let our faithful cenobite remember his vows, which he promised to the Lord, and especially the renunciation of all ownership, namely in possessions, judgments, and will; and, moved to compunction, let him pray thus to the Lord in fervor of spirit.

Prayer.

Almighty and merciful God, who sanctify and receive with gracious favor the vows of your servants, offered to you from faithful devotion: grant that I may render to you all that I have promised, through the virtue of humility, chastity, obedience, and poverty,

with such purity of mind that I may be worthy to enter, by your mercy, into your kingdom promised to the poor in spirit. Through our Lord.

After Vespers.

After Vespers, let our good cenobite consider that all visible things are vain; and, with his mind lifted up to the love of invisible goods, let him cry to the Lord with as much devotion as he can.

Prayer.

Almighty, eternal God, who willed to lead us, through the visible things which you created, to the knowledge and love of invisible things: grant me so to know the vanity of present things that I may be worthy to think with a pure mind on the eternity of future things.

Another.

O protector of those who hope in you, God, without whom nothing is strong, nothing holy: multiply your mercy upon us, so that, with you as our ruler and guide, we may pass through temporal goods in such a way that we do not lose eternal ones.

Another prayer.

God, who have prepared invisible goods for those who love you: pour into our hearts the affection of your love, so that, loving you in all things and above all things, we may obtain your promises, which surpass every desire. Through our Lord.

After supper and collation.

After supper or collation, let the faithful cenobite put on a desire for good zeal, and especially for souls, with whatever fervor of charity he can; and, praying to the Lord, let him say.

Prayer.

Lord God, greatest lover of faithful souls, who for the salvation of human beings willed your Son to die on the gibbet of the cross: grant me, through the example of that same Son of yours, always to make progress in good zeal, and to fear no labors, no adversities, for the salvation of souls; so that, strengthened by the fire of your love, I may dare to resist, at the proper time, those who oppose you.
