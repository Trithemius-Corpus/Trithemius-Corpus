The first of them, namely the intellect, is raised up to know God, but it is not sufficient to come to the mountain of divine darkness, because it proceeds weighed down by phantasms of sensible things. Therefore affection, the simpler eye of the soul, enters the dark mountain without any images, contemplates God through love, and, separated from all creatures, thinks nothing sensible; resting in mystical silence, it is free and bare. This is the simple eye that makes the whole body luminous; it is the one of which the bridegroom boasts in the Canticles: “You have wounded my heart, my sister, my bride; you have wounded my heart with one of your eyes.” For affection, through the fervor of love, enters alone, without the guidance of the intellect, into the secret of divine contemplation; and therefore the bridegroom is wounded in one eye and not in both.

But whoever, through the exercise of devotion and purity, has deserved, by Christ’s bounty, to reach this region of union and simplification, let him learn always to remain prudently in profound humility, lest through pride he lose the gift that he rejoices to have obtained. For Bernard, that profound and most cautious contemplator of divine things, says: “One striving toward higher things must be deepened in humility and think humbly of himself, lest, while he is vainly lifted above himself, he fall from the height to which he seemed to have ascended.” Therefore, while he remains in this region, let him prudently return at times to the steps of humility described in the second region; let him humbly learn to remain in profound resignation of himself, because as soon as the mind ceases from self-denial, it falls from the highest mountain into an immense precipice.

For many, trusting in the jubilation of their sensual devotion, have been deceived, who climbed this highest mountain without the foundation of humility. Others, with their heads injured and their senses destroyed, have become fools, because, before their passions had yet been mortified, they presumed to enter the darkness of mystical silence. Others, lifted up in pride while they were still devoted to sensuality, descended through the vice of their own will into the depth of hell. For Scripture says concerning the ascent of this

summit: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” A beast is said to touch the mountain when a person, not yet purified through the exercise of the second region, attempts to ascend to the third. For as long as affection has not been purged from the passions of its sensuality, the beast still lives in it. But if the human mind, with its bestiality, presumes to approach the mountain of God, it will be overwhelmed by as many stones as it is wounded by movements of the passions. For the wise man says: “The sluggard is stoned with a muddy stone, and all will speak in contempt of him.” By the muddy stone carnal affection is expressed, because man is compared to mud. For it is written in Job: “Remember, I ask, that you have made me like clay, and you will reduce me to dust.” And again he says: “I have been compared to mud, and made like embers and ashes.” Thus the sluggard is overwhelmed by a muddy stone when the mind, still unclean with carnal and sensual passions, attempts to ascend to the mountain of contemplation; there, as unworthy, it is forced to endure repulse and ruin from the hard stones set against it. For no one will be able to reach this most tranquil region of the spirit who has not first cleansed himself through long exercise in the furnace of the middle region. Whoever, while still devoted to carnal passions, tries to approach this supreme region of the spirit is striving, as it were, to draw water in a sieve. Indeed, this region does not admit carnal minds into itself, for it is prepared for hearts purified from every affection.

That this supreme region of the spirit is magnificent in its reduction.
