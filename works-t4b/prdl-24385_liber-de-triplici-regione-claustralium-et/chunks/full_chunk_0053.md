For those who inhabit the empyrean heaven and those who dwell in the third region have one and the same object. But this is the difference: these contemplate God in an enigma, while those do so in clear vision. The sweetness of contemplation is marvelous in both places: here it is great, there greatest; here it is in the desire of fervent love, there in the possession of the most blessed vision.

In this highest region of divine contemplation the rational spirit calls its powers and capacities back into its own chamber. There, free from every phantasm, it is at leisure; it raises itself above itself into the density of the divine darkness, where, lulled by ecstatic love, it rests sweetly. No troublesome confusion of thoughts is active here, no imagining of phantasms, but the naked mind is raised upward and contemplates without any thought or imagination. Indeed, when the mind itself turns back to thoughts, it shows that it has again descended to the middle region, which belongs to reason. But since the human mind cannot always remain unmoved at the summit of divine contemplation, it must often return to the rational exercise of the second region. If, however, it has fallen into sensual passions, then it has again descended to the lowest region.

Yet the soul of the true lover of God, although because of the frailty of the flesh it cannot always remain in the pleasantness of contemplation, nevertheless strives often to raise itself to it through fiery affection, so that it may fulfill the exercise of reason in such a way as not wholly to lose the sweetness of inward delight. O how good it is to cling to the Lord; how sweet it is to attend to God alone, so that the mind may thirst for the Lord Jesus before all things and always long to embrace him with burning desires. Indeed, while the mind stands in this region it is very near the empyrean heaven, and therefore is inflamed toward it with the greatest desires of love. O how many groans and tears the mind, fervent with love, is accustomed to pour out; how ardently it sighs for heavenly things, because the nearer it is to the empyrean heaven while standing through the secret of contemplation in this highest region, the more ardent its affection is known to be for leaving the prison of the body. That this region of the soul is divine by instruction.
Article Three.

Third, this highest region of the soul is called divine by instruction, because in it the mind, fervent with divine love, is taught not by man but by the Holy Spirit. For Christ said to his lovers in the sacred Gospel: “When that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth.” And again there is that saying of the prophet: “And they shall all be taught by God.” For the Holy Spirit inhabits the heart of one who loves Jesus and illuminates it by his invisible teaching.

In this region the spirit reads true wisdom, Mary’s best part, in silent tranquility; mystical theology teaches in the secret of the divine darkness with ineffable sweetness; the mind, disposed by the fervor of most holy love, speaks with its beloved in secret and is continually raised toward him by desire. This wisdom, or mystical theology, can indeed be commended by a man, but it cannot be taught except by divine instruction. For the work of the Holy Spirit is being done when the mind is illuminated in true contemplation.

But we must consider that mystical theology is acquired through the exercise of purified affection and is taught through the fire of love. Speculative theology, however, which we call scholastic, is learned through the exercise of the intellect, while the mind binds itself to continual study. For the acquisition of mystical theology, purification of the affection from every entangling thing is required, so that the will, depraved by aversion, may be reformed toward its principle through the conversion of inward love. Indeed, unless the affection has been purged of all disordered desire, it does not ascend to the sublimity of this most sacred wisdom.
