On Regular Refection.

When the signal for refection is sounded, have heartfelt compassion for the sick, the needy, and the poor who suffer necessity; and pray for them, since you can do nothing else. Consider that among Christians there are many far better than you, whom harsh poverty oppresses in this life; to them, in their need for the things necessary for sustenance, poor fare is offered, and not even the thinnest garments are supplied to clothe the body. But you, although you are a monk and a despiser of the world, are clothed in excellent garments and sit at table like a prince. Brothers better than you set food before you to satiety; all things necessary, even to the point of disgust, are ministered to you, an unworthy sinner, by the servants of God. Therefore consider what end this abundance of things will have in a monk, since by entering religion you professed poverty, but now in religion you feast more splendidly than the rich of the world. Take care, then, not to be ungrateful for such great benefits of God, and live according to the rule you have professed. Pray for those who contribute temporal substance to your monastery, that almighty God may mercifully grant them eternal life.

On Mental Reading.

When sitting at table, be composed while eating. Do not look this way and that, but take care to eat, with humility and thanksgiving, only the food set before you for the necessity of nature. Carefully consider what is read at table, so that just as the body is refreshed by its own food, so the soul may not be deprived of its own refreshment. Learn, while eating, to raise your mind frequently to heavenly things, and through the word of sacred reading,

which is never lacking while we eat, to kindle it sweetly with divine compunction. For the true and inward worshiper of God, whenever he hears something read, even during meals, about the bitterness of the Lord’s passion, the fervor of divine love, or the kingdom of the heavenly fatherland, immediately groans from great desire, kindles his affections in the ardor of love, and, compelled by excessive sweetness, weeps. For truly the mind which genuinely loves Jesus, as soon as it hears his name or sees the image of the Crucified, is dissolved in compunction and by affection is lifted to his feet or his embrace.

At the Thanksgiving.

When the refection is finished, give thanks to the Lord; and while you go to church under the psalm, direct your mind upward and intercede for benefactors living and dead. If after the thanksgivings you add some special prayer for the departed, you do well.
