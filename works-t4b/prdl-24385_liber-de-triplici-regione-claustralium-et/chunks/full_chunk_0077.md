At the Sanctus.

With your mind lifted upward, when the Sanctus is sung, glorify the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God and Lord, and humbly adore his majesty at all times with the holy angels.

During the Canon.

After the Sanctus, during the Canon, you may take up meditation on the Lord’s passion, and offer it to God the Father, through its several greater principal articles, for yourself, or for others, or for any necessity. Say, therefore, in this or a similar manner:

O holy Father, I offer you the bloody sweat and tears of our Lord Jesus Christ, your most beloved Son, in that charity and union in which he voluntarily offered himself to you in death for us: for my sins, or for that brother, or for this or that necessity. And I ask you, through the pain of the wounds of that same sweetest Son of yours, and through the shedding of his most holy blood and the power of his most innocent death: have mercy on me, or on him, or grant us this or that, etc. Through the same our Lord.

You may do the same with the other articles of the Lord’s passion, and expand them in offering according to the intention of your affection.

At the Elevation.

If, at the elevation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your mind is not occupied with higher things, you may pray to the Lord, who is truly present, in this way:

O Christ, king of glory. You are the eternal Son of the Father. You, to deliver us, etc., to the end. For it is read that Saint Thomas Aquinas prayed this part of the hymn to the Lord during the elevation.

At the Agnus Dei.

When the Agnus Dei is sung, lift up your mind to the Lord Jesus with singular confidence, and humbly pray that he may receive you into perpetual peace. Direct your affection to him for whose love you have left all things, and ask of him for a holy embrace and a stable possession through reception of holy communion.

At the Ite missa est.

When at the end the Ite missa est is sung, see that your mind is lifted up with the holy angels returning into heaven, so that through desire you may deserve to behold the Lord Jesus presiding in majesty, whom now on earth, in the sacrament of the Mass, you can no longer see going to the Father. Therefore kindle your desire through the image of desolation, in the manner of those who are unwillingly separated from their friends.

Say, therefore, in your heart:

Meditation.

Behold, my Lord has returned to heaven; he has left me desolate on earth. What am I doing here with mortals, when I have an immortal one? Why do I not follow him? Woe to me, for my sojourning has been prolonged; I have dwelt with the inhabitants of darkness; my soul has been long in exile. I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ; but who will free me, unhappy man that I am, from the body of this death? O most loving Lord Jesus, my hope, O God and savior of my soul, draw me after you; let us run in the fragrance of your ointments. O my sweet love, do not forget me to the end, but deign frequently to lift up my mind to you, so that, since I cannot see you bodily while set in this mortality, at least, lifted up by affection and desire in your most holy love, I may more often deserve to behold you in spirit in glory. O my salvation, O heart of my heart, most loving Jesus, do not abandon me or despise me; be a gracious helper to me, and direct me always in your most holy will, so that I may truly be able to please you.
