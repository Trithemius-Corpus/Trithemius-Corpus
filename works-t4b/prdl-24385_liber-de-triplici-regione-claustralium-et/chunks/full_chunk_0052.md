Thus, just as the lowest region of the flesh sends its citizens, when they die, to hell, and the middle region of reason sends them to purgatory, so this region sends its perfect worshipers, when they depart, to heaven. The dignity of this third and supreme region is set forth in many ways and is especially commended in seven articles, as will be said in order.

That this supreme region of the spirit of cloistered persons is pleasing by comparison.

Article 1.

First, then, this third region of the soul is called pleasing by comparison, because it is compared to the highest empyrean heaven. For just as in the empyrean heaven all things are tranquil, so on this summit of the supreme region the mind of the person contemplating God is disturbed by no agitations. Here is the highest rest of the contemplating soul; here no crowd of vices dwells; no bitterness of mind at all draws near.

In the empyrean heaven, indeed, the majesty of God is seen by the holy spirits through an ineffable gift; here, in darkness, through the longing of love, it is seen above understanding. There God is all in all; here nothing among all things is pleasing except God. There it is granted to the saints to rest from their labors without end; here the mind is permitted, while resting, to taste beforehand the unspeakable sweetness of inward delight.

There is no greater blessedness than in heaven, and there is no tranquility in the world so sweet as in this region. Blessed indeed are those who have deserved to ascend this mountain of divine contemplation, who love nothing at all in this world and desire nothing other than always to cling to God. O how secure is the conscience of these citizens, how joyful their way of life, where no evil is committed and nothing else is desired except that highest, unchangeable good.

The mind that has truly and perseveringly ascended this region has been purified through divine love: it desires nothing carnal, nothing disordered; it raises itself wholly in contemplation of the highest majesty through longing; it rests sweetly in God alone. of one who truly loves God, when the mind recalls all the things and powers of the soul from the dispersion of multiplicity into one, and, raised upward by fiery affections, lifts them into the inner chamber of the spirit? Then it directs its gaze through love toward that highest, unchangeable good; it wholly casts away from itself every created and creatable multitude; the soul gathers itself into one, so that, united within itself, it may be able to rise without the hindrance of multiplicity to its own principle, which is one. To the one principle of all things, indeed, unity alone can attain, because unity flees multiplicity and seeks what is one. For the more you yield to multiplicity, the more you withdraw from unity.

Therefore the mind of a person dwelling in this supreme region of the spirit, when it seeks to raise itself in love toward the contemplation of its own principle, is accustomed to set aside every multiplicity clinging to it in imagination or understanding, so that, gathered in its own unity, it may be able to turn ardently, with a simple affection of love, toward him whom it knows to be above every position.

That this supreme region of the spirit is sweet and lovable by nearness.

Article 2.

Secondly, this supreme region of the spirit is most sweet and pleasant in likeness and by singular nearness. For the citizen of this most holy region is separated from the citizens above by only one earthen wall, so to speak, because as long as the spirit inhabits the flesh, it cries out to heaven as if through a wall. The earthen wall of the flesh, however, is the frailty of the body, from which the spirit, once freed, will go out to migrate to heaven when it is separated from the body by death.

But a spirit wounded by sins, which cannot raise itself to this region through the pure affection of love, is still said to stand farther away from the empyrean heaven. Those, however, who dwell in this most quiet region are very near the empyrean heaven, because, weighed down by no stains of sins, they despise all earthly and carnal things and always desire to live in mind and spirit among heavenly things.
