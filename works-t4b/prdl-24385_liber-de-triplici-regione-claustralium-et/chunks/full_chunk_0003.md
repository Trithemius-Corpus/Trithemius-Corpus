And this lowest region is compared to the airy heaven, because just as hail, snow, lightning, and various storms are generated in the air, so also in the minds of carnal men inhabiting this dark region various storms of vices follow. Woe to carnal monks who inhabit this dark region, in which the movement of crimes is frequent and the storm of pleasures continual.

These are monks given over to animal passions, who subject reason to sensuality, who live according to the flesh in bestial desires. Now the works of the flesh in one dwelling in this lowest region are manifest: fornication, uncleanness, shamelessness, luxury, service of idols, sorceries, enmities, rivalries, wrath, quarrels, dissensions, sects, envies, murders, drunkenness, feasting, and things like these. Concerning them the holy apostle proclaims that those who do such things will not obtain the kingdom of God.

These are the marks of the works of the lowest region; these are the deadly storms of the minds of carnal monks, who live irreligiously in religion, who despise the traditions of the holy fathers, who contemptuously scorn the precepts handed down by elders. These are the unclean inhabitants of the lowest region, given over to vices and carnal desires, who do not care to have God in their knowledge, who serve the pleasures of the flesh, who stubbornly break the vow of holy religion which they have professed, who, scorning the salvation of their souls, despise following when the grace of God calls.

Therefore he gave them over to a reprobate sense and to false judgment, so that they judge evil to be good, and falsely think that the things which in truth are worst are the best. These are those about whom the holy apostle prophesied: carnal monks, whisperers, detractors, hateful to God, insulting, proud, puffed up, inventors of evils, disobedient to superiors, foolish, disorderly, without affection, without covenant, without mercy, filled with all iniquity, malice, wickedness, contention, deceit, and malignity. Although they know the justice of God, they do not wish to understand that those who do such things are worthy of perpetual death; and not only those who do them, but also those who consent to those who do them, and those who, by reason of their office, in no way oppose evil pursuits.

For indeed the ruler of souls who does not correct his erring subjects entangles himself in damnation for the crimes of others; by silence he becomes the accomplice of those whose faults damnation will follow. For whoever, placed as pastor of souls, neglects to correct sinners according to canonical rule, even if he is free from his own fault, will nevertheless be condemned before the strict judge for another’s guilt.

For the supreme shepherd says to the prophet: “Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel; and you shall hear the word from my mouth, and shall announce it to them from me. If, when I say to the wicked man, ‘You shall surely die,’ you do not announce it to him, nor speak so that he may turn from his wicked way and live, that wicked man shall indeed die in his iniquity; but I will require his blood from your hand.” And our blessed father says in the Rule: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd will fall upon him if the father of the household has been able to find any lack of profit in his sheep.”

O sentence to be feared by all spiritual pastors, by which not only the depraved conduct of subjects is ascribed to the ruler, but he is held guilty before the inner judge even for their lesser progress. O what shall we wretched pastors say in that strict examination, we who neglect to correct according to the rule the monks committed to us when they err in many ways; who do not call those lying in the lowest region to higher things, but, carnal with carnal men, grow sluggish in the shadow of death through pernicious idleness?

Woe to all who remain in this dark region; for unless they do penance, they must be assigned with those who inhabit the outer darkness, where no order dwells, but everlasting horror. Therefore, just as monks who walk according to the rule in the spirit are to be happily crowned without end in heaven with the holy angels, so those who persevere until death in this lowest region of the flesh, contrary to the norm of monastic institution, worse than pagans, are to be eternally tormented in hell with demons.

For that blessed man truly says: “Just as I have never seen any better than those who have made progress in the monastery, so I have never found any worse than those who have failed in the monastery.” For the monastic profession requires the perfection of the spiritual life and tends, as to its proper end, toward the summit of the virtues.
