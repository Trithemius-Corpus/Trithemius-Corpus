From the book On Ecclesiastical Writers by Johannes of Trittenheim, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict.

Hucbald, a monk of Elnon, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, a Gaul by nation, a man most learned both in divine Scripture and in secular letters, in music, poetry, philosophy, oratory, and the other humane arts second to no one in his time, as the many little works elegantly written by him attest. Of these I have thus far found only the following: to Charles the Bald, emperor, a marvelous work of one hundred and thirty verses, all of whose words begin with the one letter C, as in: Carmina clarisone, calvis cantate Camene. He entitled it Eclogue on the Praise of Bald Men, one book. On Music, one book. The Deeds of Saint Lebuin, one book. Letters to Various Persons, one book. He also described the lives of many saints in ornate eloquence, and composed chants in their honor in sweet and regular melody. He flourished in the time of the emperor Charles the Bald, in the year of the Lord 880.

Here begins the eclogue of lord Hucbald, monk of Elnon, of the Order of Saint Benedict, addressed to Charles the Bald, emperor.

Stricken by abuse, he who tried to censure bald men sang songs; let bald men be celebrated in song. All of you, recognize the bright song in clear sight.

Preface, in which the Muses are invited to the praise of bald men.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses; I shall try to commemorate bald men in fitting song. Against the curl-haired, let the hairs of the neck confound them. Let the skilled Muses celebrate bright songs together. Let them praise bald men together; let them praise hard things under the charge of those trying to attack bald men with curling laughter. Let the summit of the spear rise for the cause of bald men. Let all the long-haired, with matted hair, be silent. Let harmonious songs together sing to bald men, terrifying with a song all those who are going bald.

That baldness seems to come to each person as a certain foretelling of things to come. Chapter 1.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses, when the crown of the head grows at the nape. It marks the companion of the clergy as a bald man, destined to receive the bright crown, when Christ grants it. As though plucking hairs from the head, he plucks sins from the heart. In heart he looks upon the Creator, in body upon heaven. Desiring to reach the heavenly citizens by worship, he condemns all sins together with fleeting cares. Desiring eagerly the ascent to heaven, the harmony of the heavenly ones, he praises the Creator of all things.

That bald men are singers, abbots, doctors, and also bishops and priests. Chapter 2.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. Bald men, tuneful, rejoice together with a lofty cry, and they continue the choirs, singing chaste dances. All conformed to the head, with concordant body, they strive to establish the most pleasing folds of the clergy. Taking up the horn, they sound the heavenly trumpets. They make the dear chrism in the name of Christ. They join in one band the assemblies sprinkled with chrism. In concord, carefully, they celebrate together the banquets of the supper. They satisfy Christ’s guests with flesh and blood.

That bald men are kings and emperors, and also consuls, legislators, and judges. Chapter 3.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. Bald men reign together; illustrious, they mount the heights, conspicuous, adorned with a golden crown upon the neck. Merciful in judgment, they establish the counsels of clients. Beware, O long-haired one: bald men condemn crimes. It is clear that a bald censor establishes judgment. Believe that the biting bald man, prudent, is to be avoided. The blind man judges that the balding man is blind in body. Cease, blinded man, to censure the baldness of the bald man. Cease to gnaw at the bald-headed man; cease, cease.

That bald men are leaders of an army, and themselves also learned and strong warriors. Chapter 4.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. Bald men lead companies renowned in battle. Bald men fight together, crested with the cone of the helmet. They hurl, they brandish; swift, with slaughter, they strike the troops. They seize chariots; they trample the long-haired under heel. With the spear they pierce them; with the flashing hilt they cut them to pieces. Necks fall; bald men celebrate the sailors’ cry with a shout. The bald man, shrewd when stirred to fight in battle, tramples, strikes down, and compels the long-haired to yield. He takes captives; he binds the captured by the neck.

The praise of bald men from the experience of the art of medicine, both pharmacological and surgical. Chapter 5.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. You will find that bald men bring support to the brain. You will find that bald men cure catarrhs of the head. You will find that bald men cure blind cavities. A chronic disease with cancer, and cachexy too, yields to the bald man. Heart-sickness, gnawing the heart, is abolished; colic ceases. Drawing together the iron, he holds it fast in the cut flesh. He treats the corrupted blood of the head when the neck has been heated. Why do I sing of many more things? In a failing body he refutes all hidden ailments, and cures bodies that have collapsed.

An undaunted invective against the reviler who abuses bald men. Chapter 6.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. In your heart, O curly-haired man, beware of contending with the Creator, and cease to direct abuse against the creature. The all-powerful Creator of causes established all created things, taking care and forethought to bestow care on what he had created. He bent the bristle so that the bright crown of the bald man might rise, granting him a summit to which he compelled all things to yield. Terrified, why does he bark with a doglike cry? Let the fallen gourd consider the bald man in the filth. Let the filthy slander of that man be silent at once.

Likewise against the same man, and praise of bald men for humility, charity, and chastity. Chapter 7.

Sing songs in clear tones for bald men, Muses. Why do you think the fellow of butchers tears the skin from the bald man? The bald man tears out the sins of the heart. Why do you think bald men sprinkle ashes on the head? The bald man knows that the body grows together from ash. Why do you think the flesh is burned with hot charcoal? Bald men burn their hearts, thoroughly kindled by the heat of Christ.
