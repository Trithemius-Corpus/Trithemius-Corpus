Why do you think that the bald man, chaste in body, is castrated?
In his heart he is quickly castrated from all faults.
Restrain your butchering mockery against the bald man in song.

The reproach of his flesh, and an example from the prophet Elisha and the boys who mocked him. Chapter VIII.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
In clear songs I take care not to join blind things together.
Let the heights that must be trodden down be trodden down with the heel.
You filthy one, singing filthy things, close your reed-pipe.
Certainly it is judged, kept safe in the book of judgment,
that the clearer-sighted bald man will have conscience as his attendant.
When the little dog was stirred up and barking around him with laughter,
they cried: “Go up quickly; go up, bald man.”
It is clear that he at once condemned foul crimes,
and bodies, gnawed by abuse, fall captive together.

On the excellent bald man Paul the Apostle, that he was called by Christ and carried up into the third heaven. Chapter IX.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
Let songs for the bald be heaped up with the bald man’s song,
that conspicuous man whose cruel caution in slaughter
had stood ready to butcher the worshippers of Christ.
Let all rejoice together over the bald man shining round with charismatic radiance,
when, blinded, he was summoned by the cry of Christ.
He quickly fell, pierced as if by the point of a lance,
and at once undertook ascents to the summit of heaven,
beholding the Almighty with clearer sight.
The heart whose heavenly chastity shines was made his companion.

That from a persecutor he became a preacher, and that he forbids nourishing hair and uttering shameful speech. Chapter X.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
Gather choirs together; celebrate dances for the bald man.
We have found that the famous bald censor should be joined and revered,
the consul of heaven, consulted for counsel about taking care.
He counsels that all filthy crimes be restrained.

He judges that common benefits should be shared with all.
He confounds chariots; he founds civic assemblies
by reconciling the fellow-spouses of Christ-worshippers.
He stirs hearts, striving to join the body to the head.
Let the frightened man cease to despise famous bald men.

The poet addresses the Muses concerning the mocker, indicating that he was blinded by the judgment of the bald king. Chapter XI.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
Restrain the cruel man, caught in crime,
who strives with vain effort to tear down all bald men.
Shut the swiftly convicted man in blind enclosures,
convicted by abusive fictions, the deranged man’s perishable inventions.
The baldish censor judges that the man caught in crime should be blinded.
Blind dog, cease to despise the bald man.
Bow down, dog, fabricating crimes against the bald.
Bow down, dog, barking against bald men in song.
Bow down, dog; cease gnawing at bald men.

An epitome of the praise of bald men from the disposition and beauty of the body, and because the bald man is a microcosm. Chapter XII.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
When you see the bright assembly, the gathered company of the bald,
understand the bald man as the circle of heaven;
know the summit of baldness as the center of heaven.
The other parts of the bald man commend the circuit of the cosmos.
Associate bald men with bald men; gather shining bald men.
Cynthia will cease to bring golden colors;
darkening her horns, she will yield to growing together with bald men.
Bald men shine; the helmet of bald men will gleam,
glowing red like the bright abundance of heaven.

Conclusion of the poem.

Clear-sounding Muses, sing songs for the bald.
May he undertake to set bright reeds in enclosures.
The song fit to be sung for illustrious bald men is completed.

Here ends the poem of the monk Hucbald to Charles, on the praise of bald men.
