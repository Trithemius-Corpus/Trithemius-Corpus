The *Ecloga de Laude Calvorum ad Carolum* — "An Eclogue in Praise of Bald Men, to Charles" — is one of the legendary curiosities of medieval Latin literature: a 133-line poem in which **every single word begins with the letter C**. It is a lipogrammatic tour de force, an entire mock-encomium conducted under that one constraint — the bald-headed are like the sun, like the moon, like polished bronze, like a clear and unclouded sky — and the constraint is the joke as much as the praise. Composed in the Carolingian ninth century and addressed to the emperor Charles the Bald, it earned its author a small immortality outside his more serious musical and hagiographic writings.

**Hucbald of Saint-Amand** (c. 840–930) wrote the poem. The Mainz pamphlet first prints a notice from Trithemius’s *De Scriptoribus Ecclesiasticis* (also in this corpus, `prdl-70280`). The notice gives the length as 130 verses and quotes the opening line of the proem. The poem itself follows. The project’s survey of prior English translations remains inconclusive. The proem and the twelve numbered chapters have ten lines each. The separately headed three-line *Clausula carminis* brings the count from the proem through the close to 133. Three introductory verse lines precede the proem. [Opening, fol. 2r](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00039449_00005/full/full/0/default.jpg) · [Close, fol. 4r](https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/iiif/image/v2/bsb00039449_00009/full/full/0/default.jpg)

The Latin is metric (mostly hexameters with elegiac interludes) and word-play-dense; the English here flattens the metrical play to readable prose while keeping the chapter structure (Prooemium, then Cap. I–XII) and the prose argument-headings Hucbald gave each verse block. This is the **Mainz edition, ca. 1496** (BSB scan).
