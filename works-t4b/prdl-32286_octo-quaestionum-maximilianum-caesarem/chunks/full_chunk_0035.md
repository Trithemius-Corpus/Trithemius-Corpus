And from the witches’ own confession we have quite often heard that attendance at Masses and the use of things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons. Nor do they bless these things in the Church of God in vain, but so that they may be feared tokens, showing demons the defenses of divine protection. Concerning boys and infants, however, who, having been reborn in Christ, are sometimes killed by the sorceries of demons or of human beings, we do not doubt that divine providence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish by sorceries because of the sins of their parents.

On the Providence of God.

Question Eight.

This was the eighth question of your Serenity: whether it can be proved by natural reason, as it can by the holy Scriptures, that God has

--- Page 115 ---

care for human affairs and actions, and has a certain and infallible providence over all and each of the things that are done in the world.

I remember replying to this question as follows. The whole world, heaven and earth and all things that are in them, are subject to divine providence; by its will and command they were brought into being from the beginning. For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us briefly establish this opinion, first from the Scriptures and then by arguments, as far as is permitted.

In the book of Judith we read: “All your ways are peaceful, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the most wise king Solomon speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth so as to make your soul sin, nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest perhaps the Lord, angered by your words, destroy all the works of your hands.” The clearest testimonies of providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to his disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for an as, and not one of them falls upon the earth before God?” This is a great commendation of divine providence, which has care not only for human beings, but also for the smallest birds. Indeed, if the Almighty cares for such small things, who would not believe that he has care for greater

--- Page 116 ---

things? “Look,” he says, “at the birds of heaven, which neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them.” And a little afterward he adds, saying: “If God so clothes the grass of the field, which is today and tomorrow is cast into the oven, how much more you, O you of little faith?”

Almighty God, because he created all things and brought them into being when they did not exist, rules and governs the whole universe lest they slip and fall away. This could in no way happen if he did not have providence and care for all things. For if providence is removed, the ordered continuity of the whole universe must necessarily be thrown into confusion. The Creator’s providence cares for all things, since he who feeds the birds, gives beasts their food, fills every living creature with blessing, and clothes the grass of the field, adorning violets and lilies with beautiful splendor, cannot forget the human being whom he created from the beginning in his own image. He who governs all things cares for all things, and he has providence not only over human beings, but also over all beasts; all things are placed under his rule, and there is nothing that can resist his will in anything.
