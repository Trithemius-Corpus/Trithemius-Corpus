QUESTION TWO.

From what has been set forth there arises another question of no small difficulty, which human curiosity is accustomed to raise against those who think rightly about the faith of Christ: Why does almighty God, since he is just and merciful, not cease creating so many thousands of human beings whom he has most certainly foreknown are to be damned? Why does he not spare the wretched, and why would he bring forth to destruction, in such great multitude, those whom he foreknew would turn aside to evil? For it would seem better not to exist than to exist badly.

Let him answer this question, if anyone thinks himself God’s counselor. Who am I, that I should explain the cause of the divine will? For the Lord has done all things whatsoever he willed in heaven and on earth, and there is no one who can resist his will. Hence a certain wise man says to God: “Who will hold it against you, if the nations you made perish?” The Almighty established for things an order of succession, which he will never revoke by any regretful change. For just as he permits his sun to rise upon the good and the evil, so until the end of the age he allows human beings, pious and impious, to be conceived, born, and grow.

The thoughts of human beings are not in God, nor does he change his counsel because of time; but since he is unchangeable and everlasting, willing all things together and once for all, he has from eternity preordained all things invariably. Once

Therefore God permits, by a most just reason, that human beings should be born good and evil, pious and impious, faithful and faithless, sinners and saints, those to be saved and those to be damned: he who assigned this order to all things. Nothing perishes to God, who is Lord of all; and Christ Jesus, no less over evil than over good human beings and spirits, everywhere holds his empire strengthened: God from God the Father without time, and man from the Virgin in time, appointed mediator of God and human beings. He is the one through whom we have access to God; he is the savior of all the faithful. A man thinks wrongly of him if he rashly concedes that anyone can be saved without the Christian faith.

The omnipotence of God has regard to the order of things that he established, according to whose inviolable sequence of rectitude no one will be saved without faith in Christ; no one who does not believe in the Son of God is justified. Therefore they are moved by a vain and utterly foolish piety who think that anyone outside the faith of Christ, in the whole world and in any sect whatever, is to be saved, since his judgment is manifestly to the contrary. But Christ will undoubtedly save only those who believe in him in heart, speech, and deed. For he himself said in the Gospel: “Whoever is ashamed of me and of my words, of him I too will be ashamed before my Father and his holy angels.”
