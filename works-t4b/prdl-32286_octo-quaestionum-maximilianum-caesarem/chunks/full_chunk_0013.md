For the holy angels reveal to pure men who are fervent in the love of God secrets hidden from others, and by command of the Creator they make many things manifest to them. For the Lord is near to those who call upon him in truth. The Lord will do the will of those who fear him, and he will hear their prayers and save them. For whoever is united with Christ through grace is refreshed by the frequent visitation of angels; and often, when necessity demands, he is made illustrious by signs and powers through their ministry.

This is why those oppressed by various illnesses also betake themselves to the aid of the saints; and, turning over in their minds the promises of the Lord, they obtain by the merits of others what they cannot obtain by their own. Their holy devotion in the faith of Christ receives salvation from the Lord, because, aided by the purity of their own conscience and supported by the merit of another’s holiness, it rises, not rashly, in sure hope.

Miracles are done in the Church of God by divine power in a threefold respect or means. For sometimes Almighty God works a miracle through the ministry of angels solely by the merits of the worker himself, just as Saint Martin healed a blind man and a lame man against their will. Sometimes

Third, there is no doubt that healings of this kind are done by the merit of both, namely of the sufferer and of the worker together, as Christ says to someone in the holy Gospel: “If you can believe, all things are possible to the one who believes.” For faith is required for a miracle to such a degree that our Lord Jesus Christ, coming into his own country, marveled because he could not do any mighty work there because of their unbelief. But none of these things takes place among unbelievers, where neither holiness is found in the worker nor orthodox faith in the sufferer.

Third, therefore, man works miracles through the cooperation of diabolical wickedness as an intermediary. And this can happen in three ways: namely by manifest invocation of demons, by implication, and by supposition. By manifest invocation, necromancers and little women whom we commonly call witches do wondrous things; through likeness of depraved will they have passed into fellowship with demons. For just as in the Church of God the sacraments are signs of the invisible grace of the spirit of holiness, so in the school of demons certain characters and modes of invocation are marks of a pact between a man and a malignant spirit.

QUESTION THREE.

Hence, when signs of demons are presented through works of witchcraft, malignant spirits, once invoked, are accustomed to appear willingly and to display both horrible works of witchcraft and certain wonders. But witches and necromancers produce wondrous effects by invocation of demons in different ways. Witches are subject to demons by a certain kind of profession; by their ministry they disturb the air, raise storms, devastate crops, and make men and beasts ill. They carry on foul commerce of the filthiest pleasure with demons, and by pernicious spells they call back from the lower world into sight whomever they wish. Their deeds should not be called miracles, but rather works of witchcraft, and they deservedly ought to be punished by the ultimate penalty.

Necromancers, however, subject themselves to demons not by manifest profession, but by an implicit condition, when they claim that they compel them to appear visibly in a circle not only by conjurations and the display of characters, but also by sacrifices, fumigations, and vows. Their crimes are infinite, since they all manifestly conspire into fellowship with demons, whether implicitly or explicitly.
