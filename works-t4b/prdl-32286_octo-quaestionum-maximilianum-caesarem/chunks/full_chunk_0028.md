Nor do they all have one form, but many, and they change these quite often according to the variety of their affections, either when they come, summoned by the spells of witches, or when, driven by disturbance, they are impelled to do harm. For they have an altogether violent and furious disposition, full of disturbances. Hence, being exceedingly wicked and disordered, they very often contrive sudden ambushes; and while they make their assaults, some wish to lie hidden, others inflict violence. Witches, supported by the cooperation of these demons, are the more powerful for working witchcraft, the higher in their order the demon they have obtained as an accomplice.

In the life of Saint ANTONY, the divine Athanasius said that the air is full of demons; Hermes Trismegistus had also said this before, namely that no part of the world is bereft of the presence of demons. The holy bishop AMBROSE also says: “The world is full of holy powers, because it is full of wickednesses.” Hence the Platonists have handed down that demons flying in the air, like very dense fiery snow,

The third kind is called that of terrestrial demons, whom we do not in the least doubt to have fallen from heaven to earth for their own deserts. Concerning these, that most holy bishop and martyr of CHRIST, Theophorus, once Ignatius, wrote thus in a certain letter to the Ephesians: “Nothing is better than to have peace, in which every weapon of the aerial and terrestrial spirits alike is made void.” Of these demons, some dwell in woods and groves, laying snares for hunters; others live in open fields, making travelers wander off course at night; some dwell in hidden places and

caverns; the rest, less furious and disturbed than the others, delight in dwelling obscurely with human beings. They are not led by one affection, but by various ones, since some are less depraved than others, although all are full of evil disturbances. Some of these rejoice in frightening human beings with mere illusions; others desire to draw their hearers into admiration of themselves by predictions of future events. Some, however, strive with the greatest effort to terrify, injure, or kill human beings troubled by irrational frenzy or raging with melancholy; and we have learned by experience that this has often happened. Hence Saint JOHN CHRYSOSTOM, in the third book On Providence to the monk Stagirius, says: “The greatness of grief is more powerful than every action of the devil in preventing harm; for whomever a demon overcomes, he overcomes through grief. If you take that away, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon.” This kind of demon, however, is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy of their affections and the lightness by which they delight more in terrifying many people than in being subject to the command of one little woman. Yet sometimes, to flatter the insane, they dwell in glass or in crystal

And this kind of witch sometimes differs greatly from the rest, because what those others make infirm by witchcraft, these promise to cure. But because they very often repel witchcraft by witchcraft, access to them is forbidden by the Church to every Christian.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, because, immersing itself in waters, it dwells around lakes and rivers, full of anger, troubled, restless, and deceitful. It raises storms at sea, sinks ships into the deep, and takes life from many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, it more often appears in the female sex, and is more rarely seen in the male, because they dwell in watery places and follow a softer manner of life, nor can they easily assume the vile form of the others by customary practice. Hence antiquity long ago named Naiads, Nereids, and nymphs of the waters not in the masculine sex but in the feminine.
