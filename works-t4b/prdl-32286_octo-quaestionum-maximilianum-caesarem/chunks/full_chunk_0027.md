But another person, who is not subject to the demons by pact and dedication, even if he knows the things suited to witchcraft and also the manner of working, and although he displays the signs, nevertheless rarely obtains the effect, because he is not counted among the demons’ familiars. For the devil, swollen with pride, imitates the Church of Christ to such a degree that, just as an unordained man cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he may utter the words of consecration, so also a demon either never or rarely comes to the aid of one who invokes him and shows his signs or characters, if he does not know that person to be subject to him by pact.

For if he came indiscriminately to all who invoked him without pacts having been made, he would not possess so many witches dedicated to him by right of ownership. Hence many who are given over to profane arts, striving by dreadful conjurations to call demons to a circle, a mirror, or any other receptacles, obtain no effect, because either God’s command stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man who has not been dedicated to him by homage. fury in the mind and a depraved will in the witch, the cooperation also of a familiar demon, and before all else divine permission. If one of these does not concur, witchcraft cannot be perpetrated. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not meet the will of the wicked woman, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch were lacking, the devil would by no means bring the witchcraft to completion.

For the devil uses the will of the raging witch as an artisan uses an instrument for working; without it he could not inflict witchcraft. And just as demons differ from one another in power and in sharpness of subtlety, so the power of a witch is not one and the same in all, but differs in each, according as demons of a subtler or grosser nature concur with them. For there are many kinds of demons, and they stand apart from one another by fixed degrees, according to the places into which they were cast down when they fell in the beginning.

has no commerce with human beings on earth. That they remain beneath the moon is attested by Saint Augustine in the work On the Christian Combat, saying thus: Demons do not dwell in the heaven where the sun and stars are, but all move about beneath the lunar region.

Moved by this, as I think, Apuleius said that demons are fiery animals, and for this reason Aristotle confirmed that in burning furnaces certain small animals, fitted with little wings, have often been seen flying about; they spend their whole life in fire, since they arise with the fire and are extinguished with it, and, vanishing, return to their own place from which they had come. They have no fellowship with witches, since, because of their subtlety, they dwell in fire and cannot assume a gross or airy body.

The second kind is called airy; wandering in the air, it dwells near us. Indeed they can descend to the lower regions and, by assuming bodies from the denser air, sometimes appear visibly to human beings. By God’s permission they often disturb the air, stir up thunder and storms, and all conspire together for the destruction of the human race.
