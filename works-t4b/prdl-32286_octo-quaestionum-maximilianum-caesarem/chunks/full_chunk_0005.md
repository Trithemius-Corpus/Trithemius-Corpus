But because a cause of just consideration moved Your Most Serene Highness to ask these things, not because you do not know what must be held in this matter according to the Catholic faith, but so that you might have something true to set against those who attack the truth, I shall answer the questions briefly, as best I can.

If anyone could be saved outside the Christian faith, Christ, the Son of almighty God, would seem to have become man superfluously and without cause. For if there is salvation outside Christ, then Christ is not the Savior of all. But because it was necessary that the only-begotten Son of God, for the salvation of the human race, be born as man in the world, suffer, die, and rise again on the third day according to the Scriptures, he did not come into this world in vain, nor superfluously, nor without cause. Since this is so, whoever asserts that anyone can be saved outside the faith of Christ shows himself not a Christian, but an adversary of the faith and a deserter of Christ. For whoever does not believe in Christ, the Son of God, promises himself eternal salvation in vain. For he who is the truth, Jesus Christ, says in the holy Gospel: He who does not believe will be condemned. And again: He who does not believe is already judged, because he does not believe in the name of the only-begotten Son of God. And again, a little later, he adds,

THE SECOND QUESTION.

saying: He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. The holy Apostle also says: Without faith it is impossible to please God. Hebrews 11.

The solution of the proposed question is now clear to all who truly believe in Christ: salvation is through him, the Savior of the world, and whoever does not believe in him cannot be saved. Nor is it lawful for a Christian in any way to go beyond the boundaries of his religion, so as to presume to explain this question otherwise than Christ has taught. Whoever goes outside this rule of faith by asserting the contrary is plainly shown not to think rightly about Christ, the Savior of the faithful. For whoever promises salvation to anyone outside Christ wishes to declare Christ a liar. But we, confirmed by the most sacred ordinances of the Gospel, firmly believe and without doubt pronounce that no one can be saved outside the faith of Christ, however much he worships one God, however holy or just his life, however much he trusts in his paternal religion or in another. Christ is salvation, and outside the Christian faith no one will be saved. For why would Christ the Son of God have willed to die for the sins of the world, if each

person could have been saved outside his faith in his own paternal tradition? From the beginning of the world, after the fall of the human race, it was impossible for anyone to be saved unto eternal life otherwise than in the blood of our Lord Jesus Christ. What of the fact that the greater part of humanity today remains outside the faith of Jesus? Did not the Savior of the world himself long ago foretell in the holy Gospel: Many are called, but few are chosen? And again he says: Enter through the narrow gate, because wide is the gate and spacious is the way that leads to perdition, and there are many who enter through it.

What do we think our salvation can contribute to God, who has no need of good things? For whether many are saved or many are damned, God himself is the unchangeable Lord of all. Nothing is added to him if all are saved; nothing is lacking to him if the whole world perishes. The order of the providence of the divine majesty is infallible; it embraces those who are to be damned no less than those who are to be saved, each and every one. Certainly no one among those to be damned suffers injustice, since, just as in the first man we all fell, so no one is saved except in the blood of Jesus Christ through mercy, and no one is damned except in his own unbelief and according to desert through justice.
