QUESTION ONE.

Let the human intellect now raise itself as much as it can; and if it strives to comprehend God, let it rightly understand and comprehend the center of this imagined sphere, together with its circumference ending nowhere. But if it is not sufficient to understand this proposition of its own likeness, why does it contend to investigate the essence of God, which exists above every positive assertion?

May it not weary you, most unconquered Caesar, to hear another likeness concerning God from Cusanus, once a cardinal priest of the Roman Church, my fellow Mosellan, a man most learned in those times. He says: God is a straight line of infinite length, which makes a circle. Let us see, then. If the line is straight, how does it make a circle? But if it makes a circle, how is it straight? For a circle and a straight line are naturally opposed. Nevertheless, a line of infinite length, since it has neither beginning nor end, necessarily makes a circle, although it is proposed to be imagined not as curved but as straight.

Since, therefore, God is infinite, greatest without quantity, best without quality, all things in all things without circumscription, above all things without excluding positive assertions, it was not possible for him to be comprehended by the circumscribable human intellect, which uses the service of the senses for understanding; and

But after our mind, at last released from the flesh, has come to its own center, which is God, clean, simple, pure, perfect, and consummated, then, made equal to the angels, we shall know God perfectly and all things, just as Saint Paul says to the Corinthians, 1 Cor. 13: We see now through a mirror and in an enigma; but then face to face.

For this reason we said that the angelic intellect, by the guidance of its nature, cleaves immovably to its own principle, because it is pure act and habit; and since it is simple by natural condition, it cannot be impeded by any commerce with composition. But the human intellect, as has now been sufficiently declared, since it is potency and privation, is impure because of its commerce with its body, and is indeed delayed from perfect act until at last, released from the flesh, it passes into the fellowship of likeness with the angels; then for the first time it is to be admitted to the true knowledge of God. But if in the meantime the affection of love necessary for salvation is joined to present Christian faith and to future divine knowledge, the same everlasting blessedness prepared with the holy angels will be given to the worshippers of God. Therefore, to conclude the solution of the proposed question as far as I have been able, I say that it is not possible for almighty God to be understood perfectly by us as long as we, being mortal, dwell in the flesh.
