He is wounded by errors of this kind as though by blows of stones. For just as all things are clean to the clean, so all things seem profane to the profane. Timothy 6. God therefore placed his hiding place in the height of the Scriptures, Psalm 17, which is so inaccessible and closed to the reprobate that it nevertheless always lies plainly open to the saints.

But why did almighty God not give holy Scripture as sufficient and manifest for everything that concerns the Christian faith? We answer briefly: there are two things which the Most High provided for believers for the confirmation of the Christian faith, namely the Church and Scripture, so that what is not expressed clearly enough in the one may be declared more fully by the other. Hence, if we find anything in the holy Scriptures expressed less sufficiently concerning the rules of faith, we at once have recourse to the Church; and where the Church has need of saving instruction for piety, it immediately turns to Scripture. From this it follows that neither would the Church have authority without Scripture, nor Scripture faith without the Church, so that from the two one judgment may be made.

For Augustine says: “I would not believe the Gospel unless the authority of the Church compelled me.” But John Gerson says: “I would not believe the Church unless the Gospel compelled me.” Moreover, the holy

Scripture confirms the Church, and it in like manner is confirmed by Scripture. The Church abounds whenever Scripture is lacking; and if the Church has any doubt, it returns to the counsel of Scripture. For there is one Holy Spirit of God, who founded the Church in the faith of Jesus Christ, the very same almighty one who alone inspired Scripture. And this is the threefold cord which is not easily broken, where divine Scripture through the Holy Spirit is joined to the authority of the Church, to such an extent that the Church commends Scripture, and Scripture in turn commends the Church.

And so, O Caesar, it seems to me that we have assigned a sufficient reason, for your unconquered Majesty, for the question proposed: because if all things that concern the piety of faith were clearly expressed in the holy Scriptures, then our chosen mother Church would not possess so great an authority, and the merit of saving obedience would be emptied out in large part. At the same time, I think that enough has been answered for the principal question, where certain people judge sacred Scripture to be confused and tangled in very many places. Yet if they read it with due purity of mind, they would certainly find it perfect and solid.

Great is the authority of the catholic Church, which alone, in doubtful matters concerning those things that regard the faith, has the right to interpret the Scriptures, and to which the necks of all powers must be subjected. If anyone departs from its definition with rash daring, he has denied the Gospel
