But if anyone thinks that this whole vast magnitude of the universe cannot be ruled and governed by one God, who alone exists, he is greatly mistaken, and is shown not to know how great the power of divine might is. For indeed, he who could bring the whole world from nothing into being can also govern forever, most wisely, what he made. For he knows all things, understands all things, and can do all things, who alone, almighty, created all things. Therefore we, believing the holy Gospel, should above all venerate divine providence; and let us raise our minds upward from these passing things with what strength we can, and, brought back into ourselves, be united, so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God present everywhere. Since it is wholly uncircumscribable, it holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, surpasses all things, and sustains all things. Yet it does not sustain from one side and surpass from another,

nor fill from one side and encompass from another; rather, by encompassing it fills, and by filling it encompasses; by sustaining it surpasses, and by surpassing it sustains all things. Through bodily bulk he is nowhere, and through his uncircumscribable substance he is nowhere absent; he is not seen by the eyes of flesh, but is seen through faith in purity of heart. O inexplicable power, O incomprehensible goodness, O ineffable wisdom, O uncircumscribable divinity: you do not consist of parts like a body, nor are you set apart by passions like a soul, nor do you underlie forms like all the other things that were made by you. You are in all things and above all things, ruling and governing the universe most wisely.

By these testimonies, then, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, it has, as I think, been sufficiently proved that this whole universe is governed by the providence of God. Since he is pure intellect, simple, uncreated, and eternal, he knows all things, understands all things, recognizes all things; and since by nature he is almighty, he can do all things, to whom nothing is difficult, nothing impossible. He created such wondrous things by his power, established them in truth, perfected them by reason, sustains them by strength, and illumines them by goodness. To him be praise, to him honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, glorious and exalted God, forever and ever. Amen.

*Question Eight.*

The author's declaration to Caesar.

These, unconquered Caesar Maximilian, are the things that it seemed to me should be said in response to the eight questions proposed by your Majesty. For you wished to have from me a judgment on them in writing also, judging me, beyond the truth, more highly than is fitting; for I am not to be compared in ability with those whose opinions you heard before me. Let this make me worthy of pardon if I have said something less fitting: that, being on the road by command of Your Highness, I dictated, as I could, in haste, what occurred to me as needing response.

But I say from my heart and openly declare that I am a sincere confessor of the Christian faith, and that I wish to remain until death in union with the most holy Roman Church. Therefore, if in this little work of mine or in others I should have said or written anything otherwise than she holds, thinks, believes, and teaches, as a son of true obedience, by the attestation of the present writing I deny it, and I wish it simply and forever to be held as revoked.
