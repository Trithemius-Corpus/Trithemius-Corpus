QUESTION EIGHT.

And all individual things that the Almighty makes, he understands.

There is no doubt that he disposes, rules, and orders all things that he has made, from whom things have not only that they exist, but also that they are certain causes of other things. And so, if things have from God that they are causes, from him too they have received the proper office of a cause, that is, to preserve as far as their powers allow and to care for what they have made. Therefore, if all causes have this property from the first cause, insofar as they are certain causes, with good reason that first cause has this natural goodness and providence, so that it cares for all things and every individual thing that it has made, inasmuch as it is most truly the cause of all of them and of each one.

Indeed, unless the things that preserve common realities in individuals are guarded individually by him who makes them, those common realities are never guarded. For one will not preserve the whole, which consists of parts, if the parts themselves are neglected. Therefore, from the intention of the first, greatest, and most common intelligence proceeds the universal distinction of things; but that intelligence itself distinguishes all other things from others in themselves, and distinguishes the rest among themselves by the rule of its own conception. Hence, just as God knows all things and each individual thing most distinctly, so too he most distinctly rules, moderates, and cares for them.

I think it is therefore manifest, most merciful Emperor Maximilian, and sufficiently shown, that the providence of God, the highest ruler, embraces, disposes, rules, and moderates all lower things and each individual thing;

for otherwise, without the provident disposition of one who watches over them, they could in no way maintain this most beautiful order that we discern. Nor does it escape me how variously the wise men of this world once held opinions concerning providence. Some of them thought that this world was made not by providence but by chance. If their opinion were true, no place would be left for providence, but all things would necessarily drift, since the world could perish by the same chance by which it was made.

Against their false opinion, others taught that the world could neither have been made without reason nor certainly continue to exist unless it were ruled by the highest providence of its maker. Hence Boethius, a most Christian man, said: “O you who govern the world by everlasting reason, creator of earth and heaven, who command time to proceed from eternity, and, remaining steadfast, give all things motion.” And again he said: “The generation of all things, the whole progression of changeable natures, and whatever is moved, in some way draws its causes, order, and forms from the stability of the divine mind.”

Others, however, thought that God cared only for incorruptible things, such as the heavenly bodies and separate substances, together with the species,

and that he truly did not know all the rest at all and utterly neglected them. But these men erred shamefully, because the Almighty has providence and care not only for all incorruptible things, but also for particular and corruptible things. Hence Saint Boethius again says: “The high creator sits upon the summit, and, ruling all things, turns the reins: king and lord, fountain and origin, law and wise judge of equity. This is the common bond of things, the continuous love by which they seek to be held by the end of the good; for otherwise they cannot endure unless, with love turned back again, the causes flow back to him who gave them.”

Some, not tolerating the thought that they themselves should be neglected, removed man from the number of corruptible things because of the excellence of his intellect, saying that God has care and providence for man, but despises all the remaining earthly things. Against their false opinion, I think enough has already been said, and to produce many more arguments would be altogether superfluous. For Scripture says: “You will save men and beasts, Lord.” And in another place a certain wise man said: “The Lord made the small and the great, and he has care alike for all.”

Therefore divine majesty’s providence orders, rules, disposes, and cares for all lower things and each individual thing, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible; its most powerful will made all things from nothing.
