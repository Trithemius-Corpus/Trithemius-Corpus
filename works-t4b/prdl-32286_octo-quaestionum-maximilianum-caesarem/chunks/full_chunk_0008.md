They desire to be merciful toward lost men, and they do not fear to show themselves cruel toward God. Those whom he, by justice, has assigned to death, these men assert, with the worst presumption, are to be recalled to life. It is neither good nor useful for a Christian, whether seriously or in jest, to contradict Christ. He is impious, not Christian, who says that any unbelievers are to be saved.

In Adam, the first man, when he fell, we all sinned, and the wrath of God remained upon the whole human race from the beginning of the world until Christ. Christ came, born of a virgin, God and man, innocent of sin; he voluntarily underwent the death of the cross, and, having been made mediator between God and man, removed the wrath of original guilt from those who believe in him. For thus he says, John 4: “He who believes in the only-begotten Son of God has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him.”

Indeed, without faith in Jesus Christ no one will be saved, however justly and holily he may seem to live in his own religion. For almighty God can save a believer without material baptism, but he cannot save one who denies without faith. No one is saved unwillingly,

because no one believes except willingly. For the Lord knows those who are his, since he saves those who believe in the Son through repentance, but condemns unbelievers by the equity of justice.

Hence, with the way closed off, a new question has knocked at the mind: what should be thought concerning the infants and simple-minded persons of unbelieving pagans and Jews? To this one must answer briefly: just as the infants of Christians, baptized in the faith of their parents, are saved, so the unbaptized children of all unbelievers will be damned in the unbelief of their parents. The judgment concerning the simple-minded of each religion is similar: just as we believe without doubt that baptized simple-minded Christians are to be saved, so we say without doubt that those belonging to unbelievers are to be damned.

Again, it is asked here what is to be thought in a catholic manner about those men who inhabit the newly discovered islands in the great sea, of which there is no mention among the ancient cosmographers, and who, as we have been informed, have never heard anything at all about the Christian religion. he concluded in a general saying: “He who believes and has been baptized shall be saved; but he who has not believed shall be condemned,” Mark 16, “and the wrath of God remains upon him.” Who removes the wrath of God from man except our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the mediator of God and men? 1 Timothy 2. By what means, except through faith formed in him who tasted death while innocent?

Since, therefore, no one is saved without faith, we cannot promise salvation to those ignorant of Christ, who, it is agreed, do not have faithful piety toward him. Accordingly, we must necessarily say that all are to be damned who do not believe in the Son of God, whether they have heard about him or are wholly ignorant of him. Yet we grant one thing: if any among those who are ignorant of Christ have lived well according to the law of nature, invoking one God, even if they have sometimes sinned, provided they performed repentance before death, they have been damned, or are to be damned, less severely than those who either hear of Christ and despise him, or have lived wickedly and basely among both groups. For God is just; he does injury to no one, but renders to each according to his works.

But the wrath of God remains upon everyone who does not believe in his only-begotten Son, because of the original sin of the first man, from which no one is justified except through faith and the baptism of the Lord and Savior.
