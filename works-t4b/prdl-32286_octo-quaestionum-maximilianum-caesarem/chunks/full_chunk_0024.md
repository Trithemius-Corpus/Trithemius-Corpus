But all those whom malignant spirits obey when summoned by the conjurations of necromancers have without doubt passed into their fellowship through an explicit or implicit pact. The number of the rest, however, is greater: those whom malignant spirits disdain to obey, although they are not afraid to occupy themselves at all times with the profane arts of necromancers.

And because, especially in our times, iniquity has begun to abound and the charity of many has grown cold, these accursed and most deceitful men have become very powerful. Although they are despised by demons, nevertheless, persisting in their pernicious doctrines, they deceive many. Believe me, Caesar, this most deceitful class of men is very harmful to your holy empire, and therefore, no less than the brood of witches, must be utterly exterminated.

They promise great things, and they lie in everything. Indeed, all the things promised in their books are empty and false, and they provide those who believe them nothing except tricks, frauds, and the most subtle deceptions. But if at times they seem to produce what they have promised, it should be clear to everyone that this happens only through the cooperation of demons, from whose fellowship one must flee no less than from the most savage

For demons very often attend these necromantic operations, and, in order to keep their invokers obstinate in the treachery they have begun, they grant them what they ask, if they can. Yet sometimes, when summoned by conjurations, they disdain to appear, and nevertheless they have led away the minds of those who invoke them.

There is also another kind of men, no less foolish than superstitious: those, namely, who do not wish to have any commerce with demons, and nevertheless use in their vain operations things assigned to demons and, for that reason, wholly illicit. For they think that there is great and hidden power in characters, and therefore they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But since this is not the time to recount their madness, let us pursue our subject.

Why good and just men do not obtain command over demons must now be stated briefly. Just as likeness of wills makes evil and impious men peers of demons, so unlikeness of wills renders good and just men wholly opposed to them. Between opposites and those unlike one another there can be no familiar

association; rather, continual opposition and hatred prevail. But where no familiarity contracted through likeness of wills is found, there can be neither confidence of one toward another nor a mutual marvelous concurrence in operation. Nor do you, glorious Caesar, a man who are a great ruler and prince of men, admit as familiar to yourself anyone wholly contrary and unlike you in morals and continually resisting your will.

Good and upright men, because they always oppose the most depraved will of demons, can in no way pass into their fellowship, since they neither wish nor consent to be familiar with them, but with all their strength flee from and detest encounter with them. Therefore they do not obtain over them that fictitious command of which we have spoken. For no familiarity is secure unless likeness of wills has preceded it; nor can there be harmony between opposites, nor friendship between those of unlike will.

Nor do demons everywhere obey all evil and reprobate people, but only those whom they have bound to themselves by pacts and services; and when invoked by those whom they cannot otherwise have as faithful servants, they freely come, although they falsely say that they are compelled. It is therefore not to be wondered at, excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to submit to depraved and perverse men.
