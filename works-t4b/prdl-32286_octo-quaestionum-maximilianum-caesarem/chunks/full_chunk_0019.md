FOURTH QUESTION.

It is the slowness of our intelligence and the impurity of our desire whenever we do not understand the divine Scriptures rightly, since the veil of pernicious occupation remains spread over our hearts and does not allow us to arrive at true understanding. Hence it happens that we do not understand the meaning which the Holy Spirit infused into the divine Scriptures, but, grasping the bare words of the writers, twist them into another meaning.

Indeed, if our minds were cleansed of carnal vices; if, through the continual practice of sobriety, our intelligence were exercised in the Scriptures; if faith and reverence were shown to the holy Scriptures as their dignity requires, every diligent student would understand more clearly than light that there is nothing confused in the sacred readings, nothing discordant, nothing unfitting, nothing absurd, or inserted without the most just reason.

For all the passages of divine Scripture are full of mysteries; and whenever anything in them seems to be set down obscurely, it was done by divine providence, so that the studious mind might have the necessary exercise of devotion, and the faith of the simple might not lose its place of due reverence. Therefore it was not by chance, but by everlasting providence, that God wished the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that they might abound in as many mysteries as there are meanings to be explained in a Catholic manner by teachers under the inspiration of the Spirit of God. For, as Saint Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture

is useful and very profitable, because it brings forth many statements of truth and produces them into the light for human beings; for when one person draws out this meaning, and another draws out another, in expounding it in a Catholic manner, it becomes known how high and profound Scripture is.

Therefore let no man swollen with secular learning suppose that he understands sacred Scripture unless he first lays aside the veil of pride and of every disordered desire. For secular learning is of more value for exercising the intelligence than for enlightening the understanding toward true wisdom.

Thus without doubt divine Scripture is clear and manifest to minds purified through continual exercise of the fear of God and frequent study of reading; yet to the filthy and to those puffed up with secular wisdom it seems confused and obscure. Therefore the man who understands the holy Scriptures well and rightly is the one who, with purified affection and frequent in the study of them, expounds them as did the one who wrote them. For if they read in a spirit other than that by which the Scriptures are believed to have been inspired so that they might be written, the mind must necessarily be wholly shut away from true understanding.

But when we encounter some dissonance in them, let us understand that it belongs only to the words, not to the meanings; and that these things arise not by chance, but by the providence of God and in orderly fashion, so that the mind, exercised in the Scriptures, may be strengthened for a longer time and not grow wholly sluggish.

FOURTH QUESTION.

For God’s counsel cannot be deceived; but since the divine Scriptures were written according to the counsel of his will, it necessarily follows that they are true and in no way false. They were human beings by whom the divine Scriptures were handed down to us; certainly they wrote words used according to human custom, by which they might signify to posterity what that supreme excellence of divinity wished to be known to mortals.

Therefore you now, who are devoted to secular wisdom and attack the divine Scriptures, say this: from where do you believe the Church received the Scripture of the New and Old Testament, which it has accepted and venerates as true, authoritative, and holy Christian Scripture? From God, or from human beings? If from God, why do you not believe it? For everyone who attacks Scripture which he believes to have been inspired by God very plainly resists divine ordinance. But if it is from human beings, why do you hope for the eternal salvation promised in it, which a human being cannot give?

Unless perhaps, in your madness, you go on to say that all things are inventions, and that nothing remains after death except ashes, and that the mind undergoes destruction together with the body; such a profession would show you to be no less insane than foolish.
