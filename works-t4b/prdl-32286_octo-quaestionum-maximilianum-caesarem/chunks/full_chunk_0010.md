THE THIRD QUESTION.

Good Christians work miracles through public righteousness; evil Christians, through signs of public righteousness; but unbelievers through private contracts with demons, namely implicit or explicit ones. Concerning the good the Lord said in the holy Gospel: “These signs shall follow those who believe: in my name they shall cast out demons, they shall speak with new tongues, they shall take up serpents, and if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them. They shall lay hands upon the sick, and they shall be well.”

But concerning evil Christians who work miracles in the name of Jesus Christ, we read thus in the Gospel: “Many will say to me on that day: Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in your name, and in your name cast out demons, and in your name done many mighty works? And then I will declare to them: I never knew you; depart from me, all you who work iniquity.”

Outside the Church of God, however, where the name of our Lord Jesus Christ is not invoked, if any marvels occur beyond the custom of nature or of human art, no good Christian doubts that they are done by the cooperation of demons. Your Caesar’s Serenity should therefore judge works of this kind to be called wonders rather than miracles, by a discernment of power. Nor does this judgment depart from the principle of truth, so that a necessary distinction may be maintained between prodigies of light and of darkness.

I find four producers of wonders, but I acknowledge one alone who truly works miracles in the principal sense: God, as the cause of all good things. Therefore the first and greatest producer of all wonders is God, who has filled and illumined the whole world with signs, miracles, and powers. None of the others is to be compared with him, who created heaven and earth, the sea, and all things that are in them; who alone is almighty, just, holy and upright, good, incomprehensible, greatest, glorious, immense, and everlasting. All other things are beneath him, since whatever can happen wondrously in this world is certainly less than the whole world itself, which he was able to create out of nothing. Likewise every miracle that can be done through a human being is less than the human being himself, whom God, the creator of all things, formed from the beginning in his image and likeness.

And although the miracles of God in visible natures have now, among mortals, almost become commonplace through the constancy of seeing them, nevertheless, if we look upon them wisely, they will appear greater and rarer than all unusual things. Now the manner in which God works miracles is the unchangeable will of God, which, since it is almighty, does all things whatsoever it has willed in heaven and

in heaven and on earth. For he spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created. The will of God is ordered and everlasting; therefore his miracles are incomparably greater than the rest. For greater than all the miracles of this world is the whole universe that we behold, and whatever is contained in it, created by the will of God. The unchangeable divine will, enduring, works all things, since it lacks both beginning and end. But whatever God wills, he always wills; because, since willing and being able are the same in him, it does not belong to the unchangeable one to labor.

Hence the creator of all things has displayed for both good and evil people all the universal miracles that he makes, so that they may be seen; and after the general ones, which have now become commonplace through use among mortals, he has also shown, in almost individual creatures, innumerable things especially worthy of wonder.

The second producer of miracles is the good angel, who very often works wonders among mortals both by natural power and by divine power. For those most blessed spirits are sent by divine ordinance into service for the faithful, Heb. 1, and they provide to both good and evil people the benefits that belong to nature; but to those alone who believe in God and in our Lord Jesus Christ they administer the gifts of saving grace. Hence there is no doubt that those miracles which are done by invocation of the divine name in the Church of the saints are displayed through their ministry and cooperation.

But the wonders of unbelievers, if any are done today or are read to have been done formerly, are either fabricated or falsely claimed, or are accomplished by the deception of demons. For it cannot happen that any unbelievers work miracles by the ministry of good angels, since where Christ Jesus, the Son of God, is not believed in, no signs of grace are displayed through good spirits to impious and reprobate human beings. Indeed, since they are obstinate in the error of false religion, if they have attained anything miraculous in their own estimation, there is no doubt at all that it is done through the ministry of demons. For since they are wholly unlike the good angels, they obtain no familiarity with them.

No despiser of the Christian faith can please God; rather, whoever is proved to displease almighty God is without doubt deprived of the familiarity of the holy angels. Yet by this we do not deny that every human being is entrusted to some good spirit, by whose ministry, even if he is an unbeliever in the Son of God, he receives the benefits of nature, although he does not reach the gifts of grace. But to those who believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, they confer not only the benefits of nature, but also administer the gifts of grace.
