The learned man who burns under the cover of the flesh and with vain desires reads it in one way; the man whom the love of God truly inflames, stripped of pleasures, understands it in another. Hence it is no wonder if men who think carnally do not understand the divine Scriptures, since, blinded by the pleasures of the flesh, they have never perceived the sweetness of the Spirit of God.

It is just as if you were to show to a boy learning the first elements of letters some written volume whose script was unlike that of his own little book: when he could not read it, he would at once cry out that the writing was bad and not properly formed. In the same way, men who approach the reading of the divine Scriptures while thinking only carnally, and who do not grasp the things that belong to the divine Spirit, weighed down by vices and rashness, although they ought to have blamed their own ignorance and unworthiness, reproach the holy Scriptures because they do not understand them.

For it is not lawful for everyone indiscriminately to interpret the divine Scriptures, lest the beast that touches the mountain be overwhelmed with stones. Now many arise, in a perverse order, who have spent the whole time of their life on humane letters, and when they have become old men, by wrongly interpreting the divine Scriptures which they have never learned, they do not explain them but confound them, and discuss them according to their own sense. comes the fact that whoever do not understand the sacred Scriptures in the Spirit by which they were infused into those who wrote them have the veil of ignorance and rashness spread over their hearts, in the manner of the Jews, and very often draw from them the poison of pernicious error, from which the humble, approaching in the Spirit of God, have received the cup of salvation.

For heavenly doctrine is not found in the words of Scripture, but in its meaning; not on the surface of its expressions, but in the understanding; not in the leaves of ornate speech, but in the hidden place of mystical inspiration. Hence the divine pope Gregory says: “Sacred Scripture far surpasses all the sciences of this world in its manner of speaking, since in one and the same discourse, while it narrates the text, it discloses a hidden mystery; and just as it refreshes the simple on the surface, so it exercises the wise with mystery. For it has in public what may nourish little ones, and it preserves in secret what may suspend the minds of the exalted in admiration of its sweetness, like a certain wonderful river in which both the simple little lamb may walk and the great elephant may swim.”

And Saint Augustine says on Genesis: “Divine Scripture speaks in such a way that by its height it laughs at the proud, by its depth it terrifies the attentive, by its virtue it feeds the great, and by its affability it nourishes little ones.” Greater

Therefore, in order that one may come to their true understanding, the mind must first be cleansed of every carnal affection, and the aid of divine goodness must be earnestly implored with humility, so that by his Spirit he may illumine the understanding of the learner. For to the authority of the divine Scriptures the necks of all powers must be submitted.

And as the divine Jerome says: “One must never flatter a man in such a way that the truth of the holy Scriptures is neglected; for in both Testaments it is wholly true in the narration of deeds, right in its admonitions, certain in its promises, and terribly to be feared in its threats.”

For this reason it has not been granted to all to be able to penetrate the mysteries of the divine Scriptures, but it is permitted only to those to whom both love gives continuity of study and the Spirit of God grants true understanding in purified minds. But whoever is befouled by the darkness of vices, whoever walks proudly with a swollen mind, whoever doubts the truth of the Scriptures of God either in part or in whole, should never trust that he can attain to understanding them.
