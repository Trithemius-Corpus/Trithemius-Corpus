Then let us contemplate all earthly things, how each and every one of them is born and proceeds in its own natural disposition in the most orderly way. This cannot happen by chance; rather, they must be moved by the will of him by whose power they were made and created. Next, let us consider the earth, placed in the middle of the world like a point or center, which unfolds the circle of the whole universe at an equal distance. Nothing more beautiful, nothing more adorned, nothing, finally, more delightful could present itself to the sense for contemplation. In this point of an immense mass, wonderfully enclosed by that very mass, we see all things ordered, and we see that nothing can be left unordered, so that whatever sometimes seems to us somehow to drift, we may know with certainty that, within the order of the universe, it does not lack its own particular order. For by God’s simple and wholly unchangeable nod, the whole structure of the entire universe, assembled from so many diverse and movable things, works together in the most orderly way and is governed. We certainly see in those things that present themselves to our senses that, the greater the intelligence and power that arises over time in any governor, the less can anything disordered occur there by random chance.

Question Eight.

Therefore, since that supreme intellect, the creator, preserver, and ruler of all things, almighty God, rules and governs the world by his unchangeable providence, holding all things under his command, no place is ever left for rash chance, which might obstruct the will of so great a majesty. Thus the whole universe, whatever exists anywhere or can exist, is contained under the command of the one divine mind, whose infallible judgment nothing can escape. Unexpected events happen to us because we are changeable and are changed almost every day, having no determinate knowledge of future things. But that best maker of all things, God, since he is altogether unchangeable, knows all things, beholds all things, and understands all things at once and together. His providence is not deceived; his will is never varied; his power is not broken; his ordering is not violated; his counsel is never interrupted, even if at times his judgment seems to be changed. Hence, if we attend carefully, we shall plainly understand, as the ends and order of motions will declare, that all these lower things are made, preserved, disposed, and moved by divine providence, from whose command absolutely nothing is alien.

For God himself, the creator of the universe, both understands all things and is not divided in a particular way, as we are, into many things, but remains in himself an indivisible one. By beholding himself as the exemplar of the whole universe and the cause of causes, he contemplates the cause of all things through their causes; and by contemplating all things he cares for them and provides for them, directing each thing to a good end by an everlasting order. Come now, most wise Celsus: in order that we may understand divine providence governing all things, let us use arguments, as far as we can, more briefly for our purpose.

Thus, if God’s action is his own proper understanding, which no wise person will deny, as the most eminent and at the same time the most inward thing, then as much as he acts, so much in any case he understands, and conversely. But he acts in individual things, not only in great things but also in the very smallest. Therefore he understands individual things. For the first being, through individual things that are in any way beings, following upon fixed orders, is the most ample cause; and the things that act after him are everywhere moved by his power and act by it. Either, then, God understands or acts nothing outside himself, which is false, or, by acting all things and each thing, he also understands those same things, which is true. For they would never come into being unless they were first, to speak in our manner, understood by God himself, who makes them, just as things are not brought into being by the sun and not first seen by it.
