Before the Lord’s passion they enjoyed greater freedom of power than afterward; but when Christ tasted death for us on the altar of the cross, their power was thereafter greatly diminished, as the Savior says in the Gospel: “Now the prince of this world will be cast out.” For the humility of Christ and the perseverance of the saints have greatly restrained the boldness of demons, so that they can rightly be called instigators of evils, but by no means compellers. Nor would the devil prevail against us unless he received strength from our sins. Hence Scripture warns us, saying: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.” And again: “Do not give place to the devil.”

Therefore, in concluding this discourse for the understanding of the proposed question, why God permits so many acts of maleficium to occur, I believe I have made the reasons sufficiently clear. For even if the will of demons is always wicked, their power can never be unjust, since what they wickedly will they have from their own choice, but what they are able wickedly to do they have from God alone. And these things have been said insofar as they concern those who do evil. But why does almighty God permit demons to harm, through the agency of witches, people who have been baptized in the name of the Holy Trinity and faithfully believe in his only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus

Christ? For if they could harm only the wicked, the perverse, and unbelievers in body and possessions, but were kept away from approach to and injury of the good and faithful, the judgment of this permission would rightly seem to be just. But now more often the good and innocent are weakened by the demons’ acts of maleficium and sometimes are even deprived of life, to pass over in silence the loss of temporal goods which those bewitched often suffer. It troubles many people why the Almighty permits these things, he who permits nothing unjustly.

For this question now proposed, I shall briefly set out in order, as far as I can, the necessary reasons, no others than those I have received from the ancient and Catholic teachers. The first reason, then, is the testing of Christians’ faith and the proving of their integrity, so that, when scourged by the demons’ maleficia, they may learn in the sufferings of the flesh what faith and love for God they possess in the hidden part of the mind. In prosperity it is not difficult to be long-suffering, but in adversity to await the Savior’s help with humble patience is not the mark of the soft, but of the strong and of truly faithful Christians. In his own tribulation most holy Job learned how great a confidence of faith he had in God, he who previously, in tranquillity, had preserved piety without the dart of temptation.

At the same time it should be noted from this that those whom the maleficia of demons or human beings have in any way made ill, and who, in order to recover health, either approach or consult malefici and enchanters, or do this either through themselves or through others against the precepts of God and the Church, have neither true faith in God nor love. For they put the health of mortal flesh before God, and seek salvation not from Christ, but from the devil and his minister. They prefer to live healthy, but unhappily, in the name of the devil, rather than to suffer a little here for love of Christ and afterward rejoice with him forever.

But someone who does not approach malefici because God has forbidden it, preferring to live in poverty and sickness while observing God’s commandments rather than against God in wealth and bodily health, truly loves God and rightly preserves the integrity of the Christian faith. Among Christians, most sacred Caesar, people corrupt in mind and reprobate concerning the faith are not to be tolerated: those who cure the bewitched by maleficia, who reveal thefts and adulteries through the ministry of demons, and who foretell future things by inspecting stones or mirrors.
