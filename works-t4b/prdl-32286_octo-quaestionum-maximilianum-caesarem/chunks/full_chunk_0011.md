It is therefore clear that if any miracles occur among unbelievers, they could not occur by God’s gift or by the ministry of the holy angels, since all such people are placed outside the unity of the Church, and signs cannot be worked by the impious through public righteousness. For God, as has been said, does not remove his original wrath from a person except through faith in Jesus Christ; but everyone who is unbelieving toward the Son of God will not see eternal life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. John 3.

Therefore everyone upon whom the wrath of God remains cannot please God; and so, if any wonders are done at his prayers, they should be believed to have been performed not by God’s power nor by the ministry of angels, but rather by the agency of demons. The third worker of wonders is the devil, who, like an ape, longs to imitate whatever he sees; and he deceives the minds of men, especially unbelievers, with various illusions, and seduces those who believe him by a certain likeness of miracles. For I do not judge it difficult for demons to be able to delude the senses of men, since it is agreed that by nature they were created subtler than all mortals. But they cannot know the thoughts of mortals except by a kind of conjecture, namely when we make our inward things manifest by certain signs. Demons foretell future things, and sometimes speak truths,

sometimes they are deceived in their conjectures, and they deceive others. From the beginning of the world they have known certain signs that precede events, and by experience and practice with these they sometimes foretell truly what is going to happen; for when they have seen the signs known to them that precede an event, they at once conjecture what is in a person’s thought. Yet they very often deceive, because they themselves are deceived.

For when they are questioned about some matter that they do not know, for example whether or on what day a messenger sent far away will soon return, in the blink of an eye they immediately reach the place where they know him to be. When they see that he has already begun the journey back, they carefully count the day’s stages; and, returning at once to the questioner, they affirm that the absent man will certainly come on a fixed day. But if he either delays on the road or turns aside for other reasons, then the demons, deceived in their own judgment, must necessarily be wrong.

They gladly involve themselves in predictions, and they are quite ready to foretell future things to human beings for the sake of honor and esteem, because they especially desire to be regarded as worthy of admiration. They seek divine honors and greatly desire to be feared by mortals. Just as boys hiding in a secret place or running about in masks suddenly burst out from their hiding places and shake with unexpected terror their companions who suspect nothing of the sort,

and then hide themselves again so as not to be recognized, and when they see the unknowing ones terrified they boast, as though they seemed to have gained great honor from this; after their example certain demons, more closely bound to the earth and very restless in their dealings around human beings, whom the ancients called bacucei, frighten people at night and seek nothing more from mortals than the esteem of divinity.

Hence also, in the presence of those before whom they are permitted to appear in some likeness, they sometimes perform various delusions; and they show themselves as terrifying in people’s minds, as occasion allows, whenever they can. They gladly take on the likeness of a human body, but they do not always find suitable airy matter with which to clothe themselves. Therefore they quite often appear monstrous and horrible, because they have not had suitable humors or vapors for the perfection of the appearance they wanted.

Moreover, the more they are bound to earthly substances, the duller they have become through the accident of matter. Hence, because of the ignorance they contracted through disobedience, the predictions of demons are frequently false, although by divine permission they are sometimes also verified.
