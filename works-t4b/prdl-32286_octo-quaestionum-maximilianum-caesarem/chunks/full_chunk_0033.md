QUESTION SEVEN.

The second reason why almighty God permits the innocent to be harmed by sorcery is the increase of merits: namely, so that, when their patience has been tested in tribulation, it may more worthily deserve the crown of glory in heaven. For, as holy Augustine says, a person is unknown to himself as to what he can bear and what he cannot. Sometimes he fears that he cannot bear what he nevertheless can; when temptation arrives, as if some examiner were questioning him, he is discovered by himself, who before, hidden, did not know himself.

The blessed chief apostle Peter, before temptation came upon him, was hidden both from himself and from others as being thoughtless and weak; that temptation came, and the weakness that lay hidden appeared. Therefore temptation is the greatest testing of a person, as sacred Scripture attests: “The furnace tests the potter’s vessels, and the trial of tribulation tests just people.” Patience is easy in prosperity, when no adversities strike the mind. “But the tribulations of the just are many; from all these our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.”

The third reason for divine permission is the preservation of humility, so that a faithful and just person may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or justice. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: “They are not in the labor of men, and they will not be scourged with men. Therefore pride has held them; they are covered with iniquity; from their impiety, as if from fat, their iniquity has come forth.” It belongs to the mercy of God that he sometimes permits good and just people to be shaken by adversities, whom he knows cannot be humbled in prosperity. For tribulation has made many humble whom favorable fortune would have made proud.

Finally, the fearful imagining of sorceries makes a good person fear his God and diligently call upon his help, so that, fortified by it against the assaults of demons, he may be able to trust that he is safe. But let no one who is proud and presumptuous of his own strength think himself safe from the sorceries of demons, because the person whom, swollen with pride, God’s protection abandons undoubtedly lies open to the sorceries of demons.

The fourth reason is so that there may be a distinction between the good and the evil. For Scripture says in the Acts of the Apostles concerning good people beloved by God: “Through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God.”

And Saint Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: “All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution.” The most wise King Solomon also says in Proverbs: “Whom the Lord loves, he corrects, and as a father he is pleased with his son.” It is a Christian thing, and not without glory, to endure adversities patiently for God’s love, whether they are brought on by evil people or by demons. For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and just people not for their ruin, as the perverse suppose, but rather for the very great and certain consolation of the soul.

Indeed, when a just person lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does wrong to no one, loves, fears, and venerates his God with his whole heart, keeping his commandments with all care, and is nevertheless afflicted by sorceries, whether in body, in possessions, or in both, that person is truly blessed; for when he has been tested, he will receive the crown of life that God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the Catholic Church of Christians, and who, receiving evils in this world, patiently endures them for God’s love, has a sure sign that he will persevere in God’s grace and be saved.
