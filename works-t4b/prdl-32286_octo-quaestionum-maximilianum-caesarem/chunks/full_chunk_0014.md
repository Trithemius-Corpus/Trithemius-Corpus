There is a great multitude of enchanters and necromancers in the East, who by their illusions miserably deceive the human race, and long ago turned almost all Asia, both Greater and Lesser, away from the faith of Christ. Illusions can be produced in three ways: by the ministry of demons, by the operation of nature, and by the deception of human art. No true Christian will doubt that the miracles of unbelievers occur in one of these ways, when the definite root of the worker is established.

Today, moreover, there are many among Christians who are devoted to the works of demons: enchanters, witches, and men reprobate concerning the faith, who deceive many by excessive curiosity and promise impossibilities by their arts. But by the involvement of demons wondrous things sometimes occur, whenever, that is, a man not sufficiently and rightly grounded in the faith does or desires something other than befits a Christian, even if he does not believe it to be any sin; and although this ought not to happen, nevertheless the devil, intruding himself, often supplies the desired effect.

QUESTION THREE.

Such is that which those suffering from spasms commonly use. On the sacred day of Parasceve they make a ring of any metal during the office, and afterward always wear it on the heart-finger. These things are utterly vain and have no root of truth at all. Yet I cannot deny that I have seen certain people cured of spasm by this same vanity. Likewise, I know that certain very strong locks have been opened by words and herbs, which I do not think was done either by the power of God or by the force of the things applied.

I could introduce innumerable similar things that I have seen and heard; what else are their manifest effects but certain miracles of demons? Finally, there are some excessively curious people who stubbornly place faith and confidence in characters, slips of paper, rings, figures, necklaces, and unknown words, and often obtain wondrous effects. We do not doubt in the least that the devil has supplied these, not by the merit of holiness nor by the mercy granted by God, but rather by faithless credulity. For demons, having the greatest envy of the human race, readily come to the vain operations of the impious in order to destroy the souls of mortals. Alas, how many Christians today, how many clerics, how many priests, to spare their betters, use many and various superstitions in their daily actions; and in these implicit pacts with demons are equated, to the dishonor of the Christian faith and to their own condemnation. And because these evils have now multiplied excessively among Christians, the Almighty has withdrawn his hand and handed them over into the power of a faithless people, so that they may feel them as exactors of punishments, since by imitation they served persistently not the living and true God, but vain superstitions.

Moreover, we shall clearly recognize that wondrous things are done by the substitution of demons, if we inquire into the truth more carefully. Jews, Saracens, pagans, or gentiles, who do not believe in our God Jesus Christ, if they perform any miracles, we believe these are done not by the power of God, not by the ministry of angels, not by the merit of holiness, but by the substitution of demons.
