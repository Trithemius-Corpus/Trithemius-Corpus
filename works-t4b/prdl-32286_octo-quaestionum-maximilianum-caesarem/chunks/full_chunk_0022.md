Hence no one among mortals is good except by the counsel of his own will, aided by the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil except by his own accord, deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin. By uprightness of will we are made like the good spirits, if by holy and necessary works we prove the piety of the faith that we have in Christ. And just as the consummation of a good will makes good people equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious makes them like demons. Hence evil and perverse people, the more they have been depraved by the malice of their own will, become so much the more like demons in affection.

Through likeness, moreover, the familiarity of friendship is introduced, from which mutual confidence is gradually generated. From the confidence of the lesser in the greater arises command, whether it is true or simulated by cunning. Therefore, if wicked people seem to have any command over demons, it arises from the greatness of their depravity: namely, when their affection is so far depraved, and by a reprobate will they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons that join with them. or in intelligence; rather, one is much craftier than another in quickness of understanding, and far worse in depraved affection. For those who, falling from their high seats, are more closely joined to the earth and inhabit caves and caverns are certainly duller than the others, to such a degree that they fear swords and rods, and seem to be moved by the threats of men who terrify them, as may be observed in those whom we call the possessed. But the demons who dwell farther away from the earth are subtler by nature and meet with human beings more rarely.

People full of malice, however, the more they advance in perversity of will, become so much the more like demons. Once admitted by them into familiarity, they then become bold, and receive over the demons whose familiarity they enjoy a command not of violent power, but of feigned simulation. For demons, in order to keep those people whom they have once received into surrender voluntary in their depravity, gladly subject themselves to them, as though moved by a simulated domination, and what they do willingly they pretend to do as though compelled to obedience. Indeed, no one obtains true command over demons unless faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made him acceptable and dear to God. Jesus, Scripture says: “And he gave them power over all demons.” Therefore evil people command demons: people whom malevolence has made like them, likeness has made familiar to them, and familiarity has made their own and subject to them. It is as if, with a great prince, as often happens, some servant born in a humble station, joined to him by long familiarity, is said to command him, because he obtains from him, without contradiction, everything he has asked for, so that he seems more to rule his lord than to be ruled by him.

In accordance with this likeness, evil and reprobate people, who are admitted by demons into heartfelt familiarity and become very bold through prolonged confidence, receive over them a simulated, not to say familiar, command, which they have merited by likeness of a most depraved will. Hence whatever they request from demons who join with them by an agreement, whether implicit or explicit, they receive, unless at times, by divine ordinance, the demons are forbidden and cannot provide what is asked. Yet they do not everywhere join in this way with all reprobate and evil people, but only with those with whom, through the intervention of some compact, they have entered into covenants: such as witches, who have rendered homage to the devil, and those invokers of demons whom in common usage we call necromancers.
