But the human intellect, since it was created in all ignorance and privation, and was destined for the body for its own exercise and by degrees, must by continual action, motion, and art come to the knowledge of God and of all things. The end will at last make it equal to the angelic intellect, changing its potency into act and bringing it forth from mere potency and possibility into essence; from empty it will make it full, from imperfect perfect, from deprived possessed, from dark luminous, from nocturnal diurnal, from lunar solar, and from earthly heavenly. This cannot happen while the mind is bound in the body, as the Christ-loving Saint Paul says: As long as we are in the flesh, we are away from the Lord.

To know God belongs to a pure, simple, and perfect intellect; but the human intellect, so long as it dwells in the body, is impure, mixed, unstable, and imperfect, and for this reason, without faith, wholly ignorant of the knowledge of God and of heavenly things. The object of each intellect, both human and angelic, is the same: that highest and immutable good, God, the one principle of all things, to which they return by a natural desire for knowledge. But the angelic intellect, if compared with the human, is as nature is to art, and as simple intellect is to reason, which touches the principles of learning; but reason, through

Hence it is manifest that the angelic intellect immovably apprehends its proper object, to which the human intellect, through the angelic medium, after many exercises of its own, as has been said, at last, by reasoning, prepares an ascent for itself. The human intellect, however, is separated, by the medium of angelic knowledge, from the perfection of its natural object, and therefore is necessarily mobile, returning through that medium to its own principle. For every motion proceeds from an extreme through a medium to an extreme; but where there is no medium, the extremes are already immediate and near, and for this reason immovable.

But the human intellect, placed as it were in a dark prison, as long as it is held in the body, is moved by sense and reason toward understanding, though confusedly and all too imperfectly, and therefore it has a medium between itself and God, which we have called angelic. But God, existing far above every position, remains subject neither to mortal senses nor to reason, just as he is read to have said to Moses: Man shall not see me and live. Hence also the divine evangelist John says: No one has ever seen God. Nor is it any wonder. For since God is the first and eternal intellect, most pure, most simple, most perfect, immovable, incomprehensible creator, highest, and far above every position immense and uncircumscribable, he could not be comprehended by the human intellect, which is impure, subject to composition, imperfect, mobile, created, comprehensible, lowest, and constituted under the measure of position.
