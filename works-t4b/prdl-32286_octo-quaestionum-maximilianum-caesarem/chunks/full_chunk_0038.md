JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF SPANHEIM

He produced all things, as sacred Scripture again bears witness: “But your providence, Father, governs all things from the beginning.” Therefore let those men blush who are corrupt in mind, impious, and reprobate concerning the Christian faith, who, puffed up with vain talkativeness, take away the providence of him who knows all things and do not consider how great an injury they do to God.

For what, to use the judgment of Firmianus, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he foresees nothing, if he neglects lower things, if he does not care for mortal things, he has already lost all dignity. Yet, as Saint Anselm attests, it is impossible for God to lose his honor; for, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, no corruption, and no sin. Therefore whoever removes providence removes the whole substance of God; and what is he saying except that God does not exist?

But let those who deny that God’s providence cares for lower things now tell me this. If God neglects lower things, let them consider what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is no longer any reason why we should pursue him with worship or venerate him with religion, since from him we obtain no benefit; rather, being neglected and despised, let us neglect him ourselves. But if, not knowing us, he despises and neglects us, then there is plainly no reason why we should worship one who does not know us and, for that reason, cannot bestow any good, or invoke him with prayers.

QUESTION EIGHT

For if God does not care for human affairs, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every reason for piety ceases, since there is no hope of salvation. What consideration of piety can there be among mortals, what hope for the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human affairs? Indeed, if providence is taken away, whom do we worship? whom do we adore? If there is no providence for mortals with God, surely he neither bestows benefits on human beings, nor imposes punishments on deserters; he neither can help mortals, nor wishes to, nor cares at all, nor notices what we do, and he hears neither the prayers nor the petitions of anyone.

Why, then, do we offer him any worship? Why honor, and why prayers? Or why do we so greatly dread or fear a God who does not know us, who, once we have been neglected and despised by him, can do us neither good nor harm? For once providence has been removed, neither holiness nor religion will endure; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disturbance of life and a dreadful confusion will follow. Once piety toward God has been removed, what will the commonwealth be but everlasting confusion? Public trust among human beings, the fellowship of the human race, and justice will necessarily perish; all these will vanish and perish together.

What must be thought of the Christian faith, if God does not care for human affairs? What, then, of the savior of all, our Lord Jesus Christ? Unless there is a providence of God caring for mortal things, one will regard him as nothing but an empty tale and a wholly fictitious thing. Therefore whatever good we do in this life, whatever evil we endure for the love of God, is all vain and useless to us if he has no care for us at all.

Let those who deny the providence of God now see and be horrified, when they behold the sane outcome of their own opinion: the worst despair of all. Therefore let no one, clothed in the vain speech of secular philosophers, train himself to contempt of God; let no one remove the providence of the highest and unchangeable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of, and ungrateful for, the benefits of the most merciful savior.

For it must be firmly believed, and in no way doubted, that this whole universe, both visible and invisible, and all things individually, whether they are in heaven or on earth or in the sea, are ruled and governed by divine providence, and that absolutely nothing in the whole universe is alien to his care. For crops, too, and all the other things that the earth brings forth, as well as storms and the variations of the weather and of the sky, [unclear].
