But saints or angels, because they are not varied by passions, always appear uniformly in male form. For nowhere do we read it written that a good spirit was seen in the form of a woman or of any beast, but always in a male form. Demons, however, have often been seen around rivers and springs in the form of women, sometimes dancing, sometimes spreading out their hair in the manner of women, and sometimes speaking with human beings and practicing various deceptions. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have dealings with witches.

The fifth kind is called subterranean, because it dwells in caves and caverns and in the remote hollows of mountains. These demons are the worst in disposition, and they especially attack those who dig wells and mines, and those who seek treasures hidden in the earth, being most ready for the destruction of the human race. They make openings in the earth, stir up flame-breathing winds, and shake the foundations of buildings. Sometimes, coming out from the mountains by night in troops, they lead marvelous and astonishing dances in the fields; and, as though fearing the command of one leader,

they suddenly vanish at a signal and return to their hiding places. At times the sound of bells is heard among them, and sometimes they falsely claim that they are the spirits of human beings who have departed this life. They seek nothing more than the fear and amazement of human beings. Hence we have found that they have sometimes led certain simpler people into their hiding places in the mountains, showing astonishing sights to those who marveled; and, as though the dwellings of the blessed were there, they falsely claim to be friends of the living.

Among these demons, too, some brood over the treasures that the greed of mortals has hidden in the earth; and lest they come again into human use, they carry them off, steal them, guard them, and sometimes transfer them from place to place. And this kind of demon is not accustomed to have any dealings with women’s witchcraft.

The sixth kind of demons is called light-fleeing, because they especially dread and detest the light: for they never appear by day, nor can they assume a body in any way except by night. This kind of demon does not consort with witches, nor can it be constrained by incantations, because it flees both light and the voices of human beings, and likewise every disturbance of the air.

The aerial kind is especially accustomed to come to women’s witchcraft, because it is subtler than all the others and of the boldest rashness. Some of the terrestrial demons also, as has been said, serve acts of witchcraft; yet, being held to be of a lower rank, they cannot bring about those greater acts of witchcraft. It is therefore plainly established that the whole power of witches, by which they do such astonishing things, is granted to demons within limits set by the Lord God; and when he does not permit it, they accomplish nothing.
