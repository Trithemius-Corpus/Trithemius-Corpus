Hence the holy prelate Augustine says: If the disease were slight, the physician would be despised, not sought; if the physician were not sought, the disease would not be ended. Therefore where sin abounded, grace also abounded, which alone distinguishes the redeemed from the lost, whom the mass of perdition, drawn from the origin of creation, heaps together into one. Thus the mercy of the Lord is over all his works.

The fourth reason for divine permission is that the justice of the Creator may be declared against those who have sinned and have not done penance; for if everyone acted well and no one sinned, God’s justice would have nothing to punish. Therefore, so that justice may have its place, the creature sins by divine permission. For the Lord is just, and he will repay each person according to the works of his will.

The fifth reason is the dignity of the human condition, because if God did not permit man to sin, he would seem worse off than brute animals, which, with no violence compelling them, freely use the motions of nature. For although brute animals lack reason, nevertheless they freely possess the motion of nature which they follow; in comparison with them man would seem to be of an inferior condition if he did not freely have the choice of his will. But God permitted man a free will,

which neither kings nor princes command. The sixth reason is the praise due to the merit of steadfast human virtue, when we praise a just man because, although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, with God’s grace assisting him, remained steadfast in a good will.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits demons to do such great evil deeds through the intervention of witches is that those who, having despised the truth, delight in falsehood may be seduced and utterly deceived by the devil, the inventor of falsehood. For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who scorn to follow the saving teachings of Christ, should be deceived by demons and all plunged down to hell to be punished forever. They seek help from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have worshiped. For it is just that one who, of his own will, abandons the truth and adheres to falsehood should be hurled by the devil into destruction.

The eighth cause is the manifestation of divine power, whose might and virtue are above every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits the devil great things and denies him certain small things,

he shows how weak the enemy of the human race is, and that he is subject in all things to the divine majesty. For the devil can do nothing by himself, but everything he does, he does with God permitting it.

Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God permits evil deeds to be done, is justice: by it he preserves, in inviolable order, the freedom that he granted to the creature once at the beginning. But because the power of every creature, dependent on its Creator, is restricted and limited, it is manifest that neither the devil can do everything he wishes, nor can a human being, however depraved in will. The will is indeed free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wished, nothing among human affairs would remain safe, but they would make all things totter. Job 18.

The story of holy Job plainly demonstrates by example the bound power of demons: Satan did not dare to tempt him more than had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if they were always able to use freely, according to their own judgment, the power to harm.
