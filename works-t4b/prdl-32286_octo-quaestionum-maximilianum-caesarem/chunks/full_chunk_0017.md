Still asleep, he crossed over; he stepped on all of us with his feet, and we felt his weight no more than if a tiny mouse had touched us. Wherever his sleeping body moved, all the fastenings of the doors immediately opened of their own accord. He passed through the higher parts of the house with the greatest speed, and, like a sparrow, clung to the roofs.

I speak of things seen, not heard by vague report. These seem to be offices of the soul, not of the body; by its natural power, so to speak, it uses them almost freely when, the senses of the flesh being bound, it wanders outside the dwelling-place of the body. But since this matter is not in every respect suited to the question proposed, let us defer the treatment of the causes of these marvels to another time. For we do not think such things marvelous to the wise, who have very well understood the power and nobility of the human mind, since, hindered only by the interposition of the body, it is in some respect judged equal to the angels.

Finally, we pass over the various modes and kinds of fascinations, by which a frenzied person works very threatening effects upon external things. The imagination of one who is most strongly confident can also do much toward producing marvelous effects, whether it is directed toward a true object or toward an object merely supposed. Hence, since there are so many kinds and modes of working wonders, it is not surprising if Jews,

Jews, Saracens, pagans, gentiles, and the rest who are placed outside the faith of Christ, whatever unbelievers they may be, since they cannot perform miracles by public righteousness, carry out marvels either by the assistance of demons or by artful industry.

Nor does it escape me that by the same reasoning the miracles of the saints are attacked by the impious, as though they were done not by the power of the Creator, but rather by the involvement or supposition of the devil. Whoever truly believes that the Christian faith alone is true does not doubt that all the miracles of unbelievers are accomplished through the cooperation of demons. But if anyone rashly and obstinately thinks the contrary, he shows himself to be an unbeliever, not a Christian.

and in many places full of enigmas? For many things are necessary for the more perfect understanding of the faith which are not found sufficiently expressed in the divine Scriptures.

To this most sacred question of your Majesty it seems one must reply: Every Scripture divinely inspired is a perfect work of divinity, foreordained before all time, written in time, in no other way than God willed, who inspired men that it should be written. All the works of God are perfect; and therefore, since the Scripture of which we are speaking is a work of God, it is necessary to confess it perfect in all things.

For God, the supreme judge, whose works are all perfect, bestowed upon his Scriptures, in order, what he willed and as he willed; and he undoubtedly infused them into those receiving them, so that they might be written in due measure. Almighty God bestowed such authority and majesty upon sacred Scripture that, in comparison with it, all the words of this world fail, since divine things cannot properly be comprehended by human speech.

Therefore divine Scripture, which on the surface of its verses may appear confused to the unlearned, seems manifest to those who understand it; and unless one reads it by that Spirit by which it is believed to have been infused into men, one by no means arrives at its very understanding. Otherwise
