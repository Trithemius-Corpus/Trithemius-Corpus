Otherwise, if anything could be done when he did not will and consent to it, it would necessarily follow that God was weak, since he could not prevent what he would be forced to permit against his will. But since every human being, insofar as he is a creature, depends on God, he can do nothing without the will of him from whom he has both the will and the power.

Whatever witches do, therefore, is done by God permitting it; without his will they can do nothing. For unless God had granted that they could do what they wish, they would obtain it neither from themselves nor from demons. Thus all power is from the Lord God, in whom all things exist and are moved, as in the first cause of all things; for from him from whom we have received freedom of choice, from him also we have the power to do what we will.

But since the understanding of witches is very confused, they do not perpetrate the evils they do by human art, but by the cooperation of demons. Nature has made these demons subtle, and long experience has made them swift in producing wonders. For the wondrous effects of witches are not caused by such means as they use for witchcraft, such as heads of the dead, toads, serpents, characters, images, and the like, but by the devil who instituted these signs; since he is most proud, he desires in all things to be seen as comparable to God.

QUESTION SIX.

Indeed, just as almighty God instituted certain sacraments in his Church, which, when worthily administered, signify and produce a saving effect with his own assistance, he who instituted them working salvation, so also the devil, who desires nothing more than to be thought equal to God, instituted certain signs as sacraments. When invoked by those with whom he has been joined by pacts, the malignant worker assists them, and as often as God permits, he brings about the evil that is requested; if God does not permit it, he does absolutely nothing.

For the devil is the worker of all the effects of necromancers and witches. By applying the means which he himself first instituted to the subject matter, he produces the desired work, sometimes in reality, sometimes only in appearance. But witch-women, who have now passed by depraved will and consent into the fellowship of demons through pacts, receive from them certain marks and symbolic signs, by which the demons, once summoned, immediately appear. In these destructive operations of demons nothing is stronger than the foolish credulity of witches, which is increased by pacts and vows; for the firmer the faith they have placed either in demons or in their signs, the more swiftly they obtain

the operation, as the demons cooperate with them. For the too-savage depravity of the witches’ will turns them in a certain way into frenzy; and when this has seized their mind, the demons immediately attach themselves to them in their madness and, invited by pacts and signs, with God permitting it, furnish the effect of destruction to them in their insanity.

Plato recounts something similar in the book On Poetic Frenzy, saying: “Just as Bacchant women, when not of sound mind, draw honey and milk from rivers, but when of sound mind cannot draw them. Their spirit, carried outside itself into fellowship with an evil demon, brings this about.” We have ascertained that witches, at certain times, have drawn butter from stirred-up rivers; this cannot in any way be done otherwise than by the ministry of a demon.

In the same way, when they have been led into hatred of some person or animal, and sigh for its destruction, they are driven by a most grievous frenzy, thinking of nothing in their mind except vengeance.
