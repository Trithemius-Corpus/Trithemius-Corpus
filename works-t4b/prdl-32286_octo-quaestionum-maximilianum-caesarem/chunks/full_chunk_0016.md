Hence, if miracles had been wholly lacking to Muhammad as he was persuading an uncultivated people to a new religion, perhaps no one would have put faith in him. For he too, both by the subtlety of his intellect and by the cooperation of the devil, showed certain wonders to the peoples, so that he might seem to have confirmed a new doctrine also by new miracles.

Miracles were not lacking even to pagan idolaters, if we do not deny the histories. The Jews, however, before the incarnation of the Son of God, receiving his law through Moses, were frequently illumined by divine miracles. But after the Lord’s resurrection, when the Mosaic law had been made void, those who do not believe in the only-begotten Son of God remain under the wrath of his indignation, and all miracles of truth have ceased among them. Hence, if they perform any wonders today, they are the works of demons and manifest signs of error.

For demons keep watch with the greatest zeal so that those men whom they have once made to wander from the way of truth, which is Christ, they may, by continuing their fraud, make obstinate in error even unto death. Indeed, when God for just reason withdraws his grace from unbelievers, their deceiver, the malignant spirit, approaches unrecognized; and, when he sees that salvation is far away, by false miracles he seduces the minds of the ignorant as though he were the Lord present,

For now demons, lying hidden in ambush, long since defeated and confounded by the trophy of Christ, fear to appear openly as they did before the incarnation of the Lord; and therefore among unbelievers they falsely present themselves as good spirits.

There are also some who add a fourth to the three modes set forth above, saying that the mind, or the spirit of the human being itself, can naturally perform wonders, provided that it knows how to recall itself from every adventitious thing back into itself, above sense, into unity. For those who assert such things promise to do wonders of nature, to predict the future, to disclose people’s secrets, to remove diseases, and suddenly to change people’s plans. But this mode of miracles cannot be simply natural, certain, common, and infallible; for if it is true, which I have not experienced, it is necessary that something of an external power should now intervene, and that either a good angel or a malignant spirit should occupy the one thinking in some part. But if none of the external powers mingles itself in, the one prophesying must form the principle of his speaking either from conjectures or from bold presumption,

Hence I would not wholly oppose them if they grant that our confused and unformed intellect is aided by an equal ministry.

For we see among those who, contemplating the measure of the flesh, often pass beyond themselves because of an inner love and desire for Jesus, that they are lifted upward from the earth into the air; and we think that they exceed bodily weight not merely by their own keenness of mind, but also by divine power. Therefore the three principles of the aforementioned opinion, unknown to many, in my judgment satisfy few, namely, that the mind, while placed in the body, should by its own power produce wonderful effects outside itself.

For what then? Let anyone ignorant of nature tell me whether bodily weight can be lightened by the operation of the mind. With two men bearing witness together with me, I report a thing experienced. When I was pursuing the studies of letters as a youth, one night four of us were sleeping in one bed. A contemporary of mine rose from my side while asleep, as he was accustomed to do, and, with his eyes closed and the fifteenth-day moon shining in, walked about through the whole house as though awake.
