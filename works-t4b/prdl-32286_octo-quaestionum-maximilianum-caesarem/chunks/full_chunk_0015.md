The devil uses this substitution all the more freely insofar as he is less hindered by the faith of Christ and the sacraments of the Church. For the wrath of God remains upon all unbelievers who do not believe in his only-begotten Son; and they are in the power of the devil, and therefore he thrusts himself upon those who invoke him in place of God. Hence, blinded in their errors, they invoke God, in whose Son they do not believe; and when he disdains to hear them while they remain under wrath, the devil, the false worker of miracles, comes near. Thus they believe the devil’s mockeries, who scorn to believe the true miracles of God. From demons they receive false miracles suited to their errors, because they have refused to believe the truth, that is, Christ. For there are many among those unbelievers who worship one God, the creator of all things, do not adore idols, and firmly believe that they will be saved in their own faith. But because they scorn to believe the mysteries of the Lord’s incarnation, the wrath of God remains upon them; and they vanish away in their follies, thinking God near to them, while in those things in which they do not believe rightly, they are subjected to demons. they are confirmed in their errors by a lie. It may not be irksome to introduce, O Caesar, a likeness of this matter. There was a certain man, a keeper of swine, who had enclosed in a staff a little written sheet containing the name of the blessed martyr Blasius. He placed so much faith in it that, when the staff was fixed in the ground in the middle of the herd, he believed every wolf was prevented from approaching. When this foolish confidence had at length been strengthened by long experience, the man went securely wherever he wished, leaving the swine in the wood without danger, since no wolf now had access to them. It happened that a passerby saw this in the shepherd’s absence; and, perceiving that a demon had charge of guarding the herd, he said to him: “What are you doing here, wretched one?” He answered: “I am guarding these swine.” The other said: “Who entrusted the care of the swine to you?” Again he answered: “The foolish confidence of the shepherd. For he thinks that a certain little sheet enclosed in the staff is of great power, by which, through the merits of some Blasius, wolves are kept away from the swine. But he has sinned against his own law and follows our vanities. Therefore, since the one invoked does not appear, I, lest the man lose his confidence, guard his swine and willingly substitute myself for Blasius.” Thus, he said, mortals trouble us with vain conjurations, so that unless we wish to lose our reputation and honor among them, it is altogether

necessary that we very often come to the aid of their foolish invocations. By a likeness of this fact demons deceive miserable unbelievers, and the miracles that they ask of God in prayer are often obtained by the substitution of the more malign spirits. For in those things that concern the worship or honor of God, demons gladly thrust themselves in; and those who cannot obtain manifest honors are, of course, delighted by hidden ones. At the same time it should be noted that from the beginning of the world miracles of outstanding excellence have never been lacking to any religion or sect, although they have not all been done from one principle. For whatever miracles were done in the true religion were done from no other source than God. The law of nature had its miracles; the written law had them; and now grace also has them. Almighty God, who gave religion, himself also works miracles. But in false religions, where truth is not worshiped, if any miracles are done, it is not God but the devil who works them. For since God is truth, he did not establish false religions; and therefore he has never adorned a lie with his miracles. Muhammad, who, filled with an evil demon, established a false religion, adorned it with false miracles not from God but by the cooperation of demons. For the demon who gave the cause of error also gave the miracle, when permission was secretly received from [unclear].
