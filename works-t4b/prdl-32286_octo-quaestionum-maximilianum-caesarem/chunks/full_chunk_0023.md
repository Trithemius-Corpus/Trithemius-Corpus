FOURTH QUESTION

... whom the common people call necromancers.

There are two kinds of men who can lawfully command demons. The first is that of those men who, beloved by God through the merit of holiness, have received from him the power to trample the head of the ancient serpent, such as in the primitive Church were the apostles and their followers and disciples, of whom the Lord speaks in the holy Gospel: “In my name they shall cast out demons,” and “they shall take up serpents.”

The second kind is that of those men to whom, by reason of their order in the Church of God, it is granted to drive demons from human bodies by the power of the ministry they have received. And this ministry is entrusted to exorcists in the Church of God when, that is, they are ordained by the bishop.

Again, there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons; and the utterly wicked conditions they accept show how unlawfully they presume to do so. The first and most pernicious kind is that of witches, who enter into pacts with demons and, after making a solemn profession, hand themselves over to some demon as his property and for perpetual service. What great evils this most pernicious kind of human beings commits in your empire, most wise Caesar, no one can express.

For first of all, denying God, the faith of Christ, and sacred baptism, they offer themselves in mind and body to demons; by their witchcraft they sicken, harm, and kill men and livestock. There is no disease which, God permitting, they cannot bring on, because, when in observance of the covenanted pact they present themselves as servants to demons, by alliance with them they dare whatever they wish.

Therefore they are not to be tolerated anywhere, but rather are to be utterly exterminated in every place, as God the creator of all commands: “You shall not suffer witches to live.” Some witches summon demons by spells; others entice them by prayers and sacrifices; the rest, provoking them by unspeakable acts and ceremonies, draw them into the service of their impiety.

It is therefore no wonder that witches seem to command demons, to whom they have become like through the impiety of a perverse will, and with whom they are in harmony through mutual association for the harm of the human race.

The second kind of depraved men who desire to command demons is that of those whom we commonly call necromancers. They profess arts full of execrations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly visible, enclosed in a crystal or some other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of lying and superstition, commit such great evils that no good person could imagine them. For, in order to entice demons into obedience to themselves, they generally use the most shameful sacrifices and enter into an explicit pact with them.

They carry about many varied little books full of filth, impiety, and falsehood, falsely inscribed with the names of ancient philosophers and wise men; by displaying these they deceive many unwary and curious people and drag them with themselves into the pit of demons. They promise great and almost incredible things, and by hearing these things men are deceitfully held suspended in wonder.

They say that by rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations they can compel malignant demons to appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal, or in some other receptacle, and to answer distinctly all questions put to them.
