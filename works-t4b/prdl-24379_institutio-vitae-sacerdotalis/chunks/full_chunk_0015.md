For what is better for a man than if, by long reading, he fixes his mind there, whence life comes to him? Therefore continual study must be devoted to reading the Scriptures, because reading nourishes the mind and supplies nourishment to the virtues.

Therefore, so that you may not lose the value of time, read the sacred Scriptures, write books, persist in devout prayers, and at the proper times also work with your hands. Do not be ashamed to learn what you do not know, because then you will be able usefully to instruct others, if you always seek to be informed by teachers. Do not be ashamed to take on the name of disciple, if you desire to attain to the honor of mastery. For the Holy Spirit, who teaches man knowledge, is a lover of humility.

When you sit at table to take food, remember first that the sacred letters are to be sounded forth, so that while the flesh is fed, the mind may not be deprived of its own nourishment. For in the Council of Toledo it is read as follows: Out of reverence for God and for priests, the whole synod established this, that since idle tales are often accustomed to be introduced at meals, reading from the divine Scriptures should be mingled with every priestly banquet. For through this souls are built up toward the good, and tales are not prohibited by necessity. For the word of God is the food of the spirit, because man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God. The ancient fathers and priests devoted the greatest diligence to the holy Scriptures, so that they might healthfully feed the mind and provide useful things for the instruction of posterity. Let us follow in their footsteps, so that we may deserve to be found worthy ministers before God.

On Avoiding Avarice and Cupidity. Chapter 5.

Although at the beginning of our discourse we argued against avarice and cupidity, nevertheless we are led by a certain reason to speak again in this place about this very matter. For action is never sufficiently taken against this vice unless it is uprooted from the foundation. But our Savior, knowing how difficult it is for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God, said to those who followed him: Unless someone renounces all that he possesses, he cannot be my disciple. Hence the psalmist admonishes us, saying: If riches abound, do not set your heart upon them. And the apostle says: Having food and clothing, let us be content with these. What more do we seek, we who are ministers of God? Greed is shameful in a priest of God; shameful is avarice, and damnable the continual hunger for gold.

Therefore, most beloved brother, if you desire to live in freedom of spirit, do not seek perishable money; do not love riches. Cast your thought upon the Lord, and he will nourish you; he himself will feed you when you are hungry; he himself will guard you. Nor do I wish you to cast off domestic provision entirely, but that you not go beyond measure through avarice. The root of all evils is cupidity.
