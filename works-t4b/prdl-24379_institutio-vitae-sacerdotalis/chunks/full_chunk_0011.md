Therefore the beginnings of temptations must be resisted, because lust is never better conquered than if it is avoided by flight. For if through habit it has once entangled you, it never lets you go free without the special grace of God.

If you hesitate to put trust in my words, read the examples of the elders. But there is no need to seek examples in books, when you can find proofs before your eyes. Look: we know many old priests today whose lives we know to be incontinent. We see age growing old in them, and the vices of youth by no means ceasing. And when they come to extreme old age, they do not give up their depraved habits; rather, such as they always were from the beginning, such also they go to death.

You would see among them priests now decrepit who have so drunk in the poison of lust that, although because of advanced age they can no longer act lasciviously in deed, they burn for their accustomed sin in thought, word, and gestures. Of such people the Lord says through the prophet: “If the Ethiopian can change his skin, and the leopard his spots, then you too will be able to do good, though you have learned evil.” In old men intercourse with women can cease, but the desire for the act remains as before. Therefore, as you ought to be as an old man, so also now live as a young man. For it is difficult for an old man to correct his habits and to think new thoughts in an old mind. Indeed, old age has habits; it does not receive them. He who as a young man has sported wantonly with a woman is mad as an old man. Therefore it is good for a man when he has borne the yoke of the Lord from his youth, since if someone has been evil in youth, he will rarely be good in old age.

¶ On the Remedy against Lust.
Chapter III.

Physicians are accustomed first to show the sick, by careful discussion, the seriousness of their disease, and only then to apply help by remedies. For once the gravity of the sickness is known, the bitterness of the potion causes less fear. I am going to do this with you also. First, then, by many kinds of demonstration I have disclosed to you the danger of cohabitation with a woman, and, so far as I could, I have declared what the end of this disease is. It follows now, therefore, that we should consider the remedy.

For the flesh always lusts against the spirit, and unless it is restrained by wholesome exercise, it casts both itself and him into the depth of damnation. Hence the poet rightly admonishes, saying: “If you remove idleness, Cupid’s bows perish; the torches, despised, lie without light.” For Holy Scripture forbids this, because every idle person is in desires.
