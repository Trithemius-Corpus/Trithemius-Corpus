Woman is the gate of the devil, the door of hell, the way of iniquity, the terrible sting of the scorpion, and a nocturnal kind of deception to a chaste man when she is familiar with him. When she approaches, she stirs him; when she speaks, she inflames him; when she touches, she burns fiercely. We do not condemn sex itself, but we warn of the danger to chastity. We read that Saint Augustine so greatly fled women that he would not permit even his own sister to enter his episcopal house. And we ourselves have known very many priests who for many years governed both the house and the people entrusted to them innocently, without the service of a maidservant. Nor will it be hard for you to imitate what you see others have done.

Therefore take care not to approach the company of young women, since it is difficult to live chastely even among old women. Remember whose body you handle at the altar, and do not pour out your mouth, consecrated by divine ministries, in the kisses of prostitutes. It is most shameful to touch a woman with polluted caresses by hands consecrated with the oil of holy anointing, by which the Son of God is held and sacrificed. Do not give blemishes to your glory, and your name as a reproach to everyone. All will tear you to pieces in public, and in the fields the rustic plowmen and vine-dressers will do so every day with vigor, if, being yourself a young man, you presume to live with women, especially younger ones. You will become a proverb and a mockery to all the people if you frequently enter the dwellings of young women.

Keep watch at all times against your invisible enemy, who is always flying around your side and laying snares. And, according to Naso: “Resist evils at their beginnings,” while the temptations are still weaker. For as we see, after a cleric has once offered his body to the embraces of prostitutes, rarely or never does he return to perfect amendment. For all who are vexed by perverse love, the longer they have lain in the filth of lust, the more difficultly they return to health of mind. Vice, when strongly grown old, fixes its roots in the human heart.

Oh, how many priests there are today in the Church of God who, submerged in the depth of lust, despise the patience of God, as it is written: “When the impious man has come into the depths of evils, he despises.” No doubt there are many among the clergy befouled by lust, who, although from the beginning of their promotion they had resolved to live chastely, have been deceived by negligent custody of the senses, and now, without any fear of God, mingle with prostitutes as though lawfully and by custom. Nothing is worse than the custom of sin, by which the gravest crimes come to be considered as the smallest. The strongest bond of sin is the habit of sinning, which grows with time and gains strength by delay. Therefore he gave the best counsel against the fires of the flesh who said in verse: “Resist at the beginnings; medicine is prepared too late when evils have grown strong through long delays.”

“But hasten, and do not put yourself off until future hours: he who is not ready today will be less ready tomorrow.” Scripture says: “Do not delay being converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day,” because the longer you defer repentance, the less fit you will be for repenting.

Now climb the watchtower of your mind, and see the madness of foolish priests, who are led captive by vile concubines, so that they do not do whatever they themselves wish, but what is commanded them by their whores. You would meanwhile see the solid cleric sitting beside his concubine, gazing at her with open mouth; and if she smiles at him, he laughs; if she becomes angry, he flatters her until he is reconciled into her favor. The unhappy priest is led captive by a woman, and he who ought to have ruled others is overcome by the dominion of his maidservant.

Oh, who would believe how many wretched groans he utters amid these things, whenever he thinks of casting off this most harsh yoke of the arch-devil, what pains he carries in his heart? Sometimes, indeed, he is touched by such great displeasure against his beast that he plans to drive her away from him the next day. Meanwhile he gives and receives insulting words; then after words they come to blows: he strikes and is struck back; he curses and is blasphemed. Oh astonishing spectacle: the head of a priest, which it is not lawful for a prince to touch, is torn, struck, and bruised by the hands of a prostitute.

Then, after curses and blows, the woman turns herself to weeping; tears are brought forth, with the devil pouring them in, until at last she recalls the unhappy captive to mercy and makes him gentle toward herself. You would now see the cleric suddenly changed, and so moved by the tears poured in by the devil, that he who had suffered the injury first begs forgiveness. The table is then set out; the feast is restored; sighs are mingled with words; negotiations for new marriages are undertaken.

Amid these things the solid priest sits there, disputing about patience and meekness. He sighs that he has sinned against his mistress, and labors with all his strength to recover her favor. What happens after the friendship has been renewed you may more properly think about than I may say. There is no fear of God before the eyes of a priest bound by the chains of a concubine, no dread of the pains of hell, no thought of priestly dignity, no memory of public honor. This vice of incontinence has now prevailed so far in the clergy that it has almost begun to be lawful. What a memorable condition of the human race: those who ought to have corrected the crimes of others commit worse ones.

To our shame, priests publicly defamed by intercourse with their concubines sit in villages and cities. So great a number of them is found today that if any prelate wished to correct such men in his diocese alone, defeated by their mere multitude, he would be frightened away from his purpose. If you should enter the house of any one of such men, you would sometimes find your priest in bed with his concubine.
