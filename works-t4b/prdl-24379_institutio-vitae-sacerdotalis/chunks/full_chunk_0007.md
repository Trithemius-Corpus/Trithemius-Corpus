You would also see, when they rise from bed, the ill-omened father receive the child on his knees, and, after offering kisses, place him on his right knee and the breviary on his left. While praying, he smiles at the smiling infant; he flatters him when he cries; he addresses him when silent with frequent words and nods. Meanwhile the flattering mother runs up, snatches the sweet pledge from the father’s hand together with the book, handles the leaves, tears them, and with saliva flowing from his mouth, with his parent’s permission, stains them. Amid all this there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention is applied to the words, but every concern is spent in consoling the crying child. The words fall confused from the mouth of the one praying, interrupted by the child’s frequent kisses, so much so that when the prayer is completed he scarcely remembers that he has prayed.

Meanwhile the bell-ringer pulls the bell, the altar is prepared for the sacrifice, and the priest is awaited in the church by the people. The priest runs in with washed hands, in place of the boy, puts on the sacred vestments as quickly as he can, and distractedly performs the sacrifice of God. O conscience, where do you remain meanwhile? Where do you lie despised? O wretched man, you who handle the Son of God with polluted hands, how long will you wound, tear apart, and destroy your soul? With what rashness, finally, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul? If the great compassion of almighty God did not spare you for a time, the angels, provoked by indignation, would strike you heavily at the altar.

Where is the vow of perpetual chastity that you promised into the hands of the bishop when he ordained you? Where is the reverence due to the priesthood you assumed? You have sinned; why do you not do penance? How long do you despise the long-suffering of God? Or do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But according to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of the just judgment of Jesus Christ. I think, indeed, that you intend to repent in the future; but you give too little thought to death, the nearest cutter-off of repentance. For the merciful God, who promised pardon to the penitent, did not pledge tomorrow to the one who delays.

But when a woman living with a cleric has once led him away captive, she never, or only rarely, lets him go free unless death intervenes. I pass over the fact that many, through the tricks and evil deeds of their concubines, are often driven to such madness that they can by no means free themselves from their foulest love for them. Hearing shudders at the crimes of harlots, and the purity of chastity does not allow one to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by priests’ concubines.
