be strengthened by the foundation of humility. For holy Pope Gregory says: “He who gathers virtues without humility carries dust into the wind. For where he is seen to be carrying something, there he is more gravely blinded.” Therefore the greatest ornament of a priest is charity, and the most precious pearl in his garment is humility. You too, brother, express these twin virtues in yourself, and you will be a fruitful preacher in the Church of God. Whatever you say, you will easily persuade your hearers if you have charity and humility in your heart and diligently show them in your work.

Let cursed ambition have no place in your heart; once it has invaded a mind, it so blinds it that it brings on forgetfulness of God and of one’s own salvation. Be a Christian lover of men and an enemy of vices. Keep the commandments of God, and do not neglect ecclesiastical sanctions. Honor all men for God’s sake, and never deem anyone worthy of contempt. What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another. Let not the sun go down upon your anger. Flee the flatteries of the rich, and never indulge sinners. Deny yourself to your own desires, so that you may become a disciple of Christ. Chastise your body, according to the Apostle, bringing it into servitude. Spurn delights; love chaste fasting. Be dutiful toward the poor. Feed the hungry, clothe the naked, visit the sick, free the captive, console the sorrowful, bury the dead.

Do not love the world; do not seek riches; do not run after vanities. For no one serving as God’s soldier entangles himself in secular affairs. Do injury to no one, but learn patiently to bear injury inflicted on you, after the example of your Lord, who, hanging on the cross, prayed for his persecutors, that they might not perish. Have peace with all men; have concord and unanimity only with the devout. Do not be proud and arrogant, not sleepy and lazy, not given to wine and much eating, not a slanderer and one who rashly judges anyone. Guard your actions at every hour, and let the memory of future things always turn in your thought. For Scripture says: “Son, remember your last things, and you will not sin forever.” Therefore always think that you will die; fear the day of judgment; dread the torments of hell. If you do these things, you will easily overcome all temptations of the flesh.

Spurn the pleasures of the world, so that you may be able to enter rejoicing into the joys of heavenly blessedness. Do not deceive your soul: no one can rejoice here with the world and afterward reign with Christ. For through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. Therefore, having spurned present things, let us hasten with every desire toward eternal joys. With all watchfulness guard your heart, because from it life proceeds. If you have been overtaken by some sin, do not delay to seek the remedies of penitence and to cleanse yourself in confession through true contrition. Strive to enter into life by the narrow way,

that is, not to fulfill the desires of the flesh, to hate the world, and to live by the spirit. You have been called to serve God as a soldier against the vices of the flesh. Do not rest before victory; do not withdraw from the fight, because no one will be crowned unless he is victorious. In this life there is no certain victory, but continual battle. He who perseveres to the end will be saved. A man will be tested at his end. The life of a good man upon earth is warfare. For you are a soldier of the highest king; it is not permitted you to desert his contest. A good soldier follows his king wherever he goes.

Where, then, is your king? In heaven. Why do you not follow him? What are you doing on earth, if you are a soldier of that heavenly king above? Why, then, do you not follow your own king? For he himself says: “He who ministers to me, let him follow me; and where I am, there also will my minister be.” Let him follow, he says, not with the steps of the feet, but with the steps of the affections. The Apostle also says: “No one serving as God’s soldier entangles himself in secular affairs, so that he may please the one to whom he has pledged himself.” Therefore, just as those who serve an earthly king obey all his commands and are ready and prompt wherever they are sent, offering no excuse on account of wife or children, so the soldier of Christ must obey his commands in all things and make no excuse at all.

You therefore, who await the unfading crown of victory, labor like a good soldier of Jesus Christ. Let no care of earthly anxiety weigh you down; let no enticement of fleshly pleasure touch your mind. Nothing is happier than the soldier of Christ, to whom the kingdoms of heaven are promised; nothing is more laborious, because he daily places his life in danger; nothing is stronger, because he conquers the devil; nothing is weaker, because he overcomes the flesh. Let not household choke you, nor kinship impede you, nor the flesh constrain you. I want you to be without anxiety, so that you may freely think on the things that are God’s. Go naked and unencumbered to heaven, because naked your mother bore you, and naked the earth will receive you.

Love what you do not lose; contemn what you cannot hold; seek that in which you may rejoice happily forever. Live in such a way that you do not fear to die. A good conscience has not deserved death, because through it it knows that it will come to the eternal joys of the heavenly homeland.

You now have, most loving brother, what you asked of me. You have some pattern of good conduct on which you may imprint yourself. You have a rule of holy exhortation to which you can diligently have recourse. I have done what I could, not as much as I wished. For the rest, live according to the Gospel and the constitutions of the holy fathers, in which the whole form of Christian perfection is seen. Farewell, remembering us.

From Spanheim, on the Kalends of April, in the year 1486.

Here ends the little book of Lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, On the Life of Priests.
