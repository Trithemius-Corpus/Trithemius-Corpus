And he who once submits to shameful love is scarcely ever freed from it entirely. For fornication is more easily fled than amended. Therefore I warn and beseech you to flee the company of women, lest you lose the reputation of a good name. For a priest once defamed for fornication will never recover the honor he has lost. Avoid also conversations with women; shun frequent greetings in the marketplace, because through slight words one comes to the worst deeds. Careless speech with women has deceived many.

Be watchful at all times, and never think that you should trust yourself, so long as you live in the flesh. For we have known very many who fell in old age, who seemed secure to themselves because of a past victory. Indeed temptation is never lacking to a man as long as he lives in this life. But if you cannot manage domestic care without the service of a maidservant, take an upright old woman from your own kin, one from whose living with you no suspicion could arise among the people. Nevertheless, beware of this before all else: do not take a married woman as a maidservant. For what God has joined, let man not separate. The living together of another man’s wife is not free from suspicion of evil. I wish you, as far as possible, to have good testimony from everyone. Let the dangers of others make you cautious; and where you see that many have fallen, do not wish to walk incautiously.

Remember how many very brave and very wise men, from the beginning of the world down to this day, have perished through the company of women. A woman deceived Adam, the first-formed man, and when he had been overthrown she slew us all. From the beginning of the created world, through women the sons of God sinned, and by their crimes brought the flood upon the whole world. Likewise, when the children of Israel were going out from Egypt, the women of the foreigners overthrew them in the desert. Why do we recount these women, when a vile harlot, having captured Samson, the strongest of all men, by her love, handed him over to death and disgrace? Let no one presume on his own strength; let no one trust in former chastity, since often the man who today is seen to stand bravely is tomorrow seen to fall into the depth of fornication. Most wise Solomon could not guard against the deceits of women; who, then, will now be safe? The strong and wise man falls by the sword of women, and we, fragile and foolish, presume upon security. For the one who fell says: Better is the wickedness of a man than a woman who does good. It is safer to live with a wicked man than with a good woman.

For the devil, the adversary of chastity, never ceases to lay snares for the chaste man, if he can find material for deceiving him. Since he is cunning, he knows how to dissemble for a time, until at last in a single hour he can overthrow all the labor and reward of past continence. He gradually insinuates himself among those who love one another, and under the guise of Christian charity he sows sweet words about virtues and vices, until he draws the unwary into the snare of crime.

Then, from that point, they begin to speak more familiarly; they disclose to one another all their more secret matters; little by little they grow accustomed to touching one another with their hands. Finally, through mutual laughter, future unions are prepared; and while by custom they live familiarly, they suddenly fall into the pit of the devil. But afterward, when they have once fallen into mutual guilt, the deed seems somewhat abhorrent to them, but they cannot easily draw back from what has been begun. They therefore continue on the road on which they had set out; and although in the beginning conscience abhors the crime, nevertheless by habit it gradually bears it more lightly. And at first, indeed, they begin to behave wantonly with a certain shamefaced fear; but thereafter, blinded by depraved habit, they are no longer ashamed. Meanwhile the belly, aware of the secret, begins to swell, and to make public the crime that had lain hidden. At last a little infant is born; for a short time father and mother blush, and immediately return to their accustomed crimes. Then, from that point, all shame is cast far away; the eye of conscience is blinded; the fear of God and of hell is utterly driven from memory. They now live licentiously in sins, bound together with devilish glue; they live defamed before men; they live alienated from God. Soon the long-standing crime turns into despair; the remedy of penance is mocked; the long-suffering patience of God is despised.

Meanwhile the priest is made so much a slave to lust that he seems to himself unable to live without a concubine. He can indeed amend his sin, but he refuses, because he feels himself pleasantly entangled in the chains of lust. He promises himself that he will take up the resolution of penance in the future; he promises to amend his life. But while, amid so many crimes, he puts off the will to do penance, concubinary priests rarely come to the remedies of saving penance, because they hope that their life will still be prolonged for a long time. Indeed some are so maddened by diabolical enticements that they are able to think of nothing but carnal things and care nothing about future recompense. They love a criminal concubine more than eternal happiness. What sort of penance do you think these men perform at the end, when they are pressed not by will but by necessity? Those who have consumed the whole time of their life in lust rarely obtain true penance in the final moments of their life. For even if the late penance of such persons, by God’s mercy, is sometimes genuine, nevertheless they will not be able to reach the kingdom of heaven without the most grievous torments of purgatory. Finally, if revelations are to be believed, the very great multitude of clerics in the lower world suffers, because of the vice of incontinence, inestimable eternal punishments.
