For it is better to send a penitent with mercy to purgatory than, by the severity of the confessor, to consign one in despair to hell.

Do not neglect to pray your canonical hours with reverence and devotion at the times appointed by the Church. Likewise, do not omit to read the Hours of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin, the vigils of the dead, and the rest that are owed according to the priestly order.

Have care for your household in the fear of God. If you have a household, persuade them by word and example to preserve modesty and honor. For indeed the reputation of a priest is harmed among the people when the people speak ill of his household.

Let your speech be strengthened by the testimony of the Scriptures, because whatever is taught apart from the holy Scriptures is despised with the same ease with which it is approved. I do not want you to imitate those who preach in church without learning, who frequently cite fables and phantasms of dreams, and who are ignorant of the mysteries of the divine Scriptures. Chattering and talkative priests, speaking without reason, do not edify the people, but labor to draw them into admiration of themselves with empty speeches and Aesopian fables. For it is a foolish and damnable abuse, with the Scriptures and Gospel set aside, to teach Christian people the fables of Aesop and the invented deeds of the Romans.

Nor should you be surprised if the untrained people listen to these things more gladly than to the Scriptures, since the Apostle foretold this, saying: “There will be a time when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires they will heap up teachers for themselves, having itching ears; and from hearing the truth they will turn away, and they will turn to fables.” Hence Jerome says: to roll out words and, by rapidity of speech, to create admiration for oneself among the untrained crowd belongs to unlearned men. A bold forehead often interprets what it does not know, and when it has persuaded others, it also usurps for itself the knowledge that it does not possess. Nothing is so easy as to deceive a lowly little crowd and an unlearned assembly by fluency of tongue, which admires all the more whatever it does not understand.

But you, speak the things that befit sound doctrine, and do not seek the applause of the untrained if you desire to find a place among the learned. Do not imprudently desire the care of souls, unless you have first learned to govern yourself and others justly according to the ecclesiastical sanctions. For the office of teaching the people exposes one to many dangers and is troubled by daily anxieties. For the ruler of souls must be without fault, as a steward of God: not proud, not wrathful, not given to wine, not a striker, not greedy for shameful gain, but hospitable, kind, sober, just, holy, continent, embracing the faithful word that accords with doctrine, so that he may be able both to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict it.
