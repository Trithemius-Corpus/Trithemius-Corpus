Instruction in the priestly life of Dom Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of the divine father Benedict, of the diocese of Mainz. To Nicolaus Mernicensis, priest of the diocese of Trier.

ROYAL
LIBRARY
OF MUNICH.

III. 28.

--- Page 007 ---

Thomas Ruscher of Gmund, professor of sacred letters, unworthy preacher of the evangelical word in the church of Mainz, sends perpetual greetings to the well-reputed and catholic priest Nicolaus Mernicensis.

Lest I should seem cold, most learned Nicolaus, in giving thanks before him from whom comes every best gift and every perfect gift, there occurs first the Scripture in Deuteronomy 30: “I have set life and death before your face.” I give thanks because today he also grants this to the one who clings to him who is the true life. And I speak of him who, in my recent days, breathed into the face of a certain man the breath of new life; for this I return thanks to the Creator of all.

And to you, O Trithemius, father of praiseworthy understanding, because you have chosen life not only for yourself, but also that I and many others may live, by means of the life set forth in this so flourishing little work of instruction. As soon as it had been brought as far as me, admitting no other business, as though in need of the breath of life, I longed with the affection of my mind and the desire of my soul until I might look into it.

But what, O father of praiseworthy understanding, you have accomplished in me, most devoted to you, through this so flourishing little work of your instructions, or what I felt while reading it, I would not wish to be hidden from my contemporaries. For when I proceeded to its inner parts, it so flourished in me that my cheeks grew wet.

O brother Nicolaus Mernicensis, most closely joined to me in prayer and charity, how blessed and how holy, by means of this flourishing little work, was your request. O man and son of the spouse of Christ, Nicolaus Mernicensis, and confessor of God, how necessary in this little work are the things that are contained there, especially in these present stormy times, when the Christian faith is torn and oppressed by harmful errors that everywhere are sown and spring up. Necessary, I say, beyond measure. For when the flourishing instruction of priestly pilgrimage has been cast aside, all vices and disgraces arise.

For I know, by the tradition of our elders, that the human soul is nourished by understanding and knowledge of things as by a certain food. I rightly judge the souls of those who have been trained by no disciplines, by no instruction in morals or life, and who have drunk in nothing of the good arts, to be fasting and almost starving; yet their souls too are full, but full of vices and wickedness.

Come then, Nicolaus, know that I would wish myself and my contemporaries to be persuaded to turn wholly to this flourishing little work. For through it we can not only avoid the penalty of crime, but also obtain the favor of praise. For he who does not apply the sense of smell of his soul to this will quickly fall into ruin. Therefore this very thing, before the first printing of its letters,

you know is fruitful for me. Especially since it has been gathered, compiled, and handed down with no slight labor by the father and lord of praiseworthy understanding, Dom Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, who is far more abundant than I in varied grace of letters; who is watchful in intellect, ready in speech, burning in zeal, and a useful father of a household in the house of the Lord. By his flourishing little work, providing instruction in holy life, he trains me, weak as I am, and at the same time all the unskilled and learned clergy, in precautions.

And surely, O brother, if this instruction in life is lacking to us, who, I ask, judges that we live? Come then, Nicolaus: if this is lacking to us, we are not worthy. For in it, indeed, everything is supplied to us so fully that without these things no one is worthy of life. For if, according to the flourishing instruction of our reverend father in the Lord, Trithemius, which is in hand, we have wholly ordered ourselves while we live here, true salvation is truly promised to us; and he who lives worthy of life will never take it away from any. Therefore all who are worthy must order their life according to the things handed down in this flourishing little work, so that they may not die, but rather live forever.

That you may comprehend this, most worthy Nicolaus: farewell always in him who created all things, and may he preserve by his mercy us who cannot stand by our own powers. Amen.

Given at the studium of Mainz, in the year of our salvation one thousand four hundred ninety-four, in the month of October, on the twenty-second day of the same month.
