Therefore one who wishes to extinguish the vice of lust must keep himself continually exercised in some holy work. For idleness is the enemy of the soul. Hence blessed Jerome admonishes someone, saying: Always do something good, so that the devil may find you occupied. Indeed, the best remedy against evil thoughts is occupation in some holy work. For when the mind is pleasantly intent upon some good exercise, it does not think of other things. Access will not lie open to the devil if the mind is occupied in some exercise. Take an example in holy David: while occupied with military affairs he subdued lust; while idle and staying in his house he committed adultery. When the hand is free from work, the mind grows weak in thought. Therefore, if you desire to conquer lust, flee idleness.

But with what exercises you ought to fill your time, receive briefly, if you please. In the morning accustom yourself to rise from sleep before daylight, and to devote some little time to both the canonical and the votive prayers. And when by praying devoutly you have completed the duty of your service, you will then devote yourself to sacred readings, until you understand that you are called by your office to celebrate mass. After mass one must again devote oneself to prayers and apply oneself most devoutly to sacred meditations. Then one must work with the hands, attend to the affairs of the house, and, if anything has to be arranged by some necessity, let it be disposed with the fear of God. The manual work of a priest is to write, to bind books copied either by himself or by others, or printed books, and to apply himself to other honorable labors according to his condition or necessity. Then, after food has been taken, praises must be rendered to God, who feeds us here on earth, and one must pray again that after this life he may feed us without end in heaven. When these things have been duly completed, one must not immediately go off to idle tales, but again devote oneself to sacred letters. Then one must work with the hands until vespers, which should not be extended beyond the fourth hour in manual labor.

But when, now that the day is declining, it is time to sleep, first have recourse to most devout prayers. When these are finished, set an account of your actions throughout the day, and, declaring yourself guilty before God for your negligent way of life, ask for pardon and indulgence. For if you judge yourself, you will be absolved by God. And unless you keep yourself steadfast in the fear of the Lord, the house of your conscience will quickly be overthrown. But if you have been caught beforehand in some fault, soon have recourse to the lament of penitence, and choose such a confessor, if it can be done, as knowledge of the Scriptures proves fit and as his way of life commends. For one whose conduct is reprobate rarely opens in truth the way of salvation to those who confess. But if, at the enemy’s suggestion, you begin to feel temptations of the flesh in your soul,

do not strengthen them by delay, but drive them out as quickly as possible through prayers and holy exercises. The serpent of lust is slippery, and unless it is crushed in the head, it is not easily destroyed. While the adversary is still weak, he is easily overcome. Nor, if you have conquered once, should you think yourself secure, because the victory is not complete where battle is waged daily. Therefore, as long as you can be subjected, do not believe that you have conquered. Do not trust in your own strength; do not claim victory for yourself, but know that your hope rests in the aid of God alone. For no one is chaste by his own powers. Good will, helped by the grace of God, conquers the flesh.

One must pray to the Lord without ceasing against the temptations of the flesh, and say: Pierce my flesh with your fear, for I have feared your judgments. Do not love the delights of this world, which will make you quickly vanish away and will easily inflame you with the heat of lust. In your diet let excess be avoided, and let hunger, not variety of foods, move the appetite. Let not enticements of lusts be set before you, but things that nourish the body and do not inflame it. Wine, in which there is wantonness, should always be suspect to you, lest you be set on fire by a double heat, namely of drink and of youth. He who constantly uses wine fosters the fire of lust in his lap. The shameful uncovering of Noah, and the incest of Lot, should always admonish you to fear wine. For drunkenness is the burial of a living man: it destroys the senses, extinguishes reason, binds the mind, wounds the conscience, diminishes the health of the body, and kills the soul. Drunkenness is a spring of evils: it stirs the flesh to lust, defiles the heart with the worst thoughts, and loosens the tongue into the foulest words. It is the saying of most holy Jerome: A full belly easily foams into lust.

Therefore let the chaste man’s refreshment be moderate; let his drink be sparing and thin; let his speech be modest. Let him beware the familiarity of women. Let him direct his senses with every guard, lest he fall into the pit of lust. Whenever a base thought begins to strike the mind, let the memory of the Lord’s passion come upon him; let him place his thought in the wounds of Jesus Christ. For it is written: Blessed is he who will seize and dash his little ones against the rock. But the rock, says the Apostle, was Christ. For the recollection of the Lord’s passion is the most effective remedy for all temptations. Indeed, one whom the passion of Christ does not soften, no exercise will cure. Therefore, as soon as the lust of the flesh begins to attack the spirit, let the cultivator of chastity lift his hands to heaven and say: Lord Jesus Christ, lover of chaste counsel, you know that my strength to resist is slight. Help my weakness through your bitter passion. I believe in the Lord that, if he persists in praying with devotion, he will easily obtain victory against the flesh.
