Nothing is better for a man than, by reading divine things, to fix his mind there whence life comes to him. Therefore continual effort must be devoted to reading the Scriptures, because reading nourishes the mind and supplies supports for the virtues. Therefore, lest you lose the value of your time, read the sacred Scriptures, write books, devote yourself to devout prayers, and at the proper times also work with your hands. Do not be ashamed to learn what you do not know, because then you will be able usefully to instruct others if you always seek to be instructed by teachers. Do not be ashamed to bear the name of pupil, if you desire to attain the honors of mastership. For the Holy Spirit, who teaches man knowledge, is a lover of humility.

When you sit down at table to take food, remember first that the sacred letters must be sounded, so that, while the flesh is fed, the mind may not be deprived of its own nourishment. For in the council of Toledo it is read as follows: “For reverence toward God and the priests this universal synod decreed that, because idle tales are often accustomed to be brought in at meals, reading of the divine Scriptures should be mingled with every priestly banquet. For by this both souls are built up toward good, and unnecessary tales are forbidden.” For the word of God is the food of the spirit, because man does not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God. The ancient fathers and priests devoted the greatest diligence to the holy Scriptures, so that they might both feed the mind wholesomely and provide useful things for the instruction of posterity. Let us tread in their footsteps, so that we may deserve to be found worthy ministers before God.

*On fleeing avarice and greed. Chapter 5.*

Although at the beginning of our discourse we have argued against avarice and greed, nevertheless we are led by a certain principle to speak even in this place about this very matter. For nothing is ever done sufficiently against this vice unless it is utterly rooted out. But our Savior, knowing how difficult it is for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God, says to those who follow him: “Whoever has not renounced all that he possesses cannot be my disciple.” Hence the psalmist admonishes us, saying: “If riches abound, do not set your heart upon them.” And the apostle says: “Having food and clothing, let us be content with these.” What more should we seek, we who are ministers of God? Greed is shameful in a priest of God; shameful is avarice, and the continual hunger for gold is damnable.

Therefore, most beloved brother, if you desire to live in the freedom of the spirit, do not seek money that will perish, and do not love riches. Cast your thought upon the Lord, and he himself will nourish you; he himself will feed the hungry; he himself will guard you.
