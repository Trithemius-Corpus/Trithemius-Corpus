For nothing so raises minds from vain anxiety to things above; nothing so quiets the fires of the flesh as diligence in sacred reading. In short, study is especially suited to your office, as Malachi says: The lips of the priest keep knowledge, and they shall seek the law from his mouth, because he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts. For, as we said a little before, a priest of Christ must be learned in the divine law, so that he may know, and have the means from which to bring forth, a sermon. For one who has undertaken to rule souls, for whom he will render account to God, the supreme judge, must necessarily read and understand many things.

Indeed, as that great Gregory Nazianzen said, and after him the Roman pope: The governance of souls is the art of arts. But if a man has learned nothing in the Holy Scriptures, what will he teach others? Or what benefit in preaching will a priest be able to bring to his hearers if he does not know the Scriptures? Alas for our present times, in which as the people are, so is the priest. Our priests cast aside the study of Scripture and neglect learning; instead of books of Scripture they rear birds and dogs. Let the bishops see to it who promote such idiots and ignorant men to the dignity of the priesthood, who entrust the care of Christ's sheep to unskilled pastors. Withdraw from the company of those who cast aside knowledge, who neglect the reading of Scripture, who despise study. They savor nothing but what is worldly; they love nothing but vanity; they understand nothing except what is carnal. They will quickly overturn you if you often pass into their assembly. They sit in ambush with drinkers in taverns; they give themselves to games and feasts; there is no fear of God before their eyes. They grow angry if anyone begins to speak of the Scriptures in their presence; they disdain to hear a reading, but turn instead to fables. What have you to do with them? Are they not priests, but asses in their way of life? They understand absolutely nothing of the Scriptures; they despise learning. They do not know how to write or speak the Roman tongue; they have scarcely learned to expound the Gospels in the vernacular. How many errors, fables, and heresies they proclaim to the people by preaching in the church: who could believe it unless he had experienced it? Instead of books they get children for themselves; instead of study they love concubines. These are blind guides of the blind, who do not instruct the people of God in justice, but rather lead them astray. O how many poor Christians live in the countryside who, because of the ignorance of their priests, do not know what befits a Christian. Amid these things there is no one who takes notice, no one who grieves over the danger to souls, no one who applies a remedy. All savor the things that are earthly; all seek their own; all run after profits. But what are these things to us? Each one stands or falls before his own lord. And although it does not belong to me to correct these things, nevertheless I cannot help grieving when I see every evil in the church proceeding from the ignorance of priests.

For who would not grieve that those men do not know the commandments who have been appointed to instruct others? Behold, no knowledge is sought in the ordaining of priests; those who have not been called are permitted to come forward; the life of the man to be ordained is never examined, as above all it ought to have been. Therefore the Christian people are led away captive by the cords of sins; the ardor of charity is extinguished; the harmony of brotherly love is violated. Our priests flow over with worldly desires; they do not dare correct the vicious, because they know themselves to have sinned in greater matters. For there are few who duly instruct the flock by word and example. Nor is it any wonder if lesser men are free from the study of Scripture, since among prelates you find few who cling to the examples of the ancients. For those chosen are generally not the more learned, but those more eager to gather riches. They have either no copies of the Scriptures or few, since in fact they pursue knowledge with hatred. Nothing so diminishes ecclesiastical dignity and makes it contemptible as the ignorance of prelates. When formerly bishops, abbots, and the other prelates and priests applied their minds to the Scriptures, so easy an entrance did not lie open to crimes and sins. For love and knowledge of the Scriptures teach men to despise the world, flee vices, and love the most sacred virtues. As long as the Israelite people remained in the law of the Lord, no power of the enemy prevailed against them. But after they cast aside knowledge of the law, they were subjected to many adversities. For Scripture says: Because they did not have wisdom, they perished through their own foolishness. Today also priests and rectors of churches are very numerous, but few of them attend to knowledge of the law and of the Scriptures. And because, as the Lord speaks through the prophet, the priests have cast aside knowledge of the law, therefore they have been made contemptible among the people. They do indeed cry to the Lord in tribulation, but he does not hear them, because whoever turns his ears away so as not to hear the law, his prayer will be abominable. Therefore priests should not wonder that laypeople despise them, since they themselves spurn the commandments of Christ. But I greatly fear that more serious things will soon come against the clergy. But enough of these matters.

You, however, should consider not what others do, but what you yourself must do. For many are called to the priesthood in the church of God, but few are chosen. Therefore we must take the utmost care that we who are born into the Lord's portion may also be chosen. What does it profit here to exercise the priesthood for a very brief time, if one happens to be separated from the sight of God? Nothing, then, is better than to fix desire on heavenly things, to devote oneself to the study of sacred reading, to despise present things for the love of God, and to think on future things.
