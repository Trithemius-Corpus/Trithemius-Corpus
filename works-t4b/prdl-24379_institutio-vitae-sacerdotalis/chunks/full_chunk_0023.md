Be diligent in examining all the mysteries of the Scriptures. And if any uncertainty occurs to you as you read, know that you must refer it to the interpretations of the elders. You have nearby the school of the most ancient city of Trier, in which there is an abundance of learned men, to whom you can have recourse in the ambiguity of questions. Let gold not be dearer to you than knowledge, because the fruit of wisdom is eternal, but the use of riches is brief and momentary. Let there be no one who prevents your saving study: not mother, not brother, not kinsman, not sister. Do the work of an evangelist; learn what you are to teach, lest you exist as both shepherd and idol. You are one man and have no second; for whom, then, would you store up riches? Live temperately. Do not visit weddings and immoderate banquets, because they strip the mind of virtues and leave the purse empty of coins. Buy for yourself, from the alms offered, books of divine Scripture, so that, taught by constant reading of them, you may be able worthily to instruct the people committed to you, and to raise yourself by contemplation to heavenly things. Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and it will exalt you before God and men. Do not contend with the pleasures of the flesh; restrain the impulse of anger, and never forget death.

What sort of priest he ought to be in his inner conduct. Chapter seven.

Since indeed it is not enough for a minister of Christ to lead an honorable life before men unless he also strives to appear clean from every crime before God, I exhort you to direct your heart in the fear of the Almighty and never admit any thoughts against God. For a priest of God must be holy in mind and body, and clean in thought. Hence the Lord says: Be holy, since I am holy, the Lord your God, who sanctify you. And Peter, the chief apostle, says: You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of acquisition, that you may proclaim the virtues of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light. First, let charity hold a place in your heart, for it is the fullness of the law and the consummation of the prophets. For the greatest of all virtues is charity, which consists in love of God and neighbor. God must be loved with the whole heart for his own sake, and the neighbor for God’s sake. No one truly loves God if he hates his neighbor in the faith. For Christ is God and man. Therefore he does not love the whole Christ who hates a human being. God accepts no good work unless the flame of charity has kindled it on the altar of the heart. For charity holds the chief place among the virtues. Humility follows it, which is remembered as the guardian of the virtues. So great is the excellence of this virtue that all glory quickly perishes unless humility preserves it.
