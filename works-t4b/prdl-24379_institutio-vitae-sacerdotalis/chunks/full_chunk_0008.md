You would see them rising from bed and, on bended knees and with kisses offered, receiving their ill-fated father; placing him on the right knee and the breviary on the left. While he prays, he smiles at the child, caresses it as it cries, and when it falls silent he addresses it with words and nods. Meanwhile the flattering mother runs up, snatches the dear pledge from the father’s hand, seizes the book, turns the leaves, licks them, and with the father’s permission soils the fountain of salvation with her flowing mouth. Amid these things there is no devotion in the one praying, no attention applied to the words, but every concern is spent in consoling the crier. The words from the mouth of the one praying fall away in confusion, interrupted by the child’s frequent kisses, so that, when the prayer is finished, he scarcely remembers that he has prayed.

Meanwhile he pulls the bell with his teeth; he prepares the altar for sacrifice, and is expected here by the people in the church. With hands washed in the boy’s filth, the priest hurries up and puts on the sacred vestments with all possible haste. With a distracted mind he completes the sacrifice of God. O conscience, where do you remain meanwhile? Where do you contemplate the holy things? O wretched man, you who handle the Son of God with polluted hands, how long will you wound, tear, and destroy your soul? With what rashness, finally, do you approach the altar, polluted in body and soul? Unless the great mercy of almighty God spared you for a time, the angels invoked at the altar would strike you with grave indignation.

Where is the vow of perpetual chastity that you promised into the bishop’s hands when he ordained you? Where is the reverence owed to the priesthood you have assumed? You have sinned; why do you not do penance? You have fallen; why do you not rise? How long do you despise the long-suffering of God? Or do you not know that the patience of God leads you to penance? But you, according to your hardness and impenitent heart, treasure up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and of just judgment. I think, indeed, that you plan to repent in the future; but you scarcely consider that the cutting off of penance may be very near. For the merciful God who promised pardon to the penitent did not promise tomorrow to the one who delays.

But the cleric whom a woman once cohabiting with him has led away captive she never, or rarely, releases free, unless death intervenes. I pass over the fact that many priests are so ensnared by the deceits and sorceries of their concubines that they cannot in any way free themselves from those women’s most shameful love. The heard crimes of prostitutes cause horror, and the sincerity of modesty does not permit one to hear what things are sometimes perpetrated by priests’ concubines. They prepare a dish for their masters from menstrual blood; and when they wish to entice their hearts to their love, they turn them to colic and madness. With horrible sorceries and superstitions they entangle priests in their desires, until, their strength gradually exhausted, they render them wholly useless.

Blessed is the man whom God has guarded from an evil and lustful woman. There is no wickedness like the malice of a woman who enjoys evil loves. Whatever she contrives, she attempts; whatever she can, she dares. No one believes what efforts, ways, and inventions she uses so that, when she plans to deceive someone, she may cast him down into her pleasures with an indissoluble bond. She joins herself devotedly to her master, not because she loves him, but because she desires him. For the crowd of concubines follows the prey, not the priest. So long as he is rich and abounds in everything, she does not leave her master’s side; but if he begins to lack bread, the concubine attaches herself to another.
