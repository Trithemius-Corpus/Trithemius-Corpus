...of all evils is covetousness, which does not allow a person to live for God in freedom of spirit, but always twists the mind with bitter cares and does not permit him to think of anything except riches. Moderation, however, is the safest thing: it is neither oppressed by poverty nor lifted up by abundance into pride. We see among the priests of God worshippers of idols, who are always watchful in gathering money, who devote themselves to avarice, and who think nothing, or very little, about eternal life. They live as if they had been born for gain; they cast piety toward the poor far away; they desire nothing other than to seek and find gold.

You would see among them certain avaricious and wretched rich men, who have a very great supply and abundance of temporal things, but lack the ability to use what they have acquired. All the days in which they dwell on earth they live wretchedly. They do not dare to eat and drink according to the necessity of the body; they always desire to add something to the heap of money; they keep watch over profits; they care absolutely nothing for the salvation of souls. They grieve more over a lost denarius than if ten souls entrusted to them were to perish. But it is no wonder if he neglects another's soul who damns his own. For whoever has once subjected his heart to avarice and covetousness will never, or only rarely, be free. He always burns for greater profits in money; he always pants after an increase of gold; and his desire to possess grows in proportion as his money happens to have increased.

Henceforth one benefice, however rich, is not enough. To obtain still others, the avaricious priest runs about; by right and wrong he labors to get what he wickedly desires. Meanwhile he runs to the apostolic see; he relates the grounds of his petition, which, when examined according to the truth, are immediately found false. Then one man obtains many prebends; he leases the care of the sheep to hirelings; he receives wool and milk from the sheep, but scorns to feed the flock. The prophet says: Woe to the shepherds who feed themselves. Those who obtain many benefices by dispensation of the supreme pontiff think themselves free from guilt, but they do not consider what is required for that obtaining.

But you, stand firm and do not transgress the ancient boundaries which your fathers set; rather, set a limit to desire, lest you fall into sin. I want you to be content with one benefice, which is sufficient for you according to the moderation of your state, lest, if you take on more without just necessity, you be judged more severely for it before God. Do not take as your example the multitudes of those who act otherwise; do not walk along the broad road of sinners, if you wish to reach life. Let him who serves the altar live from the altar; but he who neglects to feed the sheep, by what presumption does he ask wool and milk from them? Ecclesiastical benefices were instituted for the support of those who serve them, not for the avarice of those who seek their own interests.
