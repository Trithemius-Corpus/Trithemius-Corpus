For it is better to send a penitent with compassion to purgatory than, by a confessor’s severity, to consign one driven to despair to hell.

Do not neglect to say your canonical hours with reverence and devotion at the times appointed for the church. Do not omit to read also the Hours of blessed Mary ever-virgin, the vigils of the dead, and the other offices due by priestly order. Have care for your house in the fear of God. If you have a household, persuade it by word and example to preserve modesty and honorable conduct. For a priest’s reputation among the people is indeed harmed when the people speak badly of his household.

Let your preaching be strengthened by the testimony of the Scriptures, because whatever it teaches apart from holy Scripture is rejected as readily as it is asserted. I do not want you to imitate those who preach in church without learning, who frequently cite fables and phantoms of dreams, and are ignorant of the mysteries of the divine Scriptures. Ranting and garrulous priests, speaking without reason, do not build up the people, but labor to draw them into admiration of themselves by empty speeches and Aesopian fables.

For it is a foolish and damnable abuse, with the Scriptures and the Gospel set aside, to teach Christian people fables and invented deeds of the Romans. Nor should you wonder if the ignorant people hear these things more gladly than the Scriptures, since you read that the Apostle said this: “There will be a time when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires they will heap up teachers for themselves, itching in their ears; and they will turn their hearing away from the truth and turn to fables.” Hence Jerome says: “To roll out words and, by speed of speech, make the ignorant crowd admire oneself belongs to unlearned men.” A brazen brow often interprets what it does not know; and, after persuading others, it claims for itself knowledge that it does not have. Nothing is so easy as to deceive a base and unlearned common crowd in an assembly by fluency of tongue; whatever it does not understand, it admires all the more.

But you, speak the things that befit sound doctrine, and do not seek the applause of the unskilled if you wish to find a place among the learned. Do not shamelessly desire the care of souls unless you have first learned to govern both yourself and others justly according to ecclesiastical sanctions. For the office of teaching the people is subject to many dangers and troubled by daily anxieties. For the ruler of souls must be without crime, as God’s steward: not proud, not angry, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for shameful gain, but hospitable, kind, sober, just, holy, continent, holding fast the faithful word that accords with doctrine, so that he may be able to exhort in sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict.

He who has been appointed ruler of souls must teach no less by example than by speech. Even a robber can accuse avarice; but only one who does what he teaches can correct it. He is a delicate teacher who debates fasting on a full stomach. Therefore life must be required before doctrine, because one teaches more fully by deed than by word. For the most excellent kind of teaching is the demonstration of example. And indeed the Lord Jesus first acted, and afterward taught. In teaching, the man whose speech is confirmed by the witness of his work has the greatest authority. The ruler’s speech easily profits his subjects if it is supported by honorable conduct. But whoever teaches well and lives badly destroys himself by the sentence of his own mouth, and does not turn his subjects away from evil instruction.

Consider at the same time how dangerous the care of souls is, especially in these times, when iniquity abounds everywhere and the charity of many has grown cold. For the days are evil, and men are slow to justice; the whole world lies in wickedness, and faith is rare on the earth. Amid these things the word of God is despised by the people, the discipline of Christian religion is neglected, and priestly authority is held in contempt. Lay life, liable to many sins and crimes, neglects the remedies of penance, gives itself to deceits and frauds, and disregards the warnings of priests. If the pastor of souls has not diligently announced to the people committed to him their crimes and the penalties due to sinners, he will also be guilty before God of their blood.

And after these things God will bring everything into judgment, where the pastor of souls is compelled to render accounts for each one; and if he has been negligent in this world, he is damned more severely. Therefore it is safer to flee humbly from the care and rule of souls than to usurp it arrogantly. For if each person in that dreadful judgment can scarcely suffice to render account for his own life, how will he answer for so many thousands committed to him? O how well he lives who lives for himself. It seems good and pleasant among men to live in this world as a bishop, abbot, pastor, or any prelate; but who will be able to consider how terrible the end of a negligent man is? For a most severe judgment will be made against those who rule, and the mighty will suffer mighty torments. He passes well who lives simply, to whom nothing of earthly honor is entrusted. For before God the shepherd, the earthly shepherd will have to be presented to render account. Here already the crisis of final condemnation threatens, where the account of a badly administered stewardship is demanded, and the blood of many sheep is required from the hand of one shepherd. What will the unskilled shepherd then be able to answer to the charges, who was negligent in the care of the sheep? He must necessarily fall silent, because he has nothing to answer.
