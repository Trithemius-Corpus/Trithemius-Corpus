It is disgraceful for a priest of God to enter a tavern with rustics, and for a mouth consecrated to God to be defiled by impure speech. The word of one who preaches is easily despised if it is poured out familiarly among rustics. From the habit of familiarity they will begin to despise you and to count you as one of their own. For no reverence is paid to superiors if their life is narrowed down among their subjects.

The one who presides must surpass all by a certain reverence of maturity, and wisely avoid excessive familiarity with those subject to him. For the eminence of authority is broken when it is indiscreetly mixed with the actions of subordinates. Slander no one, nor take scandal against anyone. It is shameful for a priest to pollute his tongue with detraction, when it has been appointed at the altar to pray for all. Nor should you give ear to detractors, because the detractor and the willing listener are worthy of equal condemnation: the one carries the devil on his tongue, the other in his ear. Let such a person learn not to slander easily when he sees that you are unwilling to listen.

Do not be hasty with your tongue, and do not stand in the company of detractors, because they are hateful to almighty God. Hedge your ears with thorns, lest you hear the words of detractors. Let no lie come forth from your mouth. Let no foul speech proceed from you, but be a guardian of your tongue, lest you offend in speech. Do not be slothful or idle, nor given over to sleep and laziness, but always kindled with the fervor of the Holy Spirit.

Do not neglect the frugality of hospitality. Meet the poor and strangers mercifully with whatever diligence you can, knowing that this is proper to a priest: to have care for the needy. Have gold not to keep it, but to spend it in alms for the poor. Let the hungry bellies of the poor praise you, not the belching banquets of the rich. Let your table be with the poor and needy, because in such people Christ is received and the heavenly court is honored. Avoid the banquets of the proud rich, especially of the powerful, at which nothing is done concerning the salvation of souls, and absolutely nothing is treated concerning spiritual conduct.

Let your clothing be honorable, not costly. In giving alms, do not seek glory from men, but expect praise and reward from God alone. Be a father of the poor and a guardian of orphans. Let no poor person go away from you unconsoled. Do not be a striker, nor derided among the people. Injure no one with insult, but do good to all, as much as you can, even to your enemy. Never flatter sinners, but rebuke them as one who has received authority from God. Do not be a striker of the consciences of the people entrusted to you, nor bring forth the word of God indiscreetly.
