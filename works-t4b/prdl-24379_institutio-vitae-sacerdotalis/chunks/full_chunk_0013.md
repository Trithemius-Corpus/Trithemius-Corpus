Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. Vain speech is the mark of a vain heart. The speech a man utters proves what sort of person he is inwardly.

There are some, and indeed many priests, who by their wanton habits violate the purity of consciences, even though they do not physically proceed to acts of lust. They sit with women, speaking obscene things, mixing touches and kisses with their itching desire, and amid all this they boast that they are chaste. And where, then, does that divine saying remain: “Whoever looks at a woman to desire her has already committed adultery with her in his heart”? If it is not permitted to a chaste man to look at a woman in order to desire her, how much less is it permitted shamelessly to touch, kiss, and handle her with his hands? For the Apostle says: “Abstain from every appearance of evil,” as befits the saints.

Or does the appearance of evil not seem present to you, when men thought chaste sit among women and are handled with kisses and touches? I would be lying if I said that I had not seen priests, and men of greater dignity, wholly consumed in obscene talk, touches, and kisses with women, and showing no reverence for their own rank. Is it not a sign of inward lust when someone has become accustomed to speaking filthy words and to touching with wanton touches? Amid this, will chastity of mind remain there?

But you, dearest Nicolaus, if you desire to present yourself as a worthy minister to God, if you wish to escape the most harsh yoke of lust, must abstain not only from deeds but also from filthy words and from the company of women. It is impossible for the mind not to be wounded when you touch a woman with a kiss and a wanton hand. Remember your last things, says the wise man, and to the end you will not sin. For nothing restrains a man from sin so much as an intent remembrance of death.

On the reading and study of the Scriptures. Chapter iii.

I know from experience that the study and reading of the Scriptures is the most effective remedy against all temptations. For blessed Jerome says: “Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” Scripture alone is what promises us heavenly remedy in tribulation. What will we be able to know or believe about our own eternal homeland, if we do not have the Scriptures? For the Apostle says: “All Scripture is divinely inspired,” so that through patience and the consolations of the Scriptures we may have hope. Therefore I warn you to take up the study of the holy Scriptures as a consolation in this pilgrimage, because if you are assiduous in reading them, you will easily despise the world with all the emptiness of its vanities.
