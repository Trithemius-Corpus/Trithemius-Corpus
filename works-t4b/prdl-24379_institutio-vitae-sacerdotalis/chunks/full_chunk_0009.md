Avoid also conversations with women; decline frequent greetings in the marketplace, because through light words one comes to the worst deeds. For the incautious speech of women has deceived many. Be watchful at every time, and never think that you should trust yourself as long as you live in the flesh. For we have known very many who fell in old age, though they seemed secure to themselves because of past victory. Temptation is never absent from a man so long as he lives in this life. But if you cannot manage domestic care without the service of a maidservant, take an honest old woman from your own kin, from whose living with you no suspicion can arise among the people. Yet take care of this before all else: do not take a married woman as a maidservant. For what God has joined together, let no man separate. The living together of another man’s wife is not free from suspicion of evil. I want you, so far as possible, to have good testimony from all. Let the dangers of others make you cautious; and where you see that many have fallen, do not wish to walk incautiously.

Remember how many of the strongest and wisest men, from the beginning of the world down to this day, have perished through the company of women. A woman deceived Adam, the first-formed man, and, having supplanted him, killed us all. From the beginning of the created world, the sons of God sinned through women, and by their crimes brought the flood upon the whole world. Likewise, when the children of Israel were going out of Egypt, the women of the foreigners laid them low in the desert. Why do we recount these women, when a cheap little prostitute delivered Samson, the strongest of all men, captured by her charms, to death and disgrace? Let no one presume upon his own strength; let no one trust in his former chastity, since often the man who today is seen to stand strongly is tomorrow seen to fall into the depths of fornication. Solomon, the wisest man, could not beware of the deceits of women; who, then, will be safe now? The strong and the wise fall by the sword of women, and do we, fragile and foolish as we are, presume upon security? For the man who fell says, “The wickedness of a man is better than a woman who does good.” It is safer to live with a wicked man than with a good woman. For the devil, the adversary of chastity, never ceases to lay snares for a chaste man, if he can find material for deceiving him. Since he is cunning, he knows how to dissemble for a time, until at last, in one hour, he can overturn all the labor and reward of past continence. Little by little he inserts himself among those who love, and under the appearance of Christian charity deceives the unwary.
