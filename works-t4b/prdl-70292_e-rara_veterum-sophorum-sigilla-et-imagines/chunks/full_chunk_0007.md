*Magic.* 23

Place at your neck a man carved in green jasper, who has a shield hanging at his neck, and above his head a helmet with an upright sword, and who treads a serpent under his feet; and you will fear no enemy. In all things you will be victor, especially in martial matters; and it must be bound in bronze.

A horse bearing a crocodile on its back, carved in white jacinth, makes one lovable to men and beasts.

When the figure of a seated man and of a woman standing before him, having hair extended down to her thigh and raising her eyes upward, has been carved in cornelian, know that its virtue is to render every person, whether male or female, obedient. It must be bound in gold of the same weight as the stone itself; beneath it put betony and amber.

A foaming horse, with a man upon it holding a scepter in his hand, carved in amethyst: know that this seal is useful for all the affairs of the one who carries it, and all princes and nobles will be obedient to him. It must be bound in gold or silver in double the weight of the seal.

If the image of a man’s head with a long beard and a little blood about the neck is found carved in adamant, know that it confers victory and boldness, preserves the body from every injury, and is effective for obtaining favor from princes.

24 *Seals.*

The figure of a man wishing to play on instruments, carved in sapphire, makes one most pleasing to all.

The image of a rooster, or of three girls, when found in agate, makes a person pleasing before God and men.

The figure of a lion carved in garnet provides riches and honors, brings joy, and drives out sadness.

The image of a stag or dove carved in onyx provides the bearer with courage against demons.

The figure of a man in the likeness of a merchant carrying goods for sale, or of a man sitting under a centurion, when found in emerald, gives riches, makes one victorious, and relieves one from distresses.

The image of a bull or calf found in magnet-stone provides the bearer with fortunate journeys through all places, and is effective against all enchantments.

The figure of a horse or wolf found in jasper drives away fevers and stops blood.

The figure of a crowned man in topaz makes the bearer good, compliant, and beloved by God and men, and provides honors and dignities.

*Magic.* 25

When the image of an armed man with a sword in his hand is found carved in sardius or amethyst, it makes the bearer acquire a perfect and good memory, and makes him prudent.

The figure of a stag or billy goat found carved in chalcedony gives the virtue of increasing riches when it is kept in a money-box.

The image of a hare in jasper does not allow the bearer to be harmed by any demon.

A sea locust carved in beryl reconciles quarrels and joins marriage in love.

The figure of a night-raven, because it is the bird of Pallas, carved in a stone that conduces to knowledge, makes a person most learned and eloquent.

The image of a peacock carved in a suitable stone makes the bearer rich.

The figure of a man killing a lion, or another beast, with a sword makes the bearer clever, victorious, and lovable.

The image of a man holding laurel or a palm in his right hand makes the bearer victorious in lawsuits and trials, lovable, and powerful in his own region.

*Seals.*

The figure of a winged man having a serpent under his feet and holding its head in his hands makes the bearer rich, prudent, and lovable to all.

The image of an ant having an ear of grain or a kernel, carved in some stone, is effective for acquiring riches.

The figure of a rooster holding a crown or belt in its beak will give victory in a duel, especially if it is in rooster-stone.

When you find the figure of a standing man whose head is that of an ox and whose feet are those of an eagle carved in some gem, and impress it in wax, no man will speak evil of you.

When you find a basilisk and a dragon fighting each other, carved in cornelian, and there is also the head of an ox there, apply it to your neck when you are about to fight, and you will conquer all beasts, both marine and terrestrial.

The figure of a naked, swollen, and crowned man, holding a cup in one hand and a branch of an herb in the other, carved in jet and bound in a ring made of any metal, immediately frees the bearer from fever when he carries it.
