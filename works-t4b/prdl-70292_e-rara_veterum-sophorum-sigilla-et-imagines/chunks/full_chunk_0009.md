The figure of a tree, or vine, or ear of grain carved on some stone makes the wearer abound in food and clothing, and renders princes favorable.

Mars armed, or a maiden in a stola with a flowing garment, holding laurel in her hand, if found on jasper, renders the wearer powerful in accomplishing deeds of arms, and frees him from violent death, from drowning in water, and from adverse misfortunes.

The figure of an armed man on a horse with a lance, carved on amethyst, makes the wearer victorious in war in the field.

The figure of Hercules, holding a club in his right hand and killing a lion or some other monster, if you find it carved on a stone, know that it grants victory in battle in the field.

The figure of Jupiter, which is in the form of a man with the head of a ram, makes the wearer lovable and quickly obtaining whatever he wishes.

A virgin or girl with a lamp, carved in crystal, has the power of preserving the light of the eyes.

The figure of the fiery triplicity, that is, of Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius, carved on a precious stone, is said to be effective for all cold illnesses

and to assist phlegmatic fevers; and, because of the dignity of the planets that hold dominion in those signs, to render the wearer pleasing to God and men, eloquent, endowed with talent, and able easily to obtain honors and dignities. It must be carved in such a way that, in the triangle, Leo holds the apex, Aries the right side of the base, and Sagittarius the left.

The image of the earthy triplicity, that is, of Taurus, Virgo, and Capricorn, engraved on a stone will provide help for all hot and moist illnesses, and will incline the wearer toward rural works, such as ploughing, planting, sowing, cultivating vineyards, and the like. Capricorn must be at the apex, Taurus on the right side of the base, and Virgo on the left.

The image of the airy triplicity, that is, of Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius, carved on a stone frees one from all cold and dry illnesses arising from melancholic humor, especially quartan fever. Libra will be at the point of the triangle, Aquarius on the right side of the base, and Gemini on the left.

The figure of the watery triplicity, that is, of Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces, engraved on a stone frees one from hot and dry diseases, such as hectic fever, and from all diseases of choler; but it renders the wearer inconstant, unjust, and a hater of truth. Cancer must be at the crown of the triangle,

Pisces on the right side of the base, and Scorpio on the left.

The figure of Saturn engraved on stones is of this kind: an old man holding a sickle in his hand, having a beard that is not very hairy. This figure, carved on a stone of the nature of Saturn, renders the wearer powerful, with a continual increase of power.

The image of Jupiter is of a man sitting on a seat or on a four-footed chair, holding a rod in one hand and a ball in the other; or holding an image of Cancer or a fish, and having an eagle before his feet. But among the doctors of magic it is fashioned otherwise: for they carve the figure of a man with a ram’s head, wrinkled heels, and a slender chest. If this is engraved on a stone, especially krabatus, it renders the wearer fortunate, lovable, and pleasing to religious men, and raises him to honors and dignities.

The image of Mars is fashioned on stone in many ways: sometimes with a standard in his hand, sometimes with a lance or another instrument of war, yet always armed, and sometimes equestrian. The power of this stone, when carved in this way, renders the wearer victorious, bold, and warlike, especially if it is carved on a stone of similar virtue.

The figure of the Sun is found in different forms: sometimes in the likeness of the Sun, with rays all around,

sometimes in the likeness of a man sitting on a seat, with loose hair and flowing garment, and also in a chariot, as if he were being carried by four horses, so that the twelve signs of the zodiac are all around the chariot. The power of this figure carved on some stone renders the wearer commanding and pleasing to great men.

The images of Venus likewise are many; nevertheless the following is more frequently engraved on stones: a woman with a large garment and stola, holding laurel in her hand. Its power is to give agility in accomplishing tasks, to bring all things to the desired end, to remove fear of drowning in water, and to bring honors from women.

The figure of Mercury is a man with a slender chest, having a fine beard and winged sandals, holding the caduceus rod, with a rooster before his feet, or with a serpent under his feet. Its power is to assist in acquiring knowledge and oratory, and it also gives very great help in enriching merchants.

The images of the Moon are various. For sometimes she is fashioned in the proper figure of the Moon, with horns; sometimes in a chariot, as a girl with dogs and a quiver, with dogs following a stag. The power of this image is to provide success in legions, and from these good things, etc.
