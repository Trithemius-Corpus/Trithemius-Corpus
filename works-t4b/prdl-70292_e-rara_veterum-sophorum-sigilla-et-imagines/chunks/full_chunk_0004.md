The figure of a young man wearing a crown on his head and sitting upon a four-footed throne, and under each foot let there be a man standing, holding the chair at his neck; and let there be a circle around the head of the seated man, and let his hands be raised toward heaven: if you find this figure carved under a white hyacinth, and place this stone in a silver ring of equal weight to the stone, with a little mastic and turpentine beneath the stone, and make an impression in wax and carry it at your neck, you will obtain from kings and princes whatever you wish. It must, however, be carried most justly and most chastely.

The figure of a serpent having a man upon its back, and a raven upon the same: if you find it carved in some stone, whoever carries this stone with him will abound in all things, and will be shrewd and prudent.

The figure of a man standing upon a dragon, holding a sword in his hand: if you find it carved in hematite, put it in a leaden or iron ring, and all subterranean spirits will obey him, and will reveal to him all treasures

The figure of a man standing, wearing a breastplate, with a helmet on his head and a drawn sword in his hand: if you find it carved in some stone and set it in an iron ring of the same weight, no one will be able to resist the bearer of this in battle.

The figure of a bearded man with a long face and curved eyebrows, sitting upon a plough between two bulls: if you find it carved in some stone, it is effective for plantations and every kind of cultivation, for finding treasures, and for warfare; it will turn enemies into friends, and is effective in many illnesses. If anyone carries it, serpents will flee from his face. It also heals epileptics; and if a child carries it at the neck, it drives away fear from him, and the harassment of evil spirits. If a sick person carries it, he will recover health. And in order that it may have greater power, it should be placed in an iron ring, with double weight in relation to the stone.

The figure of an eagle standing: if you find it carved in aetites and put it in a leaden ring, the one who carries it will be accepted and loved by people, and animals will obey him.

TREATISE III.

THE SIGILS OR IMAGES OF HERMES.

Hermes, in book 14 of the Quadripartite, records these images.

The image of a man carved in diadochos, standing and of great stature, holding an obol in his right hand and a serpent in the other; and let the figure of the Sun be above the man’s head, and let him hold a prostrate lion beneath his feet: when it has been placed in a leaden ring with a little artemisia and fenugreek root, and you have it with you on the bank of a river and call upon the aquatic spirits, you will receive from them answers concerning the things asked.

The figure of a man standing and holding a bundle of herb hung from his neck, with thickness of the loins and breadth of the shoulders: when it has been carved in green jasper, know that it provides aid to those suffering fever and frees them from fever. And if someone practices the art of medicine and carries it with him, it grants him the greatest favor in distinguishing illnesses, recognizing medicines and herbs, and giving potions; and in every hemorrhage and flow of blood it helps and immediately restrains it.
