ON THE METHOD OF MAKING SEALS AND MAGICAL IMAGES.

TRACTATE VI.

Having set out the magical seals and images, and the method of making them, so far as it has indeed been handed down by the most ancient writers themselves, it remains that a more exact method of making them also be added here, and that the preceding matters be explained more precisely.

And indeed, before everything else, in their composition the time must be considered: the time of election, I mean, must be carefully observed in inscribing or carving figures on stones. For they receive a greater power from heaven if they are carved at fixed and suitable times, since in this way the stars exert their influence more strongly. Hence one who wishes to arouse love uses favorable aspects, but one who wishes to kindle hatred uses unfavorable ones. Therefore they wish the image of Saturn to be fashioned when he himself is in Capri-

Thus the image of Leo or Cancer ought to be set into a ring or stone when the Sun is passing through Leo and the Moon through Cancer, yet in such a way that each is free from every impediment, and especially from the rays of Saturn or Mars.

It must also be held that any sign, so long as the Sun is in it, becomes living, rules the others, and has its effect felt before the others; and therefore the planet must always be directed to the place, that is, to the sign, face, and degree that the Sun illuminates.

Thus far concerning the magical images of the ancient sages. I have chosen to enumerate them here chiefly for this reason: so that it may be plain that they were constructed from the principles of magical philosophy, that is, of true and natural magic, not of Goetia and Necromancy; although it is certain that superstition has crept into many of them through abuse. Use has praise; abuse has blame. For if anyone should wish, with superstition, to direct them toward doing evil, he will be contrary to the divine order of the art itself and to the principles from which they flowed; and he should fear lest, ensnared by mad superstitions, he become wholly mad and superstitious.
