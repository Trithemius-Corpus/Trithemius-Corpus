The image of a bat, if it is carved in heliotrope, gives the bearer power to resist demons.

The image of a well-adorned man holding something beautiful in his hands, if it is carved in carnelian, has the power to staunch blood, and contributes to honors.

The image of a lion or Sagittarius, if made in a stone, is effective against poisons and frees one from fever.

The image of an armed man with a bow and arrow, if carved in iris, has the power to guard the bearer, and the places in which he stays, from evil.

The image of a man with a sword in his hand, if made in carnelian, has the power to preserve the place where it is from lightning and storm, and it guards the bearer from vices and from bewitchment.

The image of a bull, if made in prase, is said to assist in works of harmful magic, and grants favor in masteries.

The figure of a hoopoe with the herb dragontea before it, if made in beryl, will have the power to summon aquatic spirits, to compel them to speak, and to receive answers from them concerning questions asked.

TRACTATE II.

THE IMAGES, OR SEALS, OF CHAEL.

Chael, one of the sons of the sons of Israel, a very ancient teacher, when he was in the desert, observed and had many figures carved according to the course of the signs and planets, and recognized a very great effect in them. And so that these powers of the images might be known to posterity, he composed a book, from which the following have been excerpted. And blessed be God, who gave such powers to the earth for the salvation of the human race. (These words are found in the very book written by Chael, immediately at the beginning.)

The figure of a man sitting behind a plow, with a long beard, a long face, and curved eyebrows, on whose neck there are four men lying, and who holds a fox and a vulture in his hands: if such a stone, on which these things have been carved, is hung from the neck, it is effective for all planting and for finding treasures. For if, when going to bed, you place it beneath your neck, in sleep you will see treasures and the manner of extracting them. It also has another power, namely to help the illnesses of brute animals when they have drunk water in which this stone has been washed. It must be carved on the day and hour of Venus.

The image of a man having a shield on his neck and a helmet on his head, holding a sword in his hand and trampling a serpent under his feet, carved in red jasper: if it is hung from the neck, he will overcome everyone in battle, especially if the engagement takes place on the day of Mars.

The image of a horse having a crocodile upon it, carved in hyacinth, has the power to render the bearer victorious in civil cases; it makes him loved, obedient, and amiable. It must be set in gold, because its power is increased by gold.

The figure of a seated man, and of a woman standing before him with her hair spread down toward her lower back, and of a man looking upward, carved in carnelian: its power will be to acquire goodwill from all those touched with this stone. A little amber and turpentine must be placed beneath the stone.

The figure of a foaming and fiery horse, upon which a black man sits with a scepter in his hand, carved in hematite, grants the power of ruling and restores lost favor. It must be set in equal weights of gold and silver.
