You have no care for the sheep, you who take the wool and the milk and entrust their keeping to faithless hirelings. You avaricious men, you unclean men, you proud shepherds, you devour the blood of the poor; by the plurality of your benefices you despoil poor priests who are better than you in learning and holiness. Behold, you shamefully consume the substance of Christ’s patrimony; behold, day and night you give yourselves to the pleasures of the flesh, and you give not the least help to needy priests, learned and holy. Therefore the wrath of the just judge Jesus Christ will rage against you; he will require the blood of his sheep from your hands.

I do not wonder at the hatred of laymen toward clerics, who by their conduct deserve no affection; for, as the Lord says through the prophet, because of their crimes priests have become contemptible among the people. Deservedly we suffer, O priests, deservedly we suffer the reproaches of the people, because we despise the commandments of God. For the Wisdom of the Father says: Those who despise me shall be without honor. And would that only wicked priests were despised, and that injury were not also done to the true and holy ministers of Christ. Would that the ignorant crowd did not spread the crime of a few over all, and that what the wicked destroy in the ecclesiastical commonwealth, good priests might somehow restore. But now the just are condemned among the ignorant together with the impious; and because of the hatred owed to the wicked, even from the good the honor customarily given them is withdrawn.

Such now is your commonwealth, O holy mother Church: among your priests and judges those who might correct the wicked are lacking; among them only those who need correction are found. Woe to the shepherds who feed themselves, who neglect the sheep of the Lord, who serve earthly gains, who seek their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ. Alas, mother and virgin, bride of Christ, Church, to what great misery you have come. You once grew and expanded amid the storms of persecutions; you humbled the necks of the proud; you trampled upon the savagery of tyrants; you subjected the kings and princes of this world to your rule. Now you are torn by your sons, despised, and reduced almost to nothing. O how many saints, how many martyrs, how many virgins, how many confessors at last

did you once send on to Christ with joy in your tribulation; but now in peace your bitterness has become most bitter. Once, in tribulation for the faith of Christ, you were full of bitterness; yet because of the innumerable multitude of your holy sons you were joyful, glad, steadfast, cheerful, and consoled by the Lord. Now, however, in your peace, because of the small number of good sons, you are filled with the bitterest bitterness. Nor is it strange. Your shepherds, with few exceptions, are mute dogs unable to bark. They buy the leadership of your sheep and sons for a price; they acquire it with innocent blood; they violently invade it with sword, spear, and lance, not for love of Christ, not for your honor, but because of avarice, pride, arrogance, and an insatiable lust for ruling. They come to prelacies uncalled; they approach unelected; unworthy men fraudulently thrust themselves in; and under the name of ecclesiastical liberty, as though they had done well, they wickedly defend themselves.

These are ravening wolves, who come in sheep’s clothing, scatterers and enemies of the Lord’s flock: they unworthily seek ecclesiastical dignities; they buy prelacies with gold and blood; by right and wrong alike they acquire rule over the people. These are blind guides, evil guardians, perverse servants, unjust judges, who destroy the commonwealth of the Church; who kill the sheep of Christ by their corrupt conduct; who give laymen an occasion to persecute the Church; who grant the devil boldness to rage against the Lord’s flock. These are the foxes that demolish the vineyard of the Lord, namely the holy Church, because, though appointed as watchmen, they negligently fail to stand carefully on their guard, and offer adversaries the power to do harm. From this follows among the people a plague of crimes and the destruction of souls. Because of an unjust leader, an army is often destroyed.

As we now see priests standing in relation to God, so also we see laymen standing in relation to them. Contempt for God is now quite justly punished in the priests of the Church by the contempt of the people, and the negligence of his ministers is avenged by the seizure of temporal goods by laymen. Believe me, venerable priests: God permits nothing against you without cause. Without doubt he would defend his Church from the assault of plunderers, if our conduct did not provoke him to vengeance. Therefore let us impute to ourselves what we suffer; let us ascribe it to ourselves.
