For Jericho is interpreted as the moon, or its waning. Therefore he goes down from Jerusalem into Jericho who, falling away from justice, comes to the spring of vices. But before I proceed further to the explanation of our theme, since in all actions, especially theological ones, one must begin with prayer, lest we strive in vain, we must implore divine aid, without which the speech of the speaker profits nothing and the intention of the hearer bears no fruit. Therefore, to obtain the grace of speaking and to secure the fruit of hearing, let us greet the most holy mother of God, the inviolate Virgin Mary, with devout mind and sincere intention, bending our knees to the ground in adoration, saying: Hail Mary.

I should very much wish, reverend fathers, for the glory of almighty God, that such power of speaking had been bestowed on me from above that, for your edification, compunction, and devotion, I might be able most fittingly to develop, according to the condition of our commonwealth, the theme taken from the Gospel of the present Sunday. For just as that man going down from Jerusalem into Jericho, after wounds had been inflicted by robbers, was despoiled and left half alive, so the commonwealth of the universal Church, so the condition of almost all religious communities and orders, so too your commonwealth, O fathers, lies despoiled and half-destroyed, with blows of wicked deeds inflicted everywhere, with wounds of crimes, with punctures of vices.

And to begin with the general commonwealth, which regards all faithful Christians, who through the Catholic faith are reckoned as one: where is the condition of its former beauty? Where is faith? Where is love of God? Where is love of brotherhood? Behold, we see in the Christian commonwealth that avarice, lust, pride, discord, anger, envy, and the other plagues of the soul abound everywhere; with savage cruelty, in the manner of robbers, they lacerate the condition of the Church with very grave blows, despoil it, strip it bare, and slay it, so that, left half alive, it has neither hope nor the beauty of its ancient loveliness.

What will you do, O holy mother Church? What physician for your wounds will you call upon? A priest? But having seen you, he passes by; he spurns your distress, despises your pains, scorns your wounds. A Levite? But the wicked minister of a wicked priest does not love your honor, casts aside care, hates the healing of wounds. A Samaritan? But you are far from Samaria, nor will you easily be able to find the one you seek. What then will you do? How will you be able to return to your former

health? There is no one who grieves over your affliction; there is no one who pours wine and oil into your wounds; there is no one who entrusts care of you to a faithful innkeeper. Behold, those who ought to judge crimes commit them, and, lest their own be reproved, they willingly dissemble those of others. Justice is sold for the cheap price of silver, and virtue is measured by defense of statute. The rage of Christians at odds with one another rages savagely, and, peace being broken, quarrels are stirred up among citizens.

Once kings and princes were accustomed to build and endow monasteries and churches; but now, with a few exceptions, turned into robbers, they violently seize ecclesiastical goods, spurn the priests of God, and scorn the commands of Christ's prelates. The love of God has withdrawn from human hearts. Reverence for priests has departed. Honor for the sacraments of the Church, alas, has expired; and as the people are, so is the priest, because among priests just as among the people, avarice reigns, lust rages, pride is mad, anger is savage, and uncleanness of both mind and body renders them contemptible.

O priests, priests, who by your sins and crimes throw the ecclesiastical commonwealth into confusion; who devote yourselves to avarice and luxury; who neglect the worship of God; who shamefully consume the alms of the faithful: how harsh a sentence of sudden damnation you are going to undergo, you who lead into guilt the people whom you ought to have instructed by the example of truth and holiness. Woe to you, ministers of pleasure, for your overthrow is very near. There is no fear of God before your eyes, no dread of the laws, no terror of the inward judge. Your damnable way of life has given laypeople an occasion for sinning, since, because of your evil life, you do not dare to teach rightly.

How will an avaricious priest rebuke avarice? I am lying if I have not known priests, sons of peasants, so inflamed by the torches of avarice that some possess ten, others twenty, and some thirty ecclesiastical benefices, both with cure of souls and without cure, and yet they neither feed nor visit the flock committed to them.
