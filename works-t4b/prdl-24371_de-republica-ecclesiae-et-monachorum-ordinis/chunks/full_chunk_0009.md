Innkeepers were the other pontiffs, princes, and many abbots, whose names we omit for the sake of brevity, who in various provinces, places, and times reformed our order according to their ability. Innkeepers also are you, O reverend fathers, who have each assembled for the preservation of our commonwealth, who cultivate regular observance, who love the Bursfelde union. You, I say, are innkeepers; see to it that you are found faithful, you to whom the true Samaritan has entrusted the sick order. It is required of you that you pour into its wounds the wine of the holy Scriptures and the oil of devotion. For, as we said above, our order once shone gloriously through these two things.

Therefore, if you desire to reform the commonwealth, or even to preserve it in reform, devote yourselves to the study of letters and to holiness of life. Let none of you say: “What need is there for me to go through many things in study, when few things can suffice for living well?” Believe me, fathers: for an abbot to live well, it does not suffice to know few things. For if few things sufficed, the holy fathers would have composed so many volumes for the instruction of morals in vain. For an abbot must be learned in divine law; and even if the study of letters conferred nothing else, it is nevertheless useful for the extirpation of vices, as blessed Jerome says: “Love the knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” Nothing so despises the world, nothing so subdues the flesh and lust, nothing so kindles the love of God, as ardent study and love of the Scriptures.

Therefore let no one excuse himself from holy study; let no one blame the lack of teachers: love conquers all things, charity can do all things, humility endures all things. If a teacher is lacking, O brothers, read the holy fathers in their volumes for yourself as your instructors. Nor do I wish any monk whatever to run about to schools, because in the monastery he will very well be able to learn thoroughly what he is commanded to know. For the proverb says: true love

of learning does not require Athens, because at home a sufficient Minerva is available to the diligent. But let us beware lest we become teachers of error to ourselves. For study, especially solitary study, unless it burns with the love of Christ, often produces error. Therefore let us apply the oil of compunction, and the learning of the Scriptures will be useful.

Let us look upon our commonwealth, fathers, and labor for its just healing as much as is possible for us. Let each one of us strive to reform himself according to the Rule of Saint Benedict: let him flee vices, spurn pleasures, not love riches, and despise the world. Let us be abbots of our brothers according to the Rule: shepherds in solicitude, fathers in charity. Let us feed them with the word, let us feed them by example, let us feed them with sustenance. If anyone omits one of these, he will be a useless shepherd.

Therefore remember, you shepherds, to whom you are going to render an account; and so stand diligently on guard over your keeping, so that for the sheep entrusted to you, you may be able to answer securely to the highest shepherd. Adorn the commonwealth with your morals, adorn it with saving knowledge, adorn it with piety, adorn it with love, so that when you have ministered well in this brief misery, you may acquire the best rank in eternal happiness, which may the highest shepherd, Jesus Christ, grant to us, who lives and reigns without end. Amen.
