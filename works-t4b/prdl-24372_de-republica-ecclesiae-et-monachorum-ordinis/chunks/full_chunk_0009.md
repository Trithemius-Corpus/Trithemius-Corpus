The innkeepers were also the other pontiffs, princes, and many abbots, whose names we omit for brevity, who in various provinces, places, and times reformed our order according to their ability. You too, reverend fathers, are innkeepers: you have each assembled for the preservation of our commonwealth; you cherish regular observance; you love the Bursfelde union. You, I say, are innkeepers, and see to it that you are found faithful. The true Samaritan entrusted the sick order to you. It is required of you that you pour into its wounds the wine of the holy Scriptures and the oil of devotion. For, as we said above, by these two things our order once shone gloriously.

Therefore, if you desire to reform the commonwealth, or even to preserve it in reform, devote yourselves to the study of letters and to holiness of life. Let none of you say: “What need have I to go through many things by study, when a few things can suffice for living well?” Believe me, fathers: for an abbot, knowing a few things is not enough for living well. For if a few things were enough, the holy fathers would have written so many volumes for the instruction of morals in vain. For an abbot must be learned in divine law. And even if the study of letters conferred nothing else, it is nevertheless useful for rooting out vices, as blessed Jerome says: “Love knowledge of the Scriptures, and you will not love the vices of the flesh.” Nothing so despises the world; nothing so subdues the flesh and lust; nothing so kindles the love of God as ardent study and love of the Scriptures.

Let no one, therefore, excuse himself from holy study; let no one blame the lack of teachers. Love conquers all things; charity can do all things; humility endures all things. If a teacher is lacking, brothers, read the holy fathers speaking to you in their volumes. Nor do I want any monk to run about to schools, since in the monastery he will be able to learn very well what he is commanded to know. For the proverb says: “True love of learning does not require Athens,”

for at home sufficient Minerva attends upon the diligent. But let us beware lest we become teachers of error to ourselves. For study that is too solitary, unless it burns with the love of Christ, often begets error. Let us therefore apply the oil of compunction, and the learning of the Scriptures will be useful.

Let us consider our commonwealth, fathers, and, as far as is possible for us, let us labor for its just restoration. Let each of us strive to reform himself according to the Rule of Saint Benedict: let him flee vices, spurn pleasures, not love riches, and despise the world. Let us be abbots of our brothers according to the Rule: shepherds in solicitude, fathers in charity. Let us feed them by word; let us feed them by example; let us feed them with sustenance. If anyone omits one of these, he will be a useless shepherd.

Therefore remember, you shepherds, to whom you are to render an account, and so stand watchfully over your charge that, for the sheep entrusted to you, you may be able to answer securely to the supreme shepherd. Adorn the commonwealth with your morals; adorn it with saving knowledge; adorn it with piety; adorn it with love, so that when you have ministered well in this brief misery, you may acquire an excellent rank in eternal happiness. May the supreme shepherd, Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns without end, grant this to us. Amen.
