*De Laude Scriptorum Manualium* — "In Praise of Scribes" — is Trithemius's short *tractatus* defending the monastic labour of hand-copying manuscripts at the very moment the printing press was making it obsolete. He composed it in 1492, as abbot of Sponheim, and addressed it to Gerlach, abbot of Deutz. The argument turns on the spiritual discipline of the scribe — the patient, prayerful labour of the copyist as a form of contemplation and a defence against the devil — rather than on any hostility to print as such (Trithemius had his own works printed, and was an enthusiastic patron of the new art). It is one of the most charming and most-quoted of his shorter writings.
