For even the most holy fathers, rulers of cloistered men, once had very many fugitives and apostates; their malice neither harmed the good, nor was their flight a scandal to the innocent. Hence Saint John the Apostle says of certain such men: “They went out from us, but they were not of us.” There must be fugitives and apostates in religious communities, so that the perseverance of the good may be proved.

But you, O shepherd, consider your office diligently; observe in all things the tradition of the Rule. And if any of your men departs from you without fault on your part, the supreme judge will impute it to him, not to you. For what if an evil and impious man departs? For if a monk is good, he will not flee from the monastery against his vow; but if he has withdrawn, he was not truly good. It is better for one perverse and incorrigible man to flee of his own accord than for a multitude to perish by his depraved example.

But you who perniciously conceal the faults of your subjects, who fear to disturb a community of malicious men, even if you have lived innocently according to the Rule for yourself, will without doubt perish for the sins of those committed to you who are perishing. And what difference does it make whether you perish by your own deserving or by another’s? Therefore either direct the care of souls according to the norm prescribed for you by the holy fathers, or, if you cannot preside usefully, relinquish the government. For it is required of you not only that you yourself live well, but also that by word and example you instruct those committed to you to live well.

Now therefore, shepherds, fear your danger; govern the sheep committed to you according to the Rule. Feed them with the word; feed them by example; feed them with nourishment, so that, when you have ministered well, you may attain the joys of heaven to be rewarded.

I have spoken.

End of the oration On Pastoral Care by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the order of the divine father Benedict, delivered by him in the general chapter of the abbots of the province of Mainz and the diocese of Bamberg, in the monastery of Seligenstadt, in the year of the Lord 1496, on the first day of the month of May.
