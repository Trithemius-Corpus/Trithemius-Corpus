IV. Fourth, care must be taken that whenever the discourse of the open speech has been brought to a complete sentence, the following sentence should begin with a larger, thickened letter, according to the form of the preceding example, in which, according to the sense of the Latin discourse, we have used large letters at the beginning and small ones for the rest, in the common manner.

When the secret of your mind is lengthy, so that it exceeds the narrative of the open discourse, if much remains, you should begin again from the start; if little remains, let the discourse be recalled at will from any place. For no one even moderately skilled in this technique will be unable to know where you have begun again. Nor is there need to fear the correspondence of the discourse, for the manifest narrative will always be varied together with the hidden one, and whenever a beginning is made from the start, a new appearance of speech will follow. The continuation can also be made from the end of the first book through the second.

V. Fifth, we advise that whenever you wish to write by this art in an important matter, prepare for yourself a small booklet in which you may write your secret mystery in manifest discourse. Whoever sees or reads this booklet will suppose that no secret lies hidden, nor, even if he has suspicion, will he be able to discover any of the things you write.

VI. Sixth, it should be noted that if you transpose the order of the words throughout the whole Polygraphy in each individual alphabet by changing it through metathesis, and if you write two volumes according to that transposition, one for yourself and the other for any friend, you will write between yourselves whatever you wish most securely, and no one lives on earth who could penetrate your secrets.

This transposition, moreover, can be made in infinite ways.

VII. Seventh, we warn you in advance that one must write polygraphically in one way to a man ignorant of the Latin language, and in another to one skilled in it. For the former, all the words must be written out completely, without abbreviation, distinct, complete, fair, and wholly legible, without partition, without division, and without confusion, lest, deceived by an error, he think one word is two, or suppose two words to be one. The things set down must be heeded.

VIII. Eighth, care must be taken that the person to whom you write mystically by this art has a book in every respect like your own; otherwise you would write in vain and fruitlessly to someone who does not understand.

IX. Ninth, if you wish this technique to remain forever secret and hidden, rewrite the Polygraphy anew, but do not place the alphabet letters before the words, and no one will easily suspect the method of the secret. But so that no difficulty may occur for the person writing and reading the mystery, let the letters of the alphabet be written on a sheet in the order corresponding to the words, and let that sheet be fitted movably to their headings. The letters may also be engraved on a copper plate, so that they can be moved from one order into another.

X. Tenth, it should be known that although we do not use the Pythagorean Y in German and Latin words, we have nevertheless admitted it on account of the Greek language, so that we might have a doubled twelve. Likewise we have accepted the doubled W as the twenty-fourth letter, which is wholly necessary for us in the German language.

that understanding is needed in the reading. And therefore, whenever he receives a sheet or booklet written polygraphically, let him go to a secret place and, with the book of the Polygraphy opened, which he must have with him, let him examine each word throughout all the alphabets, and let him most diligently note with chalk on the table, or with ink on paper, what letter each one represents. And when he has inspected the whole letter in this way, let him compose the letters into words, and he will easily understand what lay hidden in concealment.

XII. Twelfth, kings and princes who have many deputies in different places should note that it is not prudent to write to all of them with one kind of hidden narrative. Therefore, so that they may have for each person his own secret mode of writing, let them change the order of the words as many times as they wish to have separate modes. Thus they themselves will possess the modes of writing of all, and none of their subjects will understand the mode of another. For the variable modes of writing more secretly by this art are infinite.
