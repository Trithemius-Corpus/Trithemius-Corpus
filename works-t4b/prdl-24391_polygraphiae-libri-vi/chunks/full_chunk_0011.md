On this occasion I have not thought fit to pour higher matters into the light, since an inopportune time seems to stand in the way. I have prefixed the letters of the Latin alphabet in order to the individual words with this consideration: that the operator may have an easy method in his undertaking, and that the reciprocal party, in reporting back, may be relieved of excessive labor. The paraphrases that are set opposite, outside the straight order of the lines, are not without the mystery of the art, since they serve light-bearing propositions for the necessary consummation of perfection, not obscure ones. For this reason they are also taken up in the former and neglected in the latter. For they themselves are certain means by which every suspicion of an adversary is removed. Everything has been set down according to reason, so that not even one proposition is introduced idly. For example, the farther the sun advances from the equinoctial circle, the more the days are lengthened for those who dwell in those regions.

Therefore, so that unity may proceed in the right order, through the ternary, into the binary, the paradigm that we hand down must be observed with greater care. And first indeed, after prior consideration, let the operator, understanding his intention, note what he is going to grant to the ternary through the binary; let him divide the day together with the night by minutes, and let him distinguish the minute divisions of the winds not by an alien but by the proper supposition of the phrase. In every mystical business let him carefully beware lest he overturn the established order of the art, or substitute one paraphrase for another, or grant to one what is owed to another; otherwise unity does not admit the binary, and the whole operation will be confused and useless. Let night not, through excessive length,

wander without direction; not advancing, it will stand beyond the limits appointed for it by the art. Let curiosity not change water into snow, nor let him sleep by day who keeps watch by night. Let the mind, solicitous in its intelligence, also make use of art, lest the hand, through its own lightness, commit an error, which, though slight and small in the monad, is always accustomed to appear large and very great in the binary. Only one paraphrase from any order is admitted into the service of the secret; following the example of a servant, whenever a small particle of light has demanded it of the day, one phrase likewise adhering to it is taken up, not several. These things must be commended to a vigilant mind, and care must be taken lest the wind be animated.

After this, a rule is prescribed for one approaching the practice of the art: that, insofar as he can, he should imitate by mystical art the single person of a human being, composed of two natures, namely the soul, surpassing visible things, and the flesh, remaining visible. For just as the soul is not seen, although it has been placed in a visible body, so the secret of the mind, in order that it may remain secret and nevertheless operate at will, requires the guidance of light, while it endures in its workshop of darkness. Therefore, whenever the body serves the soul, the servant the master, and the will reason, see that the principal power and virtue of the paraphrastic potency in the light is preserved in its natural vigor. For by this lesson of the Peripatetics, the first will duly enter the second, and the monad the binary.

Consequently we likewise advise the willing student that, whenever the leaf is moved by the wind, he should discern the property of each nature, compare the night itself with the day by degrees and minutes, and conform darkness to light.

But if Phoebus should prejudice the night, or night the sun, through which the confusion of an eclipse would arise, then either let unity take back the binary that it released in the light, or let the shadow of night be brought back to the first line of the sun. Keep fixed in memory that whatever exists anywhere has been produced in number, weight, and measure. Therefore, when you have cut into the knot of the difficulty just mentioned, count the minutes of the night; also note the paraphrases of the daylight; and so adjust one from the other that the necessary proportion may follow the action. Beware lest, during the sun's eclipse, you interrupt the necessary labor.

Pay no less attention to remembering the ancient fathers: Thuisco, who is the grandson of Noah and the father of the Teutons, and Marcomer, who is read to have been the first to lead the people of the Franks from the borders of Scythia into Germany at the mouths of the Rhine. For the Franks are brothers of the Greeks and mingled with the Germans, once making two languages from one. Hence, according to the oracle, they take up g.g. who have lost one g.

Finally, studious inquirer into Polygraphy, you are advised that after, in your desire for these inventions, you have obtained the true method of operation, you should prudently guard everything and not pour it out indiscriminately into the knowledge of all people, especially filthy ones. For this reason, do not ask, since you know that there is nothing in the world so good and holy that the wicked do not very often abuse it.
