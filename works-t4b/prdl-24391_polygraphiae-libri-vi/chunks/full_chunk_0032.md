“Bede, our countryman, in England.” On the computation and speech of the fingers. This invention is found in Bede, *On the Divisions of Times*. When you say one, bending the little finger on the left hand, you will fix its joint in the middle of the palm. When you say two, you will place there the second finger from the little finger, bent. When you say three, you will likewise bend down the third. When you say four, you will raise the little finger again. When you say five, you will likewise set upright the second finger from the little finger. When you say six, you will also raise the third, with only the middle finger, which is called the medical finger, fixed in the middle of the palm. When you say seven, the little finger alone, with the others raised, you will place above the root of the palm. Next to it, when you say eight, place the medical finger. When you say nine, set the shameless finger opposite it. When you say ten, you will fix the nail of the index finger in the middle joint of the thumb. When you say twenty, you will insert the tip of the thumb between the middle joints of the index and shameless fingers. When you say thirty, you will join the nails of the index finger and thumb in a gentle embrace. When you say forty, you will draw the inside of the thumb over the side or back of the index finger, with both alone held upright. When you say fifty, you will bend the thumb, curved like the Greek letter called gamma, toward the palm at its outer joint.

When you say sixty, with the thumb curved as above, you will carefully encircle it in front with the index finger bent around it. When you say seventy, you will fill up the index finger, bent around as above, by inserting the thumb, with only its nail held upright across the middle joint of the index finger. When you say eighty, you will fill up the index finger, bent around as above, with the thumb stretched lengthwise, that is, with its nail fixed into the middle joint of the index finger. When you say ninety, you will fix the little nail of the bent index finger into the root of the thumb. Thus far for the left hand. But one hundred you will make on the right hand in the same way as ten on the left; two hundred on the right as twenty on the left; and the rest in the same manner up to nine hundred. Likewise, one thousand on the right as one on the left; two thousand on the right as two on the left; three thousand on the right as three on the left; and the rest up to nine thousand.

Furthermore, when you say ten thousand, you will place the left hand high against the middle of the chest, with the fingers only raised toward the neck. When you say twenty thousand, you will lay the same hand broadly spread upon the chest. When you say thirty thousand, with the same hand facing downward but upright, you will set the thumb into the cartilage at the middle of the chest. When you say fifty thousand, with the same hand facing downward but upright, you will place the thumb on the navel. When you say sixty thousand, with the same hand facing downward, you will grasp the left thigh from above. When you say seventy thousand, you will lay the same hand on the thigh. When you say eighty thousand, you will lay the same hand, facing downward,

on the thigh. When you say ninety thousand, with the same hand you will grasp the loins, with the thumb turned toward the groin. But one hundred and two hundred thousand, up to nine hundred thousand, you will complete in the same order on the right side of the body. When you say ten times one hundred thousand, you will interlock both hands with the fingers inserted into one another. And so much concerning numbers expressed by the gesture of the fingers.

To these the same author immediately adds: From the very computation that I have noted above, a certain manual speech can be practiced, both for exercising ingenuity and for playing a game; by it, when the letters have been expressed one by one, someone may hand over words contained in those letters to another who also knows this art, even if he is standing far away, for him to read and understand, either signifying necessary matters more secretly by these means or deceiving the unskilled as though by divination. The order of this game or speech is as follows. When you wish to indicate the first letter of the alphabet, hold one with the hand; for the second, two; for the third, three; and so the rest in order. For example, if you wish to warn a friend placed among ambushers to act cautiously, show three, one, twenty, ten, nine, and five fingers; for the letters of this order signify “act cautiously.”

Thus far the example of dactylology according to Bede, which is varied in many other ways. For if, as was also said before, the sign of unity is set to signify the letter a, and so on in order, let one be assigned on the left hand to the thumb, two to the index finger, three to the middle finger, four to the ring finger, or the third from the thumb, five to the little finger.
