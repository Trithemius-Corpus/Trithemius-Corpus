Mystery. The Nortmanni, having once gone out from the borders of Gothia, when they were devastating Gaul by a cruel invasion, summoned *bubones* from Greece and prepared a safe hiding-place for their secrets, and so forth.

Explanation. The Nortmanni, roaming through Gaul, as Bede the monk in England testifies, in order to write their secrets safely among themselves, substituted Greek numerical letters for Latin letters, from A, the first, up to the twenty-fourth point, such as W or *gu*, according to the custom of the Gauls. This manner of writing is altogether very fine: in it all the letters are Greek, and according to the Pelasgian order of numbering they designate our Latin letters, as A, 1; B, 2; C, 3; D, 4; E, 5; F, 6; G, 7; and so consecutively up to W, which is 24. Neither a Greek nor a Latin will understand this writing unless he has first learned the method and art of the device.

Mystery. Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the deed of the Nortmanni, described several inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made the path, bright with mysteries, obscure. He himself set down artrology, and so forth.

Explanation. This monk Bede, in his little book on the inventions of the ancients, reviewed many and various methods of writing and noting secretly, by which they safely announced their secrets to friends and spoke without voice to those present. He was the first of all to commit artrology and dactylogy to writing; by their use one person reveals to another, with complete safety, whatever secret he wishes, which no one else sitting by will notice,

Bede himself taught that this same thing can be done in many other ways as well; since we have written nothing about these in the *Polygraphy*, we rightly decline to say more about them here. We long ago assigned the proper place for these difficult matters in the *Steganography*, which we are not yet thinking of publishing publicly.

And who could describe all the ancient methods of secret writing? For some had as familiar assistants in the service of the proposed art *bubones* called Philophotos, others Misophotos, and others called both into practice from the art. Again, some transposed the known letters of the common alphabet; others fashioned their own characters for themselves under the guise of barbarian letters. Again likewise, some used the service of visible letters, writing with minium or common encaustic; but others formed invisible characters at will according to their purpose.

The methods of visible characters for secret writing are exceedingly many and altogether innumerable; to mention them all is neither our desire nor within our power. Of invisible characters, however, there are three kinds more commonly used among mortals today. The first is called Dormatic, in which a certain artificial writing is made on the backs of messengers, visible only by washing with a certain liquid; no one can penetrate it who does not know the secret of the art. The second is called Hyphasmatic, by which writing is made on cloth with certain substances; when dried, it cannot be seen at all,

The third kind is called Aleotic, which is manifold and varied both in form and in material, and has almost innumerable methods of writing secretly. Its writing is done on paper or without paper, with *nemilua* and *cala*, with *raphocam*, *lapolce*, and various other things. Letters are also written and hidden in various ways and places, as under *race tabulata*, under *rofírila cubior*, in *leopi*, in *necoflas*, in *ceocali*, *locubat*, in *ratera*, in *lispilia*, and in other similar places almost infinite in number.

But because none of these things serves our present purpose, it is therefore fitting that we proceed to the matters which pertain to our subject. There is also another kind, *dactyletron*, not of writing, however, but of signs, by which one person can most subtly communicate the conception of his mind to another. This too is varied by so many differences that there is no one in the world who could enumerate all the species of so great a mystery. This is done by the principal method, and at the same time by many others descending from it, artfully, while sitting at table or in any other place whatever, very quietly and without anyone’s suspicion.

Finally, the means by which you may announce the secret of your mind without letters to someone absent, even without the knowledge of the very person who performs the office of messenger, remains assigned to the inventions of *Steganography*.
