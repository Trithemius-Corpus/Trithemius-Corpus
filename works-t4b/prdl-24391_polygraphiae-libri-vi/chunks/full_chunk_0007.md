Likewise, the Bishop and Martyr, wishing to provide for the usefulness of Christians, considerably enlarged the very teachings of the Ciceronian system and added what had been lacking for our practices.

Pharamund also, the forty-third after Marcomer, most powerful king of the Franks in Germany, when in the 424th year of the Lord’s nativity he powerfully entered the territories of the Gauls, bound foreign minutiae to his secrets so that everything might be done safely.

Clodius Crinitus too, Pharamund’s elder son, succeeding his father in the kingdom of the Franks, after he had violently subjected all Gaul to his rule, ordered all the Gauls, as conquered men and slaves, to be shorn and their beards shaved; but he commanded the Franks, victors and lords over the Gauls, to grow their hair and beards. Hence he was called “Crinitus.” In imitation of his father, he wrapped the mysteries of counsel in signs both his own and foreign.

Charles, surnamed the Great, the sixty-third king of the Franks after Marcomer, who was the first of the Franks to rule the Romans, with Nanno the grammarian as his teacher, wished to adorn the German language, which was his native tongue, with grammatical rules after the manner of Latin. He began an arduous work, which, however, being entangled in many affairs and forestalled by death, he did not complete. He bound bubones with various and numerous minutiae, a man greater than whom Brabant, or Namur in Germany, has not produced. To each prefect of the kingdom he gave individual bubones by art. they once cruelly devastated Gaul, called bubones from Greece and prepared a safe hiding place for their secrets, which today is known to few.

Our Bede, a monk in England, not ignorant of the Northmen’s deed, described many inventions of the ancients similar to these, by which they made a dark path bright with mysteries. He first commended artrology to the memory of posterity, and invented dactylogy. There were some who discerned minutiae by visible accidents; by their prudence night was turned into day, and without the service of bubones light shone in darkness. Concerning these matters, moreover, the illustrious Count Palatine, Prince Elector, formerly a requester of our steganography, received from us a manifest experiment of tested instruction.

But others, multiplying minutiae by regulated division, after the manner of barbarians overturned the patterns of nature, hiding in darkness the dances of shadows as they arose; and where the secrecy of counsel required it, they supplied cabala to the absent through diapyromata.

Great labor is involved in these things, and long exercise of the mind over many years; in this no one suddenly becomes skilled for practical use. Necessity bears all things. There were also those whom celestial harmony taught to compose foreign words, of which there was a double use for those who understood: while one suspended the sense, the other lifted the mind into the ether. painters of phantoms divided one wind into two at a point; one of them, a misophos, always lurks in darkness, while the other, a lover of light, a philosophos, hates darkness, loves light, and guards the secret by means of a bubo. Again, with Bede as witness, there were some whose advancing purpose bound the wind, invisible to the eyes, to visible things, and compelled many plants, and no less stones and fruits, which lack voices, to speak with a human tongue. Why should I bring in brute creatures, when all things obey art? This secret is rightly preferred to all inventions up to now; through it the hidden mystery will take on strength for small things, and by no means cast pearls before swine. From nothing, nothing is brought back. The known multitude of visible things, if these things are prudently adapted to them, the enlightened mind of man will know and behold wonders. But to treat more fully of these matters is not part of the present purpose; since they are greater and weightier, they require another work and another time.
