With God’s mercy, we have brought the sixth and last book of our Polygraphia to its close; in it, as we conclude our discourse with the wandering stars, we give the reader certain points of advance warning. First, whenever he uses the alphabet of any sign or planet, he should prudently add its character or that same sign, for the sake of a double advantage. By this, excessive labor is removed from the friendly reader, and the mystery will remain secret, while anyone ignorant of the art will think that a table written in numbers pertains to signs or planets, and will suspect no hidden mystery at all in the letters. But we have adopted letters from a distance, so that suspicion is not to be aroused among writers; for a clever reader, when he is touched by suspicion of something hidden, if he discovers the function of one number, will easily compute the whole alphabet.

Second, whoever desires to be a keen practitioner of this matter, remember that, as often happens, a doubled letter should be written only once and marked above with a point. For example, I wish to write Ioannes from the alphabet of Aries: I do not double 18, which is n, but write thus: 14. 16. 7. 18. 10. 23. Or abbas: 6. 7. 6. 23.
