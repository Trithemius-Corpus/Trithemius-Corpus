Many men flourished in those times, most learned and most eloquent in every kind of knowledge: theologians, astronomers, physicians, orators, historiographers, and others like them, not only among the gentiles but also among Christians. At last the persecution of the unbelievers against the Church ceased, after Constantine Caesar the Great accepted the faith, in the year of the world 5539, after the midpoint of the rule of Anael himself, the angel of Venus. Although afterwards it was several times disturbed in part by the impious, nevertheless it remained for a long time entirely very quiet. From then on the human race, which from the time of King Ninus had wandered miserably for 2300 years in the worship of idols, was mercifully recalled to knowledge of the one God.

The various arts of subtlety were increased in these times, and through what was fitting to the nature of Venus they took on adornment and growth. For human customs change with time, and lower bodies are disposed according to the influence of the higher. The mind indeed is free, and does not receive the influence of the stars, unless, by bending itself too much toward the commerce it has with the body, it stains its own affection. For the angels who are the movers of the orbs destroy or overturn nothing that nature has established. A comet of unusual size preceded the death of Constantine. The Arian heresy disturbed the holy Church in many places at the end of this rule. In the time of Julian Caesar, crosses appeared on people’s linen garments.

In Asia and Palestine there followed wars, pestilences, and famines in those places where the crosses had appeared. In these times also, around the year of the Lord 360, the Franks took their beginning in Germany; later, after they devastated Gaul, they named the conquered land Francia after their own name. The description of Francia was great in length and breadth; its metropolis was Mainz, but now Würzburg alone. The Bavarians, the Rhinelanders, the Saxons, the Thuringians, and the surrounding bishoprics today occupy a great part of Francia in Germany.

Likewise, in the 280th year of the government of this Anael, the Roman Empire began to decline, when the city was captured and burned by the Goths, the imperial seat having first been transferred under Constantine into Greece; this was the beginning and cause of the whole decline of the monarchy. For around the end of this rule of Anael there arose Radagaisus and Athaulf, kings of the Goths; likewise afterwards Genseric of the Vandals, and also Attila of the Huns, who wretchedly tore the empire apart, as is clear in these histories.
