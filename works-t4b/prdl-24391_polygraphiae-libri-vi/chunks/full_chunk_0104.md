What we promised at the beginning of the third book of our work Polygraphy, to provide the shadow and likeness of a new gloss before the inexperienced, we have sufficiently fulfilled by way of example, with economy of labor. We have entrusted the night, safely serving secrets, to be guided by the shadows of periphrases, and we have inserted the numbers in the order of four lines, all the way to the end, adapted for the use of those who wish it. The places are fixed degrees of minutiae, numbering three thousand one hundred fifty, although, if you wish, the moments of the hours remain infinite, for concealing whatever secret may arise in every nation and language of the world. The orb, coming into being linearly from a point, as often as night exceeds the image of light, you will be able, as much as you wish and as need requires, to bring back to the path by which you came. If much of the night remains, bring the dragon’s tail back to the original caudal place at the head. But let the binary be altogether certified by the marks and signs of unity. He who has been very hungry should labor much, so that he may have something to eat. Nor is it a matter of great labor to lead wherever you wish something that proceeds of its own accord. Here, therefore, we have set the end to the third book of our Polygraphy, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Gentle reader, farewell.

From Peapoli, on the 7th day of the month of March, in the year of our Lord Jesus Christ, our Savior, 1508, in the eleventh indiction.

The Fourth Book of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of the monastery of St. James the Greater, Apostle, at Wuerzburg, formerly of Sponheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, begins.

The order of reasoning required that our fourth book of Polygraphy be made unlike the preceding books in character, invention, and ease, so that those who wish to share in the secrets of our matters may attain this not by one method but by various and diverse ones. We have extended a magpie comprehensible by a very easy invention, so that night, always joined through a minute point to the monad, may be recognized in the light of day in any order. Here no disagreement is found between unity and the binary; nor will the person wishing to perform the art need to have the instrument of the art at hand, since the thing following the first, without an intermediary, reveals the hidden mystery.

From this, as a prudent, understanding, and experienced person in the science, he will satisfy the operation at every time and place. The binary receives from unity, whence it produces the ternary. The knowledge of this cryptology is easy at first sight, once the moon has been illuminated by the sun even at a single point. Although it can also happen that a knowing person becomes skilled without the guidance of a master. Yet I think this is to be understood only in this book, and in none of the preceding ones. As for the following books, judgment will be given in its proper place. Among all the cabalistic methods of secrets that we have given up to the present, the procedure of this fourth book can most easily be extended to infinity. Therefore it is enough that I have handed down to those who desire fuller things both the form and the reason for the example. The light of understanding easily penetrates all these things.

I wrote at Peapoli, on the ninth day of March, in the year of the Lord one thousand five hundred eight.
