EXPLANATION OF THE FOURTH BOOK OF OUR POLYGRAPHIA.

In the fourth book of the Polygraphia, in a similar way, we have placed words devised by us in alphabetical order, but in one point alone we deliberately differ from the preceding book. For in the preceding book the words contain nothing else in themselves by way of proper meaning, except that they represent the letters placed before them at the head. But in the fourth book, which we now have in hand, each word contains its own force and nevertheless designates the letters placed before it. They have within what they display without, because the second letter of each word always serves the hidden mystery, as we shall make clear in the following example: Ecorach hanafar dulay femalas, chorion furiel anephor scabaras busarat vimalo tropen tumelech amasar. To understand this mystery, take only the second letter from each word,

In this very secret method of writing, if anyone has been trained by long practice, he does not need the book, but will readily compose, on the spot, words suited to his purpose. But anyone less practiced in composition necessarily requires a copy of the book from which to write. A copy of the volume will not be necessary for the reader, since without the book he will easily be able to find the hidden secret by means of the second letters everywhere.

Yet in great, difficult, or dangerous matters, this method of writing should not be trusted as entirely safe, because the diligence of a skilled man might by chance find the hidden mystery. This method of writing can also be changed by an intelligent man, so that the significant letters are placed in the third or fourth position of the word, with the words altered.

A BRIEF EXPLANATION OF THE FIFTH BOOK OF OUR POLYGRAPHIA.

In the fifth book of the Polygraphia we have handed down various methods of writing secretly by transposition of common letters, arranged in such an order that under their covering we shall be able to write and announce everything safely to an absent friend. Now, to speak in our own way, the progression of the transposition of letters is almost infinite, since countless things arise from one alphabet by metathesis.

First we described a straight table containing twenty-four alphabets; through understanding it, as many alphabets can be composed as there are stars counted in the firmament of heaven. For as many characters as there are in that table, so many times there arise by the art, in order, ten hundred thousand alphabets. After this we set out a reversed table distributively, which will arise as often into another table as we have changed the first letter at the head. The first letter in the first straight table is B, and in the reversed table Z. As often as you put any other changed letter in their place, so often you will find a table new in every respect, and so on to infinity.
