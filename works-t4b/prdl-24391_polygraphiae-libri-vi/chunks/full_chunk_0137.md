24 ON THE SEVEN SECONDARIES

The Tartars, invading the lands of the Christians, Constantinople, and Greece, inflicted much damage on the Christians. The Saracens seized many cities in Asia and killed more than four hundred times a thousand Christians. After Rudolph died, Adolf of Nassau was elected king and ruled for six years; Albert, Rudolph’s son, later defeated him in battle near Worms and killed him, and was elected emperor in the year 1298. He ruled ten years and was killed by his brother’s son. The Order of the Templars was destroyed by order of Pope Clement V. The island of Rhodes was taken by the Christians from the hands of the Saracens, in the fourth year of the siege and continuous war against them. After Albert was killed by his nephew, Henry VIII, Count of Luxembourg, was made emperor; he ruled five years. When he died, Louis IV of Bavaria ruled for thirty-two years, beginning in the year 1315; the Roman pontiffs gave him the crown. Frederick, Duke of Austria, opposed him, but was defeated by him. After Louis, Charles IV, King of Bohemia, was made emperor; he raised the bishopric of Prague to an archbishopric and ruled thirty-one years. There were very great earthquakes. This Charles newly instituted many things concerning tolls in favor of the prince-electors. Gunther, Count of Schwarzburg, calling himself king, opposed Emperor Charles, but he had no success against him. After Charles, his son Wenceslaus ruled twenty-two years; after him Jodocus, Margrave of Moravia, succeeded Sigismund,

brother of Wenceslaus. Wenceslaus was deposed. Leopold, Duke of Austria, while fighting in battle against the Swiss, was killed together with eight counts and more than four thousand men. Under Wenceslaus, King of the Bohemians and emperor, the doctrine of the Hussites had its beginning. After he was deposed, Rupert, Count Palatine of the Rhine and Duke of Bavaria, was elected, and ruled ten years. In the year of the Lord 1369 the Christians brought war upon the Saracens, but it ended badly because of the arrogance of the French, since more than 100,000 of our men fell, besides the captives, among whom was John, Duke of Burgundy. There were many wars in these times. In the year 1407 Sigismund became emperor for twenty-seven years. He tried to devastate Bohemia in order to extirpate heresies, but made little progress. The kingdom of France was cruelly devastated and afflicted by the English and the Burgundians. When Sigismund had departed this life, Albert, Duke of Austria, his son-in-law, succeeded him in the year of the Christians 1438, and ruled only two years, an excellent man and worthy of empire. When he died, Frederick III, Duke of Austria, his son, was made emperor by the election of the princes, and ruled fifty-six years, a man divine in intellect and peaceful in spirit. He began to reign in the year of the Lord 1440. In the year of the Christians 1453 Constantinople was taken by the Turks through the betrayal of a certain Genoese, and gradually all Greece fell away from the Christian faith.
