Not everything is to be communicated indiscriminately to everyone, especially things whose use can be turned to good or evil. Moved reasonably by this consideration, we have openly handed down many things in these six books of the Polygraphy to those who wish to know; but we have allowed many more secrets to lie hidden beneath what is manifest. Finally, we have written a key that explains the whole work, showing how it is to be understood in each metaphor. Yet, by the counsel of certain men, we have ordered it to remain hidden at home, so that students may not lack material for exercising the memory, and that the wicked may be deprived of the occasion for knowing things which they are not willing to use well. In individual passages many mysteries lie hidden; and the greater the things you discover from these, the greater still are those that remain to be discovered. A way is prepared for beginners, in which it will not be difficult for those who advance, in the course of time, to discover many things, rare and very great. Here one who did not know how will learn to write in Latin, and prudently to wrap all the mysteries of his will in a foreign speech. But that an unlearned person may be able not only to understand his own mysteries which he has wrapped up, but also openly to set forth the matter in a language of which until then he had always been ignorant, is a task of no great labor, if the paraphrast has fully grasped our intention in this volume. For beneath the bark lie the kernels of our Steganography, buried with just reason, lest they be exposed to unworthy babblers. A Booklet of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Spanheim, on the heavenly intelligences governing the spheres after God.

It is the opinion of very many of the ancients, most wise Caesar, that this lower world is governed, by the ordinance of the first intellect, which is God, through secondary intelligences. Assenting to their opinion, the Conciliator of the Physicians says that seven spirits were set over the seven planets from the beginning of heaven and earth. Each one of these governs the world for 354 years and, in order, four months. Very many most learned men have hitherto given assent to this position. I make it known to Your Most Sacred Majesty only by reporting it, not by asserting it.

--- Page 624 ---

The first angel, or spirit of Saturn, is called Orifiel. God entrusted the world to him to be governed from the beginning of creation, and his rule began on the fifteenth day of the month of March, in the first year of the world, and lasted 354 years and four months. Orifiel, however, is a name of office, not of nature, attributed to the spirit by reason of his action. Under his rule human beings were rude and rustic, dwelling in solitude like beasts. This needs no proof from me, since it is manifest to all from the text of Genesis.

The second governor of the world is Anael, the spirit of Venus. After Orifiel he began to rule the influence of his planet in the year of the world 354, in the fourth month, that is, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of June, and he governed the world for 354 years and four months, up to the 780th year of the created world, as is clear to one who calculates the period. Under this rule of Anael, human beings began to be more cultivated, to establish houses and cities, to discover manual arts, weaving, tent-making, and things similar to these; also to indulge more fully in the pleasures of the flesh, to take beautiful wives for themselves, to forget God, to depart in many things from natural simplicity, to invent games and songs, to play the cithara, and to devise whatever pertains to the order and worship of Venus. And this wantonness of life endured among human beings until the flood, taking up the proof of its own depravity.
