*BOOK I.*

Let us rejoice that the end of the first book of our *Polygraphy* is at hand; made readier by it for what remains, we judge that we must attempt what the powers of our talent may be. Newly discovered things sometimes trouble the beginner, because one whom all will follow has no one preceding him. The first beginning of any invention is harder; afterward, once it has been made firm, it gives the author greater confidence.

And so, completing the first book of our invention with God's favor and aid, we have divided the whole into three hundred eighty-four small units, assigning to each, in order, its proper grades of paraphrases, as may be seen. And these form a sufficiently beautiful Christian harmony, with sixteen varied stations, joined throughout in due order by more than two hundred nine thousand phrases. Nothing is lacking here for students of these inventions of ours, so that they may have the fullest knowledge and capacity for announcing mystical, secret, and hidden matters, even very far off, with the greatest safety.

Therefore, whenever you have crossed the straight line of day and have not fulfilled the purpose of the nocturnal syntax, turn the head back to the tail, and, with a binding made, let there be a fastening of both to one another.

Arrange the shadow in such a way that it does not confuse the sun by likeness of sound. Do not fear embarrassment if, in the first calculation, the day has not included the shadow; rather, count up the small units of the lines until the necessary proportion follows according to your wish. You will also be able to make a leap from the first into the second, knowing that in the first creation of days evening and morning were made one day. Therefore, when evening has been completed, lead the day back into the following one, and, once the sign has been given through the point, fix the known mystery for the binary and the monad together.
