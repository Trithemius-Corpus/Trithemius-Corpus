FROM THE GRAMMARS OF OTFRID, MONK OF WEISSENBURG.

We read that Charles, king of the Franks, surnamed the Great, taking pity on the barbarism of his nation, attempted, with Nanno, Theobald, Albinus, and Berenger as helpers, to regulate the German language. And although, as Turpin bears witness, he had begun so arduous a work, he did not complete it, being delayed by frequent incursions of wars, but, overtaken by death, left it unfinished; nor did another arise after him who would recall the care of this matter to mind. But Otfrid, the aforementioned monk, once a hearer and disciple of Rabanus of Fulda, under Louis the Pious, the son of Charles himself, attained that Grammar, although imperfectly, and wrote much in our German language, which seems to sound with the wonderful dignity of regular instruction. For he composed many poems according to the same rule, in both heroic and elegiac meter; moreover he also composed some things in prose for Louis, king of Germany, son of Louis the Pious and brother of the emperor Lothar; also for Otgar, archbishop of Mainz, for the monks of Saint Gall, and for others. Charles the Great, king and most Christian emperor, waged war with the Saxons for no fewer than thirty years. At last, overcoming them by the sword, he converted them to the Christian faith.
