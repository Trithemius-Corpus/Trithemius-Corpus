THE KEY TO THE POLYGRAPHY OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF SAINT JAMES OF WURZBURG, formerly of Spanheim, of the Order of Saint Benedict, of the observance of the Fathers of Bursfelde.

A Preface to the Explanation of the Whole Work of the Polygraphy of Johannes Trithemius, Abbot of Pæapolis.

No one who reads these things, as I trust, will judge me either proud or superfluous because I myself have commented on the Polygraphy recently composed by me, especially if he can weigh exactly what cause led us to speak obscurely. For whatever, if made public, can do harm had reasonably to be concealed in mystical language. But the subject matter that we treat in the Polygraphy is very good, useful, and honorable among good men for the advantage of the commonwealth, and for this reason, persuaded by reasonable cause, we wrote it: that by its service we might render good men safe and secure from the investigation of the wicked when announcing their secrets to one another.

Yet lest an opportunity be offered to wicked and perverse men, who even more frequently misuse good inventions, to deceive the simpler and less cautious in their judgment of things, we hand down in it mystical, or hidden, methods of writing, and we have covered the truth lying hidden in the marrow with certain coverings of figures. Still, we do not at all doubt that any industrious man, eager with desire for these matters, will easily grasp everything if he applies even moderate persistence in searching it out. For the things I have handed down are not so difficult, nor wrapped in such great mysteries, that they cannot be thoroughly penetrated by a studious man. For everywhere we have so tempered the riddles of our diction that they are readily open to learned and studious men, and nevertheless remain inaccessible to sluggish men of darkness.

But after it pleased me to offer the work of the Polygraphy itself, dedicated to the most serene and unconquered Maximilian, Caesar of the Romans, Emperor of the world, ever Augustus, I judged it worth the effort to make it clear and open by a brief and concise interpretation, lest the mind of his sacred Majesty, which is daily stretched by many affairs and cares, be drawn back from understanding it by the darkness of the mysteries. For unless my opinion deceives me, the work will be the more pleasing and useful to his Majesty in proportion as it is shorter and clearer.

44 EXPOSITION

First, therefore, with the Lord granting it, we shall briefly explain the meaning of our proposal, which is contained in the Prologue wrapped beneath various riddles; then, through the individual books of the Polygraphy, we shall succinctly set forth, according to their order, the methods of operation, rules, precautions, and distinctions that remain necessary, as far as the powers of our talent allow. All these things, so that they may more quickly become accessible to those who desire to know, we shall show step by step through examples, and we shall be as brief as the matter and reason permit.

THE KEY OF THE EXPLANATION OF THE POLYGRAPHY OF JOHANNES TRITHEMIUS, ABBOT OF PÆAPOLIS.

Exposition on the Prologue.

Our entire intention in the whole work of the Polygraphy turns upon this purpose: that we might hand down fixed methods of writing secretly, through which kings and princes, nobles and commoners,

priests and laymen, learned and unlearned alike, may safely and without any suspicion of a hidden secret announce to one another, and to any friends, even those far distant from each other, by the service of letters, whatever conception of their minds may be needed, and as often as it may be needed.

And lest we seem to have presumed to do this either first or alone, we forewarn the reader that many of the ancients long ago, especially tyrants, princes, and kings, moved by similar consideration, devised for themselves familiar and varied methods and rules of writing, by which they attempted the same thing in general. From their number, for the sake of an example of the proposed matter, we first introduced Caesar Augustus, whose hidden method of writing you should take openly in this way.

Mystery. He changed the breathings of the conceived voice from bright signs into dark ones; and by a certain beautiful invention, through the movement of metathesis, he made their resisting barbarism of sound conformable to his command.

That is: the great Caesar Augustus, in order to write secretly and safely what he wished, changed the letters of the alphabet by various transposition, so that he placed B for A, C for B, and so on for each. And so that the writing might remain more intricate and more secret, at his own judgment he transposed the letters across their breadth and length to any distance of order, number, and place.
