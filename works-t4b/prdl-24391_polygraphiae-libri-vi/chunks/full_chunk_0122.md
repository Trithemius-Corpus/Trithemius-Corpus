ON THE SIGNS AND THE WONDROUS BUT EXCESSIVELY LABORIOUS METHOD OF WRITING OF M. T. CICERO, AND AFTER HIM OF S. CYPRIAN, BISHOP AND MARTYR.

Marcus Tullius Cicero, the eloquent orator of the Romans, wrote a book of signs of no small extent. S. Cyprian, bishop of Carthage and martyr, enlarged it with many signs and words, adding vocabulary necessary for Christian uses, so that the work itself became useful not only to pagans, but much more also to the faithful.

The manuscript is rare, found by me only once, and bought at a cheap price. For when, in the year of the Lord's nativity 1496, I was examining many libraries out of love for books, I found the aforesaid manuscript in a certain monastery of our order, neglected because of its extreme age, cast away under dust and contempt. I asked the abbot, a doctor of law, how much he valued it. He answered that he would prefer to it the recently printed little works of S. Anselm. I went to the booksellers, since the matter took place in a metropolitan city, and bought the requested little works of Anselm for one sixth of a florin; to the abbot

and to the rejoicing monks I handed them over, and I rescued the manuscript, already brought almost to destruction. For, out of love for the parchment, they had decided that it should be scraped.

Almost two years after this, I rode to Strasbourg on business of my order, and, being admitted by Ioannes Keisersberg, the distinguished preacher of the place, into the library of the greater church, I found a psalter written entirely in the same signs of Tullius and Cyprian, and most handsomely written with golden capitals. But an inscription had been placed on the outside by someone ignorant of the mystery: Psalterium in Armenica lingua. I brought in a learned man, showed the falsity, and advised that it should be rewritten thus: Psalterium notis Ciceronianis descriptum. Whether he did so or not I am uncertain, since I did not afterwards return to that library. So that you may see, reader, the form of Cicero's notation, we have decided to append a few signs.
