But if, because of the number of affairs or of one's household, the alphabets we have marked out should not suffice for everything, or if some of them should seem too open and obvious, we can introduce various new transpositions, whose number may be very great, while the method of the secret always remains perpetually hidden. Let us give an example of this: let b stand for a, f for b, l for c, p for d, t for e, z for f, c for g, m for h, and so on successively through the whole usual alphabet, so that between any assumed letter there lie three unused and transposed letters. Afterward four, then five, more or fewer, yet in such a way that the whole alphabet, as though turned back into a circle, comes together, and no letter at all is omitted, but each one is placed in the same sequence for another in its own order. In whatever way you have written it, you must either know it firmly by heart or entrust it firmly to memory, lest through forgetfulness

you fall into error and can no longer read what you yourself had written. For the transposition of letters can be varied in as many ways as, according to the estimation of the human intellect, which, enclosed in temporal operation within a mortal body, does not properly grasp infinities, there will arise a number innumerable to a mortal man. Hence anyone who wishes to have more methods of writing by metathesis, and methods removed from our instruction, if he is even a little ingenious and diligent, will easily attain the desired effect in his operation. For the human mind can accomplish very great things if it is aided by study and art. But he who despises the smallest things will never reach the greater ones. I know that there will be very many who will mock these studies of ours as empty and childish; to them we give this answer: that we shall be entirely content, provided that these things please those at whose request they have been composed by us.
