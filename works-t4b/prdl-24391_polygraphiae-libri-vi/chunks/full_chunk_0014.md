For the pupil of the new art will write a language he did not know, and he will understand and interpret Latin discourse composed under regulated instruction, if you yourself have sufficiently directed him word for word. Finally, this instruction can be extended to all the affairs and business of the world. If anyone wishes to learn from me how these things can be done, let him first remember to show himself such that he deserves to receive it. For not everything is owed to everyone, but one man is to be preferred to another.

The fourth utility is that, by extending the likeness of the thing received, all these things can be extended into any language whatever, once the instituting binary has been reduced to unity. Yet not just any person can do this indiscriminately, but only the one whom art and nature have dignified, with God going before by his special grace.

The fifth utility is that, by imitation of the likeness, we can communicate to one who knows the art of our invention whatever secrets of our mind you wish, however abundantly, in manifold ways, and most widely, by various methods, which cannot be known or noticed by any other person. Here there is given a manifold speech without voice, through which, safely, securely, very widely, with all suspicion removed, we shall be able to indicate fully to those who know the art all the conceptions of our mind, however difficult. And this marvelous invention has innumerable progressions,

and is capable of all mysteries. The sixth utility is that, through understanding this work, you will be able to discover any regulated metathesis or transposition of letters whatever, and nevertheless to form them in such a way that they serve only those who know the art, and absolutely no one else in the world. And there is great force in the form and also the operation of metathesis, which both has several modes and is varied in many ways.

The seventh utility is that, through understanding this volume, a very great faculty is given to those who desire to enlarge their memory without labor, whenever the seeds of minutiae are set in their proper degrees. For the sake of goodness, all things lie hidden, wrapped in the secrets of words and figures; if anyone desires to understand them more fully, let him recall to mind that persistent labor conquers all things. For it will have been enough for me to have discovered the principles of the art, and to have handed down a crude, and deliberately unformed, method of writing symbolically. For there was neither time nor reason for our inventions to be shaped in a more polished way, lest honey produce vomiting in the nauseated, or truth produce hatred in the indignant. Every age must be valued according to its own merits, and swine are not to be adorned with pearls, nor burdened with precious gems. Everything is untimely that does not accord with the morals and times of the mortals upon whom it is thrust. Many of the noblest talents of men have belonged to times not their own, and almost every day more new things are discovered in times not proper to them. UTILITIES OF THE WORK.

Bartolo, under Pope Innocent VIII, to have had a marvelous invention of nautical art, which in a certain little book he offered for sale for one hundred thousand ducats, when neither the Supreme Pontiff himself nor the Genoese, to whom he proposed that they buy his matter, deigned to come to terms with him even for a small amount? For he promised that by his art he could keep a ship unharmed in the middle of the sea for thirty months, repair it when broken, and recall without danger anything cast into the deep. But no one cared. Nor does it escape me that many, with Naso, have perished by their own genius. Therefore it is better to set a limit to knowledge than to expose oneself fruitlessly to the hatred and detraction of many, for whom it is more familiar to despise any knowledge whatever than to have learned it.
