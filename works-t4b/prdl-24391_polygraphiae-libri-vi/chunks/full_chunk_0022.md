Therefore in the following four books we shall provide easier methods of writing, though they are not entirely free from the suspicion of those who look at them, because they offend the understanding of most people. But in the Steganographia there are richer methods of writing most secretly and without any suspicion, where one word does not represent a letter, but each word represents another whole word suitable to the mystery.

Let us give an example of this. If you wish to forewarn a friend about some imminent danger under such a narrative of words as: “Melancius, Tyberius, Johannes, and Petrus, servants of the Count of Asoto, have conspired for your death, and on the fourth day after Lawrence’s day, in the night, they will surround your house; take thought what you should do.” Steganography disguises this secret as follows: “I ask you, dearest friend, to send me by the bearer of the present letter ten florins as a loan, because they are very necessary to me for the construction of a certain building; I shall faithfully restore them to you.” Who would believe or suspect that this secret lies hidden in the discourse written above?

Let us put the same thing also under another narrative: “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who willed to become man, to suffer, and to die for us, have mercy on me, an unworthy sinner, now and in the terrible hour of my death.” See, now you have one hidden meaning of your heart veiled, within the bounds of steganography, under two narratives, in speech brief but faithful,

We have arranged fabricated words, containing nothing in themselves but merely representing the letters of the alphabet set before them; through these you will be able to write and make known all the secrets of your mind to a friend, however far absent, with the greatest security. But those unskilled in the art and ignorant of it, when they see the writing, will think it is some foreign language, and will be wholly ignorant of what it contains.

But when you wish to write or communicate something to an absent person by the method of this book, take care first of all that you properly distinguish each and every word from the others, so that each one can be very clearly discerned from another, lest the person to whom you write take one for two, or two for one, because that would produce a great error. Let us set down an example of this matter: Abrin madon badis, cadulur pasu, adin loroff masara damis, boda omo dromidrus vares mastron phosat, galos, prosol fane lemoros, aro soca bilneso, hamol heliet rhamil festa doron hubor rodex.

In this narration of invented discourse there is contained this brief secret message of a friend warning a friend about danger: “Do not come to court, because the king is angry with you.” You see how easy, safe, and secure this method is of writing and communicating whatever you wish to a friend who understands it.
