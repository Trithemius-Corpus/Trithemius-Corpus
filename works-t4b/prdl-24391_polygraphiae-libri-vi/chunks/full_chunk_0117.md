Up to this point we have set out, by way of example, the method of the ancient Franks of Germany for writing numbers with Latin letters, so that we might also provide knowledge of it to those who do not know it. But the fact that, in setting down the figures of the algorism, we departed from the common rule of computation was done not from error but for a reasonable cause, namely so that we should not be burdened by excessive progression. The sixth book of the Polygraphia of Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Saint James the Greater the Apostle at Würzburg, begins.

Before any invention and use of letters, in the beginning of the world, when human beings were still crude in understanding and uncultivated in manners, under the government of the shared succession of three intelligences, as some suppose, succeeding one another in order through one thousand sixty-three years, the influences of the planets were in the sphere, presiding in their own way over bodies on earth. The ancient speculators on this matter say that Saturn, from the beginning of the world, chiefly presided by influence over things germinating and being born on earth, with his intelligence governing him, whom they called Orisiel, that is, the life-giver of God, for three hundred fifty-four lunar years, likewise four months, and as many days and hours. We recount these things as poetic, not as things we confirm. After Saturn, Venus is said to have assumed dominion; after Venus, Jupiter. But after Jupiter, Mercury, the fourth ruler in order, adorned the minds of human beings

with various arts. Under the three intelligences just mentioned, that is, while Saturn, Venus, and Jupiter were ruling, they assert that human beings did not have the use or knowledge of letters. Therefore, whenever one person wanted to announce some secret to another, he did this not in writings, since the service of letters was lacking, but symbolically, by certain likenesses of animals, trees and plants, or of some other sensible things, according as each had agreed among themselves.

The time of Saturn, as they say, gave to human beings manners properly suited to his influence. For it made them farmers, hunters, founders of cities, crude, woodland-dwelling, gloomy, simple, uncultivated in life and manners, and living a long time in the flesh. After Orisiel, the intelligence of Saturn, as those who hold this opinion say, Anael, the mover of Venus, received the government of the world by influence and continued it for three hundred fifty-four years, and for four months, days, and hours. Under him human beings began to be more cultivated, to build houses and cities more frequently, and to invent various manual arts, namely weaving, wool-working, and many other things similar to these. They also began to sink more deeply into the pleasures of the flesh, to take beautiful wives, and, after many children had been begotten from them, they fell notably away from natural simplicity. They invented games and songs, began to play the cithara, and, following the movement of the flesh, more eagerly devised whatever they affirm belongs to the influence of Venus.
