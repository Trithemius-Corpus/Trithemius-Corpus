Near the end of the governance of this spirit of the Moon, Nabuchodonosor, king of Babylon, took Jerusalem, destroyed King Sedechias, and led the whole people away captive. The prophet Hieremias was illustrious; he had foretold this destruction, and likewise the future liberation from Babylon.

After Gabriel, Michael, the spirit of the Sun, again assumed the fourteenth rule of the world. He began on the first day of the month of May, in the year of the world 4606, in the fourth month, and ruled the world in his order for 354 years, down to the year from the creation of the world 4960 and eight months. In the time of this rule, Evil Merodach, king of Babylon, restored liberty and a king to the people of the Hebrews, under the direction of Michael, who, as Daniel writes, stood for the people of the Jews, who had been entrusted to him by God. Likewise in these times the monarchy of the kingdom of the Persians began; its first king, Darius, and its second, Cyrus, utterly destroyed that most powerful kingdom of Babylon in the days of Balthasar, as Daniel and the prophets had foretold. In these times the Cumaean Sibyl was illustrious. She offered nine books to King Tarquinius Priscus to be bought for a price; in them was contained the course of future warnings and the ordering of the whole republic of the Romans. But when the king refused the price, the Sibyl burned the first three books while he looked on, and demanded the same price for the remaining six. When he again refused it, she also gave three others to the fire to be burned.

She would have done the same with the last books, had the king not been persuaded by the counsel of his men and redeemed them from destruction by paying the price that had been demanded for all of them. Likewise, the Romans abolished kings and established two consuls each year. Phalaris the tyrant occupied Sicily in those times. Magic also was held in great esteem among the kings of the Persians at that time. Pythagoras the philosopher and many others then flourished among the Greeks. The temple and the city at Jerusalem were rebuilt. Esdras the prophet, using memory in place of the archetype, restored the books of Moses burned by the Chaldaeans, who were also called Babylonians. Xerxes, king of the Persians, led an army against the Greeks, but accomplished little. Rome was taken, burned, and destroyed by the Gauls, except only the Capitol, which was protected by a goose that roused the weary fighters. The very famous wars of the Athenians also occurred in these times. Socrates and Plato, the philosophers, lived then. The Romans again abolished the consuls and instituted tribunes and aediles, and in these times they were involved in many disasters. Alexander the Great also, after the rule of Michael was completed, reigned in Macedonia. He destroyed the monarchy of the Persians under Darius and subjected all Asia, with part of Europe, to his rule. He lived thirty-three years and ruled for twelve years and five months. After his death, countless wars followed, and many evils, and the monarchy was divided into four parts. Among the Hebrews, contention began over the high priesthood. The kingdom of Syria begins.

After Michael, in the fifteenth order, Orifiel, the spirit of Saturn, now for the third time assumed the rule of the world, on the last day of the month of September, in the year from the creation of the universe 4960 and eight months, and he presided for 354 years and four months, down to the year of the world 5315. Under his rule the Punic War began between the Romans and the Carthaginians. The city of Rome was almost entirely consumed by fire and water. The Colossus, a cast bronze image 126 feet high, fell in an earthquake. In this one year there was peace for the Romans after the Punic War; for 440 years they had never been without war. Jerusalem, together with the temple, was burned and destroyed by Antiochus Epiphanes. The history and wars of the Maccabees took place. In these times, in the 606th year from the founding of the city, Carthage was destroyed and burned for seventeen continuous days. In Sicily there was a conspiracy of 70,000 slaves against their masters. Great portents were seen in Europe in those times: domestic animals fled to the woods, blood flowed, and a fiery globe flashed from heaven with a crash. Mithridates, king of Pontus and Armenia, waged war with the Romans for forty years. The kingdom of the Hebrews was restored, having been interrupted for 575 years, from the time of Zedechias down to Aristobolus. Likewise the German Teutons invaded the Romans and, after many battles, are said to have been defeated; 160,000 were killed, apart from countless others who killed themselves with their households, under the consuls Caius and Manlius.
