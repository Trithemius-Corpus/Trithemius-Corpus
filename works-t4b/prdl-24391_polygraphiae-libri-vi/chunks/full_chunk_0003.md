For the presidents of the annual chapter of the Bursfeld observance, he abridged the statutes of the same union, one book. *The Spiritual Exercise of Monks*, one book. An epitome or breviary of the same, one book. *On Ecclesiastical Computation*, one book. *On the Illustrious Writers of Germany*, addressed to Jacob Wimpfeling of Sélestat, one book. Letters from Spanheim to various persons, four books. The orations delivered in chapters and elsewhere are twenty-four in number, making one book. He wrote sermons to his brothers of the monastery of Spanheim, forty in number and very fine, one book. Sermons to the people on feast days, one book. Several supplicatory prayers to God and the saints, one book. From the Greek language he translated into Latin Maximus the Abbot, *On the Incarnation of the Lord*; also the anathemas of St. Cyril, archbishop of Alexandria, promulgated against the heretics in the third synod of Ephesus, and certain letters of the same synod. He also wrote, for the edification of monks, a notable work which he entitled *On the Threefold Region of Cloistered Persons*, three books. He wrote certain other smaller works as well, whose titles he did not record for me. These, most beloved brother Nicholas, are the Trithemian works which, during the twenty-three years in which he was abbot, he composed at Spanheim, for the honor of God, amid cares and the continual occupation of household affairs, reading or writing by day and night whenever he was able to steal leisure for himself.

*Lucubrations*, 11

During the years in which he presided, he brought into the library of Spanheim a very great treasury of books in every faculty, not only by the art of printing but also in manuscripts. For when I was with him for the space of one year, in order to learn Greek and Hebrew letters from him, I counted two thousand volumes in the library, of which at least eighty had been handsomely written with the pen on parchment and paper. I often heard from his own mouth that he had spent more than two thousand florins on books. He had not received these from the substance of the monastery, which was always very slight, but since he was known and dear to many princes, he spent the gifts with which he was honored by them, for the most part, on books. For he is the greatest lover and cultivator of books, above all those whom I know living in the flesh today. It is said by many, however, that his successor, who is now abbot at Spanheim, has alienated very many volumes. In the year of the Lord 1504, when a fierce war had arisen between the interregnum and Philip, Count Palatine, Trithemius, for fear of the enemies, withdrew with his library and the possessions of the monastery to Kreuznach, the nearest town, and I likewise returned to my own affairs. But in the sixth year above five hundred and a thousand, in the month of October, Trithemius himself, spurred on by the envy of certain men, accepted the abbacy of St. James at Würzburg, and resigned that of Spanheim.
