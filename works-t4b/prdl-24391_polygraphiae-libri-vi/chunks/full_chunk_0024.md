Now this method of writing is as follows: in the first black alphabet, take one letter of the secret sentence; from the second, another; from the third, a third; and so successively to the end. When you have reached it, remember that you must return to the first order as often as necessary, until by concealing it you have completed the secret mystery of your mind. But so that you may see the order, let us set down an example. Hxpf gfhmczfueib gmbt gx hsr egerbd qopma wvz. fxeqk ak tnr qxyz. The meaning of this mystic discourse is: Beware this man, because he is evil, a thief, a deceiver, a liar, and unjust. You now see, reader, what a marvelous transposition of the letters of the alphabet this table produces, since there is no one who, without knowledge of it, could penetrate this secret. For this method of writing surpasses every common transposition of letters, since each letter is always changed from one series of the alphabet into another. From the reverse table also, which we expanded in order by a similar distribution, let us set down this example by way of introduction. Rdkt, ftz nyb, teuqz, fu zf, fd. drgh, vfd. Its hidden meaning is this: Beware this man, because he is evil. And note that under the example of the straight table already given we carried the hidden series through from the beginning throughout it, and then continued likewise through the reverse table; and again we make a circle, as you see, to the beginning of the straight table. Hence it must be considered that one series of writing can be carried through both tables, just as we have already done in the demonstration of the first example,

The strongest proof, moreover, which increases knowledge, is frequent use in any art. After the expansion of the tables there follow certain orchemata, in which there is not only a transposition of all the letters, but also an ordered transition of one over another. For orchema in Greek is called transitio in Latin. And first we marked the leap of the letters in expanded form; afterward we reduced it into an orchema. In the operation of this orchema, a marvelous leap occurs. There are twenty-four alphabets in it. In the first no letter comes between. In the second, one. In the third, two. In the fourth, three. In the fifth, four. In the sixth, five. In the seventh, six. In the eighth, seven. In the ninth, eight. In the tenth, nine. In the eleventh, finally, ten; and so successively up to the twenty-fourth alphabet, in which twenty-three letters come between; afterward a return of likeness occurs to the beginning. The method in the orchemata for writing a mystery is similar to that which is observed in the tables of transposition of letters: namely, when you are going to write according to the rule of the orchema, you take only one black letter from each alphabet, which the letter written in red at the head indicates. When, in writing, you have reached the end of the orchemata, and the begun secret of your heart has not yet been completed, you should know that you must return to the beginning as often as necessary, until the mystery has been completed in every respect. And know that from one orchema of letters as many alphabets are composed as the diligent investigator may wish to have. Finally, this method of writing by orchemata, that is, by transitions and leaps, is the safest of all, because there arises a transposition of letters that is not uniform, but varied in many ways. And it should be noted that true and perfect knowledge of the tables and orchemata, if you are diligent and studious, gives understanding of all writings that are written out in any way by transposition of letters; and it is a great secret, which few know: to be able to discover all meanings concealed in any transposition of letters whatever.
