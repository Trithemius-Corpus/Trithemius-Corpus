Indeed, so far as concerns me, I would rather appease my rivals by silence than provoke them by letters or writings to madness. For I judged it better to condemn all my marvelous discoveries to perpetual silence than to incur, even through men’s false estimation, the charge of magical or necromantic pernicious superstition. Let Steganography, therefore, lie hidden in darkness, and let it not become common to Bouillus’s mendacious company, which is accustomed to judge of matters unknown and to corrupt the reputation of a good man merely from a lust for depravity.

Nevertheless, at the urging of friends, so that our rivals may understand, along with Bouillus, how rash and lying an injury he has done us, while Steganography remains within its own bounds, we consent to publish the present work, which we call Polygraphy, divided into six books. Let those who wish read it, and let friends read this volume without resentment; when they have understood all its hidden mysteries, let them themselves, without envy, be judges whether they are natural or superstitious. For I know and am certain that all these things are sincere, pure, natural, and far removed from every pursuit of evil superstition.

But whenever the discourse proceeds rather obscurely, it is for this reason: lest a madman abuse, for destruction, the sword of defense which a man sound in mind and thought carries for his own protection. Yet nothing evil is contained in it on that account, since even good things are abused for evil by the perverse. But whoever presumes to condemn this edition of ours

For I am a Christian; I am a priest; under the rule of the divine father Benedict I am also a monk. I love Christ, and with whatever sincerity of mind I can, I always worship him devoutly. I have no dealings with demons, I have had none, and, with God as my protector, I shall have none; no pursuits in magical, necromantic, or profane arts; no occupations; no teachings. Whoever thinks otherwise of me thinks wrongly, does me injury, and defends Bouillus’s open falsehood. But now let us set these things aside, and let us recount in order what is contained in each book of our Polygraphy.

PINAX, OR INDEX OF THE THINGS THAT ARE TREATED IN THE FIRST BOOK

In the first book of this Polygraphy of ours, we arrange the words of the Latin language, as many as are discerned, by placing them in the order of our ternary. By their use and service, all princes throughout the world will find a secure aid in any secrets whatsoever, one that will remain perpetually hidden without danger to the mystery, without mark, and without suspicion of the Bacucei. For the Bubones this road is secure, and it is to be violated by no investigation by anyone. Knowledge of the hidden matter is granted to two only, if the binary is skillfully brought back to the monad.

Ignorance is the mother of error, and he who scorns to learn the good that he does not know loves blindness. The paraphrases themselves are set out in the manner of one either warning or entreating, and they can be varied for every visible subject of this world. For they are suited to individual mysteries, and are set before the secrets of the Greeks no less than before our own. When you have understood the things you see, do not rashly despise the hidden things you do not see. You see the accidents; the substance remains invisible. Hence greater things lie hidden, lesser things are open to sight. Since this has been done according to reason, let no good man take it in an evil sense, since from these things every capable person could, if only he wished, make progress to higher matters.
