*Of Trithemius.*

He recently sent me these titles when asked. To the emperor Maximilian he published a remarkable work, which he entitled *Polygraphy*, six books. To the same, the key to that same *Polygraphy*, one book. Replies to eight questions of the same emperor, one book. To the same, *On the Seven Intelligences Moving the Spheres*, one book. To Joachim, margrave of Brandenburg and prince-elector, twenty books of various questions, orations, and propositions. To the same, *On Foreknowledge Permitted and Not Permitted to a Christian*, two books. To the same prince he wrote that remarkable and laborious work, the *Steganography*, eight books, which that rash Bouillus, not understanding it, criticized most ignorantly and most falsely. To the same prince, one book of letters. To the same, *Against Sorcerers and All Arts and Superstitions Forbidden by the Church*, five books. To the same, *On the Causes and Cure of the Falling Sickness and of Sorcerers*, three books. To Jakob Trithemius, his brother, an itinerary of his life, two books. To Burkhard von Horneck, physician, *On the Peculium of Nuns*, one book. He wrote a history, or annals, *On the Origin, Kings, and Deeds of the People of the Franks and Germans*, in three large volumes, beginning the narrative from King Marcomer, who in the 433rd year before the incarnation of the Lord led the people from the borders of Scythia into Germany, to the mouths of the Rhine, down to the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth year of the emperor Maximilian. To Lorenz, bishop of Würzburg

and duke of eastern Franconia, he wrote an epitome or breviary of the histories in those same three volumes, three books. To the same, *On the Dukes and Bishops of the Franks of Würzburg*, one book. *On the Miracles Shown at the Memorial of Our Lady St. Mary at Dettelbach*, two books. *On the Miracles of the Same Shown in the Nettle-Patch at Heilbronn*, six books. An apologetic work against Charles Bouelles, who slandered matters he did not understand, one book. *On the Origin and Kings of the Franks*, one book. A chronicle of his monastery, over which he now presides, of St. James at Würzburg, one book. To Albert, of the margraves of Brandenburg, archbishop of Mainz and Magdeburg, the life of the holy arch-prelate Rabanus, three books. On that same man’s translation from Mainz into Saxony, one book. To the same, the life of St. Maximus, bishop, one book. To Johann, count palatine, duke of Bavaria, and count in Spanheim, residing at Simmern, *On the Origin, Kings, and Dukes of the Bavarians, and also of the Counts Palatine and Counts of Spanheim*, four books. He has in hand a laborious work *On Demons and Profane Arts, Sorcerers, and Superstitions*, which will have twelve books, in which he drives out, confounds, and overturns all the tricks of the diabolical arts. Letters of Würzburg, which he wrote in that abbacy, four books. He also wrote many other smaller works, suited to his own devotion and that of his friends, such as offices, a rosary, a missal

office, and various prayers, together with proses or sequences on St. Anne, the grandmother of our savior. He wrote praises, prayers, and a rosary on St. Mary Magdalene; a rosary on St. Joseph, foster-father of the Lord; a rosary on St. Peter the Apostle; a rosary on St. John the Evangelist. At the request of Johann, archbishop of Trier, he wrote a sequence on Anne, which begins, “Jesu, of the king of heaven,” under the melody *Ave praeclara*. Another, to be sung on her feast, which begins: “Let them exult on this day.” A third, addressed to the provincial of the Carmelites at Augsburg, which begins: “Let us rejoice in honor.” A fourth on St. Hildegard, virgin, which begins thus: “Praise be to you, Lord, king of eternal glory.” A fifth on St. Rupert, duke of Bingen, which begins: “Let them exult on this day.” A sixth on St. Joseph, foster-father of the Lord, which begins thus: “Let us sing, renowned ones.” A seventh on St. Rabanus, archbishop. An eighth on the wounds of Christ, which begins thus: “Let us adore Jesus Christ; let us weep for the crucified.” A ninth on the compassion of the Blessed Mary ever Virgin, which begins thus: “Come, let us weep with devout mind, sharing the suffering of the blessed Virgin Mary,” under the melody, namely, *Ave praeclara*. A tenth on St. Joachim, father of Our Lady, which begins thus: “Let us adore the Trinity; let us praise together the unity of Father, Son, and Spirit.” An eleventh on each person’s own good angel. A twelfth for the proper mass of each priest. A thirteenth on St. Maximus, bishop of Mainz. A fourteenth on St. Martha, virgin, Christ’s hostess. A fifteenth on St. Mary Magdalene.
