For indeed, just as all perished in the flood outside Noah’s ark, so now all who are outside the unity of the catholic Church of Christians will without doubt perish eternally in their sins: Jews as well as pagans, heretics likewise and schismatics. The justice of God condemns those whom his goodness created. But mercy saves those whom grace has confirmed, because, as the holy Apostle says, it is not of the one who wills, nor of the one who runs, but of God who has mercy; for he has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens. If we rightly weigh the freedom of human choice, we shall also know the just will of God.

ON THE MIRACLES

Of Unbelievers. Third Question.

The third question of your most serene majesty was: If no one is saved outside the faith, whence come miracles among unbelievers, since we know from the report of many that astonishing things occur even among unbelievers?

The solution of this question, as it seems to me, will not be difficult if we give credence both to the Scriptures and apply the necessary consideration to the natural powers of the soul. A miracle is a new and unusual event, which occurs beyond ordinary custom and draws human beings into wonder. Hence good Christians perform miracles in one way, bad Christians in another, and unbelievers in yet another. Good Christians perform miracles through public righteousness; bad Christians, however, through signs of public righteousness; but unbelievers through private contracts with demons, that is, implicit or explicit ones. Concerning the good, indeed, the Lord said in the holy Gospel: These signs shall follow those who have believed. In my name they shall cast out demons; they shall speak with new tongues; they shall take up serpents; and if they drink anything deadly, it shall not harm them. They shall lay hands upon the sick, and they shall be well.

Concerning bad Christians, however, who perform miracles in the name of Jesus Christ, we read thus written in the Gospel: Many will say to me on that day: Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in your name, and in your name cast out demons, and in your name done many mighty works? And then I shall declare to them: I never knew you; depart from me, all you who work iniquity. But outside the Church of God, where the name of our Lord Jesus Christ is not invoked, if any marvels are done beyond the custom of nature or human art, no good Christian doubts that they are done by the cooperation of demons. And works of this sort your Caesarean Serenity, by discernment of power, judges should rather be called marvels than miracles. Nor does this judgment depart from the reason of truth, insofar as there should be a necessary distinction between the prodigies of light and of darkness.

I find four agents of wondrous things, but I recognize one alone, God, who truly works miracles, chiefly as the cause of all good things. Therefore the first and greatest agent of all marvels is God, who has illuminated the whole world by filling it with signs, miracles, and mighty works; none of the rest is to be compared with him. He created heaven and earth, the sea and all things that are in them; he alone is omnipotent, just, holy, and upright; good, incomprehensible, greatest, glorious, immeasurable, and everlasting. Let all other things be beneath him, for whatever can happen wondrously in this world is certainly less than the whole world itself, which he was able to create from nothing. Every miracle, too, that can be done through a human being is less than the human being himself, whom God, the creator of all things, formed from the beginning in his own image and likeness. And although the visible miracles of God among mortals, through the constant habit of seeing natural things, have now almost become commonplace, nevertheless, if we behold them wisely, they will appear greater and rarer than all unusual things. But the manner in which God performs miracles is the unchangeable will of God, which, since it is omnipotent, does all things whatsoever it has willed in heaven and on earth.
