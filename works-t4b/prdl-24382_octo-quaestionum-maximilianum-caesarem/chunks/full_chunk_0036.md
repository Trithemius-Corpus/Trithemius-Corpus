ON THE PROVIDENCE OF GOD
The Eighth Question.

The eighth question of your Serenity was this: whether it can be proved by natural reason, as also from the Holy Scriptures, that God has care for human affairs and actions, and a sure and infallible providence over all and each of the things that are done in the world.

To this question I remember that I answered as follows. The whole world, heaven and earth and all things that are in them, are subject to divine providence; by its will and command they were brought forth into being from the beginning. For there is nothing in the whole universe that is not governed by the providence of the Creator. Let us confirm this opinion first by the Scriptures, and afterwards by reasons, as briefly as possible. In the book of Judith we read: “All your ways are peaceful, and you have placed all judgments in your providence.” And in Ecclesiastes the most wise king Solomon speaks thus: “Do not give your mouth so as to make your soul sin, nor say before the angel, ‘There is no providence,’ lest perhaps the Lord, angered against your words, destroy all the works of your hands.”

The most manifest testimonies of providence are confirmed in the holy Gospel, when our Savior says to the disciples: “Are not two sparrows sold for an assarius? And one of them does not fall to the ground before God?” This is a great commendation of divine providence, which has care not only for men but also for the smallest birds. Indeed, if the Almighty cares for such small things, who would not believe that he has care for great things? “Look,” he says, “at the birds of heaven, which neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and your heavenly Father feeds them.” And a little later he adds, saying: “If God so clothes the grass of the field, which today is and tomorrow is cast into the oven, how much more you, of little faith?” Since God is almighty, because he created all things and brought them into being when they did not exist, he rules and governs the universe lest it fall and pass away. This could in no way happen if he did not have providence and care for all things. For if providence is removed,

it is necessary that the order of continuity of the whole universe be thrown into confusion. But the providence of the Creator cares for all things, since he who feeds the birds, gives their food to the beasts of burden, fills every living creature with blessing, and clothes the grass of the field, adorning violets and lilies with beautiful grace, cannot forget man, whom from the beginning he created in his own image. He who governs all things cares for all things; and he carries out providence not only for man, but also for all beasts of burden. All things are placed under his dominion, and there is no one who can resist his will in anything.

Let us now, therefore, consider this vast structure of the whole universe: how it always perseveres, most orderly, in its original arrangement and motion. Unless we shamefully go astray in mind, we shall understand by the most manifest reason that the divine and unchangeable providence preserves, directs, and governs these things. Next let us contemplate all earthly things, each of which, most orderly, is born and proceeds in its own natural arrangement. This cannot happen by chance; rather, they must be moved by the will of him by whose power they were made and created.

Then, in turn, consider the earth placed in the middle of the world like a point or center, which unfolds the circle at an equal distance from the whole universe; nothing more beautiful, nothing more adorned, nothing finally more delightful to the sense could present itself as suitable. In this point of an immense mass, enclosed by that very mass, we discern all things wonderfully ordered, and nothing could be left unordered. Thus all things that sometimes seem to us somehow to float about, we may know for certain, do not lack their own particular order within the order of the universe. For by the simple and altogether unchangeable nod of God, the whole structure of the entire universe, composed of so many diverse and moving things, is ruled and harmonizes in the most orderly way.
