But since we all offend in many things, everywhere God finds something to remit; everywhere the guilty person appears, in whom the kindly Creator may show his mercy. Hence the holy bishop Augustine: If the disease were slight, the physician would be despised and would not be sought; if the physician were not sought, the disease would not come to an end. Therefore where sin abounded, grace also abounded, which alone distinguishes the redeemed from the lost, whom the mass of perdition, drawn from the origin of creation, gathers into one. Therefore the mercy of the Lord is over all his works.

The fourth reason for divine permission is that the justice of the Creator may be declared toward those who have sinned and have not done penance, since if all did good and no one sinned, God’s justice would have nothing to punish. Therefore, so that justice may have a place, by divine permission the creature sins. For the Lord is just, who will render to each according to the work of his will.

The fifth reason is the dignity of the human condition: because if God did not permit man to sin, he would seem worse than brute beasts, which, with no violence driving them, freely use the motions of nature. For even if brute beasts lack reason, nevertheless they have a free motion of nature which they follow; in comparison with this, man would seem of a lower condition if he did not have free choice of the will. But God permitted free will to man, over which neither kings nor princes rule.

The sixth reason is the praise of the merit of steadfastness in human virtue, since we praise a just man for this reason: that although he could have transgressed, he did not transgress, but, with the grace of God assisting him, remained in the steadfastness of a good will.

The seventh reason why almighty God permits demons to do such great evil deeds through the agency of witches is that those who, scorning truth, delight in falsehood may, seduced by the devil, the inventor of falsehood, be utterly deceived.

For it is fitting that enchanters and witches, who despise the saving teachings of Christ, should all, deceived by demons, be plunged down to hell to be punished forever. They seek aid from demons, the enemies of the whole human race, and they despise the Savior of the world, whom alone they ought to have adored. For it is just that he who, deserting truth by his own spontaneous will, adheres to falsehood should be cast headlong by the devil into destruction.

The eighth cause is the manifestation of divine power, whose might and virtue are over every creature. Indeed, when almighty God sometimes permits great things to the devil and denies certain small things, it is apparent how weak the enemy of the human race is, and subject to the divine majesty in all things. Whatever he does, he does with God permitting it. Therefore the first cause of all, on account of which almighty God permits evil deeds to be done, is justice, by which he preserves in inviolable order the freedom that he once bestowed upon the creature from the beginning. But because the power of every creature, depending on its Creator, is restricted and limited, it is manifest that he does not wish the devil to be able to do everything, nor man, however depraved in will. The will indeed is free, but power is limited by fixed and necessary degrees. For if demons could do everything they wish, nothing in human affairs would remain safe, but they would overthrow everything.

The history of holy Job demonstrates by a manifest example the bound power of demons: Satan did not dare to tempt him beyond what had been permitted to him by divine dispensation. For no one in the present world, however just or strong, could resist the fury of demons if they were always able, according to their own choice, to use free power to do harm.
