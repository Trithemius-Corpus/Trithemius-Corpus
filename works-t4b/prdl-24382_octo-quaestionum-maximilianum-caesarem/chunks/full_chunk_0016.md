Through manifest invocation necromancers and the little women whom we commonly call witches perform wondrous things, having been corrupted by likeness of will and passed into the fellowship of demons. For just as in the Church of God the sacraments are signs of the invisible grace of the Holy Spirit, so in the school of demons certain characters and modes of invocation are marks of a pact between a human being and an evil spirit. Hence, when witches display the signs of demons, the invoked evil spirits have commonly been glad to come to them and to exhibit both dreadful works of witchcraft and certain wondrous things.

But witches and necromancers produce wondrous effects by the invocation of demons in different ways. Witches subject themselves to demons by a certain kind of profession; by their ministry they trouble the air, raise storms, ravage crops, and make human beings and beasts ill. They carry on foul commerce of the filthiest pleasure with demons, and by pernicious charms call back from the lower world into sight whomever they wish. The deeds of these women should not be called miracles, but rather acts of witchcraft, and deservedly punished with the severest penalty.

Necromancers, however, subject themselves to demons not by a manifest profession, but by an implicit condition, when they promise to compel them to appear visibly in a circle, not only by conjurations and the display of characters, but also by sacrifices, fumigations, and vows. Their crimes are without number, since it is clear that all, whether implicitly or explicitly, conspire into the fellowship of demons. Although not all necromancers are equal in either art or practice, yet all are united, to their own destruction, by a damnable curiosity, by which they desire, with the aid of demons, to attain what they cannot discover naturally. All their works and volumes are full of superstitions and impiety, and for that reason must be driven far from the borders of believers.

There is in the East a great multitude of enchanters and necromancers, who by their illusions miserably

deceive the human race, and long ago turned almost all Asia Major and Asia Minor away from the faith of Christ. Illusions can be produced in three ways: by the ministry of demons, by the operation of nature, and by the deception of human art. No true Christian will doubt that the miracles of unbelievers are produced in one of these ways, once the determinate root of the agent has been established. Today, moreover, there are many among Christians devoted to the works of demons: enchanters, witches, and those reprobate concerning the faith, who deceive many by excessive curiosity and promise impossibilities by their arts.

Through the implication of demons, however, wondrous things are sometimes done: namely, whenever a person not sufficiently and rightly grounded in the faith works for or wishes something otherwise than befits a Christian, even if he believes there is no sin in it; and although it ought not to happen, nevertheless the devil, inserting himself, very often supplies the desired effect. Such is the practice which sufferers from spasms commonly use. On the holy day of Parasceve, during the office, they fashion a ring of any metal, which they thereafter always wear on the heart-finger. These things are utterly vain and have no root of truth at all. Yet I cannot deny that I have seen some people cured of spasm by this same vanity.

Likewise, I know that certain men have opened very strong locks with words and herbs; I do not think this was done either by the power of God or by the force of the things applied. I could introduce countless similar cases which I have seen and heard: what else are their manifest effects but certain miracles? Finally, there are some exceedingly curious people who, by stubbornly placing faith and confidence in characters, slips, rings, figures, necklaces, and unknown words, often obtain wondrous effects. We by no means doubt that the devil has supplied these, not by merit of holiness nor by mercy granted by God, but rather through faithless credulity. For demons, having the greatest envy of the human race, gladly come to vain practices in order to destroy the souls of mortals.
