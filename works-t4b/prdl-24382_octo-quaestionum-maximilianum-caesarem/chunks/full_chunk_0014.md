Immediately, in the blink of an eye, they arrive at the place where they know him to be; and when they have seen that he has already begun the journey of return, they carefully count the stages, and, suddenly returning to the questioner, affirm that the absent man will come on a definite day. But if he has either delayed on the road or turned aside elsewhere, then certainly the demons, now deceived in their own judgment, must be mistaken.

They gladly involve themselves in prophecies, and are quite ready to foretell future things to human beings for the sake of honor and esteem, since they very much desire to be regarded as worthy of admiration. They seek divine honors, and greatly desire to be feared by mortals. Just as boys, hiding in secret or running about masked, suddenly burst out from their hiding places and shake with unexpected terror their companions, who suspect no such thing, and then hide themselves again lest they be recognized; when they see the unsuspecting ones terrified, they boast as if they had obtained some great honor from this. After the example of these, certain demons, more bound to the earth and very restless, who move about among human beings, whom the ancients called Bacucei, terrify people at night; and they seek nothing more from mortals than the reputation of divinity. Hence also, before those with whom they are permitted to be by reason of likeness, they sometimes perform various tricks, and present themselves as terrifying in people’s minds as occasion permits. Whenever they can, they gladly clothe themselves in the likeness of a human body; but they do not always find suitable material of air with which to be clothed. For this reason they often appear monstrous and horrible, because they have not had suitable humors or vapors for perfecting the appearance they desired. But the more they are bound to earthly dwellings, the more, by that circumstance, they have been made dull with respect to matter. Hence, because of the ignorance which they contracted through disobedience, the predictions of demons are frequently

false, although by divine permission they are sometimes even verified.

For, as that most holy John Chrysostom says, it is sometimes granted to the devil to speak the truth, so that he may commend his lie by rare truth; because if he never spoke the truth, he could deceive no one, and his seduction would by no means suffice for tempting. It is therefore not astonishing if demons sometimes seem to foretell true things to unbelieving human beings, since they once could offer prophecies to the gentiles in idols. Nor is it astonishing if they sometimes provide care for the sick, since they know the natures of all herbs and can very easily apply themselves to the ill, and either remove diseases which they had previously inflicted, or for a time withdraw from the senses those which chance has inflicted. For almighty God, by the right of imperial majesty over all things, uses even the ministry of demons both for the testing of the faithful and for the punishment of the impious. Since they are perverse in affection, obstinate in their rebellion, and proud, they greatly desire divine esteem and honors; and therefore they gladly answer the invocations of unbelievers which they are accustomed to pour out to the gods, as if they themselves were gods.

Among unbelievers they sometimes seem to raise the dead to life, and they show marvelous things to curious people, so that they may render them altogether obstinate in their former error, as if confirmed by miracles. Yet they do not truly raise the dead, but deceive the senses of human beings in various ways: for either they themselves enter the corpses of the dead and carry them about for a time as if alive, or, after those bodies have been taken away to a more remote place, they display to the spectators some image in the appearance of the person who has departed life, as the experience of certain people has informed us. We believe it manifest that demons can show, in a certain false likeness, all the things whatsoever that the saints do as miracles in truth.
