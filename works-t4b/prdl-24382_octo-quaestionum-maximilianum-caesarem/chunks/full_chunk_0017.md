Whoever puts faith in characters that promise marvelous effects has implicitly denied both the faith of Christ and the Church of the saints. Alas, how many Christians today, how many clerics, how many priests, to spare those of higher rank, use many and various superstitions in their daily actions; through these they follow compacts with demons bound up in them, to the insult of the Christian faith and to their own condemnation. And because these evils have now multiplied too greatly among Christians, the Almighty has withdrawn his hand and handed them over into the dominion of the faithless, so that they may feel as exactors of punishments those in imitation of whom they were stubbornly serving, not the living and true God, but empty superstitions.

But that marvelous things are done by the substitution of demons we plainly recognize, if we inquire more carefully into the truth. For Jews, Saracens, pagans, or gentiles, who do not believe in our God Jesus Christ, if they perform any miracles, are believed to do them not by the power of God, not by the ministry of angels, not by the merit of holiness, but by the substitution of demons. For as often as they invoke almighty God, in whose only-begotten Son they do not believe, for the mercy of some marvelous effect, the devil, knowing that there is no salvation for sinners, and that the Most High does not hear unbelieving sinners, secretly applies himself in place of the God invoked, in order to keep them obstinate in their errors; and, if he can, with the Lord permitting it, he grants to those praying what is requested. The devil uses this substitution all the more freely, the less he is hindered by the faith of Christ and the sacraments of the Church. For the wrath of God remains upon all unbelievers who do not believe in his only-begotten Son; and they are in the power of the devil, and therefore, when invoked, he enters in place of God. Hence, blinded in their errors, they invoke God, in whose Son they do not believe; and when he, holding them in wrath, scorns to hear them, the devil, a false worker of wonders,

comes near. Therefore they believe the mockeries of the devil, they who disdain to believe the true miracles of God. From demons they receive false miracles suited to their errors, who by believing were unwilling to follow the truth, that is, Christ. For there are many among the unbelievers who, worshiping one God, the creator of all things, do not adore idols, and firmly believe that they will be saved in their own faith. But because they disdain to believe the mystery of the Lord’s incarnation, the wrath of God remains upon them, and they vanish away in their foolishness. Thinking God to be near to them, though they do not believe rightly in him, they are subjected to demons; and, putting faith in their false miracles, they are confirmed in their errors by falsehood.

It may not be wearisome to have introduced a likeness of this matter, O Caesar. There was a certain swineherd who had enclosed in a stick a small sheet containing the name of the holy martyr Blaise; he put such faith in it that he believed every wolf would be prevented from approaching if the staff were fixed in the ground in the middle of the herd. This foolish confidence, at last strengthened by long experience, made the man go safely wherever he wished, leaving the pigs in the wood without danger, since no wolf now had any access to a pig. It happened that a certain man passed by, while the shepherd was absent, and, seeing a demon keeping guard over the herd, said to him: “What are you doing here, wretched one?” He replied: “I am guarding these pigs.” But he said: “Who entrusted the care of pigs to you?” “The foolish confidence of the shepherd. For he enclosed a certain small sheet in a stick, thinking it to be of great piety and virtue, so that by the merits of some Blaise wolves might be kept away from the pigs; he has sinned against his own law and follows our vanities. But because the one invoked does not appear, I, lest the man lose his confidence, guard his pigs for him; and I willingly substitute myself for Blaise. Thus ungrateful mortals harass us with vain conjurations, so that, unless we wish to lose all esteem and honor among them, we must, I confess, often come in answer to their foolish invocations.” By the likeness of this deed demons deceive unbelievers.
