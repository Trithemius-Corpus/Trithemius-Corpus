Nor do you, Caesar, glorious man, since you are a great ruler and prince of men, admit as familiar to you anyone whose character is altogether contrary and unlike to yours, and who continually resists your will. But good and upright men, because they always withstand the most depraved will of demons, can in no way pass over into fellowship with them; they neither wish nor consent to be familiar with them, but with all their strength flee from and detest meeting them. And therefore they do not obtain that fictitious command over them of which we have spoken. For nowhere is there secure familiarity where no likeness of wills has preceded it, nor can there be concord among opposites.

Yet demons do not obey all evil and reprobate persons indiscriminately, but only those with whom they are joined by pacts and services. And when invoked they willingly come to those whom they cannot otherwise keep faithful, although they lie that they are compelled. It is therefore not to be wondered at, most excellent Caesar, that malignant spirits sometimes seem to comply with depraved and perverse men, whom, as we have said, both malevolence and destructiveness toward the human race have made alike and concordant. But let it be far from every Christian to wish, through a depraved likeness of will, to command demons, because if anyone in this life begins to have fellowship with malignant spirits, in the life to come he will burn with them without end.

But why are good and just men unable to command the holy angels of God, into whose likeness of good will they have passed? I think the solution to this question could easily be given from what has been said above, if the difference of intellects is recalled to mind. For although there are many men established in the Church of God whose righteousness benefits many unto salvation, nevertheless in our times very few advance so far that, because of the merit of sanctity, they are either feared by demons or received by angels into the fellowship of familiarity. Hence it happens that the men of our age neither command malignant spirits by the merit of sanctity,

But if any have advanced to that degree of sanctity by which, performing miracles in the Church, the saints once advanced, then they can powerfully command demons and have familiar fellowship with the blessed spirits. For it is certain that holy men beloved by God, in the primitive Church of Christians, once bestowed greater benefits upon mortals through the cooperation of good spirits than the impiety of depraved men could ever have inflicted as evil deeds through the ministry of demons. For whoever is a true worshipper of God and has become like the holy angels can do many wondrous things by prayers.

ON THE POWER

Of Witches. Question Six.

The question of your most unconquered majesty was this: whence comes this so great power in witches, by which they can accomplish so many varied and wondrous things, even in a single hour, such things and so many as no good person could do in his whole life? To this question, if we wish to answer briefly, we must necessarily say that all power is from the Lord God, without whom no creature can do anything. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: Without me you can do nothing. Hence it is manifest that every power of doing anything is granted to human beings by the Lord God and from no other source, whether they do good or evil.

Hence the most holy prelate Augustine says: The will of God is the first and highest cause of all bodily appearances and motions; and nothing happens in this most vast and immense commonwealth of the whole creature, visibly, which is not either commanded or permitted from the inner, invisible, and intelligible court of the supreme emperor, according to the ineffable justice of rewards and punishments. Therefore, Caesar, there is no doubt that that power of witches, by which they produce wondrous and horrible effects through the cooperation of demons, comes from the Lord God.
