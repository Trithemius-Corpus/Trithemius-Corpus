Therefore the three principles of the opinion just mentioned, unknown to many, in my judgment are enough for a few: that the mind, placed in the body, by its own power works wondrous effects outside itself. For what? Let anyone ignorant of nature tell me whether bodily weight can be lightened by the operation of the mind. I speak of a thing I experienced, with two others bearing witness with me. When, as a youth, I was pursuing literary studies, four of us were sleeping one night in a single bed. One of the same age arose, and, asleep as he was accustomed to do in dreams, walked about the house with his eyes closed, the full moon shining within, almost as if he were awake. He climbed walls and outdid a squirrel in his agility. He also, while asleep, crossed over the bed a second and a third time; he trod on all of us with his feet, and we felt no greater weight than if some little thing had touched us. Wherever his sleeping body moved, suddenly all the fastenings of the doors opened of their own accord. He very swiftly passed through the higher parts of the house and clung to the roofs like a sparrow. I speak of things seen, not heard by vague report.

These seem to be functions of the soul, not of the body: the soul, by its natural power, so to speak, uses itself almost freely when, the senses of the flesh being bound, it wanders outside the dwelling place of the body. But since this matter is not in every respect suited to the question proposed, let us put off the treatment of the causes of these marvels to another time. For we do not believe that these things are wondrous to wise men, who have very well understood the power and nobility of the human mind, since, hindered only by the interposition of the body, in other respects it is judged equal to angels. Finally, let us pass over fascinations and their various modes and kinds, by which a very furious man works externally very wondrous effects. The imagination of one who trusts very strongly also has much power for producing wondrous effects, whether it is directed toward a true object or toward one imagined. Hence, since there are so many kinds and modes

Nor does it escape me that, by the same reasoning, the miracles of the saints are attacked by the impious, as if they were not done by the power of the Creator but rather by the involvement or substitution of the devil. Whoever truly believes that the Christian faith alone is true does not doubt that all the miracles of unbelievers have been performed by the cooperation of demons. But if anyone rashly and obstinately thinks the contrary, he shows himself to be an unbeliever, not a Christian.

ON SACRED SCRIPTURE

Sacred. Fourth Question.

The fourth question of your most serene majesty was this: Why did almighty God not give sacred Scripture, containing the mysteries of our faith of both the New and the Old Testament, open, manifest, uniform in all things, plain, and sufficient for all things, but rather wrapped up and in many places full of enigmas? For many things necessary for the perfection of faith are not found sufficiently expressed in the divine Scriptures. To this most sacred question of your majesty, it seems that one must answer: Every Scripture divinely inspired is a perfect work of divinity, ordained before all time, written in time, in no other way than God willed, who inspired men that it should be written. But all the works of God are perfect; and therefore, since the Scripture about which we speak is the work of God, it is necessary to confess that it is perfect in all things. For the supreme judge, God, whose works are all perfect, willed it, and conferred upon his Scriptures the order that he willed; and without doubt he infused into those who received them the manner in which they should write.
