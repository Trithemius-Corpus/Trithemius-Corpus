Again here he asks what, according to Catholic teaching, is to be thought about those people who inhabit the newly discovered islands in the great sea, of which no mention is made among the old cosmographers, and who, as we have been informed, have never heard anything at all of the Christian religion. A brief answer is given, conforming in all respects to what has been written before: Truth has utterly excepted from damnation no one who does not believe: not an islander, not one contiguous with our world, not anyone else, whether dwelling near or far away; but without distinction and without limitation of persons he concluded, saying, “He who believes and has been baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned,” and the wrath of God remains upon him.

Who removes the wrath of God from man, except our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the mediator between God and men? By what means, except through faith formed in him who, though innocent, tasted death? Since, therefore, no one is saved without faith, we cannot promise salvation to those ignorant of Christ, since it is clear that they do not have the piety of faith in him. Necessarily, then, we shall say that all who do not believe in the Son of God are damned, whether they have heard of him or are wholly ignorant of him.

Yet we grant one thing: if any among those who are ignorant of Christ lived well according to the law of nature, invoking one God, even if they sometimes sinned, provided they had done penance before death, they have been or are to be less gravely damned than those who either hear of Christ and despise him, or who among either group lived wickedly and foully. For God is just; he does wrong to no one, but renders to each according to his works. But the wrath of God remains upon everyone who does not believe in his only-begotten Son, because of the original sin of the first man, from which no one is justified except through faith and the baptism of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. But if original sin alone is present in a person, as in little children, unless it is removed through Christ it condemns the one who has it to the penalty of loss only, by which, namely, he is barred

from the vision of God and from the enjoyment of the happiness of the saints forever. But an adult who sins mortally will be punished after death with a double penalty, namely of loss and of sense, so that he both does not see God and, feeling the pains of the fire of Gehenna without end, never escapes them.

Jews, however, and Saracens, and all the rest who hear the faith of Christ and despise it are without doubt damned to both penalties, of loss and of sense, whether otherwise they live innocently according to the law of nature or the contrary. But among those who have never had any hearing of Christ, if any should live innocently according to the law of nature until death, I do not think they have furnished cause for it to be said or believed that they are to be damned to the penalty, although they are deprived forever of the vision of God, which theologians have called the penalty of the damned. For no one will behold the face of God the Father almighty unless he has been reborn through faith in Christ.

¶ Now let us pronounce judgment against those who, when we assert these things in Catholic fashion according to Christ’s doctrine, object with foolish reflection: Why did God, since he is supremely good, wish to create so many thousands of human beings whom he foreknew from eternity would be damned? For if Christians are compared with unbelievers, scarcely a twentieth part of the people of the whole world today believes in our Lord Jesus Christ. The boundless curiosity of this question has often struck many; if their faith in Christ were firm and their devotion fervent in love for him, they would not ask things so unusual and difficult. For who is found among mortals of such honor in learning or holiness that, apart from God himself, he might disclose to us the cause of the divine will? The will of God is incomprehensible, because he himself is the cause of his most righteous will. The everlasting order is inviolable; and, as I said above, God does wrong to no one.

When God judged that a universal flood should come upon the earth, were not only eight people saved in the ark, while all the rest perished by the inundation of the waters? The cause of their being struck down was sin. That they were created had no other cause than the uncircumscribable goodness of God.
