Since he is far above all things that we, or any mortal, can understand concerning him, he is far beyond, outside, and above every affirmation, as holy Dionysius says. For he is neither soul nor sense; nor does he have imagination or opinion, or reason or understanding; nor is he reason or understanding; nor is he spoken of or understood. Nor is he number, multitude, order, magnitude, smallness, equality, likeness, or unlikeness; nor does he stand or move; nor does he bring about rest or have power; nor is he power or light; nor does he live, nor is he life, substance, age, or time. There is no spiritual contact with him; he is not form, truth, kingdom, wisdom, one, unity, deity, authority, or spirit, so far as we can know him. Nor is there a designation of Son or Father; nor is he anything else among those things known to us or to anyone else in the world. Nor is he anything of the things that are not, nor of the things that are; nor do the things that are know him as he himself is. Nor is there speech, name, or knowledge of him; nor is he darkness or light, error or truth; nor is there any affirmation or negation of him at all. For he, the perfect and singular cause of all things, is above every affirmation; and his excellence is above every negation, namely the excellence of him who is simply free from all things and above all things.

Therefore, Dionysius says, we say that since he is the cause of all things above all things, he is neither without substance nor without life; nor does he lack reason or mind; nor is he body, figure, or species; nor does he have quality, quantity, or mass. Nor is he in a place, nor is he seen; nor does he have sensible touch; nor does he sense, nor is he sensible; nor is he subject to rashness or disturbance, agitated by fleshly passions. Nor is he weak, subject to sensible accidents; nor is he in need of light; nor does he admit change, corruption, division, or flux; nor is he, or does he have, any other thing belonging to sensible things.

Hence, since he is far above every affirmation, excelling what is posited, he could not be comprehended by a creature that necessarily uses the senses for understanding, as the true Peripatetic said: nothing is in our intellect unless it has first been in the sense. Therefore God, who is not subject to the senses of mortals, far exceeds human understanding. According to affirmative positing he is truly altogether in all things; and according to negative removal, the nearer one approaches, he is none of all the things that are subject to mortal knowledge. Indeed, since he is above all things, he can be none of those things that are subject to him. Therefore Empedocles beautifully gave a likeness, saying: God is a sphere whose center is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere. Let human understanding now lift itself as far as it can; and let whoever strives to comprehend God rightly understand and comprehend the center of this imagined sphere, together with its circumference ending nowhere. But if he is not able to understand this proposition of the likeness properly, why does he strive to investigate the essence of God, which exists above every affirmation?

Most unconquered Caesar, may it not weary you to hear another likeness concerning God from Cusanus, formerly cardinal priest of the Roman Church, my fellow Mosellan, a man most learned in those times, who says: God is a straight line of infinite length, which makes a circle. Now then let us see: if it is a straight line, how does it make a circle? But if it makes a circle, how is it straight? For a circle and a straight line are naturally opposed. Nevertheless, a line of infinite length, since it has neither beginning nor end, necessarily makes a circle, although it is proposed to be imagined not as curved but as straight.
