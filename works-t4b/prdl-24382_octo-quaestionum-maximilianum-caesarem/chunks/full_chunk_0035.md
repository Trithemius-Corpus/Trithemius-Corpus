For the evil works of the impious benefit good and just people as a remedy, but bring damnation upon those who inflict them. The sixth reason is that a person, harmed through the assaults of demons, may be recalled to penitence and may resolve in his heart on the necessary amendment of life. For Almighty God does not immediately send vengeance when we sin, but waits for our amendment until a predetermined time. But if we have neglected to do penance, the avenger of evils comes to us when we do not expect it; and we who, as sinners, have scorned to acknowledge ourselves by doing penance in tranquility, will at last acknowledge ourselves late, in tribulation. Hence the holy prophet speaks in the person of God, saying: If his sons abandon my law and do not walk in my judgments and do not keep my commandments, I will visit their iniquities with the rod, and their sins with blows; but my mercy I will not take away from them, nor will I do harm in my truth.

The seventh reason why Almighty God permits the evil works of demons to prevail over the good and innocent is that an excessively unrestrained occasion for sinning may be cut away from them. For there are many poor and sick people, pleasing to God, good, devout, and humble, whom poverty and bodily illness preserve in the love of God; if they were rich and raised high with honors, they would quickly fall away from the resolve of holiness into the depth of vices. It is not expedient for everyone’s salvation to have honors and riches together with health, nor does a longer life always make a person better. Therefore Almighty God, wishing to show his mercy toward those whom he has chosen, permits the good, the just, and the innocent to be subject to various calamities.

And sometimes through demons, sometimes through evil people, and sometimes through various other ways and means, he takes away from such people the riches and honors which he foreknew they would use badly, so that in poverty and abasement they may keep the humility which, placed in wealth and high station, they would by no means have preserved. He also often permits them to fall into many great illnesses, because cheerful bodily health would have brought upon them the greatest sickness of mind. Finally, he permits certain people to be driven to death by sorcery, so that the license for sinning may be cut away from them, which they would have abused for longer had they been allowed to remain in life. Hence Scripture says of the just man: He was snatched away lest malice change his understanding, and lest deceit deceive his soul.

The eighth reason for divine permission is that people may venerate the sacraments of the Church, which have a powerful virtue against all the evil works of malignant spirits. For a person who venerates the sacraments and rites of the catholic Church with due honor is rarely harmed by the evil works of demons. And by the very confession of witches we have often heard that attendance at masses and the use of things blessed by the Church greatly weaken the powers of demons. Nor are things blessed in the Church of God in vain, but so that they may be, as it were, defenses of divine protection and tokens to be feared by demons. But concerning children and infants who, reborn in Christ, are sometimes killed by the evil works of demons or of human beings, we do not doubt that divine providence permits this to happen for their salvation, since it knows how to order all things for the better for mortals. Yet it often happens that infants perish by sorcery because of the offenses of their parents.
