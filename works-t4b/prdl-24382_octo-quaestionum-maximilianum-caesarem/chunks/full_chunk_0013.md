For greater than all the miracles of this world is what we behold: the whole universe, and whatever is contained in it, created by the will of God. The unchangeable divine will works all things, enduring, because it lacks both beginning and end. But whatever God wills, he always wills; since in him to will and to be able are the same, labor does not belong to the unchangeable one. Thus all the universal miracles that the Creator of all things performs he has presented for good and evil alike to behold; and after the general miracles, which have now grown cheap among mortals through familiarity, he has also shown, in almost every single creature, countless things especially worthy of wonder.

The second performer of miracles is the good angel, who very often works wonders for mortals both by natural virtue and by divine power. For those most blessed spirits are sent by divine ordinance in ministry to the faithful; they furnish to both good and evil the things that are benefits of nature, but only to those who believe in God and our Lord Jesus Christ do they administer the gifts of saving grace. Hence there is no doubt that those miracles which are done in the Church of the saints by invocation of the divine name are displayed by their ministry and cooperation. But the wonders of unbelievers, if any are done today or are read to have been done formerly, are either fabricated or falsely reported, or are accomplished by the mockery of demons. For it cannot happen that unbelievers perform any miracles by the ministry of good angels, since where Christ Jesus, the Son of God, is not believed, no signs of grace are displayed by good spirits to impious and reprobate men. Indeed, since they are obstinate in the error of a false religion, if in their own estimation they have obtained any miracle, there is no reason whatever to doubt that it is done through the ministry of demons. For since they are wholly unlike the good angels, they attain no familiarity with them. No despiser of the Christian faith can please God; but whoever

is shown to displease almighty God is without doubt deprived of the familiarity of the holy angels. Yet by this I do not take back the claim that every human being has been entrusted to some good spirit, by whose ministry, even if he is unbelieving toward the Son of God, he may receive the benefits of nature, though he may not attain the gifts of grace. But to those who believe in our Lord Jesus Christ they confer not only the benefits of nature, but also administer the gifts of grace. It is therefore manifest that, if any miracles are done among unbelievers, they cannot be done by the gift of God or by the ministry of the holy angels, since all are placed outside the unity of the Church, and signs cannot be worked by the impious through public righteousness. For God, as has been said, does not remove his original wrath from man except through faith in Jesus Christ; but everyone who is unbelieving toward the Son of God will not see eternal life, but the wrath of God remains upon him. Therefore everyone upon whom the wrath of God remains will not be able to please him; and so, if any wondrous things are done at his prayers, they should be believed to have been performed not by the power of God nor by the ministry of angels, but rather by the intervention of demons.

The third performer of wonders is the devil, who, like an ape, is eager to imitate whatever he has seen, and deceives the minds of men, especially unbelievers, with various illusions; and he believes that, by a certain likeness of miracles, he can seduce those who believe in him, for it is agreed that they were created by nature subtler than all mortals. They cannot know the thoughts of mortals except by a kind of conjecture, when, that is, our inward things are made manifest by certain signs. Demons foretell future things, and sometimes they speak truly; sometimes, making conjectures, they are deceived and deceive. From the beginning of the world they have known certain signs that precede events; by experience and use of these signs they sometimes foretell as true what is going to happen, because when they have seen signs known to themselves preceding a matter, they immediately conjecture what is in a person's thought.
