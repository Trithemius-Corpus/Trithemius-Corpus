Hence no one among mortals is good unless, by the counsel of his own will, he is aided by the grace of the merciful God; and no one is evil unless, of his own accord, he is deprived of grace because of the obstacle of sin. By uprightness of will, however, we are made like the good spirits, if we prove the piety of the faith that we have in Christ by holy and necessary works. And just as the consummation of a good will makes good men equal to angelic spirits, so the perversity of the impious renders them like demons. Hence evil and perverse men, the more they have been depraved by the malice of their own will, the more they become like demons in disposition.

But likeness introduces the familiarity of friendship, from which mutual confidence is gradually generated. From the confidence of the lesser toward the greater there arises command, whether true or feigned by cunning. Thus if any command of depraved men over demons appears to exist, it arises from the greatness of depravity: namely, when their disposition is depraved to such a degree that, by a reprobate will, they either surpass or equal the malice of the demons who join themselves to them. For demons are not all equal in malice or intelligence; rather, one is much more cunning than another in quickness of understanding, and far worse in depraved affection. For those who, falling from the high seats, inhabit the hollows and caves of the earth are certainly duller than the rest, to such a degree that they fear swords and rods and seem to be admonished by the threats of men who terrify them, as can be observed in those whom we call energumens. But demons who dwell farther removed from the earth are subtler in nature and meet with men more rarely.

Men full of malice, however, the more they have advanced in perversity of will, the more they become like demons; and once admitted by them into familiarity, they thereafter become bold and receive command over the demons whose familiarity they use,

not by violent power but by feigned pretense. For demons, in order to keep in their depravity those men whom they have once received into surrender as willing subjects, gladly submit themselves to them as though moved by a simulated dominion; and what they do voluntarily, they pretend they are compelled to do in obedience. For no one obtains true command over demons except him whom faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, through the merit of holiness, has made acceptable and a friend to God. For thus Scripture says of those who truly believe in the Lord Jesus: “And he gave them power over all demons.”

Evil men, therefore, command demons, whom malevolence has made like to them, likeness has made familiar, and familiarity has made their own and subject. It is as if some servant of a great prince, born in a humble station, as often happens, and joined to him by long familiarity, were said to command him, since he obtains from him without contradiction everything he asks, so that he himself seems rather to rule his lord than to be ruled by him. According to this likeness, evil and reprobate men, because they are admitted into the demons’ familiarity and become very bold through more prolonged confidence, receive over them a simulated, not to say familiar, command, which they have deserved by the likeness of their most depraved will. Hence whatever they have demanded from demons who join with them by an implicit or explicit compact, they receive, unless at times, being forbidden by divine ordinance, the demons cannot provide what is sought. Yet they do not thus join themselves indiscriminately to all reprobate and evil persons, but only to those with whom, through the intervention of a compact, they have entered into certain agreements: such as women who are witches, who have rendered homage to the devil, and those invokers of demons whom the common people call by the usual word necromancers.

¶ The first, indeed, is of those men who, beloved by God by the merit of holiness, have received from him the power of trampling the head of the ancient serpent,

such as the apostles and their followers and disciples were in the primitive Church; concerning them the Lord speaks in the holy Gospel: “In my name they shall cast out demons,” and “They shall take up serpents.”

¶ The second kind is of those men to whom, by reason of order in the Church of God, it is granted that they may, by the power of the ministry received, drive demons out of human bodies. And this ministry is entrusted to exorcists in the Church of God when, that is, they are ordained by apostolic authority.

¶ Again, there are two kinds of men who desire to command demons; and the fact that they presume to do this unlawfully is shown by their most wicked conditions.

¶ The first kind, most pernicious, is of witches who enter into pacts with demons and, after a solemn profession has first been made, hand themselves over to some demon under the right of ownership and perpetual service. What great evils this most harmful kind of people commits in your empire, most wise Caesar, no one can express. For, first of all, denying God, the faith of Christ, and sacred baptism, they offer themselves in mind and body to demons; they weaken, injure, and kill men and beasts by their malefices. There is no disease that, God permitting, they cannot bring on, because when they present themselves to demons as servants in the observance of the contracted agreement, they dare whatever they wish in association with them. Hence they are nowhere to be tolerated, but rather are to be utterly exterminated in every place, as God, the creator of all, commands: “You shall not suffer witches to live.” Some witches summon demons by charms; others entice them by prayers and sacrifices; the rest, provoking them by unspeakable things and ceremonies, draw them into the aid of their impiety. It is therefore no wonder that witches seem to command demons, since by the impiety of a perverse will they have been made like them, and by mutual agreement familiar and subject to them.
