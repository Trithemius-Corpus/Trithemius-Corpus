For the devil, puffed up with pride, imitates the church of Christ to such a degree that, just as one who has not been ordained cannot consecrate the body of Christ, however much he may utter the words of consecration, so too a demon, however much he may be invoked and however much signs or characters may be shown to him, either never or only rarely comes to the aid of someone whom he does not know to be subject to him by pact. For if he came indiscriminately to all who invoked him, without pacts having been interposed, he would not possess so many witches dedicated to him by right of ownership.

Hence it is that many who are given over to profane arts, laboring with dreadful conjurations to summon demons to a circle, to a mirror, or to any other receptacles, obtain no effect, because either the command of God stands in the way, or the devil despises the curious man who has not been dedicated to him by homage. For three things are required for witchcraft, without which it is agreed that none is perpetrated: a witch raging in mind and with depraved will; also the cooperation of a familiar demon; and, before all things, divine permission. But if one of these does not concur, the witchcraft cannot be perpetrated. For if God did not permit it, neither the devil nor the witch could do anything. And if the devil did not come to the aid of the wicked woman’s will, she would perpetrate no witchcraft. Again, if the witch were lacking, the devil would in no way complete the witchcraft. For the devil uses the will of the raging witch as an artisan uses an instrument for working; without it he could not inflict the witchcraft.

And just as demons differ from one another in power and in the sharpness of their subtlety, so the power of witches is not one and the same in all of them, but differs in individuals according as demons of subtler or grosser nature come together with them. For there are many kinds of demons, and they are separated from one another by fixed degrees, by reason of the places into which, when they fell at the beginning, they were thrust down. fiery, because it wanders about the upper air and is never plunged down to the lower regions before the day of judgment; rather, remaining continually beneath the lunar regions, they have no commerce with human beings on earth. That they remain beneath the moon, Saint Augustine testifies in the work On the Christian Struggle, saying as follows: Demons do not dwell in heaven, where the sun and stars are, but all pass their time beneath the lunar region.

Moved by this, as I think, Apuleius said that demons are fiery animals, because Aristotle confirmed that in burning furnaces certain small animals have often been seen flying about, furnished with little wings, which spend their whole life in fire, since they arise with the fire and are extinguished with it, and, vanishing, return to their own place from which they had come. They have no association with acts of witchcraft, because, since they dwell in fire, on account of their subtlety they cannot take on a gross or airy body.

The second kind is called aerial, because it wanders in the air and dwells near us. These, indeed, can descend to the lower regions, and, with bodies assumed from denser air, sometimes appear visibly to human beings. God permitting, they often disturb the air; they stir up thunder and storms; and all together they conspire for the ruin of the human race. In the manner of human beings they are moved by the affections of passions, especially pride and envy, and they are driven by continual disturbances; they are not clothed with a solid body, nor do they remain in one place. Nor do they all have one form, but many, and they often change them according to the variety of the affections by which they either come when summoned by the spells of witches, or are driven by disturbance to do harm. For they have an altogether violent and furious disposition, full of disturbances; hence, when strongly wronged and disturbed, they very often contrive sudden ambushes.
