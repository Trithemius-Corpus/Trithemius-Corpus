At the same time it must be noted that from the beginning of the world no religion or sect of chief eminence has ever lacked miracles, although they have not all been done from one principle. For whatever miracles have been done in the true religion have been done from no source other than God. The law of nature had its miracles; the written law had them; grace now has them as well. The almighty God who gave religion also himself performs miracles. But in false religions, where truth is not worshiped, if any miracles are done, it is not God but the devil who works them. For since God is truth, he did not establish false religions; and therefore he has never made falsehood illustrious by his miracles. Mahomet, who, full of an evil demon, established a false religion, adorned it with false miracles not from God but by the cooperation of demons. For the demon who gave the cause of the error could also, once the occasion had been taken, display a false miracle to those deceived. Hence, if miracles had been wholly lacking to Mahomet as he was persuading a rude people of a new religion, perhaps no one would have put faith in him. For he too showed certain wondrous things to the peoples, as much by subtlety of intellect as by the cooperation of the devil, so that he seemed to have confirmed a new doctrine also by new miracles. Miracles were not lacking even to the idolatrous pagans, if we do not deny credence to histories. But the Jews, before the incarnation of the Son of God, receiving his law through Moses, were often made illustrious by divine miracles. Yet after the Lord's resurrection, when the Mosaic law had been made void, those who do not believe in the only-begotten Son of God remain under the wrath of his indignation; and all miracles of truth have ceased among them. Hence, if they do any wondrous things today, they are works of demons and manifest signs of error. For demons, with the greatest zeal,

keep watch so that those men whom they have once made to wander from the way of truth, which is Christ, they may, by a continuation of fraud until death, make stubborn in error. Indeed, when God for just reason withdraws his grace from unbelievers, their deceiver, a malignant and unknown spirit, approaches; and when he sees that salvation is far away, by false miracles he seduces the minds of the ignorant, as though the Lord were near. Therefore whatever miracles belong to unbelievers are works of demons; for just as there is no salvation for mortals outside the unity of the Christian faith, so without Christ the most holy power does not grant wonders. For now the demons, hiding in ambush, long since defeated and confounded by the trophy of Christ, fear to appear openly as they did before the Lord's incarnation; and therefore among unbelievers they falsely present themselves as good spirits.

There are also some who add a fourth mode to the aforesaid three, saying that the mind or spirit of man himself can naturally do wondrous things, provided it knows how to recall itself from every adventitious thing into itself, above sense, into unity. For those who assert such things promise to perform the wonders of nature, to predict future things, to disclose men's secrets, to remove diseases, and suddenly to change men's counsels. But that mode of miracles cannot be simply natural, certain, common, and infallible; for if it is true, which I have not experienced, some external power must necessarily intervene and partly take possession of the one thinking, whether a good angel or a malignant spirit. But if none of the external powers mixes itself in, the one prophesying must necessarily form the beginning of his speech either from conjectures or from bold presumption, and pronounce a judgment sometimes true, sometimes false. Indeed, as long as the spirit dwells in the body, it naturally understands nothing without the ministry of sense. Hence I would not wholly resist if they grant that our confused and rude intellect is aided by a like ministry.
