For the authority of Scripture itself says that unless you believe, you will not understand. It is the slowness of our intellect, and likewise the impurity of our desire, whenever we do not rightly understand the divine Scriptures: namely, when the veil of harmful preoccupation remains spread over our hearts, which does not permit us to come to true understanding. Hence it happens that we do not understand the sense which the Spirit has infused into the divine Scriptures, but, grasping the bare words of the writers, twist them into another sense. Indeed, if our minds were purified of carnal vices; if the intellect were exercised in the Scriptures through continual pursuit of sobriety; if that belief and reverence were applied to the holy Scriptures which their dignity requires, every diligent person would understand more clearly than daylight that there is nothing confused in the sacred readings, nothing discordant, nothing unsuitable, nothing absurd, or introduced without the most just reason. For all the passages of divine Scripture are full of mysteries; whenever something in them seems to have been placed obscurely, it has been done by divine providence, so that the diligent mind may have the necessary exercise of piety, and the faith of the simple may not lose the place of due reverence. Therefore it was not by chance, but by eternal providence, that he willed the holy Scriptures to be obscure in many places, so that they might abound in as many mysteries as senses in which, by the inspiration of the Spirit of God, they are expounded in Catholic fashion by the doctors. For, as holy Augustine says, the obscurity of divine Scripture is useful and very profitable, because it begets many judgments of truth and brings them forth into the light for human beings; for when one person, and another another, draws out this sense or that by expounding it in Catholic fashion, how lofty and profound it is becomes known. Hence let no man puffed up with secular learning suppose that he understands sacred Scripture unless he first lays aside the veil of pride and of every disordered concupiscence.

For secular learning is of greater value for exercising the intellect than for illuminating the understanding toward true wisdom. Therefore, without doubt, divine Scripture is both manifest and clear to minds purified through the continual exercise of the fear of God and frequent study in reading; to minds that are filthy and swollen with secular wisdom it seems confused and obscure. Hence he understands the holy Scriptures well and rightly who, with purified affection and frequent in their study, expounds them just as he did who wrote them. For if they are read in a spirit other than that by which he is believed to have inspired them to be written, the mind must necessarily be shut off altogether from true understanding. But when we encounter some discordance in them, let us understand that it is only of words, not of senses; and this arises in ordered fashion, not by chance, but by the providence of God, so that the mind, exercised longer in the Scriptures lest it become wholly sluggish, may be strengthened. For the counsel of God cannot be deceived; but the divine Scriptures were written by the counsel of his will; it follows necessarily that they are true and in no way false. They were human beings by whom the divine writings were handed down to us; certainly they wrote words used according to human custom, by which they might signify to posterity what that highest excellence of divinity willed to be known to mortals. Therefore now, you who, devoted to secular wisdom, attack the divine Scriptures, say: the Scripture of the New and Old Testament, which the Church has received and venerates as the true, authoritative, and holy Scripture of Christians, from where do you believe it was received? From God, or from human beings? If from God, why do you not believe it? For everyone who attacks Scripture which he believes to have been inspired by God most plainly resists the divine ordinance. But if it is from human beings, why do you hope for the eternal salvation promised in it, which man cannot give?
