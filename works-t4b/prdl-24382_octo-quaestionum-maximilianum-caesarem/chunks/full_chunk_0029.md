Witches, supported by the cooperation of these demons, are more powerful in doing harm in proportion as they have obtained the concurrence of one higher in their order. In the Life of Saint Anthony, Saint Athanasius said that the air is full of demons; Mercury, the Thrice-Greatest, had also said this earlier, namely that no part of the world is without the presence of demons. The holy bishop Ambrose also says: The world is full of holy powers because it is full of wickednesses. Hence the Platonists have handed down that demons flying in the air, like very dense fiery snow, are visible to those who desire it, if for some time, while the sun is shining, they keep their gaze directed toward heaven with eyes not beaten back. But I do not know whether the object in the air is caused by a real consistency, or whether the interrupted motion of the eyes presents false images; nevertheless, not without slight damage to the sense, I have made trial of the things promised.

The third kind, however, we call terrestrial demons; we do not in the least doubt that they fell from heaven to earth according to their deserts. Concerning these, the most holy bishop and martyr of Christ, Theophorus, once called Ignatius, wrote thus in a certain letter to the Ephesians: There is nothing better than to have peace, in which every weapon of the aerial and likewise the terrestrial spirits is made void. Of these demons, some move about in forests and groves, laying ambushes for hunters; others live in open fields and make travelers wander at night; some dwell in hidden places and caves; the rest, less frenzied and disturbed than the others, delight to dwell with human beings in the dark. They are not led by one affection but by various ones, since some are less depraved than others, although all are full of evil disturbances. Of these, some rejoice in terrifying human beings by illusions alone; others desire to draw their hearers into admiration of themselves by predictions of future things. Hence Saint John Chrysostom, in the third book On Providence to the monk Stagirius, says: In every diabolical action, the magnitude of grief is more powerful for doing harm, because whomever a demon overcomes, he overcomes through grief. If you take that away, no one will be able to be harmed by a demon. This kind of demons, however, is rarely familiar to witches, because of the inconstancy of their affections and the levity by which they delight more in terrifying many people than in being subject to the command of one little woman. Yet sometimes, as they promise to madwomen, they dwell in glass, or in crystal, or in a mirror, and, when stirred up by incantations, they give answers to women; and if someone coming on the road has spoken ill of them, they announce it beforehand. And this kind of witches sometimes differs greatly from the rest, since what the others make infirm by sorcery, these women promise to cure. But because for the most part they repel sorcery by sorcery, every Christian is forbidden by the Church to have recourse to them.

The fourth kind of demons is called aquatic, because, immersing itself in waters, it dwells around lakes and rivers: full of wrath, disturbed, restless, and deceitful. It stirs up storms at sea, sinks ships into the deep, and takes the lives of many in the waters. And whenever these demons assume a visible body, they more often appear in the female sex, and are seen more rarely in the male, because they move about in damp places and follow a softer course of life, nor can they easily assume a lowly form according to the custom of the others. Long ago antiquity named the Naiads and Nereids, nymphs of the waters, not in the male sex but in the female. But others are commonly called aquatic women, that is, wasser frawen.
