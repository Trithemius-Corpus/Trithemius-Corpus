When temptation comes, like a kind of interrogator, a man is discovered by himself, who before, being hidden, did not know himself. The holy arch-apostle Peter, before temptation came upon him, was hidden both from himself and from others, namely that he was heedless and weak; it came, and the weakness that had lain hidden appeared. Therefore temptation is the greatest proving of men, as sacred Scripture bears witness: the furnace proves the potter’s vessels, and the temptation of tribulation proves just men. Patience is easy in prosperity, when no adversity strikes the mind. But many are the tribulations of the just; yet from them all our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver them.

The third reason for divine permission is the preservation of humility, so that the faithful and just man may always fear God and never presume upon his own strength or justice. Hence Scripture says of the reprobate: They are not in the labor of men, and with men they will not be scourged. Therefore pride held them; they were covered with their iniquity and impiety; their iniquity came forth as if from fatness. It belongs to the mercy of God that he sometimes permits good and just men to be shaken by adversities, whom he knows cannot be humbled in prosperous circumstances. For tribulation has made many humble whom favorable circumstances would have made proud. Finally, the fearful imagining of malefices makes a good man fear his God and diligently call upon his aid. But let no one, proud and presuming upon his own powers, think himself safe from the malefices of demons; for the man whom the protection of God abandons, inflated with pride, without doubt lies open to the malefices of demons.

The fourth reason is so that there may be a distinction between the good and the evil. For Scripture says of good men, beloved by God, in the Acts of the Apostles, that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God. And holy Paul, lover of Christ, says in a certain epistle: All who wish

to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution. Likewise the wisest king Solomon says in Proverbs: Whom the Lord loves, he corrects, and, like a father, he is pleased with his son. It is a very glorious thing for a Christian, suffering for the love of God, to endure adversities, whether they are inflicted by evil men or by demons. For power is given to the devil and his ministers to harm good and just men, not for their destruction, as the inexperienced suppose, but rather for the very great and certain consolation of the soul.

Indeed, when a just man lives well, harms no one, offends no one, does injury to no one, loves his God with his whole heart, fears and venerates him, and keeps his commandments with all care, yet nonetheless is troubled by malefices, whether in body, in possessions, or in both, truly blessed is that man; for when he has been proved, he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him. Everyone who lives rightly in the unity of the faith of the catholic Church of Christians, and, receiving evils in this world, patiently endures them for the love of God, is a sure sign that he perseveres in the grace of God and is to be saved.

On the contrary, concerning the evil and reprobate we read it written thus: Why do the impious live? They have been raised up and strengthened with riches. Their houses are secure, and the rod of God is not upon them. They spend their days in good things, and in a moment they descend to hell. For it is a manifest sign of damnation if you live badly here in sins and vices, opposed to God, and yet prosper in the goods of fortune and in bodily health.

The fifth reason why the Almighty permits good and just men to be troubled by the evil and reprobate is for the complete purification of the mind. For, as Scripture says, there is no just man on earth who does good and does not sin. But since God lets no sin pass unpunished, he ordains temporal punishments for just men, through mercy, for their faults, but eternal punishments for the impious and reprobate, through justice.
