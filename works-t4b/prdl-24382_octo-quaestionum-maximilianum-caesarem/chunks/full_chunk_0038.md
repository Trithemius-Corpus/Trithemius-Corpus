Some of them thought that this world was made not by providence but by chance; if their opinion were true, no place would be left for providence, but all things would necessarily drift at random, since the world, which was made by chance, could perish by chance.

Against their false opinion others taught that the world could not have been made without reason, nor certainly continue to exist unless it were ruled by the supreme prudence of its maker. Hence Boethius, a most Christian man, said: O you who govern the world by eternal reason, sower of earth and heaven, who command time to proceed from eternity, and, remaining stable, cause all things to move. And again he said: The generation of all things, and all the progressions of mutable natures, and whatever is moved in any way, derive their causes, order, and forms from the stability of the divine mind.

Others, however, thought that God cared only for incorruptible things, such as the heavenly bodies and separate substances, together with the species of things, but that he knew nothing else at all and utterly neglected all the rest. But these men erred shamefully, because the Almighty has providence and care not only for universal and incorruptible things, but also for particular and corruptible things. Hence the same Boethius again says: But on earth also the lofty founder and ruler of things guides the reins: king and lord, source and origin, law and wise arbiter of equity. He is the continuous love for all things, and the good things seek to be held by their end, because they cannot endure otherwise unless, turned back again by love, they flow back to the causes which he himself gave.

Others, however, unable to bear that they should be neglected, exempted man from the number of corruptible things on account of the preeminence of his intellect, saying that God has care and providence for man, but despises all the other earthly things. Against their false opinion enough, I think, has been said, and to produce many arguments would be altogether superfluous. For Scripture says: You will save men and beasts, O Lord. were made by the Lord, and he has care for all alike. Therefore divine majesty's providence orders, rules, disposes, and cares for all lower things, each and every one, great and small, corruptible and incorruptible; his most powerful will produced all things from nothing, as sacred Scripture again bears witness: But your providence, Father, has governed all things from the beginning.

Let men of corrupt mind, impious and reprobate concerning the Christian faith, therefore blush with shame: those who, puffed up with empty talkativeness, take away the providence of him who knows all things, and do not consider what great injury they do to God. For what, to use Firmianus's judgment, is so worthy and so proper to almighty God as providence? But if he foresees nothing, if he neglects lower things, if he does not care for mortal things, he has already lost all his dignity. But it is impossible, as holy Anselm attests, for God to lose his honor, since, as the Peripatetic says, in eternal things there is no evil, nor corruption, nor sin. Therefore whoever takes away providence takes away the whole substance of God. And what does he say except that God does not exist?

But now let those who deny that God's providence cares for lower things tell me: if God neglects lower things, let them consider what necessarily follows. If God knowingly neglects and despises us, there is no longer any cause why we should attend upon him with worship or venerate him with religion, since we obtain no benefit from him; rather, being neglected and despised, let us neglect him ourselves as well.

But if he despises and neglects us unknowingly, there is now no reason at all for us to worship one who does not know us, and on that account is unable to confer any good, or to invoke him with prayers. For if God does not care for human affairs, whether knowingly or unknowingly, every cause for piety ceases, since there is no hope of salvation. What consideration of piety can there be among mortals, what hope for the salvation of souls, if God does not care for human affairs?
