Why do we offer him any worship, why honor, why prayers? Or why do we so greatly fear or dread a God who does not know us, who, with us neglected and despised, can neither do us good nor harm?

For once providence is removed, neither holiness nor religion will remain; and when these have been extinguished, the greatest disorder of life and dreadful confusion will follow. Once piety toward God is removed, what will the commonwealth be except everlasting confusion? Public faith among human beings will necessarily perish; the fellowship of the human race and all justice together with it will vanish and perish. What must be thought of the Christian faith if God does not care for human affairs? What, finally, of the Savior of all, our Lord Jesus Christ, except that he will be reckoned an empty fable, wholly fictitious, if there is no providence of God caring for mortal things? Therefore whatever good we do in this life, whatever evil we suffer for the love of God, is wholly vain and useless to us if he has no care for us.

Let those who deny the providence of God now see and shudder, when they perceive that the end of their opinion is the worst despair of all. Therefore let no one, induced by the vain-speaking of secular philosophers, train himself to contempt of God; let no one take away the providence of the highest and unchangeable good; let no one corrupt the dignity of the creator of all things. For all who deny divine providence show themselves both unworthy of the benefits of the most merciful Savior and ungrateful.

For it must be firmly believed, and in no way doubted, that this whole universe, both visible and invisible, and all things individually, whether they are in heaven or on earth or in the sea, are ruled and governed by divine providence, and that absolutely nothing in the whole universe is alien to his care.

For the fruits of the earth and all the other things that the earth brings forth, and also storms and the various changes of the seasons by which all the things that the earth produces grow naturally to maturity, divine providence supplies for the human race; and it provides for all things individually, not only the great but also the smallest things, to such a degree that not even the thinnest leaf of a tree falls to the ground without the will of almighty God the Father, who created it and rules and cares for it. But if anyone thinks that this vast greatness of the whole universe cannot be ruled and governed by one God, who alone exists, he is greatly deceived, and is shown not to know how great the power of divine might is. Indeed, he who was able to bring the whole universe from nothing into being is also able to govern forever, with the greatest wisdom, what he has made. For he knows all things, understands all things, and can do all things, he who alone, omnipotent, created all things.

Therefore we, believing the holy Gospel, should above all venerate divine providence; we should also raise our minds upward, with what strength we can, away from the things that are transitory, and, brought back to ourselves, be united, so that we may faithfully understand the majesty of God present everywhere. Since it is wholly uncircumscribable, it holds all things, fills all things, embraces all things, surpasses all things, and sustains all things. It does not sustain from one side and surpass from another, nor fill from one side and encompass from another; rather, by encompassing it fills, by filling it super-encompasses, by sustaining it surpasses, and by surpassing it sustains all things. By the mass of a body he is nowhere, and by his uncircumscribable substance he is nowhere absent; he is not seen by the eyes of flesh, but is seen through faith in purity of heart. O inexplicable power, O incomprehensible goodness, O ineffable wisdom, O uncircumscribable divinity, which does not consist of parts like a body, nor of passions.
