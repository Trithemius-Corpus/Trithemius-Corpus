God therefore placed his hiding place in the height of the Scriptures, which is so inaccessible and closed to the reprobate that it nevertheless always lies plainly open to the saints.

But why God did not make Holy Scripture sufficient and plain for all things that concern the Christian faith, we shall answer briefly. There are two things which the Most High provided for believers for the confirmation of the Christian faith: namely, the Church and Scripture, so that what is not expressed clearly enough in the one may be more fully declared by the other. Hence, if we find anything in the Holy Scriptures concerning the rules of faith expressed less sufficiently, we at once have recourse to the Church; and where the Church has need of saving instruction for piety, it immediately turns to Scripture. From this it follows that neither would the Church have authority without Scripture, nor Scripture credibility without the Church, so that the judgment of the two may be one. For Augustine says: I would not believe the Gospel unless the authority of the Church compelled me. But John Gerson says: I would not believe the Church unless the Gospel compelled me. Thus the Church confirms Holy Scripture, and is itself confirmed in like manner by Scripture. The Church supplies whenever Scripture is lacking; and if at any time the Church is in doubt, it has recourse to the counsel of Scripture. For there is one Holy Spirit of God, who founded the Church in the faith of Jesus Christ; the very same Almighty one alone inspired Scripture. And this is the threefold cord that is not easily broken: where divine Scripture is joined through the Holy Spirit to the authority of the Church, to such a degree that the Church commends Scripture, and Scripture in turn commends the Church. Therefore, O Caesar of unconquered majesty, as it seems to me, we have assigned a sufficient cause for the proposed question: since, if all things in the Holy Scriptures that concern the piety of faith were plainly expressed, holy mother Church would not seem to be of such great authority, and the merit of saving obedience would in large part be emptied out.

At the same time I think sufficient answer has been given to the principal question, where certain persons suppose Holy Scripture in very many places to be confused and imperfect; yet if they read it with due purity of mind, they would certainly find it perfect and sound. Great is the authority of the Catholic Church, which alone, in doubtful matters concerning the faith, has the right to interpret the Scriptures; to it the necks of all powers must be subjected. If anyone, with rash daring, has departed from its definition, he has denied the Gospel of Christ. There is one congregation of the faithful, the holy Church, mother of Christians, outside whose unity no one will be saved; and even if it sometimes appears weak in morals, nevertheless, always constant in faith, it has never erred, while the loving-kindness of Christ remains intact.
