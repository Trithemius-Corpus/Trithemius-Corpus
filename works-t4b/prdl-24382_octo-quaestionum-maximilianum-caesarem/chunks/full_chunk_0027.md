For the holy doctors conclude that neither demons nor witches can do anything, whether to harm a person or an animal, unless God himself wills it, consents, and grants the power, so that they may do what they have already resolved upon with a perverse will. Unless God consents and wills, no creature can do anything; otherwise, if anything could happen when he did not will and consent to it, it would necessarily follow that God was weak, since he could not prevent what, though unconquered, he would be forced to permit to happen.

But since every human being, insofar as he is a creature, depends on God, he can do nothing without the will of him from whom he has both the will and the power. Whatever, therefore, witches do is done with God permitting it; without his will they can do nothing, because unless God has granted it, they will obtain neither from themselves nor from demons the ability to do what they wish. And so all power is from the Lord God, in whom all things exist and are moved as in the first cause of all things; for from him from whom we received freedom of choice, from him also we have the power to do what we will.

But since the understanding of witches is very confused, they perpetrate the evils they do not by human art, but by the cooperation of demons, whom nature has made subtle and long experience has made swift in producing wonders. For the effects of witches are not caused by such means as they use for witchcraft, such as heads of the dead, toads, serpents, characters, images, and things similar to these, but by the devil who established these signs; since he is most proud, he desires in all things to be seen as comparable to God. For just as God once instituted certain sacraments in his Church, which, when worthily administered and with him assisting, signify and produce a saving effect, while he himself who instituted them works salvation, so also the devil, who desires nothing more than to be regarded as equal to God, instituted certain signs as if they were sacraments. When he is invoked by them by those to whom he is allied by covenants and pacts, the malignant operator assists; and as often as God has permitted it, he effects the evil that is asked. If God has not permitted it, he does absolutely nothing. For indeed, of all the effects of necromancers and witches

the devil is the operator; by the application of the means which he himself first established to the subject matter, he produces the desired work, sometimes really, sometimes only apparently.

But women witches, who by now, with a depraved will and consent, have passed into fellowship with demons through pacts, receive from them certain marks and symbolic signs, by which, when summoned, the demons immediately come. Nothing in these destructive operations of demons is stronger than the foolish credulity of witches, which is increased by pacts and vows, since the firmer the faith they have placed either in the demons or in their signs, the swifter the operation they attain with the demons cooperating. For the excessively savage depravity of the witches’ will in a certain way turns them into madness; when this has preoccupied the mind, the demons at once apply themselves to those who are raving, and, invited by pacts and signs, with God permitting it, they supply the effect of the petition to the women in their frenzy.

For Plato related something similar in the book On Poetic Frenzy, saying: just as bacchant women, when of unsound mind, draw honey and milk from rivers, but when of sound mind they cannot draw them. Their spirit, carried outside itself into fellowship with an evil demon, effects this. We know by experience that witches at times have drawn butter from rushing rivers, which can in no way happen except by the ministry of a demon. In a similar way, when they have been brought into hatred of some person or animal, and sigh for its destruction, they are driven by a most grievous fury, thinking of nothing in their mind except revenge. They take up the things known and familiar to them for witchcraft, invoke demons, and show the signs established by them together with the characters; and what they cannot accomplish by their own power, they obtain by the demons’ ministry.
