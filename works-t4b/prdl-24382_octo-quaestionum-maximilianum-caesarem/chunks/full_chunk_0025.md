...those whom we commonly call necromancers. They profess arts full of execrations, by which they promise that they can summon demons into a circle, or show them openly and visibly enclosed in a crystal or some other vessel. These most pernicious men, full of falsehood and superstitions, commit such great evils as no good man could imagine. For in order to entice demons into obedience to themselves, they very often use the foulest sacrifices and enter into an explicit pact with them. They carry about many various little books full of filth, impiety, and falsehood, falsely inscribed with the names of ancient philosophers and sages; by displaying these they deceive many unwary and curious people and draw them with themselves into the demons’ pit. They promise great and utterly incredible things, the hearing of which keeps men suspended in wonder at the falsehood. By rings, pentacles, images, exorcisms, and conjurations, they say that they can constrain malignant demons so that they appear visibly in a circle, or in a crystal, or in some other receptacle, and answer distinctly to all questions asked. All indeed promise this, but in our times very few are found whom the malignant spirits obey in this way, even in evil matters, since God, for a most just reason, does not permit it: lest human curiosity, prone to novelties, after despising the religion of Christ, should in necessity seek help from demons. But all those whom malignant spirits, summoned by the conjurations of necromancers, obey have without doubt passed into fellowship with them by an explicit or implicit pact. Yet the number of the rest is greater: those whom the malignant spirits disdain to obey, although they do not fear to occupy themselves always in profane necromantic arts. And because especially in our times iniquity has begun to abound and charity has grown cold in many, these accursed and most deceitful men have grown too strong; and although they are despised by demons, nevertheless, persevering in their most pernicious doctrines, they deceive many.

Believe me, Caesar, this most deceitful kind of men is very pernicious to your sacred empire, and therefore, no less than the brood of witches, must be utterly exterminated. They promise great things and lie in everything. Indeed, all the things promised in their books are empty and false, and they furnish believers with nothing other than tricks, frauds, and the most subtle deceptions. And if at times they seem to produce what they have promised, it is manifest to everyone that this happens only by the cooperation of demons; fellowship with them must be fled no less than the most savage torments of hell. For demons very often come to these necromantic operations, and, so that they may keep their invokers obstinate in the treachery they have begun, they grant them, if they can, the things asked. Sometimes, however, when invoked by conjurations, they disdain to appear, and nevertheless they have carried away the minds of those who invoke them.

There is also another kind of men, no less foolish than superstitious: namely those who do not wish to have any commerce with demons, and yet use in their vain operations things appropriated to demons and therefore utterly illicit. For they think that great and hidden power resides in characters, and so they commit various errors against the faith of Christ. But because this is not the time to recount their madness, let us pursue our proposed subject. Why good and just men do not obtain command over demons must be stated briefly. Just as likeness of wills makes evil and impious men equal to demons, so unlikeness of wills renders good and just men wholly contrary to them. But among things contrary and unlike there can be no familiarity; rather, continual opposition and hatred are found. Where, however, no familiarity contracted by likeness of wills is found, neither can there be confidence of one toward the other, nor any mutual concurrence in the working of wonders.
