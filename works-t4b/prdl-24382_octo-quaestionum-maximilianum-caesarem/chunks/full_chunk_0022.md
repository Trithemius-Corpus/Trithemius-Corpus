For if you truly believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, you will necessarily say that the divine Scriptures were inspired by the Holy Spirit. But if, having joined the apostates in a reprobate mind, you say that they were fabricated by men, you impiously show how far you are from true wisdom, wandering in darkness. You plainly show yourselves to be ignorant and rash judges, since you condemn Scripture, which you do not understand. But because it is more familiar to you to despise wisdom than to learn it, it is not the business of this work to weary the mind any longer against the rashness of the ignorant. This one thing I want you to know: that no one understands unless he receives; but because you interpose the filth of your affections so that you may not receive, for that reason understanding of true wisdom is not possessed.

For God, the illuminator of all, deigns by grace to be present to those who, stripped of carnal desires, burn with the fire of inward love, who desire nothing in this world except God. To them the Spirit of God brings help by his presence; those whom he has found rooted in the love of truth and purged of every extraneous thing he leads into the knowledge of all things. For truly, to those who love God, all things are open, all things naked, all things manifest, since whoever through love beholds in the faith of Jesus Christ the one who creates and governs all things knows all that is necessary for his salvation. But, on the other hand, the illumination of God dwells very far from the slothful, wicked feasters, the envious, the greedy, incubi, the filthy, the lustful, murderers, the impious, the proud, and those alien to piety; it leaves them to the judgment of the avenging demon, who, driving in the sharp point of savage fire, afflicts the senses and the mind, and all the more arms and drives the impious to commit crimes, so that, guilty of a more shameful fault, they may also become liable to a more bitter punishment. Hence, entangled in worldly vices and desires, they are not only unable to penetrate the divine Scriptures with understanding,

but, neglecting themselves, they do not even know themselves.

Since, therefore, sacred Scripture is the perfect work of God, Caesar, we must so establish the mind that we believe it to be true, right, complete, solid, divinely inspired, and most abundantly sufficient for all who understand it for the confirmation of the Christian faith. For indeed, unless divine Scripture is believed to be true, holy, perfect in all things, and complete, the Christian faith would be deprived of its foundation. Therefore every Christian must take the greatest care not to overstep the bounds of piety by examining the divine Scriptures more than is fitting, because the author of the Scriptures is not man but the Holy Spirit; whoever struggles against the integrity of the Scriptures of God is proved to resist his ordinance. Scripture, moreover, by God’s ordering, is usefully interwoven in its figurative text with parables, enigmas, likenesses, and comparisons, so that readers may be exercised toward understanding, and, once its inward sweetness has been tasted, the mind may be strengthened toward piety.

And so, if with purified minds we attend to the sense of the Scriptures, not the words, we shall understand how rightly and properly all things have been ordered. For it was not fitting that the sacred Scriptures, in which knowledge of the divine majesty is handed down, should be so plain and manifest that they could be understood everywhere by all; for this knowledge is set forth to be understood only by the good and holy, not also by evil, filthy, and polluted minds. Hence it is written: “The beast that touches the mountain shall be overwhelmed with stones.” What is designated here by the beast, if not human beings who, in imitation of brutes, live without the love of God in the pleasures of the flesh? And what is expressed by the mountain, if not the height of the divine Scriptures? When the beast approaches this holy mountain, it is overwhelmed with stones, because a man impure in mind and weak in faith, who strives to search out the height of the divine Scriptures without a master to teach him, is struck down by various and pernicious errors, as though by stones.
