...you are not divided as a soul is; you do not underlie forms as do all the other things that have been made through you; you are in all things and above all things, ruling and governing the universe with the greatest wisdom. By these testimonies, therefore, most sacred Caesar Maximilian, it has been sufficiently proved, as I think, that this whole universe is governed by the providence of God. Since he is pure intellect, simple, uncreated, and eternal, he knows all things, understands all things, foreknows all things; and since he is almighty by nature, he can do all things. To him nothing is difficult, nothing impossible; he created such wondrous things by his power; he established them in truth; by reason he perfected them; by strength he sustains them; by goodness he illumines them. To him be praise, to him honor, to him glory, who lives and reigns, God glorious and exalted, forever and ever. Amen.

THE AUTHOR'S DECLARATION TO CAESAR.

These, most unconquered Caesar Maximilian, are the things that seemed to me should be said in reply to the Eight Questions proposed by your majesty. For you wished to have my judgment on them also in writing, making an estimate of me beyond the truth, since I am not to be compared in talent with those whose opinion you heard before me. Let pardon make me worthy if I have said anything less proper; for, at the command of your highness, set as a traveler on a journey, I dictated as I could, at intervals, whatever occurred to me to answer. But I say from my mind and openly declare that I am a sincere confessor of the Christian faith, and that I wish to remain until death in the union of the sacrosanct Roman Church. Therefore, if anything either in this lucubration of mine or in others should be found said or written otherwise than that Church holds, thinks, believes, and teaches, as a true son of obedience, by the attestation of these presents I now simply revoke it, and wish it to be and to be held perpetually revoked.

--- Page 085 ---
Wherefore, most wise Caesar, I both humbly ask and earnestly admonish your majesty that, before you cause my responses to come to the knowledge of those for whom they are destined and kept, you have them approved by the judgment of many. For great care must be taken that nothing be proposed to those for whose salvation your serenity keeps watch except what is solid, approved, manifest, and lawfully confirmed in the Catholic faith by the opinion of very many wise men. May almighty God by his providence direct and guard you always to the praise of his name, Maximilian, greatest of emperors. From the city of Neometana, on the tenth day of the month of July, in the year of Christians one thousand five hundred and eight.

Johannes Trithemius, Abbot.
