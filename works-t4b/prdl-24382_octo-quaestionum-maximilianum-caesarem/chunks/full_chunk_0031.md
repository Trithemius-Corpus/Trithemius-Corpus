Let the answer we have given not seem absurd to your majesty, O Caesar, since it in no way prejudices divine goodness that he permits evils to be done for a most just reason; rather, indeed, it would prejudice it if he did not permit evils to be done in the present world. For truly, if he did not permit evils to be done, man would now be unable to sin; he would certainly be impeccable, which does not belong to his nature, to which the Creator of all gave free will from the beginning. For since man is a creature and not the Creator, he is necessarily capable of being turned toward good as well as toward evil; his being depends on the supreme maker of all creatures, just as every caused thing depends on the cause of its own being. But to create has been left to God alone, who is incorruptible and everlasting; everything corruptible, however, necessarily fails, but is preserved by the goodness of the Creator so long as it does not lose the influence of its cause. Hence it neither could nor ought to have happened that man should be created impeccable by the condition of his nature, a thing that only God, the maker of all, naturally enjoys, who is immutable and everlasting. Whenever, indeed, it is granted to man that he seems unable to sin, freedom of choice is not removed, nor is the possibility simply taken away, but the will is confirmed in good. And whenever this gift is conferred on a creature, it is a gift of grace, not of nature. For Scripture says: God from the beginning created man and left him in the hand of his own counsel. For, properly speaking, it cannot be that mortal man, conceived in original displeasure, should both have free will and be unable to commit sin. For the most holy bishop Augustine says: The first freedom of the will was to be able to sin; the last, in glory, will much more be to be unable to sin. Therefore, if man could not sin, he would not have mastery over his own will. Hence, although almighty God does not will sin, yet he justly permits men to sin and to do evil, so that, while freedom of choice remains, both the virtue of a good work may receive its reward, and voluntary depravity may receive the evil it has done. does good or sins unwillingly. Therefore almighty God justly permits men to sin and to do evil, since he granted them freedom of choice from the beginning. There are, moreover, many reasons long ago assigned by the holy doctors why the Almighty permits such great sorceries to be done through the cooperation of demons; of these we shall necessarily make brief mention in order.

The first is so that the excellence of divine power may be made manifest, for he alone is immutable and above every ordinance of law. Every mutable creature has no comparison with the immutable Creator. Hence in Numbers we read: God is not like a man, that he should lie, nor like a son of man, that he should be changed. In Isaiah also it is said: God is faithful, without iniquity, just and upright. In the book of Tobit it is also written: You are just, O Lord, and all your judgments are true, and all your ways are mercy, truth, and judgment. And in the book of holy Job we read: Impiety is far from God, and iniquity from the Almighty. For he will render to a man his work, and according to the ways of each he will restore to them. Therefore every man who rightly understands his own ignorance and weakness plainly recognizes how much he is nothing in comparison with his Creator. For God justly permits man to sin, both so that freedom of choice may be continued and so that the excellence of the great God may become known to human beings.

The second reason is so that the inscrutable wisdom of God may become known to mortals, since he knows how to draw good out of our evils, when he makes one person’s sin become another person’s merit for the increase of virtue. For if he did not permit evil to be done, which of us would understand good? For God makes and orders good; evils, however, he does not make, but he disposes that those done by the impious should not come about in a disordered way. Nor does the malice of men impede the will of God, nor can man do anything that God does not permit.
