Anne, take heart: behold, your daughter stretches toward your milky breasts. Hail.
With tender hands she seeks your neck. Hail.
Anne, you break off delay, baring breast and bosom. Hail.
You receive her tender limbs in your tender lap. Hail.
Anne, you cherish your daughter; from your breast she draws sweet juices. Hail.
With rosy mouth she sounds pleasing murmurs. Hail.
Anne, if you knew what great sufferings your daughter will bear, Hail,
and how many deaths she will endure, you would now be no more. Hail.
Pater noster. Ave Dei.
Anne, it is dear to your heart to adorn your daughter with approved Hail
manners, and to have prepared her for the highest God. Hail.
Anne, you prepare to bring your daughter into the lofty shrine, Hail,
but of her own will the gracious child reached the step of the heavenly ones. Hail.
Anne, with slow step and labor you follow her as she hastens on. Hail.
The ready little Virgin comforts her weary mother. Hail.
Anne, why do you marvel at strength in the tender Virgin? Hail.
You see not human things, but holy signs of God. Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to give gifts to the poor and to sacred temples. Hail.
Now you give that than which no woman has given anything greater. Hail.
Anne, when you wished your consecrated daughter to dwell there, Hail,
she gladly entered the cloisters and the house of virgins. Hail.
Anne, as you are about to send away your holy daughter, you wet cheeks and eyes Hail
with weeping; deep love holds you unmoved. Hail.
Anne, return home; give your daughter over to the Thunderer. Hail.
Let her think of nothing but the Jesus who is to come. Hail.
Anne, let your daughter fly through the airy recesses. Hail.
Let love, sent from the highest summit, possess her. Hail.
Anne, it is enough for two Marys to serve you. Hail.
They too will honor you with no small devotion. Hail.
Pater noster. Ave Dei.
Anne, by your own weeping you have learned to succor one who weeps. Hail.
Therefore you too readily favor the wretched with your prayers. Hail.
Anne, you are accustomed to overturn the fixed laws of nature. Hail.
And so, when you will, let the barren woman be a joyful mother. Hail.
Anne, you also stay the gifts of fleeting fortune, Hail,
lest, slipping away suddenly, they flee from our bosoms. Hail.
Anne, you drive off disgrace; you grant the gifts of fair renown. Hail.
You turn back sorrows, and love yields to you. Hail.
Anne, you help whether hostile force terrifies someone, or whether Hail
the chains of a dark prison hold him bound. Hail.
Anne, be present as sure relief for sick mortals. Hail.
With you as guide, lost life returns to the languishing. Hail.
