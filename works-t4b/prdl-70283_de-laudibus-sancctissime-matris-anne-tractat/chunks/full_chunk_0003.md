John Trithemius. 5 D. 6716.

A tetrastich by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, on the most holy mother Anne.

Venerable Anne, mother of the most gracious Virgin,
excelling in merits and powerful in piety,
no one goes away sorrowful who honors and venerates you;
for you bring to all the gentle aid of salvation.

A distich by the same, which he placed before an image of Saint Anne.

Whoever in adversity has called upon holy Anne
with devout prayers, he will be well protected.

A tetrastich by Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate, on Saint Anne.

Anne has never sent away anyone’s prayers empty,
but, gracious, has always brought the desired help.
Therefore let us heap great honor upon the loving mother,
that no prayers may be empty.

A tetrastich to Saint Anne by Theodoric Gresemund the Younger of Mainz.

Hail, Anne, parent of the Virgin Mary,
worthy to be called the work of our salvation,
through whom human redemption was received by the one who was sought,
continually covering our sins with prayers.

A distich on Saint Anne by Rudolf Langen, canon of Münster.

Great Anne, mother of the second Virgin,
hear the pregnant women who bring vows to you from the womb.

A tetrastich on Saint Anne by Jodocus Badius of Ghent.

Anne, glory of the world, mother of the mother of God,
your grandson, God, through the merits of his mother, possesses the stars.
Holy Anne, first parent of our salvation,
bring us, together with your child, to that grandson.

Rudolf Agricola, among other things, wrote thus in a poem on Saint Anne.
