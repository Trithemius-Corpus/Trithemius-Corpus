Prayer to be said after the rosary

Almighty and most loving God, who deigned to choose blessed Anne as the mother of the mother of your Son, graciously grant that we who honor her memory with faithful devotion may obtain, by her merits and prayers, the helps of eternal life. Through etc.

A poem by Ade Wernher of Themar on most holy Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.

To the praises of the illustrious grandmother we turn our song;
dear grandson Jesus, move the plectra of the heart.
Nor may you, daughter, fail the singer’s choir,
chaste mother Anne.

Anne, offspring of kings, endures long with her husband,
unfruitful, and laments that she is barren.
Weeping, she prays for a child,
and her tears do not flow in vain.

Heavenly messengers promise offspring;
pregnant after union with her husband, then as a mother,
she is proved by the child born,
through whom our salvation came.

Thus the venerable stock of Jesse grew green;
a fruitful rod comes forth with fertile shoot.
Refreshed by its flower,
we soon live again from death.

By this birth Joachim becomes worthy
to stand in the shrines, removing the priest’s reproaches.
So, Anne, prepare to carry
pure prayers from us to your grandson.

Commend us to the child whom your holy chastity
bore as mother; by prayer we ask this,
O blessed matron.
Christ denies you nothing.

To Christ, who reigns in fellowship
with the Father and the Spirit, be glory through all ages;
him your prayers, O Anne,
make reconciled to us. Amen.

This hymn is sung to the tune of the one often used in many churches on the Nativity of Saint Mary, beginning “Gaude visceribus mater in intimis,” etc. From the castle at Heidelberg, on the third day before the Kalends of February, 1493.

A poem or hymn by Johannes Herbsluterburgensis on Saint Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot.

Rejoice, Anne, refuge for the wretched.
We pray, we sing, we bring forth songs.
May you be gracious on this feast, with your offspring;
now we speak praises to you.

Rejoice, shining earth, fertile with grass,
and moistened from the rain with heavenly dew,
from which first was born in the upper air
the Virgin, without knowledge of sin.

Rejoice, you who were of royal seed;
now joined to a husband, you had entered
upon a lawful marriage, long without offspring,
then made a mother by your vow.

Rejoice, you who blushed at the high priest’s reproach;
confounded, you call upon God with tears.
Having been heard, you receive your husband as he comes to meet you
at the gate called Golden.

Rejoice, you who embraced Joachim,
glad as your husband came to meet you;
taught by a heavenly power, and so, by your offspring,
you conceive in the womb.

Rejoice, simplicity wise with angelic knowledge,
you who, praised by the choir and divided in threefold fashion,
share your goods with the Temple and the poor,
and a portion with your servants.

Rejoice: the people of the faithful celebrate your feasts,
that, as their patroness, you may make the Spirit with the Father

An elegiac poem on Saint Anne by Rutger of Venray, canon regular in Heyna, in the diocese of Worms.

Splendid mother of the virginal mother,
of the Father’s radiance and of the brightness of untouched light, hail.
You, holy one, bore for the Lord the divine girl,
from whom came forth kindly Jesus, the light of the world.

You must steadfastly call the child born of your daughter your grandson,
although he himself made heaven and earth.
Your grandson himself, Jesus, joined flesh
to the highest Godhead; for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always existed, still he chooses
and deigns to be called your grandson.
Do not hesitate to urge such a grandson with prayers,
Anne; we beg you, and you will obtain all you ask.

If at first he does not bend easy ears,
run quickly, Anne, to your daughter.
Let your daughter show her breasts to her son Christ;
at once the kindly mother will bring what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to ask for,
let the daughter seek help together with her own mother.
My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that at once
the Lord will hear both as they pray with gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers we ought
to seek the help of Anne and Mary together.
It is established that, if you honor the mother of the Lord’s mother,
you yourself also revere the titles of the Virgin Mother.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it becomes clear
that you prepare praises for the Lord in his mother.
To Anne’s daughter-born Son be praise, power, and honor,
and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

A Sapphic poem by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, on the day of Saint Anne.

Let us celebrate the feasts of sacred Anne,

rich through the merits of her exalted mother,
who bore the parent of the Son
of God the Father for the world.

Her life, always to be venerated by us,
is rightly praised as blessed,
marked by a gift, bringing comfort
to afflicted minds.

She heals the weak, purges all evils,
brightens sorrowful hearts with joy,
sets in order, rules, and governs what is fitting,
and makes rough places smooth.

Mighty, seated in the court of the highest King,
she wipes away stains and sins,
bestows grace, and leads faithful souls
into the stars.

Here, brothers who throng your feasts,
uttering vows and praying to you for good things:
help, preserve, refresh, and govern us,
illustrious mother.

Honor and power be to the Thunderer above,
who, one simple God and likewise three,
gave the crown of great glory
to Anne forever. Amen.

This Sapphic hymn is sung to the melody of “Ut queant laxis,” etc.; “Vita sanctorum, Virginis proles”; “Quod chorus vatum”; “Iste confessor”; and similar hymns that are only Sapphic. From Spanheim, on the Nones of July, in the year of the Lord 1493.

An elegiac poem by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, on Saint Mother Anne.

How great the rewards are that Anne gives back to her worshipers,
no one grasps in mind or can speak with mouth.
She drives off hardships, obtains pardon, increases
adornments of honor for her own, and makes them dwell in heaven.

By contrast, many exceedingly bitter things accompany
despisers of Mother Anne; look out for yourself, wicked man.
She crushes the proud, casts the powerful down from honor,
and often permits them to bear great scandals.

This is evident and remains fixed: Anne offers joyful things
to those who honor her, but brings back many sad things to her enemies.
