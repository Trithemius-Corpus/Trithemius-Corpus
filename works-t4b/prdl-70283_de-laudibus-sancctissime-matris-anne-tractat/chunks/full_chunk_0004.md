Johann Theutheinus

*On the praises of the most holy mother Anne*

Anne, mother, venerable mother of the highest parent,
you who first open the way of salvation to the peoples:
all diseases flee from you, and pallor and pain;
all harmful things that settle in mind and body;
whatever there is among men that wearies cares and hearts
flees from you, as clouds, driven by the winds, are wont to do.

Rutger of Venray, regular canon of Heyna, at the image of Saint Anne

Say deep prayers devoutly, with knee bent,
where mother Anne sits with her blessed offspring.
Do not doubt: you will obtain whatever you ask of her,
for Anne shines continually, powerful in prayers.

Distich by the same on Saint Anne

Anne, mother, rejoice in the mother of Jesus Christ,
and, we pray, drive every sin away from us.

Rosary of Jodocus Beissel, patrician of Aachen, on Saint Anne, addressed to the reverend father and lord Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim, of the Order of the divine father Benedict.

Whoever wishes to read this rosary in honor of the most blessed mother Anne, in pious devotion, should first say the Lord’s Prayer with the salutation of Saint Anne, which he should always repeat after two verses. The salutation is as follows:

Hail, venerable Anne, mother of the Mother of God, most pleasing to the Holy Trinity, honored above all women. Blessed are you by the Lord, and blessed is the offspring of your womb, most devoutly born of the Virgin: Jesus Christ. Amen.

Our Father. Hail, Mother of God.

Anne, may I, wretched as I am, bring forth your praises;
for if you allow it, I shall bring garlands and roses to you.
Hail.

Anne, you shine as a venerable offspring of the kings of Judea,
and also as one renowned through your pious husband Joachim.
Hail.

Anne, long barren, you wept in love for your husband,
the unique glory of married women, and their one honor.
Hail.

Pious Anne, you seek offspring, not for vain uses,
but so that, by holy posterity, it may worship God.
Hail.

Anne, a swift messenger from the heavenly court stands by you
and says, “Rejoice; behold, as a mother you will have offspring.”
Hail.

Anne, you will bear offspring blessed with such great virtue
that, as mother, she will shine with virginal honor.
Hail.

Anne, you will bear the pleasing one whom the world does not contain;
the willing God himself will enter her chaste womb.
Hail.

Anne, at last from your blood there will arise blood
which, poured out, will bring hope always to wretched sinners.
Hail.

Anne, so great an expectation befitted your childbirth;
it is no small thing to be held the mother of the mother of God.
Hail.

Anne, the praises of your offspring will fly above the stars;
all ages will bear you as happy by night and by day.
Hail.

Our Father. Hail, Mother of God. Hail.

Anne, you are believed to have conceived Mary pure from evil,
and soon to have held her holy for God.
Hail.

Anne, you carry one other than the figures signified,
the one to come, whom the holy fathers sang.
Hail.

Anne, you carry the closed paradise, the tree of life,
happy and sacred with all the honor of the countryside.
Hail.

Anne, pregnant, you carry the garden which the flaming sword
of the cherubim guards, glowing night and day.
Hail.

Anne, you carry a flower that surpasses white lilies,
humble violets, and the crimson rose.
Hail.

Anne, in you the Mosaic ark is recognized,
and the golden urn of heavenly bread lies within.
Hail.

Anne, you carry the bride of the true Solomon,
and a house adorned within and without with every jewel.
Hail.

Anne, you carry the temple whose inner chambers
sweet sounds, music, and every rejoicing filled.
Hail.

Anne, you carry the veil, under the beautiful image of the fathers,
holding within itself what it bore, which covers the deep things of God.
Hail.

Anne, you carry the offspring whose head a golden flame
of Phoebus clothes, and whose snow-white feet the bright moon clothes.
Hail.

Our Father. Hail, Mother of God. Hail.

Anne, now made possessor of the longed-for Mary,
you scarcely contain in your whole heart the joys that have arisen.
Hail.

Anne, all the stars applauded Mary newly born,
and an unfamiliar radiance flowed from the pole of heaven.
Hail.

Anne, it is right to believe that angelic choirs
celebrated your child’s birth around you.
Hail.

Anne, the deep shades felt your childbirth;
then the new light itself terrified the Stygian pools.
Hail.

Anne, as mother, you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands,
more ruddy than the sun and than the lights of heaven.
Hail.

Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl?
She smiles on her mother; she does no sad things.
Hail.
