Anne, you will bear the beloved daughter into whose chaste womb
God himself, whom the world does not contain, willingly enters. Hail.

Anne, at last from your blood will arise that blood
which, when poured out, will bring hope forever to wretched sinners. Hail.

Anne, so great an expectation befitted your childbearing;
it is no easy thing to be held the mother of the mother of God. Hail.

Anne, the praises of your offspring will fly above the stars,
and all ages will proclaim you also blessed. Hail.

Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, you are believed to have conceived Mary pure from evil,
and soon to have held her, holy, for God. Hail.

Anne, in your womb you carry her whom the figures marked as coming,
whom the holy fathers sang of. Hail.

Anne, you carry the enclosed paradise, the happy tree of life,
sacred with every honor of the field. Hail.

Anne, pregnant, you carry the garden which the flaming sword
of the cherub guards, burning night and day. Hail.

Anne, you carry the flower that surpasses white lilies,
lowly violets, and the crimson rose. Hail.

Anne, in you the Mosaic ark is discerned,
and there lies the golden urn of heavenly bread. Hail.

Anne, you carry the bride of the true Solomon,
adorned with every jewel, within and without the house. Hail.

Anne, you carry the temple whose inner chambers
sweet sounds and every rejoicing music have filled. Hail.

Anne, you carry the veil under a beautiful image,
holding in itself the deeds of the fathers, which covers the high things of God. Hail.

Anne, you carry the offspring whose head Phoebus' golden flame clothes,
and whose snow-white feet the shining moon clothes. Hail.

Our Father. Hail of God.

Anne, now made possessor of longed-for Mary,
you scarcely contain in your whole breast the joys that have sprung up. Hail.

Anne, all the stars applauded Mary newly born,
and an unwonted radiance flowed from the pole. Hail.

Anne, it is right to believe that angelic choirs around you
celebrated the birthday of your child. Hail.

Anne, the deep shades below felt your childbearing;
then the new light itself terrified the Stygian pools. Hail.

Anne, as a mother you hesitate to touch your daughter with your hands,
though she is more radiant than the sun and the lights of heaven. Hail.

Anne, why do you fear to handle the heavenly girl?
She smiles on her mother; she does nothing sorrowful. Hail.
