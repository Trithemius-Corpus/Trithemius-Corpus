of the Mother of God and Virgin, I have noted beforehand. And lest anyone take this otherwise than my intention holds, I have thought that the reader should be warned. For I know, and it has not escaped me, that many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the stain of original sin; these men flourished long before the Council of Basel. Granting their opinion to its time, I pass them by entirely untouched, and I do not intend to say or write anything at all against them. But if anyone sees that something has been said by me rather harshly, let it be referred not to those who think otherwise than we do, but only to the obstinate, who, chattering without learning, do not hesitate to call the supporters of the other opinion heretics. And this is what my words indicate, since they mark the present time and not the past. For when I say, “What do the foolish bark? What do the ignorant murmur?” this belongs to the present time, not to the past, and today it reproves the obstinate, not the ancients who held another opinion. And when I ask what learned man has ever written the contrary, I am not speaking of the ancient fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the aforesaid council, hold a different opinion. Thus I condemn only the obstinacy of certain unlearned men of this time, not the opinion of learned men, whoever they may be, who hold another view, since indeed I am not unaware of the declaration of Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff.

Moreover, it has seemed to me that faith in the most pure conception in no way departs from the holy Roman Church: she believes and venerates it, as is shown today by clear documents. Finally, together with the doctors of many universities, Paris, Cologne, Heidelberg, Erfurt, Vienna, Leipzig, Tübingen, Ingolstadt, Basel, Mainz, and many others, and with almost all the orders and faithful of Christ spread throughout the whole Christian world, I do not fear to venerate as pure the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary. Yet I do not admit obstinacy, but I wish in all things to obey the Roman Church, as the most reverend mother of all churches. This is the pure and sincere intention of my aforesaid chapter, in which I judge that no injury is done to anyone who holds an opinion soberly and without obstinacy. Farewell, reader.

This letter of the aforesaid lord Johannes, abbot, is missing from some copies through carelessness and negligence; nevertheless, he ordered it to be added to all copies and decreed that it be regarded as added to each one.
