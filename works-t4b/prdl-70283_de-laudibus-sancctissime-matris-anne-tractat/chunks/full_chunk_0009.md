A Sapphic Poem of Rutger Siccambri,
for a hymn in praise of Saint Anne

We sing your praises, venerable
Anne; favor those who praise you with holy measures,
obtaining pardon for all,
granting the rewards of salvation.

You, Anne, parent of the mother of Christ,
shining in the highest citadel of heaven,
obtain whatever you ask from the begotten Son
of the Almighty.

You heal the languishing; you wipe away stains;
you make faithful servants
possessors of their vow; at last you grant them
to climb the heavenly kingdom.

Anne, approved in morals, you bent
the highest Lord with many prayers,
that he might grant you to be made the parent
of so great a child in the world.

To the highest Father, to the heavenly Son,
to the Holy Spirit be honor and power,
brightness, virtue, holiness, and strength
through every age.

Another by the Same

Let us all resound the diligent praises
of Anne, mother of the Virgin mother,
with all our strength, from the marrow of the soul,
with ringing voice.

She poured forth for the wretched race,
stained by the blemish of Eve its mother,
a noble offspring, and a radiant beam
she brought forth for the world.

This light of the world drove out
the darkness of the darkened world,
shining upon all with heavenly light,
as holy John proclaims in the sacred book.

Anne, mother of the divine maiden,

Another Iambic Dimeter Poem by the Same,
which is sung to the melody of the Ambrosian hymns

Bowed down, let us honor Anne,
the feast of the blessed one; due
thanks we sing with our voices
and with our inmost hearts.

This is the mother of the kindly Virgin,
who brought forth the light of the world;
this is she who by her virtues
made herself most pleasing to God.

She drives delays away; she confers
grace by her prayers;
she relieves the most dreadful diseases,
of body as well as spirit.

And in praise of this one, King of heaven,
to you with sonorous songs,
and likewise to the Son and the Spirit,
we bring forth devout praises. Amen.

An Alcinanic Poem by the Same, which is sung to the melody
“Crux fidelis,” or “Pange lingua”

This one, with clear voice, with vows
and devout hearts,
desires to set forth the feasts
of Anne, so illustrious a mother.

Be present gently, we pray,
to those praising you together;
you are the parent of Mary's mother,
therefore most pleasing to God.

You preserved the beginnings of the Law,

Wonderful and gracious gifts

by prayer pleasing

You heal the sick and grant

We ask you, Father,

as we sing praises
of illustrious, most sweet Anne,

Poem of Rutger, a venerable German, canon regular of Bödingen, on the Book Concerning the Praises of Saint Anne

Whoever you will be, reader of this excellent book,
put aside empty fear: it contains nothing frivolous.
This book, pregnant with wondrous meaning and sweet with charm,
Abbot Trithemius has produced by his art.

Keeping watch over books, guardian of the sheepfold of Spanheim,
he cultivates the sacred writings of the threefold tongue.
You will find here the titles of Anne, full of reason and art;
only turn them over with a devout heart.

He does not pursue empty and doubtful tales everywhere;
he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence.
Therefore, I pray, read with love the worthy praises
of venerable Anne, which this learned man has brought forth for you.
