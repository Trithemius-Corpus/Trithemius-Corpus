*Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim: a tetrastich on the most holy mother Anne*

Venerable Anne, mother of the gracious Virgin,
excellent in merits and powerful in piety:
no one goes away sorrowful who honors and venerates you;
for you bring to all the gentle aid of salvation.

*A distich by the same, which he placed at the image of Saint Anne*

Whoever in adversity has called upon Saint Anne
with devout prayers will be well protected.

*Conrad Celtis Protucius, poet laureate: a tetrastich on Saint Anne*

Anne has never dismissed anyone's prayers empty,
but, bright one, has always brought the desired aid.
Therefore let us heap much honor upon the pious mother,
who allows no one's prayers to be without effect.

*Theodoric Gresemund the Younger of Mainz: a tetrastich to Saint Anne*

Anne, parent of the Virgin, hail, auspicious mother of Mary,
worthy to begin the work of our salvation.
Since human redemption was begun through you, I pray,
continue, by your prayers, to cover our sins.

*Rudolf Lang, canon of Münster: a distich on Saint Anne*

Great parent Anne, mother of the fruitful Virgin, hear
the prayers that pregnant women bring to you in their womb.

*Jodocus Badius of Ghent: a tetrastich on Saint Anne*

Anne, glory of the world, mother of the mother of Jesus,
may your grandson grant you the stars for the merits of your daughter.
Holy Anne, bringing forth the first fruits of our salvation,
help us with your offspring and with your grandson.

*On the praises of the most holy mother Anne*

Anne, mother, venerable mother of the highest parent,
you who first open the way of salvation to the peoples:
all diseases flee from you, and pallor and pain;
all harmful things that settle in mind and body;
whatever there is among men that wearies cares and hearts
flees from you, as clouds, driven by the winds, are wont to do.

Rutger of Venray, regular canon of Heyna, at the image of Saint Anne

Say deep prayers devoutly, with knee bent,
where mother Anne sits with her blessed offspring.
Do not doubt: you will obtain whatever you ask of her,
for Anne shines continually, powerful in prayers.

Distich by the same on Saint Anne

Anne, mother, rejoice in the mother of Jesus Christ,
and, we pray, drive every sin away from us.

An elegiac poem on Saint Anne by Rutger of Venray, canon regular in Heyna, in the diocese of Worms.

Splendid mother of the virginal mother,
of the Father’s radiance and of the brightness of untouched light, hail.
You, holy one, bore for the Lord the divine girl,
from whom came forth kindly Jesus, the light of the world.

You must steadfastly call the child born of your daughter your grandson,
although he himself made heaven and earth.
Your grandson himself, Jesus, joined flesh
to the highest Godhead; for he is God and man.

Therefore, although Jesus has always existed, still he chooses
and deigns to be called your grandson.
Do not hesitate to urge such a grandson with prayers,
Anne; we beg you, and you will obtain all you ask.

If at first he does not bend easy ears,
run quickly, Anne, to your daughter.
Let your daughter show her breasts to her son Christ;
at once the kindly mother will bring what is desired.

If the matter is very difficult to ask for,
let the daughter seek help together with her own mother.
My faith is firm, and I do not doubt that at once
the Lord will hear both as they pray with gracious ear.

Therefore, brothers, with continual prayers we ought
to seek the help of Anne and Mary together.
It is established that, if you honor the mother of the Lord’s mother,
you yourself also revere the titles of the Virgin Mother.

Again, if you venerate the mother of the Lord, it becomes clear
that you prepare praises for the Lord in his mother.
To Anne’s daughter-born Son be praise, power, and honor,
and to the highest Father and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

An elegiac poem by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, on Saint Mother Anne.

How great the rewards are that Anne gives back to her worshipers,
no one grasps in mind or can speak with mouth.
She drives off hardships, obtains pardon, increases
adornments of honor for her own, and makes them dwell in heaven.

By contrast, many exceedingly bitter things accompany
despisers of Mother Anne; look out for yourself, wicked man.
She crushes the proud, casts the powerful down from honor,
and often permits them to bear great scandals.

This is evident and remains fixed: Anne offers joyful things
to those who honor her, but brings back many sad things to her enemies.

which is sung to the melody of the Ambrosian hymns

Bowed down, let us honor Anne,
the feast of the blessed one; due
thanks we sing with our voices
and with our inmost hearts.

This is the mother of the kindly Virgin,
who brought forth the light of the world;
this is she who by her virtues
made herself most pleasing to God.

She drives delays away; she confers
grace by her prayers;
she relieves the most dreadful diseases,
of body as well as spirit.

And in praise of this one, King of heaven,
to you with sonorous songs,
and likewise to the Son and the Spirit,
we bring forth devout praises. Amen.

An Alcinanic Poem by the Same, which is sung to the melody
“Crux fidelis,” or “Pange lingua”

This one, with clear voice, with vows
and devout hearts,
desires to set forth the feasts
of Anne, so illustrious a mother.

Be present gently, we pray,
to those praising you together;
you are the parent of Mary's mother,
therefore most pleasing to God.

Poem of Rutger, a venerable German, canon regular of Bödingen, on the Book Concerning the Praises of Saint Anne

Whoever you will be, reader of this excellent book,
put aside empty fear: it contains nothing frivolous.
This book, pregnant with wondrous meaning and sweet with charm,
Abbot Trithemius has produced by his art.

Keeping watch over books, guardian of the sheepfold of Spanheim,
he cultivates the sacred writings of the threefold tongue.
You will find here the titles of Anne, full of reason and art;
only turn them over with a devout heart.

He does not pursue empty and doubtful tales everywhere;
he teaches the clear streams of sacred eloquence.
Therefore, I pray, read with love the worthy praises
of venerable Anne, which this learned man has brought forth for you.

Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Spanheim, to the reader: greetings. When recently, at the urging of friends, I had resolved to write something concerning the praises of the most holy mother Anne, certain fathers of the Order of the Brothers of Blessed Mary Ever-Virgin of Mount Carmel asked that I not omit a defense of the most pure Marian conception. And so, persuaded by their prayers and otherwise by just reason, I [unclear] the seventh chapter of this work, concerning the conception

of the Mother of God and Virgin, I have noted beforehand. And lest anyone take this otherwise than my intention holds, I have thought that the reader should be warned. For I know, and it has not escaped me, that many most holy and most learned men once wrote that the Mother of God was subject to the stain of original sin; these men flourished long before the Council of Basel. Granting their opinion to its time, I pass them by entirely untouched, and I do not intend to say or write anything at all against them. But if anyone sees that something has been said by me rather harshly, let it be referred not to those who think otherwise than we do, but only to the obstinate, who, chattering without learning, do not hesitate to call the supporters of the other opinion heretics. And this is what my words indicate, since they mark the present time and not the past. For when I say, “What do the foolish bark? What do the ignorant murmur?” this belongs to the present time, not to the past, and today it reproves the obstinate, not the ancients who held another opinion. And when I ask what learned man has ever written the contrary, I am not speaking of the ancient fathers, but I mean those who, after the determination of the aforesaid council, hold a different opinion. Thus I condemn only the obstinacy of certain unlearned men of this time, not the opinion of learned men, whoever they may be, who hold another view, since indeed I am not unaware of the declaration of Sixtus IV, supreme pontiff.

Moreover, it has seemed to me that faith in the most pure conception in no way departs from the holy Roman Church: she believes and venerates it, as is shown today by clear documents. Finally, together with the doctors of many universities, Paris, Cologne, Heidelberg, Erfurt, Vienna, Leipzig, Tübingen, Ingolstadt, Basel, Mainz, and many others, and with almost all the orders and faithful of Christ spread throughout the whole Christian world, I do not fear to venerate as pure the conception of the Mother of God and Virgin Mary. Yet I do not admit obstinacy, but I wish in all things to obey the Roman Church, as the most reverend mother of all churches. This is the pure and sincere intention of my aforesaid chapter, in which I judge that no injury is done to anyone who holds an opinion soberly and without obstinacy. Farewell, reader.

This letter of the aforesaid lord Johannes, abbot, is missing from some copies through carelessness and negligence; nevertheless, he ordered it to be added to all copies and decreed that it be regarded as added to each one.
