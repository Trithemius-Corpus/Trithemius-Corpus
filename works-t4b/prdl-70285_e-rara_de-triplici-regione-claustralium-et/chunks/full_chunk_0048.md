Take from past things a consideration of present things, and see that what has passed away is not, and what now seems to be will not be after a little while. Where are the kings of the nations? Where the princes? Where the leaders? Where the rich? Where those once powerful in the world? Where are all the lovers of the world? Where the men who despised the arduous road to heaven? Finally, where are all those once known to you in the world, whom death has meanwhile snatched away while you have been in the cloister? Behold, from the origin of the world, both those who lived for many years and those who lived for few have all died in their own generation; and, according to the diversity of their merits, at the dictate of justice, they have received diverse rewards. The lovers of God, joined to the Creator of all, receive everlasting bright dwellings filled with inestimable joys; and the worshipers of the world, contemners of God's commandments, once swollen here with honors, puffed up with knowledge, immersed in riches, and devoted to pleasures and vices, have descended to hell to be tormented without end. I ask you, what harm now does the once laborious contempt of the world, contrary to the flesh, do to the saints in heaven, for which they now rejoice forever in the glad vision of God? Or what profit now is the once-enjoyed consolation of the flesh to the impious buried in hell, for which they now receive the torments of eternal damnation? Think on these things while it is profitable; meditate on these things while the opportunity of taking care is present.

Again, ascending higher, try, with the gaze of your mind, to fix yourself as far as you can in the abyss of infinite depth, and to consider how long that is which is eternal. Eternity is time that is enclosed by no end and bounded by no span. The saints of God will rejoice forever, blessed in glory; sinners will burn forever, wretched in hell. O eternity, name terrible without end: who will be able to think of you, or to speak of you, without fear? Truly, he who thinks of you will never sin forever. Eternity has no end. Hear a comparison; and when it proves insufficient for eternity, take up fear. If a heap of sand were set in the size of Mount Olympus, stretched up to heaven, from which after ten thousand times ten thousand years a small bird consumed only one grain,

that mountain would by no means be said to be going to last forever, but would be subject to an end; and although after innumerable years, nevertheless it would at some point cease. Yet all that time during which some part of the mountain remained, however very long it might be, would nevertheless seem nothing in comparison with eternity. For eternity admits neither the imagination of an end, nor the hope of completion, nor a boundary.

O therefore how foolish, how witless, how insane is the man who casts away this consideration of eternity, and spends uselessly the acceptable time granted to him for meriting eternal goods. The hour will come, and already hastens near, in which the wretch, when he can no longer act better, will learn what eternity is. But you, my monk, who desire to be faithful, cast away from yourself every care of the world; place all your concern on the love of God. Do nothing, think nothing, except what can lead you to the love of your Creator, or what you know pertains to the edification of others or the pure necessity of the body. Separate yourself, I pray, from low things, so that, good as you are, you may be delighted by eternal things. Let no care of the flesh remove you from the inward tranquillity of the mind; let no pleasure corrupt you. Strive always, as much as you can, to persist in prayers with devotion, and again to raise yourself more often through the affection of love.

On Always Having Zeal for God.

The Seventh Root of Fear.

The monk is commanded to exercise himself in the seventh root of fear in no small measure. For no one tolerates calmly the injuries of his lord if he truly loves him. Therefore you, monk, who desire to be a faithful servant of Jesus Christ, do not neglect zeal for him in yourself; when you see an injury against God, do not pretend not to see it, because whoever does not at once resist those who detract from his lord is proven to love him less. Let people know you as such a lover of God and enemy of vices that they do not hear evil spoken in your presence, nor dare to do anything evil while you look on.
