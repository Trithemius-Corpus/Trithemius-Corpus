Certainly those who inhabit the empyrean heaven and those who dwell in the third region have one and the same object. But this is the difference: these contemplate God in a riddle, while those contemplate him in clear vision. In both places there is sweetness for those who contemplate: here great, there greatest; here in the desire of fervent love, there in the possession of most blessed vision.

In this supreme region of divine contemplation the rational spirit calls back its powers and faculties into its own inner chamber, where it too, free from every phantasm, is at rest; it raises itself above itself into the dense darkness of God, in which, lulled by ecstatic love, it rests sweetly. Here there is no troublesome confusion of thoughts, no imagining of phantasms; rather, the naked mind is lifted upward and contemplates without any thought or imagination. Indeed, when the mind itself again gives access to thoughts, it shows itself to be descending once more to the middle region, which is that of reason.

But since the human mind cannot always remain unmoved at the summit of divine contemplation, it must often return to the rational exercise of the second region. If, however, it has slipped down into sensual passions, then it descends again to the lowest region. Yet the spirit of the true lover of God, although because of the frailty of the flesh it cannot always remain in the delight of contemplation, nevertheless strives often, through enkindled affection, to raise itself to that region, so that it may carry out the exercise of reason in such a way that it does not entirely abandon the sweetness of inward delight. O how good it is to cleave to the Lord; how sweet it is to attend to God alone, so that the mind may thirst for the Lord Jesus above all things and may always long with burning desires to embrace him. Standing in this region, it is indeed very near the empyrean heaven, and therefore is inflamed toward it with the greatest desires of love.

O how many groans and tears the mind fervent with love is accustomed to pour forth; how ardently it sighs for heavenly things, since the nearer it is to the empyrean heaven through the secret of contemplation while standing in this supreme region, the more ardent its desire is known to be to leave the prison of the body. *That This Region of the Soul Is Divine in Its Instruction. Article 4.*

Third, this supreme region of the soul is said to be divine in instruction, because in it the mind, pierced by divine love, is taught not by a human being but by the Holy Spirit. For Christ said to his lovers in the holy Gospel: “When that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth.” And again there is that saying of the prophet: “And they shall all be teachable by God.” For the Holy Spirit dwells in the heart of one who loves Jesus, and illuminates it by his invisible teaching.

In this region the spirit reads true wisdom, Mary’s best part, in the tranquillity of silence; mystical theology is taught in the secrecy of the divine darkness with ineffable sweetness; the mind, disposed by the fervor of most holy love, speaks with its beloved in hiddenness and is continually lifted up to him by desire. This wisdom, or mystical theology, can indeed be commended by a human being, but it cannot be taught except by divine instruction. For the work of the Holy Spirit is being done when the mind is enlightened in truth through contemplation.

But we must consider that mystical theology is acquired through the exercise of a purified affection and is taught through the fire of love. Speculative theology, however, which we call scholastic, is learned through the exercise of the intellect, while the mind, burning continually with zeal for study, is devoted to it. For the acquisition of mystical theology, the purification of affection from every entanglement is required, so that the will, depraved through aversion, may be reformed through the conversion of inward love toward its source. Indeed, unless affection has been purified from every disordered desire, it does not ascend to the height of this most sacred wisdom.
