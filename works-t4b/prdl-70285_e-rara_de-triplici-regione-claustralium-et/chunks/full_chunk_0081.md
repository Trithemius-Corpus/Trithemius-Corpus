After Vespers Until Compline.
Consideration VIII.

After Vespers, or the vigils of the dead, return to your cell with the customary prayers and bows; and there, on bended knees, give thanks to your most merciful preserver, because he has not only graciously rescued you from the vanities of the world, but has also guarded you in the order from many evils and dangers. Then reflect how many bodily and spiritual goods your loving Father has bestowed on you, though you have deserved nothing; and, what is greatest, how he has promised himself as a reward to you if you faithfully persevere in his love. Be grateful, and always keep watch in prayer, so that you may burn sweetly in the love of God.

But when the signal has been given for supper, prepare yourself humbly to go to it, and observe what we noted above concerning dinner. After supper you may return to your cell and devote yourself to prayer or meditation until the signal is rung for the collation.

At supper, when you hear the signal for the spiritual collation, strive to go to it at once with gravity, and to hear the last reading of the day with fervor of spirit. For the word of holy exhortation that has last been heard will cling more tenaciously to the mind throughout the whole night. But if it is a fast day, the collation has customarily been observed in the refectory while drinking. Therefore be attentive to sacred reading in every place, because when the word of holy exhortation is gladly received with attention, it cannot happen that the mind is not often lifted up.

At Compline.

When you come to Compline, beseech the Lord that he may make you always despise all worldly and carnal things, and grant you continually to remain in his most holy love, so that when the course of the present life is completed, you may deserve to enter the temple of God not made by hands, and rejoice forever in the Lord with all the saints. But at the end of Compline, when you hear the signal of the angelic salutation being rung,

After Compline.

As soon as Compline has begun, return at once to your cell; and, after the customary prayers have been said, take care to prepare yourself most diligently for the following recollection of mind.

On Recollection after Compline.
Consideration IX.

Since we cannot remain wholly unmoved in one holy state of mind, it is necessary for us to renew our holy purpose frequently, so that as often as, during the day, by wandering along the byways of sins, we depart from the one beginning of our mind, the highest God, so often we may return to him through contrition and compunction, with amendment firmly resolved. Hence our holy fathers established for us that we should devote ourselves to recollection after Compline and examine ourselves concerning the negligences committed during the day, so that the purpose of holiness, which we know to be quite weak and infirm, we may renew to fervor of spirit through the continual exercise of recollection. Therefore our devout monk, wishing to recollect himself fruitfully and with useful continuance, ought chiefly to examine himself in order on five points.

The First Thing to Be Observed in Recollection.

The Rendering of Account.

The first thing to be observed is the rendering of account, by which the monk is commanded to bring back before the eyes of the mind, for correction, whatever he remembers that he has committed in the day by failing negligently, badly, or less well. And so, turned toward the Lord, he should lie before his bed with hands clasped, and say in this or a similar manner:

I give thanks to you, almighty and merciful God, for all your benefits, which you have deigned to bestow on me, an unworthy sinner with no merit at all, from my youth up to this hour.

For I, who have deserved no good, have received many and innumerable gifts of your goodness. Would that I could give worthy thanks to you, the highest and unchangeable good; would that I could praise you in your holy service with a pure heart and clean conscience. But, alas, with weakness, concupiscence, and evil habit prevailing, I, who ought to have clung to your most holy love with a pure heart and conscience, have offended you again and again by many negligences and faults; and as often as, according to the inward inspirations of your goodness, I have tried to rise to fervor of spirit, alas, so often I grieve that I have fallen again.

Behold, good Jesus, how many faults, how many negligences, how many offenses I have fallen into today alone; how many vain, how many useless, how many pernicious thoughts I have admitted in so few hours; how many useless and harmful words I have uttered; how many negligences and sins I have perpetrated in my actions this day.

Woe to me, wretched man, who could not spend even one very brief day in your most holy service without impurity of heart and negligence; I who have committed so many faults and various imperfections today. I confess to your goodness, O most gentle God, all my sins into which I have fallen from the beginning of my age. Before your feet, O sweetest Jesus, I place all the negligences that I have committed today. I ask for pardon, which I am not worthy to receive; I beg for grace, which I confess I have so often received in vain. O Jesus Christ, most loving mediator of God and men, do not cast away me, a wretched sinner, whom you willed to purchase with your own blood. I confess to you all the negligences into which I have in any way fallen today, and the faults that I have committed.

Here the monk, prostrate in mind before the Lord, should recall the more serious negligences to memory, and, humbly asking pardon, should lament his imperfection. Above all, let him consider whether he has offended anyone by word or deed; whether he has said anything against a brother; whether he has committed anything against charity and reverence; or whether he knowingly saddened someone wrongly.
