Second, the mortification of the will requires the emptying out of one’s own affections, because no one can stand rightly ordered amid adversities and comforts, and truly subject to God, if he does not firmly restrain his carnal affections. Third, for the mortification of one’s own will that poverty of spirit is required, lest the monk’s affection be held captive by customary things, food, clothing, and the rest; for whoever loves these too much shows himself, against the Rule, to be a lover of his own will.

Therefore the true cenobite, as a cultivator of this middle region, must cling fervently to the teaching of holy humility, freely despise all worldly things for the love of God, and love absolutely nothing except God. If he has anything for use with the Rule’s permission, let him beware of setting the affection of his heart upon it, and let him be ready, at the judgment of his shepherd and for the benefit of the brothers, to surrender everything with a peaceful heart. Let him always choose what is simple and more lowly; let him give thanks to God in all things; and, placing his hope and sufficiency in him alone, let him say cheerfully: In you, Lord Jesus, possessing all things, I freely despise all things for your love; I shall care absolutely nothing for transitory things.

Therefore let our devout cenobite always direct the faithful eye of his intention, with consideration, toward this end of monastic instruction. Let him never withdraw from looking upon it, because whoever forgets the final end in his action wanders like an unskilled and blind sailor amid the storms of the sea, soon to perish. Therefore let the faithful monk imitate a sailor skilled in devotion: just as he, always keeping the harbor before his eyes, strives with intent solicitude to direct the ship there, so the mind of a good man should always be directed toward God as toward the shore and the final end. Archers set up for themselves some fixed mark, and, lest their sight be scattered from the appointed target, they close one eye. But just as the arrow of the shooter is directed toward the mark, so the intention of the good monk is always ordered toward God; in this too the eye of lower affection is closed, lest the sharpness of higher consideration be scattered by things that are empty. close the affection of earthly curiosity and the understanding of carnal wisdom as though not knowing them, if we desire always to lift the mind’s gaze toward its proper end. And just as the eagle is said to look at the sun with eyes undazzled, so the monk, serving God, contemplates without interruption the end of monastic instruction. Just as a prudent traveler, proceeding with delight, considers the lodging to which he is heading, so the good cenobite, in all that he does, always pants with fervent desire chiefly toward his own end, which is God. But foolish is the traveler who, set on the road, forgets the lodging toward which he is heading, and, wandering after other things, knowingly becomes entangled in various obstacles. And the monk who does not diligently consider the end of his destination is certainly blind and goes astray on the road.

Therefore the good monk and lover of his own salvation does not neglect his good striving in spiritual exercises, but, always reaching in affection toward higher things, lifts himself up by exercising himself in holy works. Now good striving is to omit none of the things that God has commanded; to obey the shepherd humbly in all things for the love of God; always to raise affection, removed from earthly things, to the memory of heavenly things; and through continual exercise in spiritual conduct to renew the holy purpose daily. Whatever good the devout cenobite does, let him always offer it with faithful attention to almighty God; let him despise all earthly things with a strong spirit; let him desire to please God alone.

Let the intention of the cenobitic life always be faithful, so that it wishes and desires nothing other than the praise and honor of God. Let its attention be diligent, so that it knows how to summon all the senses of the body and the affections of the soul to the service of God, in order that it may serve its Lord faithfully and seek his glory, not its own.
