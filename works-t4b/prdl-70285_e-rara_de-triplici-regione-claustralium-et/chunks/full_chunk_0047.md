For by the power of that One, union comes about everywhere in the one; nor are things more alike more easily united to one another for any other reason than that they are one in quality. Therefore the soul is most rightly said to be united with that one principle of things only when, having become most perfectly one in itself, it is separated from every multiplicity and, above all imagination, reason, and understanding, its unity alone escapes, enjoying through loving affection the unity of God, joining its own center, so to speak, with the center of the universe.

But when the human soul itself, wrongly yielding to its quasi-marriage with the flesh, is turned toward any animal passions, then that very unity is badly changed into a multiplicity contrary to the One alone; and it becomes as far removed from its own principle as it has been divided from itself. Finally, the soul itself contracts great difficulty in returning to that one ineffable divine One whenever, confused in itself, it falls into the sin of apostasy. Consider now how great and ineffable he is against whose honor sin is committed; and since you are wholly unable to understand the greatness of his majesty, learn humbly to fear his power.

Sixth, consider what you have lost by sinning, so that, once the greatness of the loss is known, repentance may hasten and restore through tears what has been lost. What, then, wretched one, have you lost by sinning? Hear what you have lost. You had been made like God by grace, a companion of the angels, a member of Christ, and an heir chosen for eternal happiness; you ought to have lived and rejoiced without end in the goods of the Lord with all the saints. But what happened when you sinned?

You have been compared to senseless beasts, and have been made like them. For when you were in honor you refused to understand; you abandoned the fountain of life; by sinning you became a companion and member of the devil, a whore of Antichrist, an heir of hell. You abandoned life; you chose everlasting death. If you fear the sentence, change your will, and you will find forgiveness.

Seventh, consider what you have deserved by sinning; for if eternal death is inflicted for one sin, think what one who commits many, not to say innumerable sins, deserves.

For the soul that has sinned shall itself die. Moreover, even through one mortal sin you have deserved to be the devil’s companion without end, to be deprived of all the joys of heaven, and to be burned forever in the fires of hell. These are the rewards that sin has brought you; these are the gifts it promised.

Now, therefore, from these seven considerations make a plaster for your wounded soul; take up for yourself the goad of compunction, so that you may reach the root of fear. But hurry, and do not put it off from day to day, because he who receives the humble confession of the penitent has not promised tomorrow to the one who delays. For where haste finds pardon, delay often cuts off life. Hope for a longer life draws many to destruction, because while they trust that they can live longer in this world, they make the time of repentance pass without repentance. But you, man, if you accept my counsel, do not defer repentance, because you do not know what tomorrow’s day will bring you. For he deserves to lose the time of repentance who so often does not fear to put off the pursuit of repentance.

On Present Vanity and Eternity, the sixth root.

The sixth root of fear is raised up in the mind of one meditating if the vanity of visible things and the eternity of invisible things are considered with studious intention. For all things that we behold in this world are wholly subject to vanity and corruption, and will last for only the briefest time. All mortal things hang beneath uncertain chance, and by their own mutability promise flight. Whatever you have today will perhaps leave you tomorrow, or now, while you are speaking, will cease to be yours. For we perceive nothing in this world as long-lasting and stable, but see all things changed with time.
