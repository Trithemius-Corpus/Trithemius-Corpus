Let the upright love you as a servant of God; let the wicked fear your face as that of a judge. Let no one in your presence presume to do anything alien to good morals, since all know that you cannot tolerate injury to God. Thus Phinehas earned an everlasting priesthood, when by zeal for the law he powerfully appeased the Lord’s anger. If you see order perishing in the monastery, that is, if your brothers have begun in any way to corrupt regular observance, do not, if you are a servant of God, keep silent about their vices. Set yourself firmly and strongly on behalf of the house of the Lord; exercise zeal for God as much as you can; destroy the counsels of the malicious; resist the rashness of the proud. If you are a true lover of God, by your face alone you will scatter the gatherings of the wicked. For no one will lay bare his plan before you if he suspects your zeal to be opposed to it. But if the evil and the wicked do not fear your presence when they do wrong, then surely they recognize no zeal for God in you.

Therefore, to show yourself a true servant of Christ, you ought gladly, for love of God, to forgive the injuries inflicted on you by your brothers, and to pray to God for those very persons who injure you. But it is not yours to forgive injuries against your Lord without his will; rather, from zeal for righteousness, you must avenge them. In these matters let love of blood-kin cease; let the bond of friendship withdraw far away: zeal for God must rightly be preferred to all things. For friendship with a man is to be observed in such a way that it is never preferred to zeal for God.

Woe to monks and abbots who so boldly cast aside zeal for God, who put human familiarities before the divine command, who conceal the vices of their subjects through foolish compassion, who observe the traditions of the holy rule neither in themselves nor in others. And what shall I say? We see among cloistered monks obedience to commands despised, zeal for righteousness scorned, observance of the rule held in contempt. Two or more make a bond of carnal friendship among themselves under the name of charity; the sick entrust the diseases of their soul to those still sicker, and

they in no way seek God’s friendship through purity of mind. Each one thinks his companion better than the rest, and each, deceived, loves the other not spiritually but carnally, since they do not raise their affections to God and love nothing but the consolations of the flesh. But when you see a monk seeking the friendship of weak brothers, you ought to judge him not a servant of God but a lover of the flesh. For one who truly occupies his whole heart in the love of God cares nothing for human friendship. The love of Jesus is enough for him in all things; outside him he seeks nothing further. But one who has sought the consolation of his mind in external things shows by his conduct that he does not love God with his whole heart.

The love of God loves solitude, hates the tumult of men, and does not seek conversations with friends. O good Jesus, how sweet is your love: when it pierces the heart of one who loves you, the spirit longs to see you face to face and, having you, to embrace you. Indeed, one who loves you forgets himself and meditates on nothing but your most sweet presence. There is nothing in the world that pleases one who loves you; nothing is found anywhere that the soul desires except you. O with how many sighs the mind wounded by love is distressed; with how abundant tears the face is watered in secret, because as often as it thinks on the face of the beloved reigning in heaven, so often it weeps that it is desolate on earth.

Meanwhile the languishing mind often tries to say something of its most beloved Jesus, and it also strives to provoke others to love him; but as soon as it begins to utter the most sweet name of Jesus, unwillingly, because of the greatness of love, it falls silent. For the love of Jesus is expressed by no words, but is felt with sweetness in the secret of the mind. I know who said, “My love has been crucified.” Who is your love, O servant of God? Why do you weep? Why do you groan? Ignatius: “My love has been crucified, and shall I not mourn? My Lord has died, and shall I be able to restrain my tears?” Do not weep, O faithful soul; for although your love died to redeem you from death, yet he has now risen from the dead so that in heaven he may crown you with the crown of life. Be faithful unto death, and he will give you the crown of life.
