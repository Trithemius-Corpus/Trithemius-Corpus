The inhabitants of the lowest region, monks and carnal men, are disordered toward one another in many and various ways. For since, stirred by animal passions like beasts, they do not direct themselves according to the right judgment of reason, it is no wonder if they pervert the order of brotherly life. For a savage, troubled conscience is always presumptuous, and an evil mind never has the joys of peace, but is continually shaken by vice and sorrow; inwardly it is tormented by the everlasting fires of crimes. And so monks who are inhabitants of the dark region, blinded by evil and disordered affections, do not consider the end of the monastic profession. They love nothing except the advantage of the present life; they neglect their own salvation; they do not rise up to the love of eternity.

First, therefore, they are greatly disordered toward one another when they cast aside the intellectual regard that should be had for brothers; when they do not take note of the image of God in man; when they do not think of the communion of grace and glory infused into human beings and to be granted in the future; nor do they take care to show one another the love of brotherly fellowship. They live together like horse and mule, in agreement according to the bodily frame, because they love whatever is pleasing to sensuality, and do not admit whatever accords with reason. One is loved by another not because he is a human being, but because he is like his lover in the affection of sensuality. For the carnal man loves in a human being not virtues, not religion, not, finally, nature itself, but flesh, blood, and bodily quality. Since therefore the love of carnal men is carnal, it is easily changeable at any occasion. For the inhabitants of this lowest region revere those whose habits they find like their own, and monks always embrace monks equal to themselves in affection. The disorderly cenobite persecutes the one whom God has loved from eternity; the one whom God himself spurns because of his demerits, this man venerates as a saint.

Second, cloistered men are disordered toward one another by reason of dissimilarity, when either the elders, stirred by austerity and pride, despise their juniors, or the juniors stubbornly set themselves against their seniors. And this disorder is proved to be very contrary to the holy Rule, which commands the seniors to love the juniors with fatherly affection, and instructs the juniors to obey their seniors humbly for God's sake.

But since the elders, inhabitants of this lowest region, withhold fatherly affection from the juniors, the younger men blaze up into grave hatred; and when they consider that as young men they are unjustly despised by them, they accuse their depraved habits every day. Therefore weak young men learn the crooked habits of the elders; and, as the proverb says, just as they see the greater ones sing, so too the lesser ones dance.

Third, monks are badly ordered toward one another when they cast aside good zeal and mutual obedience, and take up the evil kind of zeal forbidden by the Rule. In words and deeds they commit various injuries against one another. One, in his effort to rival the other, strives to watch him for evil; one is provoked by another to inflict insult. They mutually reproach one another with their infirmities; they are always panting for vengeance. Zeal for regular discipline is despised; the passion of indignation and bitterness rules. They meet one another with faces turned away; by clear signs they make manifest how much hatred lies hidden in the breast. Not so the wicked, not so: for whoever persecutes his brother with hatred is proved a murderer and unworthy of the kingdom of God. O always wretched condition of carnal monks, which is so much the nearer to hell as it has been alienated from the fear of God. For those who through evil zeal delight in being discordant in the school of peace imitate not angels but demons. For truly, just as a brute animal does not abandon the savagery of its nature, so a beastly monk does not withdraw from the worst zeal.

How monks who are inhabitants of the lowest region are disordered toward themselves. Article V.

The inhabitants of the lowest region, carnal monks, are disordered toward nothing more than toward themselves. For they cast aside every consideration of themselves; they do not attend to the end for which they were created, because while each one pollutes himself with the filth of vices, the depraved mind withdraws from the original rectitude of its condition. Nor is it any wonder if outside himself the cultivator of the lowest region becomes disordered toward others, since through various passions [unclear].
