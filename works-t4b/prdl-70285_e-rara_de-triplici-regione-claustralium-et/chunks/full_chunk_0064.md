Third, for one who wishes devoutly to attend upon the Lord God in prayers, it is necessary to raise the mind with fervor of spirit to heavenly things. For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.” The most devout orator of God, Bernard, also says: “The little recruit of Christ must be taught in his prayer to lift his heart upward, to pray spiritually, to withdraw from bodily images, and to think nothing bodily.” But because it is not granted to everyone to be able to raise the mind to the invisible God above the understanding, provision must also be made for the weak by an easier mode of elevation. Hence he also says: “For a weak person meditating or praying, an image of the Lord’s Nativity, humanity, Passion, or Resurrection is more safely set before him, so that the weak soul, which knows nothing except the body, may be moved toward divine things according to the measure of its capacity. For truly, when through faith it does not separate God from man in the form of the mediator, it somehow reaches God in man. For our Savior, the mediator of God and men, Jesus Christ, is God and man.”

Therefore let the devout cenobite learn often to turn over the first root of divine knowledge, so that he may be able to raise himself higher through love. For the more perfectly God is known, the more ardently he is loved; and the more he is loved, the better he is praised. For praise is very close to love.

But so that you may always be able freely to raise your heart in prayer to the Lord God, learn to love nothing in this world in a singular way; for one who is attached to nothing at all wanders off less often. For nothing so unloads the heart and renders it free; nothing makes one converse so generously and without constraint before the Lord; and nothing so quickly and lightly gathers a person back to interior things after various outward tumults, as to be attached in all things and events to nothing by private affection, but to be resigned to God and to one’s superiors, and to conduct oneself humbly in all things. Besides this, whatever is enjoined upon one so disposed for the glory of God and the preservation of regular discipline, the cenobite accepts all of it sincerely with humble obedience, whether it is convenient or burdensome. And this firmness of soul, despising prosperous things as much as adverse ones, wonderfully raises the mind

*At the Second Signal.*

But when the second signal has sounded, the cenobite, now sufficiently recollected inwardly, should leave his cell with composure; he should always bless the Lord in his heart; with the prudent virgins he should hasten to meet the bridegroom; he should think that he hears the signal of the great king.

*Leaving the cell, let him say:*

Protect your people, O Lord, by the sign of the holy cross, from all the snares of all enemies, so that we may offer you pleasing service, and that our sacrifice may become acceptable to you. Direct us, O Lord, in the way of peace, to the everlasting glory of your name.

*Entering the church, let him say:*

Blessed are those who dwell in your house, O Lord; they will praise you forever and ever. Cleanse me, O Lord, from every defilement of mind and body, so that I may deserve to praise and bless you in your house forever.

*Passing before the crucifix, let him say:*

I beseech you, most loving Lord Jesus, by all the bitterness of your most holy Passion which you endured for me, and especially when your most noble soul departed from your body: have mercy on my soul at its departure. Amen.

*At the holy water:*

Remove from me, O Lord, all my iniquities, so that I may deserve to enter the holy of holies with a pure mind. Sprinkle me, O Lord, with hyssop, etc.

*Before an image of the Blessed Virgin Mary:*

O flowering rose, beautiful mother of the Lord. O gentle virgin. O most fruitful vine, brighter than the dawn, pray for us continually.

*Passing before the altar, let him say:*

Glory to you, O Lord, who were born of the Virgin, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, forever and ever.

*Or:*

You, the unbegotten Father; you, the only-begotten Son; you, the Holy Spirit, the holy and undivided Trinity, I praise, bless, and adore without end. O highest unity, beginning of all things [unclear]
