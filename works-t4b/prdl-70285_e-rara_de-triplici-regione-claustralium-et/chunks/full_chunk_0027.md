For so long does God the loving Father permit us, defeated by the adversary’s temptations, to fall at intervals of change into the pit of sin, until, conscious of our weakness, we learn to hope in nothing in our own strength, but only in the grace of the one God. But you, man, who often break your purpose by sinning, never be slow to rise again, since even if you cannot perfectly subject the enemy to yourself, nevertheless it will be good for you if the last day, even after a fall often repeated, can find you standing and fighting. But those to whom the mercy of God has granted the power and grace to defeat the adversary, this counsel does not make lax. Nevertheless, let him who stands see that he does not fall.

On the Attentive Reformation of the Works of the First Region.

Article 4.

Since, as we taught at the end of the first treatise, this middle region is next to the first from its lowest part, and in it, if the coenobite has been at all negligent and remiss, he immediately falls back again, without any interval, into the former region. And therefore whoever desires to persevere in this region while making progress should always, with attentive vigilance, strive to reform the works of the lowest region. And, lest we be too lengthy, whoever desires to exercise this reformation in himself should take up in this region works contrary to the first region in eight articles. For, as is prescribed by the teaching of physicians, contraries are cured by contraries.

Therefore the citizens of this middle region, faithful monks, are first commanded to consider this: that above all they should strive to order themselves toward God. This ordering is said to be done well when God is loved above all things, according to the law, with the whole heart.

Then the devoted coenobite should order himself toward his prelate.

Let him show humble reverence to him, strive to love him with sincere charity, and observe all the things he commands according to the Rule.

Let the ruler also of souls take care to proceed in an ordered way toward the coenobites, and to show himself a true shepherd to the sheep, not a hireling. Let him not neglect the salvation of the souls committed to him; let him not set temporal things before spiritual things. Let him hate vices, love the brothers, and show himself exemplary in all good things.

Then let the brothers cleave to one another as members of one head; let them love one another mutually in the true charity of God, and not depart from good zeal. For all of us who, through faith in Christ, trust to come to eternal life by applying ourselves to good works are members of Christ, the supreme head. But whoever persecutes his brother with evil zeal or hatred or other evil affections first separates himself from the head, and then does injury to Christ.

Next, let each one show himself ordered toward himself: let him humbly subject himself to all for the love of Christ; let him prefer others to himself through humility; and let him believe himself more unworthy than all the monks.

In the use of the monastery’s goods, let them always beware of exceeding the institution of the Rule, but strive to show themselves ordered in all things and faithful everywhere. Let them seek to have nothing against the will of the abbot; let them admit no pleasure of the spirit in the use of things.

Next, faithful coenobites should take care to order themselves, according to the institution of the Rule, to contempt of the world, so that they love nothing in this world, but truly despise all things for the love of God.

Let them not neglect to order themselves most diligently to penitence. Let them often have recourse to humble confession of their transgressions, since just as no one is believed to live in this world without sin, so penitence is judged necessary for all, even monks. Therefore the good coenobite, through the practice of compunction, should always learn to have recourse to penitence, to accuse himself humbly of his transgressions in chapter, willingly to perform the imposed penance, and faithfully to amend himself in all things.
