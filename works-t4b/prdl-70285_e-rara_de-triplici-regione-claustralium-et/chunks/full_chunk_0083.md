Whoever has uttered these three truths to the Lord from sincerity of heart, without falsity in mouth or mind, however much he may previously have sinned, is now secure, if he should die in such a state even without confession, in the absence of a priest; yet in such a way that he must make satisfaction in purgatory for what he did not complete by doing penance. But he who, because of a will to sin in which he actually takes delight, or because of a wicked intention by which he refuses to avoid the occasions of sins, cannot truly say these same truths from a pure mind, is in a state of damnation, from which not even the pope can absolve him.

The Indication of Future Caution.

The second thing he prescribes for the monk to observe in his recollection is called the indication of future caution. Let him consider what have been for him the occasions of past transgression, and he will more easily guard himself from future danger. For the pit of sin is never better blocked up than if the occasion of sin is avoided. And Saint Bernard says: “For the coming night, indeed, an indication must be made, distributing one’s exercises according to the rule of the common institution.” In these things he duly pays every debt, if the spirit is subjected to God and the flesh to the spirit, and if anything has been omitted, neglected, or imperfect, it does not remain unrecompensed in manner, time, and place.

The Request for Suffrages.

The third thing in the recollection of a good monk is the devout request for suffrages; for he, understanding his own weakness very well, seeks help from those stronger than himself. Therefore, as soon as he has completed his recollection, he turns to the holy and undivided Trinity to ask for mercy; then he invokes the most blessed Mother of God; afterward he implores the prayers and assistance of all the saints. And here he remembers not only himself, but pours out prayers for the whole order and the Church.

The Blessing for Going to Rest.

The fourth thing in the recollection is the monk’s blessing for going to rest, by which he fortifies himself with prayers and commits himself to divine goodness to be guarded through the night. Therefore,

at the sound of the signal for sleeping, or at the appointed time, going to the place of rest, let him fortify himself with the sign of the holy cross, and say with devotion:

The Blessing for Rest.

May the merits of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the integrity of blessed Mary ever virgin, the sign of the holy cross, the power of the Lord’s passion, and the blessing, protection, and merit of all the saints, angels, and elect of God, stand between me and all my enemies, visible and invisible, here and forever. Amen.

Prayer.

I commend myself this night and at all times into your wounds, most loving Lord Jesus, and I beg you, by the merit of your most holy passion: have mercy on me and guard me from all snares and illusions of the enemy, so that with a pure mind and a clean body I may be able to rise at the appointed hours and praise you with fervor of spirit.

Discretion for Obtaining Rest.

The fifth and last thing to be commemorated in recollection is discretion for obtaining rest: so that, just as the monk must never wholly omit this evening recollection, so also he should not wish, too indiscreetly, to prolong it to the destruction of his head. Therefore, when all things have been completed with discretion, let him raise the desire of his fervor toward God with tears, if he can, but let him not confound his mind. As he goes up to his bed, let him carry with him into memory something about the Lord’s passion, or about the love of Jesus, or certainly about the glory of the heavenly kingdom, in which, while sleeping, he may rest sweetly. For Bernard says: “When you are about to go to sleep, carry something with you in memory or thought, in which you may fall asleep peacefully; something that may also sometimes help you to dream, and that may receive you when you wake, and restore you to the state of yesterday’s intention. Thus the night will be illuminated for you like the day, and your night will be your illumination in its delights, so that you may fall asleep peacefully, rest in peace, and wake easily; and rising, you will be ready for redemption, while you return to that from which you had not wholly departed.”
