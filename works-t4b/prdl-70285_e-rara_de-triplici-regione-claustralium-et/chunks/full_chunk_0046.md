For just as all good things are from the first Good, so every love, toward whatever good it is borne, is naturally kindled by that same Good. For since our love cannot rest in any goods as in its end, it has as its end, where it will come to rest, the same good that it had as its beginning. Therefore, whenever sin is committed against the beauty of God’s justice, the soul is separated from its own beginning. For since God is charity, he demands love in the soul; peace requires peace; he loves what is like himself and hates what is unlike.

Second, consider the enormity of sin, and carefully weigh how greatly its foulness displeases God. Behold, because of pride the just God cast Lucifer, the noblest angel, out of heaven, and condemned him with his accomplices to the burning of eternal fire. He also compelled our first parents, Adam and Eve, to go out of paradise because of disobedience, and by a just judgment condemned the whole human race, in their crime, to the sentence of a double death. Finally, let us turn over the whole history of the Old and New Testament, and we shall find that what was known to have been rashly committed against God’s justice was often gravely punished by it. Therefore the gravity of the crime is easily recognized if one considers the severity of the vengeance that followed.

Third, consider what grave scandal arises from sin, especially when one sins in the presence of others. For someone who by his evil example provokes another to sin shows himself to be an adversary of God. For God, by his will and out of mercy, has determined to save souls redeemed by the blood of his only-begotten Son; this person, through evil example and out of aversion from God, desires to destroy them. The sin of scandal is said to be gravest with respect to guilt for this reason: because, as we have said, it opposes God. But if the person who gives occasion for harm must be considered to have caused the harm, the sinner subjects himself to as many sentences of death as he has caused people by his evil example to fall into mortal crime. For Christ says in the holy Gospel: Woe to the person through whom scandal comes. And again he says: Whoever scandalizes one of these little ones who believe in me,

it is expedient for him that a millstone turned by an ass be hung around his neck, and that he be drowned in the depth of the sea. True repentance is rare in one who scandalizes the little ones, because when he neglects to restore another’s loss, he in no way attains the fruit of saving repentance.

Fourth, consider the negligence of your ungrateful heart, because, although you were created in the image of God and ought to have loved your maker above all things, you dishonor him in many ways by your sins. You ought to devote continual service of love to Christ, the Son of God, by whose blood you have been redeemed; but you do not fear to love the flesh more than Jesus your Savior. Finally, with a compassionate mind you ought daily to turn over in your mind his most holy passion, and you are accustomed to think of nothing less than that. Adopted among the sons of God by mercy alone, you ought to have shown yourself worthy of the heavenly calling as an imitator, and yet through sloth you do not fear to lie still in the lowest things. And why do I say more? Behold, you who have been marked with the Holy Spirit and counted with the angels are called to the inheritance of the sons of God; you are invited by all the saints to the feasts of paradise; you daily partake of the body and blood of our Savior Jesus Christ; and yet you do not observe the commandments of God and of the rule which you promised to keep so that you might be blessed. Examine your conscience diligently, therefore, and you will find that there are many good things you ought to do and omit, and very many evil things you ought to omit yet commit against God’s good pleasure.

Fifth, consider whom you have offended by sinning: not a human being, but God; not a creature, but the Creator. He against whom you have sinned is great and immeasurable, terrible and glorious; his majesty makes the angels tremble, and all the powers of heaven marvel at his power.
