How the citizens of this lowest region are disordered with respect to God.

Article 1.

Therefore carnal monks, the mad cultivators of this lowest region, are in many ways disordered with respect to God. They never taste the sweetness of his love; they admit no compunction from divine fear. In their affections they are bestial and proud; in their carnal pleasures they are always unclean.

And first, their disorder toward God takes its origin from ignorance: while, given over to animal passions, they do not care to hold God in their knowledge, nor do they allow the mind to think on those things that lead to fear of him; but, blinded by evil desires, they vanish away in their own thoughts. But what does Scripture say? “He who is ignorant shall be ignored.” They are unwilling to understand so that they may act well; they do not tremble before the face of the strict Judge; without penitence they continually persevere in evils. Indeed, after the manner of beasts, they do not fear to rot in their pleasures; they do not heed the patience of God calling them to penitence; they seek nothing except the conveniences of their own flesh. At all times they walk in the greatest sins without any fear; they stubbornly refuse to know the ways of the Lord. Concerning the future judgment they think either little or nothing; they do not fear the punishments prepared in hell for sinners; the grace of compunction never pierces their hearts, whose minds the love of God does not inflame. They have no care for God; no dread of death. They obey the belly rather than God; at all times they desire to live according to the flesh; they shamefully neglect their own salvation in the Lord; they descend to hell with the demons. For indeed, those who, forgetful of God, badly keep the vow of holy monastic conduct do not attain the rewards of heavenly life to be given to the just.

Second, carnal monks are disordered with respect to God through foolish profession, because they promise to live according to the Rule, yet do not truly care to fulfill what they promise. And what is more disordered than to promise holiness of life to the Lord God and to remain in the foulness of one’s former life? He is a mocker of almighty God, not a monk, whoever lives in a monastery otherwise than the monastic Rule teaches.

And we discern that many among cloistered men today live in a worldly manner; we see that many neglect the purpose of their profession; many violate the observance of regular discipline in various ways; many knowingly ignore the goodness of God; many refuse to do penitence.

Third, many cloistered men of this lowest region are disordered with respect to God through neglect of the service they owe: they serve their own affections and carnal desires in various ways, and always approach the service of God with a distracted mind. Some of them hurry lukewarmly to choir without any devotion; others come unwillingly, compelled by necessity; certain men, scattered among outward thoughts, are present at the divine offices in body, but in mind wander outside through various workshops. Others do not fear to seek false excuses by which they may be able, as if lawfully, to absent themselves from choir. Now they pretend to have a severe headache; now they mendaciously allege this, now that; and while they deceitfully withdraw from the service owed to almighty God, they incur the reproach of eternal malediction owed to the negligent.

Fourth, there are very many disordered with respect to God through the operation of habit: even if they sometimes escape the malediction of the negligent, still they never draw near to the fruit of eternal devotion. For they approach the service of God from habit alone, and are not moved to any good work by devotion. Others, placed under grave necessity, obey the commands of their superiors quite unwillingly, and they fear temporal punishments rather than the judgment of God. These carry the cross of the Lord with Simon under compulsion; like Ephraim, a heifer trained for the threshing floor, they apply themselves to good works by habit alone. But they do not do the good well who approach the service of God with an intention that is not right. They also perform the deed of the just without the reward of the just, because in good and just works they do not assume a good will.

Fifth, the citizens of the lowest region are disordered with respect to God through the vice of ingratitude, which is a very great crime of disordered will. And often, indeed, he who is found ungrateful to the Lord for the benefits he has received is exposed, by his own desert, to be scourged by various and most grievous temptations.
