What more could he have given us, he who, when he had created us although we did not exist, gave himself? But what can we return to our Creator, since by our own merit we are sinners and weak? Let us return what we are; let us return what we owe: namely, faithful service for the body that we have received from the Lord, and sincere love for the soul by which we were created in the image of God. For thus the prophet admonishes us in the psalm: “Serve the Lord in fear, and rejoice in him with trembling.” Serve faithfully; love reverently. On the part of the body, render faithful service to your Creator; on the part of the soul, love him with all your strength. Serve him because the Lord is powerful and great; love him because he is good. His power is shown in your creation; his goodness is made clear in your restoration. Since, when you did not exist, he created you by his power, he deserves faithful service; since by his goodness he restored you when you had fallen, he deserves the affection of sincere love. For truly he is our Father through love; he is Lord through incomprehensible power.

Second, he gave us faith with the sacraments, for just as he made us without our will, so he called us to faith without our merit. For Christ says to his disciples in the holy Gospel: “You did not choose me, but I chose you.” Through faith the Christian soul is led back to its beginning; we are bound to devote the sacraments that he gave with perpetual adherence, and to remain continually in his holy covenant. For in baptism we renounced the devil and all his pomps; we promised that we would cling to Christ forever, as those who raised us from the sacred font, our guarantors, stood before the Lord for us in the faith of our parents. Therefore let us always say from the heart with the prophet: “It is good for me to cling to God, to place my hope in the Lord God.”

Third, almighty God gave us the craft of righteousness and the teachings of truth. For these we owe our Creator faithful performance and perpetual service without deceit.

Therefore we are commanded first of all to do all things to the praise of God, and in everything we do to raise our intention chiefly to his honor. For only a good intention toward God distinguishes works; only love makes them fruitful. Behold, the Lord bestowed on us the craft of nature for praising him; moreover he gave the teachings of salvation through the law; he requires from us praise and love above all things. Therefore let us exercise, to the honor of God, the craft bestowed on us, and the pure eye of intention will come about through the teaching of the law.

Fourth, the merciful Father bestowed on us the world with its necessary elements; for these he requires from us sincere contemplation of himself as due. Whatever we discern in this world ought to lead us to the memory of God and continually provoke us to love its Creator above all things. Hence Bernard, well practiced in sacred matters, says: the whole and true lover of God, wherever he turns, has the familiar admonition of his Creator; he uses things as a mirror, and thus, by reflection, is lifted up from creatures to the Creator. For concerning each thing he says: if those things that were created with time, are changed, and pass away seem good, beautiful, and sweet, what goodness, beauty, and sweetness does God their author possess, remaining forever, who has reserved for himself things incomparably better than these? By this consideration the devout man regards all human beings with the simple eye of the mind, as the likeness of the image of God, and does not curiously attend to the pleasing or displeasing form of the body in them. For in a human being he considers not what he sees outwardly, but the image of the Creator, which he knows to be a sharer in eternal happiness.

Fifth, the Son of God offered himself in torments for us, and redeemed us, lost as we were, from the destruction of perpetual death by his blood. We are commanded to devote benevolent imitation to this love of our Savior, if we desire to come to his kingdom. For the prince of the apostles says to the faithful: “Christ suffered for us, leaving you an example, that you should follow his footsteps.” What shall I repay you, O good Jesus, for your benefits toward me, so great and so immense?
