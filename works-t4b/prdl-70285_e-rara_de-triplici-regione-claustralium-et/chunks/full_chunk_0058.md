Into this dwelling he mercifully receives us when we return with the prodigal son; in love he rushes into our embrace; he clothes us with the first robe; he grants the garment of charity to those who love him. Why, then, do we neglect to return as quickly as possible to the most indulgent Father, whom we see ready to receive us into his embrace even after our contempt? We wretched ones have withdrawn through the rashness of our own will; we have squandered our portion of the inheritance with the harlots of carnal affections; we have been shamefully stripped of every beauty and fatherly adornment. Why do we not return to the Father, whom we know to be ineffably merciful? At least after our fault, I beg, let us return, who scorned to avoid ruin. Return, return, wretched Shulamite; return, grievously captive in your affections; return, mortified in sorrows. Why do you delay? Why do you tarry? Return, faithful soul, to Jesus, the guide of your youth; return to your former husband; go back to your most loving Father, who awaits you with longing love. For behold, he awaits you with patience, and through the prophet Isaiah he cries to you: “Return to me, for I have redeemed you. I have blotted out your iniquities like a cloud, and your sins like a mist.” What is this, O soul, that you are in the land of enemies? You have grown old in a foreign land; you are defiled with the dead; you are counted with those who descend into hell. Remember that almighty God made you from nothing; consider that he formed you in his image and likeness; remember whence you have fallen; see where you have gone, to be trampled underfoot among the pigs of unclean affections. Your Creator placed you from the beginning in this highest region of the spirit; he entrusted to you the natural exercise of his love; he also bestowed on you the hope of life that will endure forever. But you have abandoned the fountain of wisdom; you have gone out into the lowest region of unlikeness; you have squandered the promised goods of the inheritance. Yet return, even late at last, to the exercise of repentance in the middle region, until, cleansed from all your iniquities, you may deserve to return to the delight of the highest region.

There the most indulgent Father will receive you as you return joyfully; he will clothe you with the recovered first robe of innocence; he will give you on your hands the ring of most holy covenant, and on your feet the shoes of true mortification of evil affections, so that, adorned with these ornaments like a faithful bride, you may cling to him continually and never thereafter withdraw from his love. Finally he will kill the fatted calf for your food; he himself will be the steward and at the same time the banquet; by the greatness of his surpassing love he will give his own flesh for food and his own blood for drink. There the holy Spirit of the Lord will be present and will fill you with his mystical gifts. Then, rejoicing in the Lord, you will sing: “The fruit of Jesus is sweet to my throat, for his spirit is sweeter than honey.”

Thus this highest region of the spirit is great in reduction, because the citizens whom it receives into its own dwelling it leads back to the delight of the heavenly homeland. We recognize the citizens of this most holy region infallibly from the fruits of the spirit, concerning which we know the holy Apostle to have said: “The fruit of the spirit is charity, joy, peace, patience, goodness, long-suffering, gentleness, faith, modesty, continence, chastity.” These fruits the citizens of this third region bring forth, because, filled with God and united to him, they always walk joyful in the Lord and most fervent in charity. For whatever happens to a just man dwelling on this mountain of love will not sadden him. For the soul that is always peaceful and tranquil in the love of God is lifted up by no prosperity and disturbed by no adversity.

That this highest region of the spirit is joyful and most true in treasuring up. Article VI.

For this reason this most beautiful region of the spirit is called joyful and most true in treasuring up: because whatever is acquired through the exercise of good work is possessed in simplicity of spirit. The treasure of the soul is the love of God.
