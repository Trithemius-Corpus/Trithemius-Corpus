At the elevation of the most sacred body of Christ, following the example of Saint Thomas Aquinas, he may say with attention directed to Christ: “You are the King of glory, O Christ; you are the everlasting Son of the Father,” continuing to the end of that hymn.

After the elevation the devout cenobite should turn himself to the Lord Jesus, desiring from the depths of his heart to have fellowship in so great a mystery; and, as though he were about to communicate, he should prepare his disposition. For as often as the devout lover of God desires to receive the body of Christ from the fervor of his heart, he is believed to have received the power of the sacrament. For the Lord Jesus said to a certain soul dear to him: If a man, fervent in love for me, desired to receive my body a thousand times in a day, if it were possible, then just as often, before me, through the power of my body itself, he would truly obtain his desire in his soul. Therefore, with this desire conceived in the mind of one who loves Jesus, let the cenobite pray to the Lord in this or a similar manner.

Prayer of the Minister

Would that, most loving Lord Jesus, I were so well and fervently disposed that it were permitted me now to receive your most holy body in this venerable sacrament. Would that it were permitted me to repeat this sacred communion several times in a day. O my sweetness, O sole delight of my heart, O my hope, Lord Jesus, you know the ardor of my mind toward you, and before you is all my desire. I ask you, by all your goodness, grant me the power and grace of this most holy sacrament now and at every time to receive in my soul, so that I may have strength to remain continually in you.

The Careful Preparation of the Priest Who Is About to Celebrate

The priest who is about to approach to offer this most sacred sacrament of the Lord’s body and blood should first examine himself carefully, and very cautiously consider of which region he is a citizen.

If he still finds himself a dweller in the region of the end, he ought not approach the altar unless he first strives to cleanse himself through contrition and confession, and through a firm purpose of amending himself. But if he finds himself a citizen of the second region, if he discovers that his faculties have been reformed and ordered toward the cenobitic end, he can easily dispose himself for celebration, since by daily emptying himself of his own affection he has learned to lift himself upward more frequently through desire. For divine goodness quickly enters the affection of a person who is stained by no desire of his own.

Therefore, wishing to perform this most holy priestly office with devotion and the fervor of divine love, he should first say some ardent prayers by which he may be able to stir himself to fuller compunction. For devotion is rarely granted at the altar if the mind is not prepared beforehand for fervor. For this reason our fathers, desiring each of us to advance in his own free devotion, did not wish to occupy us with many psalms before approaching the altar, as some do; not because anyone ought therefore to approach without recollection, but so that each might more freely devote himself in secret to exercises or prayers more suited to his own devotion.

Accordingly, wishing to recommend elevation of mind in the Lord Jesus to those celebrating before they approach the altar, we have arranged the following prayer from that which is attributed to Saint Ambrose of Milan, which we believe to be sufficiently effective by the merit of the Lord’s passion. Thus the devout cenobite who is about to approach the altar, if he has time and inclination, may say this or a similar prayer beforehand, or prepare himself in whatever ways he wishes.
