For thus the Son prays to the Father in the holy Gospel: Father, I will that where I am, there also my minister may be.

For great is the condescension of divine goodness in this venerable sacrament, since, as the same Hugh says, God seems to do nothing less in it, when each day he deigns to descend from heaven upon the altar for our salvation, than when, descending from heaven, he assumed human nature and became man. And just as our Lord Jesus Christ bestowed this venerable sacrament upon us in memory of his most bitter passion, as a sign of his greatest love, so he loves more fully and singularly those who dispose themselves with humble devotion and love either to receive it or to minister to those receiving it; and through it he comes to the aid, in every necessity, of those who ask in faith.

For Jesus, now presiding in heaven, will not be able to be harsh toward us when we pray with full faith, since he was so merciful and kind to those asking mercy while he walked on earth. Who ever came to his feet and was rejected? What sick person asked to be healed whom he, walking among men, did not cure? Will the Lord Jesus, then, be able now to deny mercy to us when we ask in faith, he who, while still mortal, was so merciful to those who prayed to him? For he says: All things are possible to one who believes.

Therefore, one who is about to serve at Mass should before all things strive to purge himself of every evil temptation and disordered affection, and, as much as he can, draw himself back to himself through the desire of Jesus’ love, so that, purified, he may be able to assist the Lord and pray effectively both for himself and for the whole Church, for the living and the dead. Therefore, standing behind the priest during Mass, at a suitable time, let him pray in these or similar words.

Prayer of the One Serving

Most sweet and most loving Lord Jesus, I beseech you by every anguish of your most holy heart which you endured in your passion,

I beseech you by the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death. I beseech you by the most sacred mystery of your body and blood, at the immolation of which I, an unworthy minister, am present before you: have mercy on me and purify my heart from every illicit affection, so that I may be able to serve you worthily. Grant me true contrition and pardon for all my sins, the grace of holy compunction, constant remembrance of your passion, pure devotion of heart, a fountain of tears, purity of serene conscience, and the perpetual fervor of your most holy love.

Grant me, I beseech you, most merciful lover of humankind, through the pain of your wounds, a firm purpose in holy fervor, true contempt for all worldly things, continual desire for the heavenly homeland, and sure progress in holy religion. Grant me, most sweet Jesus, constancy in adversity, patience in tribulation, discipline in conduct, and always and everywhere true humility in mind. Make me, I pray, a participant in this most holy sacrifice of your body and blood, so that my mind may feel the sweetness of your presence, may burn sweetly in your love, and may never depart from you for eternity. Amen.

Let him also pray for his own pastor; let him pray for the whole congregation, that the Lord may mercifully direct the former according to his will, and, preserving the latter under regular observance, defend it from every adversity.

Further, let him pray for the universal Church; let him pray for the faithful and the unbelieving, so that almighty God may keep believers in the faith and convert unbelievers to the Catholic faith. Let him also pray for the useful conversion of persons to the order, through whom both his monastery may advance in both states and the whole observance of the order may grow for the better. Let him not forget the souls of the faithful in the pains of purgatory, awaiting the mercy of the Creator; but, praying for them, let him know that after a little while he himself will pass to them, where the prayer now poured out will return into the bosom of the one who prays.
