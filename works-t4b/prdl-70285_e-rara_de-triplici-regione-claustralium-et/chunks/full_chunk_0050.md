*On the manner and degrees of divine love, comprising the conclusion of this second region.*

Since no one suddenly becomes supreme, nor can anyone by his own powers suddenly ascend to that highest summit of divine love, I now think it useful to note briefly certain manners or degrees themselves of loving God.

Therefore, to move the human heart into divine love, the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is, in my judgment, more effective than all other things, if it is diligently contemplated in a person’s mind purely, sweetly, fervently, and perseveringly. Truly, whoever often recalls to memory the life, passion, and death of our Savior, especially according to the principal articles, suffering with him and giving thanks, prepares a swift ascent of the heart to divine love. But this most holy occupation of the mind concerning the Lord’s passion we shall treat opportunely in the second principal part of this work, where we shall explain more fully the manner of spiritual exercise. As for the rationale of what has been said here, I find three manners of loving God, which I shall briefly explain in order.

The first manner commands that you love him who loves you sweetly, and join your whole self to him gently, so that your heart loves nothing apart from God and seeks nothing from elsewhere. For since God is the whole, he alone desires to possess the whole heart of the one who loves, and he removes it from dissimilar thoughts. Therefore, if you wish to love sweetly, love wholly. For the love of God accomplishes either everything or nothing. Let meditation on the passion of Jesus Christ our Savior possess your whole self, until love sweetly grows strong in you. You must despise everything that flatters in the flesh, if you desire to come to the love of God. For no one can love God sweetly in this life so long as he is convicted of loving anything in the world. Indeed, the love of the world is utterly contrary to divine love, because where the one is admitted into the inner chambers of the heart, the other is forbidden to be present. But how much sweetness, how much gentleness, and how much joy of mind the love of God generates in

the heart of the one who loves, no one at all understands except the one who, having despised all the things and events of the world, has loved God alone for a long time. The heart of one who loves God loves none of the things that are in the world; it despises the riches, honors, and favors of the age; it fears no adversities to be undergone for love of its Lord. The love of God is never idle; it always desires to come to its own beginning; it refuses to endure no labors in this world. Therefore let the one who does not wish to become sluggish love.

The neighbor also must be loved sweetly on account of God, and for no reason should he be offended against God or justice. Let each person always show himself to his brother in words and deeds such as he expects others to be toward himself. For he truly loves his neighbor who exposes his property, body, and soul for him. Christ taught this; the Son of God did this for us. Truly, whoever says that he truly believes in Christ ought himself also to walk just as he walked. He does not truly love Christ who does not keep his commandments. For he says: This is my commandment, that you love one another, as I have loved you. We are members of one head, Jesus Christ, in whom we then believe when we keep his commandment concerning love toward God and toward the neighbor. Therefore let us love our neighbor, because he is a member in Christ together with us, so that, just as one member in the body suffers with another, so a brother may humbly be helped by a brother for the love of Christ.

The second manner commands you to love wisely, and to seek nothing else in divine love than God. Extend your affection toward that Word which was in the beginning with God; raise your mind above everything that has been created, because unless you forsake yourself and all things that are in the world, you will not be able to come to him whom you love. Finally, if you desire to love God wisely, do not consider yourself to be anything, O man, because where a man considers himself to be anything, he already loves unwisely.

The third manner commands that you love bravely, and never fear to expose body and soul for the love of God.
