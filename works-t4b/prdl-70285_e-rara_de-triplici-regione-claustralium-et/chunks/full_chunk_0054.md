For just as the lowest region of the flesh sends its citizens, when they die, to hell, and the middle region of reason sends its citizens to purgatory, so this region sends its perfect worshippers, when they depart, on to heaven. The dignity of this third and highest region is expressed by many prerogatives, and is especially commended in seven articles, as will be said in order.

That this highest region of the spirit of cloistered persons is pleasing by comparison.

Article 1.

First, then, this third region of the soul is called pleasing by comparison, because it is compared to the highest empyrean heaven. For just as in the empyrean heaven all things are tranquil, so on this summit of the highest region the mind of the person contemplating God is shaken by no disturbances. Here is the highest rest of the contemplating soul; here no vicious crowd dwells; no bitterness of mind draws near at all. In the empyrean heaven, indeed, the majesty of God is seen by the holy spirits through an ineffable gift; here, in darkness, through the longing of love, it is seen above understanding. There God is all in all; here nothing among all things is pleasing except God. There it is granted to the saints to rest from their labors without end; here the mind is permitted, in rest, to taste beforehand the unspeakable sweetness of inward delight. There is no blessedness greater than that in heaven, and there is no tranquillity in the world so sweet as that in this region.

Blessed indeed are those who have deserved to ascend this mountain of divine contemplation, who love absolutely nothing in this world and long for nothing other than always to cling to God. O how secure is the conscience of such persons, how joyful their way of life, where no evil is committed and nothing is desired except that highest, unchangeable good. For the mind purified through divine love, which has truly and perseveringly ascended this region, desires nothing fleshly, nothing disordered, but raises its whole self in contemplation of the highest majesty through longing, and rests sweetly in God alone. O who could truly express the inward sweetness

of the soul that loves God, where the mind recalls the powers and capacities of the soul from the dispersion of multiplicity into one, and raises them upward with fiery affections into the chamber of the spirit? Here it directs its gaze and love toward that luminous, unchangeable good, rejects entirely every created and creatable multiplicity, and gathers the soul into one, so that, being one in itself, it may rise without the hindrance of multiplicity to the principle of light, which is one. For unity alone can reach the one principle of things, because unity flees multiplicity, and unity seeks the one. For the more you cling to multiplicity, the more you withdraw from unity.

Therefore, for those dwelling in this highest region of the spirit, God is accustomed, so that he may raise them in contemplation to the principle of his love, to cast away from them every multiplicity clinging in imagination or understanding, so that, gathered in their own unity, they may be able to strive ardently toward him alone with the simple affection of love, whom they know to be above every position.

That this highest region of the spirit is sweet and lovable by its nearness.

Article 2.

Second, this highest region of the spirit is sweet and most pleasant by a singular likeness of the spirit. For the citizen of this most holy region, as I might say, is separated from the citizens above by only one wall of light, because, while the spirit still dwells in the flesh, it cries out toward heaven as though through a wall. But that wall is the barrier of the flesh, from which the spirit, once freed, is about to depart and migrate to heaven when the spirit is separated from the body. But the spirit wounded by sins, which cannot raise itself to this region with the pure affection of love, is still said to stand farther away from the empyrean heaven. Those, however, who dwell in this most quiet region are very near the empyrean heaven, because, weighed down by no stains of sins, they despise all earthly and fleshly things, and always long to have their way of life in heaven in mind and spirit.
