If he finds that fervor is still present, let him strive to kindle it further with holy desires, and to summon himself to higher things. But if he senses that his affection has grown lukewarm or has been distracted by thoughts, then at once, reproving himself, let him say these or similar words: Woe to me, miserable one, who praise my God so negligently. Behold, my Lord, who suffered most bitterly for me through the whole night, had no rest, and I could not watch one hour with him? Help me, O Lord my God, that I may be able to sing psalms to you wisely.

Finally, under each psalm let him suitably stir himself by some ardent words toward the compunction of fervor, so that through inward exhortation he may inflame his affection more fully, saying: Would that, O Lord God, I could love you with my whole heart above all things, and cling continually to you alone. O good God, kindle my heart in your most holy love. My love has been crucified; and what am I doing upon the earth? O Jesus, my sweet love, possess my heart, for you are the treasure of my heart, my king and my God.

After Matins.

When the office of Matins has been completed, and confessions have been made or heard, let him not neglect immediately to read the hour of the Holy Cross. When it has been completed, let him add this little prayer.

Prayer.

I beseech you, most sweet Lord Jesus, by the pain of your wounds and by the outpouring of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death. Amen.

Then let him say:

To the holy and undivided Trinity, to the crucified humanity of Jesus Christ our Lord, to the most blessed Mary ever virgin, and to Saint Anne, her venerable mother, with all the saints, be everlasting praise and glory for infinite ages. Amen.

Since, when Matins has been finished, our custom is that they prepare themselves by confession for what is to be celebrated on that day, therefore the devout cenobite, whenever his confession has been completed, should at once devoutly carry out the penance imposed on him; and, insofar as this can be done, he should not approach the altar unless sacramental confession has always first been made.

On leaving the church.

After each hour, when the divine office has been completed, or indeed at any time whatever, on leaving the church he may repeat the order and manner of praying described above for entering the church, and observe the same rite in each of its places. But if for greater devotion he wishes to vary this manner, he will pray the following little prayers, or similar ones.

Before the high altar, bowing, let him say:

Blessed be the holy Trinity and the undivided Unity. We will confess to him, because he has shown his mercy to us. O supreme majesty, beginning of all things, have mercy on me. Amen.

Then after Matins and after each of the hours of the day, when he has returned to his cell, the devout lover of Christ should fall to his knees before his bed, humbly give thanks to almighty God because he has deigned to admit him, an insufficient minister, to his service, and there to number him among his sons. Therefore let him say this or a similar prayer.

Prayer.

I give thanks to you, almighty and eternal God, because you have deigned to call me, an unworthy sinner, to your service, not by any merits of mine but by the sole condescension of your mercy. I beseech you, by the pain of the wounds of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, and by the outpouring of his most holy blood, and by the power of his most innocent death: receive this sacrifice of my service, for the glory of your majesty, for my sins, for your holy Church, and for the souls of all the faithful departed who are placed in the punishments of purgatory, in that union of charity in which you received the same Lord Jesus Christ as he died for us on the cross;

and grant me, through the merit of that same passion, to love you in truth and to remain continually faithful in your service. Amen.

Morning recollection.

Then enter for a little while into the examination of your conscience, and see whether you still possess in yourself the conditions of a good cloistered person described in the seventh article of the second region. If you know that anything is lacking, do not neglect to reform it as soon as possible, lest little by little you fade away through sloth. For it is written: He who neglects small things falls little by little. And Augustine, the holy lover of God, says: No sin is so small that it does not grow by neglect. Therefore, when the conscience has been examined and reformed in holy fervor, say:

Prayer for perseverance.

Grant, we ask, almighty God, through the merit of the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, and through the intercession of blessed Mary ever virgin, and of all the holy heavenly powers, and of the blessed patriarchs, prophets, apostles, martyrs, confessors, virgins, and all your elect, perseverance for us in your will, so that we may continually be able to render you pleasing service. Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

Going to bed.

As he climbs into bed, let him fortify himself with the sign of the cross, and with attention of spirit say: In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, I intend to lay myself down, so that, strengthened by his divine majesty, I may be able to praise him more fully throughout the day.

Sitting in bed.

May the peace of our Lord Jesus Christ, the integrity of blessed Mary ever virgin, the sign of the holy cross, the memory of the Lord’s passion, and the intercession of all the saints guard me now and forever. In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. Signing himself, let him lie down.

The exercise of the cenobite at Prime.

Consideration 2.

When our devout cenobite has been awakened from sleep for Prime, let him keep the order and manner set out above before Matins for recollecting himself and praying.
