At the Sanctus.

With your mind raised upward when the Sanctus is sung, glorify the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God and Lord; humbly adore his majesty with the holy angels at all times.

Under the Canon.

After the Sanctus, under the Canon, you may take up meditation on the Lord's Passion, and offer it to God the Father, through its several greater and principal articles, for yourself, or for others, or for any necessity. Say, therefore, in this or a similar way:

Holy Father, I offer you the bloody sweat and tears of our Lord Jesus Christ, your most beloved Son, in that charity and union by which he voluntarily offered himself to you in death for us: for my sins, or for that brother, or for this or that necessity. And I ask you, through the pain of the wounds of your same most sweet Son, and through the shedding of his most holy blood and the power of his most innocent death: have mercy on me, or on him, or grant us this or that, etc. Through the same our Lord.

You may act similarly concerning the other articles also of the Lord's Passion, and enlarge upon them according to the intention of your affection in offering them.

At the Elevation.

If, at the elevation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your mind has not been occupied with higher things, you may pray to the Lord who is truly present in this way:

O Christ, king of glory, you are the everlasting Son of the Father. You, to deliver us, etc., to the end.

For this part of the hymn Thomas Aquinas is read to have prayed to the Lord under the elevation.

At the Agnus Dei.

When the Agnus Dei is sung, lift up your mind to the Lord Jesus with singular confidence, and humbly pray that he may receive you into perpetual peace. Direct your affection to him for whose love you have abandoned all things; seek from him, through reception of sacred communion, stable progress in your holy purpose.

At Ite, missa est.

When at the end Ite, missa est is sung, see that your mind is lifted up with the holy angels returning into heaven, so that through desire you may deserve to behold the Lord Jesus presiding in majesty, whom, sent to the Father in the sacrament, you can no longer see on earth. Therefore kindle your desire through an image of desolation, in the manner of those who are unwillingly separated from their friends.

Say, therefore, in your heart:

Meditation.

Behold, my Lord has returned to the heavens; he has left me desolate on earth. What am I doing here with mortals, when I have an immortal Lord? Why do I not follow him? Alas for me, because my sojourning has been prolonged; I have dwelt with the inhabitants of darkness; my soul has long been a sojourner. I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ; but who will deliver me, unhappy man, from the body of this death? O most beloved Lord Jesus, O my hope, O God and savior of my soul, draw me after you; I shall run in the fragrance of your ointments. O my sweet love, do not forget me to the end, but deign frequently to lift up my mind to you, so that, since I cannot see you bodily while placed in this mortality, at least lifted up in affection and desire in your most holy love, I may deserve often to behold you in spirit in glory. O my salvation, O heart of my heart, most beloved Jesus, do not abandon me or despise me. Be to me a gracious helper, and direct me always in your most holy will, so that I may truly be able to please you.
