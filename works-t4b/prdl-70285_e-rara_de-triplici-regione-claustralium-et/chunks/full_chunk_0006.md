And what greater ingratitude can there be than to live irreligiously in religion: to break one’s vow, and shamefully violate the purpose solemnly undertaken? For the monk who entangles himself in carnal vices, who neglects to live according to the Rule he has professed, voluntarily incurs the greatest vice of ingratitude.

In what way the monks of this lowest region are disordered toward their prelate. Article II.

Carnal monks are disordered in many ways toward their prelate: they tear apart his life with continual slanders; with a stubborn heart they disparage the pastoral office; they refuse, according to the Rule, to show the affection of filial love to their shepherd. For, blinded by various pleasures of the flesh, the monks of this lowest region disdain to look upon the abbot as one who holds Christ’s place in the monastery; and the one whom they ought humbly to have venerated as Christ’s vicar, they regard as like themselves in weakness and worthy of no honor. Under the snare of this disordered conduct the ancient enemy deceives carnal monks, whom he leads in various ways into displeasure with their shepherd. For very great power to rage against the monastic flock is granted to the devil when the one who presides is hated by his subjects. For just as a sick man cannot be healed if, by detesting the physician, he does not allow him to approach, so a monk cannot be saved if, because of aversion, he is unwilling to disclose his temptations to his shepherd. For how will a weak sheep be able to escape the teeth of a ravening wolf, if it cannot endure the presence of the shepherd?

First, therefore, disordered monks disparage the life of their shepherd; they obstinately accuse both his deeds and his words, because, when they see the rector proceeding according to the traditions of the holy fathers, they fear being corrected in their own depravity. For among reprobate minds the life of the good is always drawn into suspicion; envy is always tormented by the virtues of others; and the monk who voluntarily subjects himself to vices and sins

always hates a shepherd of purified conduct.

Second, the citizens of this shadowy region are disordered toward their prelate when they disparage his office, though they know that he was ordained by God to preside in the monastery. They murmur against him on any easy occasion; they do not observe his commands; and those who long ago, for the love of God, had submitted themselves with the weapons of holy obedience now, after despising obedience in the manner of Sarabaites, usurp for themselves the pernicious mastery of their own wills. They desire to have an abbot suited to their depraved habits; they reject one who is holy and useful. If they find a lax and idle shepherd, they poison him with many venoms by flattering him, calling him a most excellent and most loving father; and they subject themselves to him not humbly but gladly, since they fear no correction of their depravity from him.

Third, carnal monks show themselves disordered toward their shepherds when they withdraw from them the affection of filial love and scorn to obey according to the Rule. Yet since a monk is bound to love his abbot with sincere affection as Christ’s vicar, he will not be able to hate him without a very grave crime. Indeed, whoever does not love his shepherd for the love of Christ is not a true lover of God. For Christ, who wills that even enemies be loved, how would he permit his vicars to be hated? Therefore attend to him who wished the abbot to be over you in his stead, and from now on there will be no cause for displeasure against him.

Fourth, the disordered citizens of the shadowy valley disparage the reverence owed to their shepherd when they scorn to show him the honor due from the Rule; and, what is worse, they do not fear even to inflict insult upon him. For the Rule declares that the abbot holds the place of Christ in the monastery, and all monks ought humbly to obey him as Christ himself, because the obedience shown to superiors is shown to Christ. But perverse cloistered men, always ungrateful for the presidency of their prelates, do not fear obstinately to resist the divine ordinance; they scorn to obey the commands of the holy Rule; and they dishonor their abbot, to whom they ought to show every reverence, tearing him apart with hatred and slanders.
