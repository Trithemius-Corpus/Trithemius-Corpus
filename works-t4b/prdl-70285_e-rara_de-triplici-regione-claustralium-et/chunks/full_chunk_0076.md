Accordingly, during this angelic hymn the monk ought to be very devout, and, so to speak, attentive to each single word; in it the gift of peace is promised to people of good will, and God is humbly adored by us in various phrases.

¶ At the Collects.

When the collects or prayers are offered to God by the priest, the monk standing in choir should think of nothing else, but, listening to the words of the priest, should present his own desire to God in harmony with them.

¶ At the Epistle.

When the Epistle is read, you ought diligently to attend to everything, and with the greatest fervor of spirit give thanks to God, because, leaving behind the Jewish people, he so mercifully chose to call you and your ancestors from Gentile error to his faith.

¶ At the Gradual.

During the gradual, alleluia, sequence, or tract, consider how long a desire the holy fathers had, who before Christ’s incarnation longed for his coming. Then consider again with how many labors and injuries your Savior was wearied for you through thirty-three years in this world, he who willingly gave himself over to death for your salvation. Then call to mind the long and perilous exile of your soul in the flesh, so that still more, through this meditation on present misery, you may inflame your affection toward divine love.

At the Gospel.

During the holy Gospel, with your mind raised up in the Lord, give thanks exultantly, because the merciful Father willed that you be born of Christian parents, and because he made you a participant in his holy and most sweet doctrine.

At the Creed.

When the symbol of the catholic faith is sung, consider all the words and mysteries with humble fervor of spirit, and hasten at once to gather your whole self within yourself,

Think, if you can, indeed as much as you can, how great, lofty, and immeasurable is the Son of God who willed to be incarnate for you, and how hard and bitter were the things he endured for you.

At the Offertory.

During the offertory, apply your mind, together with the host that is to be consecrated, to the oblation; weigh carefully what is being done there mystically.

For the host is round and white. In its whiteness, purity, innocence, and the suffering humanity of our Savior are set forth as to be offered to God the Father on our behalf against all our impurities; in its roundness, his immeasurable divinity, set before us as a reward, is offered. Therefore say to the Lord:

¶ Prayer.

I have no fitting gift to offer you, almighty God; I hand over myself to you as a sacrifice. Receive me, therefore, holy Father, in union with the most holy oblation of the body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, your only-begotten Son, as an eternal gift to you and as a perpetual servant; and give me the continual burning of your holy love, so that I may be able always to render you service pleasing to you. Through the same Christ.

¶ Prayer.

I offer to you, almighty God, the life, passion, and death of the same our Lord Jesus Christ: for my sins; for the holy Roman and universal Church; for the supreme pontiff; for our bishop; for our abbot; for all my confreres; for the unity of our observance; for our whole order also; for the salvation of all the faithful; for the conversion of unbelievers; and for the redemption of the souls of the faithful who are in the pains of purgatory. I beseech you, holy Father, receive this oblation of mine in union with that charity by which you received the same our Lord Jesus Christ as he died for us on the cross; and may it be pleasing to you.
