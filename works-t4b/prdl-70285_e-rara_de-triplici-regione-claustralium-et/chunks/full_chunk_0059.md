For the Lord says in the holy Gospel: “Where your treasure is, there your heart also will be.” This is the treasure hidden in the field of the Scriptures; when a man has found it, he hides it, and going away he sells everything, so that he may buy the field with the treasure.

The field is this highest region of the spirit; the treasure is divine love. When a man finds it, he ought to despise all other things, so that he may joyfully possess the field together with the treasure. There is in the world no possession more pleasant than divine love; there is in this life no greater beauty, since whoever has this treasure of love seeks nothing further. And just as fire is naturally borne upward, so love is lifted up in this region of the spirit as if to its own proper dwelling.

For the mind, set aflame by divine love, is always raised toward God with fiery affections, and with wondrous sweetness is moved beyond every thought toward its own beginning. Purified in the exercise of the second region, it prepares itself for the ascent of this third region through loving affection. It longs as much as it can to come to him whom it loves; it thinks of nothing other than Jesus, the treasure of its heart; it adores him, because of the greatness of its love, with fiery sighs. But when the most sweet office of contemplation has been completed, the beloved suddenly slips away; the love-sick mind is recalled to the exercise of the second region. In that exercise the true lover of God sees that he does indeed dwell bodily among men, but in affection and desire he lives with his most beloved Jesus.

And although this anagogical action of the created spirit occurs by the impulse of the Spirit of God, nevertheless the mind, purified through the exercise of the second region, must advance itself toward the ascent and, disposed by a most fervent affection, cooperate with the divine Spirit. For truly almighty God, who gave us freedom of choice, will not raise anyone unwilling into this highest region of his contemplation. Therefore our will is required for our own salvation; love demands the ascent.

you will have to love very greatly. The will, once purified, receives love; love, when greatly enkindled, produces ecstasy. Therefore, if you desire to fly up to the mountain of contemplation, you must, by loving God most fervently, go wholly beyond yourself. But if you truly love God, you despise both yourself and everything else except him alone. For he himself is truly the treasure of the soul; if anyone has obtained him through love, he will always think of Jesus. This treasure is acquired by loving; no one understands its usefulness unless he has received it through love. Whoever desires to have this inestimable treasure should fix his heart constantly in the love of the most sweet Jesus. For truly, he who loves possesses; he who possesses loves. The mind that has merited to have this treasure of the love of Jesus remains constantly with him in affection in this highest region.

*That this third and highest region of the soul rejoices in the greatest freedom of the spirit.*

Article VII.

Seventh, this most holy region of the soul abounds in great freedom of the spirit; in it the soul, free from the passions of the flesh, reigns united to God through love. For the holy apostle says: “Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.” And again: “Whoever are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.” Truly, one who remains in this region of the spirit is made free from every defilement of flesh and blood; stripped of every phantasm of thought, naked and alone, he is raised into the darkness of divine contemplation. This is the true freedom of the soul: to rest in the fervor of divine love and to love none of this world’s consolations. Therefore let a man consider himself, and diligently examine his love; and if he finds himself devoutly affected, frequently lifted up by fiery desires to divine love even without preceding thought, let him trust that he can laudably be enrolled among the citizens of this region. For the Spirit of God bears witness to our spirit, and the mind that he raises into the ray of divine love he also makes know the condition of its state.
