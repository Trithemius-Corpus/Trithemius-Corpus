*An Excuse for the Length.*

If this prayer seems too long to anyone, let him read whatever part he wishes; for this reason we have divided it into short paragraphs, so that we might remove tedium. But the one who wishes to read neither the whole nor a part complains in vain about length, since he is shown to be willing to pray neither a long prayer nor a short one.

*Meditation before the Approach to the Altar.*

Finally, through meditation on the Lord’s passion, or at least on one of its more sacred articles, or on several if the opportunity of time is present, the mind of a devout person is pierced with compunction by a wonderful operation, provided that the habit of savoring meditation is possessed.

I would call meditation on the Lord’s passion “savoring” when a person, purified by true mortification, as soon as he has begun to turn over the Lord’s passion in thought, is dissolved into tears without violence, from love alone, and from the excessive desire of fervor is prepared as though for a certain transport of mind. Whoever has received this gift from the Lord will never be able to prepare himself better for celebrating than if, before the approach to the altar, he always passes over with tears at least one article of the Lord’s passion by meditating on it. Indeed, since the celebration of masses was instituted by our Redeemer himself in memory of the Lord’s passion, a person never prepares himself for it better and more fruitfully than if he is more fervently set aflame through meditation on that most bitter passion itself.

*The Elevation of the Priest’s Mind to God during Celebration.*

With how great reverence, devotion, and purity of mind we ought to stand before almighty God at the altar, what speech could express, or what understanding could penetrate by comprehension? For there the true body and true blood of our Lord Jesus Christ are consecrated in an ineffable manner; there the Lord of majesty, truly present, is adored; there the army of angels, standing by its invisible king, rejoices together; there the whole heavenly court adores Jesus, God and man. Therefore the greatest purity of mind, the greatest reverence and humility are needed when we must stand before so great a majesty, lest the Lord Jesus, who searches the reins and the heart, be able to find anything in us that offends him. Let all worldly anxieties depart far from here; let all thoughts withdraw. Let nothing be thought here except what pertains to the consummation of so great a mystery; let nothing turn in the mind except what is fitting in the presence of so great majesty and glory. For here what is enacted is not an office of human power, but a miracle of divine majesty: the Son of God is offered to the Father; the body of our Redeemer is truly received; the mystery of our reparation is celebrated. Therefore, standing at the altar, let the priest remember that he stands before an invisible majesty, and let him beware of thinking anything whatever from elsewhere.
