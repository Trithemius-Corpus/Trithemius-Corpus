At Vespers. Consideration VII.

When the first signal for Vespers has been heard, and after saying in the customary way, Blessed be the name of the Lord, along with the other things usually said beforehand, have compassion on those occupied in worldly business, and give thanks that almighty God has willed you to be free from those things in order to serve him. Then recollect yourself inwardly; or you may meditate somewhat on the Lord’s Passion, until, when the second signal sounds, you go to choir. For in my judgment there is no meditation better for beginners and those advancing toward compunction and devotion of mind than that which takes its beginning, through burning affections, from the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ.

At the Vigils of the Dead.

When, at the summons for the vigils after Vespers, you may recite the hour of the Holy Cross, and afterward think somewhat about the immeasurable charity of God by which he loves faithful souls. Then, as much as you can, consider how great is the desolation of those holy souls placed in purgatory; how great is the pain for a spirit separated from the body and longing to return to the center of its first principle, not to be able to behold the face of its Creator in friendly enjoyment, but to be compelled to wait in punishments and torments most grievous to it. For so great is the desolation, sadness, and grief of a separated soul placed in punishments and longing to come to God, that no kind of torment known to us in this world could be compared with its sorrow. Indeed, there is no comparison, in our manner of speaking, between bodily sadness and desolation and the state of a separated spirit, appointed to a delay in its return to its own principle.

Therefore, as much as you can, have compassion for the desolation of faithful souls groaning in the prison of purgatory, and pour out diligent prayers for them to the Lord. For even if the almighty and merciful Father, through his justice, delays them from the kingdom, nevertheless he loves them with inestimable charity. Therefore whatever good you now do for them by praying, you offer as a pleasing sacrifice to almighty God. And when you come there, you will receive multiplied whatever you have done here in charity for them. For Scripture says: It is a holy and wholesome thing to pray for the dead, that they may be loosed from sins.

Passing Through the Cemetery or Cloister.

Pray for the dead.

When you pass through a cemetery, cloister, or some other place of burial for the dead, do not forget your brothers and all the dead; but, moved by charity, have compassion on them, and strive to assist them before the Lord in this or a similar manner.

Prayer.

Hail, faithful souls; rest in holy peace. May he who redeemed you by his precious blood deign to free you from punishments and lead you to eternal joys, where you may rejoice and exult forever, blessed ones.

Another.

May God the Father, who created you, bless you. May Jesus Christ, who redeemed you by his most precious blood, bless you. May the Holy Spirit, who by his kindness and gift preserved you from eternal damnation, bless you. Amen.

Read the following prayer often.

I beseech you, sweetest Lord Jesus, Son of the living God, through the pain of your wounds, through the pouring out of your most holy blood, and through the power of your most innocent death: have mercy on the souls of all the faithful departed who are in the punishments of purgatory, and give them a dwelling in eternal light with all your saints.
