Preface to the second treatise of the first part, which deals with the middle region, namely the rational region, of cloisterers.

And now at last let us leave behind this dark region of the carnal, in which the whirlwind of crimes remains and the continual storm of vices. We have granted enough to it, and have lingered over it more than our purpose required. Let us therefore leave the dead to bury their dead; let us yield to the darkness; let us hasten toward the light of the spiritual life. We must go out from the Egypt of the dark region and hasten diligently to the highest summit of the spirit through the middle way of reason. Let us therefore now at last approach the middle region of the soul, through which one comes successively to the highest. This region of the soul is quite suitably called rational, since in it there is continual labor for the investigation of the highest truth. The fervor of mind of the citizens of this middle region always strives to raise them to higher things, but the weakness of the flesh, by the motion of a raging whirlwind, attempts to draw them back to the lowest things. From this comes continual labor; from this arise sighs and groans, because the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and this earthly habitation depresses the mind that thinks on many things.

This is the narrow way that leads to life, and few walk along it. In this region violence is laid upon the concupiscences of the flesh until the will is subjected to the spirit at its command. For the Lord says in the Gospel: The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent seize it. In this region the war of contest is declared against those who serve God, as the wise man bears witness, saying: Son, when you approach the service of God, stand in fear, and prepare your soul for temptation. How great the temptations are in this middle region we learn daily through the difficulty of spiritual exercise, when, driven in various ways, we cannot remain long in the fervor of the spirit. Now the weakness of the flesh recalls us from zeal for advancement; now the temptation of the ancient enemy troubles us; now the company of neighbors hinders us;

now the deceitfulness of the world leads us astray. Therefore those who wish to make progress in this region of labor need the greatest fortitude, because no one can resist his temptations to the end unless he strives daily to do violence to himself. Whoever thinks that he has come to this region should not presume upon his own powers, because just as the grace of God, in order to work good in us, requires the choice of our will, so the will necessarily requires grace. For no one is holy by his own powers. Therefore let no one dare to presume upon himself, but let him who stands take care lest he fall. The life of man upon earth is warfare, because through many tribulations one comes to the kingdom, and by the arduous labors of the virtues heaven is acquired. The prophet says: Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming, they shall come with exultation, carrying their sheaves. For indeed those who sow in tears shall reap in joy. But if someone fails in the sowing, he certainly will not rejoice in the harvest.

Since, therefore, through the continual labors of spiritual exercise the mind must be cleansed from the dust of the first region, do not seek leisure in this middle region; do not love the repose of negligence, because no one is able to stand here except the one who firmly girds himself for continual labor. Whoever in this middle region does not always advance toward better things soon falls into the negligences of his former life, and there falls into the danger of failing where he has lost the desire of advancing. This is the place of continual contest; whoever neglects to fight in it retreats backward to be conquered.

Here ends the preface.

Here begins the second region of the soul, which is called rational, in which men are engaged amid many labors.

The second region of the soul, therefore, is called rational, in which the mind is exercised with continual labor for the investigation of the highest truth; and the farther it separates itself from the lowest region, the more it approaches the highest. And this region is proposed to be likened in certain ways to the ethereal heaven, in which there is the position of the stars and their continual motion.
