After celebration.
Let careful custody of the mind be maintained.

After the celebration, or the communion of the Lord’s body and blood, let the devout cenobite render thanks to almighty God, and throughout the whole day, by spiritual exercise, preserve himself in the purity and devotion of mind already begun. Let him direct his affections toward God with fiery rays, and not grow lukewarm from his holy purpose. Before all else, let him strive to love nothing in this world, but, with his heart entirely stripped of all earthly affections, to raise it continually to the fire of divine love. Let him always consider how, from this time onward, he may establish his life as if anew in the holy fervor of his way of life, and humbly dispose himself for the more frequent repeated reception of so great a guest.

Finally, during the day, amid the labors enjoined upon him, let him more often, with some prayer, offer himself to God the Father in the virtue and union of that sacrament, so that through the exercises of holy communion and regular obedience he may be kindled more fully in divine love. And we attain this divine love especially through the virtue of this venerable sacrament, if, in memory of the Lord’s Passion, by communicating in it more often, we are renewed through the grace of compunction. For indeed the devout human mind, through communion in this most sacred sacrament, is kindled by the effective operation of the fervor of divine love; and, wholly denied to itself, united by grace into Christ, it is transformed. Therefore, the more each person, denied to himself through true resignation, has offered himself to the Lord as a living victim in memory of the Lord’s Passion, the more disposed he will be to celebrate worthily.

After private masses.
Returning to the cell.

When the celebration of masses has been completed, and the thanksgivings finished according to custom, let the monk return to his cell; and, after first saying the customary prayer, as above at Matins,

let him recollect himself for a little while, and kindle himself further from his holy purpose of perseverance. Then, turned toward the Lord, let him say:

I beseech you, sweetest Lord Jesus, by the virtue of your most holy body and blood, which I have received, though unworthy: grant me the sweetness of your love, sincerity of brotherly affection, strength in temptation, purity in heart, and discipline in conduct, so that, by your gift, I may satisfy my profession and continually advance in your most holy love.

Then, until the first signal for choir, let him read something good, meditate or pray, or certainly write; and let him fervently attend to the work enjoined upon him by obedience.

At the first signal for choir, before the high mass.

When the first signal has sounded for the hour that is customarily allowed before the high mass, let the cenobite at once put aside everything he has in his hands and read the preparatory prayers for choir, as they were set out above before Matins. Afterward let him go to choir with reverence and maturity, bowing and praying according to the rite set down above.

At the high mass.

Consideration 4.

At the ringing for the high mass, you may read the appointed hour of the Holy Cross, and afterward, as much as time permits, recall to memory something from the prologue set before the private masses. For the sum of our salvation is placed in the merit of the Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ; nor is any good work of merit believed to belong to us unless it is offered in his virtue. The memory of this Passion is enacted in the celebration of masses, where the innocent Lamb, slain for us, is offered to God the Father in an ineffable mystery. Therefore from our only Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, whom we believe to have suffered for us, we truly hope for salvation. And therefore whatever the Church asks in the collects, she humbly prays that it be given her by God the Father through him. This regard

for the merit of the Lord’s Passion our devout cenobite should have in his heart; upon it the whole office of the mass should revolve sweetly, as upon the lower millstone. For every mass, wherever it may be that is said in God’s holy Church, is at all times referred to the merit of the Lord’s Passion.

On the Introit of the Mass.

We do not intend, however, to explain the office of the mass point by point, since each of our men can find many explanations by others; but we wish to give an occasion for meditation to devout brothers, whom we know to be inflamed with inward desires. Therefore you can reduce the first part of the introit and the verse, in meditation, to the incarnation of the Lord, and refer the repetition with the Gloria to his nativity. Nevertheless, you must diligently examine what the introit contains according to its literal sense, so that you may be able to find a suitable spiritual understanding for yourself. For nothing is done in vain in the divine office, but all things are filled with mysteries and sacred actions.

On Kyrie eleison.

Kyrie eleison is Greek, and is composed of a noun and a verb; among the Greeks these are two words. The first is from kyrios, which means “lord”; kyrie is said in the vocative. The second is from eleeo, which means “I have mercy”; eleeson, “have mercy,” is uttered in the imperative. Hence comes Kyrie eleison, that is, “Lord, have mercy,” through elision for Kyrie eleison, since a vowel before a vowel may be elided for a better sound.

Therefore Kyrie eleison is said three times to the Father, so that he who, from exceeding charity, willed his Son to be incarnate of the Virgin and to die for us may deign by his mercy to assist us in our necessities. To the Son also we say Christe eleison three times, in which we pray him to assist us. We also utter Kyrie eleison three times to the Holy Spirit, in which we humbly invoke him, through his ineffable goodness, to come to our aid.
