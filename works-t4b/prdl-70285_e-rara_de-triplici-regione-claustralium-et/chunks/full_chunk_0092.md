After Compline

After Compline, when the devout cenobite has gathered his strength, let him enter alone into the secret place of his heart, and leave outside all the cares and anxieties of the world, so that, free and released from all things, he may be worthy to speak with the Lord Jesus through the desire of compunction, and to be sweetly united with him through the fire of love. Therefore, when the faults of the day have been recalled to mind for examination in the highest degree, let him pray thus to the Lord.

Prayer.

Most merciful Lord Jesus Christ, who for our redemption willed to undergo a most shameful death: grant me, I beseech you, through the pain of your wounds and through the shedding of your most holy blood, and by the power of your most innocent death, pardon for all my sins, so that, with my conscience cleansed, I may hereafter cleave to you continually through the desire of love, may daily advance in holy religion, and may humbly submit myself in all things to your most holy will.

To the Son.

Lord Jesus Christ, I commend my body and my soul to you this night and at all times, in the power and union of that love by which, hanging on the cross, you commended your beloved mother, the Virgin Mary, to the disciple. In that love, I beseech you, guard me from all the snares of the ancient serpent; suppress the illicit motions of the flesh; drive far away foul and evil dreams; repel the phantasms of the night, so that I may sleep secure in your peace, and, at the hours appointed for me, rise cheerfully to your praises. Amen.

To the Father.

O my holy Father, almighty and eternal God, who gently received us into your grace in the passion of your only-begotten Son:

I commend myself to you, holy Father, this night and at all times, in the faith and union of that charity with which your Son, our Lord, when about to die, commended his spirit to you upon the cross.

To Saint Mary.

Holy and immaculate Virgin Mary, Mother of God, I ask you, through the bitter passion and death of your sweetest Son, do not reject me, a wretched sinner, because of my unclean conscience, but remember that you are the mother of mercy, and pray for me to God, whom you bore, that he may mercifully forgive me my sins, make me his fervent lover, guard me this night from all snares and evils, and after the death of the flesh lead my soul to eternal life. To your protection, most blessed Virgin Mary, I commit myself this night and at all times, in the power and union of that entrusting by which your beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, when he was now about to die on the cross, commended you to John, his disciple.

On the Memory of the Lord’s Passion.

Since the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ is the gate of our salvation, through which the kingdom of heaven is opened to us, we ought to exercise ourselves in it often, with all the devotion we can, because we shall easily obtain the effect of our prayers if, through the love of compassion, we adore the Lord in the power of his wounds. Therefore, faithful cenobite, with all the diligence I can, I admonish, exhort, and ask you that, mindful of the benefits of God, you entirely neglect no day on which you do not give thanks to your Savior for the bitterness of his

passion and death. And if, for any reason, you are unable to do this at greater length, do not refuse to show yourself grateful to your Redeemer with at least one word. The Lord Jesus himself has often revealed to his lovers, to whom he wished, that nothing is more pleasing to him, and nothing more useful to us, than to recall the memory of his passion and death with thanksgiving, and to admonish others to do the same. Receive, therefore, a shortened word, lest a more extended prayer make you weary.

With knees bent to the ground and hands joined together, say:

I give you thanks, sweetest Lord Jesus, for all the wounds, pains, and injuries of your most innocent passion and death. I ask you, through that same death of yours, have mercy on me now and at the hour of my death.

Or thus:

To your most bitter passion and most innocent death, Lord Jesus Christ, with a compassionate soul I give thanks. I ask you, have mercy on me.

Or thus:

I praise, bless, and adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, King of heaven and earth, and I give you thanks for your holy passion, by which you freed me, wretched as I am, from perpetual death. I ask you, through the power of that same passion of yours, make me a participant forever in your most innocent death.

Conclusion of this compendium, by Johannes Trithemius, abbot of Sponheim.

Lest I be more prolix than is fitting, here I place an end to this compendium. For that venerable assembly of abbots of our annual chapter instructed me to avoid prolixity in this place, lest lukewarm and worldly cloisterers use weariness as an excuse. If the Lord Jesus grants me a life free from cares, as I have always desired, I shall try to satisfy both my own intention and another’s request.
