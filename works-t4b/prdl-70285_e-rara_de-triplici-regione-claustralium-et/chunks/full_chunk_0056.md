No one understands the sweetness of this theology unless he receives it, and no one receives it unless he seeks it alone out of all things through the sighs of love. The psalmist sought this when he prayed to the Lord: “Would that my ways were directed to keep your justifications at all times.” For by such movements of desire the holy man was borne upward toward obedience to the divine will, so that in that same Psalm 118 he spoke scarcely any verse in which he did not set down a divine word equivalent to judgment.

The exercise of this purification is begun in the second region and is completed in this one. For no small labor is required, so that the affection may be purified from the desires of the world. But once the exercise of spiritual reformation has been taken up, the mind gradually advances in love; it longs to ascend to this region by fixed degrees; through desire it hastens to the invisible school of this theology; sighing with continual groans, it knocks at the door of divine mercy. But the mind is not admitted into the secret of this mystical wisdom unless, after the complete purification of the affection, it is stripped of every creature, unites itself to itself, and withdraws far from every reasoning or imagining of thinkable things. For unless it has withdrawn from every creature, it does not approach the Creator, who is above all things.

In this school, therefore, the Holy Spirit is believed to preside over those who learn by divine instruction. He teaches a person to love nothing in this world, and to conform himself in all things to the will of God. Happy is the one who has deserved to be the disciple of this master; happy is the one who has entered this school through the desire of love, because in mystical silence he perceives wondrous things which God has hidden from those who do not love him.

That this highest region of the spirit is founded in simplicity.

Article IV.

Fourth, this most holy region of the spirit is said to be founded in simplicity, in which the mind is united to its own immutable principle, not through essence,

--- Page 118 ---

but through the love of adherence. For, as we have already said above, that highest good, God, is one; and our soul cannot approach him unless, stripped of all multiplicity which belongs to reason, imagination, and understanding, it strives to present itself wholly united to itself. For the soul itself naturally desires to reach the highest good; but the more it is divided by multiplicity in its operation, the less fit it is found to come to God. For multiplicity is opposed to unity, and the more the mind has turned itself toward many things, the more it withdraws from unity.

One, two, three, four are ten. You withdraw from the one, because you are multiplied in number. Return to the one, if you wish to have the principle. For one is the principle of all things; if you add anything to it in your soul, at once you withdraw from it. When you say “one,” you say “principle.” If you say “two,” you have already abandoned the principle. Therefore, so that you may always be able to contemplate your principle, take care at all times, once multiplicity has been stripped away, to render yourself one.

For since all good things are from the first good, and every love, toward whatever good it is borne, has been naturally kindled by that same good, our love cannot rest in any goods as in its final end unless, naked and pure, it strives finally to return to its own principle. For union comes about only by the power of the one itself, since we say that things most like each other are united more easily for no other reason than because we see that they are one in quality. Therefore the soul is believed to be able at last to be united with the one highest principle of things when, stripped of all multiplicity and united to itself, it is lifted up into God by most fervent love.

Just as there is no union between things diverse in quality, so there can be no adherence between God and a soul divided in itself. For the union of one with one comes about through likeness of quality, and the adherence of the mind, simplified and stripped of all things through the affection of love, will be with God. Thus the highest region of the spirit is founded in simplicity: in it the mind of the purified person is raised into God by the most fervent desire of love and is united by grace to its own immutable principle. Intellect and affection are two.
