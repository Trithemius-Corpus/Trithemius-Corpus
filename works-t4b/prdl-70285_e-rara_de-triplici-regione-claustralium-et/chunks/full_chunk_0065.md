Before the venerable sacrament.

Hail, living victim, truth and life, in whom the ancient sacrifices have come to an end. Through you infinite glory is given to the Father. Through you may the Church always stand fortified. Grant me rewards in eternal life. Amen. Or: O saving victim.

Standing in his place in choir.

When he has come to his accustomed station in choir, if there is time, let him consider what our holy father Benedict said in the Rule about reverence in prayer and psalmody. Then, considering the subject matter of the psalms to be sung, let him strive to subject his intention to them, and carefully foresee to what he wishes to refer each part. If the morning office is sung for some saint, let the psalmody be said before the Lord in that saint’s memory. But if the subject matter of the psalms suits the passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, it should be referred to his memory. Yet if the spirit of compunction is present to the singer, it will teach all things through love. For a mind fervent in the love of God knows very well how to direct its intention to the praise of the Creator, and how to vary it suddenly in each verse according to the character of the psalmody. For according to the subject matter of what is being sung, it exercises itself when it wishes: now it embraces Christ’s humanity; now, suddenly, with another verse it is raised to his divinity. Nevertheless, if it pleases our faithful monk, while the same signal lasts he can call to mind the fervor of the holy angels praising God without interruption, and conform himself to them through desire, according to his powers.

Before all the hours.

Since the power of the Lord’s incarnation and passion incomparably surpasses all the merits of all the saints, for this reason the lover of God, before each of the hours, should say this prayer to God the Father with attentive mind, in heart or with the mouth.

Prayer.

--- Page 137 ---
Almighty, eternal God, who created us from nothing when we did not exist, and mercifully redeemed us when we had perished, I ask you, by your unchangeable majesty: receive these praises and prayers which I am about to speak to you now and at every time, in that union of charity and love in which you sent your only-begotten Son into the womb of the most blessed Virgin Mary, and in which you received him suffering and dying for us on the cross. Amen.

Note on the aforesaid prayer.

Do not think this manner of praying frivolous, nor invented by human curiosity; for if you believe that God has often taught his friends many things by revelation, you contradict nothing in this most holy form. Rather, read the wondrous works of the Lord which he has shown to those who love him, and you will find what he revealed to Mechthild and others about this manner and form of praying. Know, moreover, that I am thoroughly persuaded that the prayer which a devout mind has resolved to offer to God the Father in union with the Lord’s passion is most pleasing to almighty God. And this offering can be placed first, mentally or orally, not only in the canonical hours, but also in other prayers, masses, and any good exercises or works. But if through forgetfulness it has not been placed first, it can be inserted during prayer, or even added after its completion.

How the monk ought to conduct himself in the hours.

The devout monk should apply great diligence, lest his mind admit images or thoughts about transitory things; for unless vain thoughts are driven back at once in the beginning, they will disturb the heart’s intention throughout the whole office. Therefore the faithful one who prays should gather himself wholly within, and, according to the Rule, prefer nothing to the divine service. While singing psalms, let him refer the very subject matter of the psalms to the fitting focus of his intention; let him prudently attend to the sense of the prayer; let him pronounce the words distinctly and in order; let his mind strive upward.
