Finally, it is necessary for one who wishes to occupy himself profitably in these spiritual exercises to have a joyful and free heart, alien to every anxiety and to the cares of the world. For the Holy Spirit is a spirit of freedom and joy: he loves a free heart, joyful in God, and, so to speak, applauds one like himself. For just as a bird tied to the earth cannot lift itself up to heaven by flying, so a mind occupied with perishable things cannot raise itself to heavenly things. Nor should the spirit be cast down by too much frequent practice; otherwise the brain too is confused, and one labors in vain through dry habit. Therefore let the cenobite, fervent in spirit, learn always to persist in these things with moderation, and by his intention continually refer the purpose of every exercise to God, who is the beginning and end of all goodness. Let him faithfully direct his effort toward fear and love of him, and fix his mind in profound humility, since one who is rooted in love is not easily abandoned by God.

Here ends the spiritual exercise of cloistered persons, by Peter of Friedberg, in the noble city of Mainz, on the eighth Ides of August, in the year of salvation M.cccc.xcviii.

The Compendium of the Spiritual Exercise Begins.
Johannes Trithemius.

Although this formula of spiritual exercise, published by us for the edification of cloistered persons, might appear very brief to any devout persons who have leisure, nevertheless we fear that to those occupied with household affairs it may seem rather lengthy. For when their thought is stretched by continual concern in arranging the necessities of their subjects, the mind is easily wearied even by very brief spiritual exercises. Therefore, lest our monks who are either devoted to exterior matters, or are less devout in spiritual conversation, be able, by occasion of its length, to take refuge in their customary excuse, we have been ordered to abbreviate the same instruction, so that the fervent may keep the longer one in their devotion, and the lukewarm may not omit the shorter.

The Practice of the Spiritual Exercise Follows.

Before each and every hour, in general, the devout cenobite should strive to direct his effort toward this end: that with all his strength he may desire to magnify God in his heart, by the root of his benefits. Let there be in him so great a most fervent ardor of devotion, so great a compunction, and finally so great an outpouring of tears, that whoever sees him chanting psalms may be moved by his example to magnify the Lord. Secondly, let him remember that he can be stirred to charity toward his neighbor, since love is directed toward God in vain if one’s neighbor is hated. Therefore, when all the thorns of displeasure have been expelled, let the most devout cenobite, turned toward the Lord, pray.
