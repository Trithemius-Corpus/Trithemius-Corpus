A ruler of souls who does not correct his offending subjects entangles himself, to his own damnation, in the crimes of others; by keeping silent he becomes the companion of those whose faults damnation will follow. For whoever, having been appointed a shepherd of souls, neglects to reform sinners according to the canonical rule, even if he is free from any fault of his own, will nevertheless be condemned before the strict Judge for another’s offense. For the supreme Shepherd says to the prophet: “Son of man, I have given you as a watchman to the house of Israel; you shall hear the word from my mouth and announce it to them from me. If, when I say to the wicked man, ‘You shall surely die,’ you do not announce it to him, nor speak so that he may turn from his wicked way and live, that wicked man shall indeed die in his iniquity, but I shall require his blood from your hand.”

And our blessed father says in the Rule: “Let the abbot know that the fault of the shepherd will fall upon him if the master of the household has been able to find any lack of profit in his sheep.” O judgment ever to be feared by all shepherds: by it not only is the wicked conduct of subjects charged to the rector, but he is also held guilty before the inward Judge for their lesser progress. O what shall we wretched shepherds say in that strict examination, we who neglect to reform according to the Rule the monks entrusted to us when they offend in many ways; who do not call those lying in the lowest region to higher things, but, carnal among the carnal, grow numb in the shadow of death through destructive idleness?

Woe to all who remain in this dark region, for unless they do penance they must be assigned with those who inhabit the outer darkness, where no order dwells but everlasting horror. For just as monks who walk according to the Rule in spirit are to be happily crowned without end in heaven with the holy angels, so those who persevere until death, against the norm of monastic institution, in this lowest region of the flesh are worse than pagans and are to be tormented eternally in hell with demons. For that blessed man spoke truly: “Just as I have never seen better men than those who have made progress in the monastery, so I have not found any worse than those who have failed in the monastery.” For monastic profession demands the perfection of the spiritual life, and tends toward the height of the virtues as toward its proper end. But the more anyone established in a monastery falls away from this effort to make progress, the farther he withdraws from the purpose of a monk.

How carnal monks, citizens of this lowest region, are disordered in many articles.

Although the description of this lowest region applies generally to any carnal human beings who dwell with beasts in sins and crimes, nevertheless, to attend to our own concerns, we intend to speak of lukewarm and carnal cloistered men, doing no injury at all to the good and devout. For always and in all things we wish the character of good cloistered men to remain untouched and unharmed whenever we discuss the evil pursuits of the wicked. Thus we rebuke wicked cloistered men and corrupt habits, while everywhere honoring the good and praising them as is fitting. And what else can we say of the wicked except evil, since in them we find no good things to speak of? They live disorderly lives in a holy order; they despise the traditions of the elders; they do not admit the words of salvation.

So great, indeed, is the disorder of the monks of the lowest region that, in my judgment, it would be easier to convert a publican than to recall a disorderly monk to penance. We include this disorder of carnal monks under eight articles, although we are not unaware that they are disordered in many matters.

First, then, the citizens of this lowest region, beastly monks, are disordered toward God; then toward their prelate; third, the prelate toward his subjects; fourth, the members toward one another; again, fifth, they are disordered toward themselves; then toward the property of the monastery; seventh, toward contempt of the world; and finally, toward penance for sin. These are the principal disorders of the citizens of the lowest region; these are the beastly affections of carnal monks, who dwell in the depths in mind and body and do not hasten to advance toward the heights. I shall therefore take care to set forth briefly, in order, these disordered conditions of the lowest region, and to demonstrate, so far as I can, how far carnal monks are from God.
