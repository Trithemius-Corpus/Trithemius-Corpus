But from their fruits we know plainly what sort they are: namely, they violate peace and charity in the monastery; they disturb concord and unity; they sow discord between the brothers and the abbot; they stubbornly refuse to obey the commands of the rule. These are the works of evil cloisterers; these are the signs of the citizens of the lowest region: as most wicked defenders of their own will, they cast away the weapons of holy obedience; they live by the flesh, not by the spirit; they show either no deference, or little, to the statutes of the fathers; they do not acquiesce in sound counsels. Their condemnation is just. For just as it is impossible that a flaming fire should not rise upward, so it is impossible for a monk without obedience to penetrate the secrets of heaven.

How the shepherds of this lowest region are disordered toward their subjects.

Article III.

Since, then, we have shown that very many monks of the lowest region are disordered toward their shepherds, shall we not also show the disorders of prelates toward their subjects, as though sparing ourselves? Neither does the name of abbot sanctify anyone, nor does the eminence of pastoral care save the undeserving. Therefore, since many among the prelates dwell with the beasts of the earth on the mountain of the lowest region, they must by no means be passed over by us in silence. For very many rulers of cloistered communities show themselves disordered toward their subjects: they not only neglect to feed according to the rule the souls committed to them, but tear and kill them in various ways. Whenever someone from the lowest region is taken up as shepherd, the pursuits of the wicked are strengthened for the worse. Therefore the man to be promoted as abbot should be good and devoted to God, one who has long since overcome this lowest region, who has learned to tame the passions of the flesh, who both wills and is able to ascend to that highest region of the spirit. For the abbot of monks ought to be of higher merit than the monks, since whoever is appointed ruler of souls

must necessarily be adorned with many virtues. For if the shepherd, still weak, dwells in the lowest region with the sheep, he kills the feeble by neglecting them; he does not strengthen them. For if a blind man offers guidance to a blind man, he must necessarily fall with him into the pit. But the shepherd who does not show himself ordered toward his subjects according to the rule should be called not a shepherd but a hireling, and he will not remain in the house of the Lord forever. There are, moreover, many disorders of carnal prelates, which do harm all the more because they are more rarely corrected.

First, then, a ruler is disordered toward his subjects if he is a hireling: if, neglecting the sheep, he seeks his own advantage of flesh or soul; if, so that he may be able to obtain his own will, he denies nothing contrary to the rule to those who ask. For he who is a hireling does not truly love the sheep; he seeks his own glory; he desires to be over others; he does not care to profit them according to the rule; he rejoices to be called abbot, but does not know how to show himself a shepherd. Therefore he receives the empty reward of his vanity, who seeks not God’s glory but his own; who would sooner allow the souls of his subjects to perish than resign his abbacy to a better man. He is a hireling, and it does not belong to him to care for the sheep. He is not a true monk, not a good shepherd, not a sincere lover of God, who, when presiding over monks, is unwilling to yield if, by the counsel of those who fear God, it should seem expedient to do so. The care of ruling souls rejects a proud man; it requires one humble and resigned to God in all things. Therefore, if you wish to be found a good shepherd and a true monk, remember that your intention must at all times be formed in such a way that you are ready, from love of God, humbly to feed the souls committed to you according to your strength, and ready also, at the judgment of God and of good men, to resign the pastoral office from the integrity of your heart. If you always keep this intention abiding in your mind, you will be a good shepherd both to yourself and to others. But if in pastoral care you love your own honor, if you prefer your own advantage to the usefulness of your subjects, you still dwell with the beasts in the lowest region, and you make manifest how disordered your heart is.
