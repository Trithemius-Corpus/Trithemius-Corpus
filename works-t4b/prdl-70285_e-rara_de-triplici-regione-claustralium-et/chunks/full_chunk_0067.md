Prayer.

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful, and kindle in them the fire of your love: you who, through the diversity of all tongues, gathered the nations into the unity of faith. Send forth your Spirit, and they shall be created. And you shall renew the face of the earth.

Prayer.

O God, to whom every heart is open, to whom every will speaks, and from whom no secret is hidden: purify the thoughts of our heart by the pouring-in of the Holy Spirit, that we may deserve to love you perfectly and to praise you worthily. Through Christ our Lord. Let him add O flourishing rose, and, if he wishes, anything else according to his devotion.

At the first signal.

Then, at the first signal of any hour, or also at the striking of the clock, unless he is occupied with higher meditations, let him say: Blessed be the name of the Lord, from this time now and forever. And let him add some brief little prayer formed from the ardor of his mind, in this or a similar manner.

A Little Prayer.

Receive me, sweetest Lord Jesus, as a perpetual servant, that I may always have you as my one good.

Another.

I beseech you, most merciful God, grant me to live so innocently in holy religion that I may be able to come to you, the beginning and end of every good.

Another.

Would that my ways may be directed to keeping your justifications at all times.

Another.

Most loving Lord Jesus, I ask you, by the merit of your most holy Passion, teach me to despise all things, that I may continually deserve to find you, the supreme good of the soul.

Then let the general preparation for prayer be observed,

in the fervor set out above before Matins, and let the conscience be cleansed if anything has been committed against its purity. Yet if the mind has been raised in contemplation of divine love through continual desire, it will have less need of the exercises of beginners.

At the second signal.

When the second signal sounds, both for Prime and for the other hours of the day, let the manner set out above for Matins be observed, so far as the time permits each person to pray. But when our cenobite enters or leaves the church, let him not neglect to show reverence to God before the altars and the crucifix; rather, bowing humbly, let him say the appropriate prayers according to custom. For the modest bending of the body is regarded as an outward showing of the inner person well ordered toward God.

In the chapter-place after Prime.

After Prime, when the devout cenobite enters the chapter-place, let him remember to give thanks to God for the victory of the holy martyrs, whom he knows to have served the Lord, in the greatest fervor of spirit, through the testimony of death after despising the torments inflicted on them. Therefore, when you have heard the brave deeds of the saints read, pray to the Lord that, by their merits, he may deign to forgive your sins and mercifully strengthen you to bear the yoke of monastic discipline, so that you may obtain fellowship with them. And this desire is proposed for you to take up when, after their passions have been read, divine help is invoked three times by all. We do these things also at the beginning of the day so that, strengthened for patience by the examples of the saints, we may fear no adversities throughout the whole day. But when you hear that some martyr stood bravely in the agony of contest, you will pray to God in these or similar words.
