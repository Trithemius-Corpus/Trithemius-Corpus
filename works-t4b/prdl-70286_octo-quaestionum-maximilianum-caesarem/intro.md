*Liber Octo Quaestionum ad Maximilianum Caesarem* — "A Book of Eight Questions to Emperor Maximilian" — is Trithemius's answer to the eight questions Maximilian set him: on faith and reason, on the faith necessary for salvation, on the miracles of unbelievers, on witches and harmful magic, and on related matters. Composed in 1508, it is his principal statement on witchcraft and had a long reception in early-modern demonology, furnishing later writers with both argument and authority. The questions are enumerated at the head of the book and answered in turn, in the same blend of scholastic reasoning and pastoral judgement that marks Trithemius's shorter treatises.
