# Cluster register notes — monastic reform / exhortation

This work belongs to Trithemius's monastic-reform corpus (*De Statu et Ruina Monastici Ordinis*, *De Triplici Regione Claustralium*, the *Sermones ad Monachos*, the *Compendium*). Apply these conventions:

- **Benedictine technical vocabulary** retains its precise sense:
  - *conversio morum* = conversion of life (Benedictine vow; not generic "conversion")
  - *stabilitas* = stability (vow of place; not "stability" in modern sense)
  - *obedientia* = obedience (vow; in monastic context render as the canonical English "obedience")
  - *coenobium* / *cenobium* = monastery (specifically a Benedictine house with common life)
  - *abbas* = abbot; *prior* = prior; *praepositus* = provost
  - *regula* = the Rule (typically Benedict's); when general, "rule"
  - *capitulum* = chapter (both the daily monastic meeting and the section of the Rule read at it)
- **Hortatory register.** Trithemius writes in the prophetic-reforming mode of Bernard of Clairvaux and Peter Damian. Imperatives, second-person address ("*frater carissime*" — "dearest brother"), and direct exhortation are the norm. Preserve the second-person register in English.
- **Scriptural anchoring is dense.** When a phrase echoes Vulgate diction (humility from Mt 11, the cross from Lk 9, the disorder of the world from 1 John 2), let the English match the Douay-Rheims register rather than novelizing.
- **The "ruin" topos.** Phrases like *ruina ordinis*, *statum corruptum*, *negligentia patrum* are the recurring vocabulary of late-medieval monastic reform — translate consistently across passages.
- **Avoid "decline" for *ruina*.** *Ruina* is stronger: "ruin", "collapse", or "fall" — Trithemius means it.
