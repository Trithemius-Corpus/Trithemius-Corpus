# Cluster register notes — Marian / hagiographic

This work is in the Marian devotional and hagiographic register (the *De Laudibus Sanctissimae Matris Annae* witnesses, *De Purissima et Immaculata Conceptione Virginis*, *De Laudibus Ordinis Fratrum Carmelitarum*). Apply these conventions:

- **High hymnic register.** Praise-vocabulary, epithets (*Virgo sanctissima*, *Mater clementissima*, *Regina coeli*, *Stella maris*), anaphora ("*Ave...Ave...Ave...*"), and parallelism. Preserve the rhetorical structure rather than smoothing it into flat prose.
- **Marian titles** have settled English conventions: *Mater Dei* = Mother of God; *Theotokos* = Theotokos / God-bearer (preserve the Greek); *Mater dolorosa* = Mother of Sorrows; *Virgo perpetua* = Ever-Virgin; *Stella maris* = Star of the Sea.
- **St. Anne specifically**. The Annan works praise Mary's mother. *Beata Anna*, *Mater Mariae*, *Anna sterilis* (referring to her late-life conception) are the recurring epithets. *Anna* is consistently "Anne" in English (Annan veneration peaked in Trithemius's century).
- **Song of Songs typology is dense.** Marian writers read the *Canticum Canticorum* as a typological account of Mary as bride / mother / hortus conclusus. When you see phrasing that mirrors the Vulgate Cant. 4:7 or 6:8 or similar, let the English match the Douay-Rheims register on those verses.
- **Carmelite material**: *Beata Maria Virgo de Monte Carmelo* = Our Lady of Mount Carmel (settled English); the Carmelite Order's founder narratives invoke Elijah on Carmel as the proto-Carmelite — preserve this typology.
- **Avoid Protestant-inflected language.** Trithemius's Marian theology is high-medieval Catholic; "Blessed Virgin Mary", "Most Holy Mother", "Queen of Heaven" are correct register. Don't flatten to "Mary" or generic "the Virgin" alone.
