# Cluster register notes — cryptographic / occult

This work belongs to Trithemius's cryptographic and angelological corpus (the Polygraphia, the Clavis works, the Sigilla, the Liber Octo Quaestionum). Apply these conventions in addition to the base rules:

- **Preserve spirit-prince and angel names verbatim**: Camuel, Aseliel, Gediel, Pamersiel, Padiel, Caspiel, Aciel, and the dozens of secondary spirit names and their substitute-letters do not get anglicized. Keep the Latin form.
- **Cipher tables are not prose.** When you encounter a column of single words, letters, or short phrases with consistent column widths, those are substitution alphabets. Translate the column headers and any explanatory prose around them, but transcribe the table content verbatim — do not invent translations for substitution columns.
- **Conjurations are formulaic.** Strings like *Camuel aperoys, melym mevomanial, casmoyn cralty bufaco aeli lumar photirion theor besamys* are not Latin and not English. They are constructed cipher cover-text. Quote them verbatim, do not translate.
- **Distinguish the cryptographic from the magical.** Trithemius framed his cipher schemes in spirit-conjuration language for cover. Render the text as he wrote it — preserving the magical surface — without either glossing over the cryptographic substrate or treating the angels as if they were the real subject.
- **Technical vocabulary**: *clavis* = key (in the cryptographic sense); *steganographia* = the art of hidden writing; *polygraphia* = many-modes writing; *cabala* (when used) = substitution scheme, not Hebrew Kabbalah.
