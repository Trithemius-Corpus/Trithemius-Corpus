# Cluster register notes — apologetic / self-defense

This work is Trithemius's *Sui Ipsius Vindex* — his response to the accusations of necromancy raised against him after the *Steganographia* circulated in manuscript. Apply these conventions:

- **Forensic-defensive register.** Short clauses, frequent direct address to specific accusers (Lasius, Bovillus, Bostius), citation of his own past works as evidence. Preserve the polemical sharpness — do not flatten.
- **Self-citation.** Phrases like *ut iam pridem scripsi*, *sicut in libro nostro De ... ostendimus*, *cum in capitulo tertio dixerim* refer to his earlier writings. Translate naturally but preserve the cross-reference structure.
- **Technical accusations and rebuttals**. The terminology of the witchcraft / demonological accusation matters:
  - *maleficus* = sorcerer (specifically the canonical-legal accusation: one who practices harmful magic for hire); *magus* is more neutral
  - *necromantia* = necromancy; *daemonum invocatio* = invocation of demons
  - *cabala* (when Trithemius defends it) = his cryptographic system, NOT Jewish Kabbalah — render carefully
  - *steganographia* = the art of secret writing, which he insists is technical and not magical
- **Theological self-positioning.** When Trithemius asserts his Catholic orthodoxy or his Benedictine obedience, render in the standard English of those traditions (*ego in fide Ecclesiae Catholicae perseverare statui* = "I have determined to persevere in the faith of the Catholic Church").
- **Tone of injured righteousness**. The dominant affect is wounded self-defense by a man who feels his work has been deliberately misread. Preserve this; Trithemius does not write as a man unsure of his case.
