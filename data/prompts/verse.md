# Cluster register notes — Latin verse

This work is Hucbald's *Ecloga de Laude Calvorum* (in praise of bald men, addressed to Charles the Bald). Apply these conventions:

- **Metric awareness.** The poem is in Latin hexameters and elegiac couplets. English cannot reproduce the meter directly; render in clear prose that preserves the parallelism and antithesis rather than chasing meter. Line-by-line correspondence is not required.
- **Mock-encomium register.** The poem is a satirical praise-piece — every commonplace of classical encomium is invoked and inverted. Standard tropes: the praised subject is like the sun, the moon, polished bronze, the clear sky, mythological heroes, philosophers. Preserve the wit; don't translate around it.
- **Classical allusion is dense.** Hucbald cites or alludes to Virgil, Horace, Ovid, the *Aeneid* and the *Eclogues* specifically (the form-genre of *ecloga* itself). When the Latin echoes a known classical phrase, let the English echo the standard translation if possible (Dryden 1697 for Virgil's *Eclogues*, Pope 1717 for the *Iliad*-style epithets, etc.).
- **Wordplay on baldness.** *Calvus* (bald) is the central pun engine — paired with classical *calvaria* (skull), Latin *calvus* and *calvitium* (baldness), and the proper name *Carolus Calvus* (Charles the Bald, the addressee). Where the Latin builds a pun on the bald-head-as-glorious-object, render the English so the pun lands.
- **Address to Charles**: *Carole* = Charles (vocative); *Caesar* = Emperor (he was emperor of the Romans 875–877). Preserve the dignified-yet-comic mixed register.
- **Not Trithemius.** The poem is by Hucbald of Saint-Amand (c. 840–930), not Trithemius. It appears in Trithemius's *De Scriptoribus Ecclesiasticis* as a citation under Hucbald's entry; from there it propagated into early-modern Trithemius collections. Don't render as if it were Trithemius's own voice.
