# Cluster register notes — contemplative / devotional

This work belongs to Trithemius's contemplative and ascetic corpus (*De Operatione Divini Amoris*, *De Vanitate et Miseria Humanae Vitae*, *De Vera Conversione Mentis ad Deum*). Apply these conventions:

- **First-person interior register.** The "soul" or "mind" addresses God, or the author addresses his own soul, or the author addresses a fellow contemplative. Preserve the second-person *Tu* address to God ("Thou" register is fine in this corpus but not required — "You" works if consistent).
- **Augustinian and Bernardine vocabulary** has settled English equivalents:
  - *caritas* = charity (in the theological-virtue sense) or love (in the affective sense) — choose by context; default to "charity" in doctrinal passages, "love" in affective ones
  - *amor Dei* = the love of God (ambiguous between objective and subjective genitive; preserve the ambiguity)
  - *contritio* / *compunctio* = contrition / compunction (both are technical terms — "compunction" is the older, sharper term Bernard prefers)
  - *conversio* = conversion (interior turning to God); *conversio morum* = conversion of life (specifically monastic)
  - *vanitas* = vanity (in the Ecclesiastes sense — emptiness, futility — not "vanity" as modern vanity-table sense)
  - *miseria* = misery / wretchedness; *fragilitas* = frailty
- **Confessional cadence.** When the author addresses God directly in praise or lament (echoing Augustine's *Confessiones*), match the Pusey 1838 English register: serious, plain, not poetic.
- **Avoid neologisms.** Trithemius writes in standard 15th-century devotional Latin. Don't reach for unusual English words; the canonical vocabulary (Pusey for Augustine, Gardner for Bernard, the older PD translations of *De Imitatione Christi*) is the target register.
- **Be careful with "spiritual"**: *spiritualis* can mean "of the Spirit", "non-bodily", or "monastic / clerical" depending on context. Don't default to vague modern "spiritual".
